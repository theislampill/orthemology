## 1. Anas's narration and what it licenses

In Bukhari 3637, the grammatical subject of “أنه حدثهم” is Anas. It is not a statement that the Prophet narrated this episode to Anas. In the conventional chain formulation, it reports Anas narrating the request account to the receiving group in Qatada's transmission. This is more specific lower-link evidence than a bare generic attribution or proof that the two men merely met. It neither identifies all group members nor makes them witnesses to the original event. The text does not say that Anas saw that event. [Bukhari 3637](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3637).

Tabari's first report states the same speaker/group relation more fully. Its subsequent shorter hearing reports concern occurrence; they should not silently provide the missing request detail to a different wording. This agrees with the author's distinction. [Tabari 54:1](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura54-aya1.html).

Sa'id, Shayban and Ma'mar are three named pupils below Qatada preserving the relevant narrative. Bukhari 3868 is another Sa'id line; Bukhari 4867 repeats the Shayban line; Muslim's Ma'mar branch is an equivalent-meaning referral. Tirmidhi prints the Ma'mar request body and his own hasan-sahih judgment, separately from the modern website grade. This corroborates transmission of Qatada's account, not three independent original request witnesses. No manuscript or full narrator audit was performed. [Bukhari 3868](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3868), [4867](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4867), [Muslim 2802a,b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2802a), [Tirmidhi 3286](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3286).

He connects requested certification with an expectation of clear, attended evidence and wider transmission by the surrounding audience and close Companions. Conversely, neither a claimed expectation nor absent testimony automatically proves falsity. The comparison remains to be assessed at the appropriate strength.
