# Range appraisal: al-Ṣafadiyya, SF-S001-L0001–L0238

## Connected argument

The range is a long answer to a short question: may prophetic miracles be identified with psychic powers? Its immediate verdict is severe, but its argument is not exhausted by denunciation. It reconstructs a threefold account of prophecy in terms of exceptional cognition, imaginative representation and psychic efficacy, then works backwards to the metaphysical commitments said to force that reduction. If the source of the world is an eternally complete necessitating cause incapable of voluntary new acts, miracles must be accommodated within ordinary emanative/celestial/psychic processes. The author therefore concentrates first on whether that picture can explain any temporal event, before returning to the intended limits of prophecy.

The main causal argument presses observed change against an eternal total cause. If the cause is complete for its effect, the effect accompanies it. If every causal requisite was eternally present and unchanged, later events cannot be its newly arriving effects. Appeals to later receptivity, prior motions or astronomical configurations move the explanandum: the conditions themselves are events. This reasoning is explicitly scoped by the hypothesis that the Creator produces everything else, including the conditions. It differs from a finite fire or sun whose effect depends upon a recipient not produced by that finite source (L0055).

The argument repeatedly distinguishes continuous activity from a continuously existing particular product. Eternal activity in the sense of doing something need not entail that a given sphere or intellect is eternal. Each product can have been absent and then produced while production has no first instance. This distinction is made using type/individual, universal/particular and generic/determinate causation vocabularies, most exactly at L0073–L0074, L0087–L0094 and L0228–L0236. It permits an alternative to both a wholly inactive deity who later begins all action and a determinate world necessarily co-eternal with God.

A second distinction prevents this alternative from becoming a regress of self-sufficient agents. A succession of effects is not a chain of efficient producers or causal completions in which everything needs another producer and none exists through itself. The whole/member argument explicitly recognises that aggregation can produce properties absent from members, but denies that mere aggregation supplies self-sufficient existence to dependent producers (L0031–L0034). Simultaneous correlations such as fatherhood/sonship are also distinguished from vicious production-circles (L0015, L0138–L0139). These qualifications are substantive rather than incidental.

The next phase targets the denial of divine attributes allegedly used to exclude voluntary acts. The author attacks equivocal uses of composition, part, otherness and dependence, and defends multiple real meanings predicated of one particular bearer. Requiring one's own necessary attributes is not requiring an external maker. A conceptually abstract bare essence is not an externally available thing; nor is a shared universal concept a numerically common entity in several external subjects. These arguments prepare a necessary particular Creator endowed with knowledge, ability, will and action, rather than an abstract existential universal (L0011; L0153–L0155; L0173–L0187; L0198–L0217).

Finally the range returns to the initial question with a decisive concession. Prophetic special powers and real created causal powers are affirmed; what is rejected is the purported completeness of that psychological account of prophecy. The last line opens a defence of powers/natures through which God causes effects, against a view that effects are merely created at their presence (L0238). Consequently an interpretation making this range simply anti-natural-causation or anti-psychic-efficacy would invert its explicit conclusion.

## Source/perfection and the actual common-Creator hypothesis

L0132–L0135 is the most direct source/perfection argument in the range. It says a perfection in the creature comes from its Creator, invokes the opponent's maxim that an effect's perfection comes from its cause, and gives the Creator a greater right to that perfection. It immediately works within a concept of a possible perfection involving no defect, and later excludes creature-specific deficiencies (L0158). Negative predicates count as praise when they entail a positive perfection, not as sheer absence (L0133; L0201).

This is stronger than a merely nominal predication, but its scope must be respected. The argument's actual setting is a Creator who comprehensively produces creatures and their perfections. It does not formulate or establish the universal proposition that every original of every object independently supplies every requisite of that object. L0055 deliberately admits the independence of recipient conditions for finite causes. L0178–L0180 distinguish indispensable self/attribute conditions from efficient production. L0202–L0203 distinguish receptive from efficient causes. L0082 explicitly permits a later act to depend on the same agent's earlier act without dependence on an external other.

A comparative hypothesis about a shared Creator can therefore cite this range, with its additional premises stated. A universal every-original/every-requisite SourceMode rule cannot simply be imported as the text's conclusion. A modern formulation would need separate conditions for complete efficient causality, bare contributing causality, present enabling conditions, intrinsic constitution and external receipt. No new formal or Lean result is claimed here. The text's premise that the Creator of all creatures also produces their changing external conditions remains distinct from a schema treating every ontic prerequisite as an effect supplied by every 'source'.

## Particular necessary bearer and mental universal

The necessary source is repeatedly treated as a particular reality, not universal existence instantiated by everything (L0011; L0183–L0191). Yet the author does not reduce shared predication to homonymy: real external resemblance grounds a shared mental meaning (L0153–L0155). Thus the absence of one numerically shared external universal is compatible with informative general concepts. This matters equally for divine attributes and causal abstractions: a concept can help describe several items without being a separately acting item, and conceptual parts need not be externally composing efficient factors.

The distinction also bears on the eternal-activity argument. A type's duration or continuity does not transfer to each particular member. Conversely, continuous type-level activity does not provide a single eternally co-present complete causal event. The text makes a positive ontological claim about particular existents as well as a negative claim about reified abstractions, but the received philosophical terminology needs modern print control before fine-grained semantic modelling.

## Epistemic routes, signs and attestation

The range relies on observation of change, intuitive or necessary judgements, opponent concessions, dilemma arguments, semantic disambiguation and revelation. It often says a conclusion is necessary once its terms are fully conceived, while also supplying long clarifications showing that conception is contested. The notes do not convert claims of universal agreement into independently surveyed agreement.

Scriptural testimony is not simply appended: hadith variants and question-context are used to distinguish this world's origin from the beginning of divine activity (L0017–L0020), and verses supply the author's pattern of positive divine perfections with non-equivalence (L0133; L0159; L0192). This assignment has not authenticated the reports or compared their printed forms. The embedded al-Rāzī and al-Urmawī passages provide argumentative interlocutors, not independent corroboration of the author's historical claims.

Prophetic signs are framed but their positive attestation has not yet been developed in this range. The closing concession sets a boundary: real natural or psychic efficacy does not suffice as an exhaustive explanation of prophetic distinction. It remains for later ranges to show how particular signs, testimony, revelation or epistemic experience are distinguished and validated. The assignment ends too early to assign a complete theory of miracles or religious knowledge to the work.

## Wider material preserved

The reading includes the opening legal/polemical response, reports about esoteric ritual exegesis and acquired prophecy, a hadith-variant argument, the genealogy of philosophical and kalām positions, a quoted talismanic/astral cosmology, the efficient/final cause distinction, anti-sceptical taxonomy, divine vision, Aḥmad's reported ordeal disputation, oaths by attributes, the linguistic account of dhāt, physical divisibility and transformation of small bodies, and a polemical comparison with Christian Trinitarian descriptions. These are not ornaments to a predetermined source/perfection thesis. They show the range's larger project of coordinating natural philosophy, logic, theology, scripture and prophecy.

## Evidential and interpretative limits

This is one assigned fifth of the received work: 23,915 of a reported 119,021 whitespace words. Its electronic range is exact for the acquired body, not a print pagination. No scans, external editions, cited source books or manuscript witnesses were read. The source has conspicuous transcription anomalies and weakly signalled quotation boundaries; the candidate file identifies bounded control targets. Such anomalies are not silently repaired in the evidence record.

A defeat of an opponent's inference is repeatedly followed by a stronger positive proof; those levels must not be collapsed. The author's commitment to real powers, continuous divine action and particular created effects should not be read out through a uniform modern label. His attributions to schools, named figures and ancient philosophers are data about the received polemic until independently checked. The reading cannot adjudicate all modern interpretations or certify validity of every premise.

## Joins to reconcile

The only external join is SF-S001-L0238 to the next range. It cuts off during a reported kalām denial about the eye after affirming real powers/natures. The next reader should report the exact continuation, distinctions involving causation and divine production, and how the discussion returns to prophetic signs. No previous-range reconciliation is needed because L0001 begins the received body. Internal window joins have all been read continuously, including L0040/L0041, L0101/L0102, L0159/L0160 and L0236/L0237. Later readers should compare whether the present distinction between mental universals and particular bearers is reused for signs, knowledge and prophetic attestation, and whether later qualifications restrict the source/perfection maxim further.
