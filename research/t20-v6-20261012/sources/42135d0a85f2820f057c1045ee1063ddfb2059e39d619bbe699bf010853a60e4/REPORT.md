# Owed worship and the adequacy of a substituted response

10 October 2026 UTC. Bounded primary-source continuation of the original-authority inquiry. Separate research evidence only. No protected edit, integration, contemporary duty adjudication, or closure.

## Result

**Ibn Taymiyya’s al-ʿUbūdiyya supplies a concrete reason why one proposed substitute can fail to perform the same response: eliciting a bare conception or an affective state does not, by itself, accomplish a determinate acknowledgement. It does not supply an additional constitutive selection argument against a substitute that remains adequate in relevant content, intention, and practical purpose, apart from the disputed requirement to use this particular form.** Its account positively affirms prescribed worship and obedience. The absence of the additional argument is neither a denial of that account nor a new reason to suspend the already earned authority appraisal.

The strongest love argument includes submission, not merely admiration. But its own distinction between obligatory and recommended conduct prevents deriving the grade of every response from the intensity or perfection of love alone. Authenticated source-owned normative disclosure remains sufficient to warrant a particular obligation on the inherited T19 account; an independent constitutive derivation is not a further prerequisite. The remaining question is explanatory, and local.

The exact new credit is a received primary reading and a bounded application of its response-content discrimination. Actual-token responsiveness, objective target-fit, owed practical allegiance, and the distinction between testimony and constitution were already established. No new general semantic theory, duty theorem, or authority foundation is claimed.

## The controlling commitments and the strongest rival

The original-authority report, its scoped successor, the conditional-right review, and the direct self-obligation dependency return control this operation. They retain sourcehood, intrinsic necessity, Knowledge, Will, Wisdom, due worship and allegiance, the in-scope retained-control principle, and the truthful normative-testimony route. These results are not reopened. The recent Majmūʿ 18 operation correctly ended as dependency verification without a new creature-directed selection premise.

A fair rival must not grant that the source has rightful selection control here and then appeal to a previously good alternative. That would leave the inherited specification principle untouched. Nor can it grant an authenticated truthful assertion that this duty obtains while withholding assent solely because its independent constitutive explanation is unknown.

The narrower explanatory comparison concerns two responses to the same actual source. The rival acknowledges the owed worship relation, takes the source’s actual communicated preference seriously, and accepts that performance and force matter. It disputes whether this particular relation gives the source control to exclude another form that would otherwise render the relevant acknowledgement. It cannot establish that claim by indifference, mere prior nonprohibition, or an imagined grant. Equally, the affirmative cannot settle it merely by calling the preferred act “the obligatory response.”

There are still three possible ways to settle a particular case: a warranted scoped title, an independently shown failure of the proposed substitute to render the owed response, or suitably authenticated truthful normative disclosure. This operation tests the first two without making the first a condition of the third.

## What the source actually argues

### Worship includes loving submission

The opening question asks what worship and servanthood comprise. At received 10:149–152, Ibn Taymiyya includes inward and outward practices and relates them to the stated purpose of creation and prophetic teaching. At 153, his analysis requires love together with submission. Compelled submission accompanied by hatred is insufficient; love without submission, illustrated by love of a child or friend, is also insufficient. He assigns complete love and submission uniquely to God. This is the author’s endorsed explanation, rather than an adversary’s view.

The distinction at 154–158 makes the limit of a causal reading equally explicit. All creatures are subject to divine origination and governance, including those who do not willingly worship. Recognizing causal dependence therefore does not itself amount to the responsive worship under discussion. Conversely, it would falsify the account to describe what is owed as detached admiration or acknowledgement without practical allegiance.

This is substantive support for the inherited due-response appraisal. It also defeats the claim that ordinary affection exhausts the source’s conception of worship. It does not separately show that every particular form proposed as an exercise of selection control falls within the owed submission. To obtain that further conclusion by defining “complete submission” as accepting every disputed selection would put the local title into the premise. This is not an objection to normative premises or an insistence on a norm-free proof. It identifies which normative content still needs appraisal rather than a change of label.

### Love answers the beloved and can misfire in its means

The continuous discussion at 189–193 moves from attachment to wealth and position to loving what the beloved loves. The source does not equate worship with obtaining one’s own desired benefits from God. At 191–192, following the messenger is connected to realizing what God loves; strong love moves practical willing, subject to capacity. At 193, however, a person’s sincere love can be paired with deficient understanding and an ineffective path. A chosen method need not be praiseworthy merely because the love is good. The discussion concludes its twofold dependence account in the opening portion of 195.

At 205–208 the author separates love, awareness or attainment of its object, and ensuing enjoyment. He then criticizes a supposed lover who takes love as immunity from correction. Even interpersonal love can be ignorant or unjust if it does not know or seek the beloved’s good. At 209–214 the application concerns claims of spiritual privilege, the confusion of universal providence with approval, and the replacement of source-conforming action by private preference. These are intelligible reasons why feeling loving, or successfully inducing a feeling, need not supply an adequate response.

These arguments must not be weakened into pure psychology: the author makes practical and normative claims. They must also not be extended to establish the disputed deontic grade without another step. At 172 he expressly distinguishes leaving recommended acts from leaving duties. At 173–174, 212–213, and 218–219, he includes both obligatory and recommended conduct among what the source loves and prescribes. The graded account is compatible with selection authority; it does not refute it. Its consequence here is narrower: love of the source’s preferences cannot alone be used as a rule making every superior or preferred performance obligatory.

### The source’s own prescription account

At 172–174 the author presents worship’s two requirements as directing it to God and rendering it in the prescribed way, with sincerity and correctness both required. At 234–236 he returns to these principles and expressly joins belief in the messenger’s reports with obedience to his commands. It would understate the text to treat this as merely a catalogue of desirable experiences.

These are the source’s endorsed normative claims, supported in the passage through its scriptural argument. Reading their occurrence here is not a new authentication of their divine provenance or a fresh transmission adjudication. If the relevant source-owned normative content is authenticated on the inherited account, it can warrant a particular obligation without a separately reconstructed title proof. The present negative result about an additional constitutive explanation does not disable that route or leave all particular duties unknowable.

## The concrete substitution argument

The complete unit begins near the end of received 225 and concludes at 233. The preceding discussion distinguishes worship’s object from the worshipper’s subjective absorption and preserves Creator–creature discrimination. Against an asserted hierarchy that places repetition of an isolated name or pronoun above a content-bearing acknowledgement, Ibn Taymiyya gives an articulated performance argument.

At 226–227, a bare name, taken by itself, contributes a conception without affirmation or denial. The author expressly allows the possibility of accompanying heart-knowledge or state supplying benefit from elsewhere. This is a distinction between what the utterance itself contributes and what the recipient already supplies; it is not a claim that every short expression or every person using one has an empty mind.

The source provides its own controls against literalism:

- At 226 and 228, a one-word answer can convey a complete answer because the question supplies its predicate. The interpretation depends on the actual exchange.
- At 227, the fear of dying partway through a confession is rejected by reference to the person’s actual intention. An accidental incomplete surface sequence is not mechanically assigned the opposite conviction.
- At 231, the short invocation associated with an activity is understood through an omitted activity-specific predicate. The author prefers a scope covering the act itself to a scope covering only its beginning.
- At 229–233, the text gives multiple meaningful invocations and explains its grammatical vocabulary. It does not infer that all acceptable acknowledgement must be one uniquely fixed string merely because complete content matters.

### Executed application

Hold fixed an independently warranted response target: for example, consciously acknowledging the source rather than merely being put into an unspecified elevated mood. Compare an act that actually renders the relevant acknowledgement with a proposed replacement claimed to be adequate solely because it induces a similar feeling or evokes a name. If the replacement supplies neither the relevant content nor a context or intention that supplies it, the proposed equivalence fails on performance grounds. The reason is identifiable without first describing the chosen wording as obligatory.

This is a genuine discriminator, but its conclusion is conditional and modest. An incomplete contribution by an expression is not automatically an incomplete act by its user. Existing understanding, the occasion, intention, and other conduct can matter. Nor does one expression’s ability to teach or articulate content establish that repeating already-known content is useless. The passage supports no universal information-gain requirement for valuable worship.

The discriminator also does not make the complete utterance obligatory merely by making it better suited to this function. A determinate duty would additionally require an owed response at the relevant scope and sufficient grounds that the proposed substitution does not discharge it. Those premises may be established in a particular case; this operation does not invent one.

### The adequate nonidentical response survives

A nonidentical response that supplies the relevant acknowledgement through its own meaningful content or warranted context is not excluded by this semantic argument. The author’s ellipsis and intention examples prevent excluding it by surface difference alone. Whether it is nevertheless disallowed in a prescribed ritual depends on the actual norm, its content and scope, not this semantic comparison alone. This report neither licenses a contemporary substitution nor denies a separately warranted restriction.

The same qualification applies to relational response. Once a rightful obligatory specification is warranted, deliberately substituting another otherwise good act can fail precisely because it refuses that owed direction. That inherited result remains. But using that description before warranting the local requirement would not independently explain the source’s title to exclude the alternative. The selected unit eliminates an inadequate-equivalence argument; it does not eliminate the strongest surviving otherwise-adequate-response rival.

## Credit and the stopping point

The Barīra token-force report already owns the principle that appropriate responsiveness follows the actual source act and its clarified force, rather than a stronger act inferred from rank. The objective-goodness source note and its controlling CP23 account already own the distinction between actual taste or installed-objective success and fitting target-attainment. The original-authority records already own love, worship, obedience, normative modes, retained control, and the testimonial route. None is rediscovered here.

The bounded historical search found no exact earlier primary reading receipt for the selected al-ʿUbūdiyya units in the inspected authority records. The earlier CP02 appraisal expressly listed Majmūʿ 10:149 as an attributed but not freshly read source. This is limited record-search evidence, not a corpus-wide priority claim. The new contribution is direct source control and the applied comparison between an expression’s own contribution and contextual or intentional completion. Its general ingredients are inherited.

The selection-title residual survives as an additional explanatory question. Repeating the two-principles formula, collecting further descriptions of intense love, or treating causal subjection as voluntary allegiance would not add the needed local premise. No further source campaign is justified by this operation alone. A new operation would need a concrete argument for selection standing in this relation, a genuinely new adequacy discriminator, or relevant authenticated normative content. This report records a completed source operation with a bounded positive application and an exact negative result; it does not close T20.
