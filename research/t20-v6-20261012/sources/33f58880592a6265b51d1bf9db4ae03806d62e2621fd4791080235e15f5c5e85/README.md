# Full-law gradient action tails: intermediate evidence

## Exact claim boundary

For the specified characteristic law of a coercive polynomial gradient and the specified unit-coefficient transport/reaction action, every supplied initial probability has finite action length on each positive-time tail [t0,∞), t0>0. Finite initial expected energy suffices for finite length from zero. The heavy-tail counterexample shows that the initial segment can diverge without that assumption; it lies outside the all-moment initial-hierarchy class. Fixed-polynomial moment-tail bounds are not uniform reconstruction bounds across degrees, laws or data perturbations.

The known forward-law limit theorem and finite-dimensional analytic gradient inequality are inherited. The proof does not establish a general ambient infinite-dimensional Riemannian structure, reverse existence for arbitrary moment data, an algorithm for the analytic exponent, or numerical integrator convergence. The source-interface's unit action convention is retained without transferring a torus density theorem to arbitrary measures on Euclidean space.
