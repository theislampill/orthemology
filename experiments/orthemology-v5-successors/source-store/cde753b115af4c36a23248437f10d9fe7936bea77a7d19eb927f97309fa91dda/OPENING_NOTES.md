# Continuous opening reread of Darʾ volume 8

Lead reader’s reread, 7 October 2026. Bound electronic paragraphs P0001–0115; overlaps the first continuous study, not an independent manuscript witness. Source positions are in [bounded coverage](COVERAGE.json). No scan or complete apparatus reading claimed.

## Argument and voice

P1–7 distinguishes verbal disagreement, diversity of valid routes, and substantive disagreement about truth or obligation. The legality/obligation of requiring an argument differs from whether an argument is correct. Affiliation with a legal school does not automatically make every later speculative doctrine that school's founder's position.

P8–14 quotes Abu al-Faraj al-Maqdisi; P15 introduces Ibn Taymiyya's response. The reply distinguishes the prophetic call to the two testimonies from a universal demand first to demonstrate a maker by one speculative sequence. P24 makes reasoning obligatory for a person who cannot obtain the relevant duty/faith without it, not a universally identical first cognitive step. P30 rejects deliberately undoing already acquired knowledge so that it can be regenerated by an approved method. This is not a modern empirical claim that memory loss cannot occur.

P31–52 carries legal examples about prior fulfilment, maturity, knowledge, obligations and different starting circumstances. These are part of the author's analogy about order, rather than noise to be discarded. They concern historical jurisprudence, not new practical legal advice. P33 explicitly separates mere maker-knowledge from the fuller confession required by the author's theology.

P54 is Abu al-Husayn al-Basri's quoted mandatory epistemic chain, not Ibn Taymiyya's own required sequence. The author challenges the claimed indispensability of the steps in P58–62. P63–67 extends the criticism to mystical stage systems and philosophical/logical learning sequences. A route can be sound for some without exhausting every person's route. Conversely, discovering an alternative does not justify rejecting all valid content of the first route. The formal audit reconstructs one sufficient inferential architecture, not a universal psychological or epistemic prerequisite graph.

P68–75 discusses value, command, will, love and approval. P69 allows revelation both to disclose existing practical features and to introduce relations not previously present. The author's distinction between willing an occurrence and approving it prevents causal production from automatically granting normative endorsement. The universal-creation premises in this theological discussion must not be used as independent proof of their own scope.

P78–100 is a long quoted treatment by Abu al-Faraj Sadaqa ibn al-Husayn. Its claims that knowledge cannot be initially present, must come by reasoning and cannot arise by report or inspiration must not be attributed to Ibn Taymiyya without his response. P101 marks that response. P102 distinguishes his endorsement of the human-origination argument from his criticism of the accidents argument. Quoted embryological and causal examples remain historically situated; reading them is not independent biological verification.

P104–107 distinguishes a text reporting a proposition on authority from a text teaching a rational demonstration. Rational grounds learned from a book can be assessed as rational grounds; their written or scriptural medium does not make their force exclusively testimonial. This does not authenticate a text's claimed divine authorship.

P108–115 goes beyond mere epistemological permissibility. The author positively affirms actual basic recognition and argues that denying every such case requires an unsupported exhaustive survey. He appeals to common recognition and treats acknowledgment of the Creator as deeply established in human disposition. The Pharaoh interpretation is part of his source-grounded theological account. This is stronger than the lead reader's independently established empirical result, which remains limited: the passage does not itself provide a fresh population study, a universally validated faculty model or a test for diagnosing every dissenter's cognition.

## Relevance and retained orthogonal material

The most immediate use is a scope guard for the dependency DAG and source-veracity audit: conditional entailment need not be the only route by which someone knows the conclusion. A second use is the separate classification of rational content versus testimonial medium. Legal first-obligation debates, school affiliation, scholarly attribution and spiritual-stage schemes are retained because they articulate the method through different subject matters, not because they prove the target metaphysical claim.

These passages provide no new direct closure of the disputed full-source-role requirement. They also do not license dismissing the present common-source argument merely because another route may exist. They clarify what a successful argument, its interpretation and a person's knowledge need and need not share.
