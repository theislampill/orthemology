# Certification capacity and present appointment: a direct rival-source check

10 October 2026 UTC. Additive T20 investigation. No protected change, integration, T21, contemporary case, or programme closure.

## Result

**No new independent discriminator of an actual recognition or divine-attestation episode was established.** The selected capacity argument and its main application limits were already assessed. The useful source-control increment is direct verification of al-Juwaynī's own strongest reply: he expressly distinguishes an act constituting a present appointment from a report whose truth requires veracity. This is not an unacknowledged repair supplied by a modern interpreter.

That verification narrows the proper target of criticism. A public act can genuinely appoint a representative without certifying every statement the representative makes. But identifying the act as an appointment still requires warranted source, force, context and authority. Al-Juwaynī's appeal to necessary recognition of the source's indicating intention remains the operative epistemic premise. The present reading neither newly establishes that premise for a historical miracle nor defeats the retained contextual-recognition appraisal.

The positive distinction and its temporal limits were already developed in the 2 October [Asfahani source-scope addendum, §4.2](https://github.com/theislampill/orthemology/blob/7efc6217c090b92d65f2c4cb8b011c18eb96bf7c/experiments/orthemology-v5-successors/source-store/615a7aae33d622f5d0cec7eb12a49c2e2515b29501927970f0866e43f05c5717/ASFAHANI_SOURCE_SCOPE_ADDENDUM.md). They must not be credited again as a philosophical discovery. The new direct source check corroborates and properly attributes that stronger rival.

## 2. The actual rival is stronger than bare event-to-truth

Al-Juwaynī's *Irshād*, 1950 printed 324–336, was checked directly, using the complete corresponding electronic chapter and the scan of those thirteen pages. The source voices matter: the Muʿtazilite objections and Abū Isḥāq's proposed proof are reported positions; al-Juwaynī's replies and final preferred proof are his own.

- **324–328:** The bare physical event does not carry its alleged message independently of its setting. A deliberately matching royal response is presented as immediately intelligible confirmation. The king's being unjust does not, he argues, prevent recognizing what the king does. This disputes the need for antecedent moral-goodness certification, not the relevance of intentional action. [Corresponding electronic opening](https://books.rafed.net/view/3392/page/132), [continuation](https://books.rafed.net/view/3392/page/133).
- **328–331:** He denies that the royal example is an inferential analogy requiring an added common middle term; it illustrates purported necessary recognition. His divine example assumes knowledge of divine power, knowledge and the relevant exclusive control. He expressly attributes an intention to confirm. His further monopoly claim excludes ordinary signs and requires connection to a prophetic claim. Those are substantive commitments, not consequences of the word “power.” [Electronic 134](https://books.rafed.net/view/3392/page/134).
- **331–333:** He directly raises the circularity objection to establishing divine veracity by revelation or consensus. His initial answer is restricted to present appointment: an effective appointment constitutes the role rather than reports an earlier event. He then explicitly requires divine veracity for the envoy's subsequent particular reports. [Electronic 135](https://books.rafed.net/view/3392/page/135).
- **333–336:** He reports Abū Isḥāq's attempt to derive veracity from the possibility of intelligible obligation and rejects it as unpersuasive. His own replacement invokes knowledge, willing, inner speech, contraries and the unchanging character of that speech. It is a substantive, disputed theory, not an inference from force of appointment alone. This operation does not validate that theory. [Electronic 136](https://books.rafed.net/view/3392/page/136).

The [Columbia/NYU scan](https://sites.dlib.nyu.edu/viewer/books/columbia_aco002532/display?lang=ar) verifies the decisive wording and the 1950 edition. It supplies independent access to al-Juwaynī's text, not an independent philosophical argument merely because it is another presentation. The modern electronic page numbers differ: 132–136 correspond to printed 324–336 at this locus.

## 3. Executed comparison: what does the stronger reply establish?

The direct reply establishes an important conditional route already defended in the inherited addendum:

1. The source has the relevant appointing standing.
2. The act is genuinely an effective appointment of this bearer in this scope.
3. The recipient competently recognizes that act through adequate contextual grounds.
4. The recipient can thereby know the resulting present appointment without deriving it from an unrestricted premise that every assertion of the source is true.

The fact is made by the appropriately authorized act. This is why knowing the act's occurrence and force can do different work from trusting a description of an independently obtaining fact. It does not make a non-appointing gesture appoint merely because a claimant requests it.

Three tests delimit this reply without weakening it:

**Past commission:** A present constitutive act does not itself establish a commission alleged to have existed before the act. The target must remain time-indexed. This is an application guard, not an accusation that al-Juwaynī universally asserts retroactive commissioning.

**Subsequent report:** Appointment does not make every later report true. Al-Juwaynī explicitly accepts the additional veracity burden. Criticism premised on his simply overlooking it would miss his argument.

**Recognized force:** The unjust-king case shows that recognized intentional authorization need not wait for proof of complete moral goodness. It does not establish that a response stripped of known purpose, context and effective force remains certifying. Conversely, Ibn Taymiyyah's objection cannot fairly be represented as denial of all immediately recognized intentional indications. The dispute concerns what sustains that recognition under the respective substantive accounts of divine action.

The inherited token-force clarification already supplies a positive source-sensitive route for settling an utterance's actual force. The original-authority report already separates standing, intended exercise, successful normative effect, and a testimonial route to an obligation. The current comparison is consistent with both. It introduces no new requirement that every ordinary knower reconstruct a convention or prove every faculty's reliability.

The capacity argument still cannot by itself establish which particular event carries the necessary intended meaning. Replacing that missing application with an asserted act of appointment merely shifts where the same evidence is needed. Equally, incomplete reflective explanation of a previously warranted act-recognition supplies no defeater. This preserves the strongest affirmative position while isolating the exact remaining application premise.
