## What the evidence changes

The research question was not whether a source label can be copied. It was whether the actual source-use gap could be narrowed. Two kinds of evidence now do different jobs: direct observation fixes a fact about an individual printed witness, while a contemporaneous declaration supplies evidence of an editorial relationship. Neither is reducible to the other.

A date-bound witness can eliminate a proposed later stage as the first introduction of an already present feature. It cannot identify which earlier actor or operation produced that feature. A methodological declaration can provide defeasible evidence that the stated practice was followed. A claim of universal occurrence-level conformity is stronger and needs additional evidence. The distinction gives testimony real positive weight without making it infallible. Agreement with an alternative witness likewise does not identify an act of borrowing, correction or deliberate selection.

These are claim-relative assessments. They avoid both overpromotion and excessive scepticism: the narrower positive claims are established, while the stronger genealogy remains open. No probability is assigned to the remaining production hypotheses, and describing a compatible hypothesis does not establish its historical actuality.

## Relation to the original programme

The earlier source-effect theorem begins after adequate source identity and interpretation have been supplied. This investigation improves an actual input at that boundary. It does not add a runtime adapter or reuse the earlier conditional theorem as evidence that its own source assumptions hold.

The existing owners retain their credit: Sixth for copied-root conservation and action-relevant warrant; Ninth and the recovered continuation materials for task-relative evidence preservation and reacquisition; Tenth for source-context restoration, selected-origin dependence and conditional read cost; Eleventh for the bounded private role-convention assessment. A quoted source, a translated occurrence, an acquisition root and a present assertion remain different objects.

The new finding concerns textual custody and editorial source use. It does not turn an objection-role convention into source-forced authorial attribution, infer current-carrier endorsement, decide theological truth, establish numerical identity, or authenticate an original medieval utterance.

## The remaining discriminating evidence

A further advance needs evidence that singles out the local production decision: an identified working exemplar, a translator’s note, a correction list, an annotated proof or a contemporary erratum. Another mirror of the same printed wording does not supply that evidence. The available evidence already answers the bounded present question, so this lane stops at that limit rather than converting more copies into apparent progress.
