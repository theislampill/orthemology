# Existential determination and necessary particular sourcehood

## Result of the new probe

The primary argument against an undetermined realisation is stronger than a general preference for more explanation. It challenges an alleged complete account which supplies a bearer, capacities, and even a factor favouring an outcome, but leaves the outcome's actual occurrence unsettled. Freshly inspected passages develop that challenge directly and apply it to voluntary action as well as other production. They therefore deserve a stronger test than dismissing them as a definition of a necessary being.

The test does not close the necessary-source argument. Even granting that every received occurrence is fixed by its complete actual productive determination, a critic can still distinguish that principle from the claim that an original bearer's whole actuality has an intrinsic determination excluding its absence. A further transfer is required. The most effective remaining objection grants the stronger local determination claim instead of appealing immediately to randomness. It asks why an original actual basis must fall under an existential realisation principle whose clearest applications already concern received being.

This continuation supplies a source-supported assessment of that transfer. It does not supply a new actual-world warrant for INTR.N2 or INTR.N3. Its positive contribution is to identify and test the primary's more demanding anti-arbitrariness rationale, establish its exact relation to the source's asymmetric treatment of existence and absence, and show why a weaker realisation argument does not silently restore the modal conclusion. The account also specifies what an answer through direct particular recognition would have to recognise. A correct description of this evidential boundary is the result; no additional necessity theorem is claimed.

## The inherited target remains fixed

The admitted case is the evidence-supported correction of a report about the displayed Minḥāj header. Its truth concerns the actual page. On the admitted cognitive interpretation, the correction is made because the evidence is understood. The local causal, constitutive, evidential, and truth relations remain distinct. The correction supplies no independent observation of an ultimate bearer's nature.

Seventh preserved four burdens. P1 is original productive completion. P2 identifies a particular bearer of that original actuality. N2 concerns an adequate nonbrute existential explanation. N3 requires an intrinsic explanation with unconditional existence-entailing force for that same bearer. This paper conditionally grants P1 and P2 when testing the modal ascent. It does not pretend that the new passages have established them for the actual correction.

The target is whole actual productive origin, including the relevant actual exercises and receiving modes. It is not bare power to reproduce an endpoint. If a rival grants the stipulated complete-source role, it must grant it at that stronger scope. The inherited same-target uniqueness result is unchanged. Likewise, genuine intentional effective agency retains its respect-specific knowledge, will, and power; none of those predicates has been replaced by a selector function.

Sixth already distinguished absence of a producer from positive intrinsic sufficiency. It already allowed particular recognition, denied that every received power is passive, and separated efficient self-origination from constitution. Seventh added the symmetric modal-warrant discipline and rejected a shortcut from actual powers to exhaustive modal coverage and same-bearer retention. Those are inputs, not new discoveries here. The new question is whether a stronger argument about *complete determination*, now checked in its own primary context, can earn the existential bridge that those inquiries left open.

## The strongest affirmative rationale

An adequate account of a real occurrence must reach its actual occurrence. Stating a nature's permissions is not yet doing that. If a device may produce either of two outputs, an account of its permitted outputs can be perfectly informative while leaving unanswered which output actually occurred. A more detailed account may settle that question by identifying the actual operation, its inputs, and its efficacy. The difference is between two explanatory targets, not between ignorance and a mysterious new object called realisation.

The source advocate presses this distinction at the ultimate level. An account which explains everything an original bearer does while treating that bearer's own presence as an unaccounted-for favouring condition has not yet explained the actuality on which all those explanations rely. This is an argument about an indispensable resource, rather than a wish that reality be aesthetically tidy. An absent capacity is no actual supplier. A specification that merely permits instantiation does not itself instantiate its subject. If what makes the account effective is the presence of a real bearer, that presence must not be silently counted as already explained by the account of its conditional powers.

The argument need not picture a prior state of absolute nothingness. It asks about the whole actuality of a presently admitted source. Nor must it posit two positive objects, existence and nonexistence, competing for selection. There need be no positive producer of continued absence. The affirmative can instead ask what makes the claimed positive actuality available. This avoids a weak objection to rationalist reasoning: that it must find a symmetrical efficient cause for every negation.

The strongest claim is not simply that causes have effects. It is that a genuinely complete existential explanation leaves no residual realisation which is itself supplied by nothing. If a purported explanation only favours an outcome while remaining compatible with its failure, the advocate asks what explains the difference between favourable eligibility and actual obtaining. No equal probability or probability measure is asserted here. Adding another merely favourable condition would not, on this view, settle the remaining contrast. The point is not that favourable conditions have no explanatory value. It is that their contribution falls short of the complete contrastive target.

This rationale has genuine force against a familiar evasion. A theory cannot describe an actual bearer and its operation, then cite that description as though it answered why the whole bearer is actual. Nor may it redescribe its unaccounted-for realisation as a disposition and claim that the question has disappeared. The affirmative has identified something that an ultimate account must either explain or openly leave without the explanation being sought.

There is nevertheless a further commitment in saying that every such residual contrast *must* be settled. The warrant for that commitment cannot consist only in the observation that, if it were true, no contrast would remain. It can be defended as a basic rational insight about positive actuality. It can also be defended by the explanatory achievements of determinate production. But either defence has to establish its scope. The actuality of a received exercise and the whole existence of an original bearer are different targets even when both are actual.

## Trying the ascent with the strongest determination premise

Conditionally fix g as the particular original bearer established by P1 and P2. Suppose every relevant received occurrence has a complete actual productive determination, and suppose that the determination really necessitates the occurrence when it obtains. These are stronger assumptions than merely granting locally grounded capacities. They give the affirmative the full benefit of the fresh determination rationale in its clearest productive application. Restricting the concession to received production is the critic’s move in this test; it is not the primary author’s self-imposed restriction. The primary author intends the broader existential claim.

Now ask why g exists. If g receives its existence from another actual source, it is not original in that same respect. If g's own intrinsic reality unconditionally suffices for its existence, its absence is excluded. The argument reaches necessity when it excludes every remaining option between those two answers. The target is not satisfied by pointing out that one of the two would answer the question.

The outstanding option is easiest to see without an indeterministic machine. Suppose, for the test, that g is an original active basis and its complete actual determinations fix every occurrence in the target's whole productive origin. Its own existence is simply primitive. The supposition preserves a proper unborrowed productive exercise of g and covers every actual productive contributor and receiving mode in the stipulated target. It does not omit a cause of g while calling that cause irrelevant to the endpoint: g’s unreceived existence is itself the conditional original-source grant. The supposition grants no external supplier of g and no gap in those complete actual-origin determinations. That productive grant does not additionally stipulate an exhaustive existential explanation of g’s own unreceived actuality. It therefore cannot be rejected merely by finding an unsettled outcome inside one of those determinations. Its contested feature is the absence of an existential explanation for the basis itself.

The affirmative replies that the same anti-arbitrariness principle applies to that feature: if g's nature does not exclude absence, actuality rather than absence remains an unsettled existential contrast. That is the best reply. It does not depend on the rival's producing itself, having no real powers, or being a passive relay. But it is also exactly where the principle changes domain. The reason previously used to complete a received occurrence is now used to deny that a whole original actuality can be primitive.

Calling g contingent does not independently prove this option possible. Seventh's warrant discipline remains in force. The critic can make a narrower objection: nothing yet granted about received production establishes that g has the stronger intrinsic status. That objection suspends judgement about g's complete possible absence. It need not invent or certify a brute cosmos. Thus there are two different assessments: the proposed brute ontology lacks positive modal and actual warrant here, while the attempted necessity inference still lacks a warranted bridge.

This is why the fresh source is useful despite the negative result. A weak response would deny all determinate explanation or appeal to stochastic events without explaining their relevance. The stronger response grants the source advocate's complete determination premise for received occurrences and locates the remaining disagreement at the existential status of the original bearer. The remaining objection survives the stronger primary test. The same residual is tested against this more explicit primary rationale; no new logical narrowing beyond Sixth and Seventh is claimed.

## Why intrinsic realisation requires its own account

The affirmative may insist that the original bearer is positively actual *through itself*. That can mean more than the absence of an external cause. On the strongest reading, the source's own nature is what makes its existence unconditional. Such a claim is not self-production. It need not introduce an earlier efficient act, and it is not refuted by saying that an agent must already exist to act.

The term nevertheless has two materially different uses in the present dispute. In the first, g is actual and its existence is unreceived. In the second, g has a positively intelligible nature which makes absence impossible. A rival granting the first has not granted the second. The primary's own division and terminology controls make it especially important to ask which claim an argument established before drawing the stronger conclusion.

Two descriptions help expose the attempted shortcut. If the alleged intrinsic determinant includes g's already instantiated reality, it entails g's actuality as specified. That does not show that the instantiated reality obtains unconditionally. If it is only a characterisation of what a g would be, its existential force must be demonstrated. A conditional characterisation can be correct without being instantiated. Neither observation rules out a genuinely existence-entailing intrinsic nature. They show why the content and warrant of that nature matter.

A noncausal rival adds a further challenge. It proposes an autonomous explanatory ground which realises a concrete bearer without efficiently producing it and without necessitating that realisation. The fresh source rationale gives the affirmative a direct question for this proposal: why count a ground that leaves realisation unsettled as a complete answer to the existential contrast? That is a stronger challenge than the irrelevant allegation that the bearer would have to act before existing.

But identifying the question is not settling it. The rival can distinguish a real nonnecessitating explanation from no explanation at all, and decline the demand for a further fact choosing the token outcome. The affirmative can defend the demand as a condition of full existential explanation. Neither can settle the disagreement by using *complete* to mean its own favoured theory. A rule about efficient production also cannot automatically decide the adequacy of every constitutive or noncausal relation.

There is a decisive classification consequence, inherited rather than newly proved. If a ground really explains the bearer's whole existence, that bearer is received under the broad existential relation. It is not a counterexample granting broad originality and then denying necessity. It challenges the move from original efficient sourcehood to broad existential autonomy, or the identification of the ultimate autonomous ground with one concrete bearer. If the ground is merely the bearer's already actual nature redescribed, the original explanatory question returns. These alternatives must be distinguished before the new determination argument can be applied.

## The weaker actuality argument does not restore the modal step

The adjacent source material intentionally considers a route not depending on agreement about necessitation. Its availability matters: the source advocate does not have to make every original-source argument stand or fall with the strongest determination principle. Yet weakening the premise changes what the route can establish.

A merely uninstantiated supplier does not actualise anything. The affirmative can accordingly object to an explanation composed solely of specifications whose existence has never been supplied. That objection reaches a real shortcoming, regardless of whether actual causes necessitate their effects. It is not a proof that every active received order is impossible. A serious productive infinitist grants actual active suppliers throughout, rather than asking a first absent supplier to begin an infinite construction.

The difference is not erased by calling each member possible in itself. A member's lack of unreceived existence does not alone show that it is presently unreal. Treating the whole order as still awaiting realisation requires the productive-completion warrant under dispute. Conversely, the infinitist does not establish its whole order merely by assigning actual status to every entry of a description. The actual-world burden remains on the positive account, and the need for an original basis remains a substantive metaphysical issue.

For the present test P1 and P2 were already granted conditionally. Reaching an actual original supplier again cannot add the missing unconditional modal content. A weakened actuality argument might support the earlier stage if independently defended; it cannot borrow the stronger conclusion from a necessitation premise it has deliberately set aside.

Nothing here requires a first temporal event, a finite chain, or traversal of an infinite series. Nor is an original covering source inconsistent with all non-well-founded subordinate ancestry by definition. The antecedent relations and target remain those fixed in the accepted argument. The present inquiry offers no new temporal metaphysics.

## Direct recognition remains a different warrant route

A demand for a separately inferred universal principle is not the only way to justify necessity. Someone might claim rational recognition of this very reality's intrinsic existential sufficiency. Such recognition, if warranted with that content, would do work which the absence of an observed supplier cannot do. Its legitimacy need not depend on first articulating the universal rule which it exemplifies.

The fresh determination discussion does not turn ordinary awareness of dependence into that stronger recognition. The actual case establishes a correction and its identified local dependencies. It does not supply an independently checked recognition of an ultimate source's whole existential nature. Nor does textual evidence that a primary author regards a principle as rationally immediate establish that this research has acquired the corresponding de re insight.

The objection is not that basic rational knowledge is impossible, that disagreement defeats it automatically, or that it needs an empirical measurement of consciousness. The question is what is apprehended. Awareness that one is dependent differs from apprehension of the particular ultimate ground; apprehension of that ground as actual differs from grasping its unconditional sufficiency. If the asserted recognition has only the weaker content, subsequent use of necessity changes its content. If it has the stronger content, that is the warrant to defend against the precise rival, not a result supplied by the word recognition.

The primary affirmative therefore remains a serious rational proposal. It does not receive automatic evidential priority merely because the critic lacks a proven alternative cosmos. A critic likewise does not obtain a proven alternative merely because this attempted inference falls short.

## Exact change from Seventh and stopping point

The independently supported increment is source-bound. Newly inspected arguments directly challenge a merely favouring determinant, constrain appeals to voluntary action, and distinguish positive realisation from producing a negative state. Their nearby treatments of actuality and terminology show why the resulting existential claim cannot be imported with an unchanged label. The connected reconstruction tests those commitments against a rival that grants determinate actual production at the stronger whole-origin scope.

This is not a new proof of explanatory exhaustiveness. No candidate intrinsic nature has acquired independent existence-entailing warrant. The complete determination of a received occurrence has not become the necessary existence of its determinant. No argument here turns the necessity of a role into the necessity of its present occupant. The stronger modal-coverage and source-retention premises from Seventh are neither assumed nor disproved.

The strongest affirmative result is a sharper challenge: an ultimate account should identify the positive actuality doing its explanatory work and say whether it explains that actuality or candidly leaves it primitive. An alleged nonnecessitating realisation must explain why its type of explanation answers the exact existential target. These demands have substantive primary support and are not reducible to an objection against efficient self-production.

The decisive limitation is equally sharp. Applying those demands to this particular original bearer still requires a warranted general realisation principle with the relevant scope or a warranted particular intrinsic nature. The inspected sources defend the principle, but the proposed application does not independently establish its truth against the strongest surviving critic. Its anti-arbitrariness rationale supplies reason for inquiry and comparison, not a newly certified entailment from the actual correction.

Further work on this probe should resume only with evidence or argument that bears on that exact transfer. Another causal diagram, another insistence that a source is original, or another source passage merely affirming necessity would not change the assessment. At this stopping point INTR.P1, INTR.P2, INTR.N2, and INTR.N3 remain distinct; no actual rival cosmos is certified; and the retained conditional uniqueness and local intentional-agency results remain intact.
