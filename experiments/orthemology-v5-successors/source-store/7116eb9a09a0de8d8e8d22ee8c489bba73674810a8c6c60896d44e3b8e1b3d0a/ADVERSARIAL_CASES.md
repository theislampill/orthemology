# Adversarial cases and strongest alternative explanations

Research v3 companion. Each case tests a definite premise or inference. None is a metaphysical realisation certificate.

## A The equally capable outsider

Challenge: G and H can cause the same epistemic harm; both should refrain. Therefore G's maker history is idle.

Verdict: Correct against anti-sabotage alone. The main argument concedes this. The positive test concerns advance planning, actual substitution and safe release before emergency conditions make all helpers' duties coincide. If G has no distinctive responsibility for arranging the dependence it established, C2 fails.

Residual issue: Multiple grounds can require the same final action. The argument must explain the historical ground, not count forbidden outcomes. It must not obtain a special duty solely by labelling G a governor.

## B A new name for a prior promise

Challenge: The supported bounded project is really an earlier implicit assurance. The new result just moves Seventh's assurance backwards.

Response: The recipient can be unaware of G, and the source can knowingly arrange reliance through a faculty and environment without undertaking a speech act or representing an intention to R. The proposed consequence is disjunctive protection or adequate release; it need not be G's personal performance. It differs from a promise whose content requires that performance.

Concession: A broad account of undertaking can legitimately classify intentional provision as assuming a bounded role. The claim is not that no undertaking occurs in any sense. It is that a token truth-linked assurance is not the only source of the relevant prior reason. This is a substantive extension of the earlier principle, not wholly independent moral territory.

## C Gratuitous gifts cannot become permanent taxes

Challenge: G freely supplies a useful faculty. If usefulness and foreseeable reliance created an obligation to maintain it, every generous act would bind its giver indefinitely.

Response: Mere usefulness, causal production, foresight and recipient preference are insufficient. The added facts concern active installation, sustaining, shaping, inducing continuation or accepted maintenance of legitimate bounded reliance. An independently understood finite gift may end without stranding such a project. The pro-tanto reason also leaves room for legitimate burdens and alternatives; it is not an unconditional demand for successful release.

Hard case: A source continually gives short-lived benefits but knows recipients reasonably plan on recurrence. The result depends on what the source knowingly maintains and what reliance is legitimate; function alone cannot decide it. A statement of finite support may limit the role if effective and appropriate, but does not license deliberately exploiting an exposure that the source itself creates.

## D No recipient is worse off than before creation

Challenge: Before G created R, R had no independent capacity or alternative. G therefore cannot owe compensation for taking something away from a prior baseline.

Response: The proposed reason concerns the good and agency of an existing R and the relation actively established. It is not defined by whether nonexistence would have been better. Absence of a pre-creation baseline therefore supplies no automatic exemption. But the observation does not by itself give the same maintenance entitlement as induced surrender of an independent alternative. The active organisation and legitimate bounded reliance must still be shown.

Limit: This does not prove a complete theory of creation duties, a right to indefinite existence, or an actual verdict on any particular divine termination. It supports only the present relational reason under P0–P6.

## E Arbitrary reliance by the recipient

Challenge: R takes any source-created capacity and builds risky plans around it. Calling those plans legitimate makes G liable for all misuses.

Response: Keep institution fixed and vary target/version/domain. P3 fails for unsupported reliance despite an understood effective restriction. If G newly encourages the misuse, the ground changes to that later conduct. The role's scope is a fact to establish, not whatever scope makes the proposed conclusion true.

## F Information without usable authority

Challenge: G announces a replacement channel and leaves a correct address. Responsibility has ended, even if R cannot legally query it.

Response: A substitute must actually let this recipient avoid the relevant loss. It can fail by lack of availability, interpretation, permission or timeliness. A responsibility to arrange access is not itself permission for R to bypass the restriction. The Ninth continuation control owns this operational distinction.

## G An independently caused breakdown

Challenge: G did not cause the fault; only the faulty component or outside interferer bears responsibility.

For the original-source application, “did not cause” here concerns the particular interference being assessed. It does not deny original productive coverage of every relevant secondary process. The generic organiser example must not introduce a second absolute origin when transported to that stronger grant.

Response: Responsibility for causing the fault and responsibility for an ongoing dependence are distinct grounds. G's prior role can require advance contingency planning even if it does not make G blameworthy for the fault. This is the decisive case for C1–C2, rather than a further assertion that intentional sabotage is wrong.

This is due care rather than strict liability. Reasonable precautions may fail without a breach. Conversely an unexcused failure to plan can be wrongful even if the malfunction never occurs. The relevant timing is after the source has organised or assumed the dependence, before the interruption; a further pre-creation duty is not claimed.

Limit: G cannot be required to do what is genuinely beyond its control. Original productive breadth must not be silently converted into every presently executable action. In an omnipotence-grant variant, relevant feasibility still has to preserve the target and other justified respects.

## H Competing good and nonspoiling repair

Challenge: Continuing support conflicts with a real, more important good. Or a safe release requires sacrificing a lesser but genuine good.

Response: The responsibility is a special reason, not an unconditional Pareto-maximisation demand. A justified override can defeat an obligation to preserve the exact support. An outweighed loss may still block nonspoiling R_P. The paper claims no R_G without an eligible whole-profile strict improvement and no N from the fact that G should do better.

## I The actual mixed source knowingly breaches the duty

Challenge: G recognises the continuing responsibility but chooses betrayal as its overriding motivational end. It remains informed and instrumentally effective.

Response: This is a proposed breach, not a refutation of the responsibility. A motivating priority does not establish a justifying priority. The account has definite conditional explanatory content and remains metaphysically uncertified. The duty argument does not pretend to exclude it as a possible source nature.

## J Virtuous source acts in a different channel

Challenge: Genuine cognitive beneficence at A/B warrants assuming the same genealogy for C.

Response: Not without a same-channel argument. S-A does not identify every later channel's intended role, supported scope or retained governance. The present route needs the actual facts P1–P3. Conversely, merely imagining a different genealogy is not an actual undercutter of independent evidence that the channel is genuinely maintained.

Correction of scope: P1 is needed to ascribe genuine prior truth-service governance, not for every due-care reason. A manipulative organiser can have the P2–P5 responsibility without ever having valued the channel's epistemic good. Its special responsibility can concern reasonable transition or repair of the exposure it created. Hidden bad purpose cannot itself make the recipient's otherwise permissible reliance illegitimate or remove every claim.

## K Perfect structural agency closes the gap

Challenge: An omniscient original agent lacks ignorance, external pressure and material fragmentation. A practical violation would therefore destroy its rational unity, and C1 entails essential compliance.

Response: The conclusion would follow only after establishing that the relevant practical alignment is an inviolable condition of this source's agency. The truth of its beliefs, ability to reason, and successful means selection do not by themselves contain that condition. A substantive constitutive theory may defend it, and the source's lack of creaturely limitations may be relevant. Neither a norm's existence nor the absence of one familiar failure mechanism establishes the exhaustive metaphysical exclusion.

This is not a claim that every malicious story describes a possible unified source. The stronger affirmative proposal and the stronger mixed-source proposal both owe their intrinsic-constitution claims.

## L Normative authority is derived from superior power

Challenge: The whole result says that whoever can best help has the right to govern.

Response: No. The paper addresses G's reasons concerning R, not R's obligation to obey G. A better-placed helper may have responsibilities without jurisdiction. Actual source directives need their own content, authority and recipient-warrant grounds. C1 does not license a command merely because the commander created the means of complying.

## M An essential conditional is mistaken for an essential attribute

Challenge: If the responsibility principle holds necessarily and G is necessarily original, its integrity is necessary.

Response: The principle's scope is conditional: where G knowingly institutes the relevant dependence, the reason applies. Necessary applicability of a reason is not necessary compliance. Further, the existence of recipients and particular institutions may be contingent. A source nature's standing readiness to act fittingly is a stronger proposition, not the same conditional with greater confidence.

## N The recipient refuses protection

Challenge: G's duty to protect authorises it to override an informed recipient's refusal.

Response: A responsibility regarding R is not authority over R. An adequate alternative freely refused by a competent recipient can change what further measures are required. G must not manufacture acceptance through deception or conceal relevant options. Separate duties concerning third parties or past wrongs do not disappear automatically.

## O Several organisers and forked provisions

Challenge: Every agent who helped make the dependence has the full indefinite duty, and each copied service creates another guarantee.

Response: The special reasons must be allocated using actual contributions, undertakings, control, feasible alternatives and recipient relations. Copying does not multiply limited capacity or duplicate an undertaking's scope. Nor does plurality permit every organiser to assume an unaccepted handoff. This paper claims no general division-of-responsibility theorem.

## P Compensation as retroactive permission

Challenge: If G can compensate afterwards, it may knowingly impose the irreversible loss now.

Response: Compensation addresses some reparable losses; it does not automatically provide an adequate prospective exit or justify deliberately imposing an irreparable injury. The selected measure must answer the actual respect and stakes. The source capsule records an alternative in the cited discussion, not a universal licence to replace any good by payment.

## Q Foresight is mistaken for active maintenance

Challenge: H sees R using a system, can interrupt it and elects not to. H thereby maintains the dependence and becomes indistinguishable from G.

Response: That is not the operative P2. Mere noninterference is excluded. Active provision, shaping of routes, induction of continuation or accepted handoff must be established. If H actually performs one of those acts, H can incur a corresponding responsibility; that does not show the original contrast was incoherent.
