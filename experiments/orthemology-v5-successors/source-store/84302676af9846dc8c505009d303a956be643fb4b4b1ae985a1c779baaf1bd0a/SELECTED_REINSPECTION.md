## Positive argument confirmed

The composition argument separates inseparable essence–attribute coexistence from dependence on an external producer. “Other” may mean separable external thing or cognitively distinguishable predicate; treating them as one relation equivocates. The fullest divine bearer includes real knowledge, life, power and willing rather than a bare propertyless substrate.

Faces 70–72 affirm every pure perfection without importing its creature-specific defect. Incomparability is therefore the consequence of positively unshared fullness, not empty negation. Face 79 argues strict highest status cannot be shared: an equal leaves neither higher than the other; an inferior is no equal. The parallel absolute-subjugation proof says that an unsubdued equal defeats absolute supremacy, while a subdued alleged peer is no peer. This is a clear conditional uniqueness argument, supported in the text by a positive theory of perfection and by its understanding of the highest exemplar. Its applicability depends on establishing that every candidate original divine source must have that strictly unrestricted role, rather than only an upper bound or maximal status permitting ties. The author offers that thicker conception; its warrant must be evaluated rather than silently assumed or dismissed.

The final reply 14 makes an especially strong additional claim: divine actions issue from already complete predicates, not from acquired improvement, and those names require suitable effects. This is a kind-level expressive-efficacy argument. It is constrained by face 200: temporal goods can have necessary prior conditions; successive happenings cannot all occur at once; a beloved event's time and attributes matter. Therefore it is not a proof of the eternal existence of each particular product or an undifferentiated necessity of every proposed token effect.

Face 234 explicitly rejects demoting creaturely life, knowledge and power to mere metaphor. General predicates can genuinely apply with radically different modes or degrees. This confirms the need to distinguish universal sourcehood from denial of derivative agency.

## Direct scan repair of the influence contrast

Source 003:1184 is grammatically damaged. Printed 1037–1038/PDF 1115–1116 separates two questions. First, whether another does not supply an absent divine perfection: this is affirmed as necessarily known. Second, whether another cannot do what occasions displeasure, anger, joy, love or aversion: that is rejected as not impossible, with scriptural examples and prayer/response, hearing and seeing as parallels. This verifies the substance of the second reader's interpretation while correcting the electronic junction.

The corrected phrasing is not silently assigned to an independently surviving original manuscript. Printed 1038 note 1 says the h-witness has the defective phrase and the main text follows m. Introduction 64 identifies m as the manuscript of al-Mawṣilī's abridgment. Introduction 65 describes collation of two original manuscripts plus that auxiliary abridgment. Thus the reading is an explicitly labelled editorial correction from an abridgment witness, corroborated by surrounding argument, not undisclosed substitution of the abridgment for the original book.

## Direct scan repair of the final transition

The electronic source jumps after face 241's ʿArafa accounts to the reported speech of Iblīs (004:250–251). Printed 1111/PDF 1189 supplies a face 242 heading and its opening: opposition between reason and transmission is the alleged source of disorder and contrary to the messengers' teaching. Printed 1112/PDF 1190 introduces al-Shahrastānī's al-Milal wa-l-niḥal before the Iblīs narrative. This establishes the reported-speaker boundary and prevents treating the first-person questions as Ibn al-Qayyim's own assertions.

However, printed 1111 note 3 explicitly says a large original-h gap was supplied from m, and 1113 note 3 marks its end. Since m is the abridgment, the restored passage does not furnish independent original-manuscript coverage. I have read all printed 1109–1113 in the scan, including that editorial qualification. It can support a labelled reconstruction/control, not a claim to possess the lost original wording. The historical work still remains incomplete.
