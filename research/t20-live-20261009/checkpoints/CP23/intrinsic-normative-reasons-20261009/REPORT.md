# Intrinsic value, due right, and the indifferent agent

9 October 2026. Bounded primary-source return for T20. No integration, protected edit, closure, theological authentication, or new universal theorem.

## Result

**Ibn Taymiyya has a substantive, non-punitive account of normative response, not merely a threat attached to otherwise indifferent acts. His source-grounded distinction between capacity and actual willing also explicitly allows obligation without a desire to comply. But neither distinction by itself proves the further proposition that every categorical moral reason requires God.** The strongest inspected rationale concerns a fitting response to real excellence, benefaction and due right. Its warranted force must be assessed at that substantive normative level, rather than inferred from the word “owed” or dismissed for not being deduced from norm-free premises.

This return gives a precise answer to the comparison. An indifferent agent cannot remove a warranted claim simply by not wanting to meet it. The remaining question is which claim is warranted, and why its grounds count for this agent. The texts offer intelligible grounds and direct rational appraisal; they do not supply an independent exclusion of the strongest naturalist account of corrected ends. Smith remains the stronger naturalist rival for that exact target; Railton remains a rival for objective appraisal with a deliberately weaker categoricity claim.

There is also a definite textual increment: printed *Darʾ* 8:491–494 confirms a blame/punishment-producing basis before prophetic communication while withholding punishment until communication. It repairs two defects already flagged in the inherited electronic reading. Consequently neither “reason alone automatically licenses punishment” nor “nothing normatively adverse exists before revelation” represents the inspected position.

## 2. What the original sources actually distinguish

### A. Real features are not immutable moral labels

*Majmūʿ*8:428–431 separates rational good/bad appraisal from denial of divine creation or determination. Thus rationally recognizing a feature without learning a prophetic message does not make that feature ontologically uncreated or independent of God. The text itself affirms created causes and intelligible wisdom while maintaining divine creation.

At431–434 the author rejects two extremes as he characterizes them: every moral quality is an inseparable feature and revelation only discovers it; or acts have no evaluatively relevant qualities and command differentiates equivalents without wisdom. His criticism of reducing “commands the good” to “commands what he commands” is an argument that moral predicates have informative content. This is more than attaching the word good to an irresistible preference.

At434–436 he endorses three ways value can arise:

1. The act already contains a benefit or corruption even without the relevant revealed direction. Justice and injustice illustrate this.
2. The direction introduces a feature under which the act becomes good or bad.
3. A directive has a testing purpose accomplished through the response, without execution of its ostensible object being the intended endpoint.

The third case uses Abraham’s test and an almsgiving narrative. These are the source’s theological illustrations, not present permissions or independently authenticated events in this return. Nor do cases2–3 prove an unrestricted power to legislate every human choice. The retained title-and-domain question remains.

“Intrinsic” therefore needs qualification: a real feature of an act, relationship or appropriately situated agent need not be invariant when the circumstances change. The inherited *Minhāj*III L149–150 appraisal already establishes this distinction. The present return does not claim to have newly read that separate passage.

### B. Rational recognition is not actual compliance

In *Minhāj*3:104–106 the author distinguishes lacking capacity from being occupied with, or strongly wanting, an incompatible alternative. At106 he writes:

> «فالإرادة شرط في وجوده لا في وجوبه»

Willing is a condition of the act’s occurrence, not of its obligation. The same paragraph says it is not a condition of a commanded act that the subject wants it. His example of an envious person resisting beneficence is expressly about aversion, not absence of capacity.

This is the author’s reply to the opponent, not the opponent’s objection. It blocks the inference “he does not want to comply, therefore he cannot be obligated.” It does **not** independently establish the legitimacy of every command or identify a first-order obligation. Its immediate context presupposes the relevant obligation and explains how it can address an unwilling but capable subject. It would be an overclaim to promote this sentence alone into a derivation of categorical moral reasons.

### C. Right can be violated without injury to its bearer

The strongest positive original-source return is *Jāmiʿ al-Masāʾil*, collectionVI, printed234–252.

- At234–237, divine sufficiency is compatible with a right to worship. The Creator does not receive sustenance or compensating advantage from obedience. The passage expressly locates the entitlement in what the source is, not in its need to be benefited.
- At240, Ibn Taymiyya challenges an injury-only definition of injustice using his opponents’ own accepted rational obligation of gratitude before revelation. They already owe thanks to a source they acknowledge cannot be benefited or injured. The inference is an internal argument against their restrictive definition; it is not an independent proof that every opponent accepts that duty.
- At241–243, he also rejects the contrary claim that, without injury to God or a superior commanding Him, nothing can be intrinsically bad and no obligation makes sense. He affirms wise and fitting content while refusing to assimilate all divine and creaturely obligations.
- At244–245, he candidly says that “putting a thing in its place,” withholding a right and inflicting undeserved harm all require knowing the relevant due. A general account may be informative without determining every application.
- At246–248, proportions in buildings, garments, food and cultivation illustrate fittingness that is not reducible to suffering in the immediate bearer. Some failures harm a beneficiary instead. The rank and usefulness of a good can warrant appropriate treatment even where that good cannot feel injury.
- At249–251, attempted or actual wrong is distinguished from its final net effect. Later benefit or protection of a victim does not retroactively make every wrongful act right. The account therefore has resources beyond net harm and beyond the offender’s current preference.

These are positive considerations. A recipient’s real benefit explains why gratitude is directed to its actual giver; the giver’s excellence explains why recognition is fitting beyond a bargain; the continuing good of human beings and appropriate allocation explain why an indifferent preference is not the only relevant fact. They support the inherited defeasible appraisal of fitting practical acknowledgement. They do not make the duty true merely because the theorist uses the word “right.”

The limits matter equally. An artifact’s proper proportions establish a functional comparison before they establish any person’s categorical duty to preserve it. The argument from gratitude depends on genuinely beneficent provision and warranted entitlement; causal production alone does not settle the issue. The source’s move to God’s uniquely ultimate worth invokes its theological/perfective account. It is not a fresh proof that every human wrong must first be understood through that metaphysics.

The manuscript ends incompletely at252. The final “وكذلك” must not be completed with an imagined additional argument. At248–250 the editor also flags weak or interrupted reports and a damaged word. None of those reports is promoted into independently authenticated evidence here; the conceptual distinctions have explicit authorial statements apart from them.

### D. Correct desire is not whichever desire presently occurs

A focused control at *Jāmiʿ*VI186–193 shows that the author allows psychologically real yet mistaken desire. At187 he distinguishes the impossibility claimed for a **correct** ultimate intention from the observable occurrence of corrupt intentions. At188 he uses healthy and disordered taste to distinguish what is beneficial and appropriately loved from an individual’s present response. The old medical explanations are historical analogies, not independently established clinical facts.

This gives the account a substantive perfective shape: an actual preference is answerable to what suits or damages the bearer and to the object’s genuine standing. But calling the indifferent person disordered cannot itself prove the disputed standard. The relevant theological and normative conception of flourishing must be justified. The source does not make flawless instrumental organization equivalent to complete practical excellence. Nor does this bounded return claim a fresh appraisal of the full final-cause proof begun at192–193.

## 3. Punishment: the consequential qualification

The printed *Darʾ* argument at8:490–494, including its immediate fitra/covenant context, distinguishes moral defect from the completed conditions for punitive treatment.

At491, after its argument that prior inherited practice is not a sufficient excuse against naturally accessible recognition, the text says that a basis through which punishment is deserved is present. At492 it continues:

> «ثم إن الله بكمال رحمته وإحسانه لا يعذب أحدا إلا بعد إرسال رسول إليهم وإن كانوا فاعلين لما يستحقون به الذم والعقاب»

It immediately calls the bad acts a “سبب الذم والعقاب,” then again says God did not punish before sending a messenger. At493 its explicitly preferred third account affirms good/bad features warranting praise and blame, with punishment only after the message. The author calls this account the soundest. At494 the conclusion reiterates both rationally knowable praise/blame and the message condition.

A source-faithful formulation is therefore:

- The act can already contain a morally adverse, blame-grounding feature.
- The text can speak of that feature as a basis of punishment/desert.
- That does not make the basis a total sufficient condition of actual punishment.
- Prophetic communication is retained as a condition for punishment in the endorsed account.

“Pro tanto basis” is an analytical gloss on this sequence, not a technical phrase Ibn Taymiyya uses here. It is safer than flattening every occurrence of “deserve” into either completed liability or no normative significance. This passage does not settle every legal/theological question about exact culpability, excusing ignorance, worldly effects, or the terminology of obligation. No judgement about a contemporary person’s liability follows.

The print controls have a definite result. The electronic Darʾ014 witness wrongly has “لا يجوزون” in the report of those allowing punishment of the innocent; printed492 has “يجوزون.” The electronic conclusion omits the exception and says “لا يعذب أحدا بعد إرسال”; printed494 has “لا يعذب أحدا إلا بعد إرسال.” These are witnessed corrections to the retained transcription problems, not conjectural emendations of the author.

## 4. The exact informed-indifferent-agent test

Hold fixed an agent’s capacity, knowledge of another person’s severe harm, absence of consent or justification, and lack of self-interested motivation to desist. Separate three claims.

**First, textual position.** Ibn Taymiyya does not make actual concern a condition of obligation. His account permits a genuinely wrongful response to a genuine good despite indifference or aversion. That conclusion is clearly supported.

**Second, substantive warrant.** The strongest premise asks the agent to recognize that genuine human goods, beneficent relations and due treatment have practical significance which the agent’s current preference cannot erase. A powerless victim’s need for protection is not made unreal by inability to retaliate. A benefactor’s claim is not erased by absence of further advantage to the recipient. In the theological application, appropriate acknowledgment answers actual perfection and provision, rather than serving as a payment that improves God.

This has defeasible normative force. It is not refuted by “I do not care.” Nor must it be deduced from weaker, norm-free premises before it can have any warrant. But the premise is stronger than the descriptive judgement “someone is harmed.” The fully informed amoralist who grants harm yet denies all categorical reason must reject **the practical authority of these goods and relations for this agent**, or reject the specific relation/entitlement alleged. That is the real disagreement.

**Third, semantic unpacking.** If the rival has already granted a “due right” in the full normative sense, saying it is owed merely draws out the concession. No additional normative discovery occurs there. If the rival grants only objective welfare or functional failure, the move to an obligation is substantive and must be supported by the fittingness/justice appraisal. The artifact analogy and the capacity/willing distinction cannot make that transfer unaided.

The positive result is consequently bounded: the inspected sources supply a credible non-punitive rationale for practical response and show where an indifferent agent’s objection would have to land. They do not force every rationally coherent dissenter to accept the same evaluative premises, and they do not derive one complete first-order moral code. Neither limitation by itself destroys ordinary or philosophical warrant.

## 5. Strongest surviving naturalist rival

For objective welfare/appraisal, Railton can recognize real harm and impartial assessment independently of the offender’s approval. His explicit limit concerning categorical compliance reasons remains. Ibn Taymiyya’s assertion of obligations independent of current desire addresses a stronger target; it does not show that Railton secretly supplied that property or that Railton’s weaker objective claims disappear.

For the exact categorical-reason question, the retained Smith2025 argument is stronger. It treats current ends as corrigible through an ideal-advice account grounded in robust capacities for knowledge acquisition and desire realization. It therefore cannot be dismissed as “whatever the offender happens to want.” The previous full-source assessment’s remaining burdens are precise: why ideal advice tracks maximally robust lifelong agency, and why that requires universal intrinsic protective concerns.

The comparison now has genuine common ground: both accounts distinguish actual wanting from warranted practical correction and appeal to an account of better functioning or fitting response. Their standards differ. Ibn Taymiyya’s due-right and perfective account is not simply maximization of agential capacity; Smith’s account does not accept God’s intrinsic worth as its ground. Each must defend its substantive standard and its application, and the evidence need not be equal merely because both use normative premises.

The primary return does not settle Smith’s open premises. Conversely, their remaining incompleteness does not establish God as the sole possible source of categorical reasons. Even a successful naturalistic identification of the relevant human features would not by itself prove their ultimate ontological independence from God. Epistemic access, normative identification and ultimate explanation remain distinct enquiries.

No conclusion about actual nonbelievers’ morality or cognitive health, and no attribution of subjective religious experience to an AI, is made here.

## 6. Source fidelity and exact disposition

The Asfahāniyya print return444–450 supplies an important negative control. At444–446 Ibn Taymiyya quotes al-Rāzī’s report of a Muʿtazilite argument;446–447 continues al-Rāzī’s objections; the author’s own “قلت” response starts at448. The equal-pay truth/lie example at445 is therefore not an unmarked independent Ibn Taymiyya experiment. His own448–450 reply does affirm real moral features and rejects a vacuous command-only account, but it does not silently endorse every reported argument or prove every modal premise in the preceding exchange.

The new result is a source-bound comparison and two repaired textual controls. It preserves the positive due-right rationale and declines both a threat-only caricature and an automatic theological-exclusivity inference. Much of the source content was already retained; the exact comparator and print corrections are the increment. The remaining obstruction is substantive warrant for the disputed due relation/ideal correction and, separately, its ultimate ground. Another generic “is–ought gap” or additional volume coverage would not itself resolve that obstruction. This bounded operation therefore stops here.
