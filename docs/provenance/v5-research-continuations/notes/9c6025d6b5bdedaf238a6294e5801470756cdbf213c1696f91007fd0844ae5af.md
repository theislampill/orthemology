# Zero second-order margin can hide an exact cubic obstruction

Research control, 2026-10-01. Ordinary exact polynomial proof. This checks the necessary qualification in SECOND_ORDER_REPAIR_LP.md.

Let z=t−1/2 and define

    ψ(t)=z²/2+(2/3)z³+z⁴,
    r(t)=ψ''(t)=1+4z+12z²
        =12(z+1/6)²+2/3>0.

On the complement hull use F(x,y)=ψ(x)+ψ(1−y), and let G(x,y)=(x²+y²)/2. Both are finite C∞ polynomial generators with strictly positive Hessians everywhere. Their tangent Hessian projections agree at every coherent point.

At s=1/2,

    r(s)=1, r'(s)=4, (log r)'(s)=4=1/[s(1−s)].

For transformed perturbations (s+ε,s), the common second-order interval is a single point. In the notation of RANK_ONE_EXACT_REPAIR.md, μ=1/2, V=1/4, Q=0, λ_G=0, λ_F=4. Thus U_G's and L_F's second-order coefficients both equal 1/4. The LP has τ*=0: its weak constraints are feasible, and the two touching endpoint constraints prohibit a positive margin.

Nevertheless no exact common weak repair exists for any 0<ε≤1/100.

## Exact proof

Put

    u=1/2+ε/2+ε²/4−ε³/8,
    A=[(1/2+ε)²+(1/2)²]/2.

The quadratic truth-0 requirement for a coherent q is q²≤A. But

    u²−A=ε³(ε³−4ε²−4ε+8)/64>0

on the stated interval, and u>0. Hence every quadratic candidate satisfies q<u. Also 0<u<1.

Let L(q)=2D_ψ(1,q). Since

    L'(q)=−2(1−q)r(q)<0  for 0≤q<1,

every remaining candidate has L(q)>L(u). Direct expansion gives

    L(u)−[D_ψ(1,1/2+ε)+D_ψ(1,1/2)]
      = ε³ P(ε)/6144,

where

    P(ε)=9ε⁹−72ε⁸+72ε⁷+592ε⁶−816ε⁵−2304ε⁴
          +1696ε³+4992ε²−17280ε+2304.

For 0<ε≤1/100, discard positive terms and bound the negative terms:

    P(ε) ≥ 2304−17280/100−2304/100⁴−816/100⁵−72/100⁸ >0.

Thus even the generous envelope u fails the F truth-1 comparison. No common weak repair exists. The leading failure at this envelope is (3/8)ε³+O(ε⁴).

This does not contradict the sharp isolated rank-one theorem: that theorem requires its bound at every coherent s. Here the bound is only saturated at s=1/2 and is violated immediately to its right. It does refute a pointwise rule that a feasible second-order LP, or equality of its margin to zero, by itself guarantees an exact repair.

For a positive comparator in the same globally smooth class, use ψ₊(t)=exp(4(t−1/2))/16. Its curvature has r(1/2)=1 and r'(1/2)=4, so it has exactly the same second-order LP rows and zero optimum at this context. Its log slope is4 everywhere, which satisfies4≤1/[s(1−s)]. The rank-one theorem gives exact common weak repair with the quadratic score. Thus second-order zero genuinely leaves higher-order or interval structure to decide, even when every potential is C∞ on a neighborhood of the whole hull.
