## Positive argument reconstructed before assessment

V02L100–103 argues from independently necessary existence to nonborrowed intrinsic perfection. It does not merely define any lack as impossibility: it contrasts dependence for an intrinsic completion, circular mutual conferral, and an independent complete giver. L102 distinguishes something belonging to the thing's own completion from a separate item merely appended to it. L104–109 then carefully distinguishes senses of part, other, dependence, and necessity. Necessary concomitance is not generally productive borrowing. Necessary attributes need not be separately independent bearers.

L114–119 explicitly connects product interdependence with producer dependence, and L117 explicitly counts realised efficacy as perfection. The argument is therefore stronger than a bare stipulation that one product has one maker. L118 uses human material and sustenance as a concrete connected-order case. L119 supplies an account of power as either intrinsic or received; its rejection of coequal original cooperation appeals to the absence of an independent third giver, unlike derived cooperators.

V03L236–242 separates standing power, will, sufficient exercise, and actual effect. Not everything within power is done. Complete will-plus-power entails its effect, whereas an eternally complete cause for the whole changing world is rejected. These distinctions must survive any translation into a modal theorem.

L258–276 directly treats necessary agreement, disagreement, independent whole production, differentiated contributions, shared benefit, foreknowledge and coordination. The agreement objection has not been overlooked. L265 and L268 explicitly preserve differentiated acts and powers of created cooperators; L272 considers intrinsic and extrinsic acts separately. The proposed reply to foreknowledge at L272 treats occurrence-knowledge as following the occurring object, which follows productive will: it cannot simply be used as an independent explanation of that will. L273 treats mutually conditioned will as partial-cause circularity. L276 says partition and dominance are separately necessary consequences on the rival hypothesis, not a mere disjunction.

## Current appraisal and discriminating question

This reading strengthens the historical-source reconstruction, but does not yet change the lead reader’s 08:40 common-source sufficient-warrant withholding. It makes the remaining issue more exact. For a proposed coequal source, one must distinguish (a) another source conferring its intrinsic power, (b) another contribution being needed for the intended composite result, (c) the source's own act being intrinsically incomplete, and (d) necessarily concordant but non-produced intentions. The source offers reasons for moving from (b)/(d) to a defect or constitutive dependence; those reasons deserve testing as positive arguments, not dismissal because an interface records them as assumptions.

The key unresolved assessment is whether genuinely intrinsic unlimited efficacy rules out an independently fitting, self-determined limited contribution, even when its result enters a composite, and whether necessary agreement must involve productive conditioning rather than uncaused concomitance. The author distinguishes concomitance from productive circularity in volume 2, so it would be unfair to attribute a general equation of all logical conditions with efficient causes. Conversely, a finite model with independent partial powers does not by itself satisfy his stronger original-source profile or establish metaphysical possibility.

V03L254–257's rejection of a shared universal as an extramental component further guards against a spurious unity proof: two things sharing the predicate necessary are not thereby composed of a single shared necessity-object plus differentiators. Any new formal unicity proof must not reinstate that rejected semantic move.
