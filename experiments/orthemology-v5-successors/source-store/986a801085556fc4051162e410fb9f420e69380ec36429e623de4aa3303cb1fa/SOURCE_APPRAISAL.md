# Jami al Masail sixth collection source appraisal

Reader-facing study record, 7 October 2026. The interpretations and reading claims below belong to the recorded study and its bounded rereads. This edition of the notes adds no source-reading or proof credit.

## Principal result

Ibn Taymiyya expressly distinguishes an agent's possessed power, awareness and intention from jointly attained ends. He permits reciprocal conditions, instruments, real created efficacy and the added strength of cooperative action. He also explicitly allows that a cooperator may have been able to act alone. His further argument against ultimate coequal creators is that actual participation by another prevents complete sole exercise, which he ranks as incapacity or inhibition inconsistent with full divinity. Those claims and concessions must be assessed together.

The received collection supplies positive source-mode and order arguments. It does not make every relational perfection a borrowed deficiency: God can genuinely love and rejoice in new worship because He originates it. Nor does it infer the unity of an actual order solely from contrary wills: necessary concord is considered and addressed through the numerical-effect and perfection argument. The remaining question is the warrant for that stronger causal/perfection ranking, not whether the author noticed cooperation or standing ability.

## The two central passages

### Tawhid printed 87–98

This range was newly read in full page images from the independently acquired CSIIslam scan, alongside its electronic counterpart E091–102. It is a new bounded visual control, not a claim to have read every page of the volume visually. The earlier T19 extraction-only limitation remains true of that earlier work and is not retrospectively rewritten.

Printed 87–90 rejects circular productive priority but permits co-presence. The legal discussion at 90–94 then distinguishes numerical identity from sameness of ruling type. Multiple legal grounds can support multiple rulings whose practical enforcement overlaps; this is not necessarily two complete causes of one numerically identical item. Merely finding two suitable descriptions does not establish independent sufficiency, and an alternative cause cannot be invented without evidence to defeat a counterexample. These distinctions are integral to the argument, not disposable digressions.

Printed 95 distinguishes the agents' own acts, their different contributions to the carried object, and the resulting aggregate. It expressly grants that one partner could sometimes do the work alone. Printed 96 argues that the actual cooperative exercise nevertheless prevents that person from producing the whole alone in that event; actual inability and inhibited full exercise both disqualify complete divinity. Printed 97 says the familiar opposed-wills argument alone does not exclude cooperation. Printed 98 addresses necessary agreement by referring back to nonduplication and the complete-exercise argument.

This is affirmative authorial reasoning, not a reported opponent's objection. Its sufficiency depends on whether a real contribution by another necessarily imposes a defect on the first agent's intrinsic efficacy or appropriate exercise. The definition of sole production establishes that sole and joint production differ; it does not by itself establish the full normative/modal ranking. The author supplies a ranking and examples, which deserve positive evaluation, but cannot be treated as derived from necessary existence alone.

### Cooperative powers printed 179–182

These four pages were also newly read completely in the scan. Printed 181/E185 gives the dual description: each agent has power, awareness, intention and a sought end, while the ends require the two intentions and acts together. Its natural grammar refers to the two cooperators; no silent emendation is needed to recover a two-item list.

The preceding page permits tools, conditional cooperation and strength produced through cooperation. What is excluded is a closed loop in which a needed originating principle is acquired solely from a partner who derives that very principle back from its recipient. The following page distinguishes causes existing with one another from being produced through one another, and ends coexisting from being pursued for one another. A common cause or end is presented as a coherent arrangement, but the permission for co-presence is not itself a proof that every co-present pair has exactly one originating source.

Neither passage makes the finite cooperators uncreated or omnipotent. Equally, neither permits substituting dependence of their shared result for derivation of each person's own power from the other.

## Perfection and actual order

Printed 62–66 answers a real acquisition-of-perfection challenge. If newly occurring worship changes nothing, how is it genuinely loved? If something newly occurs in God, was He previously deficient? The reply points to God's originating the worship and compares love and joy with perceiving new objects. Several possible accounts are entertained dialectically; they must not all be attributed as simultaneously adopted theories. The general point is that a genuine relational exercise need not be externally supplied need.

E211–216 supplies complementary causal discipline. Contributory causes can fail through missing conditions or impediments. A complete condition complex is different. God acts through created means, not merely at their occurrence. An agent's teaching can fail to produce learning; an accomplishment description that includes successful learning has included reception as well as instruction. The claimed uniqueness of divine will as a complete cause is a further theological assertion, not the definition of every complete cause.

The ultimate-end argument rejects desire's mere occurrence as a sufficient reason for its object's goodness, ranks ends above means, and appeals to healthy fitra and experienced rest in divine remembrance. Printed 126 explicitly distinguishes misdirected worship that occurs but corrupts its agent from causal arrangements that cannot occur. Printed 127–129 offers actual celestial regularity as evidence for common creative origin and appropriate final direction. Its historical physics and universal final-value principles remain substantive premises, not facts certified by having read them.

## Two assessments kept separate

An unrestricted conclusion that every qualified original must have all perfection in complete exclusive source mode is stronger than provisionally accepting a Necessary Being. The collection's nonduplication, causal-ownership, fitting-exercise and ultimate-worth principles carry additional inferential work. Local distinctions about cooperation do not themselves settle all those principles.

A common Creator of the actual connected dependent order is a separate, potentially weaker comparative conclusion. It can have sufficient defeasible warrant without deductively eliminating every conceivable rival. Coordination, matched powers and recipients, derivative agency and coherent ends are positive evidence. A rival model's mere logical describability does not defeat that evidence, and the mere numerical simplicity of one source does not establish it.

The independent middle-section appraisal provisionally favours common creation as a preferable unifying inference within the admitted necessary-being and basic-recognition starting point, while acknowledging incomplete comparative support. The first-section appraisal credits the same positive considerations but does not yet judge this volume alone sufficiently discriminating, because its actual-order argument relies strongly on the contested causal/value ranking and historically specific celestial premises. This difference is preserved. It is not a finding about all earlier evidence, a rejection of basic recognition, or a vote that mechanically settles the philosophical question. Both appraisals give greater confidence to the precise local source/contribution/product distinctions than to an unrestricted perfection conclusion.

## Orthogonal material retained

The continuous notes cover all eighteen received pieces, not only the two causal loci. Important additional results include:

- E009–046: voluntary action and intentions, charitable interpretation of mystical language, sincerity versus correctness, evidence for negative claims, and rights retained even by wrongdoers. Sincere mistaken interpretation can excuse without authorizing imitation; proportional justice remains required.
- E035–037: reflexive knowledge and internal representation, with explicit manuscript blanks preventing a fully determinate reconstruction.
- E075–088: mixed assent, partial recognition, knowledge and action, premodern physiology and legal consequences of fetal development, and denial of a predicate through failure of purpose without denial of bare existence.
- E137–140: uncertain reports, sincerity and retreat rules; a plausible lesson is distinguished from reliable transmission of a particular narration.
- E207–219: nonexistence as evidence, condition or derivative goal, not a productive agent; not-knowing implies nonexistence only under a comprehensive-knowledge premise, not for ordinary human ignorance.
- E223–251 and printed 248–252: graded senses of Islam/faith, intended benefit versus fitting right, and wrongdoing that need not cause felt harm to its bearer. The preserved seventh treatise ends incompletely.
- Printed 253–270: the Husayn/Yazid historical controversy, competing transmitted claims and differentiated responsibility. These remain source reports, not an independently authenticated history.
- Printed 273–318: forgiveness, prayer-language distinctions and simultaneous duties. At 305 the author revisits his earlier use of a precedent, distinguishing lack of censure, correctness and excused interpretation.
- Printed 319–367: prayer times and joining, ordinary versus excused standards, operative legal grounds and real hardship. Printed 335 separates intention-based reward from the act; 350 separates legally available time from intending a legal label; 359 rejects ranking options solely by counting apparent defects.
- Printed 371–398: sibling zakat, slaughter and hunting, species/hides, and sale of a slaughtered animal. A required condition and the purchaser's direct knowledge of that condition are different at 387–388. The abridgment notice at 389 and the modern qualification of a cited sheep-sale report at 398 remain visible.
- Printed 401–423: leasing an iqta and orchard arrangements. Printed 403 distinguishes validity at formation from possible later dissolution. Printed 411 classifies bodily labour and capital as distinct partnership contributions sharing realised yield. Printed 419 distinguishes consumed seed from returned capital, while a compressed contribution formula remains textually awkward. Printed 423 explicitly confirms a copyist's abridgment of the answer.

These are historical legal, linguistic, ethical and epistemological arguments. They are not current professional advice or standalone proofs of ontological perfection.

## Witness and limits

The exact work is Ibn Taymiyya's Jami al-Masail, sixth collection, edited by Muhammad Uzayr Shams, supervised by Bakr Abu Zayd, Dar Alam al-Fawa'id. The cover confirms those identities and series 13. The 1429 first-edition specification comes from the electronic book metadata; the acquired scan's opening lacks a corresponding imprint/year page.

The CSIIslam file has 444 PDF positions, main text ending 423, contents 425–442 and blank 443–444. It lacks the separately paginated prefatory packet anticipated by its index. Eight introductory electronic pages were read, but the manuscript sample plates were not acquired/read. The electronic host declares 402 website positions and appears to stop before the final two treatises; only its continuous range E001–251 and a separate E401 probe were actually acquired. Network tunnel 403 errors for the remaining requested pages remain held. The already acquired local scan supplies the continuation, final two treatises and index. Website page numbers are not volumes.

The original IIFA PDF 403 hold was respected. Other failed objects are separately recorded; none becomes a visually inspected witness. The successful scan and electronic host are independent acquisition objects, not independently established historical textual witnesses.

The editor reports missing originals, substantial corrective work, unmarked obvious corrections, lacunae, and abridgments. Several suspicious sentences are already suspicious in print and remain unrepaired. Local verified differences are recorded separately, including printed 50 versus E054, printed 66 versus E070, and the explicit editorial substitution at 107.
