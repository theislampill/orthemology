## What the ceiling changes

Boolean corners reveal the minimal positive supports exactly under endpoint preservation. Minimality makes a base-command response a pure single-support power. The externally given multiplicity ceiling converts its nontrivial response into a positive lower bound on a support product, hence on every participating root's base rate. No known calibration map is assumed.

Every positive support meets the union A of those minimal supports. Using base rates on A but unit rates on its complement leaves every route-failure factor positive, while double mask inversion separates supports that share the same A-part. The resulting target gap is bounded below by a known rational product. This replaces an impossible generic zero test by a separated sign decision.

The unrestricted absence sequence shows exactly why the lower-bound step needs a premise: its minimal A-only count grows without bound while its common A rate shrinks. A fixed M excludes that sequence. The proof does not require a calibration modulus, but it does require exact endpoint commands and endpoint preservation.

## Negative mappings and qualifications

- Boolean corner response -> all positive supports: false by itself. Corners recover only the minimal antichain; the mixed-grid contrast supplies absorbed supports.
- Minimal-support union A -> interaction-anchored root union C: false. A may omit a root that appears only in an absorbed interaction. The proof treats those sets separately.
- Unit B commands -> raw logarithms at arbitrary all-one corners: invalid. The mixed grid is safe only because every route meets an interior A coordinate. Corner zeros are never divided or logged.
- Nontrivial minimal response -> effective root lower bound without a count restriction: invalid. The count/power-calibration gauge prevents that inference. M is independently supplied, not learned from the same responses.
- Per-support ceiling -> the same total ceiling: invalid. A per-support bound can support the local gap step, but the final joint count simplex must honor the actual total constraint supplied.
- A known upper total -> exact attained total: invalid. The residual tuple satisfies an inequality; forcing equality introduces a different prior.
- Known M -> every unary count uniquely identified: false. The exact residual isolated-unary simplex may contain many tuples; it collapses only in the stated boundary cases.
- Positive lower bounds for covered roots -> decisions for uncovered targets: unsupported. The conditional-cover corollary requires every target root to be covered at the same base commands.
- Pointwise all-name termination -> uniform precision, runtime or sample budget: invalid. Calibration can make probabilities, certified lower bounds and cut margins arbitrarily small.
- Certified population Cauchy access -> finite empirical frequencies: invalid. These are distinct observation contracts; no sampling theorem is proved here.
- A conditional count prior -> physical or metaphysical warrant: unsupported. The result does not authenticate the ceiling, route structure, interventions, occurrence identity, source ownership or productive adequacy.
