PAGE 10 A computable deterministic witness

A fresh successor removes the need for private random action choices. At every state, the controller maintains a cyclic index into its prescribed action list. It advances that state’s index after selecting an action and keeps every other index unchanged. The full vector persists throughout the phase; it is reset only at a genuine phase change.

The observed physical state alone is not Markov under this schedule. Adding the complete vector of cyclic indices produces a finite augmented Markov chain. In any closed class, returning to the same vector forces every prescribed local action to occur. Its projected physical states are therefore closed under all those actions, not merely the next choice.

Why the wait remains finite

A closed augmented class avoiding target, revelation and mismatching-row sampling would contradict the winning-region condition. The same argument inside a component reaches a specified row or a positive exit. A uniform bound holds from every accumulated counter vector, not just the initial all-zero vector.

Equation: Z = N(a)^(N),       L_{rr} = (Z)/((p)^(Z))

Here N is the physical state count, a bounds menu sizes and p is the least positive supplied transition probability. The augmented system has at most Z states, and a shortest event path has probability at least p to the power Z. The resulting geometric waiting estimate replaces the randomised scheduler’s wait bound in the ordinary cost proof.

Fixing the false-rejection allowance to 1/2 makes its two stage budgets 1/4. The statistical threshold search then uses exact rational arithmetic and terminates. The resulting history policy is computable and deterministic; it may ignore any externally supplied seed. Its source transitions remain random.

The cyclic indices are finite memory, but the empirical sample counts are unbounded. This is neither an autonomous finite-state learner nor a small practical time bound. Resetting after every action or every return to an anchor can starve an informative action and invalidates the proof.

PAGE 11 A wider hidden state model

A fresh ordinary connection reaches posterior-deterministic partially observable models. The relevant condition is precise: for each current hidden state, chosen action and received observation, the actual joint kernel has at most one positive-probability next hidden state. The controller is not told that current state.

Use each possible initial hidden state as a fixed hidden tag. The public descriptor records the current state that each surviving tag would imply after the acquired history, together with the last observation. A tag is removed when its observation probability is zero.

Why the last observation must remain visible

Different observations can lead to exactly the same state descriptor while carrying different likelihood information. Dropping the observation would collapse histories available to the original policy. Keeping it makes the observable history maps inverse on reachable consistent histories.

Tags are initial hypotheses, not merely current state images. Two tags whose current states merge remain distinct initial possibilities. Off-domain transition rows may be totalised arbitrarily because the invariant keeps the actual tag live and its descriptor coordinate equal to the actual hidden state.

Pulling a policy through either history map preserves the same independent seed. Finite-history induction equates its actions and observation/successor masses. Cylinder equality then gives the entire path law. The bad-action indicator at index n uses the current hidden state and action before the next receipt in both systems, so no dummy-action shift is introduced.

For a finite prior with positive mass on each included tag, almost-sure success under that prior is equivalent to success in every such tag. Finite prior expected bad count is similarly equivalent to finite conditional expectations. Zero-prior states are not silently added as adversarial models.

PAGE 12 Hidden parity and recurring obligations

The fresh parity extension accepts a broader kind of long-run success. Each hidden model assigns integer priorities to state/action pairs. A run wins when the smallest priority recurring infinitely often is even. The controller observes its states and receipts, but receives no priority or correctness signal.

The ordinary theorem characterises existence of one common policy attaining almost-sure parity success. Support revelation still hands control to the exact surviving family. At the final support, the controller must eventually persist in a self-verifying component whose recurrent minimum is even for every live model matching its full transition rows.

Recurrence is part of the witness

The operating policy must visit every retained component pair infinitely often almost surely. Merely remaining inside the component is insufficient. Navigation and statistical rejection bounds come from the separately reviewed Markov construction; parity’s prefix independence makes each rejected finite phase harmless to the eventual objective.

Pruning must update the matching rivals

If a matching model has an odd minimum on a current component, remove all pairs at that minimum and recompute components. A valid subcomponent cannot use those pairs because no lower priority is available there. After deletion a new odd minimum or a newly matching rival may appear, so one pass does not suffice. This preserves every valid subcomponent and terminates after at most the number of retained pairs in deletion rounds.

The same initial-tag construction preserves the entire priority sequence and gives the posterior-deterministic EXPTIME upper bound. This is an ordinary mathematical decision procedure; it is not a fresh qualification of a production parity solver.

Parity does not count every odd visit as an error

A forced cycle with priorities 2 and 3 wins while visiting priority 3 forever. There is no general finite expected odd-visit theorem. To recover the earlier coBüchi condition, assign bad pairs priority 1 and good pairs priority 2. Assigning good pairs 0 would express a different objective.

A hidden deterministic monitor can be included

A separately reviewed product adds an explicitly supplied deterministic parity automaton to the hidden state. It consumes the label of the current hidden state and current action, preserves the original receipts, and starts from one fixed monitor state. The actual monitor is never exposed. The same policy therefore has the same observable history and objective law. Posterior determinism survives, giving the corresponding EXPTIME upper bound in the combined explicit input. Automaton determinisation, succinct temporal-formula translation and finite bad-count guarantees are outside this result.

Now draw one source/scoring weight α from a Beta(a,b) prior, with positive shape parameters a and b, and keep it fixed throughout the run. Conditional on that weight, receipts are iid Bernoulli. The policy issues a coherent real report after every receipt prefix, with one common independent seed and no access to α itself.

Equation: ∃ π : C_{Beta}(π) < ∞ ⇔ (a > 1 ∧ b > 1)

Why the endpoints matter even without endpoint atoms

Every accepted report covers only a bounded multiplicative interval of possible weights near each endpoint. After an all-zero receipt prefix, the posterior cannot put arbitrarily high mass in one such interval. The same holds after an all-one prefix. These two events are disjoint once at least one receipt has arrived, and they give a policy-independent positive failure contribution.

Summing those contributions produces inverse-parameter moments. For a Beta prior they diverge exactly when a ≤ 1 or b ≤ 1. When both shapes exceed one, integrating the retained empirical upper bound gives a finite budget. This is a theorem for the Beta family, not a claimed inverse-moment characterisation of every discrete or singular prior.

The mixed receipt process is not an iid coin with the average parameter. Under the uniform prior, the probability of two zeros is 1/3, whereas an iid fair coin gives 1/4. The proof uses a fixed latent weight, not a weight redrawn independently at each receipt.

The initial report is counted once. The two extreme-prefix lower bounds start at n = 1; counting the empty prefix twice would give an impossible lower probability greater than one. Positive disturbance, independent seed, passive receipts and continued evaluation are all material assumptions.

The cutoff survives beyond Beta priors

A separate fresh review extends the same rates, budget cutoff and deterministic-clock criterion to priors whose densities near each endpoint are bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of endpoint powers with exponents a − 1 at zero and b − 1 at one. No extra singular mass is allowed in those punctured endpoint neighbourhoods. Arbitrary measure on a compact interior interval and separate endpoint atoms are allowed. The empirical learner has zero endpoint-atom error after acquisition. A full-prior inverse-variance integral may therefore be infinite while its mistake count is integrable. This extension uses unnormalised extreme-prefix masses, rather than copying a conjugate posterior argument or a Bayes-selector proof across atoms.

PAGE 17 Optimal growth and the reporting clock

Let m be the smaller Beta shape. The fresh rate theorem compares every common policy with the same retained empirical learner. At receipt count n, the prior-averaged failure probability is bounded below for every policy and above for that learner by positive constants times n to the power minus a plus n to the power minus b. Constants may depend on the fixed shapes and disturbance, but not on n or the policy.

Consequently the optimal cumulative cost through post-acquisition opportunity N has these orders:

Equation: Θ((N)^(1 − m)) for m < 1;   Θ(log(N + 1)) for m = 1;   Θ(1) for m > 1

This is empirical order-optimality, not exact Bayes optimality or a claim about leading constants. Under a uniform prior and disturbance 1/4, the empirical report after one receipt fails with probability one, while the constant report 5/8 already lowers that probability to 13/15.

An exact Bayes policy exists mathematically: at each finite history, maximise posterior mass of the report’s acceptance interval and choose its least maximiser. Compactness gives existence. Because reports do not affect later receipts, the same policy minimises every finite-horizon sum. This is not a certified numerical argmax algorithm or a compiled real-report controller. Future-tail rates are unconditional under the original prior, not uniform over realised posteriors.

More receipts per evaluated report change the objective

For a fixed deterministic nondecreasing sequence of positive receipt counts nⱼ, a finite expected evaluated-report budget exists exactly when:

Equation: ∑_{j ≥ 1}(n_{j})^(−m) < ∞

Polynomial receipt counts of order j to the power γ work exactly when γm exceeds one; geometric growth works for every proper Beta prior. This deterministic passive schedule counts only its declared report failures, not all sensing or physical action cost. It cannot erase reports required by a per-receipt interface. Adaptive acquisition needs a separate proof.
