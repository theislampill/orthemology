Common original provision and the Arabic unity argument

# The present judgement

The cumulative argument now gives sufficient defeasible rational warrant for one complete original source of the actual productively connected field under consideration. This judgement rests on explicit metaphysical and evaluative premises. It does not follow from a bare necessary being, a prediction that rival wills would disagree, or connectedness alone. A reasoned contrary assessment remains reasonable disagreement.

The central question is whether connected reality can have several ultimate suppliers, even if they always agree and none borrows its standing power. Two different proposals now receive separate treatment. Differentiated contributors divide the original provision of a result. Primitive double sufficiency instead alleges that two concurrent unborrowed exercises each supply the whole result without needing the other. The maker-and-preserver comparison addresses the first proposal more directly. It does not by itself exclude the second.

The revised conclusion therefore makes two additional judgements visible. Causal nonduplication says that distinct genuine original contributions do distinct productive work, rather than primitively duplicating the same entire production in the same respect. The stronger source-mode norm says that complete unborrowed provision is the fitting mode of a qualified perfect original’s actual productive role. I accept both defeasibly on their separate positive rationales. Neither is a neutral theorem, and causal nonduplication alone does not establish common field coverage.

The broader Arabic reading improves how those premises are understood. Speech, temporal succession, responsive action, remedies and testimony constrain the concepts used in the short unity argument. The new gains are comprehension, explicit warrant appraisal and synthesis of substantially known sources. They are not invented historical discoveries or fresh formal science.

The result excludes an independently original co-contributor within the warranted field. It does not establish numerical uniqueness of every necessary reality beyond it, authenticate an actual revelation or make every created message the source’s own assertion. The report also records a positive but bounded intentional-attestation route and the narrower outcome of the al-Walid case.

# What was already established and what changes

The retained starting point is stronger than an unspecified unexplained fact. Under the concrete whole-existence account, complete original provision of received productive reality warrants an actual original bearer. Separately defended arguments support that same bearer’s necessary existence, essential whole-existence independence, relevant knowledge and wisdom, eligible unrestricted standing productive power, and source-owned veracity. Necessary existence alone was not treated as supplying the entire profile.

Whole-field original completion and one-bearer common coverage are different achievements. The former requires an adequate actual original basis for the complete received field. It did not already exclude a plural original basis. The present judgement adds causal nonduplication, a stronger perfection of actual sourcehood and faithful mediate attribution to the independently warranted field premises.

The earlier full Minhāj assessment already considered cooperation and foreknowledge. It recognised complete versus partial token production, accompaniment versus productive supply, and standing power versus fitting exercise. Those remain valid. It would misstate the history to claim that Ibn Taymiyya simply overlooked agreement. The newly examined Jāmiʿ passages add primary support for distinguishing jointly required acts from reciprocal supply of powers or intentions. \[9\]

The first Nineteenth appraisal gave positive weight to actual source perfection. A subsequent scope check correctly identified a gap: the maker-and-preserver comparison and a complete historical record did not alone exclude primitive nonexclusive double sufficiency. The current appraisal preserves that correction. It now explicitly accepts causal nonduplication on its own rationale and affirms the cumulative field conclusion under the stronger source-mode and field premises. A change in sufficient-warrant judgement does not make the intervening objection mistaken.

Majmūʿ expressly moves from need in a common product to need in action and perfection. Minhāj 2:160–184 treats realised efficacy as a perfection and follows dependencies from products to makers. Darʾ distinguishes intrinsic action from material conditions while endorsing the fuller maker-and-preserver role. The whole Asfahānī commentary places these arguments alongside its treatments of speech, succession, wisdom and knowledge. \[1–3, 7\]

The books have different structures. The Asfahānī commentary repeatedly opens the meanings and proofs compressed into a short creed. Its long discussions help specify what an active, qualified creator would be and how prophetic authority becomes known. Al-Dāʾ begins with an unnamed affliction and develops remedies, conditions, habits, rights, repair and competing loves. Its extended objection belongs to an interlocutor before the author replies. The closing report criticism is part of that resolution. Reading only the unity paragraphs would miss controls supplied by both works’ larger arguments. \[4, 7\]

All printed content and back matter of the three selected volumes have been traversed. Five Asfahānī and fourteen al-Dāʾ manuscript plates remain partly deciphered. The selected first Wasitiyya volume retains local cover and scan-mark uncertainty; volume 2 was excluded. The finite selected reflective phases are complete; none was a second complete volume traversal. The source guide states the exact boundaries. # The strongest source owned argument

## The concrete product is the starting point

The Majmūʿ discussion begins with the existence of the common product. If each participant genuinely contributes to that product as actually produced, each must be included in its actual causal account. A description of what one participant could have produced alone is a different account. Ordinary examples such as shared lifting do not automatically show that two unchanged contributors each wholly produce the very same concrete event exclusively. Exertion, contributing powers or the resultant event differ when the second contributor genuinely does something. \[1\]

The target is the concrete effect with its actual production, rather than an indistinguishable visible outcome. Two sources cannot each exclusively produce the whole same token in the same respect. Either’s ability to supply a matching outcome alone does not establish complete original provision of the actual joint history. The further question of nonexclusive double sufficiency requires the separate CND appraisal below.

The argument has limits. Showing that an agent is a partial contributor to the external result does not, without another premise, show that its standing capacity or inward act was received from another. The author supplies a further bridge. Majmūʿ includes intrinsic actions and attributes within the concrete self, and expressly claims that need in a product implies need in the act or attribute from which it issues. The source does make the stronger inference; the inquiry must assess it rather than report its absence. \[1\]

## Realised efficacy is an excellence

Minhāj supplies the connected perfection rationale. At 2:162–163, a thing’s perfection belongs within it; simply annexing something external does not turn that thing into its intrinsic perfection. The discussion then considers inseparable attributes of a necessary self. At 2:178–180 it examines a sphere whose efficacy depends on other conjunctions, motions and materials. The dependence concerns actual operation, not merely a missing ability on an inventory. \[2\]

The decisive short passage is retained in electronically paginated text. It is not a newly collated print or manuscript reading:

<span dir="rtl">فتأثيره من كماله فإن المؤثر أكمل من غير المؤثر وهو مفتقر في هذا الكمال إلى غيره فلا يكون واجبا بنفسه</span>

English translation by dot: “Its efficacy is part of its perfection, for an efficacious agent is more perfect than one that is not efficacious; in this perfection it needs another, so it is not necessary through itself.” \[2, electronic 2:180\]

The subsequent passage extends the analysis to the sphere’s maker and to makers of a human being, its materials and its sustenance. The subject is connected actual production. It is not confined to the hypothesis that two wills disagree, nor to an assertion that agreement efficiently produces another will.

The argument places a real burden on a voluntarily partial original-source proposal. If an agent actually receives a requisite completion of its own efficacy, consent does not change that efficacy’s provenance. The ability to have acted differently also does not alter the actuality it chose. Nevertheless, the further inference from a received token perfection to failure of necessary self-existence requires care. A later section identifies the source-mode reconstruction that carries the present argument without treating every renewed act as an eternally necessary attribute.

## The maker who also preserves

Darʾ gives the most useful positive comparison for the current appraisal. Ibn Taymiyya reports Ibn Rushd’s comparison between a producer who originates and preserves its effect and a producer whose effect needs another preserver. He explicitly endorses the comparison, then disputes whether the philosophers’ account satisfies it. The proposition is therefore adopted in the response, rather than merely quoted as an opponent’s view. \[3\]

The relevant electronic wording is:

<span dir="rtl">إن الفاعل الذي يوجد مفعوله ويحفظه أشرف وأدخل في باب الفاعلية من الفاعل الذي يوجد مفعوله ويحتاج إلى فاعل آخر يحفظه بعد الإيجاد</span>

English translation by dot: “The agent that brings its effect into existence and preserves it is nobler and more fully an agent than the agent that brings its effect into existence but needs another agent to preserve it afterwards.” Ibn Taymiyya immediately describes this statement as correct. The quotation preserves the retained electronic text; its exact printed page has not been verified. \[3\]

The comparison concerns an actual causal role. The fuller agent does not merely possess an unused capacity to preserve; its actual provision includes what the effect needs. This gives positive content to the proposed perfection: adequate, unborrowed provision of the determinate result. It avoids making unity depend on an empty negation or a preference for fewer agents.

The same discussion also states an important control. External composition arises through the craftsman’s intrinsic act together with the materials’ receptivity and preserving powers. It does not say that the material therefore efficiently produces the inward act. The comparison and the distinction occur together. That is why the act–effect distinction limits a reception inference without cancelling the independent comparison of actual original sourcehood.

## Voluntary partnership and sovereignty

Ibn al-Qayyim provides a complementary evaluative premise. His discussion in al-Dāʾ wa-l-dawāʾ does not merely predict that two rulers will quarrel. It treats partnership as a deficiency incompatible with complete divinity and holds that a divine agent would not consent to that deficiency. The operative authorial wording was checked against the specified scan:

<span dir="rtl">إذ الشركة نقص ينافي كمال الإلهية، والإله لا يرضى لنفسه أن يكون إلهًا ناقصًا</span>

English translation by dot: “For partnership is a deficiency incompatible with complete divinity, and the divine agent does not accept being deficient in divinity.” \[4, p. 470\]

Consent can therefore be part of what the argument evaluates. A voluntary arrangement is not vindicated simply because no power has been depleted. The question is whether the arrangement realises a fitting form of original government. Human cooperation does not answer that question by analogy: human collaborators commonly supply one another with abilities, materials or executive contributions that they lack.

Al-Ṣawāʿiq connects the positive account of agency and self-sufficiency with a separate absolute-supremacy argument. Two beings cannot each be strictly above the other in the same respect. That is a strong conditional result. Earning the unrestricted comparative premise, however, is a distinct task. A bearer’s maximal possession of a positive excellence does not by itself establish that it is strictly superior to every other conceivable bearer. Passages which already describe their subject as the source upon whom every existent depends cannot silently replace the narrower starting witness. \[5, pp. 231–233, 656–663, 700–701\]

Ibn Uthaymin explicitly considers reconciliation. His discussion combines mutual inability to prevail, order and reciprocal fear; it does not identify received productive efficacy. Agreement is locally characterised as extremely unlikely, while the overall plurality conclusion is asserted as impossible. Those strengths differ. The surrounding perfection rationale deserves appraisal beyond a simple earthly-ruler analogy, without attributing every reconstructed scope premise to his literal text. \[8\]

# Causal nonduplication and the stronger local norm

## Why the earlier scope correction remains necessary

Three propositions must be separated. A could produce an effect if B were absent. A’s actual exercise supplies sufficient efficacy without receiving completion from B. A is the exclusive exhaustive actual original producer of that effect. The primitive double-sufficiency proposal attributes the second to A and B concurrently while denying the third of either. It specifies distinct actual exercises, one concrete effect, no missing portion, no idle backup and no reduced effort.

That proposal has not thereby been shown possible. But its stated content differs from ordinary cooperation, unused ability and merely matching alternative outcomes. If A alone actually produces the whole, B does not also produce it. This contradiction is sound once solitary production is granted. It does not, by itself, establish that nonexclusive sufficient efficacy means production alone.

The maker-and-preserver comparison evaluates provision needing foreign completion. Primitive duplication purports to place both exercises on the unborrowed side of that comparison. Recording both in an exhaustive history would establish divided attribution if both are genuine; it would not prove that either needed the other’s completion. The earlier favourable appraisal had not separately earned this extension. Its scope correction is retained. \[3, 7, pp. 134–154\]

## The positive causal rationale

The additional principle accepted here is causal nonduplication, abbreviated CND. It concerns entire original production of the same concrete contribution in the same causal respect. It is not a prohibition of multiple sufficient conditions, reasons, descriptions or levels of agency. It allows real secondary agency and cooperation through differentiated work.

Its positive rationale is productive individuation. Genuine additional production has determinate causal content: a further source supplies work, a requisite, material, reinforcement, receptivity or preservation. A second contribution need not add a visible quantity. Conversely, an unused alternative supplies no second actual production. Darʾ’s examples of bodily powers, a thrown stone, air, food and receptive capacity distinguish these roles more finely than a coarse endpoint description does. \[10\]

Determinate content does not analytically entail nonduplication. The nonduplicative organisation of entire production is the substantive judgement being made. It assumes neither microphysical atomism nor one uniquely computable decomposition of every process. The examples illuminate how genuine causal plurality is individuated; they are not an exhaustive induction over every possible case.

The primitive rival asks whether two complete original productive relations can both obtain without differentiated work. A reason to count both as actual entire productions could be metaphysical rather than mechanical. Merely repeating the two source labels, however, does not supply it. I find the account connecting actuality, determinate contribution and complete production sufficiently illuminating for defeasible acceptance. Explanatory economy reinforces that rationale. Absence of a demonstrated rival removes one potential defeater; it does not itself create positive warrant. A persuasive conceptual criticism could defeat the rationale without producing a physical counterexample.

There is a genuine difference from original existential adequacy. A wholly received account otherwise leaves an original-provision shortfall. Primitive duplication alleges surplus original supply. Bare adequacy therefore discriminates less directly against duplication. The CND judgement has its own rationale and remains more contestable than the whole-alone contradiction. Suspension is reasonable disagreement, not evidence that every dissenter misunderstands the words.

The source’s criticism of an empty “one from one” principle is a further control. Asfahānī pp. 462–467 allows several differentiated created factors and demands intelligible causal content. One cannot infer one original producer merely from the numerical unity of the effect. Ordinary plurality and duplication of entire original production must be distinguished. \[7\]

# Independence and the perfection of actual exercise

Standing power and fitting exercise answer different questions. Nonexercise does not establish incapacity, while intact capacity does not vindicate every use. A unity argument fails if it identifies nonexercise with lost power. An objection to the stronger perfection argument fails if it treats the retained faculty as sufficient for fitting action.

The source-owned veracity appraisal already recognises that distinction: a knowingly false assertion can be defective while knowledge and expressive faculties remain. Unity is not deduced by analogy from veracity. Its action-specific rationale concerns adequate unborrowed original provision of a concrete produced result.

The stronger source-mode premise is separate from CND. A qualified essentially independent and practically perfect original’s actual productive role should possess unborrowed adequate original provision, rather than depend on an irreducibly foreign original positive resource. I judge the fuller mode fitting to original practical perfection, rather than merely one optional larger accomplishment. The maker-and-preserver comparison, the realised-efficacy account and the broader intrinsic-perfection rationale support this judgement. \[2, 3, 7, pp. 507–508, 532–536\]

The distinction is clearest in a transformation. A may supply its entire intrinsic transformative exercise while B supplies the material. The causal description establishes incomplete original coverage of the resulting composition. It does not alone establish that A’s chosen role is deficient. That evaluative conclusion comes from the stronger source-mode premise. The word “undertaking” must not hide a prior assignment of the whole composite role to A.

A critic can accordingly ask why a freely chosen less comprehensive role could not be fitting. The critic need not demonstrate a good uniquely requiring an original peer before that question has force. My acceptance rests on the positive excellence of adequate unborrowed provision in the agency actually exercised, under the accepted original and practical-perfection framework. Absence of a peer-essential good is at most secondary support.

This norm concerns the mode of actual provision. It does not require creating every possible object, preserving every effect forever or maximising activity irrespective of wisdom. Selective creation, properly timed action and genuine derived cooperation can preserve the mode. If the stronger norm is judged insufficient, CND alone does not rescue common-field coverage.

The relational-good objection also earns a real control. A shared process can benefit participants in ways that a matching isolated endpoint does not preserve; Asfahānī pp. 422–423 expressly makes this point about means. The examined medieval perfect-love comparison likewise values real personal participation. It does not establish several independently original causal suppliers: personal distinction, equal relevant excellence and independent original provenance are different claims. This leaves no newly established source-mode counterexample, but supplies no second proof of the norm either. \[7, 13\]

# The source mode reconstruction and its textual tension

## Four levels of perfection

The evidence requires four distinctions. Essential standing perfections, such as the independently defended life, knowledge, power and wisdom, characterise the concrete necessary bearer. An essential original mode concerns how the source’s agency is possessed and exercised without foreign original completion. The genus of activity concerns continuing or successive agency as a kind. A token act is this particular realised exercise at its fitting occasion.

These levels cannot be collapsed. An essential mode can constrain every fitting exercise without making each exercise a timeless constituent of the bearer. Similarly, an enduring kind of activity does not make one particular act beginningless, nor make the abstract kind a separate productive source. Temporal newness does not by itself prove a choice among alternative complete histories with all complete grounds held fixed.

The retained Asfahānī commentary supplies a direct control. In the inspected edition, Ibn Taymiyya allows the prior absence of a particular act to be fitting and its later occurrence to be the perfection appropriate to its time:

<span dir="rtl">بل كان الكمال عدمه قبل وقت وجوده، ووجوده وقت وجوده</span>

English translation by dot: “Rather, perfection consisted in its absence before the time of its existence, and in its existence at the time of its existence.” \[7, p. 411\]

The surrounding pages praise actually performing the loved act at the loved time in the loved manner. They therefore support neither eternal-token maximalism nor a reduction of perfection to indefinitely idle ability. They also distinguish achieving a loved end through one’s own act from being completed by an independent supplier. Where the text invokes sole origination to explain that difference, its universal source scope must be acknowledged rather than used as an independent proof of that same scope. \[7, pp. 411–419\]

## What the compressed Minhāj argument leaves to explain

Minhāj 2:162–170 explains the existential inference most clearly for inseparable perfections included in the necessary self. At 2:174 it distinguishes necessities of existence from incidental states. The sphere example then concerns unchosen constitution, while 2:180 expressly includes actual conditioned efficacy. At 2:182–183 the discussion returns to a general argument that perfection necessarily belongs to the necessary being. The text does not there separately spell out how every renewed voluntary token fits the transition. \[2\]

The tension is real. The passage’s universal wording and its application to actual efficacy place pressure on a reading which restricts its direct existential inference to inseparable standing attributes. The wider action doctrine, however, prevents simply treating every token perfection as an eternally possessed essential constituent. Both pieces of evidence must remain visible. The retained witness’s apparent missing negation on Asfahānī p. 412 and defective ending on p. 667 are not silently repaired or made load-bearing; the clear proper-time distinction on pp. 411 and 413 supplies its own support.

The present reconstruction therefore distinguishes a source-owned thesis from the particular form of its justification. Independence in actions and effects is an explicit authorial claim. Local closure of the genuine productive requisites of an undertaken effect is a faithful but nontrivial rational application. Grounding that closure in an essential original mode, instead of inferring received whole existence from any received token state, is an inferential clarification or repair across passages. It is not a derivation explicitly completed in Minhāj 2:183.

This distinction prevents a reversed implication. If a bearer’s whole existence is received, its intrinsic concrete instantiation is received in that existential respect. It does not follow that reception in any one state proves receipt of the bearer’s entire existence. The stronger exclusion needs the additional claim that foreign completion of the relevant actual productive role violates an essential original perfection. Its immediate conclusion is incompatibility with that mode. Ibn Taymiyya’s stronger conclusion concerning necessary self-existence remains recorded at the stronger constitutive scope his argument asserts.

The repair has genuine source support, but its central classification is not thereby self-evident. It still requires the positive appraisal of actual original sourcehood. The intrinsic-act/material distinction is relevant precisely because not every condition of an external composite effect is automatically an intrinsic perfection received by its agent. The current affirmative judgement credits complete original provision as a governing source-mode excellence while acknowledging that this is interpretatively and philosophically substantive.

# How original provision follows through created causes

## Three attributions

A created agent can own an act proximally and morally. An original source can supply the productive reality through which that agent acts. A source can also be the complete original provider of the resulting effect. These are three different relations. The second does not automatically imply the first or the third.

Suppose A originally supplies productive reality actually employed by a derived agent H in producing a result r. A belongs in r’s actual original productive account, even when H genuinely performs the act. H’s exercise does not turn its received powers into an autonomous original stock. Mere temporal precedence, knowledge, concurrence or spatial proximity would not establish this attribution; actual productive ancestry does.

Complete original provision requires more. The present argument combines that genuine mediate contribution with the additional source-mode perfection principle. If some of r’s actual original basis comes from an independent peer, the original source’s role remains foreign-completed through the intermediary. The intermediary changes the immediate agent. It does not convert the peer’s original contribution into A’s provision or remove A’s actual contribution from the account.

A separate demonstration that A intentionally owns each token as its own act is therefore unnecessary for this productive attribution. Identifying a dependent state presently supplied by A is a useful sufficient test, but it is not the only bridge. Requiring intentional ownership of every mediated effect would apply the wrong kind of ownership to a claim about original productive resources.

This correction does not remove the need for intentional ownership where it actually matters. H’s assertion remains H’s assertion unless there is additional warrant for A’s own assertion or attestation. Causal provision of the powers involved does not make every creaturely representation a truthful communication by the source. The same distinction protects genuine subordinate agency from being erased by common original creation. Ibn al-Qayyim expressly allows real created efficacy while distinguishing it from coequal intrinsically possessed original powers. \[6\]

## Shared products and later modification

Directly shared production presents the simplest case. If an actual effect requires original inputs from two independent sources, its complete original basis includes both. The separately appraised source-mode norm rules out the qualified perfect source’s foreign-completed role there; causal accounting alone does not make that normative judgement. Calling each intention internally complete does not supply the missing original basis of the whole actual product.

A later modifier gives a second case. Suppose B changes material originally supplied by A. If B’s actual transformation requires that material and its relevant receptivity, B’s current productive role has a foreign original requisite. B’s unused ability to originate different material does not supply the actual material. Nothing in this analysis requires A to have intended its earlier product to remain unchanged forever.

Where two created processes interact, the same test applies to their original resources. A proposed division cannot confine each original to its earliest isolated output and then leave the later mixed result outside any original account. The derivative powers still have actual provenance when they operate. Their meeting does not generate an unexplained new original supply.

The test remains typed and occurrence-sensitive. An earlier act, its produced situation and a later act by the same bearer are not one event. A prior-created situation can occasion a later wise exercise without thereby producing the source’s existence or original power. Responsive causal roles may overlap; an intelligible reason or object is not thereby proved nonproductive. Collapsing all these occurrences into a single bearer-level node would manufacture a cycle that the account does not assert.

Similarly, a condition already included in the same source’s original provision does not introduce a foreign original peer. It need not be causally or explanatorily inert. Conversely, describing a genuinely foreign productive input as an “occasion” does not erase its origin. The relevant explanatory role and respect must be established; no single vocabulary rule about “cause” or “condition” can do that work.

## From local coverage to the connected field

At a real productive crossing between purported original domains, the common effect’s account includes productive, material or preserving resources from both sides. Faithful mediate attribution retains those original contributions. The source-mode perfection principle then excludes a qualified perfect source’s foreign-completed role at that crossing. The stronger local-coverage norm excludes differentiated foreign completion, while CND excludes alleged primitive duplication of entire original production. With both premises, source identity propagates through the genuinely connected field.

Connectedness therefore propagates coverage; it does not supply the local requirement. In the retained Sixth architecture, actual scope and faithful strict productive ancestry belong to P0 and P1, local complete coverage to P2, and the relevant connectedness to P3. The separate P4 interpretation ensures that a graph’s reflexive coverage is not mistaken for an actual productive act. The broader disposition’s original-completion principle also called P1 is a different premise from Sixth’s ancestry P1.

The Nineteenth judgement gives substantive support to local complete coverage P2 through the additional actual-source perfection, CND and mediate-attribution appraisals. It does not rename P2 as a consequence of connectedness. The actual productive witness and the complete original account are still needed, including all relevant branches and history-inclusive contributions.

An infinite field or an infinite subordinate ancestry is not automatically excluded. The argument does not require a first temporal event or global well-foundedness. It requires actual original provision throughout the relevant account. A lone root beside an unsupported derivative branch cannot satisfy that requirement. Nor can a coarse endpoint description delete real contributors merely because another possible history would have reached a similar endpoint. Retaining contributors in the history does not itself prove that primitive duplication is impossible; CND supplies that separate judgement.

The bearer qualification is schematic. The earlier identification of a bearer with essential whole-existence originality and the relevant perfections does not exempt an alleged second bearer that genuinely satisfies the same conditions. Equally, an unexplained local contribution, efficient-only nonreceipt or the label “original” does not establish those qualifications. A genuine holistic original bearer also does not make every component independently original.

# The substantive dissent and the standard of warrant

The adverse perfection appraisal accepts that intact standing power does not vindicate every exercise, chosen reception remains reception, and the maker-and-preserver has the fuller role in the stated respect. It also accepts the distinction between derivative causes and independent original partners. It withholds sufficient warrant for treating that fuller role as the required mode of every qualified original exercise.

Its question is substantive. Does superior performance of a complete-producer role show that every perfect original must undertake that role, rather than some otherwise fitting contribution? Success alone does not make an act fitting, but comparative fullness alone does not automatically make the fuller role obligatory. The proper-time and relational-good passages make this issue sharper. The present affirmative judgement supplies its own positive evaluative rationale while preserving that dissent.

The separate adverse CND appraisal correctly distinguishes needed completion from alleged unneeded concurrence. Ordinary examples display differentiated work, and the whole-alone contradiction already assumes solitary production. A critic can reasonably judge the positive individuation rationale insufficient without producing a possible plural universe. The current acceptance disagrees about its evidential force; it does not refute or withdraw the dissent.

The standard remains sufficient defeasible rational belief, as in the earlier necessity, original-completion, essential-mode and veracity appraisals. A premise does not fail merely because it is substantive and disputed. Conversely, authorial endorsement or usefulness to a valid reconstruction does not establish its truth. The positive content, proper application and actual objections determine the appraisal. Different premises can receive different confidence under the same standard.

The broader reading reinforces that discipline. Asfahānī p. 47 asks a presented proof to establish its premises and answer objections; p. 133 permits genuinely evident premises to remain unstated. Pages 638–639 distinguish someone’s actual grounds of knowledge from the argument they can articulate, while allowing multiple knowledge routes. These are compatible claims. A flaw in one public argument need not defeat its conclusion or every person’s knowledge. Unspecified other routes do not repair that particular flaw. \[7\]

Explanatory economy supplies supplementary support. Several original wills need a determinate allocation of contributions, not merely agreement on a shared end. One source avoids that further matching problem. But the exclusion of original deficiencies is a consequence of the same accepted source-mode norm, not a further independent confirmation. Repetition by several authors or assessments is not additional proof. The report claims warranted bounded belief, not universally compulsory demonstration.

# Standing power and source sensitive targets

The actual-source route does not settle every issue in the domain of standing power. An ordinary source-neutral result, a token referred to by its actual producer, a history containing a specified original contribution and another subject’s intrinsic act are different targets.

An arbitrary source label cannot exclude an ordinary eligible result from unrestricted power. But a history expressly containing H’s unreceived original contribution changes if that contribution is replaced or made received. This conditional observation does not prove the history possible; it identifies relational content a power claim must preserve.

Performing H’s willing also differs from causing H to will. Genuine secondary agency already permits an act to belong to a creature while its original productive reality is supplied by another. Ownership alone does not exempt an act from causal production.

For an essentially original H, the further issue is whether foreign production of its intrinsic efficacy contradicts its original mode, and whether that very act is an eligible transferable object of every other qualified source’s power. If the universal domain includes it with its relevant identity and original mode unchanged, the incompatibility with a second qualified original must be honoured. Nonexercise does not dissolve a conflict in the powers attributed.

The retained power rationale does not unambiguously settle that stronger domain. A principled exclusion requires an independently warranted identity or original-agency relation; an inclusion must preserve the target’s constitutive relations. The common-source judgement instead rests on the explicitly appraised CND and actual-source perfection premises, not on silently expanding standing power.

Actual causal accounting also differs from essentiality of origin across alternatives. Explaining who actually produced an effect does not prove that the same effect could never have had another origin. The field argument needs the complete actual account without importing a general theory of transworld origin-essentiality.

# Wider gains from reading the arguments together

## Source identity voice and an utterance event

The Asfahānī opening distinguishes causing a speaker or utterance from being the bearer whose speech it is. It also rejects imagining an intrinsic attribute as a detachable object migrating between subjects. The later discussion at pp. 389–391 supplies a positive transmission account: the originating speaker’s words can be conveyed through another person’s voice and bodily act. \[7, pp. 9–25, 389–391\]

Three errors are thereby separated. A new voice need not mean a new author. Preserved authorship does not make two acoustic events numerically identical. ## Real succession and correctly typed conditions

A motion, utterance or transaction can genuinely occur successively even though all its parts never coexist. The concept of the whole, a selected phase and the occurring process are different. The point bears on the unity reconstruction. Distinct acts by one bearer must not be collapsed into one event merely to manufacture a causal cycle. An object, reason, condition and productive resource can overlap, but counterfactual dependence alone does not identify donated power or operative completion. The author reconciles prayer and response with universal creation at p. 435. That account is coherent within its theology, but cannot independently prove the same universal scope. The current field argument therefore requires genuinely productive crossings, not shared topics, approval, knowledge, covariance or every explanatory relation indiscriminately.

Al-Dāʾ’s opening separates the remedy, its actual use, recipient receptivity and obstruction. Failure can therefore leave several possible explanations. The programme connection is condition-sensitive reuse. This is a structural philosophical comparison, not a new empirical causal-identification theorem, clinical validation or diagnosis of anyone’s continuing illness.

The same work distinguishes prevention, removal, mitigation and resistance short of elimination.
