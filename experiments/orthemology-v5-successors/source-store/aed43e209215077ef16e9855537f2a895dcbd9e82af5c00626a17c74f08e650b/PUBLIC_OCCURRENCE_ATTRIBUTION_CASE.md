## What is actually distinct

The public v3 capsule indexes a historical author's stance separately from the present reconstruction's epistemic status. The public acceptance explicitly includes that attribution distinction in its accepted scope. Reading the source proposition, reporting the source author's act, and endorsing a current inference are separate operations.

This is a concrete source-scholarship instance of the relational interface. A safe projection keeps at least the following bindings when answering an attribution question:

    historical occurrence ↔ historical speaker ↔ source content ↔ source stance
    modern occurrence ↔ current assessor ↔ reconstruction ↔ current warrant status

The content nodes are related by the actual argument. They must not be declared identical merely because a shortened summary discusses them together. The modern formal claim has its own scope and premises; no general equation with the historical formulation is asserted.

A projection can preserve the relevant topic and much of the propositional content yet attach a status to the wrong occurrence. Two errors then become possible: attributing the present assessor's unresolved proof burden to the historical speaker, or treating the historical speaker's assertion as a newly established warrant in the present analysis. Adding another copy of either sentence does not correct the lost binding. The repaired source capsule retains the relations explicitly.

## Why this matters to continuation

The recipient of a translated or transcluded argument inherits neither every original speech act nor every current assessor's endorsement merely by receiving related content. Continuing the research organisation faithfully requires preserving who was doing what with which claim, and which warrants have actually been established at the current stage. The successor may quote, translate, criticise or adopt; those are different new occurrences whose force must be tracked.

This case does not establish a new theorem. It gives an actual source-bound discriminator for the original occurrence-language avenue and a concrete fidelity obligation for the collective continuation question. The positive repair is less ambitious and more useful than a generic source tag: keep the speaker/occurrence/status relation at the projection boundary, then answer the particular question at its proper source and scope.
