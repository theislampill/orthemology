# Endpoint-density robustness of the literal-failure rates

Status: fresh ordinary robustness corollary, submitted for independent review. The underlying loss geometry and empirical concentration estimate are those of the separately reviewed Beta-rate packet. The prior below need not be Beta or conjugate. This is neither a kernel theorem nor a historical novelty claim.

## Result and precise prior class

Fix 0 < ε ≤ 1/4. Draw α once from a probability measure μ on [0,1], and retain that same α. Conditional on α, receipts are passive iid Bernoulli(α). A common measurable policy always issues a coherent real report after exactly n receipts, using only that receipt prefix and a private seed jointly independent of the fixed parameter and the randomness generating the entire receipt sequence. Equivalently, the experiment has product law η(dz) μ(dα) P_α(dy), with q_n a measurable function of z and y_1,...,y_n. Marginal independence from α alone would not suffice. Its earlier reports reveal no additional labels or information and do not affect the receipts. Prior knowledge is permitted; access to the sampled α is not.

A report q fails when either of the following excesses is positive:

    D0(α,ε,q) = q² − 1/4 − αε(1+ε),
    D1(α,ε,q) = (1−q)² − 1/4 + αε(1−ε).

Equality is accepted. If incoherent outputs are allowed by an interface, they count as failures.

Assume there are a,b > 0, a common 0 < δ < 1/2, and positive finite constants m0,M0,m1,M1 such that μ decomposes as

    μ = p0 δ_0 + p1 δ_1
        + f0(α) 1_(0,δ)(α) dα
        + f1(α) 1_(1−δ,1)(α) dα
        + ν,

where p0,p1 ≥ 0, ν is any finite nonnegative measure supported on [δ,1−δ], the total mass is one, and almost everywhere on the respective open intervals,

    m0 α^(a−1) ≤ f0(α) ≤ M0 α^(a−1),
    m1 (1−α)^(b−1) ≤ f1(α) ≤ M1 (1−α)^(b−1).

Thus singular continuous interior mass and any interior atoms are allowed, including atoms at δ and 1−δ. There is no singular component in either punctured endpoint neighbourhood. An arbitrary prior with such bounds on possibly different endpoint neighbourhoods has this form after reducing δ and putting the remaining mass into ν. The constants are for one fixed prior; uniformity over a family of priors is not asserted.

Let p_n(π) be its prior-averaged failure probability at index n ≥ 1. There are c,C > 0, depending only on the fixed prior parameters and ε, such that for every n ≥ 1,

    p_n(π) ≥ c [n^(−a) + n^(−b)]  for every allowed policy π,
    p_n(emp) ≤ C [n^(−a) + n^(−b)],

where the retained empirical report is q_n = 1/2 + ε h_n and h_n is the mean of the first n receipts. The empirical learner does not need the prior, exponents, density constants, or endpoint-atom weights.

These statements give matching orders for the optimal per-index risk, optimal finite-horizon count, and the empirical learner. They do not give an upper rate for every policy: an always-wrong policy can have p_n = 1.

In particular, writing H_N(s) = ∑_(n=1)^N n^(−s),

    inf_π E_μ[∑_(n=1)^N failure_n] = Θ(H_N(a)+H_N(b)).

The infimum allows horizon-dependent policies. The same empirical policy supplies every upper bound. A finite expected lifetime failure count exists exactly when a>1 and b>1. With m=min(a,b), optimal cumulative growth is Θ(N^(1−m)) for m<1, Θ(log(N+1)) for m=1, and Θ(1) for m>1. The index-zero report adds at most one failure.

For any fixed deterministic nondecreasing sequence of positive integers n_j, counting only the report at each declared index n_j, a finite expected count exists exactly when

    ∑_j [n_j^(−a)+n_j^(−b)] < ∞,

or equivalently ∑_j n_j^(−min(a,b)) < ∞. Repeated sample counts are charged repeatedly. The condition therefore also rules out an infinite bounded reporting schedule.

## 1. A prior-free missing-mass lemma

Any report accepted for some α∈[0,1] lies in [1/2,1/2+ε]. For q in this interval set

    l(q) = (q²−1/4)/(ε(1+ε)),
    u(q) = (1/4−(1−q)²)/(ε(1−ε)).

Acceptance is exactly α∈[l,u], with 0≤l≤u≤1. Writing q=1/2+x gives

    u ≤ R0 l,  R0=(1+ε)/(1−ε)>1.

For β=1−α, write q=1/2+ε−z. Its accepted β interval has endpoints

    l1=z(1−2ε+z)/(ε(1−ε)),
    u1=z(1+2ε−z)/(ε(1+ε)),

and u1≤R1 l1, where

    R1=(1+2ε)(1−ε)/((1−2ε)(1+ε))>1.

Reports outside this range have no accepted parameter. Endpoint reports accept only the corresponding endpoint.

For any s>0, R≥1, t>0, and interval [l,u] with 0≤l≤u≤R l, the mass excluded from (0,t) under the density x^(s−1) is at least

    R^(−s) t^s/s.                                      (1)

To see this, if u≤t, the excluded mass times s is l^s+t^s−u^s ≥ t^s−(1−R^(−s))u^s ≥ R^(−s)t^s. If u>t and l<t, the excluded interval (0,l) already has mass l^s/s ≥ (t/R)^s/s. If l≥t, all of (0,t) is excluded. Empty accepted sets satisfy the same lower bound. Boundary points have zero power-density mass, so weak acceptance causes no change.

This lemma uses the full complement, including mass above u when u<t. Merely claiming l≥t/R for every report would be false.

## 2. Universal lower bounds without a posterior formula

Fix n≥1 and t=δ/(n+1). On the all-zero receipt prefix and for 0<α<t,

    (1−α)^n ≥ (n/(n+1))^n ≥ exp(−1),

because t≤1/(n+1) and n log(1+1/n)≤1. Fixing a seed value fixes the report issued on this prefix. By (1), its joint probability of failure and this prefix, integrated only over α∈(0,t), is at least

    exp(−1) m0 R0^(−a) t^a/a.                         (2)

The same bound holds for every seed value. Independence gives the product law needed to average this inequality; Tonelli justifies reversing the nonnegative integrals. No conjugate posterior and no optimal-report selector are used. Arbitrary measurable real-valued randomised reports are covered, including a policy chosen with knowledge of μ.

Reflecting at 1, the joint probability of failure and the all-one prefix is at least

    exp(−1) m1 R1^(−b) t^b/b.                         (3)

The all-zero and all-one prefix events are disjoint for n≥1. Their contributions may therefore be added. Since (n+1)≤2n,

    p_n(π) ≥ c0 n^(−a) + c1 n^(−b),
    c0 = exp(−1) m0 R0^(−a) δ^a/(a 2^a),
    c1 = exp(−1) m1 R1^(−b) δ^b/(b 2^b).

Taking c=min(c0,c1)>0 gives the claimed all-n bound. This argument works from n=1 and does not use an eventual approximation followed by an unexplained adjustment of finitely many indices. The empty prefix at n=0 is not counted twice.

Endpoint atoms add nonnegative contributions and do not weaken (2) or (3). They do invalidate a tempting stronger shortcut. For μ=(99/100)δ_0+(1/100)Uniform[0,1], after n zero receipts the report q=1/2 has conditional failure probability

    [1/(100(n+1))] / [99/100+1/(100(n+1))],

which tends to zero. A constant conditional lower bound copied from the Beta posterior lemma would be false. The unnormalised tail calculation above is the reason the rate conclusion survives.

## 3. Empirical upper bounds and the interior measure

For every fixed interior α, the reviewed empirical calculation gives, with κ=ε²/33,

    P(failure_n | α) ≤ 2 exp(−κ n α(1−α)).             (4)

For completeness, the repair radius is r=εα(1−α)/4. At h=α both excesses equal −ε²α(1−α); on h∈[0,1], their changes are bounded by (3/2)ε|h−α| and ε|h−α|. Thus deviation at most r is accepted. The centred Bernoulli moment bound E|X|^k≤α(1−α), combined with k!≥2·3^(k−2), gives the two-sided Bernstein bound 2 exp(−n r²/[2(v+r/3)]) for v=α(1−α). Substitution yields exponent at least κnv. This is exactly the fixed-index bound verified in the dependency, not concentration at an adaptive sample count.

At α=0 all observed receipts are zero, and at α=1 they are all one. For every n≥1, h_n=α and the empirical report has D0=D1=0. Its endpoint-atom contribution is therefore exactly zero. Formula (4) need not be extended by a division involving zero variance.

On (0,δ), α(1−α)≥(1−δ)α. The upper density bound and the Gamma integral therefore give

    ∫_(0,δ) P(failure_n | α) μ(dα)
      ≤ 2 M0 Γ(a) [κ(1−δ)n]^(−a).

Reflection gives the right-end contribution 2 M1 Γ(b)[κ(1−δ)n]^(−b). On [δ,1−δ], α(1−α)≥δ(1−δ). Consequently, if w=ν([δ,1−δ]) and h=κδ(1−δ)>0,

    p_n(emp) ≤ A0 n^(−a) + A1 n^(−b) + 2w exp(−hn),
    A0=2M0 Γ(a)/[κ(1−δ)]^a,
    A1=2M1 Γ(b)/[κ(1−δ)]^b.                           (5)

This argument integrates a uniform pointwise bound against ν. It needs neither a density for ν nor a finite number of atoms. For example, an affine image of Cantor measure contained in [δ,1−δ] is covered without approximation.

To obtain a polynomial upper bound for every n≥1, set r=ceil(a)≥1. Since exp(hn)≥(hn)^r/r!,

    exp(−hn) ≤ r! h^(−r) n^(−r) ≤ r! h^(−r) n^(−a).

Thus C=max(A0+2w r!h^(−r),A1) is a finite positive admissible constant. Its size is immaterial to the rate assertion; the right side may exceed one at small n.

## 4. Counts, clocks, and almost-sure behaviour

Summing the per-index inequalities proves the finite-horizon comparison. Tonelli proves the lifetime statement even when expectations are infinite. The ordinary power-series test gives a finite budget exactly when both exponents exceed one. When a,b>1, the optimal unconditional expected future count after a deterministic cutoff N≥1 is Θ(N^(1−a)+N^(1−b)); if either exponent is ≤1, every policy has infinite expected tail after every such cutoff. These are original-prior expectations, not claims uniform over realised posteriors.

At a deterministic reporting schedule n_j, apply the same all-n inequalities separately to each evaluated report. A policy may also use the report number j and its previous outputs; after fixing its seed it still has only the declared receipt prefix. No independence between failures or between repeated evaluations is needed. Since n^(−m)≤n^(−a)+n^(−b)≤2n^(−m) for n≥1, the displayed clock criterion follows. It changes which reports are evaluated; it does not erase costs of any reports a physical interface still requires. Data-dependent stopping times require a separate argument.

The empirical learner eventually makes no failures almost surely for each fixed interior α by the strong law and its strictly positive repair radius. Endpoints are correct after the first receipt. Integrating this probability-one statement shows eventual acceptance under every prior in this class, including when its expected count is infinite. Finite almost-sure count and finite expected count remain different conclusions.

## 5. Assumptions that carry the conclusion

The strictly positive lower density constants are essential for the asserted universal lower powers. A pure endpoint prior has zero empirical post-acquisition cost; it cannot be assigned arbitrary positive tail exponents and made to satisfy this theorem. Conversely, lower comparability alone does not control the upper rate: adding a uniform continuous component to a prior with faster endpoint powers preserves a lower bound by those faster powers but produces the critical uniform-prior lower cost.

The inverse-moment shorthand also needs care. Summing (4) at an interior α gives a post-acquisition empirical count at most 2/[κ α(1−α)]. Its integral is taken only over (0,1), with endpoint cost zero. Under this prior class that interior inverse moment is finite exactly when a,b>1. The full-prior inverse moment with value infinity at endpoints can be infinite despite a finite mistake budget: for μ=(1/2)δ_0+(1/2)Beta(2,2), the interior inverse moment is three while the full extended integral is infinite. An endpoint atom must not be inserted into a zero-variance upper formula as if its infinite value represented actual learner cost.

The upper bounds apply to the entire measure on each punctured endpoint neighbourhood, not just to an absolutely continuous part while leaving an additional singular tail unspecified. All unspecified measure is confined to the compact interior; the endpoint atoms are separately handled. Slowly varying unbounded factors, holes in the controlled density, drifting α, informative feedback, and control-dependent sampling are outside this corollary.

The independent-seed hypothesis is substantive. If the seed disclosed α, the exact report 1/2+εα would always be accepted, contradicting the universal lower bound. Arbitrary independent randomisation, however, cannot improve that lower order.

No exact Bayes-optimal report or computable argmax is claimed here. The Beta packet's continuous-CDF argument cannot simply be copied across atoms; it is unnecessary for these matching rates. Likewise this theorem does not infer intrinsic understanding, normative authority, or a metaphysical conclusion from the statistical learner.
