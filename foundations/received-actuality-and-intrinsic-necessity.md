# Received Actuality, Intrinsic Necessity, and Original Source Unity

## Abstract

An account of received actuality must distinguish the production of an effect from the existential adequacy of the resources that produce it. A local explanation may correctly describe how available materials and powers yield a new product while leaving their own received actuality outside its scope. The argument developed here asks what a complete productive account must cover, how an original intrinsic resource supports an actual bearer, and why complete intrinsic adequacy warrants necessity of that same bearer. Its two substantive judgments are global productive adequacy and the modal reach of intrinsic existential sufficiency. The conditional resource-to-bearer inference is given explicitly, while the warrant for those judgments is argued philosophically. Two challenges receive separate treatment: autonomous collective explanation without an original productive resource, and an unreceived original whose own existence is brute and contingent. Neither challenge is answered by changing its terminology or weakening its proposed ontology. The resulting position is an affirmative, defeasible argument for the same actual original's intrinsic necessity, with the precise contribution and limits of each step preserved. The continuation develops the separate argument for one complete original source of the actual connected field, proves the local and propagation results, and reconciles explanatory countermodels and truthful self-knowledge with the affirmative appraisal. Generic explanation, productive provision and individual bearer identity remain distinct.

*Editorial status: this is a new exposition consolidating the named arguments and their recorded corrections. It does not add owner adoption, independent acceptance of this edition, a new formal verification, or fresh authentication of the underlying Arabic sources.*

## 1. The actuality to be explained

The starting point is received actuality: something actual whose relevant productive basis is not supplied by that thing's nonexistent or insufficient reality. Identifying such a target requires more than introducing a predicate called “received.” The argument needs warranted occurrence, an adequate account of the target's identity, and the relevant productive derivation. An ordinary material-generation case makes those requirements concrete. Fix an actually produced fruit, its continuing tree, and the material contributions involved in its development. The fruit is not numerically the tree, and the existence of the tree before the fruit does not establish that this fruit already existed under another name. [Material origination, §§2–3][s5]

Production from antecedent material does not erase this distinction. “From” can describe a persisting constituent, an expended material input, or a productive supplier; those roles should not be collapsed. A continuing source can produce a different individual. Conversely, some changes are alterations of an existing subject, and a change of description does not create a new bearer. The entry case therefore rests on the conjunction of a distinct product and its described material, developmental, and productive derivation. Temporal novelty alone is insufficient. [Material origination, §§2–3; independent review][s5] [Review of material origination][s6]

This is a generic ordinary case used in the source argument, not a newly recorded observation of a dated fruit. Its use does not require observing raw material arise from absolute nothing or proving that every constituent begins when the whole product begins. A persistent constituent, where warranted, retains its identity. Its persistence nevertheless leaves open whether its own existential actuality is received or intrinsically adequate. Ibn Taymiyya's stronger historical doctrine includes real transformation and the cessation of earlier qualified material realities, and criticises accounts that reduce every new animal or plant to altered accidents of retained material subjects. His continuing-tree, silver and wax examples nevertheless distinguish production of another individual from alteration of a persisting subject. This historical doctrine is not identified with modern microphysics. The selected fruit-case premise depends on distinct product identity and described productive derivation; it neither requires that whole substrate theory nor denies warranted constituent persistence. [Material origination, §§3, 5; current claim FND-003][s5] [Foundational claims][s8]

A developmental explanation can genuinely explain the fruit by using the tree, materials, and capacities as available resources. A further claim—that this explanation supplies a complete original account of those resources' own received actuality—has a larger target. The distinction is between two explanatory tasks, not between a valid local explanation and no explanation at all. What follows concerns the larger task. [Material origination, §§3–4][s5]

For that task, let **F** name the received productive field relevant to the case: the product's derivation together with the received suppliers and resources included in the account's nominated scope. F is plural shorthand for what is to be explained; it need not be an additional substance, world-object, or proposition acting as a producer. Its members need not all coexist at one time. Different effects may have different completed conditions, and an indefinitely extended history is not excluded simply for lacking a first temporal member. [Productive totality, §§3, 5; material origination, §§4–5][s4] [Material origination][s5]

The field must remain the target throughout the relevant inference. A resource contributing to complete provision of F has not thereby been shown to bear every stronger relation of relevance to each individual member, or wholly to provide one fruit by itself. The reviewed material application expressly adopted this field scope after correcting an ambiguity between the fruit as entry case and the field as Completion target. That correction preserves a concrete point of entry without inflating what the inference establishes. [Material-origination review, “Exact interface dependency and the resolved finding”][s6]

## 2. What complete productive adequacy requires

Suppose a proposed complete productive account consists entirely of suppliers whose own actuality is received. Each supplier may make a real contribution, and their coordination may explain an effect that no member could produce alone. The question is whether those facts also explain the received actuality of the productive basis itself. If a supplier's existence falls within the nominated target, its availability cannot simply be counted as an independently provided starting resource for that target. The same issue remains when the account is expressed in terms of the suppliers' arrangement. Arrangement may be productive, but its description does not by itself provide the actuality of what is arranged. [Productive totality, §3][s4]

The strongest form of the argument has five connected steps:

1. There is actual received productive reality to be accounted for.
2. A complete productive account must cover the relevant received actuality, including that of derivative suppliers it employs.
3. No derivative supplier can serve as the independently adequate provision of this entire target by borrowing its own received actuality from within that target.
4. Collecting or arranging derivative suppliers does not by itself discharge the same existential insufficiency at field scope.
5. The complete productive basis therefore includes actual unreceived productive actuality relevant to F.

The rationale for the fourth step is the inclusion of all relevant existential prerequisites within the scope of completion. The work an arrangement accomplishes does not, merely as arrangement, remove the received status of the actuality on which it depends. The claim is about sufficient productive provision of that actuality. It is not the inference that every property of each member must belong to the plurality. [Productive totality, §3][s4]

This is a substantive **global productive adequacy** judgment. Local external need alone does not prove that an entire plurality has an outside source. A prohibition of reciprocal efficient causation also does not, by itself, establish the global conclusion. The argument supplies the additional rationale that the proposed account must cover the derivative suppliers' own received actuality, and that collective form does not supply it merely by changing how the basis is described. Its force must be assessed at that scope. [Productive totality, §§3, 7; current claim FND-004][s4] [Foundational claims][s8]

No inference from individual possible absence to joint possible absence is required. Nor is a temporally first supplier required. A beginningless received history can still be the subject of the complete productive question. Equally, the argument does not infer original status from persistence across the alternatives represented by a model. A supplier could, under a proposed interpretation, exist in every represented world while receiving its whole existence in each. Its modal persistence would not make it intrinsically sufficient. A field defined by the actuality requiring productive provision must not silently exclude such a received supplier. [Productive totality, §§3, 5][s4]

The relevant sense of dependence must also remain restricted. Actual productive or existential receipt is not established merely by necessary co-presence. A bearer and its intrinsic qualification may be conceptually distinguishable without being separately produced substances. Intrinsic conditions can accompany one another without mutually producing one another. The source reconstruction expressly preserves those distinctions, so a necessary conditional or a list of a thing's features cannot by itself establish the external existential need at issue. [Productive totality, §4][s4]

A related source distinction appears in Ibn Taymiyya's *Darʾ taʿāruḍ al-ʿaql wa-l-naql*, III, 439–441. The exclusive-creation wording continuing onto page 440 belongs to his quotation of Ibn Tūmart's *al-Dalīl wa-l-ʿilm*. Ibn Taymiyya's own reply begins with *qultu* (“I say”). It argues that affirming knowledge, power or choice already affirms determinate qualifications, whereas unrestricted existence as such is a mental abstraction, not a featureless concrete existent. He then identifies the further question whether every determination requires a separate specifier. The passage therefore distinguishes concrete qualification and external specification; its quoted creation formula is not an additional argument here excluding two complete original productive relations. Nor does this limitation establish such a rival's metaphysical possibility. [Quoted passage][dar-qualification-quotation] [Ibn Taymiyya's reply][dar-qualification-reply]

Unity in kind must also be kept separate from numerical identity. In the continuation of this reply, Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes sharing a kind or description from being one individual: existing things share the description of existence, and humans share humanity. On his account, the common universal as universal is conceived rather than one concrete entity subsisting in all its instances. The inference at issue can be exposed without settling every theory of universals. From two bearers’ satisfying the same description it does not follow that they are identical; otherwise any two things correctly described as human would be one person. Nor does analysing a thing under several concepts establish that it was assembled from corresponding external components. These are controls on a proposed inference, not evidence that two entirely adequate originals actually exist. A common existential description supplies neither original productive resources nor numerical unity of their bearers. The latter claim still needs the productive argument in section 9. [*Darʾ*, III, 446–448, continuation of the reply][dar-kind-and-individual]

The positive conclusion is that the received field has a relevant original productive basis under the intended productive interpretation. It is not yet the conclusion that one bearer individually provides every requisite, that all adequate noncausal explanations reduce to this ontology, or that a particular original has been shown necessary. Those claims need their own premises. Keeping this allocation precise strengthens the argument by locating what is affirmed rather than allowing several conclusions to be carried by one ambiguous use of “complete.” [Productive totality, §§5, 7; current claims FND-004–005][s4] [Foundational claims][s8]

### 2.1 Required background and nonnecessitating production

An account can explain an occurrence through an actual productive background without claiming that this background necessitated that particular occurrence. This matters when indeterminism is offered as a defeater of the original-resource argument. Ameri's causal discussion explicitly considers an indeterministic interpretation as a concession within a wider argument that otherwise favours determinism. The concession preserves a physical setting with the requisite capacities and conditions. It does not turn that setting into nothing. [Causal foundations and the original resource bridge, §1; focused review][causal-background] [Focused causal-transfer review][causal-review]

The defensible argument has four steps. First, suppose the occurrence under discussion requires a particular obtaining background. Second, suppose that background can obtain without this occurrence. Third, the actual occurrence still establishes the actuality of its required background. Therefore the absence of necessitation does not establish the absence of every enabling reality. The first and second premises concern different directions of dependence: an event can require a condition that does not suffice for the event. A simple logical control allows three configurations of background and event: both absent, background present without the event, and both present. Every event in that model has its background, although not every instance of the background has the event. This illustrates the distinction; it is not a model of quantum physics or evidence that any particular physical theory has exactly this structure.

The conclusion earns a limited but positive explanatory credit. A proposed field, state, capacity or operative circumstance must be included in the account when the occurrence actually depends on it. Indeterminism alone does not license retaining its explanatory work while omitting its actuality. Nor does this require dismissing a probabilistic local explanation as no explanation. Such an explanation can answer a legitimate question about outcomes while leaving another question about the actuality of its background unanswered. [Causal foundations, §§1–2][causal-background]

That local result still falls short of original provision. Three targets must remain distinct: why this event occurs; why its enabling background exists; and whether a complete account of the received productive field includes an actual unreceived resource. An actual background may itself be wholly received. The proposition that an event requires it establishes neither that it is unreceived nor that one has already accounted for all the received reality used in the explanation. If the background is received and belongs within the target field, the complete productive question must include its actuality. The global adequacy argument above supplies the additional rationale; the local indeterministic example does not become an independent proof of it. [Causal foundations, §§1–2; contextual reconciliation][causal-background] [Causal context and explanatory scope][causal-context]

The distinction also fixes the meaning of Completion in section 3. Its resource must be actual, relevant to the received target, and unreceived. Its definition does not say that this resource by itself deterministically necessitates the effect. A genuinely original probabilistic productive resource would therefore not be excluded merely for failing to necessitate one particular outcome. Conversely, a deterministically sufficient but wholly received background would not acquire original status from its sufficiency. Determinism and original nonreceipt answer different questions. Neither a temporal first event nor sole entire production is built into that resource condition. [Causal foundations, §2; original resource/bearer interface][causal-background] [Original resource/bearer interface][s10]

Two further identifications remain substantive. A nomologically required condition is not automatically an efficient contributor: the truth of a law-proposition differs from an instantiated productive capacity. And identifying an actual background does not by itself identify a resource intrinsic to a particular bearer or establish that whole reception of the bearer carries reception of that resource in the same respect. Those are the separate Realisation and support-transport requirements. The later intrinsic-necessity and complete-source conclusions likewise need their own premises. Making the local occurrence deterministic would not supply them. [Causal foundations, §2; focused review][causal-background] [Focused causal-transfer review][causal-review]

The causal case also has affirmative epistemic content. A sound inferential relation between propositions is not yet an account of a person's arriving at a belief through appreciating that relation. In the latter case, the apprehended reasons must actually operate in the formation or maintenance of the belief; an accidentally matching conclusion is insufficient to show that the thinker believes it on those grounds. On the causal reading defended here, this calls for an operative, reason-sensitive cognitive route. It does not make a proposition an efficient producer merely because another proposition entails it. The source's argument thus supports an intelligible connection within actual thinking while preserving the distinction between logical support and productive activity. [Causal foundations, §3; focused review, “Causal intelligibility and selective brute existence”][causal-background] [Focused causal-transfer review][causal-review]

Empirical inquiry supplies a second, connected consideration. Investigating what a concrete thing can do concerns differentiated powers and the conditions of their exercise, rather than an entirely causally empty succession of appearances. On this account, experience can disclose causal structure without creating that structure merely by repeated association. These are positive reasons for taking stable causal relations seriously, not a deduction that every familiar expectation is a law or that every disputed causal theory is correct. Equally, considering an effect while leaving its source out of one's description does not show that the effect could exist without the requisite reality. Conceptual isolation and warranted metaphysical separability are different achievements. The causal claim still needs support for the particular connection asserted; neither the ease of imagining its absence nor the practical success of one investigation decides every such question. [Focused causal-transfer review, “Causal intelligibility and selective brute existence”][causal-review]

These considerations support causal intelligibility in cognition and the investigated world at their stated scope. They neither identify all explanatory relations with efficient production nor establish that every reality has a sufficient explanation. Moving from local cognitive dependence to an original background's intrinsic existential necessity would require an additional argument about that background and the actual epistemic route. The original-resource and intrinsic-adequacy arguments must therefore keep their own premises and rationale. [Causal foundations, §3][causal-background]

The strongest selective brute-original proposal is compatible with much of this causal argument. It can retain a concrete, beginningless productive reality, differentiated intrinsic powers, stable local causal order and appropriately connected cognition, while denying a sufficient explanation of that reality's own obtaining. Rejecting globally empty causation therefore does not yet reject that more selective claim. Equally, displaying the proposal does not establish its metaphysical possibility or defeat the affirmative intrinsic-adequacy judgment developed in sections 5–8. The disagreement concerns the original's existential adequacy, rather than whether local effects have operative sources. Inferential support between propositions must also not be identified without argument with efficient production between realities. [Causal foundations, §3; focused review][causal-background] [Focused causal-transfer review][causal-review]

Temporal claims require the same care. The source's local exclusion of a concurrent external cause does not follow from the impossibility of a thing producing itself: a distinct actual source can act concurrently with its effect without using the effect's nonexistent power. Ameri's later explicit distinction between causal and temporal priority permits that concurrence and controls interpretation of the book's considered position. His separate finite-past argument is not thereby identified with the beginningless-activity account used here. No new physical adjudication, universal causal principle or proof of original uniqueness follows from this bounded causal comparison. Its contribution is the exact separation of required actuality, outcome necessitation and original provision. [Focused causal-transfer review, “The local temporal inference”; causal foundations, §4][causal-review] [Causal foundations][causal-background]


The distinction between temporal order and productive dependence also determines what regress analogies can establish. An unbacked cheque does not supply the funds it promises merely because another equally unbacked cheque is offered behind it. Likewise, if every purported authorization only postpones the exercise of authority to another step, the description has not yet supplied an actual exercise that authorizes the action. The relevant defect is the missing operative resource or authority. It is not established merely by counting infinitely many links: a finite collection of promises without the required backing does not acquire that backing from its finitude either. [Causal foundations, §4][causal-background]

To transfer such an argument to a beginningless succession, one must establish the same dependence structure. Having no first temporal member does not by itself show that every occurrence lacks an actual enabling basis, that every productive resource is merely promised, or that a standing producer is unavailable at every stage. A proposed history in which each particular occurrence has an actual enabling basis is therefore not answered simply by renaming it an infinite chain of unfulfilled permissions. Conversely, describing such a history does not establish its metaphysical possibility, the adequacy of its whole productive account, or the absence of an original resource. The complete-field argument asks whether the received actuality used throughout has been provided; a temporal-regress argument must establish its own premise about successive origination. Neither question can be settled by substituting the other's conclusion. [Causal foundations, §4; section 2 above][causal-background]

Ibn Taymiyya's discussion of time in *Minhāj al-sunna al-nabawiyya* makes a relevant distinction explicit. Time as a measure of motion is not confined to one particular motion such as that of the sun. His account connects continuing voluntary speech and activity with the times in which particular products arise, while maintaining that each particular product is originated. Continuing activity and an individually beginningless product are consequently not interchangeable claims in this passage. This gives a positive textual reason to keep his account distinct from an argument requiring a first member of every succession. It is a source-specific doctrinal distinction, not a new cosmological observation or a proof that every proposed beginningless history is possible. [*Minhāj*, discussion of time, motion and particular products][minhaj-time]

A shared definition of time, or a citation of a reported consensus, cannot by itself identify two authors' complete arguments about the past. The scope of the definition and the alleged agreement both require independent support. No universal theological consensus is assumed here. A proposed beginning of the observed cosmos must likewise be distinguished from a beginning of all productive reality; establishing either requires evidence appropriate to that target. The present argument does not import unverified physical or comparative-scriptural premises to cross that boundary. [Causal foundations, §4; contextual reconciliation][causal-background] [Causal context and explanatory scope][causal-context]

## 3. From an original resource to its actual bearer

The retained formal interface separates a **target**, a **resource**, and a **bearer**. A resource is intended to be a concrete intrinsic instantiation in the same existential respect as the receipt question. It is not an arbitrary true proposition or an unrestricted explanatory fact. The following notation makes the existing conditional reasoning explicit. [Original resource/bearer interface][s10]

| Notation | Meaning in the declared interpretation |
|---|---|
| $`a`$ | The actual world |
| $`E(w,g)`$ | Bearer g exists at world w |
| $`D_T(t)`$ | Target t is received |
| $`A_R(r)`$, $`D_R(r)`$ | Resource r is actual, and resource r is received |
| $`I(g,r)`$ | Resource r is intrinsic to bearer g |
| $`V(r,t)`$ | Resource r is relevant to target t |
| $`D_B(w,g)`$ | g is wholly received at w in the specified existential respect |

The interface states three relevant premises:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\text{Completion:}\quad
&\forall t\,[D_T(t)\Rightarrow
  \exists r\,(V(r,t)\land A_R(r)\land\neg D_R(r))],\\
\text{Realisation:}\quad
&\forall r\,[A_R(r)\Rightarrow
  \exists g\,(E(a,g)\land I(g,r))],\\
\text{Support transport:}\quad
&\forall g,r\,[E(a,g)\land I(g,r)\land D_B(a,g)
  \Rightarrow D_R(r)].
\end{aligned}
```

**Conditional bearer result.** Given a received target t and these premises, there are a resource r and a bearer g such that r is actual, unreceived, and relevant to t; r is intrinsic to g; and g actually exists without whole receipt in the specified respect. [Interface, `original_bearer_of_completion`][s10]

**Proof.** Completion applied to the actual received target supplies r with $`V(r,t)`$, $`A_R(r)`$, and $`\neg D_R(r)`$. Realisation supplies g with $`E(a,g)`$ and $`I(g,r)`$. Suppose $`D_B(a,g)`$. Support transport would then yield $`D_R(r)`$, contradicting the resource's unreceived status. Hence $`\neg D_B(a,g)`$. The same t, r, and g satisfy the complete witness statement. This is the reasoning implemented by `original_bearer_of_resource` followed by `original_bearer_of_completion`. [Original resource/bearer interface][s10]

The proof uses no modal necessity premise. It also does not infer a bearer merely from the truth of a sentence: Realisation concerns the stated actual resource domain. Its support transport is a claim about receipt in the same existential respect, not about mere concomitance. The proof establishes the conditional inference; the argument for applying its premises to actual reality remains philosophical. In the field application, Completion receives its warrant from global productive adequacy, while the concrete-resource and receipt interpretations must be maintained through Realisation and transport. [Productive totality, §5; interface opening and definitions][s4] [Original resource/bearer interface][s10]

The intended breadth of whole-existence receipt matters. A real contribution by a dependent reality is compatible with its needing an external existential contribution. Receipt must not be defined to require total passivity and then negated to produce a spurious conclusion of complete independence. Conversely, intrinsic qualification or necessary co-obtaining is not automatically foreign existential receipt. The later application check explicitly preserves both points. [Restored result and operation decision, §2C][s7]

For the philosophical continuation, retain this actual g. The next question is whether its existential character warrants necessity. The witness is not replaced by the fruit, a proposition, or a different original selected for each alternative. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§1, 7][s1]

## 4. The strongest collective alternative

A serious objection to global productive adequacy need not offer another all-received causal chain. The inherited comparison considers a stronger qualitative proposal: an autonomous obtaining basis explains a structure collectively, and existential laws determine the dependent history within it. All concrete productive resources in that history might remain received. The proposal challenges whether complete existential explanation must include an original productive resource in the specified sense. Its explanatory ambition should be taken seriously before assessing whether it reaches that conclusion. [Productive totality, §§6–8][s4]

The proposal has positive arguments behind its separate components. Dasgupta's 2009 qualitative-first account appeals to symmetry and ontological economy: a fundamental description in terms of universals and obtaining states can avoid treating primitive material individuals as additional unexplained structure, while allowing derivative individual discourse. That is an argument about material ontology, not an elimination of every entity, bearer or possible immaterial source. The 2014 argument for collective grounding appeals abductively to coordinated discourse and actual linguistic judgments: some facts are more adequately explained together than by assigning a separate fundamental explanation to each. This is substantive evidence for an explanatory form, beyond the mere availability of plural syntax. It does not by itself establish the proposed qualitative ontology or account for the obtaining basis's own actuality. [Productive totality, §§6–7][s4] [Dasgupta 2009][d09] [Dasgupta 2014][d14]

The 2016 autonomy proposal distinguishes an autonomous ground from an unexplained fact that remains apt for further grounding. Its qualitative existence-grounding alternatives therefore deserve separate assessment: autonomy is meant to answer an explanatory demand, not simply to label a brute stopping point. The inherited reading does not, however, supply an actual qualitative essence that covers this complete productive field. The law-relative alternative is likewise strongest when its laws have existential content. Removing that content to make nonexistence easy would weaken the objection. The unresolved question is the laws' warranted existential authority and coverage of the resources and bearers at issue. The underlying Billon paper was not completely available to the inherited inquiry; this exposition does not fill that evidential gap or claim a full-paper adjudication. [Productive totality, §6][s4] [Dasgupta 2016][d16] [Law-relative proposal and access boundary][b23]

These arguments support taking qualitative ontology, collective explanation and autonomy seriously at their respective scopes. Their conjunction—an autonomous qualitative basis with existential laws covering the whole received productive field while excluding every original productive resource—is a proposed synthesis, not a result jointly established by those sources. The positive evidence must be preserved without transferring the warrant of each component to that stronger conjunction. [Productive totality, §§6–7][s4]

Three cases need different treatment. First, if the proposed basis Q is simply the contingent obtaining arrangement of the received field, collective explanatory form has not yet established Q's adequacy for its own obtaining. An account may correctly explain why the relevant explanandum is collective while leaving the actuality of the obtaining basis itself unprovided. Calling Q fundamental relative to individualistic descriptions does not by itself establish autonomy in the existential respect under discussion. The objection here does not require reducing every collective explanation to a list of separate explanations of primitive individuals. [Productive totality, §6][s4]

Second, suppose Q is independently warranted as intrinsically adequate and autonomous. It would then supply an original explanatory basis in a broader sense. The dispute is whether it also supplies, or must coexist with, original productive actuality. The defender's reason is that an account of received production must cover its actual productive prerequisites. The rival replies that autonomous noncausal grounding can settle actuality while all local production occurs within the grounded field. An additional original productive stock would then be unnecessary. The positive productive judgment bears the burden here; changing the word “ground” into “resource” would not settle it. [Productive totality, §§6, 8][s4]

An alternative adequate description also does not automatically establish that original productive resources are absent. Noncausal and productive accounts might coexist. To rebut the specific Completion claim for F, a proposed case must warrant both the actual received target and the relevant absence of an actual unreceived concrete productive resource. To undercut the inference, it can instead give an independent reason why the productive-adequacy judgment is unreliable or inapplicable. An undercutter need not prove the opposite conclusion or describe an entire alternative cosmos. Its reason must nevertheless reach the target and explanatory sense actually used. [Productive totality, §7][s4]

Third, a qualitative-first proposal could allow an actual independent concrete field with an intrinsic unreceived productive resource. Under an established intrinsic relation and the same receipt interpretation, that field could qualify as a bearer in the broad interface. It would then satisfy the relevant original-resource commitment rather than refute it. The interface does not require a primitive material individual or a personal agent. A true assertion about a field, the content of that assertion, an obtaining state, and a concrete field bearing resources remain different candidates; their substitution requires argument. [Productive totality, §6][s4]

The comparison leaves a precise result. Collective or qualitative explanation has positive philosophical significance, but it does not by itself entail the denial of original productive provision. The source argument supplies a substantive reason for global productive adequacy while leaving its exhaustive application across rival ontologies open to appropriate criticism. The current foundational record retains the affirmative, defeasible judgment; the existence of that applicability question does not itself count as a new defeater. Nor may the positive side insulate itself by defining every adequate ground to be the kind of productive resource it seeks. [Productive totality, §§7–8; current claim FND-004][s4] [Foundational claims][s8]

## 5. From complete independence to intrinsic adequacy

Having obtained the actual original g under the declared meanings, distinguish three claims: g exists and contributes an original resource; g requires no external contribution for its own existence in the relevant respect; and g's own concrete reality is completely adequate for that existence. The first claim does not entail the third merely because g produces effects. The absence of an incoming edge in a representation does not establish the second if the representation omits an existential requisite. And a negated receipt predicate must not be silently strengthened into complete independence when it excludes only some especially demanding kind of dependence. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §1][s1]

The argument connects complete independence to positive self-adequacy through the judgment that bare actuality cannot discharge existential insufficiency. If a concrete reality is not adequate through itself, saying that it nevertheless obtains does not provide what the account claims to explain. Where no external existential contribution is required, the positive alternative is adequacy through the reality itself. The terminus is thereby given a different existential character from a derivative supplier, rather than exempted from a principle that everything must have an efficient producer. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§1, 5–6][s1]

Self-adequacy is not an operation by which g produces itself. An exercise belongs to an actual reality, whereas intrinsic adequacy concerns why the bearer needs no such production or outside provision. Nor does a distinction in thought between essence and existence establish two externally separate items, one manufacturing the other. The source's concrete essence/existence identity clarifies the ontology, but does not prove necessity by itself: the same identity is also attributed to creatures. Positive existential sufficiency must do the additional work. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §2; productive totality, §2][s1] [Productive totality][s4]

The distinction between evidence and existential provision is equally important. Actual g can be the epistemic starting point from which its existential character is investigated. That does not make the reasoning circular. Circularity would arise if g's otherwise unaccounted actualization were used as the complete existential provision of that same actualization. The argument does not infer an instance from a mentally defined necessary nature. It begins with an independently established actual bearer and argues for the character its complete independence requires. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§2–3; bounded inference review][s1] [Bounded inference review][s2]

## 6. Why two familiar explanations do not answer this target

### 6.1 Conditional intrinsic content

Consider the account: if a nature is instantiated, its bearer exists and possesses certain powers. The account may be true and may explain the consequences of instantiation. It does not establish the antecedent obtaining. An explanation of what an existing original can produce therefore differs from an account of that original's own existential adequacy. Naming the nature fundamental, beginningless, concrete, or unreceived does not change the conditional structure. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §3][s1]

The criticism concerns an account's scope. It does not infer that reality lacks an adequate intrinsic basis from our failure to describe it completely. Additional intrinsic content may repair the account while preserving complete independence. Only if the conditional proposal is asserted to exhaust the intrinsic basis does its remaining unprovided obtaining conflict with the claim of complete intrinsic adequacy. At that point an outside existential contribution would relinquish independence, while leaving the obtaining unprovided would reject the positive adequacy judgment. “Further” content cannot simply be treated as “foreign” provision. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §3; review, initial finding 3 and resolution][s1] [Bounded inference review][s2]

The resulting knowledge claim is limited but substantive. The argument need not provide an exhaustive real definition of g to infer that its sufficiency for being is not conditional on an otherwise unaccounted realization of that same reality. Its reasons are the actual bearer, complete independence at the intended scope, and the defended existential judgment. A label such as “necessary nature” would supply none of those reasons on its own. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §3][s1]

### 6.2 A nonnecessitating productive exercise

A distinct effect can be explained by a producer whose standing powers do not make that effect necessary. Why could g's existence not likewise be a contingent manifestation of its own power? The analogy trades on a difference in target. When g explains an effect e, g is available as productive reality independently of e's obtaining. When the proposed effect is g's entire original actuality, the account instead relies on an exercise by the very bearer whose availability it purports to supply. A timeless exercise still belongs to an actual bearer; simultaneity does not repair that explanatory order. Maintenance of an already actual subject also differs from provision of its whole original being. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §4][s1]

This objection is restricted to an exercise invoked as productive provision by its bearer. Mere co-occurrence does not establish productive priority. A proposal identifying a bearer with concrete activity does not automatically posit a separately available subject which subsequently produces itself. A nonproductive constitutive account must be assessed for its own complete existential adequacy. The failure of the agent–act analogy does not itself refute every account in that category. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §4; review, initial finding 2 and resolution][s1] [Bounded inference review][s2]

The positive lesson is that the original's self-adequacy cannot be explained as merely one exercise of an already available subject. The analysis makes the target more exact. It does not, without a further judgment, turn all adequate explanations into necessitating explanations. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§4, 7][s1]

## 7. The modal judgment

The further judgment is explicit: **complete intrinsic adequacy for this bearer's own existence excludes a genuine possibility of that bearer's nonbeing**. The preceding arguments clarify and support that judgment; they do not derive its modal force from the impossibility of self-production alone. The final bounded review required this allocation and accepted the revised account after the modal reach was stated both at the opening and in the developed inference. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, opening and §§4–5; review, initial finding 1 and final revision check][s1] [Bounded inference review][s2]

The argument's positive rationale has several connected parts. Mere possibility does not provide actuality. An account called complete must cover its nominated existential target. The same adequacy demand applies to the proposed original as to derivative resources. And the original cannot supply its entire being by exercising a power whose actuality already requires that bearer. An allegedly optional realization left over after a complete intrinsic account would therefore be the very unprovided obtaining that the account excludes. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§4–6][s1]

An objection might say that g possesses complete intrinsic adequacy actually, while an alternative contains neither g nor its adequacy. For ordinary intrinsic properties, the absence of the subject can indeed remove the property. The present claim has a different explanatory target. If “adequacy” becomes available only after g's otherwise unaccounted being is granted, it is conditional upon the very obtaining it was supposed to cover. If it names the full intrinsic existential basis, making that basis optional contests the positive sufficiency judgment itself. The objection identifies a philosophical disagreement; it does not provide a case preserving that judgment while defeating its conclusion. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §4][s1]

The resulting affirmative inference can now be stated as a whole:

1. The received productive argument supplies an actual original g under the warranted target, resource, and bearer meanings.
2. Actual being is not adequately provided by mere possibility or by an otherwise unprovided occurrence.
3. Where no outside existential contribution is required, the bearer is adequate through its own concrete reality.
4. Because the target is g's own obtaining, that adequacy cannot leave a further unprovided instantiation of g or rely on an exercise which already requires g.
5. Complete adequacy in this existential respect excludes the genuine nonbeing of that same bearer. Thus g is necessary through itself.

The existential and modal sufficiency judgments in steps 2–5 articulate the defended philosophical foundation. They are not presented as a new chain of neutral axioms independently derivable from weaker commitments shared by every critic. The warrant claimed is nevertheless affirmative: the distinctions and reasons are taken to support assent, rather than to list an indefinitely empty condition. That assessment remains defeasible and does not entail that every dissenter is irrational or that every asserted intuition is warranted. [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§5–6; bounded inference review; current claim FND-006][s1] [Bounded inference review][s2] [Foundational claims][s8]

The formal consequence is simpler than the philosophical appraisal. Write

```math
N(g)\equiv\forall w\,E(w,g).
```

The retained contingency-need premise is

```math
\forall g\,[E(a,g)\land\neg N(g)\Rightarrow D_B(a,g)].
```

**Conditional necessity result.** For the same actual g obtained in section 3, contingency-need and its established nonreceipt yield $`N(g)`$. **Proof:** if $`\neg N(g)`$, actuality and contingency-need give $`D_B(a,g)`$, contradicting $`\neg D_B(a,g)`$. Hence $`N(g)`$. This expresses the inherited necessity step used by the interface's composition theorem. [Interface, `ContingencyNeed` and `abc_same_witness`; productive totality, §5][s10] [Productive totality][s4]

The proof does not establish its philosophical premise. Worlds must represent genuine alternatives for the same bearer, and whole receipt must express the intended existential dependence. Arbitrarily chosen histories or a weaker dependence predicate do not receive that interpretation merely from the proof's validity. Conversely, re-proving the conditional implication would not strengthen the already developed philosophical warrant. [Direct anti-brute return, “Application to ContingencyNeed”; affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §7][s3] [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument][s1]

## 8. The brute-original challenge

The strongest recorded rival permits an actual, concrete, intrinsically qualified B with unreceived productive powers. B has no external existential requisite, never produced itself, and may have no temporal beginning. It adequately supplies the received effects under discussion. Nevertheless, B could have been absent, and its own obtaining has no sufficient intrinsic or external account. This proposal distinguishes sufficiency for effects from sufficiency for the source's existence. It should not be weakened into production by nothing or efficient self-production. [Direct anti-brute return, “The strongest rival and fair adjudication”][s3]

The point of denial becomes clearer when two senses of existence “through itself” are distinguished:

| Sense | What the rival can accept or deny | The disputed judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Absence of outside existential need | B satisfies this thin description while remaining allegedly contingent. | Does complete independence require positive intrinsic sufficiency and necessity? |
| Positive intrinsic existential sufficiency | B denies both this status and receipt from another. | Is the exclusion of that third combination warranted? |

In the first formulation, division by outside need is exhaustive but does not supply the positive consequence by logic alone. In the second, exhaustiveness already contains the substantive anti-brute commitment. Neither formulation should be used to conceal the premise inside a word. [Direct anti-brute return, “The affirmative argument and its precise commitments”][s3]

The continuous source return reports that the third-option objection appears in the quoted discussion at *Darʾ* 3:287 and is answered at 288–289. The reply treats its exclusion as a basic rational judgment and distinguishes existence through oneself from producing oneself. The surrounding account allows clarification for a doubter instead of demanding that every starting judgment be proved from less evident premises. The finding matters because it shows that this alternative was explicitly addressed; it does not supply an additional deduction from premises the strongest rival retains. Epistemic necessity—classification of a judgment's warrant—must also be distinguished from an existent's necessity through itself. [Direct anti-brute return, “The continuous argument” and “What necessity and selfhood mean here”][s3]

The positive side's advantage is its account of adequate existential provision. An original whose reality is intrinsically sufficient offers a principled terminus for the explanatory demand. A contingent original left unprovided does not supply another sufficient explanation of its own obtaining; it rejects that demand at the terminus. The rival's strongest response is that modal contingency need not represent an unpaid existential requirement. B could be primitive actuality rather than an unrealized potential waiting for a producer. That is the actual philosophical disagreement. [Direct anti-brute return, “The strongest rival and fair adjudication”; affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument, §§6, 8][s3] [Affirmative intrinsic-necessity argument][s1]

Neither a modal table permitting B's absence nor the word “brute” independently warrants the proposed reality's possibility. Equally, repeating “insufficient” does not answer a reasoned objection to the adequacy judgment. A substantive undercutter can challenge the reliability or application of that judgment without constructing an entire competing cosmology. A positive defeating case would have to defend the relevant complete intrinsic account, or its denial, at the same existential target. ## 9. Original productive unity

### 9.1 The target of the unity argument

An actual original is not yet a unique complete original provider. Section 3 establishes a relevant unreceived resource intrinsic to an actual bearer. Section 7 carries that same bearer through the necessity argument. Neither inference establishes that every requisite of every effect belongs to this bearer's original provision. The additional unity argument concerns **complete actual original production of the same numerical effect, in the same state and productive respect**. Standing capacity, an unused alternative, proximate performance, and an explanation of a true proposition are different relations. [Common-source comparison, §§2–4][u-review]

Fix an effect e and the productive respect under consideration. Its full productive account includes the relevant resources, means, receptive conditions and preservation actually involved. An agent's ability to produce an indistinguishable endpoint in another history is not its actual provision of this history. Equally, two distinct intrinsic exercises do not become one act because they contribute to one effect. A shared episode, an intrinsic act, a power and its bearer must remain distinct. This permits genuine received agency: a creature can perform an act using powers whose original productive basis is supplied by another. Common original provision does not make every creaturely act the original's own performance or assertion. [Original-production exclusivity, §§1–2, 5][u-local] [Productive mediation and source perfection][u-field]

The strongest duplicate-production proposal should be stated without weakening it. It alleges two distinct original bearers, each actually and sufficiently producing the same whole effect, neither borrowing completion from the other, with neither idle, partially effective, or merely capable of replacing the other. The contradiction “if A alone produces the whole, B does not also produce it” does not decide this proposal: **exclusive** production is precisely what the proposal denies. Nor does recording both alleged sources in an exhaustive history itself prove that each needed the other. The question is whether two differentiated original contributions can each be the entire original productive content of this same effect. [Productive mediation and source perfection, discussion of primitive double sufficiency][u-field]

### 9.2 Actual efficacy and nonduplication

The source's positive rationale concerns the individuation of productive work. A real additional producer contributes actual efficacy: work, material, an operative capacity, reinforcement, receptivity or preservation. An unused backup does not contribute simply because it could have acted. An additional contribution need not be separately observable, spatially disjoint or numerically measurable. The point is that it has determinate productive content. [Original-production exclusivity, §§2–4][u-local]

Ibn Taymiyya's argument in *Jāmiʿ al-masāʾil*, VI, 94–98, distinguishes the two intrinsic acts and then advances a further claim about their external effects. That second claim must not disappear inside the first. His discussion of cooperation allows contributions to be inseparably mixed; numerical difference in the agents does not by itself yield two separable visible products. The discussion explicitly considers agreement. In *Majmūʿ al-fatāwā*, XX, 175–181, the work performed is compared while relevant operation, will, movement and instruments are held fixed. An apparent unchanged output under an added helper can otherwise reflect a change in the first producer's actual operation. These are arguments for retaining real efficacy in the productive account, not a deduction that every additional act must increase a scalar quantity. [Original-production exclusivity, §§2.1–2.2][u-local] [Primary electronic discussion, XX, 174–184][u-majmu]

The actuality of an effective exercise also matters to the perfection argument. Merely retaining a power does not vindicate every way of exercising it. Conversely, not exercising a power at an unfitting time is not incapacity. The source distinguishes standing qualification, an original mode of agency, an enduring kind of activity, and a particular act at its appropriate occasion. An essential mode may govern every fitting exercise without making each particular exercise eternal or obligatory. It would therefore be invalid to infer reception of the bearer's entire existence from reception in any one state. The stronger conclusion requires the substantive judgment that foreign completion of the relevant original agency conflicts with its essential perfection. [Productive mediation and source perfection, “Four levels of perfection” and the discussion of *Minhāj*][u-field]

The resulting local inference can be isolated exactly. Let q denote the entire external productive content of e in the fixed respect. Let c(A) denote A's actual original contribution to that content. Assume:

1. The alleged contributions are genuine, effective and coequal at the original level.
2. An entire original attribution to A means c(A) = q.
3. Distinct such original contributors have distinct external productive contents in this effect and respect.

**Local exclusivity.** At most one bearer has the entire original attribution under these conditions.

**Proof.** If A and B both have it, then c(A) = q = c(B). Condition 3 excludes A and B being distinct. Thus A = B. □

The third condition is the substantive **local nonduplication judgment**. It is not derived merely from distinct intrinsic acts, different source labels, efficient nonreceipt, or the fact that each act has some positive effect. Even small different contributions contained within equal whole contributions would not establish inequality of those wholes. Disjoint nonzero entire contributions would suffice, but are stronger than the actual nonidentity premise. Nothing in the proof requires a unique atomisation of all causal work. [Original-production exclusivity, §§3–3.1][u-local]

Its positive warrant joins actual efficacy, differentiated productive contribution and the adequacy of entire original provision. Explanatory economy supplements that rationale; absence of a demonstrated rival does not create it. The appraisal remains affirmative and defeasible. A conceptual undercutter may challenge the rationale without constructing an entire rival universe. Conversely, a relation table containing two labelled source arrows does not independently warrant two genuine entire original productions. Such a table can locate the disputed premise without settling its metaphysical truth. [Original-production exclusivity, §§4–7][u-local]

Ibn al-Qayyim's account preserves an important asymmetry. A received agent can make a real partial contribution through power, will, instruments and receptive conditions whose actuality is provided at the original level. One effect can accordingly have original creation and genuine derivative performance. This is compatible with the local exclusion of **coequal entire original** duplication. Neither a general ban on multiple causes nor an identification of cognition with production follows. Origin essentialism also cannot decide the issue unaided: an essentially plural origin is available as a proposal unless single originality has already been justified. [Original-production exclusivity, §§2.4, 5–6][u-local]

The same passage quotes Abū al-Ḥusayn's proposed distinction between two productive relations terminating in one effect, supported by the analogy of one object known by two knowers. Ibn al-Qayyim's respondent rejects that distinction alone as a warrant for two independently complete original productions. The positive alternative is asymmetric inclusion: the creature's actual power is itself originally supplied while remaining a genuine partial cause. This does not derive productive nonduplication from two thin occurrence implications, or deny that knowledge can operate in agency. The disputed causal judgment and its source-supported rationale remain necessary; the two-knowers analogy does not settle the original-level productive relation merely by sharing its terminus. [Original-production exclusivity, §2.4][u-local]

### 9.3 Concentrated capacity and eligibility

The source's argument about concentrating powers must be retained alongside actual efficacy. If compatible powers jointly accomplish some source-neutral work, their concentration in one bearer need not reduce that productive capacity. The argument is about possible possession of the powers, not a temporal merger of two necessary beings. It supplies a positive reason to regard independent productive adequacy as an intelligible excellence. [Consolidation of powers and eligibility, “The argument that must be credited”][u-concentration]

Three conclusions remain distinct:

| Question | What would answer it |
|---|---|
| Can the same external work be accomplished with concentrated powers? | A warranted account of those powers and the work they produce. |
| Can each particular existing contributor acquire that fuller power? | A further claim about that bearer's eligibility; a possible different bearer is insufficient. |
| Would every end valued by the original participants survive? | An account of those ends, including any that require two particular participants. |

The third conclusion is not needed for the first. A good constituted by two agents' participation can disappear while all source-neutral external work remains producible. Nor may the two originals' intrinsic acts be silently classified as created outputs just to make the external product require plurality. On the other side, possible concentration does not by itself show that each named bearer can acquire the concentrated powers while preserving its identity. [Consolidation of powers and eligibility][u-concentration]

That last distinction is a limit of one inference, not a surviving general objection to the source's perfection argument. A comparative argument may disqualify an essentially restricted candidate without requiring that this very candidate could be upgraded. The later appraisal expressly corrects the earlier use of identity-preserving acquireability as a standalone objection. A candidate cannot make every restriction irrelevant merely by declaring the restriction essential to its own kind. Whether the compared excellence is pure, eligible and applicable at the stated scope remains substantive. [Public source-unity judgment, “Present assent and exact reservation”][u-judgment]

The distinction between eligibility and acquireability has a precise source argument. In *Darʾ taʿāruḍ al-ʿaql wa-l-naql*, V, 273–274, Ibn Taymiyya describes a technical use of privation restricted to subjects naturally capable of the corresponding attribute: an inanimate wall is not called blind in that usage. He then gives his own reply. The convention does not settle the substantive comparison. He classifies life, knowledge and power as perfections and maintains that inability to possess them is not an exemption from deficiency; on his comparison, it is a greater limitation than their absence from something capable of them. His argument concerns the qualities and their absence, whether or not the selected technical vocabulary calls that absence death, ignorance or inability. The reported opponent's restriction and the author's positive appraisal must not be conflated. [*Darʾ*, V, 273–274][dar-perfection-and-privation]

An elementary reconstruction shows exactly what the terminology can establish. Suppose a privation predicate is defined by two conditions together: the subject is eligible for an attribute, and it lacks that attribute. An ineligible subject then lacks the privation **as defined**, even though it also lacks the attribute. Therefore the inference from “this privation predicate does not apply” to “the subject possesses the excellence” is invalid. Nor does the definition establish that the subject has no relevant deficiency. That stronger judgment requires a comparison of the subject and the excellence, not merely a rule governing the negative word. Conversely, this logical distinction alone does not prove that every attribute absent from a subject is an excellence it ought to have. Both directions matter: a technical exemption is not a positive vindication, and the failure of that exemption is not a complete theory of perfection.

The affirmative comparison must consequently identify its proper standard. A tool can be adequate for its limited function without cognition; failure to understand is not necessarily a defect **as that kind of tool**. The question here is instead whether an unrestricted original source lacks a real excellence relevant to its proposed adequacy. The source's positive classification of life, knowledge and power supplies that broader comparison, rather than deriving it from the word “original.” The reasons developed in section 12.4 give one substantive application: understanding and owning a fitting reason for effective action can contribute an excellence omitted by an account that records only the beneficial outcome. Merely proposing an entity whose kind excludes that relation does not answer those reasons. A rival can dispute their applicability or the comparative premise, but must address that disagreement rather than treating its own definition as the conclusion.

No identity-preserving transformation is required by this argument. To judge a proposed bearer inadequate for an unrestricted role is different from claiming that this very bearer could acquire every qualification while remaining the same individual. The judgment can exclude a candidate rather than describe its repair. Equally, the positive attribute and its mode of realization must be distinguished: the comparison does not, merely by naming knowledge or power, establish a particular creaturely realization, a complete theory of agency or one numerical original. It also does not show that more activity or greater product quantity is invariably better. Standing qualification, fitting exercise and the appropriateness of an occasion retain the separate roles already stated in sections 9.2 and 12.4. The resulting defense of the comparison preserves the affirmative pure-perfection appraisal while exposing the premise that a serious rival must actually contest.

A rival already credited with standing solo capacity does not need that capacity added by concentration. Its live question is whether its **actual joint manifestation** constitutes foreign completion of appropriate original efficacy. Conversely, an internally completed intention or a valued cooperative end does not establish entire actual original provision. The strongest rival retains distinct intrinsic powers and exercises, narrow efficient nonreceipt, primitive cognition and a conditional account of coordinated means. These are real credits. They do not yet establish complete original perfection, unrestricted sovereignty or the particular wisdom of every proposed undertaking. [Consolidation of powers and eligibility][u-concentration] [Public source-unity judgment][u-judgment]

## 10. From actual provision to a common connected source

### 10.1 Productive mediation and the local adequacy norm

Suppose A originally supplies a resource actually employed by a derived agent H in producing an effect. H's genuine agency does not turn that received resource into a new original stock. A therefore belongs in the effect's original productive account. This attribution concerns actual productive ancestry, not mere temporal priority, knowledge, proximity or covariance. It does not make H's utterance an assertion owned by A. [Productive mediation and source perfection, “Three attributions”][u-field]

Now suppose another original B supplies a requisite that A's actual undertaking uses. A's unused capacity to supply an alternative requisite does not provide the one actually used. Nor does an intermediary erase its original provenance. Calling a foreign productive input an “occasion” cannot remove its work; calling every explanatory condition a cause cannot establish productive donation either. Earlier acts, their resulting situations and later acts must remain distinct occurrences, even when the same bearer is involved. Collapsing them into a single bearer-node can manufacture a cycle. [Productive mediation and source perfection, “Shared products and later modification”][u-field]

The further normative premise is that complete unborrowed provision is the fitting mode of a qualified original's actual productive role. A transformation illustrates its force and its limit: A may supply the whole intrinsic transformative exercise while B supplies the material. That gives a correct causal account of incomplete original coverage. To classify A's chosen role as defective requires the additional adequacy norm. The word “undertaking” must not prejudge that classification by silently assigning the whole composite task to A. The retained positive rationale appeals to the excellence of actual, self-sufficient original efficacy. The retained dissent asks why a freely chosen less comprehensive role could not be fitting. Both must be represented. [Productive mediation and source perfection, “Independence and the perfection of actual exercise” and “The substantive dissent”][u-field]

### 10.2 An explicit propagation theorem

The conditional proof uses three different domains: sources, actual occurrences, and productive contributions. Let F be a selected field of occurrences. Write R(e,c) when contribution c is in the inventory of actual requisites of e, and O(s,c) when s entirely originally supplies c. Define:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Complete}(s,e)&\iff \forall c\,[R(e,c)\Rightarrow O(s,c)],\\
\mathrm{Participates}(s,e)&\iff \exists c\,[R(e,c)\land O(s,c)],\\
\mathrm{Overlap}(e,f)&\iff \exists c\,[R(e,c)\land R(f,c)].
\end{aligned}
```

The occurrence and contribution inventory must be interpreted independently. “Every supplied operative contribution is listed” cannot establish that the contribution domain contains all relevant reality. The applied account needs actual occurrence, sound and exhaustive requisites, the correct original level, the relevant productive history and all operative branches. These are interpretation obligations, not consequences of the graph. [Anchored-source interface and premise map][u-anchor] [Interpretation and premise map][u-premises]

Fix a qualified source g. Distinguish **classification** of a relevant incomplete role as defective from **conformance** of g's actual relevant roles to that norm. If every incomplete relevant role would be defective and no such role of g is defective, every relevant role is complete. This implication is a contradiction argument; it supplies no independent philosophical warrant for either premise. Further require:

- Represented participation in an admitted occurrence is a real original contribution there.
- Real original participation by g makes the adequacy norm applicable to g in that occurrence.
- g actually originally participates at an anchor a in F.
- Every occurrence in F can be reached from a by a finite sequence of overlaps within F.

**Coverage theorem.** Under those assumptions, g completely originally supplies every occurrence in F. Causal nonduplication is not required for this coverage conclusion.

**Proof.** The actual anchor and role-applicability premise make g's role at a relevant, hence complete. Suppose g is complete at e and e overlaps f through c. Then O(g,c), so g has represented participation at f. Use-soundness makes this real original participation, role-applicability makes it relevant, and adequacy of the role makes g complete at f. Induction along the finite overlap path from a proves completeness at every nominated occurrence. The path of length zero retains the anchor conclusion. No bound on the number of occurrences, temporal beginning, well-founded productive ancestry or qualification of rival sources is used. □ [Anchored-source interface, coverage proofs][u-anchor]

Overlap is symmetric incidence, not reverse efficient causation. For example, two earlier contributions can both be requisites of a later mixed occurrence; the overlap path connects the earlier accounts through that occurrence without asserting that the later occurrence caused its past. Conversely, an arbitrary drawn edge or common subject matter is not a productive overlap. [Anchored-source interpretation and review][u-anchor-guide] [Independent bridge review][u-anchor-review]

**Uniqueness extension.** Assume local nonduplication for every requisite in F: any two sources entirely originally supplying the same such c are identical. Every represented original participant in any occurrence of F then equals g.

**Proof.** If s participates through c, then O(s,c). Coverage gives O(g,c) for this same c. Nonduplication gives s = g. □

To extend this conclusion to **every actual original participant**, each must have a represented requisite witness. Call this visibility. Without it, an actual participant can be omitted from the inventory and evade the proof. The conclusion is about participation in F; an idle putative source or a source active only outside F is not identified by it. Global nonduplication is stronger than needed: only the nominated field's requisites enter the proof. [Anchored-source interface, represented and actual rival proofs][u-anchor]

There is a separate vacuity issue for a source merely labelled complete. If an occurrence has no listed requisite, every source satisfies its completeness predicate. To prove uniqueness of such complete providers, the occurrence must have at least one requisite. A visible actual anchor supplies one; every subsequent overlap supplies one at the next occurrence. Therefore all occurrences in the connected field have nonempty inventories. Two complete providers then share an actually listed c and are identical by nonduplication. For this narrower uniqueness result, a positive anchor inventory is sufficient; full visibility is one way of establishing it, not an irreducible additional requirement. [Anchored-source interface, positive-inventory proofs][u-anchor] [Independent bridge review][u-anchor-review]

The theorem also exposes how much the local norm does. With the fixed soundness, role, anchor and connectivity premises, satisfaction of the relevant completeness norm is equivalent to global coverage: the forward implication is the theorem, and the reverse implication is immediate by restriction to relevant roles. A participation-to-completeness formulation similarly becomes equivalent to coverage when the anchor has represented participation. This is useful transparency. It is not an independent proof of the norm or a reason to call the interpretation arbitrary. The philosophical arguments in sections 9–10.1 must do that work. [Anchored-source interface, equivalence proofs][u-anchor]

### 10.3 An abstract gluing result and its counterexamples

A shorter set formulation is helpful when complete local providers have already been established. For each target e in a nonempty field E, let U(e) be the set of sources completely adequate for e, with the same bearer identity and productive respect throughout. Suppose every U(e) is a singleton. Suppose a graph connects E by finite undirected paths and its used edges satisfy:

```math
U(e)\cap U(f)\ne\varnothing.
```

**Gluing theorem.** There is one source g such that U(e) = {g} for every e in E.

**Proof.** Choose an anchor e₀ and its unique provider g. Two singleton sets with a common member are equal. Equality therefore propagates along every finite path from e₀. All U(e) equal {g}. Existence comes from the nonempty anchor and its singleton; uniqueness follows at that anchor. No finite bound on E or choice of an infinite family of providers is needed. □ [Common-source comparison, §1][u-review]

This result must not replace its own hypotheses with weaker ones. Local **at-most-one** production does not supply an inhabitant of U(e). A relevant original resource is not already a complete provider. Ordinary causal interaction is not already overlap of complete provider sets. Topological connectedness under an unrelated topology is not graph connectivity. With several graph components, the proof gives a source per component, without deciding whether those sources agree. With E empty, all sources are vacuously globally adequate, so uniqueness can fail. A one-target field needs local existence and uniqueness but no edge. [Common-source comparison, §§1–4][u-review]

Without the singleton premise, even pairwise overlap does not suffice: {a,b}, {b,c} and {a,c} have nonempty pairwise intersections but no common member. A different sufficient theorem uses a compact source space with closed adequacy sets satisfying the **finite intersection property**. If their total intersection were empty, their open complements would cover the compact space; a finite subcover would contradict that property. This proves existence, not uniqueness. No philosophical application has established the required topology or compactness merely by using this theorem. [Common-source comparison, §1][u-review]

Under singletonhood, declaring a connected relation of “having the same provider” already amounts to declaring all providers equal. Independently evidenced sparse productive overlap can provide a useful argument; defining an agreement relation after the desired conclusion cannot provide new unity warrant. The gluing result is an exposition of an inherited conditional route, not a new discovery of the underlying philosophy. [Common-source comparison, §§1, 3–4][u-review]

### 10.4 The affirmative judgment and its exact scope

The current public appraisal affirms one complete original source of the relevant actual productively connected created order at a defeasible research standard. Its reasons combine original actuality, necessity and independence with actual adequate efficacy, the original mode's perfection, faithful mediation and local nonduplication. This is stronger than an unspecified preference. It is not a demonstration of the impossibility of every plural-original reality. The conditional theorems show how particular premises support coverage and identity; they neither create the cumulative warrant nor retract it because some premises remain philosophically disputed. [Public source-unity judgment][u-judgment] [Common-source comparison, §§3–4][u-review]

The surviving reservation concerns unrestricted application of the efficacy norm to every proposed primitive, voluntarily joint original manifestation. The strongest proposed rival has genuine restricted explanatory and agency credits, but no independently warranted full original-perfection certificate. Failure to exhibit such a certificate is not itself a proof of unity. A well-directed undercutter need not provide a complete competing cosmos. A disconnected active original, an idle putative original, all possible realities and universal mastery over every possible peer require distinct arguments. A result for the nominated actual field should neither be erased nor extended to them silently. [Public source-unity judgment][u-judgment] [Productive mediation and source perfection, scope of the field conclusion][u-field]

### 10.5 Coherent assignments across locally indexed worlds

The preceding arguments keep one source fixed while following its actual productive contributions. A different question arises when proposed worlds are described in separate local coordinates: which local entries, if any, can consistently represent one selected bearer? Equal labels do not establish that correspondence, and a coherent correspondence does not by itself establish numerical identity. The finite construction below makes the missing mathematical structure explicit. It concerns a declared alignment of descriptions; the alignment graph is neither a causal graph nor the accessibility relation of a modal logic. [Finite modal identity transport, §§2–3][transport-proof]

Let $`W`$ be a finite nonempty set of worlds with a nominated actual world $`a`$. Let $`\Gamma=(W,\mathcal E)`$ be a connected undirected graph. Each world has a finite outer domain $`D_w`$ of indices. For every oriented edge $`v\to w`$, supply a bijection $`g_{vw}:D_v\to D_w`$, with $`g_{wv}=g_{vw}^{-1}`$. Choose one orientation of each undirected edge and denote that set by $`\mathcal E^+`$. Connectedness makes the outer-domain cardinality $`n`$ common, possibly zero. An index available in the outer domain need not represent something existing at that world. Existence $`E_w\subseteq D_w`$, whole-existence reception $`C_w\subseteq D_w`$, and other predicates are separately specified. No equation such as $`g_{vw}(E_v)=E_w`$ is assumed: that would already impose persistence of existence.

For arbitrary acceptance sets $`A_w\subseteq D_w`$, an accepted section is a tuple $`s=(s_w)_{w\in W}`$ satisfying both

```math
s_w\in A_w,
\qquad s_w=g_{vw}(s_v)\quad\text{on every oriented edge}.
```

The choice $`A_w=E_w`$ asks for an everywhere-existing compatible assignment; $`A_w=E_w\setminus C_w`$ also requires nonreceipt. These are filters on a correspondence, not ingredients that define identity into existence.

Choose a spanning tree rooted at $`a`$. Write $`t_w:D_a\to D_w`$ for the composite along its unique root-to-$`w`$ path, with $`t_a`$ the identity. For any oriented edge define the permutation of the root domain

```math
H_{vw}=t_w^{-1}\circ g_{vw}\circ t_v.
```

It follows the tree to $`v`$, crosses that edge, and returns along the tree from $`w`$. Tree edges give identity permutations. The permutations from the remaining edges, in one orientation each, generate all the closed-walk constraints. Their generated group is the rooted *holonomy group*. Define

```math
K=\bigcap_{(v,w)\in\mathcal E^+}\{b\in D_a:H_{vw}(b)=b\},
\qquad
F=K\cap\bigcap_{w\in W}t_w^{-1}(A_w).
```

**Section theorem.** Evaluation at $`a`$ gives a bijection between accepted sections and $`F`$. Its inverse is $`b\mapsto(t_w(b))_{w\in W}`$.

**Proof.** The tree-edge equations force every section to satisfy $`s_w=t_w(s_a)`$. On a remaining edge the section equation becomes $`t_w(s_a)=g_{vw}(t_v(s_a))`$, or $`H_{vw}(s_a)=s_a`$. Its acceptance conditions are exactly $`s_a\in t_w^{-1}(A_w)`$. Hence $`s_a\in F`$. Conversely, a root value in $`F`$ gives a tuple satisfying every edge equation and every acceptance condition. Its actual-world component is the original root value, so the two maps are inverse. □

This also gives an exact relation-tensor calculation. Let $`\delta(x,y)`$ be one when $`x=y`$ and zero otherwise. Then the Boolean expression

```math
\bigvee_{s\in\prod_{w\in W}D_w}
\left(
 \bigwedge_{w\in W}\mathbf 1_{A_w}(s_w)
 \ \land\!
 \bigwedge_{(v,w)\in\mathcal E^+}\delta(s_w,g_{vw}(s_v))
\right)
```

is true exactly when $`F`$ is nonempty. Replacing disjunction and conjunction by natural-number addition and multiplication counts the accepted sections and therefore gives $`|F|`$. Each full tuple contributes once; independently choosing a witness at each world before enforcing the shared edge bindings would compute a different condition. For $`n\ge 1`$, explicit permutation tables, inverses, tree transports and filters can be constructed and checked in $`O(n(|W|+|\mathcal E|))`$ operations. Enumerating the full product of domains is an alternative specification, not that efficient algorithm. [Finite modal identity transport, Theorem 1][transport-proof]

Although the chosen tree changes the displayed transports and generators, it changes neither the sections nor their actual-world values. Applying the theorem without filters shows that $`K`$ itself is tree-independent at the fixed root. On $`K`$, transport to a world is independent of the path; it need not be path-independent on every other index. The argument includes a one-world graph. If the common outer domain is empty, the nonempty world set ensures that there is no section or existential witness.

### 10.6 Full charts, selected points, and the singleton correction

Existence of one accepted section does not in general supply a full system of global coordinates. The following conditions are equivalent:

1. Every rooted cycle permutation is the identity on all of $`D_a`$.
2. A common set $`B`$ has bijective charts $`u_w:B\to D_w`$ with $`g_{vw}=u_w\circ u_v^{-1}`$ on each edge.
3. There are injective maps $`j_w:D_w\to B`$ with $`j_w\circ g_{vw}=j_v`$, where $`B`$ is their common image.
4. Every root index extends to exactly one section, without acceptance filters.

**Proof.** If all cycle permutations are identity, choose $`B=D_a`$ and $`u_w=t_w`$; the definition of $`H_{vw}`$ gives the edge factorization. Conversely, chart factors cancel around a loop, leaving identity. Inverse charts give the injective maps in the third condition. Conversely, transporting an index around a rooted loop gives $`j_a\circ H=j_a`$, and injectivity forces $`H`$ to be identity. Edge compatibility and connectedness make the images of all $`j_w`$ equal. Finally, the section theorem without filters identifies the fourth condition with $`K=D_a`$, which is the first. □ [Finite modal identity transport, Theorem 2][transport-proof]

The full-chart criterion is the finite permutation form of the standard synchronization result: full group-valued synchronization is equivalent to trivial holonomy. Gao, Brodzki and Mukherjee give that criterion and an elementary propagation argument in §2.1, Corollary 2.1. The point-valued question here is weaker: a nontrivial group action can fix one index without fixing all indices. No geometric classification, noisy estimation result or new discovery of synchronization is being claimed. [Gao, Brodzki and Mukherjee][gao-synchronization]

For example, take a triangle whose domains are $`\{0,1,2\}`$, whose two tree maps are identity, and whose remaining edge swaps $`0`$ and $`1`$ while fixing $`2`$. There is exactly one section, the constant $`2`$ assignment. There is no full chart system respecting those edge maps. A loop interpreted as literal, faithful identity for every local item could not identify two distinct indices in one domain. The same loop can consistently describe a proposed counterpart matching or a faulty identification; the mathematical data alone do not choose among these interpretations.

Separate loop witnesses are also insufficient. Partition six labels into pairs $`A,B,C`$. Let $`h_1`$ fix $`A`$ and swap within $`B,C`$; let $`h_2`$ fix $`B`$ and swap within $`A,C`$. They commute, and their product fixes $`C`$ while swapping within $`A,B`$. The four group elements have respectively six, two, two and two fixed labels, but no label is fixed by all four. Two triangles sharing the root realize the two generators with identity tree maps. Thus “each loop fixes some point” does not imply “some point is fixed by every loop.” The section theorem needs the latter, with the same point retained throughout. [Finite modal identity transport, §4][transport-proof]

There is, however, a useful simplification under stronger local hypotheses. Suppose each acceptance set is a singleton, $`A_w=\{q_w\}`$, and each edge preserves acceptance: $`g_{vw}(A_v)\subseteq A_w`$. Then necessarily $`g_{vw}(q_v)=q_w`$. The tuple $`(q_w)`$ is the unique accepted section, and every rooted cycle fixes $`q_a`$ by composing these equations. A further independent selected-point holonomy assumption is unnecessary. A triangular swap with no fixed accepted point can illustrate failure when eligible fibres have several members, or when preservation fails; it cannot refute this singleton conclusion while retaining its hypotheses. This corrects the dependency interpretation of the earlier unity proposal, not the underlying graph theorem. It still establishes only a section of the declared correspondence. [Singleton-fibre correction][singleton-correction] [Unity and identity proposal, §§3–5][unity-proposal]

Passive relabelling preserves all these conclusions. For bijections $`\pi_w:D_w\to D'_w`$, put

```math
g'_{vw}=\pi_w\circ g_{vw}\circ\pi_v^{-1},
\qquad A'_w=\pi_w(A_w),
\qquad t'_w=\pi_w\circ t_w\circ\pi_a^{-1}.
```

Adjacent inverse factors cancel, giving $`H'_{vw}=\pi_a\circ H_{vw}\circ\pi_a^{-1}`$ and $`F'=\pi_a(F)`$. Coordinatewise relabelling sends each accepted section bijectively to a new one, preserving both the Boolean answer and the count. Each world's other relations must be relabelled in every argument as well. This operation does not identify the valuations at different worlds: their existence and reception predicates may still differ arbitrarily. [Finite modal identity transport, gauge covariance][transport-proof]

### 10.7 An invariant average need not identify any individual

Linear and Boolean representations need an additional distinction. Give each local index $`x`$ a basis vector $`e_x`$, over either the real numbers or the Boolean semiring, and extend an edge permutation by $`P_{vw}e_x=e_{g_{vw}(x)}`$. A parallel family satisfies $`z_w=P_{vw}z_v`$. On a two-label triangle with a swap loop there is no point section, but $`e_0+e_1`$ is a nonzero parallel vector. Over the reals, $`(e_0+e_1)/2`$ is parallel and has coefficients summing to one. Even if every local existence filter accepts both labels, neither nonzeroness nor this normalization identifies a consistent individual. More generally, real vectors invariant under a permutation group are precisely those whose coefficients are constant on each orbit: invariance gives equality along orbit steps, and orbitwise constancy gives invariance. A nonempty finite orbit therefore supplies an invariant sum even when it contains no fixed point. [Finite modal identity transport, §4][transport-proof]

Relative to the declared basis, define the linear copy map and augmentation by

```math
\Delta_w(e_x)=e_x\otimes e_x,
\qquad \varepsilon_w(e_x)=1.
```

**Point criterion.** Over $`\mathbb R`$ or the Boolean semiring, the equations $`\Delta_w z=z\otimes z`$ and $`\varepsilon_w z=1`$ hold exactly when $`z=e_x`$ for one local index $`x`$.

**Proof.** Write $`z=\sum_x c_xe_x`$. Comparing the coefficient of $`e_x\otimes e_y`$ for $`x\ne y`$ gives $`c_xc_y=0`$; comparing a diagonal coefficient gives $`c_x=c_x^2`$. Over the reals, an idempotent coefficient is zero or one. The off-diagonal equations leave at most one coefficient equal to one, and augmentation leaves exactly one. Over the Boolean semiring the off-diagonal equations likewise forbid two supported indices, while augmentation requires nonempty support. Conversely, each basis vector satisfies both equations. The augmentation excludes zero, including when the domain is empty. □

Let $`M_{A_w}`$ be the diagonal map retaining exactly the coordinates in $`A_w`$. Adding $`M_{A_w}z_w=z_w`$ to parallelism and the point criterion gives precisely the accepted sections above: once $`z_w=e_{s_w}`$, the filter is membership and parallelism is the edge equation. The construction uses a standard basis-copy structure; Coecke, Pavlovic and Vicary describe the chosen-basis copy and deletion maps and the recovery of copyable basis elements. The elementary proof here separately establishes the real and Boolean cases and does not import their general Hilbert-space classification or a physical interpretation. [Coecke, Pavlovic and Vicary, introduction and §§2–3][coecke-bases]

The coefficient assumptions are substantive. Over $`\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z`$, the vector $`3e_0+4e_1`$ is normalized and copyable: $`3^2=3`$, $`4^2=4`$, $`3\cdot4=0`$ and $`3+4=1`$ modulo six. It is not one basis vector. The criterion is therefore not asserted for every ring. Nor is it independent of the declared basis. Permutations preserve copying and augmentation, since both sides send $`e_x`$ to the corresponding relabelled basis expression. Under a general invertible linear change, one must transport the copy map, augmentation, filters and interpretation together. Keeping an old “one nonzero coordinate” reading after an arbitrary basis change is not faithful. Copying an index is an algebraic operation on a representation; it neither creates another individual nor makes every distributed system a single bearer. [Finite modal identity transport, Theorem 3 and coefficient control][transport-proof]

### 10.8 What full charts preserve in a modal description

Assume full charts $`u_w:B\to D_w`$, and separately supply the interpretation that $`u_w(b)`$ represents the same bearer $`b`$ at every world, including worlds where it does not exist. Define global-coordinate predicates and relations by

```math
\begin{aligned}
E(w,b)&\iff E_w(u_w(b)),\\
C(w,b)&\iff C_w(u_w(b)),\\
\mathrm{Dep}(w,c,b)&\iff \mathrm{Dep}_w(u_w(c),u_w(b)).
\end{aligned}
```

Here $`\mathrm{Dep}_w(y,x)`$ is a specified whole-existence reception relation, not a name for every interaction. Let $`R`$ and $`T`$ be finite resource and target sets, distinct from the bearer domain. For an actual intrinsic-resource relation $`I_a`$, set $`I(b,r)\iff I_a(u_a(b),r)`$. An actual resource predicate is not thereby turned into a world-indexed resource-existence predicate. Optional renaming of resource or target indices must consistently rename every affected relation argument. [Finite modal identity transport, §5][transport-proof]

An actual-anchored atom also needs a local interpretation at every evaluation world. For $`x\in D_w`$, define

```math
I_w^{a}(x,r)
\iff I_a\bigl(u_a(u_w^{-1}(x)),r\bigr).
```

Thus $`I_w^{a}(u_w(b),r)`$ agrees with $`I_a(u_a(b),r)`$ and with the global $`I(b,r)`$. Apply the same pullback $`u_a\circ u_w^{-1}`$ to every bearer argument of any actual-anchored mixed atom, including when it occurs under a modality. Resource and target arguments stay in their declared fixed coordinates, or are converted by their separately stipulated coordinate maps. The superscript $`a`$ identifies the fixed actual relation; the subscript $`w`$ records only which local bearer coordinates are used to evaluate it. This does not assert a different intrinsic relation at each world. In contrast, genuinely world-indexed atoms such as $`E_w`$, $`C_w`$ and $`\mathrm{Dep}_w`$ use their valuations at the evaluation world; no constancy of those valuations is assumed.

The conversion matters even when all local domains happen to use the same labels. Let $`W=\{a,v\}`$ and $`B=D_a=D_v=\{0,1\}`$, with $`u_a`$ the identity, $`u_v`$ the swap, and only $`a\mathcal A v`$. For one resource $`r`$, let $`I_a`$ hold only of $`(0,r)`$. At $`a`$, the globally interpreted formula $`\exists x\,(I(x,r)\land\Box I(x,r))`$ is true, witnessed by bearer $`0`$. Its local label at $`v`$ is $`1`$. Looking up the raw actual table at that destination label gives false and makes the displayed existential false, since bearer $`1`$ already fails the actual conjunct. The prescribed pullback takes destination label $`1`$ back to actual label $`0`$ and restores the correct truth value. With disjoint local domains, the raw lookup would not even have the declared type. This elementary example distinguishes the fixed actual relation from an accidental equality of coordinate labels.

**Language-preservation proposition.** These charts preserve the declared finite relational first-order language with equality, Boolean connectives and bearer quantifiers. They also preserve its modal extension when the local assignment semantics is explicitly the chart-induced one.

**Proof.** Interpret a global bearer assignment $`\beta`$ at $`w`$ as $`u_w\circ\beta`$. Atomic predicates agree by definition; equality agrees because the charts are injective. Boolean combinations preserve agreement. For a quantified variable, surjectivity makes every local value correspond to a global bearer value, and injectivity makes this reindexing exact. Thus both existential and universal quantification preserve truth. Now specify an accessibility relation $`\mathcal A`$ independently of $`\Gamma`$, and define the local box by

```math
\mathrm{Val}_v(\Box\varphi,\alpha_v)
=\bigwedge_{w:\mathcal A(v,w)}
 \mathrm{Val}_w\bigl(\varphi,
   u_w\circ u_v^{-1}\circ\alpha_v\bigr).
```

The transported assignment is $`u_w\circ\beta`$ when $`\alpha_v=u_v\circ\beta`$. Consequently each modal destination uses the same global assignment. Induction on formulas, including nested modalities, proves the claim. Quantification only over existing bearers is expressed by adding the appropriate $`E_w`$ guard, which the same induction preserves. □

This is a specified semantics whose preservation has been proved. It does not follow merely from an extensional translation that supplied no modal operators. Conventional nonempty-domain first-order semantics requires $`n>0`$. If empty outer domains are admitted instead, closed formulas can use the explicit conventions that an empty existential is false and an empty universal true; there are then no assignments to free bearer variables. The earlier section and tensor claims remain valid, but this is a different convention from a source semantics requiring a nonempty domain. [Finite modal identity transport and its independent review][transport-proof] [Modal-language review, §3][transport-review]

Under the fixed outer domain, the two universal quantifiers over bearers and accessible worlds commute. Thus both directions between $`\forall x\,\Box P(x)`$ and $`\Box\forall x\,P(x)`$ hold for unguarded outer-domain quantification. The corresponding unrestricted assertion is false for existence-guarded quantifiers. Let only $`v`$ be accessible from $`a`$, with outer domain $`\{0,1\}`$, existing domain $`E_a=\{0\}`$, $`E_v=\{0,1\}`$, and $`P`$ true only of $`0`$ at $`v`$. Then $`\forall^{E_a}x\,\Box P(x)`$ is true, while $`\Box\forall^{E_v}x\,P(x)`$ is false. Reversing the existence sets makes the first false and the second true. Charts alone impose neither domain constancy for actual existence nor an S5 accessibility frame. [Modal-language review, guarded-quantifier controls][transport-review]

In particular, the all-world same-bearer existence claim has the form

```math
\exists b\in B\ \forall w\in W:\ E_w(u_w(b)).
```

It differs from $`\forall w\in W\ \exists b\in B:\ E_w(u_w(b))`$, which permits a different witness in each world. The earlier resource-to-bearer argument already retains one witness; the new coordinates must implement that binding rather than replace it. For example, existence of an actual original resource with one uniformly existing, nonreceived bearer is expressed by

```math
\begin{aligned}
\exists t,r,b\quad&
 \mathrm{TargetReceived}(t)\land\mathrm{Relevant}(r,t)
 \land\mathrm{ResourceActual}(r)\\
&\land\neg\mathrm{ResourceReceived}(r)\land I_a(u_a(b),r)\\
&\land\forall w\in W\,
 \bigl(E_w(u_w(b))\land\neg C_w(u_w(b))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
```

The same target, resource and bearer remain under one existential scope. This formula neither supplies the original-adequacy premises nor identifies $`C_w(x)`$ with “has a supplier” without a separate representation assumption. Where necessity instead ranges only over worlds accessible from $`a`$, actual existence must be retained explicitly if $`a`$ is not guaranteed accessible. An empty accessibility set makes the box vacuous; adding actual existence then gives actual existence, not a nonvacuous alternative-world result. [Finite modal identity transport, §§5, 7][transport-proof]

### 10.9 Restricting bearers can erase the grounds of their reception

A selected section has less preservation power than full charts. Let $`B_{\mathrm{sec}}`$ be the set of all compatible point sections. Evaluation $`s\mapsto s_w`$ is injective at every world: agreeing at one vertex forces agreement along every path. It need not be surjective. Thus replacing every local domain by these coherent sections can delete individuals needed as witnesses of other assertions.

The three-label swap triangle illustrates the problem. Only the constant $`2`$ section survives. At $`a`$, let $`\mathrm{Dep}_a(0,2)`$ be the only supplier edge and let $`C_a(2)`$ be true. In the full local domain, $`2`$ has a supplier. In the retained domain containing only $`2`$, there is no supplier edge. Copying $`2`$'s existence and reception predicates faithfully does not preserve their asserted connection with the dependency relation. A resource borne only by $`0`$ likewise loses its realization witness. This is a failure of the proposed restriction, not a defect in a previously proved implication using the full bearer domain. [Finite modal identity transport, §5][transport-proof]

Even full coherent identity does not justify restricting the supplier domain to necessary beings. Take two worlds $`a,v`$ and identity charts on $`\{b,c,d\}`$. Let $`b`$ exist at both, $`c`$ only at $`a`$, and $`d`$ only at $`v`$. Let the only supplier edges be $`\mathrm{Dep}_a(c,b)`$ and $`\mathrm{Dep}_v(d,b)`$. Both endpoints of each edge exist where that edge holds. The target is received at both worlds, with a changing supplier. Restriction to the necessarily existing domain $`\{b\}`$ removes both witnesses. Necessary existence of a target does not entail independence, and persistent receipt does not require one fixed supplier. These are separate predicates before any philosophical appraisal of the fixture's possibility.

There is an exact smaller condition than retaining every local individual. Let $`j_w:B_{\mathrm{ret}}\to D_w`$ be injective retained-domain maps, and induce

```math
\mathrm{Dep}_{\mathrm{ret}}(w,c,b)
\iff \mathrm{Dep}_w(j_w(c),j_w(b)).
```

**Witness-preservation theorem.** At a specified retained target $`b`$ and world $`w`$, local and retained supplier existence agree if and only if

```math
\bigl[\exists y\in D_w:\mathrm{Dep}_w(y,j_w(b))\bigr]
\ \Longrightarrow
\bigl[\exists c\in B_{\mathrm{ret}}:
             \mathrm{Dep}_w(j_w(c),j_w(b))\bigr].
```

**Proof.** A retained supplier always maps to a local supplier by the definition of the induced relation. The only possibly missing direction is exactly the displayed implication. It therefore is equivalent to equality of the two existence truth values. Negating those values also preserves agreement. Conversely, agreement gives that local-to-retained implication. □

Injectivity is appropriate when retained labels are to distinguish retained individuals; the elementary existential equivalence itself needs only the stated map and induced relation. Surjectivity is sufficient but stronger than necessary. If both retained $`c`$ and omitted $`d`$ supply a target, retaining $`c`$ preserves existence of a supplier, although supplier counts and claims about $`d`$ can change. Absence of a retained supplier is evidence of full local nonreceipt only when the displayed condition is justified at that target and world. [Finite modal identity transport, Theorem 4; review, §1][transport-proof] [Witness-preservation review][transport-review]

Suppose the intended reception predicate satisfies $`C_w(x)\iff\exists y\,\mathrm{Dep}_w(y,x)`$. Copying $`C_w`$ preserves that representation only if the condition holds at every pair for which the equivalence is asserted. A contract quantified over all worlds and bearers cannot silently be replaced by a check only at actually existing targets. For actual resource realization, the analogous precise requirement is

```math
\bigl[\exists y\in D_a:\ E_a(y)\land I_a(y,r)\bigr]
\ \Longrightarrow
\bigl[\exists b\in B_{\mathrm{ret}}:
 E_a(j_a(b))\land I_a(j_a(b),r)\bigr]
```

for each actual resource in the needed scope. This requires some suitable retained bearer, not every bearer of the resource. Neither condition establishes the philosophical truth of its local relations.

Preserving these existential statements still does not preserve every premise. Retain a necessary bearer $`b`$ and omit an actual, contingent bearer $`c`$; let reception and dependency be empty, and let an actual resource be intrinsically borne by $`b`$. Both supplier preservation and actual realization hold. Yet the universal claim “every actual contingent bearer is received” is false in the full domain because of $`c`$ and true after restriction. The independent review supplies this control to distinguish the two existential conditions from preservation of an entire quantified theory. Removing counterexamples can make a universal statement easier to satisfy. Full language preservation above keeps its bijective-chart hypothesis. [Independent modal-transport review, §4][transport-review]

### 10.10 What is—and is not—unified by the construction

The correspondence itself is scientifically relevant information. On two worlds with outer domains $`\{0,1\}`$, identity alignment and empty dependency relations, set $`E_a=\{0\}`$. Compare $`E_v=\{0\}`$ with $`E_v=\{1\}`$. The structures at $`v`$ are isomorphic under a swap, so their same-world first-order sentences agree. Nevertheless, the first system has one bearer existing at both worlds and the second has none. A record of each world only up to independent local isomorphism cannot determine this same-bearer target. By contrast, passively swapping both $`E_v`$ and the alignment in the first system preserves its answer. Independent renaming of a valuation while holding correspondence fixed changes the represented data; simultaneous renaming of both does not. [Finite modal identity transport, §6][transport-proof]

This limitation does not forbid a richer qualitative account from specifying its own correspondence, nor show that unnamed descriptions lack concrete bearers. Dasgupta's *Individuals* explicitly brackets the relevant a priori modal dispute, and its extensional language comparison leaves modal expansions for further work; its comparison models have nonempty domains. *The Bare Necessities* discusses qualitative individuating roles and counterpart semantics while separating necessitation from grounding. Those observations support a scope limit here, not a refutation of either account. A many-valued or nonfunctional counterpart relation is outside the bijection theorem, and a counterpart interpretation can have a different modal target. Successful modal truth preservation also supplies no semantics for essence, explanatory priority or grounding merely by preserving extensions. [Dasgupta 2009, pp. 37, 63 n. 40, 64][dasgupta-individuals] [Dasgupta 2011, §§4–5][dasgupta-bare-necessities]

Several further notions of unity therefore need their own arguments. A unique eligible token at each world, a unique coherent section, numerical identity of its represented bearer, mereological simplicity, integrated agency, and universal productive scope are different conclusions. The earlier unity proposal separates them through useful conditional controls. Read in their corrected scope, these controls do not certify the metaphysical possibility of every named rival, and they do not cancel the later affirmative appraisal in section 10.4. [Unity and identity proposal, §§2–7; later reconciliation][unity-proposal] [Singleton-fibre correction and status reconciliation][singleton-correction]

For numerical identity, one may give each world a different token, choose flat transports linking them uniquely, and still interpret the tokens as counterparts rather than one individual. The unique section then exists without the additional identity interpretation. Conversely, one may hold a bearer numerically fixed while changing its role predicate at another world. Persistence of the bearer does not establish persistence of every role. These controls distinguish what the stipulated relations say; they do not resolve the underlying theory of identity.

For bearer-local roles, consider a system with three parts, one knowing, one selecting, and one realizing an outcome. Declaring a system token whose role predicate means “some part has this role” makes every role available to that system. It does not establish that one part has them all, or that the resulting aggregate is the individual required by another argument. A genuine organized collective may be an appropriate bearer for some purposes, but the change of granularity and incidence must be stated. Even one substrate that bears all the powers need not integrate apprehension, selection and realization in the same reason-guided episode. The affirmative agency comparison in section 12.4 concerns that stronger relation, not merely a list of capacities.

For ontological scope, a unique role bearer can have proper parts under the declared mereology, and the same incidence data can leave competing theories of properties undecided. A common root may coexist with attributes realized only by coordinated nonroot members. A single source description can also admit two proposed mappings to different worldly referents; co-reference within the source does not authenticate either mapping. Finally, a unified and integrated agent may actualize only a proper subdomain. Extending that conclusion to universal Creatorhood needs its independently justified productive domain and bridge. None of these inferences is supplied by counting sections. The source proposal's translated attribute-locality premise is likewise source-relative; it is not a new primary verification or a consensus claim about a theological school. [Unity and identity proposal, controls and source scope][unity-proposal]

The finite mathematics thus supplies exact conditions for preserving the candidate whose philosophical grounds have been separately argued. It does not reconstruct possible worlds from observations or extend finite conjunctions automatically to infinitely many worlds. Standing capacity, an act-kind, an individual act and a created effect remain different types; neither an empty act extension nor a permanent possibility supplies a necessary particular. No premise that every emergent act is created, no automatic inference from a new relation to borrowed perfection, and no actuality-from-possibility principle enters these proofs. In particular, this account of labels and witnesses neither completes the unfinished programme for an n-person noetic field nor replaces its original scientific objective.

## 11. What a complete explanatory fact does—and does not—establish

Generic explanation provides independent logical comparisons. It must not silently replace productive provision or identify explanatory facts with individual bearers. The following results reconstruct the relevant explanatory principles of Koons and Pruss and the corpus's checked consequences and controls. They concern explicit relational assumptions; neither the proofs nor their counterinterpretations authenticate a metaphysical realization. [Koons and Pruss, §2][u-kp] [Plural-explanation analysis][u-psr]

### 11.1 Self-explanation of a total explainer

Let X be a nonempty domain of actual facts. Admit its total plurality F and the singleton of each fact. Write E(x,S) for x completely explaining a nonempty plurality S. The relevant complete-transitivity clause is:

```math
E(x,S)\land y\in S\land E(y,T)\ \Longrightarrow\ E(x,T).
```

**Total self-explanation.** Suppose E(Q,F), and Q's singleton has an actual complete explainer R. Complete transitivity implies E(Q,{Q}).

**Proof.** Since R is actual, R belongs to F. Apply transitivity to E(Q,F), R ∈ F and E(R,{Q}). The resulting explanation is E(Q,{Q}). □

Unrestricted plural sufficient reason provides the two required explanations, but the local theorem needs only these two instances. It requires neither a fusion fact representing F, output projection, partial transitivity nor a ban on extended circles. Alternatively, member projection alone gives the conclusion from E(Q,F) and Q ∈ F. Thus the transitivity clause is sufficient in the displayed package, not logically indispensable under every alternative package. Full self-explanation here means E(Q,{Q}); it does not mean that Q lacks a distinct explainer, that every proper part explains itself, or that Q is necessary, supernatural or a unique agent. [Plural-explanation analysis, totality proof and terminology][u-psr]

All-actual-fact comprehension and nonemptiness are substantive assumptions. A restricted principle for basic natural facts does not automatically supply F. An empty domain can make universal conditional formulations vacuous. Likewise, a formal type of “facts” does not by itself warrant the intended universe of actual truths. The printed source proof's appeal to explanation of a member needs a projection bridge, and its circle restriction needs the stated nonparthood condition, not mere distinctness. The short proof above avoids those dependencies while preserving the source-relative existence conclusion. It does not establish novelty or the epistemological defense of sufficient reason. [Plural-explanation analysis, comparison with the printed proof][u-psr]

### 11.2 Why sufficient reason without those bridges is weaker

Use inclusive fact-parthood ≤ and define partial explanation on the **source side**:

```math
P(x,S)\iff\exists z\,[x\le z\land E(z,S)].
```

The circle restriction used here is:

```math
P(x,S)\land y\in S\land y\ne x\land y\not\le x
\ \Longrightarrow\ \neg P(y,\{x\}).
```

It allows a target that is a part of its partial explainer. It is not a blanket prohibition of every cycle or of every form of partial self-explanation.

Consider facts q₀,q₁,…, with all nonempty subsets admitted as pluralities. Define qₘ ≤ qₙ exactly when m ≥ n. For singletons let E(qₙ₊₁,{qₙ}) be the only complete explanations; for every plurality with at least two members let q₀ be its only complete explainer. Every nonempty plurality has an actual explainer, including infinite pluralities and the totality. Parthood is reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric. Exact source-side closure gives P(qₘ,{qₙ}) precisely when m ≥ n+1; every fact partially explains every multi-member plurality through q₀. [Plural-explanation analysis, countable control][u-psr]

The circle restriction holds: if target qₖ is not part of qₘ, then k < m, whereas P(qₖ,{qₘ}) would require k ≥ m+1. No fact completely explains itself, since n ≠ n+1. No fact is a proper part of its own full singleton explainer either. Both transitivity clauses fail: q₀ explains {q₀,q₁}, and q₁ explains {q₀}, but q₀ neither fully nor partially explains {q₀}. Member projection fails on the same example. Thus unrestricted plural sufficient reason, source-side partial explanation and the circle restriction do not force full self-explanation after **both** transitivity clauses and output projection are removed. This is not a countermodel retaining the source's full package. [Plural-explanation analysis][u-psr]

The chain has no disjoint remainders and fails ordinary supplementation. That limitation can be removed. Take the nonzero elements of an atomless bounded partial order with a top T. Let E(z,{x}) hold exactly when z is a nonzero proper part of x, and let T alone explain every plurality with at least two members. Atomlessness supplies each singleton explainer. The source-side definition then gives P(y,{x}) exactly when y is a proper part of x: one direction follows from y ≤ z < x; for the other take z = y. Every element partially explains each multi-member plurality through T. The same circle proof works, no fact explains itself, and a proper part a < T witnesses failure of both transitivity clauses and member projection through {T,a}. [Supplemented control][u-psr]

A concrete realization uses all nonempty **regular open** subsets of the real line, ordered by inclusion. An open U is regular open when U equals the interior of its closure. Every nonempty such U contains two disjoint nonempty open intervals, giving a nonzero proper part. For strong supplementation, if U is not contained in regular open V, then U minus the closure of V is a nonempty open set: otherwise the openness of U would put it inside the interior of that closure, namely V. A nonempty interval within the difference is a part of U disjoint from V.

For a nonempty family of regular open sets, let A be their union and J the interior of the closure of A. Because A is open, A ⊆ J ⊆ closure(A), so A and J have the same closure and J is regular open. It is the least regular open upper bound. An open region overlapping J intersects A because A is dense in J, and hence intersects a family member. The converse is immediate. J therefore satisfies the overlap condition for a fusion, not merely an upper-bound label. This supplies arbitrary nonempty fusions and strong supplementation while preserving the explanatory counterinterpretation. The generic order argument has retained formal verification; this regular-open realization is ordinary mathematical proof, not a newly kernel-checked topological development. Its complement is mereological, not propositional negation. [Plural-explanation analysis, strengthened realization][u-psr]

### 11.3 Uniqueness requires the right membership

Assume antisymmetry of parthood and the displayed circle restriction.

**Mutual partial explanation.** If P(q,{r}) and P(r,{q}), then q = r.

**Proof.** Suppose q ≠ r. Applying the circle restriction with source r and target q, the existing reverse explanation excludes q not being part of r; thus q ≤ r. Interchanging q and r gives r ≤ q. Antisymmetry gives q = r, a contradiction. □

Now let partial explanation have its source-side definition, assume reflexive parthood and member projection for full explanation. If E(q,F), E(r,G), r ∈ F and q ∈ G, projection gives E(q,{r}) and E(r,{q}); reflexivity makes both partial. Hence q = r. For a shared target F it suffices that **both explainers belong to F**. Unrestricted sufficient reason for the nonempty all-actual-fact plurality then supplies existence and uniqueness of its full explanatory fact. Neither explanatory transitivity clause is needed. [Total-explainer uniqueness][u-unique]

The membership condition cannot be dropped for a narrower target. A simple model has four distinct facts q,a,b,e with equality as parthood. q explains every nonempty plurality; a and b explain only {e}; e explains nothing. Partial and full explanation coincide. All nonempty predicate pluralities are admitted. Sufficient reason holds through q. Both transitivity clauses hold because the only target of a or b is e, which has no outgoing explanation. Projection holds, and no distinct reciprocal explanatory pair violates the circle restriction. q is the unique all-fact explainer, while the full explainers of {e} are exactly q,a,b. The model has no fusion for arbitrary distinct facts. [Total-explainer uniqueness, finite control][u-unique]

Here too the mereological limitation can be removed. Take the fifteen nonempty subsets of a four-atom set, with inclusion as parthood. Denote its four atomic facts by q,a,b,e and use the same explanatory rule: q explains every nonempty plurality of facts, a and b only {e}, and all other facts nothing. Every full explainer is atomic, so its only nonzero part is itself; source-side partial and full explanation still coincide. Strong supplementation follows by taking an atom in x minus y whenever x is not contained in y. The union of a nonempty family is its least upper bound and its fusion: a fact overlaps the union exactly when it shares an atom with a family member. The transitivity, projection, sufficient-reason and circle checks above remain valid. Distribution to parts also holds: q explains every nonempty singleton, and e has no nonzero proper part. Thus proper parts, supplementation and arbitrary nonempty fusions coexist with three distinct explainers of {e}. [Supplemented-fusion uniqueness control][u-boolean]

These controls prove relational non-entailment, not the possibility of a plural-original world. A target containing only a created field can omit candidate explainers, so the all-fact theorem cannot be transferred to it without a membership bridge. More fundamentally, a unique **explanatory fact** may concern several beings. No injective representation of individual bearers by facts has been assumed, and generic explanation has not been identified with productive origination. Neither the positive theorem nor the controls settle original-bearer unity. [Total-explainer uniqueness, semantic boundary][u-unique]

### 11.4 The restricted particular-explanans bridge

The restricted argument needs another direction of parthood kept visible. Write W(x) for wholly particular, H(x) for supernatural in the source's sense, and B(x) for W(x) and not H(x). A restricted sufficient-reason premise gives every nonempty plurality of B-facts an explainer y that is neither identical to nor a part of any target member. Assume a nonempty total basic-natural plurality N is available. It follows that y is not basic natural. It does **not** yet follow that some fact is supernatural: y might be nonparticular and contain a particular natural constituent. [Particular-explanans analysis, §§1–2][u-particular]

Let b be a member of N. Member projection supplies E(y,{b}). Grant the strong useful reading of the particular-constituent premise: y contains a **wholly** particular p. If p were not supernatural, it would belong to N, and projection would give E(y,{p}). But p ≤ y is compatible with y not being a part of p. The stated externality excludes the opposite direction. A prohibition of basic **full** self-explanation also does not exclude p being a proper part of its own complete explainer. [Particular-explanans analysis][u-particular]

A complete small control uses the three nonempty subsets of a two-atom set. Call the atoms b,u and their fusion c. Only b is wholly particular and basic natural; nothing is supernatural. Let the only full explanation be E(c,{b}). Partial explanations of {b} then have sources b,u,c. Restricted sufficient reason and its externality hold because c is neither b nor a part of b. The explainer contains the required particular constituent b, and b does not fully explain itself. Both transitivity clauses hold because b has no full outgoing explanation. Member-and-part projection adds nothing beyond the existing singleton edge. For the circle restriction, source b is exempt by identity, source c by b ≤ c, and source u has no prohibited reverse P(b,{u}). Strong supplementation and all nonempty fusions hold in the two-atom mereology. No proper part of c fully explains {b}, so ordinary part-minimality of explanation does not remove the control. An optional E(u,{u}) edge preserves these checks. Unrestricted sufficient reason fails, for example at {c}. [Particular-explanans analysis, three-fact control][u-particular]

The edge E(c,{b}) is stipulated. No claim has been established that an explanandum-containing mixed fact is an adequate real explanation. The control isolates a missing explicit bridge; an independently defended semantic account of explanation may exclude the edge. Relevant inclusion of a **being** in a fact is also not automatically fact-parthood. Neither conjunction syntax nor the names of the predicates supply that interpretation. [Particular-explanans analysis, §4][u-particular]

Two sufficient repairs are exact:

- **Exclude basic proper-part self-explanation.** Suppose no basic natural p is a proper part of a full explainer of itself. The p supplied above, if natural, would be basic; it would be part of y, distinct from y by restricted externality, and fully explained by y. That contradicts the added condition. Therefore p is supernatural. Only member projection is needed; the transitivity and circle principles add no work to this deduction.
- **Strengthen the selected explainer's externality.** Require that no member of N be part of y. A wholly particular constituent p ≤ y cannot then be basic natural, so it is supernatural. Once p is supplied, no further projection step is needed. Disjointness is sufficient but stronger than this condition.

These are substantive sufficient repairs, not claims about a uniquely weakest premise. Their philosophical and epistemological warrant must be supplied. If the wholly particular starting fact is already supernatural the existence conclusion is immediate; otherwise it supplies the nonempty basic target. An empty basic class cannot be treated as an inhabited one. [Particular-explanans analysis, §5][u-particular]

Retaining unrestricted all-fact explanation yields a different valid route. Suppose E(Q,F) for all actual facts, parthood is reflexive and antisymmetric, and a wholly particular constituent p ≤ Q is supplied by projecting to an actual particular target. If p ≠ Q, then Q is not part of p. Yet P(p,F), Q ∈ F and P(Q,{p}) hold, the last by member projection and reflexivity. They violate the circle restriction. Hence p = Q. Thus Q is wholly particular; projection gives E(Q,{Q}); and a prohibition of basic full self-explanation makes Q supernatural. No transitivity is needed. This stronger route imports all-fact explanation and comprehension absent from the restricted control; it is not a proof from restricted sufficient reason alone. [Particular-explanans analysis, §6][u-particular]

### 11.5 The modal transport needs symmetry, not all of S5

A further formal distinction concerns the modal strength of an explanatory argument. Let C mean that the relevant explanatory coverage obtains and S denote a proposed sufficient explanatory condition. Admit C → S and S → □C at the accessible worlds relevant to evaluation. On a symmetric accessibility relation, possibility of C entails actual C.

**Proof.** If w accesses v and C holds at v, the two admitted implications give S and then □C at v. Symmetry gives accessibility from v back to w. Thus C holds at w. At w it is enough to assume □(C → S) and □(S → □C); global truth of the implications is stronger than needed. Equivalently, non-C at w implies necessary non-C there. The transport uses the dual form ◇□p → p of the familiar symmetry axiom. Neither reflexivity nor transitivity is needed. □ [Local modal-transport refinement][u-symmetry]

Symmetry cannot simply be dropped in favour of reflexivity and transitivity. Take two worlds w₀,w₁ with edges w₀→w₀, w₀→w₁ and w₁→w₁. Let C and S be false at w₀ and true at w₁. Both explanatory implications hold everywhere, since w₁ accesses only itself. But C is possible and false at w₀. There is no empty-successor or terminal-box vacuity. This proves failure on that nonsymmetric frame, while the preceding proof establishes the general sufficient result. It is not a characterization of every frame or a metaphysical possibility certificate. [Local modal-transport refinement][u-symmetry]

Reducing the modal frame assumption does not establish the necessary-coverage implication S → □C, explanatory exhaustiveness or the cross-world interpretation. A richer argument that first proves invariance of a substantive profile can still use stronger modal assumptions at that earlier step. This lemma begins after necessary adequacy is admitted; it does not turn thin coverage predicates into a counterexample preserving an independently warranted rich explanatory profile. [Local modal-transport refinement][u-symmetry]

## 12. Self-knowledge, sovereignty and practical conformance

### 12.1 Knowing whose act it is

Complete truthful self-knowledge does not itself identify two original subjects. Consider a modal structure with worlds W, a fixed outer domain of bearers B, an existence relation, and interpreted powers, acts, reasons and productive support. Choose agents present throughout the represented modal range. A **center** (w,b) records a world and its distinguished agent. At that center, “I” denotes b. Ordinary bearer quantification still ranges over B, so centering does not delete a peer or supplier. A shift to agent a in that chosen persistent agent set evaluates the same formula at (w,a). B can know that A truthfully says “I am A” without B falsely self-ascribing A's identity. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §§1–2][u-center]

**Conservative centered enrichment.** Suppose the base theory has no epistemic commitments incompatible with the following expansion. At each center c give its knowledge operator exactly the epistemic alternative {c}; keep metaphysical accessibility separate, carrying the same agent from w to an accessible v. Then all old non-epistemic truths are unchanged, and every formula φ in the extended compositional language satisfies:

```math
K\varphi\ \longleftrightarrow\ \varphi
```

at its center.

**Proof.** The old-language reduct is the original structure. Induction on old formulas preserves their truth; at the modal step the same metaphysical relation and bearer assignment are used. The knowledge clause checks φ at every epistemically accessible center, whose sole member is the current center. Hence knowledge and truth coincide, including iterated knowledge and perspective-shift formulas. No old bearer, act, productive support or modal quantifier was changed. □ [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §3][u-center]

The construction preserves factivity, complete semantic knowledge and introspection. It does not repair a base theory already asserting false belief, certify a real intrinsic realization of cognition, or establish that action is **based on** recognized first-person reasons. A policy selecting an owned act and a record naming its subject can coexist without the recognized reason actually guiding selection. That practical basing relation requires its own interpretation. A stipulation that each knows “I alone provide the whole” simply imports whole provision by factivity; it does not derive it from generic self-knowledge. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §§3–4][u-center]

Consequently, a reduced two-source interpretation can retain truthful awareness of each proper act and of joint productive support. Neither agent must misidentify itself or claim sole provision. This demonstrates a limited semantic non-entailment. It does not establish that the enriched agents meet the complete original-perfection profile, or that their joint ontology is metaphysically possible. The existing positive common-source appraisal is not cancelled by this control. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §§4, 7][u-center]

Common reasons require a further distinction. Two policies can respond to the same fitting reason without one peer efficiently producing the other's will or a third efficient controller being introduced. Conversely, the shared reason does not automatically provide a complete explanation of why these particular agents instantiate those determinations. Shared reasons alone establish neither one numerical subject, a third original giver, nor reciprocal efficient donation. This preserves a genuine explanatory credit of the concordant proposal without certifying its entire original ontology or practical basing. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §4][u-center]

### 12.2 Three substantive routes to exclusion

There are stronger contradiction arguments, each with an identifiable additional premise.

**Universal mastery and unassailability.** Fix one respect of ultimate authority. Suppose every qualified original masters every distinct existing bearer, and no qualified original is mastered by a distinct bearer in that respect. Two distinct qualified originals A and B would give both A's mastery of B and its negation. This proves at most one; it supplies no existence. It requires neither an unwise possible quarrel nor a task of producing another necessary being while leaving that being unreceived. The substantive question is why the mastery domain includes every distinct bearer, rather than only created effects. Truthful self-recognition and thin nonreceipt do not establish that scope. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §5A][u-center]

**Complete productive history and proper unborrowed exercise.** Suppose every claimed complete original undertaking for x includes an actual proper unborrowed exercise of its source; completeness covers every mode genuinely belonging to x's actual full productive origin; and ultimate provision of a mode properly belonging unborrowed to t can be attributed to s only if s = t. If A and B both completely account for x, choose B's actual witness. A's history-completeness covers it, so the exclusion yields A = B. The interpretation must warrant that the intrinsic original exercise is indispensable to x's productive origin; it cannot be inserted into a list of created effects by fiat. Shared power or one common event is not automatically co-ownership of the same proper act. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §5B][u-center]

**Incompatible guarantees.** Let an A-choice guarantee outcomes in X and a B-choice guarantee outcomes in Y. If X and Y are disjoint and those particular choices have a common admissible completion, that completion would have to lie in their empty intersection. Thus the choices are not compossible. Full rectangular independence of every choice is stronger than needed, but some warranted compossibility is essential. Necessary concord does not supply it. Nor may “for every peer setting there is some own means” be exchanged for “one own means works for every peer setting.” Standing capacity, contextual control and actual whole-origin provision remain distinct. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §5C][u-center]

### 12.3 Recognition can mediate conformance

A properly epistemic argument uses self-knowledge as a detector of an independently established defect. Suppose an irreducibly joint undertaking is in fact a constitutive pure defect of each relevant original's appropriate agency. Suppose each correctly recognizes that status as its own, and a perfectly practically ordered original never knowingly enacts such a defect. Actual enactment then contradicts practical conformance. Self-knowledge does inferential work when the conformance norm is about knowing enactment. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §6][u-center]

It does not establish the defect classification. A smaller product, unexercised standing capacity, newly attained fitting end, or freely chosen received means is not automatically a constitutive defect. The relevant original-efficacy argument must show why foreign completion in this actual role is incompatible with its proper mode. Equally, a valued cooperative good does not automatically vindicate the role. The question concerns real adequate agency, not a perfect original's vanity or desire for exclusive credit. This distinction preserves the source's positive perfection argument and the strongest rival's actual claims without manufacturing an epistemic shortcut. [Centered self-knowledge analysis, §§6–7][u-center] [Public source-unity judgment, “The de se proposal”][u-judgment]

### 12.4 From truthful self-awareness to informed self-governance

The distinction between knowing one's activity and acting for an apprehended reason also supports a positive comparison of original agency. Grant an actual original producer complete knowledge of its own determinate productive reality. Grant that this knowledge includes its particular acts and their relevant objects and consequences. It then knows what it produces as its own production. That conclusion supplies particular cognitive content. It does not yet say that the product is brought about **because** the source apprehends and wills a fitting end. Factive knowledge includes the known act's occurrence among its truth conditions; it does not by that fact make the knowledge a practical reason for the act. The issue cannot be settled merely by making the self-knowledge more detailed or more explicitly first-personal. [Particular cognition, wise necessitation, and original agency, §§1–3; independent inference review][practical-agency] [Agency inference review][practical-agency-review]

Claims about the unity or complexity of cognition cannot supply that missing practical relation. Phenomenologically unified awareness gives a subject's experience as one awareness; it does not by itself establish that its metaphysical bearer is an indivisible simple substance. That conclusion would require an argument excluding relevant differentiated constitution or dependence, rather than an identification of experiential unity with a theory of the bearer's composition. The point leaves the actual unity of awareness intact while refusing to extract a stronger ontology merely from the description of how it appears. [Causal foundations, §5][causal-background]

Likewise, rich cognitive content does not by itself show mechanical assembly out of independently supplied parts. Distinguishing many things known is not yet identifying many externally contributed components of the knower. But removing that proposed cost of cognition does not show that cognition guides production: knowledge might be intrinsic and extensive while the practical relation remains the further question just identified. Nor does distinguishing knowledge, power and willing as predicates establish that they are detachable pieces supplied from outside. The two directions must be guarded together. Neither conceptual plurality proves external composition nor denial of external composition proves practical self-governance. [Causal foundations, §5; section 2 above][causal-background]

These controls do not attribute a stronger doctrine of absolute simplicity to the cited anti-divisibility material. The reported references V,321 and V,428 in the source comparison have an unresolved edition correspondence and cannot be treated as interchangeable without collation. The present agency argument depends instead on the independently stated relations among understanding, willing and effective action. It neither assumes the unresolved quotation proves indivisibility nor substitutes that claim for the practical-perfection argument. [Causal foundations, §5][causal-background]

Ibn Taymiyya's discussion in *Bayān talbīs al-Jahmiyya*, I, 510–512, distinguishes cognition that conforms to its object from cognition that also contributes to producing its object. His reply to the philosophical proposal does not treat all knowledge as productive, and it does not treat knowledge as sufficient without will and power. In the practical case it distinguishes the intended end, willing that end, the act serving as a means, and willing that act; cognition concerns these different terms. The theological example of self-knowledge is exact: knowing oneself does not produce oneself. It would be an illicit change of target to infer that self-knowledge therefore cannot contribute to producing anything else. Nor does a nonproductive self-knowledge episode counterexample every stronger claim about an original knowingly producing an effect. What fails is the proposed identification of truth-matching cognition with practical production. Nonproductive knowledge can remain a cognitive excellence; practical direction adds a relation without making all other knowledge valueless. [*Bayān*, I, 510–512][bayan-practical] [Particular cognition, §§2–3][practical-agency]

This leaves a genuine additional premise. The stronger source argument connecting knowing production to willing must justify the cognition's practical role, rather than hide that role inside the word “knows.” A diagnostic proposal can make the difference explicit: an intrinsically structured original produces definite effects and truthfully knows its own determinations, while that cognition merely accompanies the production. Calling its cognition practically idle does not call its productive activity ineffective. The proposal isolates a distinction in the thinner premises; it is not a demonstrated metaphysical possibility, a certificate of original perfection or a refutation of the stronger source argument. An independently warranted account of purposive production can exclude it. [Particular cognition, §3; agency inference review][practical-agency] [Agency inference review][practical-agency-review]

There is an affirmative reason to supply such an account. A fitting benefit and an excellent mode of beneficence are different objects of appraisal. A process might produce a valuable outcome without understanding the good, willing it as an end, or choosing the means because they serve it. An informed agent can possess those relations to the same outcome. The additional excellence belongs to the source's understanding and ownership of its activity, rather than solely to the product's usefulness. In *Bayān*'s discussion of generosity, Ibn Taymiyya rejects the proposed identification of excellent giving with beneficial output devoid of any purpose. He also distinguishes requiring compensation from the recipient from intrinsically valuing the beneficent activity. Acting for an end is not thereby bargaining with the recipient or receiving one's original essential capacity from that recipient. [*Bayān*, discussion of generosity and its proposed definition][bayan-beneficence] [Particular cognition, §5][practical-agency]

The philosophical inference must preserve that positive rationale and its applicability condition. Within the defended pure-perfection account, informed direction towards fitting ends through one's own willing is an intelligible excellence of an original source. It combines understanding, a fitting practical orientation, ownership and effective determination, without requiring an external governor or an arbitrary preference. A structured impersonal rival must consequently answer a comparison of **ways of producing**, not merely match the useful outcomes. The source-based appraisal gives this argument affirmative, defeasible weight. It is not a new deduction from efficient nonreceipt alone or an independent proof of the whole pure-perfection principle. The account of wise direction, effective performance and stable character is developed more generally in [Objective Normativity and Corrective Cognition, §6](objective-normativity-and-corrective-cognition.md#6-wise-direction-effective-performance-and-stable-character). [Particular cognition, §5; agency inference review][practical-agency] [Agency inference review][practical-agency-review]

Two source controls prevent that rationale from being circular. First, an agreed honorific does not establish every attribute placed inside a disputed definition of it. In the same *Bayān* discussion, the author challenges both the unproved attribution of generosity and the proposed interpretation that would exclude willing. Merely calling an original generous, wise or perfect cannot supply the practical relation under dispute. Second, natural directedness is weaker than conscious apprehension and owned willing. The text can describe natural inclination in purposive language without identifying it with the stronger cognitive agency at issue. A beneficial stone is not thereby a morally foolish agent, and an intrinsically directed impersonal reality is not already a conscious chooser. The comparison concerns eligibility for the original excellence under discussion; its scope must be defended rather than fixed by a name. [*Bayān*, discussion of generosity][bayan-beneficence] [Particular cognition, §5; agency inference review][practical-agency] [Agency inference review][practical-agency-review]

The strongest impersonal reply accordingly deserves its full content. It proposes an underived concrete productive reality with differentiated intrinsic powers, successive activity and ordered useful effects. It need not be a featureless abstraction, an idle source, or a natural mechanism awaiting a foreign trigger. It disputes that rational self-governance is a pure and relevant excellence applicable to an original of its kind. The affirmative responds that understanding and owning the fitting reason for one's production contributes something that outcome equivalence omits. The reply identifies the disputed normative comparison; it neither proves the impersonal proposal possible nor allows the affirmative to settle the question by describing that proposal as blind. Declaring essential nonpersonality does not by itself establish exemption from the comparison, just as stipulating the comparison's conclusion would not establish its warrant. [Particular cognition, §5; section 9.3 above][practical-agency]

Particular determination introduces a different distinction. One must explain the fitting kind of end, the actual means and occasion, and the producer's particular willing. An individually preferred object need not answer all three questions. In the source's discussion of practical realization, a generic end can be specified through knowledge of which food, garment or recipient is actually available and suitable. The individual need not have been antecedently preferred for its own sake. This is a positive account of how particular means can be selected; it does not prove that the original's choice is unexplained or supply an externally restricted human opportunity set as the original's ultimate condition. Where earlier internally originated acts establish the present circumstances, those acts and their reasons remain part of the account. [Particular cognition, §§2, 4, interpreting the connected *Darʾ* discussion][practical-agency]

Neither does necessary wise determination eliminate choice. Ibn al-Qayyim's *Miftāḥ dār al-saʿāda* answers an objection that preference for the better removes choosing: determination through the subject's own willing and wisdom differs from external compulsion. Thus an impersonal source does not become personal merely because its determinations are exact and necessary; a wise agent does not become impersonal merely because its fitting determination is necessary. The separate discussion of equally weighted creaturely options illustrates the difference between permission and determination. It supplies no premise that every complete divine practical context contains such a tie. The stronger source claim is intrinsic wise determination, not undirected randomness as the condition of agency. [*Miftāḥ*, electronic loci 2:952–955 and 2:905–909, as distinguished in the public source comparison][miftah-choice] [Particular cognition, §§2, 4][practical-agency]

This also fixes the numerical limit. Two proposed intrinsically knowledgeable, practically directed and wise originals could both possess the excellence just defended and necessarily agree for their own fitting reasons. Necessity of agreement does not make their determinations coerced, and plurality alone does not remove their practical basing. The practical-perfection argument therefore distinguishes purposive self-governance from blind or merely accompanied production without independently excluding the concordant pair. Their alleged complete original efficacy and ultimate authority remain subject to the separate arguments in sections 9–12.3. This limit does not retract the affirmative agency appraisal or the separately supported common-source judgment; it prevents one argument from being credited with another argument's conclusion. [Particular cognition, §§5–6; agency inference review][practical-agency] [Agency inference review][practical-agency-review]


## 13. Conclusion
