# Sufficient wise reasons, dispensable means, and the scope of contrast

10 October 2026 UTC. Bounded T20 source-and-inference continuation. New scratch evidence only. Originals remain unchanged. No protected repository mutation, integration, metaphysical model, or T20 closure.

## Result

**A means can have a positive wise rationale even if the agent could obtain the prominently stated end without it. The recovered text gives a concrete reason: the means can itself benefit other participants. It does not thereby establish that this means is uniquely best, or explain its every temporal particular.**

This is more precisely supported by the edited *Shifāʾ* than by the rough electronic text previously retained. At edited 2:182–183 the source compares the wisdom achieved with and without the intermediaries, then illustrates the difference with trade: buyers receive goods and sellers receive the price. The old e213 text loses part of the comparison and badly corrupts the example. The edited wording and apparatus were checked against the actual printed-page images, not reconstructed from a plausible paraphrase.

The philosophical operation distinguishes three replies that must not be collapsed:

1. Some ends include relations or conditions that cannot survive deletion of their means.
2. Even if a selected end could survive, the means may have further independently intelligible benefits.
3. Even if a kind of good can be obtained in two ways, exercising both kinds can have a further rationale in the scope of an agent's activity.

These are respectively a constitutive-condition argument, a comparative-benefit argument, and a diversity-of-activity argument. None is a theorem that an unexplained selection among completely equal full grounds is adequate. None entails a unique whole history.

**The saved detailed reports do not exhibit the alleged equation of explanatory adequacy with a uniquely best alternative.** That anti-conflation result was already earned. The new gain is a repaired source premise and an executed test of the inference from replaceability to purposelessness. At the stronger frontier, the result is deliberately limited: this passage supplies positive reasons for mediated activity and answers a definite non-futility question, but does not furnish a complete sufficient determining account of a new actual temporally particular willing. Calling the bounded reason insufficient because it does not select an entire history would be an over-demand. Calling it the complete particular determinant would be an overclaim.

## 2. Textual recovery and its consequential limit

### Reply 4 is comparative, not merely a claim of impossible direct attainment

The received e213 has the sequence:

> ولم تكن الحكمة حاصلة بعدمها

The edited 2:182 instead prints:

> ولم تكن الحكمة الحاصلة بوجودها مساوية للحكمة الحاصلة بعدمها

The edition's note says that the words from **بوجودها** through the second **الحاصلة** are absent from witness **د**. This is an editor-reported manuscript variant, not a manuscript I collated. The received web wording is not identical to the wording obtained by merely deleting that span, so no stemmatic identification of the web text with د is inferred.

The difference matters to the logical application. The fuller reading explicitly compares the wisdom realized by the two arrangements. It need not deny that some wisdom, or the narrowly stated target, could be realized without these intermediaries. Reading reply 4 as only another assertion that the same end is impossible without this route obscures why it follows replies 1–3 and precedes reply 7.

### The example is now intelligible without emendation by us

The received e213 has **البخارات** and garbled clauses about a maker, merchandise, and payment. Edited 2:183 clearly presents commerce: provision for some people is situated in trade; goods are brought to those who need them; one side benefits from the goods and the other from the price. Both sides have an intelligible benefit. The exact edited sentence and its preceding apparatus are retained in the source extract and images.

This is a source-owned conditional illustration, introduced by **إذا**. It is not a revelation identifying the complete divine reason for one dated transaction. The point is the positive structure of a productive arrangement with more than one beneficiary, not a blanket endorsement of every exchange or an all-things-considered economic policy.

### Other recovered controls

- Edited 2:184 records that د numbers reply 7 as reply 6 and continues the numbering error. The edition identifies the later diversity reply as reply 10 and the divine-names reply as 11; the received text prints two replies numbered 10.
- At 2:183–184 the permission of allegedly unpurposed acts is expressly marked **بطريق الإلزام**, and the author then affirms wise ends for all acts. This is not a doctrinal exemption for arbitrary willing.
- At 2:180 the eternal-wisdom argument is a parity response to an opponent already accepting eternal will with temporally specified objects. It must not be converted into a complete constructive theory merely because the author also offers positive arguments nearby.
- At 2:181 the argument against an eternally complete will-less necessitator is the author's endorsed polemic. It rejects an inference to necessary procession without wise willing, not conditional necessity of an effect when its actual complete voluntary production is present.

Word and PDF are two presentations of the same edited publication, not two independent historical witnesses. No earlier original file was repaired or overwritten.

## 3. The three distinct source arguments

### A. Constitutive dependence: do not change the end while claiming to preserve it

At 2:181–182, replies 1–3 distinguish the ability to produce a possible thing from the ability to obtain a relation without its necessary relatum, or an occurrence without what makes it that occurrence. The example of a son as son without a father concerns the described relation. It is not a claim that every familiar biological production is the only way an omnipotent agent could produce a person.

The philosophical use is exact: a purported alternative that removes a constitutive feature of the specified end is not an alternative realization of that very end. Omnipotence is not reduced by refusing the inconsistent description. This result is inherited and remains true regardless of the variant in reply 4.

### B. Additional benefits: preserving one target is not preserving the full practical comparison

Reply 4, 2:182–183, takes a different route. Creating intermediaries can bring further benefits, including benefits to those intermediaries. In the example, treating delivery of goods to buyers as the whole aim ignores sellers' provision. A direct provision for buyers may therefore preserve one target while changing a further good independently characterizable as another group's receipt of livelihood.

The author explicitly assigns significance to these gains; they are not called good solely because the selected route happened. This supplies substantive reason content. The means are not retained merely as an idle label for the already chosen history.

Reply 7, 2:184, then grants the opponent's direct-achievement possibility for argument: both alternatives are within power. It denies that availability of the alternative establishes futility, because equality in the selected respect is not equality **من كل وجه**. Its examples reject equating the existence and nonexistence of the intermediaries in every respect.

This sentence must not be exported as a universal principle that any difference, however irrelevant or bad, justifies whichever option is selected. Its context supplies **حكم ومصالح ومنافع**, not mere extensional nonidentity. At most the bare nonidentity of two descriptions defeats a stated identity claim. A positive practical explanation requires the independently warranted favorable difference; the trade example supplies one at a bounded respect.

### C. Diversity: the explanation can concern both ways across activity

Reply 10, 2:186, considers the two ways within power and says that exercising both kinds is more complete than exercising only one. Its continuation contrasts an achieved good through a separately produced creature with one through the original's own immanent acts, speech and self-praise. The author therefore treats the variety of activity as practically significant.

The claim is about kinds of activity and their scope. It does not require the same effect-token to be both mediated and unmediated, turn the two descriptions into two separable divine components, or assert that all complete determining conditions can remain fixed while a selection varies. Nor does it assign a dated occurrence to either kind. It is a reason against confining the source's activity to a single mode, not an algorithm scheduling every act.

One must also resist turning this into the maxim that every imaginable variant must occur. The source's comparison concerns the defended types and their asserted perfections. It is not unrestricted realization of all combinatorially describable options.

## 4. Executed contrast test: provision through trade

This is an analysis of the author's conditional example, not construction of a rival world or a choice-coordinate model.

### The contrast actually answered

Let the question be why a mediated provision arrangement has a reason, despite the original's ability to give goods directly to their recipients. In the source's example:

1. Buyers need the supplied goods.
2. Sellers obtain provision through receiving their price.
3. Moving goods to buyers and payment to sellers realizes benefits for both.
4. The compared description of direct provision specifies buyers' receipt alone. It has not specified what, if anything, happens to sellers' provision.

The first three claims identify relevant beneficiaries and benefits without the phrase “the arrangement actually selected.” The fourth exposes what the proposed replacement has left out. Thus the inference from “buyers could receive the goods directly” to “trade has no benefit or intelligible point” fails. The source has given a positive additional reason for this kind of mediation, not merely denied a logical impossibility argument.

The comparison is adequate to answer that specific futility allegation. A numerical optimum and exhaustive comparison of all conceivable arrangements are unnecessary for that conclusion. This is the limited positive sufficiency earned here.

### Stronger alternatives and what changes

**An alternative supplies buyers only.** The displayed rationale distinguishes it: the seller-side good has not been preserved. This is a substantive failure of the proposed equivalence, not an appeal to the opacity of divine reasons.

**An alternative separately supplies both buyers and sellers.** The earlier argument no longer establishes that this alternative omits the two named benefits. Further questions concern the significance of the actual interaction, its burdens, other benefits, and the relevant practical circumstances. The text's general diversity argument supplies a type-level reason to resist reducing all activity to one mode. It does not by itself rank these two fully elaborated particular arrangements.

**An alternative is stipulated to preserve every relevant good and complete determining condition.** No such case has been established by the two-beneficiary description. If supplied as a hypothesis, the current passage does not grant an exception allowing a wholly unexplained switch. The source's independent demand for particular determination remains. We must not answer that stronger question merely by repeating that trade has benefits.

This test identifies both the exact explanatory achievement and its boundary. It is not a claim that trade always dominates direct gifts, that the existence of any benefit outweighs every cost, or that the Creator has to employ markets to support people.

## 5. What follows for temporally particular willing

The term “contrastive uniqueness” can hide several demands.

**Determinate effect given the actual complete productive basis.** The inherited source requires this, with voluntary exercise included. The current means argument does not weaken that requirement.

**An intelligible difference relevant to why this rather than the stated alternative.** The source's specification argument at *Majmūʿ* 16:304–305 explicitly reaches quality, measure, and preference. It is not satisfied by renaming occurrence as a reason. The trade comparison meets a narrower contrast of arrangements in one specified respect, not the full temporal determination target.

**A unique normatively permissible alternative derivable from a separately enumerated reason-set before and apart from willing.** Neither the recovered passage nor the assigned reports establishes this. Multiple ways within power are openly discussed. But this is not evidence of fully tied complete reasons or of an indeterministic exception.

**A humanly demonstrated unique entire history from the standing essence alone.** This is neither what the recovered argument delivers nor what the saved reports require. General wise agency and the earned local explanations do not stand or fall with that result.

The source's positive account lies between the proposed extremes in a determinate sense: reasons can be intrinsic and practically operative; useful intermediate activity can have a reason even when another way is within power; actual willing remains part of completed production. A will-less emanation mechanism and a reasonless selector are therefore not the only available descriptions. This structural account is inherited. The recovered example now gives an additional contentful application of its reasons side.

However, the passage does **not** independently warrant a fully relevant love of one newly specified own act at one exact occasion. The prior own-act efficacy result cannot complete the argument by silently adding that antecedent. A generic good of commerce, or a reason for diversity across activity, does not disclose the exact content or occurrence-time of an intrinsic representation. The stronger representation-origin obligation remains open at the precise scope claimed by the saved frontier.

Edited 2:187–188 addresses the opponent's timing/regress objection, allows intrinsically sought ends, and distinguishes temporal succession from regress of producers. Those arguments resist the demand for an endless chain of further instrumental ends or a first temporal act. They do not derive the specific time from the trade example. A good sought for itself need not serve another end; that fact does not make every choice of its exact instantiation self-explanatory.

It should not merge reply 4 with the constitutive-means argument or infer a full selector from either.
