## 1. Experiment, losses and clock

Fix 0<epsilon<=1/4. The hidden parameter alpha is any member of [0,1]. Conditional on alpha, observations are iid Bernoulli(alpha). A common policy issues a report at every index n>=0 after exactly n past bits. It can use an arbitrary measurable private seed R with one common law rho, independent of the entire source stream. Its table Q_n(r,w), for a word w of length n, does not receive alpha as an input. Each seed fibre is measurable. Coherent reports lie in [0,1]; if arbitrary real outputs are permitted, incoherence counts as a failure.

The inherited all-truth excess losses are

    D0(alpha,epsilon,q)=q^2−1/4−alpha epsilon(1+epsilon),
    D1(alpha,epsilon,q)=(1−q)^2−1/4+alpha epsilon(1−epsilon).

A weak repair is coherent and has D0<=0 and D1<=0. A strict repair has both inequalities strict. Let f(alpha,q) be the indicator of weak failure, and let

    C_alpha = E_(rho × Bernoulli(alpha)^N) sum_(n>=0) f(alpha,Q_n(R,word_n)).

This is an extended nonnegative expectation counting each incorrect report once. It is not a sum of numerical excess magnitudes, nor a last-error time. The n=0 report is included.

## 2. Elementary score geometry

At alpha=0 the unique weak repair is q0=1/2: the two square bounds force q<=1/2 and q>=1/2. At alpha=1 the unique weak repair is q1=1/2+epsilon: the bounds are q^2<=(1/2+epsilon)^2 and (1−q)^2<=(1/2−epsilon)^2, and coherence makes the square roots nonnegative. Strict repair is impossible at either endpoint.

For 0<alpha<1, put v=alpha(1−alpha) and q_alpha=1/2+epsilon alpha. Both excesses at q_alpha equal −v epsilon^2<0. If b is an estimate in [0,1], write m=v epsilon^2 and h=epsilon(b−alpha). Direct expansion gives

    D0(alpha,epsilon,1/2+epsilon b)=−m+(1+2alpha epsilon)h+h^2,
    D1(alpha,epsilon,1/2+epsilon b)=−m−(1−2alpha epsilon)h+h^2.

If |b−alpha|<=v epsilon/4, then |h|<=m/4. Here m<=1, h^2<=|h| and each linear coefficient has absolute value at most2. Both losses are at most −m+3|h|<=−m/4<0. The report belongs to [1/2,3/4], so it is coherent.

The endpoint reports fail at every other model on the indicated side:

    D1(alpha,epsilon,q0)=alpha epsilon(1−epsilon)>0 for alpha>0,
    D0(alpha,epsilon,q1)=(1−alpha)epsilon(1+epsilon)>0 for alpha<1.

Positive disturbance is essential. At epsilon=0, q=1/2 weakly repairs everywhere with no errors.

## 3. Inherited pointwise learner and retained sharper upper

The policy q_0=1/2 and q_n=1/2+epsilon times the empirical mean of the first n bits, n>=1, is one common computable rational-prefix policy when epsilon is rational. Its radius, pointwise restoration and finite expected cost were established in the recovered third tranche. They are not new mathematical claims of this successor.

For completeness, finite expectation and the retained ordinary upper follow directly from the exact fourth moment. For n iid centred Bernoulli variables, singleton-index terms vanish, leaving

    E[(empirical_mean−alpha)^4]
       = [n v(1−3v)+3n(n−1)v^2]/n^4
       <= v/n^3+3v^2/n^2.

With r=v epsilon/4, the sufficient score radius and Markov's inequality give a strict-failure probability at most min(1,A/n^3+B/n^2), where

    A=256/(v^3 epsilon^4),  B=768/(v^2 epsilon^4).

Set K=1/(v epsilon^2)>=64 and N=ceil(28K). Sum the first N probabilities by one and the tails by decreasing integral bounds. The expected n>=1 count is at most

    N+A/(2N^2)+B/N
      <= [(28+1/64)+(1/98)+(192/7)]K <56K.

The present n=0 clock therefore gives C_alpha<=1+56/[alpha(1−alpha)epsilon^2] in the interior. This ordinary bound is sharper near the endpoints than the later Hoeffding recheck and is preserved as such. Tonelli justifies summing error probabilities without assuming independent errors across reporting times. Finite expected count implies almost-sure eventual repair; the same argument actually gives eventual strict repair at each fixed interior alpha.

The weak endpoint costs of this clocked policy are (C_0,C_1)=(0,1). Every post-n=0 endpoint report is exact. No strict endpoint guarantee is claimed. On a compact interval [eta,1−eta] with eta>0, its displayed bound is uniform.

## 4. All-policy scaled endpoint obstruction

Assume only that the common policy eventually weakly repairs almost surely at alpha=0. On the hypothetical all-zero history define

    E_N={r : Q_n(r,0^n)=1/2 for every n>=N}.

This is a measurable seed event; E_N increases to full rho measure by endpoint uniqueness and eventual repair. It is not an observable stopping event.

For n>=N, causality forces the same report on E_N and an actual all-zero prefix of length n. This report fails at every alpha>0. Source independence gives

    P_alpha(failure at n)>=rho(E_N)(1−alpha)^n.

Hence, for each fixed N,

    C_alpha>=rho(E_N)(1−alpha)^N/alpha.

First let alpha decrease to zero with N fixed, then increase N. In extended-real form,

    liminf_(alpha down to0) alpha C_alpha>=1.

No finite expected endpoint settling time is assumed. Symmetrically, eventual weak repair at alpha=1 yields

    C_alpha>=rho(F_N) alpha^N/(1−alpha),
    liminf_(alpha up to1) (1−alpha) C_alpha>=1,

where F_N requires the forced endpoint report q1 along every all-one prefix after N.

## 5. Finite-parameter tradeoff for arbitrary policies

If C_0 is finite, set p_n=rho(Q_n(R,0^n)=q0). Endpoint uniqueness gives C_0=sum_n(1−p_n). The same zero-prefix argument, without any eventual-success event, gives

    C_alpha>=sum_n(1−alpha)^n p_n
            =1/alpha−sum_n(1−alpha)^n(1−p_n)
            >=1/alpha−C_0.

Thus C_alpha+C_0>=1/alpha for every alpha>0. There is no infinity-minus-infinity operation: the subtracted series is bounded by the assumed finite C_0. If C_0 is infinite, the sum inequality itself is trivial. Likewise C_alpha+C_1>=1/(1−alpha) when C_1 is finite. These are all-policy literal-count bounds, not bounds limited to the empirical learner.

## 6. Lower semicontinuity removes endpoint-success from unscaled divergence

For fixed q, alpha↦f(alpha,q) is lower semicontinuous: failure is a strict positive excess of a continuous polynomial, or parameter-independent incoherence. A strict violation persists locally; an accepted boundary may have more failures nearby, which is consistent with lower semicontinuity.

For any finite word w, its probability p_alpha(w)=alpha^(ones)(1−alpha)^(zeros) is continuous and nonnegative. Therefore p_alpha(w)f(alpha,Q_n(r,w)) is lower semicontinuous for every fixed r,n,w. If the limiting mass is zero the conclusion follows from nonnegativity; otherwise it follows by continuity of the positive mass.

Fix alpha_j→a. Fatou on the common seed space and finite summation over words and reporting indices gives, for every finite horizon N,

    C_(a,N)<=liminf_j C_(alpha_j,N)<=liminf_j C_(alpha_j).

Taking the supremum over N yields C_a<=liminf_j C_(alpha_j), even when costs are infinite.

At zero, either C_0 is infinite, in which case this lower semicontinuity already forces C_alpha→infinity, or C_0 is finite, in which case the tradeoff C_alpha>=1/alpha−C_0 does so. The endpoint-one proof is symmetric. Consequently:

    Every common always-issued policy has C_alpha→infinity
    as alpha approaches either endpoint through the interior.

No endpoint-success assumption is needed for this unscaled statement. In particular, a uniform finite bound is impossible even on an open or discrete parameter family accumulating at an excluded endpoint. The policy table still defines the hypothetical endpoint experiment. The scaled constants in §4 are kept under their stated endpoint-correctness hypothesis; this proof does not silently extend those constants to arbitrary endpoint-incorrect policies.
