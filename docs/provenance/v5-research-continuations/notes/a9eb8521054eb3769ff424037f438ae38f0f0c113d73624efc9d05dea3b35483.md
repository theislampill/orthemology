# Local common-repair rigidity for quadratic accuracy

Status: ordinary mathematical proof with the general R1 iff now kernel-checked in its original positive-barycentric form and a separate literal intrinsicInterior wrapper; see FORMALIZATION_SCOPE.md for exact coverage. Isolated research, no canonical adoption or global novelty claim.

## R1. Exact directionwise compatibility theorem

Let V be a nonempty finite subset of R^n, C=conv(V), p in relint(C), and T=span(C−p). Let M be a nonempty family of real symmetric positive-definite matrices. Write ||x||_W^2=x^T W x and L_W(b,v)=||b−v||_W^2. Let P_W be the W-orthogonal projection onto T and Q_W=I−P_W. Fix h outside T, and consider the admitted forecast ray b_e=p+e h, e>0. If forecasts are constrained to a separate domain, assume this ray is admitted for all sufficiently small e.

A common coherent weak repair at scale e is one c_e in C such that

    L_W(c_e,v) <= L_W(b_e,v), for every W in M and every v in V.

The following are equivalent:

1. There is a sequence e_k>0 tending to zero at which common coherent weak repairs exist
2. P_W h is independent of W in M
3. For every sufficiently small e>0 the single affine projection c_e=p+e P_W h belongs to C and strictly improves every state under every W in M

The strict improvement in (3) under a given W is exactly e^2 ||Q_W h||_W^2 > 0. There need not be a uniform positive improvement constant over an unbounded/infinite metric family.

### Proof: sufficiency

If all P_W h equal z, relint(C) implies p+e z lies in C for sufficiently small e. Since Q_W h is W-orthogonal to T and c_e−v lies in T,

    ||b_e−v||_W^2 − ||c_e−v||_W^2 = e^2 ||Q_W h||_W^2.

This is positive because h is transverse. Thus (2) implies (3), which implies (1).

### Proof: necessity, barycentric version

Choose strictly positive lambda_v summing to one with p=sum lambda_v v. Such a choice exists because p lies in the relative interior of the convex hull of the finite V, including if V contains redundant points. Fix W_0 in M. Averaging the common-dominance inequalities with these weights gives

    ||c_e−p||_W0^2 <= e^2 ||h||_W0^2.

Hence (c_e−p)/e is bounded. Pass to a convergent subsequence with limit z in T. Divide each statewise gain inequality under any fixed W by e and take the limit:

    <p−v, h−z>_W >= 0, for every v in V.

The strictly positive lambda-weighted sum of the left sides is zero. Each is therefore zero. Since the differences p−v span T, h−z is W-orthogonal to T. Thus z=P_W h for every W. The same subsequence works: its boundedness was obtained with W_0, and the limit can be substituted into each fixed W inequality without a second extraction. Therefore all projections coincide. QED.

## R2. Local universal correctability identifies the normal projection

Requiring common repair for every sufficiently small transverse perturbation at p is equivalent to requiring the projection operators P_W themselves to be identical. The forward direction applies R1 to every transverse h; all P_W already agree on T. The reverse direction is R1. This identifies the normal correction geometry of the coherence set, not the full metric or every comparison of imperfect forecasts.

For the categorical simplex C={x>=0:1^T x=1} with interior p and diagonal W=diag(w_i), w_i>0,

    P_W h = h − (1^T h) r_W,
    (r_W)_i = (1/w_i)/(sum_j 1/w_j).

For any one transverse h (1^T h != 0), P_W h=P_Z h iff r_W=r_Z iff W is a positive scalar multiple of Z. Therefore a family of positive coordinate-weighted quadratic scores permits common repair at arbitrarily small scales along even one fixed transverse ray iff all weight vectors are proportional. Normalizing the weights makes the family a singleton.

This is stronger than the previous return's one fixed no-common-repair example: any distinct normalized weight pair fails at every sufficiently small positive scale along every fixed transverse ray through every interior coherent distribution. The antecedent is also stronger than ordinary orthability: it demands one coherent repair, blind to the unresolved standard, that never worsens any state under any candidate score.

## R3. Quantitative cell bound and approximate tolerance

Let B_T(p,r)={p+t:t in T, ||t||_2<=r} be contained in C with r>0. Fix W, write m_W=lambda_min(W)>0 and q_W=||Q_W h||_W^2. Assume e ||P_W h||_2 <= r/2, and define x_W=p+e P_W h.

Suppose c in C satisfies all approximate dominance constraints

    L_W(c,v) <= L_W(b_e,v)+eta, for every v in V,

where eta>=0 is an additive statewise worsening tolerance. Then

    ||c−x_W||_2 <= (e^2 q_W + eta)/(r m_W).

### Proof

The gain is affine in the truth vector v, so the inequalities extend from V to C. Let delta=c−x_W in T. The W-ball about x_W of radius rho=(r/2)sqrt(m_W) in T is contained in B_T(p,r), hence in C. If delta!=0, evaluate the inequality at v=x_W−rho delta/||delta||_W. Orthogonal decomposition gives

    gain_W(v) = e^2 q_W − 2 rho ||delta||_W − ||delta||_W^2 >= −eta.

Consequently ||delta||_W <= (e^2 q_W+eta)/(2rho), and dividing by sqrt(m_W) proves the claim. The delta=0 case is immediate. QED.

For two metrics W,Z set

    d = ||P_W h−P_Z h||_2,
    A = (q_W/m_W + q_Z/m_Z)/r,
    B = (1/m_W + 1/m_Z)/r.

Under both displayed smallness bounds, a common eta-repair requires

    e d <= e^2 A + eta B.

If d>0, no exact common repair exists whenever 0<e<d/A and the smallness bounds hold. More generally no common eta-repair exists if eta < e(d−e A)/B. These constants are conservative, not asserted optimal.

If eta(e)=o(e), common approximate repairs at a sequence e->0 still force equality of P_W h. Thus the rigidity is not confined to zero numerical tolerance. By contrast eta of order e can mask a fixed projection discrepancy. Tolerances of order e^2 or smaller yield the familiar O(e) bound on admissible projection uncertainty.

## R5. Binary simplex: exact repair cell and norm readout

Let b_e=(1/2+e,1/2), 0<e<=1/4, W_alpha=diag(alpha,1−alpha), 0<alpha<1, and write a coherent repair as (t,1−t). Its metric projection has

    t_alpha=1/2+alpha e,
    D_alpha=alpha(1−alpha)e^2.

D_alpha is the gain at each of the two truth states. For eta>=0 the complete set of coherent eta-repairs is

    [1−sqrt(1/4−alpha e+alpha e^2+eta),
     sqrt(1/4+alpha e+alpha e^2+eta)] intersect [0,1].

No root rounding is needed to decide whether the common cell for alpha in [a,b] is nonempty. Put A=1/4−b e+b e^2+eta and B=1/4+a e+a e^2+eta. It is nonempty iff sqrt(A)+sqrt(B)>=1, equivalently A+B>=1 or 4AB>=(1−A−B)^2. This equivalence uses A,B>=0. The lower endpoint increases with alpha and the upper endpoint also increases with alpha on the stipulated e range, so the extreme constraints come from b and a respectively.

For delta=t−t_alpha, the two statewise inequalities are

    2 t_alpha delta + delta^2 <= D_alpha+eta,
    −2(1−t_alpha)delta + delta^2 <= D_alpha+eta.

If delta>=0 the first bounds it; if delta<0 the second bounds its magnitude. Since t_alpha lies in [1/2,3/4],

    |t−t_alpha| <= 2(D_alpha+eta) <= e^2/2+2eta.

Thus every accepted repair supplies the decoder

    alpha_hat=(t−1/2)/e,
    |alpha_hat−alpha| <= e/2+2eta/e.

This identifies a parameter of the declared scoring model. It does not by itself identify a true norm or establish that the implementing system consciously represents alpha.

For exact repair, any two alpha values separated by more than e have disjoint cells. More sharply, if a=m−d/2, b=m+d/2, eta=0 and A+B<1, cell nonemptiness is equivalent to

    d^2 e^3 −4d e^2 m +2d +4e m^2 −4e m <= 0.

This is simply a polynomial rearrangement of the exact square-root condition; it is not a new premise. At small e it gives the asymptotic admissible width d <= 2m(1−m)e+O(e^3) when m stays away from 0 and 1. The conservative decoder bound is enough for lower-bound transfer without relying on that asymptotic expansion.

## R6. Norm-information consequences and their limit

For a fixed e, suppose a deterministic finite observation partitions a set of candidate alpha values into cells, and one coherent exact repair is chosen for each observation. Every observation cell must have alpha-diameter at most e by R5. For any interval [a,b] of positive length, an observation alphabet of size N therefore requires N>=(b−a)/e (rounding upward when using integer N). This follows because N subsets each of diameter at most e cannot cover an interval of length b−a unless N e>=b−a; measurable cells are not needed, since each is contained in an interval of that length.

Equivalently, an M-point alpha packing whose pairwise distances exceed e needs at least M distinct observable classes, or at least ceil(log2 M) binary messages in a fixed-length deterministic encoding. A channel randomized independently conditional on each alpha still cannot share a positive-probability output between two incompatible alpha values if it promises zero-error repair at every alpha; the zero-error support contract is the relevant one.

A single fixed finite observation partition therefore cannot support exact repair at arbitrarily small e for a continuum of candidate weights. e-dependent finer observations can. This is a real resource constraint, not a necessary-intellect theorem. A physically embodied alpha parameter may be directly coupled into a repair circuit without first producing a symbolic estimate; R5's decoder then describes information in the output, not an asserted conscious calculation inside the device.

## R7. A positive symmetry-plus-correctability norm-fixing corollary

Work on the categorical simplex with positive diagonal quadratic scores, normalize sum_i w_i=1, and let G be a group of coordinate permutations. Suppose the nonempty candidate class K is G-closed: whenever w is admitted, its G-permuted weight vector is admitted in the **same fixed comparison problem**. Suppose one common coherent weak repair exists at arbitrarily small scales along one fixed transverse ray for every candidate in K. R2 makes K a singleton {w}; G-closure then forces w to be constant on each G-orbit. If G is transitive, w_i=1/n for every i.

This is a valid conditional selection of equal-weight Brier geometry. The premises do not mention equal weights. The independently owed premises are the quadratic diagonal score family, coordinate symmetry at the intended semantic scope, and the universal standard-blind repair demand. It is stronger than saying that each score individually supports probabilistic coherence.

Two restrictions prevent overreading:

1. Merely relabeling the entire problem co-transforms weights, forecasts, truth states and outputs. Such covariance does not require one unchanged output to repair all relabelings of a fixed forecast. It therefore does not supply the G-closed *fixed-problem* ambiguity premise used here
2. If G has multiple orbits, relative weights between orbits are not fixed. Semantic asymmetries between propositions may also make the alleged permutations inadmissible. Duplicate coordinates or differences in what is being represented require separate treatment rather than an automatic uniform-weight rule

The result fixes a relative scoring geometry inside the stipulated class. It does not confer normative authority on that class or infer original intellectual hosting of the selected norm.
