## Finding

Its differential comparison core is a **direct specialization of an explicit2003 theorem**, with an elementary finite-coordinate extension, rather than merely an unresolved resemblance to quasi-arithmetic means.

## 1. Exact primary predecessor

Zsolt Páles, Remark5 in *Report of Meeting: The Second Debrecen–Katowice Winter Seminar on Functional Equations and Inequalities*, Annales Mathematicae Silesianae16(2003), printed pp.97–99. Primary PDF: https://journals.us.edu.pl/index.php/AMSIL/article/download/14111/10836/40243 . Printed97 gives the theorem; printed98–99 its proof; the remark is signed Zs.Páles on99. I checked extracted text and rendered scans of97 and99.

For C² increasing generators with positive derivatives, it characterizes comparison of two-variable means M_f,g=(f+g)^−1(f(x)+g(y)) by equality of the two derivative ratios F′/f′ and G′/g′, with that common ratio nondecreasing. Its sufficiency proof sums the corresponding supporting-line inequalities. Its necessity proof differentiates the difference of the means at the diagonal minimum.

An initial source warning sent before inspecting the later pages was incorrect: the theorem is indeed inside the meeting-report PDF, not a mismatched search result. This correction was sent promptly to both author and parent and is preserved here.

## 2. Literal substitution into the repair problem

For an ordered pair of scores k,l, define the endpoint losses

    A_ki(t)=Dψki(0,t),      B_li(t)=Dψli(1,t).

The common-repair requirement includes L_l(t)≤U_k(t), where L_l is generated by the increasing coordinate functions −B_li and U_k by A_ki. These are exactly the means in Páles's theorem, not analogous statistical objects.

For two coordinates substitute

    f=−B_l1, g=−B_l2, F=A_k1, G=A_k2.

Their derivative ratios are

    A_ki′/(−B_li′) = [t/(1−t)] [ψki″/ψli″].

Equality of these ratios across coordinates is exactly rank-one Hessian compatibility. Writing ψki″=r_k w_i, the ratio becomes t r_k/[(1−t)r_l]. Its logarithmic derivative is

    1/[t(1−t)] + (log(r_k/r_l))′.

Nondecrease therefore gives the ordered half of the score criterion. Reversing the ordered score pair gives the absolute bound. Thus the two-coordinate global coherent-endpoint condition is a direct specialization.

For n>2, impose input tuples with coordinatei equalx and all remaining coordinates equaly. The n-variable mean then becomes a two-variable mean generated by f_i and the sum of its remaining generators. The published theorem forces F_i′/f_i′=Σ_{j≠i}F_j′/Σ_{j≠i}f_j′. Rearrangement gives F_i′/f_i′=Σ_jF_j′/Σ_jf_j′ for everyi, hence one common ratio. Its monotonicity follows from any grouped comparison. Conversely, the same supporting-line proof sums over finitely many coordinates. This is an elementary extension of the printed result.

The author's every-baseline/every-transverse-ray subsequence premise supplies the same first- and second-order diagonal constraints. Turning that local premise into global endpoint repair is useful in the programme, but uses the established differential characterization rather than a new mean-comparison principle. Arbitrary families are handled by one-dimensional interval intersection; this should not be advertised as a new general Helly theorem.

Matkowski2013, *Generalized weighted quasi-arithmetic means and the Kolmogorov–Nagumo theorem*, §5 Remark3, printed45–46, is a further exact framework reference: https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/87225 . It identifies general-coordinate mean comparison with a convexity-type inequality. Páles2003 is the closer differential ancestor.

Konek's2023 binary common-nudge curvature criterion remains important score-specific ancestry, but does not supersede the older literal mean comparison. The endpoint/nudge distinction alone cannot support novelty of the exact two-coordinate differential core.

## 4. Anchor-only compatibility really fails

Take base curvature1 and two score ratios r₊(t)=t/(1−t), r₋(t)=(1−t)/t. Each has the permitted log-slope magnitude against the anchor. Their mutual log-slope is twice the permitted value.
