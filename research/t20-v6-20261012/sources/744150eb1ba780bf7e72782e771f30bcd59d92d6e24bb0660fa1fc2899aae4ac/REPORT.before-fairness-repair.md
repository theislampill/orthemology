# Repentance and the occurrence of a specified original-side response

10 October 2026 UTC. Bounded source-guided inference test. Separate research evidence only; no protected edit, integration, new tranche, or closure.

## Result

**Ibn al-Qayyim supplies an explicit actual-condition constraint on one specified response attributed to the original: rejoicing over a servant's repentance.** At edited *Shifāʾ* 2:208, the occurrence of “this rejoicing” depends on repentance, and repentance on something repented of. The author immediately counts provision of the conditions among wisdom. This is not inferred from the grammatical tense of a self-praise quotation.

The bounded application is affirmative: **a description that substitutes advance cognition, generic capacity, or the mere approval of repentance for the actual repentance does not supply the condition of this source-specified response.** The same source denies that creatures supply the original's perfection and locates their provision within its creative activity. The supported account therefore contains a genuine condition-sensitive original-side response, without converting the condition into an independent donor of essential power, knowledge, or wisdom.

This is a particular application of earned distinctions. Divine joy without creaturely replenishment was already appraised in the Jāmiʿ reading; achieved response without newly acquired knowledge was already worked through in CP23. The additional contribution is the explicit *original-side response* dependency test at this named target. No fresh doctrine, general no-receipt proof, exact timestamp, new representation requirement, or voluntary-act/volition-token individuation is established.

## 2. The direct source argument and its voice

The source is Ibn al-Qayyim, *Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl*, edited by Zāhir b. Sālim Bālfāqīh, volume two, second edition 1441/2019, Dār ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm / Dār Ibn Ḥazm. The retained Word-derived text and page-image PDF are presentations of this same publication. [Official catalogue](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/books/shfa-alaalyl-fy-msael-alkda-oalkdr-oal-59); [PDF witness](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/media/download/140).

Face 18 at 208 discusses forms of creaturely activity that require relevant objects and conditions. Face 19 then changes the bearer explicitly: “He, exalted, rejoices over His servant's repentance when he repents to Him.” It says:

> وحصول هذا الفرح موقوف على التوبة، الموقوفة على وجود ما يتاب منه

The occurrence of this rejoicing depends on repentance, which depends on the existence of what is repented of. The next clauses say that a dependent consequence cannot occur without what it requires, then connect the excellence of this rejoicing to wisely providing its conditions. This is the author's endorsed response, not an opponent's premise. The page image at printed/PDF 208 was directly checked; its wording supports the retained text.

The following prophetic report concerns wrongdoing, seeking forgiveness and forgiveness; a dream anecdote follows at 209. Neither is a newly authenticated historical premise here. The direct authorial dependency statement is not silently presented as the wording of that prophetic report. The dream carries no independent evidential weight in this inference.

The adjacent discussion makes strong claims about historical hardship and greater goods. The narrow inference does not adopt those balances, authorize wrongdoing to obtain repentance, or infer that every actual wrong is uniquely necessary. Conditions of a specified good do not establish that every way of producing those conditions is permissible or required.

Two source controls determine the metaphysical use. At 184–185 the author rejects reducing divine wisdom and attributes to creaturely need, explicitly saying that creatures receive their perfection from God rather than supplying His. At 200 the governance argument says the relevant created conditions are all from Him and depend in existence on His willing and creation. These statements are inherited substantive premises of the account, not conclusions derived solely from the word “condition.”

## 3. Executed dependency test

Fix the modest target J: **the source-described rejoicing over the servant's actual repentance**, in the respect in which the author expressly makes its occurrence dependent on that repentance. Let R identify the repentance independently: the servant actually repents of the relevant wrongdoing. R is not defined as “whatever produces J.”

1. The target is explicitly predicated of the original, whereas R is the servant's real response. Neither is a mere human recitation about the other.
2. The source identifies R as required for J. An explanation of J cannot delete R while claiming to preserve the exact source-specified response and all its operative conditions.
3. Foreknowledge of R does not perform R for the servant. The prior achieved-end analysis already establishes that distinction. Mere common capacity to produce many effects and general approval of repentance likewise are not the occurrence of R.
4. The author does not leave the conditions outside original provision: providing the relevant conditions is itself expressly treated as wise. The common-source premise includes the creature's genuine response without making the creature an independently original supplier.
5. Thus the account positively permits this specified original-side response to have an actual creaturely condition while retaining unreceived essential perfection. The condition is genuinely relevant, rather than an idle illustration; its role is not automatically whole-existence reception or acquisition of the essential faculty.

The discrimination is narrow but concrete. An account offering only “the repentance is known,” “the source can produce repentance,” or “repentance is approved,” while omitting the servant's actual repentance as the condition, has not yet accounted for this particular response in the source's sense. An account retaining the actual response and original provision satisfies that dependency requirement. It is not thereby a complete efficient explanation of the intrinsic response.

This is a premise-substitution test, not a metaphysically possible world in which infallible knowledge falsely anticipates an event that never occurs. We do not preserve a fully factive knowledge claim while deleting its truth. Nor do we assert that knowledge of a future event is knowledge of its present completion.

## 4. What kind of dependence follows?

**Actual object/condition dependence:** explicit. This rejoicing is not explained with the relevant repentance missing. The passage has more force than a bare label saying an outcome is loved.

**A complete efficient mechanism supplied by the servant:** not established. The author gives a required condition, not an account of its efficient production of an intrinsic attribute. R is not asserted to be sufficient by itself. No transmission of power from the servant to the original is stated.

**Receipt of essential perfection:** denied by the account's explicit surrounding premises. This denial is not inferred just from the fact that the original created the condition. A source's producing an object would not, by itself, prove that every conceivable later relation to it is nonreceptive. The no-receipt conclusion uses the inherited distinction between essential adequacy and this conditioned exercise, supported by the author's express denial of creaturely supply.

**Exact temporal individuation:** not supplied. “When he repents” plus the dependency assertion gives an occasion/content qualification, but no temporal metric, response latency, duration, cardinality, or one-to-one divine token inventory. We do not infer a universal prohibition on every anticipatory joy concerning a foreknown good. Such anticipation would need its own content and source warrant; it cannot simply replace the actual-conditioned response named here.

**A voluntary divine act:** not newly established. The passage's predicate is rejoicing. Calling it an original-side response preserves that predicate without identifying it with willing, an act of producing forgiveness, or a particular new representation. The separate own-act love/efficacy doctrine is not automatically transported to every attributed response.

## 5. Strongest objection and positive force

The strongest surviving objection says that genuine new rejoicing is itself an acquired intrinsic perfection, so its dependence on actual repentance remains inconsistent with perfect independence even if the repentance is divinely provided. This is stronger than denying that the source created the condition.

The present application does not refute every metaphysics of change by definition. It identifies the exact additional premise the objection needs: **every condition-sensitive realization of a responsive excellence must count as receipt of a previously missing essential perfection.** The source explicitly refuses that promotion and gives this actual-condition case within its defended distinction between intrinsic adequacy and fitting realizations. The existing necessity and essential-independence judgments remain controlling; no mere imaginable rival earns parity with them.

Conversely, a source-faithful defender cannot answer by making R unreal, by reducing rejoicing to the bare assertion that the source knows a proposition, or by claiming that every creaturely condition is irrelevant. Those replies erase the very dependency the primary argument affirms. The constructive account must retain both the real response condition and original unreceived adequacy.

Relative to CP23's almsgiving example, the target here is an expressly original-side response rather than an angel's changed directive. Relative to self-praise, the condition is expressly stated rather than extracted from tense. Relative to conditional beneficiary rights, the consequent is this named response, not an entitlement. These differences justify the bounded application; they do not create a new general principle.
