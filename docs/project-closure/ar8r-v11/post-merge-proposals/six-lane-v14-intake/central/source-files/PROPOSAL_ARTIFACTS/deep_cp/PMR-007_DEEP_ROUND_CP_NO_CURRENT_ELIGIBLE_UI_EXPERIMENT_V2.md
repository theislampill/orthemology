## Theorem CP-1 — all-finite common-policy parity

Assume `U` and `I` have the same initial neutral-state law and the same one-step
transition/output kernel `K_0` for every registered intervention in `J`.
Then, for every finite horizon and every common-history adaptive policy, the
complete declared output-history law is the same in `U` and `I`.

### Proof

Proceed by induction on the horizon. At horizon zero, the initial neutral-state
laws agree. Suppose the joint laws of neutral state and declared output history
agree through time `t`. Because the policy is a common function of the declared
history, it chooses the same conditional distribution over interventions in
both architectures. Applying the same conditional transition/output kernel
`K_0` therefore yields the same joint law at time `t+1`. Induction gives equality
for every finite horizon. ∎

1. **Self-model proxy control.** Both architectures respond identically to the
   `self_model_token`, while de-se ownership differs. Behavioral center-token
   sensitivity is not de-se ownership.
2. **Declared-fittingness control.** Both architectures respond identically to
   a carried fittingness-status bit while objective fittingness and
   because-of-fittingness uptake remain unsettled.
3. **Source-eligibility control.** Version and authority validity determine a
   declared source-eligibility report without establishing source truth,
   referent, actual-world applicability, or personality.
4. **Architecture-specific intervention control.** An operation available only
   in `U` may define a changed experiment, but it is not a common intervention
   until a target-independent typed implementation exists in `I`.
5. **Target-oracle control.** A direct architecture or personality readout
   trivially separates the pair but supplies no independent evidence.
6. **Expanded-interface exit.** An additional sensor or independently warranted
   internal measurement may refine the experiment fibre; CP-1 does not bar it.
