## Voices and argument

Printed p550 introduces the second, personal route illustrated by Heraclius. Pages551–554 report his questions and interleave Ibn Taymiyya's explanation. The questions concern previous royal ancestry, a predecessor making the same claim, prior lying, character and persistence of followers, wartime reversals, treachery, and the content of teaching. The contextual account also says other informants could contradict a false answer (p550), but that arrangement is not a modern independence experiment.

At p551 the account connects absence of royal ancestry and a prior claimant with ordinary motives of dynastic restoration and imitation. The author expressly admits that the unusual alternative can occur. At p552 he explains why stable prior honesty bears on the exceptional alleged lie, while allowing a person to depart from individual habit toward broader human habit. These admissions are important: the source weighs against alternatives; it does not deductively eliminate every lie or every possible unmentioned motive.

Pages553–558 interpret persistence, expansion, trials, and deliverance in the author's theological account of prophets and believers. They are substantive interpretive premises, not independent empirical laws newly established here. Pages559–560 link treachery with falsehood and fit of teaching with prophetic character. At p560 the author explicitly affirms that this inquiry yielded certain knowledge to the intelligent inquirer. He then reports al-Maziri and similar critics as denying that prophecy can be known this way and restricting recognition to a miracle. This is Ibn Taymiyya's report of their objection, not a fresh examination of al-Maziri's own book.

At p561 Ibn Taymiyya rejects that restriction and appeals to competent, sound recognition of how the inquiry distinguishes truthful from false claimants. He states that a collection can produce what some components do not produce alone. He illustrates cumulative effects with ordinary experiential cases and says one report gives a kind of supposition, another strengthens it, and knowledge is eventually reached. This is affirmative epistemic doctrine deployed in defense of the particular inquiry. It is not only an external commentator's analogy.

The main p561 reading includes the negative in 'قد لا يستقل بعضها به'. Footnote3 reports a positive variant in some witnesses. No manuscript collation was performed; the surrounding joint-effect examples and report-strengthening discussion support the cumulative reading independently of presenting this phrase as undisputed text.

## Proof strength and credit

Four levels must stay separate:

1. General doctrine: some acquired knowledge results from mutually strengthening grounds whose individual contributions need not suffice.
2. Particular source rationale: the Heraclius inquiry gathers content-sensitive marks, compares candidate explanations, and assesses conduct and teaching. The author claims knowledge results in the narrated case.
3. Formal model: a stipulated relation named jointly sufficient, or a toy countermodel establishing possible synergy, neither supplies nor validates the substantive historical premises above. Conversely, source argument does not prove an arbitrary formal sufficiency relation adequate.
4. Actual warrant: this investigation does not independently authenticate the reported event, all answers, the relevant comparative generalizations, or any present knower's actual basing. The source's affirmative claim is real, but citing it does not accomplish a new historical recognition episode.

No numerical confidence, probabilistic independence, minimum report count, universal threshold, or reliability certification of all faculties follows. Multiple questions put through one exchange are not thereby independent acquisition sources. Genuine cumulative discrimination can be content-dependent and non-numerical. It need not have a universal algorithm to have rational force; absence of such an algorithm is not a defeater.

## Remaining application obligations

For a claimed new application, establish:

- The particular conclusion and which considerations actually support it.
- Adequate warrant for the reported or observed inputs. A modern reader's transmitted access differs from the narrated inquirer's position; the needed transmission cannot be borrowed entirely from the authority under test.
- Why these contents, in this setting, favor the conclusion and weaken relevant alternatives. Counting agreeing signs is not this explanation.
- The actual dependencies among grounds: repeated report, shared informant, common theological premise, shared causal understanding, and dependence on the disputed source are relevant distinctions. Independence is neither to be assumed nor universally required.
- Why remaining relevant alternatives or warranted defeating considerations do not defeat the claimed level of support; no requirement to eliminate every merely imaginable scenario is added.
- Which grounds the recipient actually possesses and uses. A later researcher's reconstructed cumulative case does not retrospectively establish that an earlier assent was warranted or repair its acquisition history.
- Scope proportionate to the argument. Authentication of a claimant, reliability across all claims, universal sourcehood, and unrestricted attributes are different targets requiring their retained grounds.

These are analytical application conditions, not an extra reflective certification duty imposed on ordinary knowers. Existing affirmative appraisals are neither reset nor automatically increased.
