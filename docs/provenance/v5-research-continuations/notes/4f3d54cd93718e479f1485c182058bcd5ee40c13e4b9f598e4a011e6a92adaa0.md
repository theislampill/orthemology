## The precise characterization

`BregmanProjection.universal_common_repair_iff_affine` is a theorem about a finite categorical outcome set of cardinality at least three and two additive coordinate-generator score families. A `SmoothScalar.Score` supplies f, f', f'' with:

- continuity of f on [0,1]
- first and second derivatives on (0,1)
- continuity and strict positivity of f'' on (0,1)

The literal loss is Bregman divergence with truth in the **first** argument and forecast in the **second**. No smoothness of derivatives at the endpoints is assumed.

For one fixed outcome i0, the repair property is quantified over **every interior coherent p**. It asserts the existence, at each p, of one positive sequence epsilon tending to zero and one sequence of **interior coherent** c, shared by both scores, such that every truth-vertex loss is weakly improved relative to p + epsilon e_i0. There is no assumed Hessian compatibility, localization, normalized-repair limit or projection oracle in this premise.

This is equivalent to one **global positive scalar** a and coordinate-specific affine terms b_i x+d_i relating the two generators for every x in the **closed interval** [0,1]. The closed-interval conclusion concerns generator equality. It does not extend the repair premise to boundary forecasts. A common arbitrary perturbation radius or a computable projection algorithm is not part of the statement.

## Necessity proof audit

1. A positive barycentric average of the actual vertex inequalities gives a Bregman-regret bound at p. Nonnegativity allows each coordinate divergence to be bounded by total divergence
2. Vanishing scalar divergence forces convergence to p using monotonicity outside every smaller interior interval. This genuinely avoids an unjustified bounded-endpoint-derivative assumption
3. Local curvature lower and upper bounds, applied after localization, derive bounded normalized repairs (c-p)/epsilon
4. Finite-dimensional compactness supplies one subsequence. Every fixed family member can be tested against its same limit, so no illicit diagonal selection over an uncountable family occurs
5. Varying-direction differentiability yields first-order inequalities. Their positive-weighted barycentric sum is zero, so they vanish and give a shared Hessian-normal direction
6. The vertex direction is transverse to the simplex tangent. The residual is nonzero. Positivity of the diagonal Hessians forces proportional coordinate curvature vectors at each tested p
7. With at least three genuine outcomes, any two positive coordinates whose sum is below one can be filled out by the other positive coordinates. A spare coordinate connects all one-variable curvature ratios, forcing one common global scalar
8. Integrating equality of second derivatives twice gives affine differences on (0,1). Stipulated continuity extends equality to [0,1]

The general quadratic `LocalRigidity.local_common_repair_iff` is also sound as stated: a positive barycentric certificate, finite dimension, positive-definite symmetric forms and transversality give the simultaneous-normal characterization. The radial convex-hull accessibility is proved by explicit adjusted barycentric weights. The result is not a general boundary tangent-cone theorem, and its statement uses the positive-barycentric formulation rather than a literal intrinsic-interior API.

## Sufficiency proof audit

The code minimizes the correct objective **D(c,b)** in its first argument over the compact simplex. Existence follows from compactness and continuity, not a supplied minimizer function. As b tends to interior p, any minimizer cluster point must be p, using strict positivity of D(c,p). Thus minimizers eventually lie in the interior.

An interior simplex line variation yields the tangent first-order condition. The three-point Bregman identity plus nonnegativity gives all-vertex weak repair. The sequences are shifted beyond their common interior-entry time. Positive affine generator equivalence scales both score differences, giving the same repair under both families. This is a classical existence proof, not an executable minimization or uniform-complexity result. The final formal theorem promises weak improvement; no stronger strict statement is inferred solely from its displayed type.

## Sharp controls

- Unequal coordinatewise quadratic scalings are not one global affine equivalence. The review constructs valid smooth positive-curvature scores with weights (1,2,2) versus (1,1,1), proves non-equivalence, then proves absence of universal common repairs through the checked iff
- Nonetheless each score separately has its own local repair sequences. A compiled witness theorem shows why replacing the shared candidate by separate score-specific candidates destroys the intended content
- Zero perturbation makes simultaneous weak repair vacuous. The fixed transverse direction is substantive
- On a binary simplex, the nonconstant ratio functions r_false(x)=1+x and r_true(x)=2-x agree at every coherent pair. A compiled counterexample blocks the ratio-connectivity step below three outcomes
- Exact arithmetic checks confirm the binary arithmetic repair simultaneously improves quadratic losses and the two logarithmic products. Entropy/quadratic curvature ratios differ at 1/2 and 1/4, illustrating the non-affine binary exception. The finite checks are not presented as a new general Lean entropy theorem
- The nonseparable forms Q(x,y,z)=x²+y²+z² and R=Q+(x-y)² are positive definite and nonproportional but have identical simplex-normal projection. Exact gain checks show why additive/diagonal structure cannot be silently dropped
- Curvature agreement at just one p does not imply global generator equivalence. A polynomial curvature comparison gives a direct counterexample to that weakened inference
- Reversing the Bregman arguments changes even elementary cubic values: 1/2 versus 5/8. An affine generator has identically zero divergence, showing the content of strict curvature positivity

The retained source mutations attempt to admit only two outcomes, replace the transverse disturbance by zero, reverse the Bregman orientation, or weaken strict positive curvature to nonnegative curvature. All are rejected by the original proof chain. The positive counterexamples above explain the mathematical boundaries; rejection alone is not treated as proof of a converse impossibility.
