# Review of the modal actualisation lemma

9 October 2026. Independent, bounded review of the proposed existential actualisation step and its restricted-target diagnostic. The inspected source is Gale and Pruss, *A new cosmological argument* (1999), printed pp. 462–465. The full source and reviewed draft are hash-bound in INPUT_HASHES.json. No assessment of the rest of the cosmological argument, the 2002 reply, or a metaphysical rival is made here.

## Verdict

The conditional lemma is valid as stated in ACTUALISATION_AUDIT.md. It usefully isolates the explanation-status completeness needed for the existential conclusion. It supplies no independent warrant for weak PSR, the existence of the complete proposition, the relevant modal possibilities, or any causal or theological identification of the explanation.

The three-world restricted-target diagnostic preserves the reduced local assumptions other than the explicitly removed completeness condition. It is not a countermodel to Gale and Pruss's complete-target argument. No correction to the derivation is required. The qualifications below should remain attached to its use.

## Exact logical load

Fix an actual point a in a global domain W, one proposition P, and the proposition H_P that this P has an explanation. Suppose some v in W satisfies H_P, and H_P is factive in its explanandum. If H_P fails at a, classical negation yields actual ¬H_P. Since H_P is true at v, ¬H_P is contingent across the same domain. If P includes every actual contingent truth of this kind, P entails ¬H_P. Factivity gives P at v, hence ¬H_P at v, contradicting H_P there. Therefore H_P holds at a.

This requires the following precise commitments:

- **One explanandum across evaluations.** P designates the same proposition at a and v. Replacing it with each world's own total contingent fact changes the argument. Fixed reference for H_P also requires one interpretation of explanation and of its existential quantifier. The existential conclusion does not require an individually fixed explanans q or a constant domain of possible explainers.
- **Classical truth where used.** At a, failure of H_P must yield truth of its negation; at v, H_P must exclude ¬H_P. A gap or inconsistency about explanation status blocks these steps. Full classical logic at every other world is stronger than this proof uses.
- **Actuality belongs to the modal comparison domain.** The contingency claim uses actual truth of ¬H_P and its falsity at the witness. In a relative-accessibility semantics this must be reconciled with the modal domain used by the completeness premise. Merely shifting to a different agent-relative or world-relative sense of possibility is not licensed.
- **Closure over explanation status.** H_P and ¬H_P must be eligible propositions in the language or proposition domain over which completeness is asserted. The existence of a well-defined total proposition containing its own contingent explanation-status truths is assumed, not constructed by the proof. Predicative, typed, or subject-matter restricted totalities require their own check.
- **Truth inheritance from the total fact.** Truth of P must give truth of the included ¬H_P. This is ordinary conjunction elimination or semantic entailment. It is not explanation-distribution, and the proof does not infer that explaining P explains each conjunct.
- **A possible explanatory witness for this target.** The required weak-PSR input is the local instance for P. A general W-PSR yields that instance only if it genuinely ranges over P. Factivity in the explanans is compatible with the proof but not needed for its contradiction; explanandum factivity is essential.

The original text supplies comprehensive contingent-proposition maximality on p. 462, fixes the actual target and explicitly includes contingent acts of necessary beings on p. 463, and derives world identity using bivalence and maximality on p. 464. The explanation-distribution reasoning on p. 465 belongs to a later stage. The reconstructed argument separates these roles correctly.

## What has and has not been weakened

The minimum relevant completeness condition is: if ¬H_P is actually true and contingent, all P-worlds satisfy it. This is weaker than requiring P to determine every contingent truth or individuate a world. P-worlds may agree on H_P while differing on another proposition.

Consequently, the reconstructed proof does not invoke world identity or the modal axiom characteristic of S5. This is a statement about the proof's dependencies. It does not independently vindicate a global domain of metaphysical possibilities, and it does not show that the original complete-world ontology has been justified with weaker metaphysical assumptions. If full completeness is retained to warrant the narrow closure premise, its substantive burden remains.

The proof establishes actual existential explanation. It does not identify the actual witness with a previously named possible q. The draft correctly separates a stronger fixed-q reconstruction, which needs stable q-reference and closure over the corresponding explanatory proposition.

## Explicit check of the restricted-target diagnostic

Let W = {a,b,c}, with a actual. Let the fixed created-order target F be true at a and b and false at c. Let T be a tautological proposition. Interpret a fixed explanation relation so that E(T,F) is true at b and false at a and c, with no other explanatory witness for F. Then H_F is true exactly at b.

This realizes the draft's truth assignments as an existential explanation-status proposition. It preserves classical truth, fixed F-reference, a fixed interpretation of explanation, actual truth and contingency of F, a possible explanation of F, and both explanans and explanandum factivity. It lacks actual explanation of F. The actual contingent proposition ¬H_F is true at a and c but false at b, so F does not entail it. The negative-explanation completeness premise fails exactly where expected.

Relative to the stipulated created-order vocabulary generated by F, a and b can have the same complete created-order description while differing over the explanation-status fact outside that vocabulary. This is a logical interpretation of the reduced assumptions, not proof that this arrangement is metaphysically possible or that every intended created/received-order target excludes explanation facts. If such a target does entail the needed explanation-status truth, the lemma applies to it too.

This diagnostic verifies only the local possible-explanation instance for F. It makes no claim to preserve unrestricted W-PSR, the later constraints on acceptable explanations, or all of Gale and Pruss's premises. If P_a is the complete actual contingent fact, it includes ¬H_F; b cannot satisfy P_a. Thus the diagnostic leaves the original complete-target conclusion untouched.

## Use in the current inquiry

The clarification identifies an exact loss when replacing complete contingent truth with a narrower created or received field: closure over contingent explanation-status truths is no longer automatic. It does not show that a restricted target must fail, or that including a contingent divine act makes the act a created substance or a productive cause of its bearer. Those claims require separate premises. The sound result here is a conditional formal clarification with an explicit scope boundary, not a new metaphysical warrant or a closure of the broader inquiry.
