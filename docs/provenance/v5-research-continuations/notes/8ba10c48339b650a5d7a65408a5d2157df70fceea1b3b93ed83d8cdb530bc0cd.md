## Independence from complete coverage

Coverage R g x means R g x and, for every y with R y x, y=g or R g y. R is read as strict actual productive ancestry in one fixed existential respect. Transitivity and irreflexivity imply that g has no R-predecessor.

The statement contains no WellFounded assumption and no direct roothood assumption. It does contain a coverage premise stronger than merely being one ancestor. It does not prove that a covering g exists. The target's complete productive history must be defended if R includes endpoint-redundant actual contributors; endpoint-sufficiency alone is too weak.

The modal composition then adds explicit actual existence of g, the unchanged actual contingent-reception principle, and generic essential reception. It concludes UniformRoot of this same g over the declared world type. Source interpretation and metaphysical completeness of that world type remain substantive.

## Countably infinite controls

The rootless ancestry uses the natural numbers with R(y,x) iff x<y. Every member has a predecessor x+1; the relation is irreflexive and transitive. No member covers any target's whole ancestry.

The contingent version has two worlds. All natural-number bearers exist at true and none at false. Dependencies have existing endpoints. Every actual contingent member receives; generic essential reception holds; there is no actual root. No finite cutoff or missing first member is used.

The necessary version makes every natural-number bearer exist at every world, while each receives productively in the declared relation. There is still no root. Thus necessary existence of individuals is not independent sourcehood.

The rooted version adjoins one bearer, represented by none, to the natural-number chain. It is upstream of each member and covers the ancestry of some0. Yet the relation is not well-founded: Acc fails at every subordinate some n. Independence of an original source does not imply that every subordinate chain is well-founded.

These are formal counterinterpretations of proposed entailments. Assigning R a productive label does not prove that such an infinite causal reality is metaphysically realizable. Conversely, the modal premises alone cannot refute them.

## Finite controls

There are two disjoint productive components, each with an actual source and its own effect. Each source covers its local target; neither source is a common producer of both effects. This tests local-to-global coverage, not the accepted full productive uniqueness theorem.

A separate sole-bearer fixture exists at the actual world but is absent at the alternative, with no productive predecessor. Actual roothood without contingent-reception does not entail necessity.
