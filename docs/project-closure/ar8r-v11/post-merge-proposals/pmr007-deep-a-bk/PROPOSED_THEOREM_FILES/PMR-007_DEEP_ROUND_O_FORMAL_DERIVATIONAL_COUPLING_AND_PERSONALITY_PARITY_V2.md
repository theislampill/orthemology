## 4. GUPP-1B — neutral-reduct comparison theorem

Let `Red_0(H)` be the neutral reduct of an architecture, and let a comparison
map

\[
Q(H)
\]

factor through neutral-reduct isomorphism classes. That is, whenever
`Red_0(H_1) ≅ Red_0(H_2)`, one has `Q(H_1)=Q(H_2)`.

Since `Red_0(H_I) ≅ Red_0(H_P)`, every such `Q` ties the two expansions. Strict
preference for `H_P` requires evidence or a criterion that does **not** factor
through the common reduct.

This theorem does not claim that every legitimate comparison is neutral-reduct
invariant. It identifies exactly where a parity-breaking premise must enter.
