# Activating a previously hidden coordinate can split the same full moment record

An ordinary source-derived countercontrol, not a new kernel theorem or field-priority claim. Controlling inputs: the exact Stieltjes/Gaussian pair and signed-projection classification in `047ec278a738cdc018b7118113afa93a8fa6d0aa1a315d33440f2aa0586a53ea`, §§5–7; the full-dimensional cumulative determinacy proof `a530a5341497e3416345a7ec325e986a7e54f2c254954f80fd32f1f596e6244f`. Both bodies are fully read in this packet.

Let z∈R^r, r≥1, and y∈R. Let ν± be the two distinct lognormal Stieltjes laws with densities f(y)[1±(1/2)sin(2π log y)] on y>0, and let η±=(ν±+γ₁)/2. They have identical moments of every nonnegative integer degree, are distinct, and have all absolute polynomial moments. Put μ±=γ_r⊗η±.

For any supplied controlled-only SOS schedule satisfying the cumulative positive radial certificate in z, the laws μ± generate exactly the same entire polynomial hierarchy. Indeed each signed controlled projection is the same measure γ_r multiplied by the common j-th moment of η±. At any chosen time s>0 their actual tilted laws are ρ±=ζ_s⊗η±, where ζ_s is the common normalized controlled Gaussian-killed law. They therefore have the same full moment record at s, while remaining distinct laws.

Now permit a new post-s operation that was outside the old schedule class: damping by y² for a duration ε>0. The resulting normalized laws are

ρ±,ε = ζ_s ⊗ [exp(−εy²)η± / Z±(ε)].

These laws are distinct. If their hidden factors were equal, multiplying by the strictly positive reciprocal likelihood would give η+/Z+=η−/Z−; taking total masses would imply Z+=Z− and then η+=η−, a contradiction. The inverse multiplication is legitimate here because each side is the explicitly supplied positive tilt of a probability.

Both resulting joint laws have a finite positive Gaussian exponential moment in every coordinate. The old cumulative certificate gives one in z, and the new exp(−εy²) factor gives one in y at any rate b≤ε. Product structure combines the bounds. Hence their joint full moment records are determinate. If those records agreed, the two measures would coincide, contradicting the preceding paragraph. Therefore some finite-degree polynomial has different values after the new operation.

Conclusion: the same complete present moment record, and even the same complete prior moment path under every allowed z-only schedule, does not determine the result of subsequently exposing a hidden coordinate to damping. The old signed-projection equivalence is a congruence for the declared fixed-controlled-subspace operations; it need not remain one after the operation family expands. This is a concrete ordinary instance of the corpus's continuation/sufficient-record boundary. It does not challenge the proved uniqueness of moment continuation under the original fixed-subspace schedule, nor claim an explicit uniform distinguishing degree or computational reconstruction algorithm.
