# Intrinsic power and the modal conditions of original existence

## Result and question

A productive account of possibility can supply a valid conditional argument for the necessary existence of the same actual original bearer. Its strongest form is substantially different from complete determination of an effect. It says that an absolute possibility which is not actual must be productively realisable, and it applies that claim to existence qualified by its whole origin. The argument then excludes a world wholly lacking the bearer without asking for a cause of absence or assuming a first temporal event.

The new result is a precise comparison of two such bridges. An object-level bridge needs essential unreceivability and sufficient modal reach. An exact-origin-fact bridge avoids those particular assumptions but requires stronger productive coverage. Under the fixed actual fact and reverse accessibility, its target-specific coverage condition is equivalent to the desired necessary-existence conclusion. This identifies exactly where a general, independently warranted theory of possibility could advance the case, and why its targeted restatement cannot do so by itself.

New primary arguments also support a concrete intrinsic-power proposal and a distinct material-grounding proposal. Neither presently supplies the missing actual warrant for the stronger productive law, this bearer's indispensable modal-ground identity, or its unconditional intrinsic instantiation. Necessary existence and adequate intrinsic explanation must still be distinguished. N2 and N3 consequently remain open, with new conditional results and a sharper epistemic discriminator rather than a relabelling of the earlier gap.

## Fixed target and inherited credit

Fix g as the particular original bearer in the inherited test. P1 is granted at the full whole-actual-productive-origin scope; P2 identifies this bearer. The target includes relevant exercises and receiving modes, rather than only the final correction. Complete determination D of received occurrences is also granted. These grants are conditional and do not establish their actual application to the admitted correction.

The agreed target is not silently enlarged to every object, power or possible history whatsoever. A source which covers every actual contributor to this whole target does not thereby cover every nonactual possibility. The inherited same-target uniqueness argument remains intact. Genuine intentional effective agency retains the relevant apprehension, willing and efficacy of its subject.

Eighth already distinguished unreceived actuality from intrinsic existential sufficiency, conditional necessitation from unconditional necessity, and a necessarily occupied role from its actual occupant. It allowed direct particular recognition as a possible source of rational warrant. It also separated a criticism of an inference from a positive assertion that a brute or replacement-source cosmos is metaphysically possible. Those are starting points here, not new results.

The present inquiry adds independently inspectable modal bridges, explicit premise deletions, new primary loci on intrinsic power and productive possibility, and a comparison with the material-ground approach. It seeks the content which could justify the transfer, not merely another statement that a transfer is needed.

## What intrinsic original power contributes

Grant the strongest intrinsic-power premise: g's proper productive exercise is actual, and its enabling power belongs to its own concrete nature rather than being supplied by its dependent product or by reciprocal empowerment. The proposal has positive content, including a real bearer and unborrowed efficacy.

A rival cannot answer by treating this source as a passive relay or by granting its proper intentional exercise while removing the subject's corresponding agency. The granted target retains the whole actual productive origin.

Three further claims must nevertheless be kept separate. The power can be constitutive of g; g can possess it without a first acquisition; and g can be incapable of complete noninstantiation. The first two do not have the third as their logical content. Even a claim that this bearer never lacks this power throughout an unbounded actual history remains different from a claim that this very bearer exists under every absolute alternative.

The strongest extension says that a possibly uninstantiated original power would need an external existential condition in order to be actual. If independently warranted, that principle could connect the concrete nature to necessity. It is not efficient self-production, and the objection that a subject must exist before acting misses its intended force. But excluding particular suppliers does not establish that every possibly uninstantiated intrinsic reality requires such a condition. The power argument supplies a serious candidate for what the source is; the further principle concerns why its whole instantiation is unconditional.

An opponent need not assert that g really could be absent to distinguish these contents. Conversely, the opponent's inability to exhibit a possible g-less cosmos does not establish the additional principle. This reciprocal discipline allows the stronger positive proposal to be assessed without making either side win by default.

## The object level actualisability bridge

Use a rigid name g throughout. R connects an assessment situation to its admitted alternatives. E(g,w) means that g exists somewhere within the whole history represented by w. Received(g,w) means that g's whole existence has a productive source other than itself in w. These symbols are a language for testing arguments; introducing a structure does not assert that it represents a metaphysically possible reality.

The object-level proposal has four premises.

1. At actuality a, g exists and its whole existence is unreceived.
2. Every alternative v to a can in turn access a. This local reverse-access condition suffices to make g's existence possible from v.
3. If g is possible but wholly absent at v, an actual productive power available there can realise that same individual. A suitable alternative exercise has a manifestation u in which g's existence is received.
4. g is unreceivable throughout the relevant manifestation situations.

If an a-alternative v lacked g, premises 2 and 3 would yield a manifestation u containing received g. Premise 4 excludes it. Thus every a-alternative contains this g. The conclusion is de re necessary existence relative to the stipulated alternatives, not merely occupation of a source role.

The modal reach of premise 4 matters. If it is given globally over the relevant manifestations, no further frame rule is needed. If it only excludes reception at worlds directly accessible from a, then the proof also needs the closure which takes aRv and vRu to aRu. Full S5 is sufficient, but stronger than necessary; the exact local composition condition is enough. Symmetry alone does not provide it.

More importantly, actual unreceivedness is not premise 4. Fixing a positive actual origin can support an origin-essentialist argument for retaining that origin whenever the individual exists. Originlessness is not itself an instance of a positively fixed source relation. Its essentiality requires an argument of its own. The present proof therefore does not import essential unreceivability from P1 merely by changing a word.

A three-situation control makes the distinction visible. Let a contain unreceived g, let b contain no g, and let c contain received g. Let accessibility connect every pair. At b the same g is possible and can be assigned a productive realisation at c. Actual unreceivedness and object-level actualisability hold; necessary existence fails at b. This is an inferential counterinterpretation, not a declaration that g can in reality change its origin. Its purpose is to show exactly what the essential-origin premise contributes.

A second control retains essential unreceivedness only at a's direct alternatives. Connect a with b and b with c in both directions, with each situation accessible to itself, but do not connect a with c. The preceding assignments again work. Local reverse accessibility holds, and g is unreceived wherever it exists directly accessible to a. Yet the productive manifestation at c is outside that local range. This control identifies the composition requirement without postulating metaphysical truth for the model.

These controls are modal extensions of the same admitted actual target. Its actual productive graph, every relevant contributor and receiving mode, g's proper exercise and D can be left unchanged. Only the alternative assignments are varied. Identity labels in a model do not assert that their bearers exist there; E records existence. Thus the controls preserve the actual premise content without claiming that every formal extension is a real possibility.

## The exact origin fact bridge

There is a stronger route which does not separately assume essential unreceivability. Let U(g,w) mean that g exists at w and its whole existence is unreceived there. Fix p as that exact origin-qualified existence fact. Its positive existential content is qualified by negation of a receiving relation; it is not an unqualified positive fact.

Assume actual U(g,a), local reverse accessibility, and the following fact-level productive completeness principle:

If p is possible from v while g is wholly absent from v, some productive actualisation available at v can realise that exact p.

Add causal-content fidelity: a productive actualisation from a situation wholly lacking g manifests as reception of g's existence in its resulting history. The actualisation must draw on a genuinely available basis and have an admissible exercise; a merely conditional capacity requiring g to be supplied first does not suffice. This speaks of a power and its alternative exercise. It does not place a causal arrow between two worlds. Producing an effect in a history which contains g, or producing knowledge that g exists, is not thereby producing g.

The conditional proof is short. Take any alternative v to a. By reverse accessibility, a is an alternative from v, so exact p is possible there. Suppose g is wholly absent at v. Fact-level completeness supplies a productive realisation of p. In its manifestation u, p requires U(g,u), while causal-content fidelity requires Received(g,u). The two requirements contradict each other. Therefore g is not absent at v. Since v was arbitrary, g exists in every alternative to a.

No second-step accessibility closure is needed. Whichever manifestation u is proposed, the contradiction lies within it. No inference from actual unreceivedness to essential unreceivability is made either. The whole origin condition travels as part of the specified fact p.

Reverse accessibility is sufficient but not minimal. The exact proof only needs p to be possible from each putatively g-less alternative v to a. A different v-accessible manifestation of U(g) would supply that possibility even without an edge back to a. This possibility coverage must itself be warranted; it is not silently supplied by naming v an alternative.

This route also avoids asking who produces absence. Its required productive realisation concerns g's positive existence at an alternative which lacks g. The objection that continued absence needs no producer does not directly refute this proof. Nor does the proof rely on a temporal first event, a finite ancestry, or a power destroying itself.

## The premise relocation result

The improvement has a price. Producing an individual is weaker than producing that individual with a specified whole unreceived origin. The object-level principle quantifies over E(g); the fact-level principle preserves U(g). Replacing the latter by the former destroys the contradiction, because the manifested g can simply be received. Replacing the whole-origin target by an endpoint likewise changes the conclusion's subject matter.

There is an exact pointwise equivalence. Under causal-content fidelity, fact-level completeness at v holds if and only if possibility of U(g) at v entails E(g,v). From left to right, possibility together with g's absence would demand a received-and-unreceived manifestation. From right to left, either U(g) is not possible or g is already present, so the completeness principle's antecedent is false.

Now fix actual U(g,a) and sufficient possibility coverage at every putatively g-less a-alternative. The target-specific fact-level completeness principle holds throughout those alternatives if and only if g exists throughout them. Reverse accessibility supplies this coverage but is not required separately.

The forward direction is the proof just given. For the reverse, suppose g exists at every a-alternative. No such alternative satisfies the completeness principle's absent-bearer antecedent, so that restricted principle holds. This establishes the equivalence.

The equivalence does not show that every use of a general productive theory is circular. A general theory can be independently supported and have a surprising consequence for this case. It does show that asserting only the target-specific instance supplies no weaker, independent conclusion-relevant content. The essential-origin burden has been removed from the proof's explicit premises, while equivalent force has entered through stronger coverage of the exact origin-qualified fact.

This is the new narrowing. A defence must justify the general law's reach over that class of facts. Ordinary examples of making an object, changing a state, or exercising an already existing capacity do not automatically establish it. The newly inspected primary power passages do not explicitly demonstrate that unrestricted reach.

## Absolute alternatives and productive alternatives

The productive theory can offer concrete, epistemically accessible grounds for genuine alternatives beyond merely consistent descriptions. Unexercised capacities remain real; exercises can vary while their underlying power persists. Iterating such powers promises an account of modality without merely possible entities. [N9P7]

This rationale must not be reduced to a demand that every actual fact have a sufficient reason. The proposed law concerns what makes a nonactual possibility real. It can therefore be considered without first accepting a global PSR. Equally, refusing a global PSR does not itself refute the law.

The crucial question is whether all absolute alternatives are covered. Consider two relations. Productive access connects histories represented as alternative exercises of a fixed stock of powers. Absolute modal access connects all genuine metaphysical alternatives, if there are any beyond those histories. The two relations may coincide, but that is a substantive identification, not a consequence of using the same word possible.

Within a fixed productive setting, the preconditions of an alternative exercise can remain available and permit return in the modal sense. This supports a reverse-access argument within that setting. It does not alone establish that every absolute alternative shares the original productive basis. If the conclusion is obtained only after defining the alternatives as those generated by g's powers, it establishes necessity throughout that generated range. Identifying the range with unrestricted metaphysical possibility is precisely what needs warrant.

The historical qualification is similarly important. A present agent cannot ordinarily bring about a different already completed past. That familiar fact is not by itself a refutation of a timeless powers account, since the account may appeal to powers which were available earlier and to alternative entire histories. The stronger critic should allow that reply. What remains to establish is that every absolute origin-qualified alternative has the requisite power-ground within each situation from which it is assessed. One cannot assume that claim by treating every hypothesised situation as a moment after the same actual origin.

The comparison should therefore avoid two easy mistakes. A defence cannot borrow broad modal invariance from absolute possibility while borrowing productive completeness only from a narrower prospective possibility. A critic cannot disprove the broad theory merely by pointing to the limitations of present human agents while ignoring the original powers which the theory proposes. Neither side has thereby established the scope it needs.

## Material grounds and one indispensable possibility

Consider a distinct principle: every genuine modal content has an actual ontological basis, although this basis need not efficiently produce the possible object. It addresses why a description represents a real possibility without inferring that its object is instantiated. The preceding argument's productive-fidelity premise therefore does not automatically apply.

But several scopes are distinguishable. A thinker may need actual experience or acquaintance to understand a modal content. A true modal claim may need an actual ontological basis. Its truth at every alternative may require a basis in each alternative. And that basis may have to be the same particular g. These are increasingly strong claims. Epistemic dependence of a judgement on an actual presentation does not alone establish the final ontological claim.

The minimal useful ground lemma concerns one possibility, not all. Let q be a determinate modal content which holds at every relevant alternative. Suppose every such holding requires an existing bearer of its ultimate ground, and suppose that the identity of this ground is fixed as g across that range. Then g exists at every relevant alternative: take any alternative, use q's holding to obtain its existing ground, and use the fixed ground identity to identify it as g.

This proof does not require a PSR for all actual facts, nor a premise that one being grounds every possibility. It shows why a well-warranted direct insight into one indispensable modal basis could be enough. Its requirement is nevertheless stronger than actual grounding, actual uniqueness, or rigid reference alone. A rigid name specifies which individual is under discussion; it does not itself secure that individual's presence in every circumstance.

A two-situation control grants more than mere actual grounding. In each situation let there be exactly one concrete bearer grounding every modal content; let g occupy that role at a and h at b. Let the situations be mutually accessible and the contents invariant. Each situation has a unique full ground, yet g is absent at b. This logical interpretation refutes the move from a necessarily filled unique role to the necessity of its present occupant under those premises. It does not certify a replaceable-source metaphysics.

A second control addresses concentration. Suppose two fixed bearers exist throughout the range: one is indispensable for q and the other for r. Invariance of q and r makes each bearer necessary on this grounding theory, but it does not make either the ground of both. Thus an argument against losing a particular possibility does not by itself concentrate every possibility in one subject. Additional ontological reasons could justify concentration; mere modal invariance cannot substitute for them.

For the present target, a further identification is needed even if a necessary modal ground has been established. P1 identifies g's whole productive role for the actual target. It does not automatically identify every ontological modal ground with that productive bearer. The same-target uniqueness theorem remains valid; a proposed modal ground must satisfy its exact productive antecedent before that theorem can identify it with g.

## The admitted correction as a test case

The one-possibility lemma can be applied to a concrete candidate rather than an unspecified totality. Let q say that the admitted cognitive correction is possible. Actual occurrence warrants its possibility. A suitable modal invariance principle would then preserve q across the relevant alternatives. That supplies the lemma's invariant content without first imagining a new cosmos.

What is still required is the ground of q itself. P1 concerns the whole productive origin of the actual episode. It does not, merely by doing that work, establish the ontological ground of the episode's possibility at every alternative. Nor is the source of a person's grasp of a truth automatically the source of that truth. These distinctions preserve the correction's causal, cognitive and evidential force while preventing a transfer between relations.

The affirmative could instead offer direct recognition that this g is the indispensable intrinsic basis of that exact modal content. That would address the missing premise. It should say whether the content includes the episode's complete origin history or only its intelligible cognitive kind: the first requires exact-origin coverage; the second does not retain the actual particular episode by itself. This identifies a definite target for rational recognition without demanding an experiment or assuming that a rival alternative exists.

## Necessity and intrinsic explanation

The modal bridges above have a genuine positive consequence when their antecedents are warranted: they establish necessary existence of the same g. They do not by their logical form alone settle why the necessity belongs intrinsically to g or whether the existential account is nonbrute.

This is not the objection that every explanation must cite a further being. An intrinsic explanation is a live option. Nor is it the claim that a general law can never figure in an intrinsic explanation. General principles are used in explanations throughout reasoning. The issue is whether the principles apply because of this bearer’s independently warranted intrinsic constitution, rather than because the explanatory result has been inserted into a role description or stipulated modal range.

One can preserve all the extensionally stated modal facts while leaving the intrinsic explanatory relation uninterpreted. Adding a claim that g exists through its nature supplies explanatory content which the truth table did not contain. Conversely, an independently warranted grasp of the intrinsic nature could establish both necessity and explanation together. The point is to ask for that content without rejecting the possibility of grasping it.

The strongest affirmative package would therefore establish four connected claims: the particular g is correctly identified; its original power is genuinely intrinsic; its nature is an indispensable actual basis of the relevant absolute modal content; and that indispensability is explained by the nature rather than inferred merely from the desired conclusion. The package need not be inferred from four separately formulated premises. A direct rational recognition might present their connected content. What matters is what is known and why it has the claimed scope.

## A discriminating test for direct recognition

Direct rational recognition is not disqualified because it is unmeasured, basic or disputed. Requiring an independent inference for every basic insight would rule out basic rational knowledge by definition. A useful test must instead distinguish the proposed recognition's content from nearby weaker judgements and give the developed rival something definite to assess.

First, retain the bearer. The recogniser must refer to this g, not only to whatever fills a necessarily occupied original-source role. Second, retain whole origin. The insight must concern g's complete unreceived existence, not a present endpoint, an acquired capacity, or inability to destroy an already actual being. Third, specify the modal basis. Does the recognition disclose essential unreceivability, exhaustive productive coverage of origin-qualified possibilities, or this g's indispensable grounding of a fixed modal content? The routes make different commitments and answer different objections.

Fourth, distinguish inability to establish an absence scenario from apprehension that absence is excluded. If the only reason given is that no rival has demonstrated a g-less possibility, the judgement has not acquired the proposed affirmative content. If the presentation positively excludes absence because its intelligible basis would thereby be removed, then the recogniser is advancing the stronger claim. That claim may be basic; it is now the actual content to examine rather than a stronger conclusion retroactively added to a weaker recognition.

Fifth, preserve the explanatory direction. Knowing that a modal theory assigns g to every admitted situation is weaker than recognising that g's nature makes that range exhaustive. Knowing an argument's validity is weaker than knowing that its modal law is true. A direct presentation of intrinsically unconditional actuality would bypass those inferential gaps, but the present source record must not claim to contain it merely because a primary author asserts the conclusion.

A further contrast makes the discriminator exact. Hold P1, P2, the actual exercises and D fixed, and replace the proposed intrinsic unconditional account by primitive actuality with those same stipulated powers. If the offered reason retains all its content under that replacement, it has not yet distinguished the two existential accounts. This is a test of the reason's content, not a claim that both accounts are metaphysically possible. A basic presentation of unconditional sufficiency could distinguish them directly.

These tests are normative discriminators, not a measurement procedure or an external certification of private rational acquaintance. Passing them would remove identified equivocations; it would not make disagreement logically impossible. They identify what a positive witness would need to exhibit and what a critical response must engage. The admitted correction and the inspected arguments do not presently supply that witness.

## Exact progress and remaining claim

The new inquiry does not merely return the earlier determination argument with additional qualifications. It reconstructs a non-PSR route, proves its two different forms, exhibits the frame and domain premises each uses, and proves a local equivalence which exposes the cost of the stronger form. It also gives a minimal one-possibility grounding lemma and controls against replacing de re necessity by a unique role or against concentrating distributed grounds without argument.

The new source material supplies a concrete original-power argument and controls on the meanings of real possibility. The comparison supplies historically explicit attempts to pass from possibility to actuality and necessity, without claiming influence or universal agreement. These investigations locate substantive proposals which could advance warrant; they also show why the present source record does not yet warrant their required unrestricted coverage or same-ground identity.

No brute alternative, empty world, replacement creator, received version of g, or distributed-ground ontology is thereby established as actual or metaphysically possible. The models test entailment, and the source checks test attribution. Neither function substitutes for metaphysical warrant. P1 and P2 remain conditional starting points here; the original agency and same-target uniqueness results retain their credit. N2 and N3 remain open.
