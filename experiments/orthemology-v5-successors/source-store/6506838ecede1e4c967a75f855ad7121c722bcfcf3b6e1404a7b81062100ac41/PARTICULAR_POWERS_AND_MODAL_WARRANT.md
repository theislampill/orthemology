# Particular powers and the warrant for a necessary source

## Result and exact increment

The newly inspected primary passages support a stronger challenge to the **warrant for an asserted contingent ultimate source**, but do not independently establish the necessary-source conclusion. Their contribution is specific. They distinguish not knowing an alternative to be impossible from knowing it to be really possible; locate productive possibility in actual agents or receptive subjects; and treat an external essence as the concrete particular rather than an independently existing universal. Taken together, these prohibit granting the rival a real absence-alternative merely because an absent-source description can be written down. They equally prohibit granting the affirmative an existence-entailing nature merely because that nature can be described.

The best positive reconstruction is an argument from actual productive powers to a concrete original modal basis, followed by a demand that alternatives to that basis have the same real grounding as alternatives within it. Its first demand is well motivated by the sources. Its final necessity step needs more: that the relevant actual basis exhausts the grounds of every metaphysical alternative, and that grounding an alternative requires this very bearer to exist **in** that alternative. Neither follows from the inspected power examples. If those additions are replaced by the claim that an original source's nature unconditionally suffices for its existence, the reconstruction returns to the accepted N2/N3 obligation rather than discharging it.

There is therefore a genuine **source and discrimination increment**, not a new closure of N2. In particular, the primary text itself extends concrete essence/existence identity to created particulars. Identity in that sense cannot by itself distinguish a necessary source from a contingent bearer. The most serious reply to the positive argument is consequently more exact than a renewed assertion of bruteness: it challenges the transition from bearer-dependent capacities to the unconditional existence of the bearer while accepting real particular powers, a nonseparate essence, and the anti-conceivability constraint.

This is a targeted continuation of the current necessary-source public projection, not a replacement for it. No accepted modal table is rerun. P1 productive completion, P2 identification of the fixed actual bearer, N2 actual exhaustive nonbrute existential adequacy, and N3 unconditional intrinsic exclusion of that bearer's absence remain separate. No common cognitive bearer, unity, truthfulness, revelation, restoration input, R5 defeat, or global closure follows.

## 1. What the inspected primary sources actually say

### 1.1 Two epistemic positions, not a definition of necessity

In *Majmūʿ al-fatāwā* 9:223–226, Ibn Taymiyyah contrasts two uses of possibility. One is a mental allowance arising from lack of knowledge of impossibility. The other is warranted possibility outside the mind, supported by the thing's existence, the existence of an analogue, or an a-fortiori comparison with an existing case. The surrounding discussion uses the first creation and cases of revival to explain the method applied to resurrection. It does not infer actuality from the mere absence of a contradiction noticed by the reasoner. [P01](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/959/المقام-الرابع-القياس-يفيد-العلم-بالتصديقات)

Three qualifications matter. First, this is not the claim that only what has already occurred can be possible: analogy and the a-fortiori route are expressly included. Second, it is not a proof that every apparently conceivable alternative is impossible. A reasoner can lack knowledge either way. Third, the text's contrast is partly epistemic: it concerns knowing possibility, not just furnishing an ontology of powers. The step from this discipline to a complete theory of metaphysical modality requires further argument.

The method is also used critically against a proposed natural-theological proof. In *Darʾ taʿāruḍ* 3:358, the question is whether a thing's quantity or attributes could have differed. Each thing has its own reality and attributes; the reader must distinguish a merely mentally allowed variation from a known external possibility. The text is therefore not a one-sided permission to scrutinize only anti-theistic possibilities. It also challenges an insufficiently warranted contingency premise used to establish a maker. [P02](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/637/تعليق-ابن-تيمية-على-كلام-الآمدي)

That use bears directly on our comparison. The claim “this concrete ultimate basis could have been wholly absent” is stronger than “we have not proved this basis necessary.” But the claim “this being has an intrinsic existential nature” is also stronger than “we have not located an external actualizer.” Neither party receives its modal claim by default.

### 1.2 Real productive possibility has a bearer

The newly located *Nubuwwāt* passage is especially important. In a discussion of originated things and their prior possibility, it distinguishes mental allowance from external possibility related to an agent or to a receptive subject. Its examples are a capable agent's acting, land's being cultivable, and a woman's capacity to become pregnant. It then explains the possibility of the Lord's creating in terms of power and ability subsisting in him. The exact phrase distinguishing the alternatives is “المتعلق بالفاعل أو المحل”. [P03, hosted PDF p.36 of 168](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/alnbwat-.pdf)

This supplies more than a warning about imagination. The capacity is not a freestanding possible object that produces its own instantiation. There is a real agent or subject in which the relevant ability is present. The distinction between agent and recipient also matters: the text does not here identify every real possibility with a passive material receptacle. Nor does it say that the agent must be an independently existing universal essence. It speaks of a concrete capacity-bearing reality.

The adjacent argument concerns origination, the conditions of a new event, and the rejection of an unexplained transition from impossibility to productive possibility. It makes claims about preceding causes and the continuity of divine activity. Those claims must not be quietly promoted into an independently proved account of every metaphysical alternative. In particular, the statement that the divine essence is eternal occurs within the passage; its occurrence is not a new derivation of that premise from the bare distinction between mental and external possibility.

There is also a scope difference between the first two sources and this one. P01 and P02 identify a warrant problem: how is real possibility known? P03 identifies an ontological subject for real ability: in what does the relevant productive or receptive possibility subsist? An epistemic test and an ontological analysis can reinforce one another without being identical. The phrase “real possibility” must not erase that difference.

### 1.3 The particular essence is not a second external object

In *Darʾ* 1:293–295, Ibn Taymiyyah rejects a numerically shared external existence distributed between necessary and contingent things. The general term and general concept apply to different realities; there is no one piece of existence numerically common to them. Crucially, the passage says that the particular creature's external existence is its own external self, reality and quiddity, not two external things. It then applies the point a fortiori to the creator. [P04](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/1233/index.php?ID=90&bk_no=413&idfrom=178&idto=179&page=bookcontents_ver3)

This is a decisive safeguard for the current inquiry. The relevant identity is not uniquely divine. It applies to the concrete creature before the a-fortiori extension. If concrete essence/existence identity alone entailed necessary existence, the distinction that the passage itself retains between necessary and possible realities would disappear. The text does not supply that inference.

The same passage rejects collapsing all real attributes into one another or into the subject. Accordingly, “no separate essence-object” must not become “no real intrinsic differentiation.” The power belongs to a particular reality; it need not be an autonomous object hovering outside that reality, and its belonging does not establish that every statement about it is identical in explanatory content to every statement about its bearer.

The supplied Aṣfahāniyya translation, printed pp.31–34, provides the complementary distinction between inseparable concomitance and dependence on another efficient producer. Its printed pp.25–26, checked against the Arabic, give the affirmative formulation of intrinsic existential sufficiency. These different uses must be kept together: refusing a separate external essence does not itself prove necessity, while a claim of sufficiency may carry more than identity alone. [P05](https://www.islamicbook.ws/ageda/alaqidt-alasfhanit-001.html)

### 1.4 Efficient action, intrinsic condition and complete determination

*Darʾ* 3:110–112 distinguishes dependence on an agent from dependence on a receptive subject and distinguishes reciprocal conditions from reciprocal efficient production. It expressly warns against proving that something has no efficient producer and then importing every further meaning carried by a term for intrinsic necessity. An inseparable attribute can be a condition of its subject without efficiently producing that subject. [P06](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/496/تعليق-ابن-تيمية)

The supplied *Oneness* translation, printed pp.34–35, distinguishes active power from passive readiness, and complete power together with complete will from an essence stripped of those determinations. Its argument against an entirely made series belongs to productive completion. The passage concerning the necessity of a willed effect concerns the complete determination, not bare necessary existence alone. The footnote dismissing Aristotelian potentiality in a few words is a translator's note, not independent authorial support for a modal thesis. [P07, supplied source]

The source constellation therefore provides a positive ontology of concrete powers and intrinsic attributes without identifying efficient production, intrinsic constitution, epistemic warrant, and modal necessity. Those distinctions are not obstacles imported from an alien theory; several are demanded by the primary argument itself.

### 1.5 External possibility is not automatically contingency

One lexical distinction is indispensable. P01 allows actual existence to establish external possibility. Actual existence establishes that the existent is not impossible; it does not by itself establish that the existent could have been absent. The Aṣfahāniyya argument at printed pp.23–25 expressly adds observed prior nonexistence when establishing the contingency of originated things. Existence excludes impossibility; prior absence excludes intrinsic necessity in the respect at issue. Those are different evidential contributions.

Accordingly, the power grounding of “it can occur” does not immediately answer the question “this very bearer might never have existed.” Nor does the absence of a complete actual volition turn an effect's capacity into an independent source. The Oneness distinction between what is possible in itself and what is determined by the complete will and power also concerns a different modal respect from the bearer's unconditional existence.

A proof that silently uses *imkān* first for nonimpossibility, then for contingency, then for capacity awaiting a producer changes its premise twice. Real possibility must be specified by its subject and respect before it can do work in N2 or N3.

## 2. The strongest positive reconstruction

The following reconstruction is our argument, motivated by the passages above. It is not a claim that Ibn Taymiyyah formulated the modern rival or used our N2 labels.

### Step A: Start from actual exercise, not a modal specification

The admitted correction concerns a definite displayed page. On the admitted cognitive reading, a subject accesses the relevant header, understands it, compares it with the earlier absence claim, and revises for the evidential reason. Its actual exercise cannot be furnished by an uninstantiated description of what an ideal reader would do.

There are consequently real productive capacities and their bearers, as well as evidence and a target. The capacities may be genuinely the subject's own while their existence is received. This is the retained active-recipient point. A source inquiry becomes world-directed when the particular reception is warranted; it does not become world-directed merely by adding “actual” to a proposed ultimate role.

### Step B: Seek original supply, with P1 and P2 still explicit

The affirmative argues that complete ultimate supply cannot consist wholly in still-received possession. If this productive-completion claim succeeds, and if a further identification fixes a particular bearer g, then the inquiry reaches an actual original power-bearing reality. P03 helps explain what the source must be: actual ability subsisting in a concrete reality rather than a merely mental possibility. It does not itself defeat the genuine productive infinitist or show that jointly sufficient powers belong to one numerically fixed source.

This matters to the proposed modal reply. A necessary modal role, a distributed field of powers, and this particular g are different candidates. The powers passage supplies no shortcut around P2. Likewise, a supply account for all actual endpoints is not yet complete provision of the whole actual origination by which they occur; the Sixth origin-closure distinction remains intact.

### Step C: Challenge an unearned existential alternative

Once g is independently fixed, the rival cannot obtain g's real contingency simply by placing a sentence denying g in a table. The newly checked sources offer a principled reason. Genuine modal judgment answers to concrete realities, their attributes, and warranted comparisons; our freedom to alter a description does not establish corresponding freedom in reality.

In familiar local cases the warrant for nonexistence is stronger. An individual is observed to come to be; an actual productive history supports its origination; there are relevant powers by which production can fail or an existing thing can perish. The Aṣfahāniyya begins from observed origination for precisely this reason. These cases do not automatically warrant the same modal judgment about an original ultimate bearer whose whole existence has no admitted productive history of that kind.

The strongest positive objection is therefore: what warrants the rival's claim of complete possible absence, given the exact source it proposes? An appeal to all the actual derivative things it can generate establishes neither the possible absence of that source nor an unexplained appearance of its entire field. The opponent has to defend the transfer rather than merely repeat the word “contingent.”

This is a real improvement in the dialectic. It removes an unearned premise from the rival's account, rather than pretending to have proven the opposite premise.

### Step D: The ambitious power-based bridge

The affirmative can go further. Suppose the original concrete source g is also proposed as the ultimate basis of the real capacities by which alternatives to the actual derivative field are possible. The positive rationale is that actual abilities explain how possibilities concern reality at all, and an ungrounded possibility-space threatens to reinstall a free external resource above the alleged original source.

The argument then asks whether g's own total nonexistence can belong to that same space. If every real alternative is an exercise, nonexercise, or admissible variation of powers ultimately intrinsic to g, an alternative that removes g seems to remove the very reality making it a genuine alternative. This is the strongest form of the source-based reply. It does not say that g efficiently makes itself. It says that the concrete reality grounding all real variation cannot itself be one optional product within that variation.

The argument has an intelligible attraction. A capacity to produce different effects does not, in being exercised differently, confer existence on its own whole bearer. Nor does the unexercised capacity become a freestanding source after the bearer has been deleted. On the proposed view, real possibility has ontological discipline, not unrestricted recombination of predicates.

But the necessity inference requires two further propositions:

1. **Modal exhaustiveness:** every metaphysical alternative relevant to g's existence is covered by the modal powers of this actual source, rather than only alternatives in the derivative field or alternatives reachable from a given actual state.
2. **Source retention:** to be an alternative grounded in g, the alternative must contain this same g as existent, not merely be intelligible or grounded from the standpoint of an actual g.

With those propositions, g's total absence is excluded. Without them, the capacity examples establish only more local claims. P03's agent must exist to possess or exercise its capacity; this does not show that all metaphysical alternatives preserve that agent. P01's existence and analogy tests concern warrant for particular possibilities; they do not identify all possibility with a single actual bearer or say that modal grounding must be internal to the situation being represented.

Modal exhaustiveness is also stronger than completeness of actual productive origination. An account can purport to explain every actual occurrence yet leave open different possible histories. Conversely, a complete modal characterization can delimit histories without proving that any of them is concretely realized. The old origin-closure burden and this new proposed modal coverage burden must not be combined under one unqualified word “complete.”

### Step E: The existential route if the modal bridge is declined

The affirmative has a second, historically central move. It can say that g's reality is positively sufficient for existence, not just that g happens to occupy a modal role. This is the Aṣfahāniyya rationale: unreceived existence is understood as an intrinsically sufficient mode of actuality.

That route is not defeated by the criticism of a freestanding universal essence. A concrete reality can have an intrinsically intelligible manner of being without two external items, essence and existence, standing in a productive sequence. P04 and P06 clarify why the explanation need not be efficient self-manufacture.

Yet the existential claim must be warranted in the actual case. Calling g intrinsically sufficient does more than recognizing real power. The power explains effects on the background of its bearer. To explain why the bearer is unconditionally existent, its nature must carry the further existential content. If that content is simply stipulated, Step E reintroduces N3. If an answer is guaranteed because ultimate existence must not remain brute, it reintroduces N2. The new possibility passages do not independently vindicate either guarantee.

## 3. The strongest reply to the new argument

See N2 ARGUMENT §4.

Let C be the rival's proposed actual original concrete basis. It has real particular powers. Its local cognitive bearers, evidence and target relations are genuine.

The positive reconstruction can ask what warrants this explanatory status. That question remains decisive. But the new primary sources do not refute the proposal by themselves. Four points explain why.

### 3.1 Owning a power differs from existing in every alternative

P03 gives the rival a good reason to locate its local powers in C rather than in abstract laws. C must exist for those very powers to be possessed or exercised. The rival grants that. What it refuses is the implication from this conditional dependence to C's unconditional existence.

A possible nonproduction is instructive. A parent's capacity can make sense of an alternative in which a child is never produced, though that child actually exists. This is an illustration of the type of warrant at issue, not a proof of any particular reproductive counterfactual. Its significance is that a power-bearing subject and a product occupy different modal roles. Generalizing from that example to the total absence of the ultimate subject requires further reasoning; the rival does not get it for free. But the affirmative likewise cannot infer universal source retention from the fact that a given **exercise** retains its exercising subject.

The rival can ask whether the affirmative is investigating absolute metaphysical possibility or only histories reachable by operating the actual powers from a fixed state. The second question may legitimately retain the actual basis as background. Answering it cannot decide whether that basis could have been wholly absent. Otherwise every fixed-background capacity model would generate necessary existence for whatever objects its starting state holds fixed.

### 3.2 A real existential alternative need not be an act of self-removal

The original self-production argument rejects C's bringing its own whole bearer into being through a prior exercise of its already actual power. Its converse is not that C can be absent only if it exercises a power to make itself never have existed. Nonexistence of the whole bearer is not necessarily a transition from existence to absence; an alternative complete history is not necessarily a transformation performed on the actual history.

The rival can consequently accept that no original source produces its own prior total absence and still withhold necessary existence. If it positively asserts that an absent-C history is really possible, it owes warrant. If it merely disputes the affirmative inference, it can suspend judgment about such a history while asking how modal exhaustiveness and source retention were established. Suspension is not a new counterexample ontology. It is enough to prevent an unwarranted inference from being certified as a proof.

This creates three positions that must not be merged: the rival's asserted contingent reality; the rival's asserted intrinsic explanatory status; and a critic's refusal to infer necessity from the shared actual evidence. The first two incur positive burdens. The third need not establish a whole alternative cosmos in order to expose an unsupported premise.

### 3.3 Concrete identity does not erase an explanatory question

The rival can accept P04 completely. Its actual C need not comprise one external essence-object plus another external existence-object. It can be the concrete reality that exists. It can also have real attributes that are not one numerically shared universal spread through other bearers.

The corresponding P04 control applies to the affirmative. Its concrete g is not guaranteed existential sufficiency merely because g's actual external reality is g's external existence. The primary author's inclusion of creatures in the identity thesis makes this a particularly strong symmetric control.

P06 allows intrinsic conditioning without efficient production; it therefore prevents the affirmative from treating all explanatory differentiation in C as an unacknowledged external maker while allowing intrinsic differentiation in g.

### 3.4 A locally grounded possibility theory need not explain its own whole existence

The rival can claim that C's nature grounds or supports local capacities while leaving the existential status of C a distinct question. This is not immediately inconsistent. It distinguishes modal work done by a reality from an explanation of why that reality exists. An account need not first solve every ultimate question in order to support some determinate local modal knowledge.

The affirmative may find that stopping point inadequate. Its best response is that a complete ultimate explanation should account for this indispensable resource. That is the retained N2 reason for further inquiry. It does not become a proof merely because the resource now supports modal judgments as well as productive acts.

The right challenge asks whether the intrinsic status is contentful and warranted, and whether its supposed contingency is warranted. The affirmative's competing intrinsic status has to answer equivalent questions. The new sources sharpen those demands without selecting a winner.

## 4. Why the positive objection is nevertheless substantial

The preceding reply is not a verdict of automatic parity. The source advocate has earned three objections against weaker rivals.

First, a proposed abstract possibility-space does not by being consistent supply an actual agent. If a rival means only that C is describable, it has not explained the actual productive episode. The actual powers of a real C, if independently established, would remedy that defect; an abstract variable would not.

Second, observed variability of derivative things does not establish unlimited variability of an ultimate reality. P02 directs attention to the thing's particular nature. The original basis might have an intrinsically fixed manner of being, and an opponent cannot rule that out by supposing different predicates without warrant. A proposed intrinsic absence-alternative is therefore a claim requiring evidence, not the philosophically neutral starting point.

Third, the explanation of an actual original basis must have more content than the statement that the basis exists. No anti-conceivability argument makes a stipulation explanatory. The affirmative can press that criticism while conceding that a genuine, independently warranted constitutive explanation would need to be considered on its own terms.

Those objections can alter the comparison of developed accounts. An actually warranted intrinsic sufficient nature would beat an unsupported contingent label. An actually warranted contingent constitutive status would beat an unsupported necessary label. The present local evidence warrants neither complete ultimate characterization. The sources give disciplined ways to seek more evidence; they do not furnish the missing actual description merely by authorizing the search.

## 5. What would discriminate the accounts in the admitted case?

The evidence-supported correction itself remains the shared target. Its success has no newly discovered power to identify a total modal basis. The parent paper's actual-case test stands. The new source work specifies four additional discriminators rather than a new global result.

1. **A particular modal warrant:** identify the concrete feature that licenses the claimed absence-alternative or excludes it. A known origination, actual existential dependence, or a carefully warranted analogy could support contingency of a particular. A warranted intrinsic existential nature could support necessity. General describability supplies neither.
2. **A bridge from local capacities to ultimate modal coverage:** show why the reality underwriting this actual field also exhausts the possible alternatives relevant to its own whole existence. Covering the actual productive history is insufficient by itself.
3. **A bridge from actual grounding to source retention:** show why this modal grounding requires the same g inside every relevant alternative. Do not replace de re necessity with the existence of some adequate bearer or with the actual g's capacity to represent an alternative.
4. Apply §3.3. A role defined as “whatever explains its existence” supplies no independent warrant.

A noninferential recognitional route is not disallowed. But an appeal to direct recognition must identify what is recognized, why that judgment concerns whole existential sufficiency rather than actual self-subsistence or local power, and how the present intrinsic rival affects its warrant. The source's assurance of rational immediacy records its own epistemic commitment; it is not new evidence that the currently proposed g is independently recognized by this research.

An indirect inference to the best explanation is also available. It must compare earned explanatory content, not count the rival's outstanding premises while crediting the affirmative's outstanding premises as settled. The sources can support a preference for reality-directed rather than unconstrained modal construction. That preference does not yet decide between two proposals both claiming concrete intrinsic actuality.

## 6. Adjudication and research consequence

The particular-powers route genuinely improves the **burden placed on an assertion of contingency**. The accepted paper already withheld metaphysical-possibility certification; the new primary passages supply direct historical support for that withholding and show its positive method: actual existence, warranted analogy, concrete ability, and attention to the particular's nature. P02's application against an insufficient contingency proof prevents this from becoming a partisan evidential rule.

The route does **not** make necessity follow from actuality plus a power-bearing essence. P04 is an especially discriminating negative result: the primary ontology identifies creaturely essence and existence too. P03 supplies bearer-dependence for productive possibility, not universal retention of one bearer in every metaphysical alternative. P06 blocks reclassifying the rival's every intrinsic condition as an external efficient producer while exempting the affirmative's attributes.

The strongest new affirmative bridge is modal exhaustiveness together with source retention. It has a rational motivation but has not obtained independent warrant from the inspected loci. Taken as a statement about possibilities reachable from fixed actual conditions, it is too weak for absolute necessity. Taken as a statement about every metaphysical alternative, it adds the disputed closure. Replacing it with unconditional intrinsic existential sufficiency returns the argument to N2/N3.

The exact verdict is thus narrower and more useful than “N2 is still open.” A newly investigated closing route fails at a located transition, and two fresh primary controls explain why: productive possibility is not absolute modal coverage, and concrete essence/existence identity is not a necessity discriminator. The rival's affirmative contingency and intrinsic explanatory status also remain unproved. A critic can continue to suspend both rather than pretend that one has been established by the other's failure.

The next useful work is not a larger modal table or another definition of real possibility. It is an independent defense of either the global modal bridge, the particular intrinsic existential nature, or the rival's constitutive existential status. Until one is earned, this source continuation should be used as a constraint on the next argument, not cited as a necessary-source proof or as a vindication of the rival ontology.
