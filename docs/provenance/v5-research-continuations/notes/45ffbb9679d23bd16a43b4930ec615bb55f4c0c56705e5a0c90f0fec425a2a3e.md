# Primary-literature comparison and novelty ceiling

Readback date: 2026-09-30. This is a targeted nearest-result check, not a systematic review or a global priority certificate. Search terms included common dominator, compatible weighted proper scores, Bregman repair, positive affine scoring equivalence, and local common dominance. No searched source was found stating the exact S2 characterization, but absence from this limited search is not evidence of global originality.

## Direct mathematical ancestors

### Konek, Degrees of Incoherence, Dutch Bookability & Guidance Value (online 2022; journal 2023), Appendix 1.2

This is a close prior treatment of simultaneous local improvement under distinct additive proper scores. Propositions 3–4 characterize binary common improving directions using the positive cone of truth-state gradients, then give curvature-ratio criteria. The local gradient-cone mechanism and the project of comparing distinct proper scores must be credited here. S2 instead asks for coherent common endpoints at arbitrarily small incoherence around every interior point of a simplex with at least three outcomes, and concludes equivalence of two fixed generator families. The raw antecedents differ, but NUDGE_COMPARISON.md now proves that universal local nudging and universal coherent endpoint repair have the same positive-affine classification in the fixed additive categorical >=3 regime. This narrows methodological novelty claims substantially. The publication records an April 2023 correction, which the separate literature review is checking. [Published primary appendix](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-022-01860-z)


### Joyce, Accuracy and Coherence (2009), §§10–12

Joyce explicitly distinguishes a dominator for each legitimate score from a common dominator under all scores, discusses the normative significance of that quantifier difference, and considers contextual uniqueness of the correct score. Thus neither the common-repair problem nor the general strategy of restricting score legitimacy is new here. Our claimed research delta is the local iff projection characterization, shrinking-cell bounds, and three-outcome additive-generator equivalence theorem. [Primary text](https://fitelson.org/topics/joyce_2009.pdf)

### Schervish, Seidenfeld and Kadane, Proper Scoring Rules, Dominated Forecasts, and Coherence (2009)

This paper treats dominance/coherence under proper-score regularity conditions and carefully separates deterministic and randomized alternatives, continuity and boundary assumptions. The tr865 report sometimes passed around as a Predd paper is by these authors. The result used here is classical single-score coherence/dominance ancestry; the new candidate compares simultaneous local dominance across two score geometries. [Primary report](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/tr/tr865/tr865.pdf)

### Predd et al., Probabilistic Coherence and Proper Scoring Rules (2009)

The Bregman projection argument underlying single-score coherent dominance is established prior work. Our S2 sufficiency uses its familiar three-point/convex-optimality mechanism; it is not advertised as a new accuracy-dominance theorem. S2 necessity asks when merely having some common local dominator forces two additive generators to differ only by positive scale and affine terms. [Author-hosted published paper](https://www.princeton.edu/~kulkarni/Papers/Journals/j071_2009_PreSeirLieOshKulPoo_TransIT.pdf)

### Leitgeb and Pettigrew, An Objective Justification of Bayesianism I (2010), §5

The authors seek score restrictions that avoid conflicting epistemic prescriptions. Their shared conditions include global inaccuracy as an increasing function of Euclidean distance; additional local/global agreement or directed-urgency principles yield quadratic measures. This is an important conceptual predecessor to norm restriction from corrective consistency. Their displayed theorems concern local/global inaccuracy constructions under those Euclidean conditions. Our S2 starts with two arbitrary smooth additive Bregman generators and characterizes common domination near every interior simplex point; it does not independently select the quadratic form. [Primary text](https://fitelson.org/coherence/leitgeb_pettigrew.pdf)

### Pettigrew, Aggregating Incoherent Agents Who Disagree (2017; journal 2019), §§6–7

This paper characterizes when repair and aggregation procedures agree, including distinguished roles for squared Euclidean and generalized KL divergence. The published Theorem 12(iii) explicitly uses complement-symmetric curvature phi''(x)=phi''(1-x) for the binary fixing/pooling result, and its proof gives the arithmetic correction. Thus our binary symmetry mechanism is not a new observation. The published version differs from the 2017 draft; theorem numbers or claims must not be transferred uncritically. Theorem 10 and Section 8 also concern score characterization and multicell restrictions. S2 requires no pooling operation or equality of prescribed nearest-point procedures; it asks for some common statewise dominating coherent endpoint at arbitrarily small off-simplex errors for two fixed score families at every interior point. [Primary text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-017-1613-7)

### Pettigrew, Replies to Commentators on Accuracy and the Laws of Credence (2018), p.16 and footnote 8 of the accepted manuscript

This source gives the exact arithmetic complementary-pair correction and a reflection/convexity argument for additive continuous, 0/1-symmetric convex forecast scores. Our S3 binary quadratic/log example is therefore a specialization of an explicit prior result, not a new binary construction. The source asks how that result generalizes. The separate literature review supplies a precise three-world Boolean-algebra embedding of the categorical obstruction: atom/complement symmetry doubles the categorical score, and empty/full events add zero. Thus our obstruction rules out that blanket generalization in this finite regime, without settling every event-space question. Generator convexity and forecast-loss convexity must be distinguished. [Accepted manuscript, DOI 10.1111/phpr.12505](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/files/165202389/PPR_replies.pdf)

### Forbes, Compatible Weighted Proper Scoring Rules (2012; arXiv 2013)

Compatibility here concerns baseline maximization: when a baseline optimizes one score over a distribution set, it also optimizes a corresponding weighted score. The paper characterizes weighted families and matching proper scores. This is not the common-coherent-dominator condition of R1/S2. Similar terminology should not be treated as either theorem identity or evidence of novelty. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.1131)

### Kelley, A Contextual Accuracy Dominance Argument for Probabilism (2026)

Kelley extends the accuracy argument to arbitrary opinion sets using scorable contexts and a contextual dominance norm. It addresses infinite-domain limitations and retains explicit legitimacy/rationality premises. Our results are finite-dimensional local geometry, not an extension to arbitrary opinion sets and not a replacement for that contextual argument. [Primary publication](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/phpr.70098)

## Deliberate nonclaims

- No first proof of probabilism, projection dominance, Bregman identities, robust optimization, or the wide/narrow dominance objection
- No global novelty/priority closure for the local rigidity theorem or S2
- No inference that all legitimate epistemic scores are additive, smooth, diagonal, or have strictly positive Hessian
- No inference of normative authority from a compatibility classification
- No transfer from a mathematical theorem's necessity to a necessarily instantiated representational particular or a necessary intellectual bearer

## Review target worth pursuing

The exact candidate to compare against future literature is:

For n>=3 categorical outcomes and two coordinate-additive C² generators with positive second derivatives, existence of a common coherent statewise weak dominator at arbitrarily small off-simplex perturbations along one fixed transverse direction at EVERY interior coherent point is equivalent to positive-scale/affine equivalence of the generators.

The binary arithmetic/log counterexample, nonseparable quadratic counterexample, and fixed-point versus all-p distinctions are part of this candidate's identity. A nearest theorem matching only single-score Bregman projection is insufficient to establish identity; conversely, a theorem on equality of Bregman projections may make part of the argument a short corollary and should be credited if found.
