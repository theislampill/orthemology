# Productive completion without exhaustive contrastive determination

9 October 2026 UTC. One bounded T20 F1 source operation. Additive analysis only; no integration, protected edits, new formal model, theorem replay, or closure claim.

## Result

O’Connor’s 2013 argument is **positively aligned with the demand for an original productive basis**, while contesting a stronger demand for complete contrastive determination. It is therefore not a new direct defeater of actual `OriginalBearerBridge.Completion`. Its proposed nonnecessitating source is still a producer; the account does not replace productive actuality with a bare description, an unexplained contingent original, or an entirely derivative field.

The paper supplies a substantive rationale for separating production from necessitation. It also openly changes the explanatory standard relative to a strong PSR. It does not establish the actual existence or metaphysical possibility of the fully specified divine source. Crucially, its late discussion qualifies what is explained: if the source could have produced nothing contingent, the proposed account answers why this concrete contingent reality exists, but not the separate question why there is anything contingent at all. That qualification must accompany any use of its title or earlier completeness language.

The exact remaining dispute concerns whether a genuinely productive, reason-guided but nonnecessitating exercise can exhaust what the actual occurrence needs, while an explanation of the absence of a contrastive explanation legitimately discharges the remaining demand. The existence of an actual unreceived productive resource is a different proposition. This operation finds no reason to reduce confidence in that proposition merely because exhaustive contrastive explanation is disputed.

## Source synopsis

O’Connor rejects completion by accumulating conditional causal explanations (24–27). His constructive account assumes substantive modality and irreducible productive powers (27–31); explanatory schemas can merit qualified endorsement without full details (31–33). He rejects a strongly contrastive PSR in favour of causal explanation of concrete contingents, then a broader principle allowing a nonvacuous explanation of why some truths lack explanation (34–37). Necessary motivations can explain contingent choices without themselves being contingent; a nonaccidentally constituted divine nature is also required (35, notes 14–15). Scientific indeterminism motivates the production/necessitation distinction, while fuller defence of divine agency remains outstanding (36). Explanatory scope supports necessary-source theories, with simplicity, economy and possible incoherence retained as relevant considerations (38–40). If creation itself is optional, the account explains this contingent reality, not why anything contingent exists; necessarily creating something would answer the latter question (40–42). The concluding modal argument assumes S5 and distinguishes reality’s intelligibility from human ability to exhaust it (42–43).

## Independent analysis

The sections below are comparative judgments and objections developed in this report, not further source synopsis.

### 1. Four questions must not be collapsed

For this comparison, distinguish:

1. **Actual productive provision:** what makes the actual received things and events real, including the availability of the productive basis used in the account?
2. **Intrinsic adequacy of the original:** why is an alleged unreceived source actually there, rather than being a brute contingent original?
3. **Nonempty creation:** why is there some contingent creation instead of none?
4. **The complete history contrast:** why this entire course rather than every admissible alternative, holding the proposed explanatory basis fixed?

A positive answer to the first is not automatically a positive answer to the fourth. An actual productive power need not, just by being productive, exclude every alternative manifestation. Conversely, refusing the fourth demand does not supply the first answer: actual production still needs warrant. Likewise, identifying a producer does not by itself answer the second question about its own existential adequacy.

This separation protects both parties. Ibn Taymiyya’s totality rationale concerns a basis whose own received actuality cannot simply be borrowed as independently available. That is not equivalent to requiring a necessarily fixed cosmic history. O’Connor’s challenge to universal contrastive explanation therefore need not weaken it. Conversely, describing a source as nonnecessitating cannot evade the productive question by treating actualization as nothing more than selecting an inscription in a list.

Section 7 supplies a particularly important limit on question 3. Granting a nonnecessitating cause of this actual concrete totality does not, on O’Connor’s own account, settle the empty-creation contrast. The optional-creation and necessary-some-creation versions consequently have different explanatory reach. Necessary nonemptiness would still not make each member, each act, or the entire history necessary. No inference from necessary existence of something to necessary existence of a particular thing is warranted.

### 2. The exact Completion comparison

At lines 29–36, `ActualOriginalResource` combines actuality with absence of receipt; `Completion` requires such a resource relevant to each received target. The fixed-meaning instance remains:

**C(F): An actual received target F has an actual, relevant, unreceived concrete intrinsic productive resource.**

Neither exclusion of alternative histories nor a complete explanation of every contrastive truth appears in this definition. The original-bearer deduction at lines 75–92 uses Realisation and SupportTransport, not a necessary-history or determinism premise. Thus the following inference is invalid: O’Connor permits unexplained contrasts; therefore he denies Completion. It changes the premise being tested.

His envisaged original power is a natural **candidate** for the resource relevant to the actual products. Under a source interpretation on which that power is intrinsically instantiated, actually exercised or genuinely contributing, and unreceived in the same existential respect, the account supplies C(F). It then belongs on the affirmative side of the original-resource question, even if it rejects a further complete-determiner principle.

This is conditional compatibility, not a theorem that any “necessary agent” label satisfies the interface. Three restrictions matter:

- Actual relevance must connect the candidate power to the actual target. Merely having a dormant general ability or an abstract description of one does not suffice.
- An unreceived bearer is not automatically evidence that every intrinsic resource is unreceived. SupportTransport is one-way: whole receipt of the bearer entails resource receipt. Its converse, or an inference from bearer nonreceipt to arbitrary resource nonreceipt, is not in the file. The proposed original power needs its own intended same-respect qualification.
- O’Connor’s contingent-individual domain is not automatically the file’s whole-received domain. A modally persistent but derivative bearer cannot be silently deleted from the productive field. The author himself allows the idea of derivative necessity when discussing an emanated universe (32), so necessity language alone cannot perform the mapping.

Realisation has a natural conditional application to the hypothesized intrinsic productive powers; it is not an unrestricted fact-to-bearer theorem. Nothing in this chapter independently proves SupportTransport for all candidate ontologies, the binary ReceiptRepresentation, or cross-world ConstitutiveReception. Nor does its treatment of arbitrary collections by itself certify all the target-specific relevance instances required by an unrestricted application of Completion.

The direct defeating proposition would concern an actual received F with no relevant actual unreceived productive resource. O’Connor supplies no such case. An undercutter would need an independently supported reason against the anti-bootstrapping inference itself. Rejecting exhaustive contrastive adequacy does not supply that reason unless the inference first depended on the rejected adequacy demand. The actual file does not.

### 3. Positive convergence does not turn the premise into a neutral proof

The productive-totality report argues that a completely derivative productive field cannot supply its own existential provision just by arrangement. O’Connor’s insistence that local conditional accounts do not yet settle total existential adequacy converges with that concern. Both recognize a difference between tracing a local cause and accounting for the actuality presupposed by the whole causal account. This is substantive affirmative agreement about the explanatory task.

It is not a derivation accepted by every rival. A defender of globally complete distributive explanation can agree that each isolated local explanation is conditional while denying that the full collection leaves an unexplained prerequisite. Showing local conditionality alone does not refute that response. The additional requirement that the aggregate possess an unconditional existential basis is the contested philosophical commitment.

The cannonball comparison in O’Connor’s discussion is useful as a counter to one insufficient test: absence of a first member does not establish explanatory completeness. But an external cause of one specially constructed beginningless segment does not by itself prove that every infinite physical history has an external productive basis. Permitting synchronic or atemporal production blocks a quick impossibility argument; it does not independently establish such production. These qualifications preserve the real argument without turning an analogy into a general demonstration.

Conversely, this limitation does not erase the rationale. The affirmative side can ask why dependent actuality becomes adequately available merely by extending the dependency description. The rival still owes a substantive account of why its explanatory stopping condition is adequate. Neither side can settle that issue by graph topology or by noting that the other side uses a premise.

The new source therefore earns convergent philosophical credit, not a second independent empirical verification of a common source. Its status is especially clear because the author distinguishes tentative endorsement of an explanatory schema from possessing a completed explanation (31–33).

### 4. Necessary sourcehood is argued conditionally, not established by a modal label

The direct anti-brute return isolates the substantive Ibn Taymiyya commitment: complete absence of external existential need is positive intrinsic existential sufficiency, not merely unexplained actuality; this sufficiency excludes possible nonbeing. The stronger beginningless brute rival denies that connection. It need not claim self-production or production by nothing.

O’Connor does not offer that brute original as his terminus. His necessary-source proposal belongs with intrinsic adequacy, and thus supports the same broad anti-brute aspiration. His modal and causal assumptions, however, do not independently prove the intrinsic-adequacy thesis to a rival who denies it. His general preference for explanations supplies a defeasible comparative reason to resist a brute stopping point; the comparison remains sensitive to the coherence, scope and cost of the proposed alternative.

A hypothesis that a necessary being exists is not an established witness of one. Essential productive qualifications, nonaccidental constitution, and the appropriate explanatory connection are additional substantive commitments. Note 15 is significant because it recognizes that a primitive accidental assembly of the divine nature would compromise the claimed completion. Mutual accompaniment alone is insufficient; it is the claimed essential status of the nature that is doing the work. The detailed warrant for that status is not supplied here.

The concluding S5 point likewise cannot manufacture actuality from intelligible wording. Genuine possibility of an essentially necessary existent would have the stated modal consequence under the assumed logic. Establishing that genuine possibility is the substantive task. Suspension of judgment is not an assertion of impossibility.

There are two readings of the passage’s move from necessary existence to complete explanation. On the thin reading, a being exists necessarily and therefore the entire actual contingent field is adequately explained. That inference lacks the productive and coverage premises. A necessarily existent reality need not, solely in virtue of that modal status, explain any particular received target. The original bearer interface carefully obtains nonreceipt before applying ContingencyNeed; it does not reverse that implication from a world-quantified necessity predicate.

The stronger reading is supported by the earlier discussion. Printed 33 describes adoption of an existence-explaining schema as commitment to a necessary ontic dependency of contingent physical reality on the proposed necessary being, a structure applying across possible reality. With that rich essential explanatory profile carried into §8, the argument is not merely the thin inference. It has built in the relevant modal coverage, and its necessary-being shorthand can be read accordingly. This is the charitable interpretation. The scan’s phrase “source of an independent contingent reality” at 42 is genuine print, but earlier explicit dependency claims favour reading it as a distinct created reality or an imprecise phrase; no objection here depends on treating it as denial of causal dependence.

The remaining qualification survives this charity: the rich dependency profile is a substantive proposed commitment, not a consequence of necessary existence alone. Nor does the chapter establish that this profile is genuinely possible. A critic may deny its guaranteed coverage, or suspend judgment about it, without claiming that every necessarily existent being is impossible. Optional creation does not itself defeat stable coverage: the rich profile can require that whatever contingent reality exists is adequately supplied, without requiring some contingent creation in every world.

There is also an exhaustiveness condition. Transporting the status of this rich profile into a conclusion about **every possible complete explanation** requires that all adequate alternatives have the relevant essential structure. The admitted principle-based alternatives and the expressly qualified schema comparison prevent treating that exhaustiveness as a theorem of S5. Thus §8 has a defensible conditional modal reading, while neither bare necessity nor the chapter’s incomplete adjudication of alternatives supplies an unconditional proof that any denial of complete explanation here entails the impossibility of complete explanation everywhere. This preserves the passage’s modal point without securing the productive mapping or universal explanatory coverage by logic alone.

### 5. Free choice and the stated explanatory principles

It would be inaccurate to charge the chapter with defending an exceptionless, strongly contrastive PSR and then silently exempting free choice. It explicitly rejects that strong requirement. The meaningful issue is whether the replacement principles preserve enough of the original explanatory ambition.

The causal principle targets contingent concrete individuals, their collections, and events among them. Its distinct-source demand does not straightforwardly classify every act attributed to a necessary bearer as a separately received contingent individual. The more general alternative ranges over contingent events or truths but allows an account of why explanation is unavailable. Hence neither principle should be silently substituted for the inherited bearer-specific ContingencyNeed.

Necessary motivating reasons also do not by themselves fix which reason is efficacious on an occasion. Holding multiple suitable reasons fixed while different acts remain possible is precisely where the stronger contrastive question survives. Note 14 distinguishes the existence of the reason from its being explanatory of a particular act. That distinction is valid and blocks the inference that every aspect of the source used in explaining a contingent act must itself exist contingently. It does not provide a further necessitating answer to why this exercise occurred.

A critic may ask that latter question without treating the causal relation as an additional object requiring a second productive event. Equally, the critic cannot infer the absence of production merely from the absence of a necessitating contrastive basis. The inherited power-chapter report already established why both shortcuts miss the strongest comparison.

On the strong contrastive PSR, the remaining unexplained contrast is a failure of the standard. On O’Connor’s alternative, the actual event can be causally explained and the lack of the further explanation can have a substantive explanation in the kind of power involved. There is no immediate formal inconsistency in this distinction. Whether the latter explanation is adequate, nonvacuous and independently warranted is the issue; merely restating that the mechanism is indeterministic would not establish its instantiation or justify every claimed explanatory limit.

### 6. A genuine rationale, with an explicit cost in explanatory scope

The proposed standard has more support than renaming the actual outcome. A real productive relation and an account of the power’s operation would provide causal information absent from a merely brute occurrence. General acceptance of some informative nonnecessitating causal explanations is relevant evidence against identifying every genuine explanation with deduction of its effect. The scientific comparison is used here as O’Connor’s argument, not as a new independent verification of a particular quantum interpretation or of divine agency.

Still, three additional steps would be required for an unqualified victory: warrant that the relevant source and power genuinely exist or are genuinely possible; show that the proposed account explains the actual exercise rather than only its availability; and justify counting a principled absence of contrastive explanation as completing rather than curtailing the explanatory task. The analogy alone supplies none of those at divine or whole-history scope.

The shift of explanandum is therefore real and openly defended. Concrete production, the fact that some contingent reality exists, and the complete contrastive history do not receive identical answers. Explanations are not automatically closed under every logical consequence or redescription of what they explain, so failure to transfer an explanation across these questions is not by itself a contradiction. But the distinctions cannot be used to claim that the original stronger question has been answered. The late optional-creation concession is especially decisive on that point.

The meta-explanatory response also requires a principled asymmetry. If an alleged original can simply be assigned the feature “brute”, and that assignment counts as a nonvacuous explanation of its inexplicability, the broader principle loses its force against the brute rival. The defensible contrast is that a warranted indeterministic productive nature explains both actual production and a structural limit on further explanation, while a brute label supplies no productive or existential explanation of its own actuality. Whether the proposed nature really has that warrant remains open. Likewise, a hypothetical autonomous noncausal basis would need assessment on its actual ontology and existential authority, not dismissal by vocabulary alone. O’Connor’s expressed dissatisfaction with principle-based explanation (29) does not close that separate applicability issue.

### 7. Remaining discriminator and bounded disposition

The useful discriminator is now exact:

**Can the actual occurrence and particular exercise be fully adequate in their productive respect when all independently specified productive conditions and reasons remain compatible with alternatives, and can a warranted account of that nonnecessitation discharge the residual explanatory demand?**

An affirmative case must independently support the claimed productive ontology, its instantiation or relevant possibility, and its explanatory stopping rule. A negative case must justify the stronger differentiating-basis requirement without assuming that requirement in the meaning of production, multiplying causal relations into separately produced objects, or identifying a necessary source with an entirely necessary history.

For the present operation:

- Retain strong affirmative credit for the source’s original productive and anti-brute orientation.
- Retain its serious challenge to exhaustive contrastive explanation, with the PCE/PSR change and optional-creation qualification explicit.
- Do not certify an actual nonnecessitating original, a divine-agency possibility, an actual counterexample to Completion, or a proof of the original source’s necessity.
- Do not transport disagreement over contrastive adequacy into a negative result against C(F).
- Keep whole-field productive adequacy and restricted resource/bearer applicability at their inherited philosophical status. This paper does not resolve the autonomous-qualitative alternative or supply its missing bridge.
