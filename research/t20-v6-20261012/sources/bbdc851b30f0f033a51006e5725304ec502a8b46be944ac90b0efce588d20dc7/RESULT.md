The application keeps the same target, resource and bearer rather than replacing the argument by abstract true/false flags.

### B. Intrinsic necessity

CP23 §§1 and 5–7 affirm the fixed-g existential-adequacy judgment, including its modal reach. Its exercise argument supports that appraisal; it does not independently derive the modal force from anti-self-production. The report expressly says its steps are one developed rational argument, not newly independent neutral axioms, and that the judgment is not accepted merely because its author announces it.

The report itself identifies the relevant fixed-g need instance under intended existential meanings. A different direct formal route can avoid the adapter; this investigation is not a new proof or mathematical support-minimisation campaign.

A genuine challenge to the modal-adequacy judgment reaches the necessity appraisal. A challenge only to the quoted passage's attribution, or only to one supporting explanation, has a different target. It calls for assessing how much of the actual rational support used that attribution or explanation. It is not automatically a defeat of the substantive judgment, and not automatically harmless either. The rational ground's independent use cannot be established by giving it a different document ID.

The retained CND assessment explicitly preserves T17 P00048–P00061's affirmative constitutive rationale. It says failure of the proposed CND route to improve warrant does not withdraw that result.
