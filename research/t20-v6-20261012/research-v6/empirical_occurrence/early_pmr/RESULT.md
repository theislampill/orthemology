### Corrected AT-to-AW interpretation

AW says trace-equivalent hypotheses have distance0 for every registered root transcript. AT establishes **distributional** equality, whereas AW’s distance is defined between **deterministic** hypothesis-indexed codewords. To infer zero distance, the symbols must themselves be deterministic registered responses, or common deterministic functions of the equal trace laws, or an explicitly justified common coupling with identical outputs. This premise is not supplied merely by law equality.

A bounded control makes the issue precise: with an unobserved fair bit U, outputs U under A and 1−U under R both have the same fair-bit distribution, while their pathwise values under that coupling differ at every seed. This gives no observed discriminator: making U visible would change the experiment. It only disproves the unqualified inference from equality in distribution to equality of coupled realized symbols.

For identical observed transcript laws, every common decoder still has identical output laws. Therefore no deterministic zero-error identification of the two hypotheses from that experiment is possible. That is the correct AT consequence; it should not be expressed as a deterministic Hamming distance without defining the intervening code model.
