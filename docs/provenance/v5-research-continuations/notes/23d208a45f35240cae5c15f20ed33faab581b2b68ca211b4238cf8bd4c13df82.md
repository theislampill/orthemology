# An exact infinite three-message family

Candidate ordinary finite mathematics, independent review pending. Fixed codebook,
receiver-private randomness, informed cooperative sender, static corruption set.
No public menu randomization is supplied or used. All accepted predecessors and
the separately frozen two-message packet remain unchanged.

## Theorem

For every integer s>=3,

V_fixed(2s+1,4,3)=1/s.

In particular V_fixed(9,4,3)=1/4, closing the third previously retained gap.
This family is outside the gcd-attainment family because gcd(2s+1,2)=1.
For the inherited restoration domain u>=2b+1, take s>=4; the case s=3 is a
valid combinatorial result but is outside that inherited parameter restriction.
The endpoint s=2 also has value1/2 by a separate elementary argument below.

## Upper bound

Use disjoint uniform blocks of sizes1,s,s. If a four-root corruption set misses
the singleton, that message has zero risk. Otherwise only three corrupt roots
remain for the two s-blocks, so at least one block contains at most one corrupt
root; the sender's risk is at most1/s. Conversely, corrupt the singleton and one
root in each large block, then pad to four roots. All three message risks are at
least1/s. Thus this codebook's worst-case risk is exactly1/s.

## Lower bound

Fix any three probability rows on u=2s+1 roots and put t=1/s.

### Branch 1: some atom reaches t

If some row assigns a root r mass at least t, include r and discard this satisfied
row. For each other row p put a=p(r). When a<1, normalize its residual row on the
2s remaining roots by1-a. When a=1 it is already satisfied; substitute any
auxiliary residual probability row. Accepted arithmetic-family theorem F2 gives
V_fixed(2s,3,2)=1/s because N=3-2+1=2 and gcd(2s,2)=2>=M.
Equal blocks s+s attain that value. Consequently there are at most three further roots
whose normalized mass is at least t under both residual rows. Their union with r
has original mass at least a+(1-a)t>=t (or1 when a=1), as required.

### Branch 2: every atom is smaller than t

Consider the nonempty compact polytope
P={x in [0,1]^u : p_m dot x>=t for m=1,2,3}.
Minimize sum_i x_i and choose an optimal vertex. The uniform x_i=t is feasible,
so sum_i x_i<=ut=2+t<3. As in the accepted general lower-bound proof, a vertex
has at most three strictly fractional coordinates: more would leave a nonzero
two-sided feasible perturbation fixing the active row constraints and bound
coordinates. This argument concerns the full continuous polytope.

If at most one coordinate is1, round every positive coordinate upward. The result
uses at most1+3=4 roots and meets all row thresholds. If there are no fractional
coordinates, at most two roots are selected and the claim is immediate. The only
remaining case has exactly two unit coordinates. Let A be their two-set. The
sum of the fractional coordinates is at most t.

For each row not already satisfied by A, define the positive deficit
 d_m=t-p_m(A)>0
and its set of individual deficit-covering roots
 H_m={i outside A : p_m(i)>=d_m}.
Feasibility and the strict atom bound imply
 d_m <= sum_{fractional i} p_m(i)x_i < t sum_{fractional i}x_i <= t^2.
The strict inequality is justified because a positive deficit requires at least
one positive fractional coordinate, and each atom at those coordinates is<t.

We claim |H_m|>=s-1. Otherwise at most s-2 of the 2s-1 roots outside A reach d_m.
Using p_m(i)<t on them and p_m(i)<d_m on the others gives
 1-p_m(A) < (s-2)t+(s+1)d_m.
If fewer than s-2 roots reach d_m, this upper bound is still valid because
0<d_m<t and adding a t-bound in place of a d_m-bound only increases it.
Since 1-p_m(A)=1-t+d_m and (s-2)t=1-2t, the inequality implies
 t < s d_m,
or d_m>1/s^2=t^2, a contradiction. This proves the cardinality claim.

If at most two rows remain unsatisfied, select one member of each corresponding
H_m. If all three do, then their sets cannot be pairwise disjoint because
 3(s-1)>2s-1
for s>=3. Select a root shared by two of the H_m and any root from the third.
At most two further roots satisfy all positive deficits. Together with A this
gives a set of at most four roots whose mass is at least t under all three rows.
Padding to four roots preserves those inequalities. The universal lower bound
matches the construction, proving equality.

## Optional endpoint s=2

For u=5,b=4,M=3, each probability row has at most one coordinate strictly greater
than1/2. At most three roots can have such a coordinate in any row. Choose a
remaining root r, so every p_m(r)<=1/2, and corrupt its four-root complement.
Every row has corruption mass at least1/2. Blocks1+2+2 attain1/2.

## Exact controls

The checker uses exact Fraction arithmetic throughout. From the uniform
fractional vector it repeatedly moves in an exactly computed nullspace of the
three equality rows, choosing a direction that never increases its coordinate
sum, until at most three coordinates remain fractional. This supplies precisely
the feasible fractional point required by the rounding proof, without a floating
optimizer. Heavy-atom cases use exact enumeration of the small three-root
residual witnesses. Every returned corruption set is checked directly.
