# Epistemic warrant and explanatory scope

9 October 2026. Bounded T20 research continuation. No repository change, integration, owner acceptance, programme closure, or historical-duration credit.

Later source-coverage update: the remaining portions of the same 1997 manuscript have now been read continuously. See READING_COMPLETION_ADDENDUM.md and its separate ledger. The original targeted-reading ledger is preserved historically; the central assessment below is unchanged.

## Result

**The inference from a failure of global PSR to the defeat of every empirical warrant is not secured by the local causal requirements of knowledge alone.** It additionally requires an argument that the permitted explanatory failure extends to epistemically relevant alternatives for our actual grounds. The strongest primary treatment inspected here explicitly supplies such an argument, rather than overlooking the issue. Its force depends on substantive symmetry, modal-closeness, and epistemic-access premises.

**The positive epistemic case for causal explanation is substantial.** An appearance that is accidentally true and disconnected from its alleged object is not rescued by being vivid. Nor does a stipulated tiny probability establish that uncaused appearances are rare. But these points support a demand for adequate provenance and truth connection; they do not, without the additional bridges, entail a common external explanation of every plurality of basic natural facts.

The earned increment is a source-sensitive discrimination among three arguments, followed by a premise-independence interpretation. It is not a new theorem about human knowledge or a refutation that preserves all of the stronger argument's premises.

## 1. The actual sources change the target

The inherited Pruss chapter's §§2.1–2.2.2 supplies the initial target. Its compressed epistemological argument links unexplained perceptions, absence of objective probabilities, and empirical skepticism. The present task preserves §2.2.1's distinction: apparent self-evidence may provide a believer with basic warrant even when reporting it does not persuade a dissenter. Disagreement alone does not disprove self-evidence or destroy warrant. Nothing below turns that dialectical difficulty into a refutation. [Author chapter](http://alexanderpruss.com/papers/LCA.html).

Koons's 1997 source is *A New Look at the Cosmological Argument*, *American Philosophical Quarterly* 34(2), 193–211, as verified in the [journal issue](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i20009890). The read witness is an author-uploaded manuscript dated 18 December 1996, mirrored with its ResearchGate provenance cover; its manuscript pagination is not the journal's. In §§4–5, causation need not necessitate its effect. Empirical success supports a defeasible causal default; §5.2 invokes the causal provenance of traces, memories, records, and relevant future states. This differs from an exceptionless global-PSR proof. §§8.1–8.9 answer several restrictions and acknowledge a substantive comparative burden when the proposed cosmic cause is exceptional. Those answers are retained. [Manuscript witness](https://lionandlambapologetics.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/A-New-Look-at-the-Cosmological-Argument.pdf).

The directly relevant stronger treatment is Koons and Pruss, *Skepticism and the principle of sufficient reason*, published online 2020, journal issue 2021, *Philosophical Studies* 178, 1079–1099. The full 21-page author-hosted text, including all sections, notes, license, and references, was read. It is not merely a commentary on their earlier claims. The paper's central conclusion concerns our ability to **know that we have empirical knowledge**, and its PSR concerns **pluralities of basic natural facts**, not all true propositions. Its arguments and replies therefore govern the stronger assessment below. [Publisher and open-access text](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-020-01482-3).

## 2. The strongest argument, with its bridge premises exposed

The following is a reconstruction, separating commitments that must not be compressed into the word “PSR.” Locations refer to the 2020/2021 article.

1. **Local epistemic condition.** Knowledge depends on suitable truth-connected grounds, including appropriate causal links or modal reliability. Merely true appearances with a broken epistemic route do not suffice. §§3 and 5.
2. **Relevant counterpossibility.** If the restricted PSR is not robustly true in an epistemically accessible scenario, local appearances or their intermediary links can occur uncaused in relevant worlds of that scenario. This needs both a generalization from one explanatory exception and a claim about relevance/proximity. §§3–4.
3. **Symmetry and scope.** A priori assurance cannot arbitrarily privilege these particular data. The authors require a robust class-based causal principle whose applicability to data and intermediary links is also a priori knowable. They argue for the class of basic natural facts and against restrictions to non-first events, facts lacking infinite regresses, or facts other than low-entropy cosmic totals. §4.
4. **Chance bridge.** Objective probabilities require an explanatorily prior basis. Accordingly, genuinely unexplained events cannot simply be assigned a benign propensity or stable frequency. A low observed proportion is insufficient unless counterfactual stability is warranted, and the relevant proportion concerns belief-producing events rather than events indiscriminately. §§5.1 and 6.
5. **Defeat/relevance bridge.** The resulting uncaused alternatives must count as live threats to the actual belief-forming method, not merely as descriptions we can utter. For the safety version they must be sufficiently close; for the probability version they must prevent the relevant reliability from being determinately high. §§5.1–5.5.
6. **Second-order and access bridge.** To know that we have empirical knowledge we must exclude the bad modal scenarios. Empirical support cannot independently certify the prerequisite of all empirical support, so the required restricted PSR must be knowable a priori. §§3–4. The conclusion does not simply instantiate the unrestricted KK principle. §10 argues separately for a weaker connection between first-order knowledge and the possibility of some second-order empirical knowledge.
7. **Plural explanatory scope and concrete realization.** The principle requires an explanation external to each selected plurality of basic natural facts, including the totality. The supernatural conclusion additionally assumes that every explanation of particular facts includes a particular fact (§2, PDF lines 171–173). This is stronger than each local datum's having a suitable cause. Their natural beings are wholly composed of things with finitely measurable intrinsic attributes or bounds; their supernatural beings have a simple part unbounded and infinite in intrinsic measure. Those special definitions, and the relevance-entailment condition for a fact's including such a being, matter. Neither “supernatural” nor “particular explanans” here automatically means a Necessary Being or an original productive bearer. §9 supplies an additional argument about unexplained totality and future-directed safety.

Granting these bridges gives the transcendental argument genuine force. A model that merely declines them does not contradict the resulting conditional. Conversely, the fact that six familiar epistemic conditions are examined is not six independent derivations of the symmetry/proximity bridge: the applications share it.

Their two-dimensional framework is essential: an epistemic scenario represents an entire modal reality, with its own actual and merely possible worlds. A correct local neighborhood inside the true scenario does not by itself exclude another epistemically accessible scenario whose nearby worlds are deceptive. Actual first-order knowledge, warranted confidence that one knows, and knowledge that one knows are distinct possible achievements. No premise-independence construction below establishes the latter two merely by satisfying a first-order modal condition. Nor is having confidence in one's knowledge the same as assigning objective chances to skeptical scenarios.

## 3. Does a brute background entail uncaused perceptions?

There are three distinct cases.

**A brute universal law.** An unexplained law can still explain a particular outcome together with the relevant particular circumstances. “Its explanation is not itself explained” and “it has no explanation” are different claims, unless a further principle requires every adequate explanation to be ultimately grounded. Moreover, a universal law is not a wholly particular basic natural fact under the stronger article's explicit restriction. A world with only such an unexplained law might violate all-facts PSR without violating their restricted PSR at all. It is therefore not an adequate counterexample to their actual conclusion.

**A brute particular outside the epistemic route.** An unexplained contingent particular does contradict the singleton instance of their restricted PSR, if it is basic and natural. It does not, by quantifier logic alone, entail that a different particular can occur unexplained, still less that that possibility is close to this cognitive episode. A recombination, symmetry, or anti-restriction principle is needed. Mere contingency does not establish free recombinability of every causal history.

**A brute ancestor of an otherwise genuine route.** If an initial object or background state is unexplained but produces an appropriately reliable downstream signal, there is no immediate contradiction in the downstream signal's explaining the belief. To infer that the later epistemic relation is defective requires either a no-brute-ancestry epistemic condition or the same modal-instability argument. The broken-link case is different: if the signal appears independently of the target and only coincidentally matches it, the route to knowledge of the target has failed. This distinction does not promise that every belief about the remote past survives.

The 2020/2021 authors already confront local restriction. Their response deserves credit: an arbitrary exemption cannot justify our confidence that present experience is not itself an exempt first event or an entire tiny cosmos. An anti-skeptical localist therefore owes a positive account of the legitimacy of its local evidence, modal standards, and treatment of defeaters. The localist cannot merely announce that exceptions occur elsewhere.

But the stronger conclusion is not forced by that criticism alone. The requirement that all second-order warrant be secure across every scenario left open by a priori information is itself substantive. An externalist or knowledge-first alternative may take actual epistemic contact and appropriately local modal conditions as basic, without claiming to infer them from a global PSR. Whether that alternative earns reflective warrant requires argument; it is not ruled out simply by noting that it lacks an a priori proof of the whole causal order.

## 4. Objective chance is not automatically the measure of every epistemic alternative

The authors' strongest positive point is that **ignorance of a chance distribution does not license inventing a reassuring one**. An arbitrary low prior, a uniform count of possible appearances, or a low global event frequency does not establish the truth connection of a particular belief-forming method. The present result uses none of those devices.

However, the following statements remain different:

- A skeptical hypothesis has no objective chance.
- We do not know its objective chance.
- Its chance is high.
- It is epistemically close or salient.
- It defeats the actual grounds for this belief.

No numerical identity connects these claims. Even granting the authors' account of chance, passing from the first claim to the final two requires the relevance/defeat premises. A safety condition is stated in terms of the truth of beliefs in relevant alternatives; it does not, as a matter of its logical form, demand a numerical chance for every metaphysical hypothesis. Conversely, defining bad worlds as distant without a defensible modal account does not solve the epistemological problem.

This is not the objection that an ordinary knower cannot calculate a number. A theory may require an objectively determinate reliability without requiring the subject to know its exact numerical value. The issue is whether such a probability must exist for every skeptical alternative, and whether failure of a global explanatory principle deprives the actual local process of the epistemic condition that the theory requires.

Their §5.1 worst-case Bayesian discussion must also be kept conditional. If a probability is undefined, Bayes's theorem cannot make it numerically one. Using one as an adversarial upper bound instead yields a worst-case analysis, whose relevance to knowledge needs justification. This is a discrimination between undefined chance and a robust worst-case standard, not an independent refutation of the entire argument. The publisher HTML corroborates the relevant prose and formula; no conclusion rests on damaged PDF glyphs.

For their explicitly probabilistic accounts, a lack of determinate reliability can genuinely block knowledge. That does not establish that an unexplained background fact makes the reliability of every separate caused process indeterminate. A chance-bearing local causal mechanism and the ultimate explanation of that mechanism are distinct explanatory questions. Conditional reliability alone is insufficient if uncontrolled intrusions are genuinely live; making those intrusions live is exactly the additional bridge under assessment.

## 5. Replies already earned by the stronger authors

**Neo-Humean response:** §7 explicitly allows that if a suitable neo-Humean account of modality is true and adequate, empirical knowledge need not depend on PSR. The authors then challenge that account's justification of closeness and counterfactual direction. Consequently, this is a dispute over the best modal and causal theory, not a logical demonstration that a local alternative is contradictory. Their further causal-powers argument depends on the claim that natural laws govern caused interactions without constraining uncaused intrusions. An alternative primitive-law or modal account would need its own defense; merely invoking “the laws” does not answer them.

**Origins-essentialism response:** §8 distinguishes identity from epistemic counterparts. Declaring that an uncaused duplicate is not literally this belief does not automatically secure knowledge. Counterpart mistakes and unreliable testimony about one's origins show why a vacuous modal test can mislead. The accompanying model allows deceptive counterparts to count as genuine candidate failures; it does not define them away.

**Future-directed response:** §9 argues from a brute totality to nearby worlds with altered causal powers and diverging futures. That addresses the global totality rather than leaving it ignored. It still requires an account of why the relevant power changes are close and sufficient to defeat each claimed future knowledge. The fact that some arbitrarily small changes can cause large divergences is not alone a proof that every modest future-directed belief lacks a stable margin. This continuation does not purport to settle actual physical predictability or import a new empirical campaign.

**First/second-order response:** §10 explicitly rejects reliance on full KK. Its four advertised fallback replies are: infer the restricted PSR as the simplest explanation of first-order empirical knowledge even if metaknowledge is surrendered; give PSR at least the same epistemic status as the presumption of empirical knowledge; appeal to metaknowledge of particular ordinary cases even if a general claim is doubtful; and argue that the universal impossibility of empirical metaknowledge itself supplies a defeater of first-order knowledge. The article then adds a fifth reminder about its future-knowledge argument. The fourth claim is not that every instance of knowledge requires knowing that very instance is knowledge, nor that a subject's mere failure to reflect defeats it. The proposed defeat from the universal inability to obtain any such metaknowledge needs a separate argument. The IBE and epistemic-status fallback routes also retain the dependence claim at issue; they cannot simply inherit it once that bridge is disputed. Their potential explanatory merit should nevertheless be assessed rather than discarded merely because the deductive route is conditional.

The remaining questions are whether every scenario not excluded a priori must remain relevant for reflective warrant, and whether a demonstrated universal impossibility of empirical metaknowledge would undermine first-order warrant in the way proposed. Merely distinguishing first-order from second-order knowledge does not answer these developed claims.

### The actual symmetry and anti-neo-Humean arguments

The symmetry bridge is argued, not merely announced. Its assessment turns on the adequacy of those arguments:

- **Finite candidate elimination (§4).** Conditions (i)–(iii) require an a priori knowable, robust principle and a priori knowable coverage of the data and intermediary links. Condition (iv) selects a strongest such principle. The authors motivate a finite candidate list by the implausibility of our being wholly ignorant of the fundamental a priori principle underwriting knowledge; they argue that anthropocentric restrictions cannot have the required robustness and test three concrete alternatives. These are real reasons against those particular restrictions. They do not alone establish that the list is exhaustive. More importantly, identifying the strongest eligible principle does not show that metaknowledge requires that strongest principle rather than some independently adequate weaker one. If every adequate weaker principle really entailed or required the selected stronger one, that further connection would need to be established. The already disputed a priori-certification demand bears much of the weight here.
- **Mosaic similarity versus law preservation (§7).** The authors argue that a Humean ought to determine closeness from qualitative resemblance, so small qualitative departures should remain close even when they violate the actual laws. This shows a genuine burden for a Humean metric; it does not determine the metric uniquely. A similarity ordering that gives weight to systematic, lawlike patterns can itself supervene on the qualitative mosaic. Supervenience on the mosaic does not entail equal weight for local pointwise resemblance and global pattern preservation. Conversely, privileging patterns solely to rescue knowledge would be question-begging; an adequate rival must motivate that weighting independently. The present witness does not supply a complete motivation.
- **Direction of counterfactuals (§7).** The appeal to time-reversal-invariant laws and to asymmetrical counterfactual dependence gives a positive reason to prefer a theory with genuine directed powers. The longer Pruss 2003 argument cited for that claim is not independently re-audited here. Even success against a Humean treatment would establish that a better account of direction is needed, not by itself that every explanatory exception permits local intrusions. Directed causal powers and universal explanatory closure are distinguishable commitments.
- **Powers becoming ineffective (§7).** The authors argue that causeless changes in powers or outcomes would destroy their projectible explanatory work. This is strong against an ontology permitting unrestricted, epistemically close alterations of every local power. It is not yet an argument from merely one brute fact to that ontology. The missing generalization cannot be supplied simply by restating that all relevant restrictions must themselves be explained, unless the entitlement to that stronger explanatory demand is established. A proposed non-Humean local restriction owes a positive modal basis; the global defender owes the argument that no such basis can remain intact in the presence of the specified brute fact.

The two sides therefore face asymmetrical but substantive obligations. The permissive model only exhibits a coherent interpretation of weaker premises. The authors' dialectical package proposes why that interpretation is epistemically inadequate. The present audit finds the named inferential dependencies still substantive, not a contradiction in either completed metaphysics and not an absence of authorial response.

## 6. The precise independence result and its limits

PREMISE_INDEPENDENCE.md specifies a small family of causal interpretations. A contingent brute background particular coexists with an operative, content-sensitive local route from an object through evidence to belief. Nearby target changes produce corresponding changes in evidence and belief. A deceptive counterpart is allowed; moving it into the relevant neighborhood destroys the displayed safety. Nothing is inferred from an arbitrary count of worlds, and there is no assigned probability for the brute fact.

This demonstrates only that **local provenance and the displayed safety condition do not, by themselves, entail global PSR**. It does not establish actual human knowledge, the metaphysical adequacy of the interpretation, a complete theory of mind, second-order warrant, or a model preserving Koons–Pruss symmetry and neighborhood premises. It makes the exact competing commitments inspectable.

Inherited Dretske results constrain this test. The informative feature must actually produce the belief; a merely available correlation is inadequate. Actual epistemic use is not identical to any upstream causal contribution. Passing a necessary condition is not sufficient for knowledge. No demand for an articulated metabelief, a simultaneous causal trigger from abstract truth, or an independent introspective proof of external reliability is reinstated. The unread chapter remains unread; the inspected primary epistemology's legitimate achievements remain credited.

## 7. Productive-ground interface

The positive result is a defeasible reason to seek adequate causal/explanatory support and reject merely accidental epistemic contact. It does not uniquely select global explanation as the only admissible stopping point.

Nor does this epistemic argument itself establish the inherited productive premises: entire-existence reception, actual efficient supply, original nonreceipt, a complete determiner of the particular outcome, or the exclusion of other original contributors. Even if the restricted plural PSR and its supernatural conclusion are accepted, the particular-explanation and ontology assumptions must still be transported into those predicates. Necessary or supernatural explanation is not automatically the productive-bearer relation under investigation.

**Retained judgment:** do not dismiss PSR as epistemically baseless, and do not credit the compressed skeptical argument as a premise-free demonstration of its global productive application. Its strongest inspected defense adds serious, identifiable metaphysical and epistemological premises. The modest local interpretation shows where those premises do work; it does not defeat them while holding them fixed.
