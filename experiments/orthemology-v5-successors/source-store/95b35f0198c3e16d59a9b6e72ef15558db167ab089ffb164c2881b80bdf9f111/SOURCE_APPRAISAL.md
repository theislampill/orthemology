## Source identity, transmission and limits

The institutional Ibn al-Qayyim website supplied a Shamela-generated Word file and a 1998-page composite scan of the edition edited by Abd al-Rahman ibn Hasan ibn Qa'id, reviewed by Muhammad Ajmal al-Islahi and Sulayman ibn Abd Allah al-Umayr, Dar Ata'at al-Ilm/Dar Ibn Hazm, third 1440/2019, first Ibn Hazm. Exact source hashes are in the [source metadata](../source_manifest.json). The electronic preparer explicitly adds bracketed second-edition addenda absent from the third printing. Thus one should distinguish Ibn al-Qayyim, quoted earlier authors, Qa'id's critical text, his modern notes, electronic supplied additions and our reading notes.

The editor's preliminary attribution is cumulative, using title leaves, reception, cross-references, Meccan reminiscences and intellectual affinities. It is a reasoned editorial case, not our independent examination of every manuscript. The principal reported D witness is Ibn Bardis 766; Q is Ahmad al-Sa'idi 841, reportedly collated with the autograph, but may derive from D before correction. Their agreement cannot automatically be counted as independent testimony. T 889 is described as distinct; H and N are first-half copies; Y's quality/date are questioned; S is a selected abridgment. All these stemmatic judgments remain the editor's unless separately inspected. The scanned plate samples cannot establish a complete stemma.

The introduction openly identifies substantial unattributed borrowing, especially the nature section's relation to Dalail wa-l-itibar and material mediated through Ghazali, Razi and Ibn Taymiyya. Repeated arguments in different books may share ancestry. Reports of alternative authorships of Dalail and judgments against earlier editions are retained as editorial argument, not silently settled historical facts.

The book closes at 1603, but its promised conceptual second section on will is not fully realized. The editor I23–28 argues this from unfulfilled forward references and the received ending. A later manuscript division called a second part is not evidence that the planned conceptual second part survives. Conversely, incomplete execution of a programme is not proof that a whole written volume was lost. The distinction protects both the legitimate completeness of this received-body reading and the incompleteness of the historical project.

## Continuous argument, including material outside the central project

### Adam, history and the good, 3–127

The descent from paradise is presented as a setting for history-dependent goods: voluntary worship, patience, repentance, gratitude, prophetic teaching and recognition by contrast. Some goods have constitutive occasions; removing appetite and temptation would replace humans with another kind 13. Divine sufficiency therefore is not being expressed as the absence of all conditional relations. Yet this account does not establish that each actual suffering is indispensable or justify every token harm. The source offers a theological wisdom narrative with strong exclusivist and punitive commitments, which must remain visible.

The garden dispute 27–87 is deliberately not adjudicated. The author gives competing arguments and announces that the larger wisdom survives either answer 36/86–87. He treats logical entailment, generic/token descriptions, stage-relative predicates and evidence for consensus in detail. Ibn Qutayba's cited statement 51 is a quotation of Torah, not automatically his opinion. Ibn Taymiyya's answer 77–81 has a different apparent stance elsewhere, with chronology uncertain in the note. The argument 68 exposes an illicit converse between garden location and prior creation of heaven. Keeping rival voices distinct is necessary to reading this long section honestly.

The following covenant/guidance discussion distinguishes sufficient signs from biconditionals, fear from actual harm, and positive felicity from bare absence of punishment. Works are genuine causes but not an independently sufficient price 21; grace and others' prayers confer benefits without the same individual work 106. Jinn's ultimate placement is not asserted beyond evidence 107. The cognitive/volitional powers 108–114, inaccessible revelation 119, stages of resurrection 123–124 and Meccan authorial programme 125–127 complete this unit. None should disappear simply because its details are orthogonal to numerical-source arguments.

### Merits of knowledge and moral psychology,131–336

The knowledge encomium uses scripture, hadith, companions, poetry, analogy and educational experience. These have differing evidential roles. A statement's morally plausible content is not the same as reliable prophetic attribution; the author and editor sometimes separate them explicitly. Better knowledge is determined by its object and proper use, not mere quantity. Scholars may transmit accurately without deep inference, or understand and teach new applications; both functions matter 163–166.

The main epistemic knot 243–281 is staged rather than flat. One school makes knowledge entail guidance; the other adduces knowingly resisting people. Ibn al-Qayyim resolves an equivocation 263–264: knowledge can be an apt incomplete cause without being the entire sufficient cause. Recipient disposition, missing conditions and contrary motives intervene. The ten impediment classes include status, possessions, kinship, homeland, inherited commitments and habit, not merely lack of information. Later refusal can damage cognition itself 271–278, but later feedback does not prove initial absence of recognition. At 280 intact faculties plus acquired aversion are expressly distinguished from a coercion thesis. Historical claims about people's interior motives and group dispositions are not modern diagnoses.

Comparison of hearing and sight 288–293 is multidimensional. Learned excellence 295–300 can be intrinsic to a person yet acquired through others; intrinsic possession does not mean uncaused originality. Kind-relative perfection 301, involuntary versus acquired incapacity 313, and the rejection of an alleged necessary courage/generosity correlation 314–315 all block simplistic universal transfer. Historical disability, ethnicity, class and gender rhetoric is not adopted. The taxonomy of study, prayer and jihad culminates 336 in the requirement not to trade an obligation for an optional excellence.

### Learning, wealth, teaching and authority,337–514

Famous maxims often have weak or uncertain routes. Kumayl's report is followed by Khatīb's explanation before the author's own additions 348–351. Wax's receptivity and retention 354 show why firmness alone or softness alone is inadequate. At 355 a marginal conjecture has entered the body, and 356 a supposed consensus is narrowed by the editor. These are substantive evidence controls.

Teaching improves the teacher 363–364; shared knowledge can benefit a recipient without depleting the giver 372. That differs from the finite monetary stock whose retention and distribution compete 369–370. Social dependence may conflict under divergent interests 373–375, but these are conditions of a specific finite system, not proof that all relation is degradation. The pleasure/pain theory 376–383 and ascetic/humoral accounts remain historical anthropology rather than established science. Posthumous intellectual representation 390 is not bodily numerical persistence.

The strong intellectual ethics 396–398 demands symmetrical scrutiny of allies and opponents. The hidden-imam polemic 403–407 tests a claimed epistemic office by actual accessible guidance; the result cannot simply be universalized into every possible metaphysical plurality. Public revelation/proof, experiential certainty, grammar of political/religious titles, and Sufi quotations occupy 408–450. The author corrects quietism 437: if an action is commanded, confidence calls for exertion. The requirements of learning vary by actual duties 450, and the editor challenges whether apparently surplus grammar may nevertheless be necessary. No modern formal-logic verdict is imported from the anti-Greek rhetoric.

Entrusting preservation of knowledge 461–462 is beneficence, not a substitute for God's absence or incapacity. The apparent reliability guarantee for upright transmitters must still face the following weak-chain and interpretive apparatus 463–467. Humble requesting/attendance 478–481 becomes a positive perfection by its end. The Arabic phonetic/lexical arguments 498, gratitude's three components 499, blessings of teaching 500, causal moral responsibility 501–502, and distinction of intrinsic and instrumental goods 511–514 deserve reading in their own terms.

### Reflection as lived practice,515–537

This transition describes thought leading through knowledge and heart-state to will and action; recollection preserves what reflection acquires, and discussion can generate further knowledge. The proposed self-examination tracks desired good, its route, feared harm and its route; diagnosis distinguishes a valuable trait from actual possession and means of acquisition/removal. The difference between seeing a sign and grasping its import 535 is important. A merely completed reading is not the author's own ideal of considered understanding 537. Our bounded returns respond to that demand without pretending to have exhausted it.

## Return points for the project

1. **Nonduplication 90–91.** Different causes can ground a ruling one in type. The particular ruling is exemplified as ownership-by-sale or ownership-by-inheritance, thus already source-indexed. Transfer to a source-neutral particular effect requires an independently defended individuation premise. This source contains a nonduplication doctrine but does not remove that burden.
2. **Knowledge/will 224; 231–242; 263–280.** Knowledge's range includes impossible, possible and actual objects; power and will differ. Complete-cause knowledge entails effect knowledge only with knowledge of completeness 238. Productive and receptive knowledge are both perfections 241–242; the receptive examples concern us, so no direct doctrine of externally constituted divine perfection follows. Conditions/receptivity/impediments 264–265 and feedback 271–278 refine rather than erase action.
3. **Source and positive perfection 153; 295–300; 363–372; 461–462; 478–481.** Creaturely intrinsic good can be acquired, relational, shared and exercised through service. God-directed delegation can be beneficent without need. A condition should be classified by what it does, not automatically counted as essential deficiency.
4. **Real plural causes 501–502.** The creature acts through its own will and power. A downstream effect also depends on other factors; human acts are components and moral credit is proportionate. This is an explicit alternative to both causal erasure and independent creaturely total sufficiency.
5. **Ends 511–514.** Both knowing and acting can be intrinsic ends or means. Knowing God and worship are each ends; being intrinsically wanted does not make either independently sufficient for full felicity. Equal credited intention is distinguished from actually performing the expenditure.
6. **Identity/inference 68; 90–91; 246–247; 390; 460; 499.** Invalid converse, cause-indexed ruling, sign-based pragmatic converse, representation versus object, restricted verb versus unrestricted title, and gratitude's source-attribution each prevent a different collapse. They should not be run together as a single uniqueness argument.

## What this contributes, without premature verdict

The author offers a substantive common-Creator account: one source of creation, knowledge and wise ends can enable genuine subordinate causes and agents without needing them to repair a lack. The theory supplies positive explanatory content, not only a denial of rival gods. It can therefore enter an actual-world comparative inquiry into integrated order, intelligibility and agency.

That comparative claim is distinct from the universal proposition that every qualified original must supply every requisite for every effect. The first volume repeatedly distinguishes incomplete causes, alternate grounds, source-indexed effects, distributed knowledge and beneficent relations. These distinctions do not themselves prove independently original plurality, but they rule out obtaining the stronger universal simply by calling every condition a deficiency. Rival models must be compared at their actual explanatory costs and specific forms of dependence. No agreement with an earlier verdict was assumed.
