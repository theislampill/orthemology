# Independent inference review

9 October 2026. The reviewer read the baseline positive-agency report, specified Bayān and Miftāḥ selections, and the relevant existing wise-determination and de se reports. Initial review concerns the proposed source/inference operation, not a kernel theorem, full corpus audit, manuscript collation, or completed metaphysical rival model.

## Review returned before final report review

Complete self-knowledge plus genuine production does not, by itself, establish practical agency. It needs an independently warranted connection between cognition and production. That connection could be a practical-perfection principle, but need not take that particular form.

The useful findings are:

- Separate three claims: “the original completely knows itself”; “it knows this particular production as its own”; “it produces this because it recognizes and wills a fitting end.” Granting the first-to-second inference does not establish the third. Darʾ’s stronger exclusion of knowing-but-nonvoluntary original production supplies an additional substantive premise, rather than following from factual completeness.

- Bayān L0076–77 makes the distinction unusually explicit. Knowledge always matches its object, but only some knowledge has a productive role. Moreover, practical knowledge does not replace will and power. Crucially, self-knowledge’s failure to produce its own object does not show that self-knowledge cannot contribute to producing another object. That would be an illicit change of causal target.

- The strongest substantive increment is Bayān L0096–103’s assessment of purposeless beneficence. Producing something fitting or beneficial does not by itself constitute generosity or excellent agency: acting without an end is treated as defective despite the beneficial output. Thus the source offers a reason to require purposive production, not merely a stipulation that knowledge must be “action-guiding.” Applying that assessment to every proposed nonpersonal original still requires defending the relevant perfection comparison and eligibility. L0091–94 expressly warns against deriving disputed attributes from an agreed honorific whose meaning is contested.

The three controls consequently have different jobs:

1. An intrinsically structured blind original tests the antecedent claim of cognition. It cannot counterexample an inference that already assumes complete self-knowledge.
2. A productive original with practically idle cognition tests the cognition-to-agency bridge. “Idle” must modify its cognition, not its productive efficacy. This is a diagnostic interpretation of the thinner premises, not a demonstrated metaphysical possibility or a counterexample to Darʾ’s additional exclusion.
3. Necessarily concordant wise agents already possess the practical relation under investigation. Miftāḥ 2:953–55 protects their choice against the objection from necessary wise determination; it does not generate that practical relation from complete knowledge. Nor does it independently establish the particular end’s fittingness.

The previous de se report already distinguishes truthful cognition from practical basing. Repeating that distinction is not new credit. The bounded new contribution is the source-supported distinction between a fitting product and purposively excellent production, together with the warning that nonproductive self-knowledge concerns its own object, not every possible causal contribution. No uniqueness theorem or renewed modal-concordance test follows.

## Report-level review returned

The report is sound at its stated scope. Both corrections are explicit: §3 preserves the different causal targets, and §§1/6 limit novelty to the source-bound audit rather than rediscovering practical basing.

The Bayān L0096–0103 application has no material overreach. It expressly preserves the honorific-premise problem, distinguishes natural directedness from conscious purpose, and makes original-scope application depend on the independently defended perfection comparison.

One exact correction remains in §3: “The old test kept intentional agency” is too categorical because the old report already treated stronger practical basing as conditional. Replace that sentence with:

“The old test principally examined uniqueness under an agency-bearing interpretation, while already distinguishing truthful self-ascription from additional practical basing. This operation isolates and source-tests that latter transition without claiming the distinction itself as new.”

An optional precision sentence after the causal-target paragraph would make the other correction unmistakable:

“That self-knowledge does not produce its own object also does not show that it cannot contribute to producing another object.”

No further research or substantive restructuring is needed.
