# OSM learning-trajectory convergence crosswalk

## Disposition

The Sun et al. study was not missing from the repository. Decision 0015,
Decision 0024, `LAT-1`, the latent-state related-work note, and the dynamic
orthing application already contain its source custody, object separations,
reported model comparison, and nonclaims.

What was missing was a pointer from that existing work into the later AR8R
milestone and research-flywheel owners. This crosswalk supplies that pointer.
It does not duplicate the article, adopt a new theorem, or upgrade the paper
into validation of Orthemology.

## What the paper contributes

Within the reported two-alternative cue-delay-choice study and model
comparison, the useful constraints are:

1. identical immediate observations may require sequence-sensitive latent
   distinctions;
2. related terminal representations can arise through different learning
   trajectories;
3. high task performance can occur without the global representation geometry
   measured in the study;
4. architecture and objective can affect the representation that is learned;
5. altered cues and track geometry expose reuse, shift, reset, and rebinding as
   alternatives requiring further discrimination.

The strongest project-level use is methodological: a terminal outcome and even
a terminal geometry may leave the route and mechanism underdetermined.
Longitudinal evidence can therefore separate candidates that endpoint-only
tests cannot.

## What it does not contribute

The paper supplies no direct evidence for human noetic architecture, proper
function, objective warrant, agent identity, moral or spiritual restoration,
transcendental orthability, Necessary Being, divine attributes, divine Speech,
or revelation. It contains no Lean theorem or formal proof object. A reported
CSCG fit does not prove that the hippocampus literally implements that model.

The source's word "orthogonalized" refers to representation geometry. It does
not define or etymologically support "ortheme."

## Convergence test

The long-range orthemology program asks whether proof, knowledge, identity,
language, agents, restoration, source transmission, and grounding share formal
structure. This paper can help only if the relation is made exact.

A proposed unification must supply at least one of:

- an exact reduction;
- a shared formal model with typed interpretation maps;
- a shared invariant whose semantics are stated in each domain;
- a guarded transfer theorem;
- one countermodel architecture operating under matched assumptions.

Shared words, similar pictures, performance parity, and terminal geometry are
not enough. The machine-readable owner records five research questions that
test the possible convergence while allowing the honest result to be a guarded
specialization, an analogy, or a principled nonidentity.
