# Bounded visual scan controls: range 1

## What was actually inspected

After completing the electronic-only pass, I visually inspected 29 complete rendered page images from the supplied newer-edition PDF, [Adwa scan](https://archive.org/download/al-mulakhas/al-risala.pdf) (625 PDF positions recorded in the source metadata). The images were directly inspected; body text, visible page labels and relevant apparatus were read. This is a bounded control exercise, not a continuous reading of the full printed work or a manuscript collation.

The exact set is PDF positions 41–42 (printed 34–35), 84–86 (printed 77–79), 108–114 (printed 101–107), 128–130 (printed 121–123), and 164–177 (printed 157–170). Every printed label in this set was visually checked; the PDF-minus-seven relationship is only asserted for these inspected pages. Inspection order differed from page order while locating the endpoint; the receipt records the actual order. Additional rendered but uninspected images are explicitly excluded from coverage.

The initial five files describe the original electronic pass. This supplement records later controls without replacing or normalising that witness. Catalogue assertions about an earlier edition or the manuscript remain the modern editor's assertions. The supplied scanned printed pages, not the manuscript itself, were seen. Margin headings, footnotes and statements about authorial manuscript annotations are editorial apparatus, not automatically authorial text.

## Cluster A: complete cause, external conditions and comprehensive creation

### PDF84–86 / printed77–79; SF-S001-L0053–L0057

Printed77 begins with the proposal to relocate the successive acts to the necessary existent rather than a separate eternal soul. It confirms the author’s preference for al-Rāzī's anti-eternalist objection over al-Urmawī's suggested escape, then develops the finite-cause examples. Printed78 completes the sunlight/active-intellect examples and the explicit scope restriction:

“فهذا الذي ذكروه من توقف الفيض على استعداد القوابل إنما يمكن أن يقال إذا كان ذلك الاستعداد ليس هو من فيض ذلك الموجب”.

It then contrasts this with the necessary existent “الذي هو المبدع لكل ما سواه” and says he produces the preparations and conditions as well as what comes upon them. The continuous printed context confirms the common comprehensive Creator hypothesis. It does not turn a finite source of one effect into the producer of every external readiness condition. This is positive confirmation of a material limitation already present in L0055.

Material disagreements:

1. At L0054 the electronic “فالواجب الذي هو أصح” corresponds to printed77 “فالجواب الذي هو أصح”. This is a local lexical correction, not a new theological claim.
2. At L0056 the electronic text, after asking why a prior readiness was delayed, has the misplaced/duplicated phrase “وقول الأرموي أنه يجوز عندهم حدوث الشرائط والمعدات الفائضة بسببها ليس هو ...”. Printed78 instead reads: “قيل: فقد ثبت أن هذه الاستعدادات المتعاقبة والحوادث الفائضة بسببها ليس هو مؤثراً تاماً في الأزل لها ولا مؤثراً لها فيما لا يزال”. The genuine al-Urmawī sentence follows separately. The newer reading restores the object of the author's objection; the electronic version cannot safely support exact syntactic or logical modelling there.
3. At L0056 the electronic continuation contains “ولم يثبت أنه مؤثر تام لغيرها فبطلت حجتهم فلم يبق معهم ما يثبت علة تامة في الأزل”. Printed79 omits that stretch and passes from “فقد ثبت أنه ليس مؤثراً تاماً لها” to “وهذا يبطل حجتهم لو لم يثبت امتناع علة تامة في الأزل ...”. Catalogue PDF41/printed34, item 2, explicitly says the omitted older-edition stretch is absent from its manuscript at its stated folio. This report is not a direct manuscript verification by this reader.
4. Printed79 gives “فعلُه” with a footnote reporting “بعلة” in the editor's original, and adds bracketed “قدم” in “امتناع [قدم] شيء من العالم”, identified as contextual editorial supplementation. It also marks the phrase “الدور المعي القبلي” with apparatus reporting an overwritten alternative and suggesting possible intended wording. The electronic L0057 should not be treated as a fully settled technical label for a hybrid circle.

Result: the decisive comprehensive-source/external-condition contrast survives control. Some surrounding rebuttal sentences are materially better constituted in this edition, but the new printed text is not silently substituted for the acquired electronic body.

## Cluster B: restored causal quotation, power and present conditions

### PDF108–114 / printed101–107; SF-S001-L0094–L0105

The inspected seven-page sequence contains the beginning and end of the extended al-Rāzī quotation on talismans and astral causation, followed by the author's answer. The modern edition has marginal headings and footnotes identifying the cited work; these are separate from the author’s “قال” and “قلت” boundaries. Printed105 visibly contains the transition “قلت: فهذا غاية تقرير أصل هؤلاء ...”. The descending clod and walking-to-a-friend examples on printed104 are inside the represented argument, not the author's unqualified endorsed model.

Important controls:

1. Printed102 restores a missing clause in electronic L0097. The scan reads, in relevant part: “فإذن ذلك المجموع مفتقر لمجموعه ولكل واحد من أجزائه إلى سبب والشيء المغاير لمجموع الممكنات ولكل واحد من أجزاء ذلك المجموع ليس بممكن ولا حادث”. The received version lacks the connective “إلى سبب والشيء المغاير” and the following properly formed relation, leaving a broken sentence. The intended local argument concerns the possible aggregate's need for a cause outside the aggregate and each of its parts. Catalogue printed34 item 3 reports a physical obstruction in the earlier editor's exemplar. Again that causal explanation is the modern editor's report; I did not inspect the manuscript obstruction.
2. Printed105 reads “بل هذا يقضي بأنه مقدوره” where electronic L0101 reads “بل هذا يقتضي انقضاء مدة”. The scan therefore concerns the item’s being within the agent's power/being his object of power, not the lapse of a duration. This is a consequential lexical difference; a duration-based philosophical conclusion cannot be grounded on the electronic phrase without disclosure. The surrounding sentence immediately denies eternal presence of all requirements for producing temporal events. Rechecking enlarged catalogue PDF41/printed34 confirms that the catalogue itself quotes “يقتضي بأنه مقدوره”, with ت, while body105 reads “يقضي”. This catalogue/body lexical difference is retained; the catalogue must not be silently corrected to the body.
3. Printed106 reads “وهم لا يجعلون شرط التأثير سابقاً على وجود الأثر ليس بمقارن له”, whereas L0102 reads “وهؤلاء يجعلون شرط التأثير سابقا على وجود الأثر ليس بمقارن له”. The presence of “لا” is explicit and is the edition's catalogue item 5. This reverses the apparent polarity of a local allegation. The resulting printed sentence remains syntactically and argumentatively delicate in its context, which has just reported the prior event as a condition and denied that the opponents require a complete cause at each event. I record the disagreement, not an unlicensed harmonisation or an inference that the printed negation is unquestionably final.
4. Later on printed106 the scan reads “لا أن كل حادث مسبوق بحادث آخر”, against L0102's “إلا أن كل حادث مسبوق بحادث آخر”. Rechecking enlarged catalogue PDF41/printed34 confirms its separated “لا أن كل حادث مسبوق”, agreeing with the body. The earlier claimed catalogue/body discrepancy resulted from the provisional transcription “لأن” and is withdrawn. The received electronic “إلا أن” differs from both catalogue and body and cannot be silently retained as if confirmed. The printed wording is now visually established, while its syntax and interpretation in the surrounding argument still require care before formal reconstruction.
5. Printed107 confirms the positive neighbouring argument at L0103: the completion cannot be acquired from another since everything other than the Creator is his product, and prior acts conditioning later products are his acts rather than another agent's. Thus the broad common-Creator contrast has independent nearby textual support despite the local negation problem on printed106.

Result: this cluster changes the textual confidence of three electronic passages. It supports the overall scope distinction but forbids relying on the received duration phrase or unqualifiedly quoting the received conditional wording. The quotation and answer remain separately attributed.

## Cluster C: positive perfection and its restriction

### PDF128–130 / printed121–123; SF-S001-L0130–L0136

Printed121 provides the privation/possession objection and the response ranking non-receptivity to perfection below a deprived but capable subject. Printed122 begins the second answer with the source/perfection maxim, and printed123 continues the gift/acquisition and mutual dependence argument. The maxim, explanatory source premise and qualification are therefore read as one connected local argument rather than a detached slogan.

Printed122 confirms:

- “كل كمال ثبت للمخلوق فالخالق أحق به”;
- the premise that every perfection in the produced creature comes from its creative producer;
- “المعدوم لا يبدع موجوداً، والناقص لا يبدع ما هو أكمل منه”;
- a distinction between negative deficiency and positive perfection;
- the third answer's qualification “الكمال الذي لا نقص فيه بوجه من الوجوه الممكن للموجود”.

The printed text includes “والكمال أمور وجودية” immediately after “فإن النقص أمور عدمية”; this phrase is absent from electronic L0133. Catalogue printed34 item 6 explicitly identifies its omission from the earlier print. Printed122 also makes the divine negative predicates “الأمور السلبية العدمية” conditional on implying positive matters. The scan strengthens the need to explain perfection positively, rather than treating any negation as a perfection.

Printed123 confirms that acquiring perfection from another either identifies that other as the self-perfect source or produces a vicious circle of efficient enabling. The end moves to the two-creator argument. These pages do not establish a universal source-mode transfer principle for finite or partial sources: their subject is the Creator/creature relation and defect-free perfection possible for an existent. The qualifications in the electronic L0158 have not been scanned in this control and should retain their electronic-only status.

## Cluster D: ending, natural powers and adjacent signs/epistemology

### PDF164–166 / printed157–159; SF-S001-L0232–L0238

Printed157 gives the fifth, sixth and seventh rebuttals of the eternal-world inference. Printed158 completes the last rebuttal and explicitly returns to the psychic reduction of prophecy. A marginal heading and note label the preceding discussion an excursus; that is modern editorial assistance rather than an independent authorial heading. Printed159 contains the exact concession beginning “فنحن لا ننكر أن الله تعالى يخص النبي بقوة قدسية ...”, the truthful waking/dream representations, psychic efficacy, and real powers and natures in external things.

The closing assigned electronic line maps into printed159 and stops at “ويقولون ليس في العين”. The scan continues the reported denial of an eye's distinguishing power, bread's distinction from dust and water's distinction from vinegar, followed by the view that God creates satiety and quenching at these things, not through them. The author calls this view weak and contrary to revelation and reason and cites Qur'anic descriptions of life and fruits brought forth through water.

At the bottom of printed159 the author expressly says real powers through which effects occur are not denied by revelation or reason, but the claim that prophetic miracles are wholly of this kind is a great calumny. That continuation confirms the interpretative limit established by the assigned endpoint: rejection of exhaustiveness, not denial of all natural or psychic efficacy.

### Additional adjacent scan context: PDF167–177 / printed160–170

These pages are outside the assigned electronic range. They were visually read while locating and reconciling the endpoint and following the provided printed169 correction lead. They do not enlarge the completed electronic receipt or count as reading the next block's electronic windows. Exact later electronic matches remain the next reader's responsibility.

- Printed160 accepts a limited natural/psychological explanation of going without food under intense absorption but denies that it exhausts prophetic signs. The rod becoming a living serpent is presented as beyond those ordinary powers.
- Printed161 distinguishes creating a living transformed bearer from producing ordinary injuries or accidents, and explicitly describes the contextual sign as evidence of the powerful choosing Creator and of Moses' veracity. Reports about Ṣāliḥ's she-camel and lunar/celestial signs follow.
- Printed162–164 appeal to public recital, audience capacity for challenge, the actual questioning of other difficult statements, and transmitted reports. Printed164 says particular past narratives and detailed future reports cannot be obtained simply through intuition of a syllogistic middle term but require an informer. This is neighbouring evidence for epistemic-route differentiation, not an independently authenticated proof of those reports.
- Printed165–168 return to differences among celestial, natural and psychic explanatory schemes and criticise blanket denial of real powers, purposive causes and human efficacy, while analysing rival views of will and determination. These are attributed school accounts; their footnotes do not replace source checking.
- Printed169 contains “فإنه لا يكون إلا حادثاً [لا] لامتناع دوام الحوادث، بل لامتناع صدور المحدثات ...”. The bracketed “لا” is explicitly linked by apparatus to a marginal addition; this confirms the catalogue PDF42/printed35 item 7 against the older reading said there to omit it. The point is not rejecting continuous events but rejecting their production by the stipulated necessary complete source. This consequential later-range control should be reconciled by block02.
- Printed170 gives the represented occasionalist response and a modern footnote quoting al-Ghazālī. The main text and editorial quotation remain distinct. Neither this scan nor the electronic first range independently checks al-Ghazālī's text.

## Remaining uncertainties and unused leads

The catalogue and body agree on the printed106 wording “لا أن”; its syntax and interpretation in context still need care before an exact argumentative claim is stabilised. The hybrid circle label on printed79 also has explicit apparatus uncertainty. The printed174 acquired-prophecy correction belongs beyond this assigned electronic range; its catalogue entry was read at printed35, but its body page was not inspected. Images rendered for printed109–111 and printed171–175 were not viewed and are excluded from all reading claims. No manuscript or earlier printed edition was directly compared. These controls establish selected differences between the acquired electronic body and this newer printed witness, not the final authorial wording of the entire work.
