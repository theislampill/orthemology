## Partition thresholds and minimality

For positive integer block sizes summing to u, forcing every block's corruption fraction strictly above t<1 costs exactly the sum of floor(t s_j)+1. The strict inequality matters at rational ties. Forcing a fraction at least t instead costs the sum of ceil(t s_j). Since t<1, the requested corruptions fit inside each block. These facts justify both displayed threshold equivalences, including the adversary's ability to pad a smaller allocation up to b.

There are finitely many block partitions and allocation ratios. Their global optimum B is therefore attained and rational. In the positive regime, an adversary can hit every block at least once because b≥M, giving B>0. It cannot fill every block because b<u, giving B<1. An attaining allocation's smallest block fraction equals one of its component ratios, so the reduced denominator q of B obeys q≤u−M+1. The weighted-average bound B≤b/u is also valid.

The residue proof correctly uses *global* minimality, not merely optimality inside one fixed partition. For any partition feasible at B, write f for its floor sum, d=f−N and z for the zero-residue count. A sufficiently small left perturbation of B subtracts one from each of exactly those z floors. Thus 0≤d<z is necessary; otherwise the same partition would improve the global optimum.

If d>0, the invariant supplies two zero blocks. Transferring between them changes residues 0,0 to 1,q−1, lowers d by one and removes two zeros. The new partition remains feasible at B. It is consequently another global minimizer, so either its left-perturbation invariant holds or a contradiction has already been reached.
