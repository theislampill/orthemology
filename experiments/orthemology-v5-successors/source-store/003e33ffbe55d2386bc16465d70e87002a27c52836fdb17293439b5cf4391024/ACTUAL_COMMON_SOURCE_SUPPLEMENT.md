# Safadiyya and the actual common-Creator comparison

## Bounded conclusion

The full received reading strengthens the textual case for an actual common-Creator comparison. Ibn Taymiyya does not merely use a generic word for dependence: he affirms continuing creaturely dependence, argues that actual connected creation has one independent maker, and explicitly reasons from defect-free created perfections to the superior perfection of that maker. These are genuine positive arguments and should not be weakened into a merely convenient explanatory suggestion.

The same work does not establish an unrestricted proposition about every possible original and every requisite under one SourceMode relation. That formulation is a modern analytical question, not the text's own defined vocabulary. The difference is not resolved by observing that the work sometimes says 'all'. One must identify the domain of that universal, the kind of dependence and the inference being made. Reading an argument and controlling its wording also do not prove the corresponding formal theorem. No new Lean result is claimed.

## 1. What the positive textual case actually contains

### A concrete bearer must be identified

SF-S002-L0043–0046, controlled at Adwa printed 245–246, explicitly separates establishing a necessary existent from establishing a Maker distinct from the world. The later warnings at SF-S003-L0348–0353 prevent identifying a necessary existent, a first mover and the particular independent Creator without further argument. Rejecting an external universal does not itself supply all of those identification steps.

The positive route treats God as a particular self-subsisting bearer with necessary attributes. SF-S002-L0312–0314 offers formulations through independent existence, sustaining reality and creatorhood. SF-S003-L0267–0280, controlled at printed 450–452, describes everything else as intrinsically dependent, including its continued existence. This is not only a claim about a first moment of production.

### Actual common origin is argued

SF-S003-L0198–0208, controlled at printed 436–438, opposes partitioning a connected, mutually dependent creation among independent ultimate makers. SF-S003-L0362–0365, controlled at printed 473–474, argues from the unity of a generated individual against multiple ultimate makers of that same undivided effect. The author recognises cooperation among created agents but distinguishes their contributions. SF-S003-L0368–0373 adds an inseparability argument concerning mixing and the resultant state.

These arguments are addressed to actual created things and purported independent ultimate producers. Their intended force is exclusion of the rival account, not mere preference based on elegance. A reconstruction must nevertheless state the connection, unity and inseparability premises and explain why the relevant causal contributions cannot be distributed as the rival proposes. It cannot replace that work by calling every contributor either a complete maker or an irrelevant condition.

### Source and perfection are explicitly connected

SF-S002-L0330–0337, controlled at printed 321–322, combines two strands. A perfection which is possible and without defect cannot, the author argues, be available to an inferior dependent being yet impossible for the superior independent being. Further, all such creaturely perfections come from the Creator; their ultimate giver is more entitled to perfection. The divine possession of perfection cannot be borrowed from the very effects that receive theirs from him, or from a second ultimate source.

The earlier SF-S001-L0132–0135 is controlled at printed 122. The newer edition here includes the clause that perfections are positive or existent matters, absent from the received electronic line. Thus an interpretation confined to the electronic wording would miss an explicitly positive link in this discussion. The missing clause is preserved as a textual difference, not silently inserted into the original reading receipt.

This is strong textual support for studying a comparative inference about the actual common source of the compared creatures. It is not a demonstration that every feature of an effect belongs intrinsically to its cause, still less that every necessary condition has the same feature in a higher degree.

## 2. Necessary restrictions supplied by the same work

### Efficient production and intrinsic qualification differ

SF-S002-L0507, controlled at printed 353–354, denies that producing a quality in another makes the producer intrinsically qualified by it. God creates many states and utterances which are not therefore his own intrinsic states or speech. The perfection comparison consequently requires more than the bare statement that an effect is from its maker. It needs the relevant, appropriately understood excellence and the additional comparison defended in the argument.

SF-S002-L0337 accepts intrinsic necessitation for necessary attributes while denying that the production of creatures is of that same kind. The following SF-S002-L0340–0346, controlled at printed 323–324, distinguishes requiring one's own attributes or supposed constituents from having an efficient maker. If 'requisite' includes intrinsic concomitants, it cannot simply inherit an axiom governing produced effects.

### Finite sources may wait on conditions from elsewhere

SF-S001-L0055 and SF-S002-L0607–0610 explicitly allow this. Fire, sun and the proposed active intellect are not automatically independent producers of all the conditions for their effects. The sun can illuminate a newly uncovered place without a new intrinsic change in the sun. The argument about the comprehensive Creator turns on the fact that the allegedly external condition belongs to what he produces.

The particular act may also be conditioned by another act of the same already capable agent. SF-S002-L0521, controlled within printed 355–357, separates original creatorhood from creating this thing after or through another. A formal account which bans every conditioned act in order to preserve original independence would contradict an explicit permission in the source.

### A final cause is not thereby an efficient origin

SF-S003-L0319–0321, controlled at printed 463–464, says that an object of love or desire does not by being loved efficiently produce the lover's capacity or act. A represented goal may guide an action before the external goal exists. Exemplar, end, cognitive condition and productive origin are therefore different possible relations. Calling all of them SourceMode would require a definition that preserves their distinctions, not an assumption that they validate the same transfer rule.

### Perfection is not an unrestricted property variable

The source repeatedly specifies a possible perfection without defect and excludes creature-specific deficiency. The final partition at SF-S004-L0055–0056, confirmed at printed 578, is especially useful:

- Knowledge, mercy and wisdom can be commended in human beings.
- Some divine prerogatives do not commend human self-ascription.
- Need, humility, servanthood and petition can perfect the creature precisely in its relation to God, while being incompatible with divine independence.

This partition neither reduces all divine/human language to equivocation nor licences undifferentiated assimilation. An inference must determine the predicate's meaning and bearer-relative status before it treats it as a transferable perfection.

The surrounding historical polemic also has a textual qualification. Adwa printed 577, notes 2 and 5, challenges aspects of the author's account of al-Ghazali and cites humility and ascetic detachment as his meanings. That modern editorial voice does not erase the author's predicate partition, but it limits using the polemic as an uncontested description of al-Ghazali.

## 3. What remains to be established in a reconstruction

A faithful formal or philosophical reconstruction would need at least the following separate arguments.

1. **Referent and domain.** Show why the established necessary bearer is the particular independent Creator of the actual objects being compared. Specify whether the quantifiers range over actual creatures, all possible creatures, all alleged sources, or proposed formal objects.
2. **Causal role.** Distinguish complete efficient production from partial contribution, reception, preparation, intrinsic entailment, material constitution, final causation and evidence. If one notation includes several relations, prove the relevant rule for each included case.
3. **Predicate qualification.** Define the defect-free excellence at issue and explain why its appropriate divine realisation is possible. The premise that it is possible for a created bearer does not become a cross-kind modal result merely by reusing the same word.
4. **Comparative bridge.** Explain why ultimate giving of this excellence warrants the superior source's corresponding intrinsic excellence. The text supplies a substantive argument and excludes borrowing from products, but elsewhere explicitly rejects attributing every produced property to its producer.
5. **Common-source inference.** If the argument uses connected creation or a unitary generated object, establish the relevant unity and show why the proposed rival distribution of ultimate agency fails. Do not assume all co-contributors are independently sufficient makers of the whole.
6. **Temporal and explanatory scope.** Separate original power, successive acts, current completeness and a particular product. The text's denial of an eternally coexistent determinate effect does not entail a first moment of every action; its acceptance of successive preparation does not entail a regress of ultimate makers.
7. **Textual support.** Use the controlled reading of the decisive passage. A polarity error, omitted qualifier, changing speaker or conflation of catalogue with body apparatus cannot be cured by a sound formal derivation from the wrong premise.

These are proof obligations, not findings that every such reconstruction must fail. The present study supplies a stronger, wider textual basis for trying them, and a clearer account of what cannot be obtained simply by broadening the author's vocabulary.

## 4. Epistemic and attestation consequences

The work's account of knowledge does not demand that everyone complete the same metaphysical derivation before any warranted religious recognition is possible. SF-S003-L0414–0419 allows different routes and different quantities of doubt-removal. SF-S002-L0206–0208 recognises rational guidance within revelation. The distinction between inferential intermediates and external causes, developed later in the treatment of universals and definitions, likewise blocks turning every premise in one proof into an ontological prerequisite for its object.

Prophetic attestation is then a further question. The author combines causal discrimination with the event's setting, the claimant, communicated content and the distinction between divine election and general psychic receptivity. Some signs are said to attest both the voluntary Creator and the messenger. This provides material for a cumulative actual-world account; it does not independently establish the historical occurrence, transmission and exclusive explanation of each claimed sign.

Accordingly, the positive source/perfection case, the common-Creator argument and a particular historical attestation should be assessed separately before considering how they support one another. The text itself gives reasons not to collapse causal occurrence, reliable knowledge, communicative purpose and the truth of a reported mission into one relation.

## Final assessment

Safadiyya makes the actual common-Creator proposal more substantial as a textual and philosophical research direction. It also makes the unrestricted every-original/every-requisite schema harder to present as a simple restatement of Ibn Taymiyya. The gain is precision: genuine universal creaturely dependence, real arguments for common ultimate agency, explicit comparative perfection reasoning, and equally explicit restrictions on the predicates and relations involved. No earlier formal assessment is silently replaced, and no new formal conclusion follows solely from this reading.
