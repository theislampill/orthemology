## 2. Strongest local candidate: content through rhetoric

The received official *Miftāḥ dār al-saʿāda* Word file was used: editor ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Ḥasan ibn Qāʾid, third edition 1440/2019, Dār ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm / Dār Ibn Ḥazm. The file is byte-identified in the retained source record. The electronic text states that its numbering follows the printed edition; no fresh print-scan or manuscript collation was performed. The extraction is a bounded use of the inherited UTF-16LE decode and explicit trailing page markers, not a general Word parser.

The continuous relevant subsection is the second class of knowledge-bearer, from 1:393 through the beginning of the third class at 399. Surrounding 389–401 was read, and the quotation/authorial boundary at 347–351 checked separately. The larger discussion comments on a report attributed to ʿAlī through Kumayl. The editor questions the report's transmission at 348; the subsequent quoted comments of al-Khaṭīb end at 351, where Ibn al-Qayyim announces his own discussion. Thus the operative scrutiny argument at 394–398 is Ibn al-Qayyim's exposition, with a separately marked report of advice from Ibn Taymiyya at 395. It is not all an independently authenticated statement of ʿAlī, nor independent testimony from three sources.

At 393, Ibn al-Qayyim distinguishes following people of truth from possessing deep insight into the truth followed. He allows that such a follower can be on a path of salvation; his immediate question concerns fitness to carry and teach knowledge, not a universal verdict that ordinary reliance on testimony never yields knowledge.

At 394–395, the intellectually settled person is described as meeting an objection through understanding its defect. The author's remembered glass-and-sponge advice contains both receptivity to inspection and resistance to unexamined absorption. On its strongest reading, this is not a direction to ignore the objection: the glass sees what passes before it, while the following discussion explains how an apparently attractive objection disguises a defective content.

At 396–397, the counterfeit-coin analogy becomes an explicit argument about language. The same position can be accepted under attractive wording and rejected under hostile wording. Every party can dress its own view favourably and its rival's view unfavourably. The response is to inspect the underlying meaning, withhold prejudicial attraction or aversion, and give the rival's claim the same attentive scrutiny given to one's own side. At 398–399, he adds deliberation rather than impulsive uptake, and acknowledges the contrary practical risk of missing the occasion through delay. The advice does not simply identify maximal psychological rigidity or indefinite postponement with epistemic excellence.

The author applies the discussion to theological attribute controversies at 396. Those contested theological applications remain his; the present inspection has not independently decided the controversy merely by adopting his favourable descriptions.

## 3. Strongest inference, reply and limit

The positive epistemic point is genuine: where meaning and relevant evidence are held fixed, a change in rhetorical attraction cannot itself establish or refute the proposition. Content-sensitive attention can expose a particular error and allow a warranted judgement. This fits, rather than replaces, the earned operative-basing account.

The strongest attempted new application would use neutral restatement to distinguish truth-directed recognition from merely induced assent. Three limits block promotion of that proposal into the missing warrant discriminator:

1. **Content must actually be held fixed.** A new formulation can remove ambiguity, add an implication or alter what the learner understands. A changed judgement can then reflect improved understanding rather than arbitrary susceptibility. Semantic equivalence cannot be inferred from the investigator's choice to call two formulations equivalent.
2. **Stable assent is not sufficient.** Habit, confidence, source loyalty and competent recognition can all preserve a response across formulations. The passage supplies no independently authenticated case in which invariance establishes the operative truth-connected ground.
3. **Competent inspection can yield genuinely new knowledge.** Even if a neutral restatement removes a distortion and the subject then sees a sound reason, this need not establish that the subject was recovering earlier knowledge. The source-supported positive outcome is current competent judgement; the older acquisition history remains a separate question.

These are application limits, not an additional faculty-certification demand. Nor does a merely imagined alternative mechanism rebut an actual warranted judgement. If the observer already has adequate evidence that the subject understands and uses the relevant grounds, that evidence can support the appraisal. But then the grounds and competent use do the discriminating work; response stability or the investigator's framing intervention is not their surrogate.

The source therefore contributes a useful, nontrivial standard of fair inquiry. It does not deliver the new case or substantive proper-function bridge requested by this task. Claiming that a fresh name such as “redescription invariance” solved the problem would repeat the inherited distinction without supplying its application evidence. No such model or test was built.
