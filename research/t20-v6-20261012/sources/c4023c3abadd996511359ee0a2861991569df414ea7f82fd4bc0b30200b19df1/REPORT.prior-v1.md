# Informed election and the scope of a response-selection title

11 October 2026 UTC. Bounded source test. No protected edits, integration, historical authentication, present duty adjudication, or closure.

## Finding

The proposed Qurʾān 28:68 route does not supply the missing constitutive selection title. Its strongest inspected Ibn al-Qayyim rationale combines original provision with knowledgeable election, but its discriminating object is the fitness of those elected, particularly for prophetic commission. Transferring that rationale to control of an otherwise adequate form of an owed response requires another premise. This is a precise local obstruction, not a denial of original rightful authority or a demand for universal jurisdiction.

A useful affirmative application survives: **a recipient's unexplained dislike or apparent equivalence of alternatives does not establish their actual equivalence when the relevant selection is independently warranted as informed and wise.** Conversely, that epistemic limitation does not prove that the selected option alone is required. The present application cannot turn a possible unrecognized difference into an established necessary difference.

## Primary source units

In the site's attributed extract of *Zād al-maʿād*, ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm 1:13–17, Ibn al-Qayyim distinguishes election among created things from the more general willing of their creation. He defends the negative reading of the verse's final clause through six arguments. The contextual comparisons concern appointment of messengers and distribution of favor according to knowledge of fitness; the concluding argument connects election with wisdom. This is not bare arbitrary power. The modern webpage supplies the edition locator; no print scan or apparatus was collated. [Attributed primary extract](https://www.ibnalqayem.net/articles/774).

The corresponding *Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl* discussion, received electronic e31–34, distinguishes the same election from general will. Qurʾān 33:36 occurs in its lexical illustration. The subsequent subsection explicitly extends informed selection to legislation: an agent can dislike a command while lacking knowledge of its beneficial consequences. The following supplication discussion separates knowledge, ability, facilitation, continuing blessing and contentment. These are the author's endorsed arguments, apart from expressly attributed quotations. No new authenticity judgment about the transmitted reports is made. [Received primary transcription](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/shfa-alalil-001.html).

## Strongest reconstruction and discriminating test

The candidate can be reconstructed without reducing original authority to force:

1. The actual original supplies the creatures and the relevant benefaction.
2. The original knows their capacities and the relevant ends and consequences.
3. Its election therefore answers a real, informed suitability rather than the challenger's uninformed preference.
4. The challenger cannot replace that election merely by proposing its own favored recipient.

This supports source-owned wise appointment and rebuts an unsupported assumption that worldly prestige establishes superior fitness. But the target residual has a different object: two performances by the same recipient, each otherwise adequate to the already owed relation. Step 4 cannot be transported simply by substituting “response form” for “commissioned recipient.” The text's reason for discrimination is not an established power to make either equally adequate discharge uniquely due at will.

Two readings of the residual must therefore remain separate:

- **Merely apparent adequacy:** the recipient sees no relevant difference. Informed wise guidance can defeat confidence in that assessment, conditional on its actual attribution and normative mode. Further practical reliance can be reasonable without independently knowing every underlying reason.
- **Genuinely adequate alternative:** the comparison holds fixed adequacy apart from the disputed specification. Possible undisclosed benefit does not refute this comparison. Greater benefit alone also does not establish obligatory grade, as the inherited obligatory/recommended distinction already shows.

The second is an explanatory comparison, not a claim that an actual warranted command may be ignored. The first is an epistemic limitation, not proof that all challenged alternatives are secretly defective. The strongest source-faithful alternative interpretation is thus that the passage explains why an informed appointment or command should not be rejected merely because its wisdom is not accessible; it need not independently derive the title presupposed in that guidance.
