# 3. Essence, existence, attributes, qualitative grounds and explanatory facts

**Claim type:** source reconstruction and mathematical interpretation audit.  
**Main sources:** A09, A12–A14, M05–M07; inherited checkpoints 15 and 18.  
**Result in one sentence:** explanatory-fact uniqueness is real conditional mathematics, but it cannot silently supply unique concrete bearerhood or the elimination of real intrinsic attributes.

## 3.1 Two opposite errors must be excluded together

The source position under investigation does not identify Knowledge with Power, and does not make those real attributes separately subsisting beings. The relevant distinctions are between conceptual discrimination, real qualification, independent subsistence, and productive derivation.

The direct *al-Radd ʿalā al-Manṭiqiyyīn* passage matching the agent's essence/existence quotation is a correction of reference. If both expressions pick out the concrete particular, there are not two extramental things. If one expression picks out the mental conception and the other the concrete existent, their referents differ. This does not make every use of “essence” denote something merely mental. [A09]

Consequently, neither of these transformations is licensed without argument:

`one bearer, distinct real attributes → several independently subsisting bearers`;

`one concrete bearer → all its attribute predicates have identical meanings or extensions`.

This is why an untyped mathematical domain is hazardous. Suppose a model's domain contains a bearer g, its knowledge, its power, a proposition describing g, and a fact that g exists. Counting all five as “necessary things” does not show five necessarily existing deities. Equally, collapsing all five into one syntactic identifier does not prove their roles interchangeable.

A good representation may legitimately encode attributes as objects, relations, dependent records, or predicates. The encoding is not automatically a metaphysical thesis. What matters is the interpretation of quantification, equality, subsistence and the relationships between these sorts.

## 3.2 “Necessary” must keep its modal and ontological indices

Four claims must remain distinct:

- g exists at every world in a selected formal frame;
- g is not existentially received at the actual world;
- g is unreceived at every world in the intended scope;
- g exists necessarily through itself under the defended metaphysical account.

The first does not imply the second. A model may contain a necessarily dependent being. The second does not by itself imply the first. An actual graph root can simply be marked contingent unless a substantive bridge excludes that interpretation.

Nor does a necessary attribute become an independent root merely because it is present wherever its bearer exists. The relevant *Darʾ* distinctions explicitly differentiate uses of necessary existence which include intrinsic attributes from the stronger sense of a self-sufficient original creator. [A12]

There is also a temporal index. A necessary bearer may have different relations to created objects at different times. A particular act's occurrence after another act is not, by syntax alone, createdness or the receipt of a formerly missing essential perfection. The model needs separate predicates for external production, intrinsic occurrence and time. Report 5 develops this point.

## 3.3 What the strongest qualitative rival actually says

Dasgupta's three projects should not be bundled into one alleged counterexample. *Individuals* targets primitive material individuals; *On the Plurality of Grounds* permits a collection of individualistic facts to be grounded together without each member being grounded separately; *Metaphysical Rationalism* develops a grounding-based explanatory programme with a necessitarian consequence. These are different claims. [M05–M07]

In particular, a qualitative ground is not automatically a written equation, and metaphysical grounding is not automatically efficient production. “The formula does not cause an object” does not refute an account of constitutive grounding. Conversely, a successful explanation of how facts depend constitutively does not, just by existing, show that the actual received order needs no original productive resource.

This suggests a useful explanatory matrix rather than an all-or-nothing judgement:

| Question | What would count as an answer? | What does not suffice by itself? |
|---|---|---|
| Why does this description apply? | A truth condition satisfied by an actual particular | A syntax tree alone |
| In virtue of what does this fact obtain? | A defended grounding relation | Merely naming it `Grounds` |
| How does this occurrence arise? | A source-appropriate productive account | A constitutive description without a production bridge |
| Why is there an obtaining order at all? | An adequate existential explanation at the intended scope | Assuming the order and explaining its internal pattern |
| Why must this original be unique? | A supported exclusion of distinct original alternatives | Qualitative symmetry or parsimony alone |

The same theory can answer more than one row, but its success in one row cannot be automatically carried to another. That is the principal use of the semantic audit, not a reason to impose one ontology on all rivals in advance.

## 3.4 What the inherited explanatory-fact proof establishes

Checkpoint 18 contains a useful result. Under a no-extended-circles condition on partial explanation and antisymmetric parthood, mutually explaining facts become identical. Two all-fact explainers therefore coincide, and cross-membership can suffice even for differently specified target classes. No new empirical claim follows. [I02]

The proof's core is unusually economical. Let q partially explain r and r partially explain q. If q and r were distinct and r were not part of q, the no-extended-circles rule would exclude the second explanatory direction. Hence r is part of q. Symmetrically q is part of r. Antisymmetry gives equality.

This does not require calling either fact a person. It does require keeping the explanatory relation and its anti-circularity meaning fixed. If a reader rejects the source's treatment of self-explanation or mutual explanation, that is a premise dispute, not a failure of the deduction.

The independently implemented finite replay here uses the stronger Boolean control from checkpoint 18, not merely its four-fact equality-parthood example. There are 15 nonempty subsets of four atoms, all 32,767 nonempty pluralities, all nonempty fusions, proper parts and strong supplementation. One atom explains every nonempty plurality; two other atoms explain one designated local fact. The model passes the stipulated constraints while preserving local nonuniqueness.

This confirms a useful distinction: **global explanatory-fact uniqueness can coexist with multiple explanations of a narrower target**. Restricting the target to created effects rather than all facts can remove precisely the cross-membership needed by the proof.

## 3.5 Additional consequence: the calculus does not permit harmless source padding

The inherited atomicity result has a revealing consequence. Suppose q explains all facts. Add a principle that a larger fact containing q can explain everything q explains—an apparent analogue of weakening in classical proof systems. If q has a proper fusion with another fact, that fusion would also be a total explainer. Uniqueness would identify it with q, contradicting proper enlargement.

Therefore these assumptions cannot all hold:

1. a unique total explaining fact q;
2. a genuinely larger fact r containing q;
3. unrestricted preservation of full explanation when the explanans is enlarged.

The written proof and an explicit reseated Boolean negative control are included in the mathematics note. This is **not** a novel theory of grounding; relevance-sensitive and nonmonotone explanations have established philosophical ancestry. It is a useful consequence of this particular formal package.

Why it matters for Dot: an explanatory calculus can exclude gratuitous additions to an explanation without claiming that its concrete bearer is devoid of distinct attributes. Conversely, a user who silently treats its explanatory relation as ordinary proof consequence—where adding a premise is normally safe—changes the calculus. This is an actual difference in formal behaviour, not just a warning about terminology.

Dasgupta's discussion of explanatory relevance is particularly useful comparison material here: the grounds are not simply every larger list which entails the explanandum. That observation does not identify his entire theory with Koons–Pruss's axioms. [M06]

## 3.6 Atomless or attribute-rich reality is not refuted by fact atomicity

A fact being atomic under a chosen fact-parthood relation means it has no proper **fact**-parts according to that relation. It does not mean a divine bearer has no Knowledge, Power, Will or Wisdom. The inference would require an interpretation map preserving the relevant distinctions and parthood.

An explicit two-sorted control suffices to show the missing implication. Take one explanatory fact q and only reflexive fact-parthood. Take one bearer g with two distinct intrinsic attribute tokens. There is no map from attribute tokens to proper parts of q. Fact atomicity is true; identity of the two attributes is false. This is a counterinterpretation to an unqualified inference, not a claim to have constructed divine reality.

A stronger positive mapping theorem would have to say which attributed facts count as parts of which explaining fact, whether parthood is strict, and whether the map reflects distinctness. If all distinct attribute tokens are mapped to distinct proper parts of q, atomhood indeed conflicts with that mapping. But that tells us the mapping and the atomicity package are incompatible; it does not privilege the mapping by default.

The distinction works the other way too. One explanatory fact could have a plural ground or be realised by a relational arrangement. Its numerical identity alone is not enough to identify one concrete original bearer. A separate single-bearer realisation theorem is required, and that theorem's existence assumption cannot simply be attributed to the fact calculus.

## 3.7 A productive way to test the qualitative alternative

The useful test is not “can the word `qualitative` be placed before `ground`?” It is:

- specify the actual obtaining claims and which are being explained;
- specify whether the explanation is collective, memberwise, productive, constitutive, or some defended combination;
- preserve the relevant noncircularity and source-adequacy conditions;
- state whether the theory explains the obtaining of its ground or treats it as autonomous;
- identify how autonomy is justified, rather than equating it with necessary existence or a special label;
- compare the proposed costs with the original-resource explanation under the same explanatory demand.

The source's refusal to turn universals into extramental substances is a real objection to an ontology that does exactly that. It is not yet a refutation of every qualitative account, including those whose qualitative grounds are obtaining facts rather than separately floating abstract objects.

## 3.8 Research outcome

The semantic work has yielded a stronger diagnostic of explanatory systems: their permission or prohibition of source-side weakening. It has also provided a fresh exact replay of a substantial inherited model rather than merely repeating a review string.

The remaining bearer-realisation issue is now explicit. A complete fact explanation may be structurally unique and source-sensitive without having supplied a unique intrinsically necessary concrete agent. The original-bearer programme must defend that mapping in its own right. This is where the positive Taymiyyan ontology and the strongest qualitative rival should meet.
