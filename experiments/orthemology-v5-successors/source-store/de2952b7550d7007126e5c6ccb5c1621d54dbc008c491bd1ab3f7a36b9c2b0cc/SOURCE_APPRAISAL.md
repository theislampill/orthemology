# Source appraisal: Shifa al-Alil, electronic pages 202–307

Historical appraisal recorded during the Shifa study on 7 October 2026, before the later Miftah, Nubuwwat and Safadiyya studies. Its stated judgement and reading limits are preserved; subsequent final appraisals are separate.

## Finding first

This tranche strongly and repeatedly affirms **real created causal efficacy under complete divine original sourcehood**. It does not equate a secondary cause with an independent creator, nor a power with its unconditionally realized effect. Its most direct evidence is E206 (deposited natural powers and causes), E233 (coordination of multiple agents' actual acts), E278 (acquisition must influence the production of an act), and E305–306 (originating, preparing and sustaining a receptive power; sufficient versus contributory causes).

The argument from integrated coordination to one source is substantial positive evidence and expressly called compelling by the author (E231–233). Integration genuinely favours common coordination over disconnected accidents. **Sufficient warrant here may be defeasible; failure to obtain a deduction alone does not justify withholding it.** The unresolved comparative burden is narrower: why must concordant, role-differentiated, or nonexclusively sufficient ultimate agents be deficient under an independently warranted norm of complete divinity? The received argument does not yet settle that burden for this reader. A critical sentence at E232 is incomplete already in the underlying HTML. The final ultimate-object argument (E306–307) also relies on an exclusive interpretation of being intended for itself, plus strong fitra/perfection premises. These cannot be supplied merely by defining a determinate external object.

## Main conceptual findings and controls

### 1. Efficacy is real; independence is denied

E206 rejects both a purely nonvoluntary cosmos explained by celestial/natural necessity and the denial of causal powers in favour of bare will. God's voluntary action is through deposited powers, natures, dispositions, causes and effects. The polemical descriptions of opponents are not independently authenticated histories of those schools.

E233 is especially explicit: God organises the acts of many agents, connects their contributions, and makes some causes and ends for others. Organ-function and human division-of-labour examples depend on genuine local contributions. E236 states that cause, wisdom and will must not cancel one another. E278 rejects an acquisition consisting merely in simultaneous occurrence with will/power but no influence; its own corrective calls acquisition influential in producing the act and a cause of its existence.

Read E272–273's 'none owns benefit or harm' and poison/sorcery causing harm only by divine permission alongside these affirmations. The negation concerns independent ownership, control and original sourcehood, not all dependent causal production. Otherwise the source's explicit synthesis is lost.

### 2. Self-sufficiency does not mean absence of intrinsic wise agency

E206–207 presents Razi's objection in a serious form: a preferred result seems to improve the agent, while an indifferent result cannot explain choice. Replies distinguish an externally supplied perfection from intrinsic wisdom; all created perfection comes from God, not vice versa. Realizing an intended event at its fitting time is not lack of a perfection that should already have existed before that time.

E214 says God is intrinsically independent of everything else and receives no perfection from creatures. E220 addresses relational kingship directly: everything required for its manifestation is itself from Him, so His kingship does not depend on an independent supplier. Yet E215, E219 and E243 also make manifestation of already-complete attributes a perfection. This is a genuine positive thesis requiring assessment, not an ambiguity to suppress. It defeats an automatic inference from any relation or intended effect to external dependency; it does not alone prove uniqueness of every conceivable necessary being.

### 3. Different sorts of conditions must be distinguished

E213–214 argues that an end essentially involving a condition cannot be obtained as that very end without the condition, as sonship without fatherhood. Contradictions are not possible objects omitted from divine power. A separate argument allows direct production but says mediation brings additional goods; direct and mediated histories are not equivalent in every respect. Do not convert these two answers into the single claim that every observed causal connection is metaphysically necessary.

E305 distinguishes creation of the receptive locus, its preparedness and continued sustaining help. Removing ongoing help prevents actualisation even where receptivity exists. E306 explicitly distinguishes a cause sufficient for its effect from an efficacious contributor requiring an additional factor. E289 and E300–305's fitra argument must consequently be read with its development, awareness, no-impediment and reinforcement qualifications. Intrinsic directedness is not intrinsic self-sufficiency.

### 4. Will, love, command and actual efficacy

E270 and E280–283 systematically distinguish cosmic/effectual will from normative love/command. The former entails occurrence but not moral approval; the latter may be disobeyed. Positive assistance and removal of obstacles are distinct from issuing a command. Divine act is distinguished from created effect; the same effect can be evil in its creaturely relation yet the overall wise creative act is not thereby evil.

E275–276 treats justice as real fitting placement, not whatever happens to be within power or mere logical impossibility of wrongdoing. E278 distinguishes obligatory acceptance of divine command/blessings, prohibited approval of sin, and recommended rather than universally required contentment with affliction. Thus belief in decree does not excuse sin, erase responsibility, or require approval of wrongdoing.

### 5. What the integration argument contributes, and what remains unproved

E231–233's case joins ordered diversity, reciprocal need, common direction, replacement over time, and local agents serving wider ends they do not individually intend. Its strongest point is organised coordination across heterogeneous contributions, not bare repetitive regularity. It supports a common intelligent ordering source and pressures explanations that merely list unrelated powers.

Nevertheless, an army or cooperative society also exemplifies many effective contributors. This does not show that plural ultimate sources explain the evidence equally well, and merely imagining an alternative does not defeat defeasible warrant. The substantive comparative question is whether concordant, role-differentiated, or nonexclusively sufficient ultimate agents must lack complete divinity, and why. A sufficient assessment needs an independently warranted norm connecting complete divinity with the relevant independence, unrestricted sovereignty or exclusivity, rather than building the desired numerical uniqueness into the definition. It must then assess whether the proposed alternatives genuinely violate that norm and how well each accounts for the integrated order. Those norms may have arguments elsewhere, but this reader has not read the earlier tranche and cannot certify them by proxy. E232's damaged plural-gods/kings analogy cannot supply a missing bridge without another reliable witness.

Accordingly I retain a **bounded withholding of common-source sufficient warrant from this tranche as presently assessed**, for that unresolved comparative burden, not merely for absence of deductive entailment. Integration substantially strengthens a common-coordination explanation over disconnected accidents; it can contribute to defeasibly sufficient warrant even without a proof eliminating every conceivable alternative. This reading does not establish that every concordant-plural alternative is equally credible or that common-source warrant must remain withheld after the relevant divinity norms and comparative evidence are independently assessed. It is not a refutation of monotheism or of the author's intended unity. Nor is the earlier provisional **necessary-being sufficient warrant** automatically vindicated here: E207 deploys divine independence and unique creation as premises, and E307's possible/necessary-being contrast criticises a merely conceptual referent. These pages do not independently settle the earlier necessary-being argument's premises or their defeasible sufficiency.

### 6. The final ultimate-object proof needs its own premises

E306–307 argues from ineliminable willing to termination of instrumental ends, then to a determinate external final object, then to the one divine source. It includes fitness/perfection reasoning: inanimate objects are inferior to living subjects; a living dependent object itself has an end; multiple imagined gods differ across cultures; an abstract universal has no separate external concrete existence.

Its 'intended for itself' to 'alone intended' transition imports substantive exclusivity. A concrete object does not by concreteness alone exclude another intrinsic good, nor does each subject having some final object yet entail all subjects sharing one object. The author's strong universal fitra and perfection claims do that work. Give the cumulative case its full strength without presenting those premises as logical truths already granted by the opponent. The adjacent E306 apparatus corruption adds a textual limit, not a license to dismiss the intact surrounding argument.
