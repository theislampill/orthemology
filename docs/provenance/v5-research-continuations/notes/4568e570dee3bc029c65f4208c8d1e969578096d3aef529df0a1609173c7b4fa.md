## Semantic findings

1. **Actual causal access.** `advance` reads oracle coordinate k only when `ask` is true, increments k precisely on that branch, and supplies a literal false input otherwise. `next`, `ask` and `score` are shared, measurable functions, with no hidden alpha argument. The observable trace contains only the evolving state and actual query count. It does not append the unused infinite oracle tape.
2. **Whole-run locality, including N=0.** A bound on one run suffices to prove the entire second run identical when the first N bits agree. Boundedness of the other run is derived. Even the event of never requesting bit N+1 factors through the initial state and first N bits. No random last-query stopping-time assertion occurs.
3. **Derived measure domination.** The bounded trace law is explicitly a restricted finite-input pushforward. The reference uses an actual fair infinite product whose N-bit atom mass is `(1/2)^N`, including mass one at N=0. Restriction and countable union give finite-query trace domination. Infinite actual oracle laws can be singular, deterministic, dependent across time, or have zero-probability words.
4. **Correlation handled.** `joint_dominated_by_countable_reference` uses the common dominating first marginal and a countable second alphabet, not independence. Its measurable countable union of sections and product-null-set argument are valid. It is applied to finite Boolean words. The end theorem assumes all initial-state marginals equal the same probability law. The intermediate theorem already permits mere domination. Correlation may depend on alpha; arbitrary parameter-coded marginal changes are excluded. No regular conditional distribution or standard-Borel disintegration is smuggled in.
5. **The exact event.** Monotonicity and the integer counter prove finite query count iff eventual absence of queries. On actual executions this combines with eventual `BinaryGood` into a single eventual conjunction. Its probability is transferred back from the measured trace law using a proved measurable event. The final statement has no assumed trace-domination or exact-readout premise.
6. **Literal geometry.** `BinaryGood` is two weak weighted-quadratic loss inequalities. Under alpha in [0,1] and epsilon in [0,1/4], it implies an interior coherent q. Eventual validity plus strictly positive epsilon tending to zero forces the decoder `(q-1/2)/epsilon` to converge to alpha. Disjoint measurable limit events under one finite reference then yield countability.
7. **Constructive complement.** The separate sufficient estimate tolerance `|estimate-alpha| <= alpha(1-alpha)epsilon/4` gives strict improvement at both truth states for interior alpha and positive epsilon at most 1/4. It does not confuse the necessary decoder bound with a sufficient repair condition.

## Sharp boundaries and mutations

- A fixed known norm has a no-query exact-center repair forever. The conclusion is **at most countably many alpha with positive eventual-target probability**, not impossibility for each alpha.
- With a parameter-coded initial state the same evaluator shape can repair every norm without querying; the common marginal/domination condition is substantive.
- At epsilon=0 the fixed q=1/2 satisfies `BinaryGood` for every norm. At fixed epsilon=1/4, q=9/16 works throughout alpha in [1/4,5/16]. Positivity and shrinking disturbance cannot simply be dropped.
- At endpoint norms there is no two-state strict improvement in the admitted probability domain, although exact-center weak repair exists. Outside that domain a superficially similar strict statement can hold; the domain qualifications matter.
- The exact rational q=21/32 at alpha=1/2, epsilon=1/4 satisfies the necessary decoder tolerance but fails `BinaryGood`.
- Two opposite deterministic tapes produce identical no-query observed traces while the raw tapes differ. Appending raw unused coordinates would invalidate the domination interpretation.
- Source mutations that leak an unused oracle bit, fail to increment the query counter, replace the fair reference by a degenerate bit law, or replace the final eventual AND by OR all fail kernel elaboration. Their logs bind the expected rejection, not a successful theorem.
