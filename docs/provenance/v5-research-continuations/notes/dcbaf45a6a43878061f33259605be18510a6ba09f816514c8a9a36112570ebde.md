# Complete fixed-codebook classification

Candidate for independent review. This is a deduction from an established
partition-Kneser hypergraph theorem plus elementary arithmetic. No general
mathematical novelty is claimed. Previously frozen and reviewed packets remain
unchanged. The topological theorem is a stronger external proof dependency than
those used by the earlier elementary slices.

## Model and theorem

Let u>b>=0. The sender knows a static corruption set C and chooses one of M
fixed probability rows p_m on u roots. The receiver makes a private draw from
that row. There is no public codebook randomization. Write

V(u,b,M)=inf_{p_1,...,p_M} max_{|C|<=b} min_m p_m(C).

For 1<=M<=b+1 let N=b-M+1, and let B(u,b,M) be the best risk among M nonempty,
disjoint uniform blocks partitioning the u roots. The full theorem is

V(u,b,M)=B(u,b,M)
 = max_{1<=q<=u-M+1} [ floor((q N+M-1)/u) / q ].

For M>=b+1 the value is zero, using b+1 singleton action choices; extra messages
may duplicate them. Thus the formula above covers the entire nonzero range and
its zero endpoint. For b=0, one message already has zero corruption risk.
For M=1 the displayed formula reduces to b/u.

The original inherited diagnosis model is the subset u>=2b+1. The combinatorial
theorem is stated more generally for all u>b; no additional diagnosis capability
is inferred for parameters outside the inherited subset.

## Elementary endpoints

For M=1, averaging over all b-subsets gives a worst-case mass at least b/u,
and the uniform row attains it. The explicit formula is also b/u: every
candidate floor(qb/u)/q is at most b/u, and q=u attains it. For M>=b+1, the
zero-error singleton menu applies. Accordingly the nontrivial proof below
assumes 2<=M<=b, so N>=1 and the optimal partition value satisfies0<B<1.

## 1. Two exact facts about partition codebooks

For a partition with positive block sizes s_1,...,s_M summing to u, let R(s) be
its worst-case risk. For 0<=t<1, the adversary can force risk strictly above t
iff it can choose floor(t s_j)+1 corrupt roots in every block, at total cost
sum_j floor(t s_j)+M. Therefore

R(s)<=t iff sum_j floor(t s_j)>=N.

Also, it can force risk at least t iff it can choose ceil(t s_j) corrupt roots
in each block, so

R(s)>=t iff sum_j ceil(t s_j)<=b.

These conditions are exact, including ties. They imply the corresponding
statements for B by minimizing over the finite set of positive integer block
partitions. Each R(s) is a maximum of finitely many allocation ratios, so B is
attained and rational. In the range 1<=M<=b, one has0<B<1. Moreover B is one of
the ratios c/s_j, so when written in lowest terms as a/q, its denominator obeys
q<=s_j<=u-M+1. Finally B<=b/u: for any allocation, the smallest block fraction
is at most the weighted average b/u.

## 2. Arithmetic lemma at the optimal partition threshold

**Lemma.** Suppose 1<=M<=b and B=a/q in lowest terms, with 0<a<q. Then

0 <= a u-q N <= M-1.

The left inequality follows from sum floor(B s_j)>=N for an attaining partition.
We prove the right inequality. Put delta=a u-q N and suppose delta>=M. Choose
any partition s attaining risk B, and write
 f=sum_j floor(a s_j/q), d=f-N,
 r_j=a s_j mod q in {0,...,q-1},
 z=#{j:r_j=0}.
We have d>=0, and sum r_j=delta-q d.

For a sufficiently small positive eta, replacing B by B-eta decreases each
floor by exactly1 at the z zero-residue blocks and leaves the others unchanged.
Optimality therefore requires
 d<z;
otherwise that same partition would have risk below B. In particular z>=1.

Since gcd(a,q)=1, choose k in{1,...,q-1} with a k congruent to -1 modulo q.
A block with zero residue has size a positive multiple of q. Removing k roots
from it leaves it nonempty. Transfer those k roots to a second block. The donor
residue becomes1; a recipient residue r becomes r-1 modulo q.

If d>0, then z>d implies at least two zero-residue blocks. Use one as donor and
one as recipient. Their residues0,0 become1,q-1, reducing f (and d) by1 and z
by2. If the new d>=z, a slightly smaller threshold is already feasible, a
contradiction. Otherwise repeat. This finite procedure either gives that
contradiction or reaches d=0 while retaining z>=1.

At d=0 the residue sum is delta>=M. While z>=1, fewer than M residues are
nonzero, so at least one nonzero residue is at least2. Transfer k roots from a
zero-residue donor to a block with such a residue r. The residues become1,r-1,
both positive. Their sum is unchanged, so f=N remains unchanged while z drops
by1. Repeat until z=0. Then at a sufficiently slightly smaller threshold all
floors remain unchanged and still sum to N, contradicting optimality of B.
This proves delta<=M-1. All transfers preserve the total u, the number M of
blocks and strictly positive integer block sizes. ∎

## 3. An explicit formula for B without partition enumeration

For any positive reduced fraction t=a/q<1 satisfying
 a u-q N<=M-1,
every block partition has risk at least t. To see this, for that partition write
f=sum floor(t s_j), z for the number of zero residues, and R for the sum of the
nonzero residues. If f-N>=z, then
 a u-q N=q(f-N)+R >=qz+(M-z)>=M,
since each of the M-z nonzero integer residues is at least1. This contradicts
the assumed bound. Hence f-N<z, and
 sum ceil(t s_j)=f+M-z<=N+M-1=b,
which proves R(s)>=t and thus B>=t.

Set L=u-M+1 and define
 T=max_{1<=q<=L} floor((qN+M-1)/u)/q.
Each nonzero candidate is<1 and satisfies the displayed arithmetic inequality;
reducing the fraction preserves that inequality because N>=0 and M-1>=0.
Therefore B>=T. Conversely, the optimal reduced fraction B=a/q has q<=L and,
by the arithmetic lemma, a<=floor((qN+M-1)/u). Thus B<=T. This proves B=T.
When N=0, all numerators are floor((M-1)/u)=0 because M=b+1<=u, so both B and
T are zero. This is an elementary exact formula for the partition baseline.

## 4. The external theorem, with precise hypotheses

Use Soheil Azarpendar and Amir Jafari, **On some topological and combinatorial
lower bounds on the chromatic number of Kneser type hypergraphs**,
arXiv:2002.01748v3 (7 October2020), Theorem1.1, printed p.2:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01748v3
Publisher: Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B146(2021),372-381,
DOI10.1016/j.jctb.2020.09.014:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S009589562030099X

Their theorem applies to integers n,k,r with n>=rk and r>=2, and a partition
P of an n-element ground set whose blocks have size at most r. Form a hypergraph
whose vertices are k-subsets taking at most one element per P-block; an edge
is a family of r pairwise-disjoint such vertices. Its chromatic number is

ceil((n-r(k-1))/(r-1)).

The statement is for all integers r, not only primes or prime powers. It is the
partition theorem itself that we use below. Thus no appeal is made to the
older unrestricted multiplicity claim whose composite-r proof had a gap.
Repeated projected subsets are permitted automatically by the explicit copy
construction below; the expanded hypergraph itself uses distinct disjoint
vertices. We do not silently switch multiset and simple-edge definitions.

## 5. Universal lower bound via copy blocks

Fix any codebook and suppose its worst-case risk is strictly below B=a/q.
For each (u-b)-subset D of roots, its corruption complement C=E minus D has
some row m with p_m(C)<a/q. Color D by one such m.

Replace each root i by q-a distinct copies and partition the expanded universe
into the u copy-blocks. The expanded size is
 n'=(q-a)u,
and each block has size q-a<=q. Put r=q and k=u-b. The theorem's required
n'>=rk holds because B=a/q<=b/u, equivalently
 (q-a)u>=q(u-b).
Also q>=2 and k>=1, as established above.

An admissible k-subset of the expanded universe uses distinct root labels.
Project it to its k-subset D of original roots and inherit D's color. This is
an M-coloring of the partition-Kneser hypergraph. Its chromatic number is

ceil(((q-a)u-q(u-b-1))/(q-1))
 = ceil((q(b+1)-a u)/(q-1))
 = ceil((q M-delta)/(q-1)),

where delta=a u-qN<=M-1 by the arithmetic lemma. The quotient is strictly
larger than M, since qM-delta>M(q-1). Hence the coloring has a monochromatic
edge consisting of q pairwise-disjoint admissible expanded k-sets.

Project these q sets to D_1,...,D_q. Projections need not be distinct, and no
argument requires them to be distinct. Each original root appears in at most
q-a of the D_j because there are only q-a copies and the expanded sets are
disjoint. Thus each root belongs to at least a of the corruption complements
C_j=E minus D_j. For their common color m,

sum_{j=1}^q p_m(C_j)
 = sum_i p_m(i) * #{j:i in C_j}
 >= a sum_i p_m(i)=a.

But the color definition gives p_m(C_j)<a/q for all q sets, making the same sum
strictly less than a. This contradiction shows that every fixed codebook has
risk at least B. Partition codebooks attain B, proving V=B and the complete
explicit formula.

## 6. Computing the value and recovering an optimal policy

The denominator scan evaluates u-M+1 exact rational candidates, so computing the
value uses O(u) candidate evaluations. This is not an O(u) policy-construction
claim. Once B=a/q is known, an optimal partition can be recovered by integer DP:
let D(j,n) be the maximum sum floor(a*s_i/q) over j positive block sizes summing
to n, with D(0,0)=0 and infeasible states excluded. The recurrence is
 D(j,n)=max_{1<=s<=n-j+1} [D(j-1,n-s)+floor(a*s/q)].
At D(M,u)>=N, backtracking gives an attaining partition. The recurrence uses
O(Mu^2) integer operations; its floor sum is a directly checkable upper-bound
certificate. Codebooks beyond the zero-error threshold need only b+1 distinct
messages, so duplicated extra labels cause no construction difficulty.

The standard-library checks independently compare the formula with exhaustive
integer block partitions in2,288 parameter cases (8,559 partitions), execute
9,034 residue-transfer tests including both positive- and zero-excess branches,
check85,319 copy-block hypothesis/chromatic-bound parameter instances, and recover
7,139 exact block constructions. Boundary controls include M=1, M=b, N=2,
M>b including M>u, and the accepted gcd family. These are regression checks of
the proof's arithmetic; they neither enumerate arbitrary real codebooks nor
replace the external hypergraph theorem.

For N=2 and M>=2, the previously established partition characterization reduces
to1/floor((u-M+2)/2), so this entire last-but-one positive range is now a
corollary, not only the earlier sufficient tails. For example the three old gaps
are exactly1/3 at(7,3,2),3/8 at(11,5,2), and1/4 at(9,4,3).

## 7. Interpretation and limits

This classifies arbitrary overlapping and nonuniform probability rows, not just
a rational grid. Real-valued rows enter only the strict coloring inequality,
so no computability or finite-denominator assumption is made about the codebook.
The rational fraction comes from the finite partition optimum, not an assumption
on candidate protocols. The proof yields existence of an adversarial corruption
set; it does not confer integrity knowledge on an uninformed recipient.

The q-a copies are auxiliary combinatorial vertices used only in the lower-bound
proof. They are not additional operational integrity roots, independent
observations or evidence carriers. Projecting them back and bounding each
original root's multiplicity is precisely what preserves the original root
identity. No probabilistic independence is assumed in this copy construction.

The shared-random frontier remains the distinct value choose(b,M)/choose(u,M),
with a static adversary preceding the public seed. This proof neither averages
public codebooks into private rows nor moves the adversary after a seed.
Application warrants, source trust, target identity and actual disclosure remain
separate. The full theorem is a deduction from cited prior mathematics,
not a claim of new foundational mathematics or an actual-world identification.
