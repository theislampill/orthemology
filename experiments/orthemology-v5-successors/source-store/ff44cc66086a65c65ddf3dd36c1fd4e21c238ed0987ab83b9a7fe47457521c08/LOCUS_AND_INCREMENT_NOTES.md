# Locus-sensitive modal reading and increment notes

## Technical vocabulary is assigned by locus

There is no corpus-wide replacement rule translating every *mumkin* as either “contingent” or “not impossible.” The following readings concern the inspected sentences and their immediate argumentative contexts. Quotations below are from the historical Arabic primary text, not a modern commentary. The paper's paraphrases are our translations and interpretations.

### P01: Majmūʿ al-fatāwā 9:223–226

Context: the author criticizes the inference from mentally abstracting an essence to its external realization. He then distinguishes kinds of possibility and discusses resurrection through actual cases, the first creation, and a-fortiori comparison.

The mental clause is: “ذهني وهو أن يعرض الشيء على الذهن فلا يعلم امتناعه”. The external clause begins: “خارجي وهو أن يعلم إمكان الشيء في الخارج”. Its next sentence gives the grounds: knowledge of the thing's existence, the existence of its analogue, or the existence of something further from admission of existence. The concluding paragraph at displayed p.226 again contrasts knowing external possibility by existence with merely not knowing impossibility.

At **this** locus, actuality can warrant nonimpossibility; it is not thereby evidence of possible absence. The source does not limit all warranted possibility to already actual token events, because it expressly admits analogy and a-fortiori reasoning. Nor does it give an exhaustive modern modal semantics.

### P02: Darʾ 3:358

Context: can every quantified or qualified body have been otherwise, and can this establish a maker? The opening sentences say each thing has its own reality, quantity and attributes, and compare possible variation in quantity with possible variation in qualities or reality.

The decisive question is: “أتريد به الإمكان الذهني أو الخارجي؟” It is directed at proposed changes of quantity or description. The text then identifies mental possibility with lack of knowledge of impossibility and external possibility with knowledge of possibility outside the mind.

At this locus the author is testing an **alternative-condition premise** in an affirmative theological argument. It is not an observed origination proof and does not establish the complete possible absence of every qualified body. This is an important symmetry control: demanding real modal warrant cannot be reserved for opponents of a source argument.

### P03: Nubuwwāt, hosted PDF p.36/168

Context immediately before the modal distinction: the author discusses arguments that an originated accident requires a subject and a self-standing originated entity requires matter. He describes an event whose coming-to-be is possible, whose existence and absence are admissible, and whose coming-to-be is preceded by possibility. He then notes disagreement over whether possibility is an external attribute requiring a subject or only a mental judgment.

His answer distinguishes two kinds. The mental allowance has the mind as its locus. External possibility is “المتعلق بالفاعل أو المحل”. The next examples are: a capable agent's acting, land's being cultivable, and a woman's becoming pregnant. The clause “وهذا لا بد له من محل خارجي” follows those examples. The divine application says that “يمكن أن يخلق” means being able and empowered to do so, “وهذه صفة قائمة به”.

The following sentences are equally important. A proposed occurrence without a new cause is rejected; the author then discusses a cause subsisting in the divine essence, already described here as ancient/eternal, and the specification of a time by will or completed ability. The surrounding argument proceeds into causes of distinct originated events.

Thus this locus is explicitly about **coming-to-be, agent ability, receptive capacity and their conditions**. It is not merely about a generic sense of nonimpossibility. The words concerning acceptance of existence and absence concern the originated target before the agent/recipient analysis. They cannot silently become a proof that the ultimate capacity-bearer is itself contingent, or a proof that the eternal bearer asserted in the passage has been established by the possibility distinction alone. No claim is made here about resolving the wider scholarly dispute over creation from matter.

### P04: Darʾ 1:293–295

Context: the author distinguishes the general expression/concept of existence from the particular external existences to which it applies. He explicitly divides reality and quiddity, as well as existence, into necessary and possible. He then says of the **particular creature** that its external existence is its external self, reality and quiddity, not two things. The creator follows a fortiori. The subsequent paragraph rejects making distinct attributes identical or multiplying one concrete subject into several independently existing generic substances.

The modal opposition “واجبة وممكنة” belongs to the same discussion as concrete essence/existence identity. This is why the identity alone cannot discriminate between the two sides of that opposition. The report does not interpret *mumkin* here as merely “not impossible” in order to erase that opposition. It preserves the primary division and uses it to constrain the proposed inference.

### P05: Aṣfahāniyya, Arabic hosted section001; supplied translation pp.23–26

The primary author's positive argument is stronger than a thesis about ordinary capacities. He first uses observed origination: prior absence excludes intrinsic necessity, while actual existence excludes impossibility. He then addresses the existence of the contingent, which admits existence and absence, and argues that what is independent of another is sufficient through itself. The decisive Arabic phrase is “إذ نفسه كافية في وجوده”. Its continuation considers either distinguishing ipseity from existence or taking their identity; it concludes that what admits absence is not existent through itself and that every contingent needs another.

This is **whole-bearer existential contingency and sufficiency**, not just the possibility of an act. The report does not deny that the source advances the positive inference. It asks whether P01–P04 supply a new independent warrant for its disputed transition against the currently specified intrinsic rival. The answer is no: the ontology of actual power and concrete essence does not independently establish that every original existential explanation has unconditional existence-entailing content.

The supplied translation at printed p.26 has wording about what is non-existent where the inspected Arabic concerns what admits nonexistence. The paper retains the Arabic modal reading. This is the already recorded narrow translation issue, not a fresh general criticism of the translator.

### P06 and P07: conditions, agents and effects

Darʾ 3:110–112 distinguishes efficient reception from reception in a subject, then distinguishes mutually accompanying conditions from efficient priority. Its warning concerns importing additional meanings into a term for intrinsic necessity after establishing absence of an efficient producer.

The Oneness translation, pp.34–35, concerns actual agency, active power and the complete will-power determination of an effect. Possible-in-itself effects and necessary-through-the-complete-determination effects must not be confused with unconditional necessity of the bearer. No claim about the real contingency of every particular divine will is established here.

## Increment relative to the controlling research

- Retained: actuality is not an uninstantiated rule; active recipients can really possess received powers; P1, P2, N2 and N3 are separate; whole actual origination exceeds endpoint capacity; no hypothetical model certifies metaphysical possibility.
- Newly source-grounded: P01–P03 locate the epistemic and ontological uses of possibility, and P02 applies the demand for external warrant to an affirmative contingency proof.
- Newly discriminating primary control: P04 extends external essence/existence identity to created particulars while retaining the necessary/possible division.
- New reconstruction tested: move from original productive reality to exhaustive modal grounding, then require the same bearer inside every relevant alternative.
- Exact unsupported transitions: actual productive coverage does not entail exhaustive metaphysical coverage; a capacity's needing its subject does not entail that every alternative grounded by that capacity contains that subject.
- Verdict: a genuine source/type and comparative-warrant increment; no new independent warrant closing N2/N3. The rival's contingency and intrinsic explanatory status remain unproved too.
