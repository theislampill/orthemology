# Primary textual checks and research consequences

## Scope of the verified transmission warning

Al-Dhahabi's complete biographical entry for Abu Talib Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Fath al-‘Ishari combines praise of his learning, honesty and devotion with an explicit warning that material entered his transmitted collection without his detecting it. This is not a claim that every report from him was fabricated or that he personally invented the material. [Siyar18:48–50](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/60/4305/العشاري).

The opening of the corresponding Mizan entry was returned by search, but its original-host page could not be fully fetched (HTTP502). **No direct Mizan edition or scan collation is claimed.** Ibn Hajar's Lisan, entry1019, supplies a separately inspected classical textual witness to the Mizan criticism. It names a creed attributed to al-Shafi‘i among the inserted material, then discusses other reports and the conflicting positive evaluation. Its continuation considers how a particular ‘Ashura report could have entered a transmitter's material. That latter discussion is not a separate proof that every word of the creed is false. [Lisan witness, PDF156–157](https://www.cia.gov/library/abbottabad-compound/9F/9FC6BBEDA90B8A48C6F0360A27D6BF32_VOL5.pdf).

Disposition: preserve the question about the specific transmission. Later acceptance of some doctrinal content and the authenticity of its attribution to a named earlier person are different claims. No assertion here settles the complete manuscript history, alternative transmission paths, or all al-Shafi‘i attributions.

## Positive controls on fallible scholarship

In the Malik biography, al-Dhahabi himself distinguishes a narrator's being trustworthy in Malik's assessment from that judgment binding every other critic: another critic might know something about the narrator that Malik did not. The surrounding unit reports Malik's repeated refusals to answer beyond his knowledge. This is a stronger research connection than a generic exhortation to humility: an expert's genuine competence does not eliminate evidence-relative revisability. [Siyar8:72,77–78](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/ar/library/content/60/1295/صفة-الإمام-مالك).

The Shafi‘i unit reports correction of his positions by authenticated prophetic evidence, with explicit transmitter attributions to Harmala and al-Rabi‘. It also reports reasoning from creation while refusing inquiry beyond one's capacity. These are distinct reports from the disputed creed. Locating them in al-Dhahabi verifies their presence in a classical collection; it is not an independent grading of every chain. [Siyar10:31–35](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/ar/library/content/60/1670/الإمام-الشافعي).

## Divine action and fiṭrah remain bounded leads

The inspected al-Fiqh al-Akbar text distinguishes divine action from its created product and places its fiṭrah account within primordial rational address and acknowledgment. This verifies the textual lead in a named work, not the personal authorship of every recension. The act/product distinction alone supplies neither complete attribution of every actual product to an original agent nor a theory of immediate epistemic warrant. [Inspected Arabic text](https://usul.ai/ar/t/fiqh-akbar).

## Consequence for current research

The useful addition is a source-level correction rule: separate content, transmission, translation, scholarly adoption and our later formal reconstruction. Do not use disputed attribution to give a modern model ancient personal authority. The current T20 cooperation/complete-efficacy problem remains where the exact-source audit left it. No new exclusion theorem, model execution, historical time credit, or closure follows.
