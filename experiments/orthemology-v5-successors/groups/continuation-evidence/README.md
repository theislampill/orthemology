## Joint support without private integration

The supplied five-state model has state masses $(.1,.2,.2,.2,.3)$, actual state 0, propositions $A=\{0,1,2\}$, $B=\{0,1,3\}$, $C=\{0,2,3\}$, and $H=\{0,4\}$. Actual private cells are $\{0,1\}$, $\{0,2\}$ and $\{0,3\}$. Each of $A,B,C$ is known by two of the three members, but no member privately knows their conjunction or $H$. The majority inventory contains $A,B,C$, whose intersection is $\{0\}$ and therefore entails $H$. Announcing that inventory or disclosing the others' actual observations would change the epistemic model and is not allowed as a hidden premise.

Under the original probability measure, private-cell support for $H$ is $1/3$, below the prior $2/5$, while joint support is 1. Restricting the comparison background to $D=\{0,1,2,3\}$ changes its prior to $1/7$, so the same private posterior becomes an increase. This witnesses the role of the comparison background. It does not stipulate an outright-belief policy, show that a real group possesses or uses the inventory, or refute every theory of group evidence. Theory-specific access, integration and exclusion premises must be stated separately.

The probability-baseline control is equally narrow. If every included item $e$ has evidential probability $P_E(e)=1$, then $P_E(p\mid e)=P_E(p)$. Factivity alone does not supply that probability-one premise. A candidate-excluding background $B$ can instead support the strict comparison $Q(p\mid B\cap e)>Q(p\mid B)$. Choosing and retaining that background, handling redundant information and defeaters, and justifying possession are additional tasks. Replacing strict increase by a weak inequality would also admit irrelevant information.

## Unknown outcomes and the unrun proposal

For a binary whole-cell endpoint, an assessment may be known success, known failure or unknown without inventing a third substantive outcome. A demonstrated failed component can establish failure even if another component was not measured; a missing recording alone need not establish failure. With assigned denominator $N=S+F+U$, the all-assigned success rate lies in $[S/N,(S+U)/N]$. The contrast of arms $a,b$ lies in

$$[S_a/N_a-(S_b+U_b)/N_b,\;(S_a+U_a)/N_a-S_b/N_b].$$

The width is $U_a/N_a+U_b/N_b$. These are sharp identification bounds when the extreme completions are jointly possible. They assume neither independence nor missing-at-random, and are not confidence intervals. The source's numerical examples are illustrations, not study observations.
