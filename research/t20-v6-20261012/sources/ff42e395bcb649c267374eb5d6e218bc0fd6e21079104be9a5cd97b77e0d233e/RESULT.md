## 2. Domain, metric and action

Let I={1,…,n} be a fixed finite nonempty set of retained origin labels, and let P={I_1,…,I_G} be a fixed partition into nonempty groups. The state domain is

    M = Δ_(n−1)° × ∏_(i∈I) X_i,

where each X_i is a nonempty connected open Euclidean set. Singleton simplexes are points. All weights are strictly positive and sum to one. Each X_i may have its own finite dimension. Labels, groups and positions are retained; no atom merging or quotient is performed.

For a fixed α>0 use the same metric as the received corollary:

    g((u,v),(u,v)) = (1/α)Σ_i u_i²/w_i + Σ_i w_i ||v_i||²,
    Σ_i u_i = 0.                                                   (1)

An admitted audit is specified by arbitrary a_g>0 and acts by

    T_a(w,x)_i = (a_g w_i/Z, x_i),     i∈I_g,
    Z = Σ_g a_g m_g.                                               (2)

It changes only the masses of the groups. It does not modify their memberships, origin identities, conditional weights, positions, objective, metric rule, or other context fields. The word “every” below means the whole continuous positive family (2), not just a list of source ratios or a lattice-generated family. We compare the same time parameter.

The metric rule is fixed in (1), but its value depends on the state. An audit need not be an isometry. “Fixed metric” does not mean evaluating g at the old weights after auditing.

For E∈C²(M), the negative gradient is C¹ and has unique maximal local solutions. A flow commutation assertion uses the maximal flow on this domain; no forward completeness is presumed.

## 3. Block decomposition and the vector-field criterion

Write w_i=m_g r_i for i∈I_g, with r_g∈Δ_(|I_g|−1)°. This is a global smooth coordinate change

    M ≅ Δ_(G−1)° × ∏_g Y_g,
    Y_g = Δ_(|I_g|−1)° × ∏_(i∈I_g) X_i,
    y_g=(r_g,x_g).

Each Y_g is connected. Since dw_i=r_i dm_g+m_g dr_i and Σ_(i∈I_g)dr_i=0,

    Σ_(i∈I_g) dw_i²/w_i = dm_g²/m_g + m_g Σ_(i∈I_g)dr_i²/r_i.

Consequently

    g = (1/α)Σ_g dm_g²/m_g + Σ_g m_g h_g,
    h_g = (1/α)Σ_(i∈I_g)dr_i²/r_i + Σ_(i∈I_g)r_i||dx_i||².         (3)

Each h_g is a positive smooth metric on Y_g independent of m and of other groups' states. The zero-dimensional conditional simplex causes no degeneracy: its tangent is zero.

In these coordinates T_a(m,y)=(B_a m,y). Received J's log-odds proof applies with m as the audited weights and y as the retained internal state. Explicitly, u_g=log(m_g/m_G), g<G, turns every audit into every translation of R^(G−1). Therefore a C¹ vector field is equivariant precisely when

    ydot = v(y),
    mdot_g = m_g[c_g(y) − Σ_h m_h c_h(y)],                          (4)

for functions independent of m. For arbitrary vector fields, v_g and c_g may depend on other groups' internal states. The stronger separation below comes from the gradient requirement and the metric (3), not from symmetry alone.

For G=1 the action is the identity, (4) only says ydot=v(y), and no restriction has been introduced.

Equivariance is equivalent to flow commutation by differentiation at time zero and ODE uniqueness. Maximal existence intervals also coincide for z and T_a z: T_a is a domain diffeomorphism with inverse T_(1/a), so either strictly longer solution would transfer to extend the other. This argument does not assert completeness.

## 4. Exact energy classification

### Theorem

On the domain and metric of §2, for E∈C²(M), the following are equivalent:

1. The negative-gradient flow commutes with every audit (2).
2. The negative-gradient vector field is equivariant under every audit (2).
3. There are C² functions V_g:Y_g→R and a constant C such that

       E(m,y)=C+Σ_g m_g V_g(y_g).                                 (5)

The common constant C can be absorbed into every V_g. The statement includes G=1 and singleton groups.

### Necessity: affine group-mass dependence

Use independent simplex coordinates m_1,…,m_(G−1), with m_G=1−Σ_(g<G)m_g. At fixed y, represent the m-slot differential by coefficients A_g, defined up to a common scalar. The Fisher block of (3) gives

    mdot_g = −α m_g(A_g − Σ_h m_h A_h),
    d/dt log(m_g/m_G) = −α(A_g−A_G)
                      = −α ∂E/∂m_g.                             (6)

By (4), every expression on the left is independent of m. Thus every intrinsic m derivative of E is independent of m. The entire positive simplex is connected and convex, so E is affine on it, for each fixed y:

    E(m,y)=Σ_g m_g A_g(y).                                        (7)

A common y-dependent term has been included in the coefficients A_g. They may be chosen C²: evaluate the affine function at G fixed affinely independent interior mass vectors and invert the resulting constant matrix. No extension to zero masses or regularity at the boundary is used. For G=1, (7) is simply E(y)=A_1(y).

### Necessity: no other group's internal variables

For a tangent in Y_j, metric (3) and (7) give

    ydot_j = −(1/m_j) grad_(h_j) E
           = −(1/m_j)Σ_g m_g grad_(h_j) A_g(y).                    (8)

By (4), ydot_j is independent of m. At a fixed y, multiply by m_j. The resulting vector identity on the open simplex is

    Σ_g m_g [grad_(h_j) A_g(y) + 1_(g=j) v_j(y)] = 0.             (9)

An affine combination that vanishes for every interior simplex point has every coefficient zero. For example, subtract nearby mass vectors to show all coefficients equal, then use their common weighted sum zero. Hence

    d_(y_j) A_g = 0 whenever g≠j,
    v_j = −grad_(h_j) A_j.                                       (10)

This is an intrinsic tangent statement; it also handles the constrained r_j simplex correctly. Positivity of h_j turns its zero gradient into a zero differential. In the connected Cartesian product ∏_jY_j, each A_g is therefore independent of every other Y_j: fix y_g and connect the other coordinates by paths. Thus A_g(y)=V_g(y_g), proving (5).

### Sufficiency

For (5), the same gradient calculation gives

    ydot_g = −grad_(h_g) V_g(y_g),
    mdot_g = −α m_g[V_g(y_g) − Σ_h m_h V_h(y_h)].                  (11)

The internal fields and group fitnesses are independent of m, so (4) applies. Therefore the field and maximal flow commute with all (2). This proves every direction. □

For clarity, the internal equations can be written using any smooth extension of V_g off its conditional simplex:

    rdot_i = −α r_i[∂_(r_i)V_g − Σ_(j∈I_g) r_j∂_(r_j)V_g],
    xdot_i = −(1/r_i)∇_(x_i)V_g,              i∈I_g.               (12)

Changing the derivative representative by a common scalar leaves the conditional equation unchanged. Nonlinear dependence on r_g is fully permitted.

### Positive replay consequence

For a solution of the internal equations, define

    A_g(t)=exp[−α∫_0^t V_g(y_g(s)) ds].

Then

    m_g(t)=m_g(0)A_g(t)/Σ_h m_h(0)A_h(t).                         (13)

Changing the initial group masses by a positive audit leaves every y_g path and A_g unchanged, so the same audit on the currently retained group masses gives the revised trajectory. The final conditional weights r_g(t) need not equal r_g(0); their independent internal evolution is the additional freedom.

This is the received commuting-positive-factor mechanism at group level. It does not identify optimization normalizers with report probabilities, remove the inherited need to retain occurrence/version information, or justify an audit factor empirically.

## 5. Exact joint counterexample to singleton overreach

Let n=3, X_i=R, P={{1,2},{3}}, and define

    s=w_1+w_2,       r=w_1/s,       d=x_2−x_1,
    E(w,x)=(w_1w_2/s)d² = s r(1−r)d².                            (14)

Here V_1(r,x_1,x_2)=r(1−r)d² and V_2=0, so the theorem proves covariance under every (a,a,b), a,b>0. The energy is smooth on the complete positive simplex and is nonnegative. Its position equations are

    xdot_1 = 2(1−r)d,
    xdot_2 = −2r d,
    xdot_3 = 0,                                                   (15)

and its conditional equation is

    rdot = −α r(1−r)(1−2r)d².                                    (16)

Neither (15) nor (16) depends on s or on the other group's mass. However, within-group relative reweighting changes them.

Set α=1, w=(1/3,1/3,1/3), and x=(0,1,2). The position velocity is (1,−1,0). A singleton audit a=(2,1,1) changes weights to (1/2,1/4,1/4), hence r to 2/3, and changes the position velocity to (2/3,−4/3,0). The differential of a weight-only audit leaves the old position velocity unchanged, so equivariance fails by the exact position residual (−1/3,−1/3,0). A changed group frame admitting this ratio cannot reuse the old covariance assertion.

Both sources of forbidden singleton structure are explicit:

    ∂²E/(∂x_1∂x_2)=−2w_1w_2/(w_1+w_2)≠0,
    ∂²E/∂r² at fixed s,x = −2s d².

Thus (14) cannot be written as Σ_i w_i U_i(x_i)+C, and is non-affine in token weights when d≠0. The conclusion is stronger than a nonlinear weight-only example. Removing d² gives the parent's weight-only candidate w_1w_2/(w_1+w_2), which has the same grouping advantage.

### Exact nontrivial path subfamily

With initial r=1/2, equation (16) keeps r=1/2. If c=(x_1(0)+x_2(0))/2 and d_0=x_2(0)−x_1(0), then

    d(t)=d_0 e^(−2t),
    x_1(t)=c−d(t)/2,       x_2(t)=c+d(t)/2,
    A_1(t)=exp[−α d_0²(1−e^(−4t))/16],       A_2(t)=1.

For initial group mass s_0∈(0,1),

    s(t)=s_0 A_1(t)/(s_0 A_1(t)+1−s_0).                          (17)

These are exact moving-position, changing-mass paths for every finite t≥0. Replacing initial odds s_0/(1−s_0) by (a/b) times those odds is exactly the same as auditing the endpoint group masses. The controls differentiate the displayed formulas and verify the group-mass identity symbolically. This subfamily illustrates flow commutation directly; the general classification is proved above rather than inferred from this family.

## 6. Fixed groups, changed partitions, and provenance guards

1. **Groupwise reweighting is a declared action, not a conclusion from shared provenance.** Reports from one person, process or document can have different justified likelihood ratios under a revision. Shared provenance by itself does not make the multiplier constant on their retained origin states. Use this theorem only after the actual revision contract supplies (2). The audited states are the actual model's origin alternatives, not automatically one coordinate per report token.

2. **A simultaneous relabelling is coordinate transport.** If labels and group memberships are permuted together and the objective is transported, this is a conjugate presentation of the same theorem. Erasing labels, merging equal-position atoms, or changing which tokens share a group is a different operation. The original law may not retain enough information for the later declared audits.

3. **Changing the partition changes the admitted audit family.** If a revised partition refines P, it permits more independently varying factors. Existing covariance need not survive; (14) fails when {1,2} is split. Coarsening P weakens the audit family, so covariance already proved for P remains valid for its coarsening while all other assumptions remain fixed. No theorem here supplies a transition rule when the objective, metric, state interpretation or origin catalogue also changes.

4. **Several partition families cannot be replaced by their intersection cells merely by multiplying factors.** For a 2×2 label array, all row multipliers and all column multipliers compose to factors R_ij=a_i b_j. They obey R_11 R_22=R_12 R_21 and preserve the positive-law odds ratio w_11w_22/(w_12w_21). Their common refinement is the four singleton cells, but the factor (2,1,1,1) is not generated. Consequently a generic vector-field symmetry argument cannot infer full singleton-audit invariance from those two families. This is only an action-generation guard; no classification of gradients under several partitions is claimed.

5. **The domain is load-bearing.** On a full connected Cartesian product, vanishing cross differentials imply global separation. Distinct-atom configuration spaces may have disconnected components or non-product fibres. The inherited review's locally constant component-energy counterexample already rules out blindly transferring the global formula to such domains. On smaller product charts the local calculation remains valid; global gluing needs its own assumptions. We make no boundary-support, collision-stratum, unlabelled-law or changed-rank claim.

6. **No epistemic or metaphysical conclusion is supplied.** Numerical independence of group-internal correction from group mass does not establish empirical source independence, truth-directed correction, trustworthy reliability assignments, valid correlated-ground representation, or normative warrant. A metaphysical common source is not thereby one epistemic audit group. G=1 makes the mathematical audit action trivial; it supplies no source-unity proof. The actual useful consequence is narrower: the singleton rigidity must not reject within-group correlation or interaction that a justified groupwise revision contract preserves.

## 7. Inherited positive numerical repair, applied to groups

This is a direct application of received K and its already-attributed numerical predecessor, not a new integrator. Suppose a selected internal map U_g:Y_g→Y_g for each group preserves its declared domain and satisfies V_g(U_g(y_g))≤V_g(y_g). The maps and their selections are independent of the group masses. First set y'_g=U_g(y_g) at fixed m. Then, with h≥0 chosen independently of m, put

    b=min_g V_g(y'_g),
    A_g=1/[1+αh(V_g(y'_g)−b)],
    m'_g=m_g A_g/Σ_k m_k A_k.                                    (18)

This joint step preserves positive normalized weights, commutes with every group audit, and does not increase E. Internal descent gives Σ_gm_gV_g(y'_g)≤Σ_gm_gV_g(y_g). The remaining difference equals Cov_m(V,A)/E_m[A]≤0 because A is decreasing in V, exactly K's covariance proof. The new internal states, baseline and selected h are unchanged by a mass audit, so the two positive mass reweightings commute. The α in (18) matches the mass-reaction speed in (11); without it the same construction has unit reaction speed.

Arbitrary individually descending internal maps need not be consistent time steps for (12). First-order consistency additionally requires U_g(y_g)=y_g−h grad_(h_g)V_g(y_g)+O(h²) in local coordinates. No convergence order, boundary control, stiffness property or implementation is claimed. A mass-dependent internal step or line search can break the audit contract. This corollary establishes constructive utility for permitted within-group interactions while retaining the original numerical method's ownership.
