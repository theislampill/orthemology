# Explicability parity and the burden of a principled exception

9 October 2026 UTC. Focused T20 philosophical assessment. Additive research only; no integration, repository modification, new formal model, or closure claim.

## Result

Della Rocca supplies a genuine additional reason against arbitrary exemptions from explanation: a philosopher who uses unexplainedness to reject otherwise describable cases owes an account of why that consideration loses force in a relevantly similar case. This is independent of simply announcing a universal PSR. It can strengthen the affirmative case against a brute original or an unexplained particular selection.

The earned force is **defeasible parity pressure**, not a demonstrated entailment from a few accepted local arguments to universal explanatory ontology. The stronger claim that any contrary intuition or theoretical cost becomes question-begging once this pressure exists is not established. A reason for a conclusion does not by itself silence independently warranted reasons against it. The paper expressly leaves open a legitimate principled boundary at its conclusion.

O'Connor offers the right *kind* of response to the challenge: distinguish productive explanation from an exhaustive determination of alternatives and support that distinction through a positive account of powers. PCE II's general form does not itself establish the distinction's adequacy. More precisely, it preserves a local explicability argument only where both an explanation and a justified explanation of its absence are excluded. This identifies what his reply would have to establish in the accepted examples, rather than merely repeating that his principle is general.

Neither result equates a contingent act with a contingent original bearer. The completed stopping-asymmetry report already isolates the separate question about the original's existential adequacy. The fresh gain here is an assessment of the **warrant that cross-case parity adds**, of the premise that converts it into a compulsory universal conclusion, and of the precise way a PCE reply can resist that conversion.

## Source synopsis

Della Rocca moves from familiar local explicability arguments to existence (2–7), challenging selective acceptance without a relevant distinction (7–8). He treats opposing intuitions and unwelcome necessitarian consequences as inadequate replies once that challenge is mounted (9–11). He rejects two proposed boundaries but concedes that another may succeed (12–13). Note 13's collapse sketch uses explanatory sufficiency to transmit necessity and disallows contingent self-explanation. O'Connor instead permits causal adequacy without exhaustive contrastive determination, and permits a substantive explanation of why a contingent truth lacks explanation (34–37). His note 16 claims that the generality of these principles undercuts Della Rocca's argument. The comparison turns on the warrant for the boundary, not its grammatical generality.

## Independent assessment

### 1. The strongest parity argument is a challenge about reasons

A charitable reconstruction needs only these commitments:

1. In at least some independently credible cases, inexplicability itself contributes a reason to reject a proposed state of affairs.
2. The target case resembles those cases in the respect responsible for that reason.
3. No relevant difference has been established that defeats or limits that reason here.
4. Consequently, the target inherits a reason against its being inexplicable.

This is not the trivial deduction that if every fact must have an explanation, this fact must have one. Its starting point is an already accepted way of reasoning, and its comparative demand can expose special pleading. Someone who rejects arbitrary specifications everywhere except at a favoured terminus cannot discharge the challenge merely by renaming that terminus.

But two promotions must be distinguished. Moving from some familiar cases to a new case needs relevant similarity in the *explanatory respect*, not merely the recurring form “why does this obtain?” Moving from a reason against an exception to its impossibility additionally needs the reason to be undefeated and strong enough for that conclusion. Neither promotion follows from the existence of the initial reason alone.

These are not demands for a proof from weaker premises before any explanatory judgment may be rational. The judgment can have direct rational warrant. Rather, they identify the scope and strength that must be defended when that warrant is transported. This preserves the affirmative force while identifying the point a serious rival must address.

### 2. Three different kinds of arbitrariness must be kept apart

There can be an unreasoned *choice of theory*, an unexplained *difference between the appropriate explanatory standards*, and an unexplained *occurrence in reality*. These are different claims.

A theorist can give a reason for accepting that some occurrences are irreducible or unexplained without thereby explaining those occurrences. Conversely, someone may reasonably suspect an explanatory difference before possessing a theory of its basis. Their inability to articulate the basis is not automatically the metaphysical fact that no basis exists. A demand for reasons governing philosophical judgment therefore does not, by itself, entail an explanation of every fact judged about.

This distinction does not license an arbitrary exemption. It makes the burden exact: the rival needs a warranted account of why the relevant explanatory demand has a limited scope, or an independently justified reason to doubt the purported similarity. “This is where explanation stops” supplies neither. A positive account of the kinds of dependence involved potentially does.

There is an important difference between rejecting an argument by merely restating its conclusion's negation and presenting independent grounds for a proposition inconsistent with that conclusion. The former is dialectically empty; the latter is a possible defeater. If an argument for universal explicability has some force, that does not make every independently warranted commitment to contingency inadmissible. Which grounds should prevail remains a substantive comparison. The existence of an argument does not confer immunity on it.

The strongest reply at 10–11 is not overlooked: Della Rocca argues that even partial explanatory weight gets his argument started, so a coherence-based response would have to deny explicability any independent weight. That additional inference is precisely what this assessment contests. A reflective comparison can credit explicability with some force while allowing independently supported counterconsiderations to outweigh its extension. A counterconsideration's entailing the denial of PSR does not show that its warrant presupposes that denial. Merely repeating an unexamined dislike of PSR remains inadequate; comparing independently warranted commitments is a different response.

On this reading, the parity argument remains valuable even where it is not decisive. It requires consistency in the use of one's reasons; it does not make the most expansive extrapolation the only rational position before every boundary has been exhaustively derived.

### 3. A concrete test using the balance

The balance example permits a particularly exact diagnosis. The mathematical distinction is standard and already inherited in the project's symmetry work; only its application to this argument is new here. Consider the inference from a setup unchanged by interchanging left and right to an outcome unchanged by that interchange. That inference succeeds if the setup and governing relation determine a **unique** outcome and respect the interchange. The same unique result must then be carried to itself.

Without uniqueness, symmetry has a weaker consequence: if an asymmetric outcome is admitted, its interchanged counterpart is admitted too. Symmetry of the setup or of the outcome possibilities does not alone require symmetry of each actual outcome. This is an inference distinction; it does not establish a metaphysically possible indeterministic balance or certify any physics interpretation.

The strong explanatory demand can still insist that the actual asymmetric result would need a differentiating reason. That is precisely the point under dispute. The balance's intuitive force cannot independently establish the demand's unrestricted scope if the force partly depends on an already accepted account of what determines the balance's motion. A principled rival may retain the ordinary conclusion within that account while disputing its extension to every productive relation. An independently warranted physical dynamics can justify the ordinary equilibrium result; that result is not refuted by this scope distinction. Della Rocca himself presents the example as plausible without expressly endorsing its inference (2).

This is stronger than the reply that different subjects need different rules. It identifies a particular premise whose retention supports the local inference and whose denial blocks its automatic generalization. Whether the premise is genuinely absent from a proposed original power remains to be established; calling the power free or probabilistic does not establish it.

Nor does this single diagnosis defeat the entire parity argument. Fission, dispositional differences, and other cases may provide additional support. Their relevant constitutive or dependence conditions must be assessed in their own right. The point is that their numerical accumulation does not remove the need to determine what each case licenses.

### 4. Irreducible natures and explained occurrences are different targets

A causal type's not reducing to noncausal terms does not entail that an occurrence of its exercise has no productive explanation. Likewise, an attribute's being intrinsic does not make its instantiation a separately produced object. A requirement for a constitutive account of a type, a requirement for productive provision of an occurrence, and a requirement for exhaustive selection among alternatives cannot be transported into one another without argument.

This matters for O'Connor's primitive powers and for the inherited treatment of intrinsic qualifications. An actual power could feature in explaining an occurrence without a reduction of power to a nonpower vocabulary. Its adequacy would still need positive warrant; primitiveness by itself confers no explanatory authority. But a refusal of reduction is not simply the assertion that the occurrence appears without any producer or intelligible basis.

Della Rocca's argument does **not** require a critic to accept all the controversial reductions used among his examples. Rejecting those examples alone therefore misses his strongest case. The narrower challenge survives: even if only some comparatively uncontroversial examples are retained, why is their reason against inexplicability not applicable here? The appropriate response must supply the relevant explanatory distinction and support its application. It cannot merely deny reductionism and declare the whole parity challenge answered.

### 5. What PCE II would have to do to answer that challenge

Write E(p) for there being an adequate explanation of p and M(p) for a nonvacuous adequate explanation of why p has no explanation. These are analytical abbreviations, not a new formalization or a claim that the predicates already have an agreed metaphysical interpretation.

A familiar local anti-brute inference rules out a proposed p because, if p obtained, it would lack E(p). For a contingent p within PCE II's domain, lack of E(p) is insufficient: M(p) may remain available. To preserve that local rejection on the alternative principle alone, the account must show that, if p obtained, neither E(p) nor M(p) could be satisfied. To admit a different contingent case q, it must justify the actual provision of E(q) or M(q).

That gives O'Connor a prospective principled answer. His boundary need not consist in a named exemption for divine choice; it can be the availability of a warranted productive account together with a warranted limit on a further explanatory demand. Such a position can value explanation uniformly while declining to infer that every worthwhile question has a first-order answer. It is exactly the sort of substantive boundary that the parity argument needs to address.

But a universally quantified disjunction does not establish which side is satisfied in a particular case. Nor does it show that explaining the absence of an answer fulfills the relevant demand rather than merely identifies its frustration. The claim in note 16 is consequently strongest when read as a proposed **undercutter of a forced slide**, conditional on the power account and its adequacy, rather than as an automatic refutation supplied by general wording.

The same test prevents a cheap reply to Della Rocca: one cannot give every unwanted contrast the label “inexplicable by nature” and count that as M. The nature and the claimed limit must be independently characterized and warranted. Conversely, the PSR defender cannot exclude M merely by declaring that a complete explanation must be a first-order answer to the very question whose legitimate form is contested.

There is also a domain qualification. Generality over contingent events and truths is not unrestricted generality over every necessary nature or truth. The alternative must justify any relevant boundary there as well; necessary status by itself neither supplies an explanation nor creates a need for an external producer.

The resulting verdict is not a stalemate by definition. Evidence for genuine productive indeterminism and for its explanatory adequacy would weaken the demand for universal exhaustive selection. An argument that every complete productive account necessarily differentiates the actual exercise would strengthen it. The present reading locates these competing warrants; it does not replace their assessment with a quantifier or a label.

### 6. Collapse needs more than the existence of explanations

The modal step can be separated with unusual precision because the relevant assumptions are visible in the source's note 13. Grant a necessary explanatory premise R and a true explained proposition P. P's necessity follows only if the explanatory relation provides the appropriate necessary entailment from R to P. An explanation that is adequate but nonnecessitating does not support that step just because R obtains necessarily.

There are further requirements in the collapse route: an admissible total conjunction of contingent truths, an explanation applicable to that totality, explanatory transmission to the relevant conjunct, and the exclusion of a contingent proposition's sufficient self-explanation. These requirements may be defensible. They are not consequences of the bare statement that each existent or truth has an explanation.

This gives a second, logically distinct principled restriction. Someone may endorse wide explicability while disputing necessitation or the explanatory treatment of the total conjunction. That is not yet a vindication of every contingent choice, but it blocks the identification of all explanatory principles with one collapse-producing package.

The reply must remain fair in both directions. O'Connor does not defeat Della Rocca merely by pointing to the unattractiveness of necessitarianism; the defended parity warrant still deserves consideration. Della Rocca cannot obtain explanatory necessitation solely from the fact that local explanatory demands are plausible. A source whose own existence is necessary need not thereby have a uniquely fixed entire history of exercises. The bearer-level and history-level conclusions require different premises.

### 7. What this adds to the brute-original dispute

The strongest inherited brute original already has actual downstream productive powers. Nothing here replaces it with an uncaused downstream event, self-creation, or production by nothing. The completed stopping-asymmetry report's comparison therefore stands unchanged.

The new parity argument adds a genuine demand for consistency to the defense of that original: if its defender rejects unexplained actuality in relevantly comparable cases, why is the original's obtaining exempt? Its beginninglessness, causal priority, or ability to produce effects does not alone answer a question about the sufficiency of its own existence. Those features can classify the case without establishing that the explanatory demand no longer applies.

However, the transport still requires a reason to identify the relevant explanatory respect. A defender might distinguish a received exercise's need for an actual producer from an unreceived bearer's alleged primitive actuality. Naming those categories does not prove that the latter is possible or adequate. Equally, the existence of the distinction prevents a purely verbal assimilation of the two questions. The positive intrinsic-sufficiency judgment in the direct anti-brute report remains the substantive route to rejecting the primitive original, with its foundational warrant intact.

Della Rocca therefore contributes convergent rational pressure, not a new derivation of the bearer-specific ContingencyNeed premise from agreed local causation. His unrestricted explanation demand also does not supply the original-bearer interface's concrete realization and same-respect transport conditions. A reason, truth, modal status, intrinsic resource, and concrete bearer are not interchangeable ontological categories.

Nor may the full modern package be attributed to Ibn Taymiyyah simply because both resist brute existence. The latter's intrinsic existential sufficiency and account of particular determination must be evaluated in their actual source-specific senses. Selecting Della Rocca's anti-brute pressure while refusing a necessitarian consequence is coherent only if the added modal and explanatory assumptions are explicitly separated and assessed; the needed separation is now identifiable.

## Bounded disposition

Retain the parity argument as independent, prima facie warrant against unmotivated exemptions. Do not describe it as a deduction that every philosopher accepting one local explanation must accept an unrestricted ontology, explanatory reductionism, or necessitarianism. Do not dismiss it merely because its premises are substantive or because the author admits a possible principled boundary.

Treat PCE II as a substantive candidate boundary whose success depends on warranted branch assignments and a defended conception of adequacy. Its generality can make the response systematic; it cannot make it true. The fresh discriminators are the relevant-similarity premise, uniqueness in the symmetric-outcome inference, the difference between a positive reason and an indefeasible dialectical entitlement, and explanatory necessitation in the collapse argument.

These results neither certify a possible brute original nor reduce confidence in actual original-resource Completion. They refine the assessment of the additional parity warrant and the exact response it requires. No new research tranche, model, integration, or T20 closure follows.
