# Creation-worthiness, complete reasons, and particular original determination

9 October 2026 UTC. Bounded primary-source assessment for the active T20 continuation. No integration, protected edit, closure, new campaign, source redistribution, or metaphysical-possibility certificate.

## Result

**The chapter supplies an independently motivated original-scope proposal about what makes creation fitting, but it does not establish the requested combination of complete normative latitude and adequately determined particular willing.** Its positive case concerns the shape of worthwhile creation. Its plurality claim remains qualified by the considerations examined. The decisive control is p.146 n.15, where further reasons capable of settling one array remain expressly open.

There is also a more precise limit within the proposed value argument. **Unbounded goodness within each value-type cannot by itself be promoted into a sufficient admissibility or equal-best criterion while retaining the chapter's memberwise-improvement judgement.** That is a conditional sufficiency objection, not a refutation of the final, expressly incomplete proposal. It locates an additional burden before the apparent remaining options could constitute a genuine complete normative tie.

The chapter therefore improves the rival's account of general creative reasons without furnishing a particular selection principle. It also positively supports distinguishing intrinsic wise necessity from alien coercion. It does not independently establish the existence of a perfect creator, an infinite created plurality, agent causation, or a complete actual divine history.

## Concise source synopsis

Conditionally on perfect-being theism, O'Connor motivates creation through goodness, then considers value, unbounded improvement, and Rowe's challenge (134–139). He proposes evaluating creation-types, with qualifications about indeterministic futures and individual thisness (139–140). An initial infinite-aggregate approach encounters transfinite difficulties; his preferred direction emphasises qualitative excellence, varied kinds, thresholds and unbounded achievement within kinds (141–144). The resulting plurality remains provisional. Divine freedom rests on unreceived sourcehood rather than maximal alternatives; unexamined reasons could determine one array (145–146, n.15). The final section applies the proposal to evil without claiming a complete theodicy (146–148); references follow (148–149).

## Independent discriminating analysis

The following is this report's assessment, not an expanded synopsis or an attribution of conclusions the author claims to have established.

### 1. The reasons have positive content, but at a different explanatory level

A reason for creating cannot be reduced to the rule that whatever happens must avoid wickedness. Beneficent communication, excellent realization and diversity of worthwhile forms are intelligible positive ends. They can explain why a perfectly good source undertakes productive activity, why a deliberately impoverished range needs explanation, and why certain kinds of created goods matter even when they are not highest on one scale. This earns the proposal more credit than a merely negative filter.

The original-scope argument also avoids a familiar creaturely limitation: it does not motivate selection by fatigue, scarce resources, limited information, or a need to acquire essential excellence from recipients. Its claimed goods are suitable objects of a source's activity rather than benefits that complete its constitutive nature. At that level it addresses the inherited question directly.

Nevertheless, an end can explain undertaking a class of actions without selecting each member of that class. The additional step would require a substantive reason why one concrete creative exercise or whole pattern expresses these ends appropriately while alternatives do not, or a defended account of permissible nonunique reasons together with an adequately grounded particular exercise. The chapter supplies neither simply by having a rich account of the end.

Nor is an independent particular preference actually supplied. The resistance to value-detached divine tastes at p.136 puts pressure on a proposal that settles the issue by assigning an unexplained favourite. The invitation to further grounds at p.141 and the explicit reservation in n.15 leave room for a wider practical account; they do not articulate one. This is an open explanatory place, not an established contradiction between the passages.

The reasons offered for compulsory beneficence also deserve a calibrated appraisal. A lack of an intelligible countervailing reason counts in favour of the proposal, but is weaker than an exhaustive demonstration that none is possible. Similarly, finding indefinite qualitative aspiration fitting does not yet establish that every such aspiration requires one specific global arrangement. These are positive philosophical motivations with defeasible scope, not deductions from the bare word “perfect.”

### 2. Which value argument survives the chapter's own revisions?

Three assessments must be distinguished:

1. A creation meets specified threshold and coverage conditions.
2. A creation is not worse than any feasible alternative under every relevant comparison.
3. Several creations are permissible after all relevant normative reasons have been included.

The first does not automatically establish either of the others. This distinction matters to the actual progression at pp.141–144. The early suggestion that infinite totals flatten a ranking is not a safe statement of the final case: the chapter subsequently confronts transfinite magnitude and changes its preferred normative emphasis. Reading the initial equality proposal as a completed proof would erase the objection and reply.

The final criteria have a serious rationale. If excellence is not reducible to multiplication of instances, a conception of perfect creative ambition need not demand an unlimited amount of numerical duplication. If worthwhile kinds are irreducibly different, selecting only the kind with one high score can neglect another genuine good. Those considerations can motivate both nonaggregative evaluation and breadth.

But neither rationale settles all comparison questions. Rejecting sheer quantity as the governing end does not imply indifference to every additional instance, every better instance, or every relation between realizations. Nor does causal separation of created regions alone settle whether their common intentional organization has value. A rationale excluding integrated or process-related value would be particularly important at the inherited original-scope interface, where the excellence of how the source acts cannot be inferred solely from its products' scores.

The threshold likewise has different roles. Uncertainty over its precise location may be harmless to an argument for some extensive creation. It is not harmless to a claim that a fully specified array has already been shown permissible: one must know that its members pass the relevant tests, whether exclusion of other candidates matters, and how conflicting considerations are handled. No numerical threshold is supplied or inferred here.

### 3. The typewise-unboundedness repair does not settle memberwise improvement

The pressure at p.144 is more specific than the inherited observation that an outcome ranking need not select a causal implementation. Here the question is whether the proposed adequacy condition satisfies the very comparative judgement that motivates adding it.

Consider the chapter's assumed comparison between arrays whose members are matched by value-kind, with the second member of each pair better than the first. Requiring each array to attain arbitrarily high values within every represented kind does not, as a matter of the content of that condition, forbid such a comparison. An entire unbounded range could in principle have a jointly feasible kind-preserving improvement while remaining unbounded. Thus the revised coverage condition alone has not excluded the pattern it was introduced to address.

An elementary written witness makes the limitation exact. In a conditional representation with one value-kind, fix a real threshold τ and choose c > τ. Let n range over the nonnegative integers. Represent the first array by a_n = c + n + 1 and the second by b_n = c + 2(n + 1). Both arrays lie above the threshold and are unbounded within their sole kind. The specified index-preserving bijection is kind-preserving, and b_n > a_n for every n. Therefore, if both arrays are jointly feasible creative options and this strict matched-improvement rule is normatively valid, the first meets the structural conditions but is inferior. This is a standard order-theoretic illustration, not a novel theorem or an assertion that goodness is actually measured by these numbers.

The broader conditional is equally elementary. If an admissible class is closed under a jointly realizable uniform improvement of every member that preserves the structural constraints, and every such improvement makes an array strictly better, the class has no maximal array under that norm. Closure under such global improvements is an extra premise: unbounded individual possibilities do not entail it. Likewise the strict comparison rule is an extra normative premise, not a consequence of cofinality. The one-kind illustration is enough; duplicating it across kinds would add no relevant philosophical warrant.

Two restrictions prevent this from becoming an invented refutation. First, a mathematical assignment of larger values is not proof that the corresponding universes exist as possibilities or are jointly creatable. The philosophical issue includes feasibility, composition, identity and the relation of value to kinds. Second, an arbitrary enumeration is not automatically a normatively privileged matching. Any inference from a specified pairing to superiority needs an account of why that pairing has comparative significance, especially for infinite arrays.

On the most source-faithful reading, this is an obligation left open by a necessary condition, not a contradiction in a claimed complete characterization. The explicit concluding direction at p.144 goes from creation-worthiness to conformity with the constraints. The subsequent latitude is qualified by the present stage of the argument. Note 15 makes the incompleteness unmistakable.

Accordingly, the chapter cannot be recruited to prove that every array meeting its final structural conditions is equally best. It can be used to motivate investigating that candidate class. A substantive further advance would defend the relevant global comparison or show why the memberwise objection ceases to apply. Specifically, one could reject the unrestricted matched-improvement norm, establish a genuine joint-feasibility restriction that blocks the proposed improvement, or add independently justified normative conditions that change the comparison. The first two require reasons appropriate to the source's ontology; the last must be more than a condition chosen to save a favoured array. Any such repair may yield a unique optimum, several permitted options, or continued incomparability. The structural constraints alone do not decide among those outcomes. Re-running the arithmetic illustration would not supply the missing defence.

### 4. Complete normative context is more than a complete output description

For the live question, completeness would require an account covering the reasons relevant to the choice, not merely an omniscient list of whatever actually happens. At minimum, the following interfaces need determination:

- Which proposed creations are genuinely feasible, jointly realizable, and individuated as distinct options?
- Which goods and disvalues enter evaluation, including intrinsic, relational, procedural and source-activity goods where relevant?
- What makes the chosen threshold, coverage requirements and comparison principles normatively authoritative?
- Are there further agent-relative or occasion-sensitive reasons, and are those appropriate to an unreceived source rather than borrowed creaturely needs?
- Do prior intrinsic acts establish a practical situation, and, if so, how is their occurrence adequately accounted for?
- Are the relevant reasons actually operative in the productive willing, rather than descriptions that fit the realized result after the fact?

This is not a demand that a finite investigator enumerate an infinite divine history. It is the logical and evidential distinction between warranting a complete reason structure and conceding that the present structure is partial. Our inability to specify all reasons neither proves a real tie nor proves that there is a unique optimum.

It is also not a demand for an externally given rule. An original may intrinsically possess the relevant normative and productive grounds. But intrinsic location is not sufficient evidence that some proposed preference exists, fits perfection, or determines the particular act. An unspecified individualizing character can perform the same unsupported work as an unspecified selector.

### 5. Identity and foreknowledge change the option space

The choices cannot simply be treated as rival numerical labels for identical finished worlds. Some of the proposed variation concerns qualitatively different creations or ranges. Such differences survive removal of mere naming distinctions and must be appraised on their own merits.

Conversely, a theory in which a not-yet-existent individual's thisness is unavailable as a prior object of deliberation cannot casually invoke antecedent personal attachment to that very individual as its selection reason. The account of thisness at p.140, with the contrast made in n.16, therefore changes which particular-preference explanations are available. It does not itself explain the occurrence of the resulting numerical individual or of the particular creative exercise. Removing an antecedently represented identity option is not a universal answer to particular-determination questions.

The treatment of indeterministic futures at pp.139–140 similarly belongs to a substantive metaphysics of knowable content. It should not be advertised as simply denying omniscience: the intended position concerns what determinate facts or choice-informing truths there are. But it cannot be imported into an account retaining exhaustive foreknowledge of the actual history without argument. Evaluation of branching types and evaluation informed by a completely known realized history are different explanatory situations.

These qualifications are expressly part of the chapter's framework; the notes point to controversy rather than resolve it. For this comparison the right result is a transport limit, not a verdict on those separate controversies. Neither ignorance by stipulation nor a fully factive knowledge label can supply the missing productive reason.

### 6. Original sourcehood and the 2009 noncontrastive account

A particularly useful convergence survives: being determined by one's own wise nature is not the same as being compelled by a foreign source. Unreceivedness can matter to freedom even if relevant reasons leave no alternative act. That is consistent with the inherited Arabic distinction between wise necessitation and external coercion, and it blocks a purported disagreement resting only on the word “necessary.”

This convergence also limits a synthesis with the 2009 rival. The 2009 account supplies a principled defence of production without complete contrastive antecedent determination. But the 2008 discussion treats original and created sourcehood differently. One cannot simply transfer a condition proposed for dependent human control into divine agency and call the transfer established. The unreceived source need not obtain freedom by defeating an externally received determining character.

A combined proposal could say: general perfect-being reasons make several options permissible; the original directly produces this intention; noncontrastive explanation is sufficient. That is a substantive rival explanatory standard. It still contests the inherited demand for an adequate differentiating basis rather than satisfying it. Conversely, a combined proposal could say: further intrinsic grounds settle the act while the stated end remains generic. That is compatible with the open Arabic position but needs evidence for the further grounds.

The 2008 chapter does not decide between those proposals. In particular, it does not establish both that all normative reasons have been included and that the resulting plurality is actual. Its willingness to allow uniquely settling further reasons makes it a poor source for a categorical divine libertarian conclusion.

### 7. Voluntary activity, effects, and histories remain distinct

The inherited account distinguishes the original's intrinsic voluntary activity from its created effects, and allows successive completed acts without accepting endlessly deferred completion of one act. The present chapter's cross-option comparison does not address those distinctions in enough detail to replace them.

A plurality of possible complete creations is not a plurality of successive acts in one actual history. Conversely, a single intended creation-type does not prove that its realization consists of only one intrinsic act. Nothing here authorizes either identification, or the attribution of a first temporal creative event. No conclusion about the permitted sequence of original act-tokens follows merely from the cardinality of the proposed created plurality.

Likewise, a universal source premise in a perfect-being framework is not an independent derivation of comprehensive original provision. Conditional use of that premise is legitimate for this chapter's purpose. Transporting its conclusions back as proof of the premise would be circular.

## Bounded disposition

Retain the source as a substantive original-scope proposal for positive creative reasons and intrinsic freedom. Do not call it a complete normative tie, an independently selected divine act, or a demonstrated ontology. Its contribution is stronger than merely forbidding bad choices, but weaker than the required complete determination account.

The exact residual issue is now narrower: can the qualitative-coverage rationale be supplemented by a justified comparison and a complete account of relevant practical goods, and does that full account leave latitude requiring a separately defended intrinsic determination? The source does not settle this. The newly identified within-type improvement pressure tells us where a sufficiency claim would need repair, without pretending that a numerical construction proves the metaphysics.
