## 3. Positive trace reduction: what it really establishes

The source-oriented trace reduction is useful. With independently real contribution tokens, nonempty added contribution, suitable locality, disjointness and complete retention, a joint created history contains a token absent from the solo history. This does not require a positive conserved physical magnitude.

The new preservation join evaluates the premise roles explicitly. Taking contribution in the weak sense of actual participation in a real per-stage existence fact:

- nonempty real created-stage participation holds;
- A's manifestation equals the solo-stage facts;
- all actual stage facts are retained;
- contribution sets of A and B overlap, so source-disjointness fails;
- whole-event CND still holds with no sole entire provider of that jointly supported event.

CND yields disjointness only after each relevant token is independently established as that agent's **entire original contribution**. It does not turn every causal participation relation into such entire ownership. Calling the weak participation sets “contributions” therefore cannot make the positive theorem applicable; calling every act's contribution individually entire cannot supply its own warrant.

The source's distinct-effects-in-one-product argument remains relevant and substantial. The candidate denies that two actual supports must realize disjoint, independently entire-owned created tokens; it does not deny the two intrinsic acts or two actual causal incidences. Whether those incidences are themselves real distinct created effects in the relevant respect is exactly the remaining source/metaphysical classification.

### A small proof-strength distinction

The reviewed generic `fixed_manifestation_disjoint_retained_gives_growth` theorem assumes retention of peer tokens and proves a joint token absent from solo. It does not by itself prove solo is a strict subset of joint, because it has no retention premise for all solo/own tokens. The finite census does assume both contribution sets are retained.

An independent counterfixture has solo=own={0}, peer={1}, joint={1}. It satisfies the theorem's stated premises and conclusion, while solo is not a subset of joint. Adding retention of own tokens establishes true strict inclusion. This does not refute the theorem. It separates nonidentity from the stronger more/greater reading of Majmu L908. The author's prose about retaining all actual contributions can support that stronger premise, but it must appear when a strict-growth conclusion is claimed.
