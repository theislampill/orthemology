## 1. Common definitions and the retained construction

Let T be the finite raw terms generated by I, K, S, two inert markers 0 and 1, and application. R is **full compatible reduction**, not merely weak-head reduction:

- I x → x;
- K x y → x;
- S f g x → (f x)(g x);
- reduction is closed under both application contexts.

The executable evaluator uses a deterministic head strategy; it is a strategy inside R, not the definition of the whole relation. This corrects the review's conflation of the two.

Let ≈ be the reflexive, symmetric, transitive closure of R. A code A is a subset of T invariant under each R step, equivalently a union of ≈-classes. The universe C consists of all these codes. El(A) is the subtype of terms belonging to A; it retains executable term identity.

Define bottom=∅, top=T, A⇒B={f | for every x∈A, f x∈B}, and

    All(B) = intersection over A∈C of B(A), for every B:C→C.

Arrows and arbitrary intersections are invariant. Therefore All(B)∈C. A realiser of All(B) is one raw term r belonging to every B(A); instantiation changes its membership evidence, not r. Hence instantiation at A=All(B) is permitted. The identity code Id=All(A↦A⇒A) has realiser I and admits I[Id] I→I. Bool=All(A↦A⇒A⇒A) has distinct selectors K and K I. The original soundness induction remains valid.

This is standard uniform realisability-style impredicativity. It is not a new foundational discovery, not literally a PER construction, and not the full set-theoretic dependent product. The isomorphism between El(All B) and common-program sections is primarily an unpacking of the definition; it receives no novelty credit here.

## 2. The sharp probabilistic boundary: almost-sure universal typing

### Theorem T01 — atomicity is exactly the arbitrary-intersection condition

Let (Q,Σ,ν) be a probability space whose singletons are measurable. Put

    Sν = {q∈Q | ν({q})>0}.

Consider property P: for every family (A_i) of measurable sets with ν(A_i)=1, **whenever their intersection is measurable**, that intersection has measure one. Then

    P holds  iff  ν(Sν)=1.

Thus arbitrary-intersection almost-sure reasoning is valid exactly for laws concentrated on their positive point atoms, under the stated singleton hypothesis.

**Proof.** For each positive integer n, at most n points have singleton mass at least 1/n. Their union over n contains every positive atom, so Sν is countable and measurable. If ν(Sν)=1, every measurable full-measure A contains every member of Sν: omitting an atom of positive mass would give its complement positive measure. Any intersection of full-measure sets therefore contains Sν and, if measurable, has measure one.

Conversely, for each q∉Sν use A_q=Q\{q}. Every such A_q has measure one. Their intersection is exactly Sν, which is measurable. Property P gives ν(Sν)=1. ∎

**Why measurability is not being assumed away in the Lean logical core.** `AlmostSureBoundary.lean` quantifies over arbitrary predicates and treats `Full` abstractly. A valid probability interpretation is the upward closure of the conull measurable sets:

    Full(P) iff some measurable A⊆{x|P(x)} has ν(A)=1.

This interpretation is monotone even for nonmeasurable predicates. The positive-atom and omitted-null-singleton laws are then valid. The file proves the logical intersection/atom criterion under those explicit laws. It does **not** prove the measure-theoretic instantiation, countability argument, or weak-convergence results in Lean. The ordinary measurable theorem above does not depend on pretending arbitrary intersections are measurable.

### Counterexample T02 — exchanging the quantifiers is unsound

For uniform U on [0,1], each event A_a=[0,1]\{a} has probability one. But

    for every a, Pr(U∈A_a)=1,
    while Pr(U∈intersection_a A_a)=Pr(U∈∅)=0.

Consequently a probabilistic universal-introduction rule cannot simply mean “for each type, success has probability one”, with unrelated null exceptions allowed. This is a semantic defect even though every finite or countable conjunction passes.

### Corollary T03 — the correct level is the observational quotient

Let q:X→Q be a measurable observation map, and suppose the available type events are inverse images q⁻¹(A) of measurable A⊆Q. Apply T01 to the pushforward q_*µ. Arbitrary-intersection almost-sure typing is valid precisely when q_*µ is concentrated on its positive point atoms, provided the type family contains the necessary singleton complements and the relevant intersections.

Continuous raw randomness is not itself prohibited. It may lie inside an observational class. The restriction concerns diffuse mass across **type-distinguishable outcomes**. If the code repertoire is coarser than all measurable events, atomicity remains sufficient but necessity need not follow: the separating complements used in the proof might not be expressible.

### Theorem T04 — a precise compactness/typing trilemma

For a class K of Borel probability measures on [0,1], the following three requirements cannot all hold:

1. δ_x∈K for every x∈[0,1];
2. K is convex and weakly closed;
3. every ν∈K validates property P of T01 for the full Borel type-event family.

**Proof.** By 1 and convexity, the grid laws

    ν_n = (1/n) Σ_{k=1}^n δ_{k/n}

belong to K. For every continuous f:[0,1]→R, their integrals are Riemann sums converging to the Lebesgue integral of f. Thus ν_n converges weakly to the uniform law λ. Weak closure gives λ∈K. But λ has no positive point atoms, so T01 contradicts requirement 3. ∎

Each pair can hold:

- 1+2: all Borel probability laws on [0,1];
- 1+3: all finite-support laws, which are convex but not weakly closed;
- 2+3: laws supported on the fixed set {0,1}, a compact convex line segment which omits other Dirac laws.

This is a sharply stated three-way trade-off of the kind requested by the review, but with **different, explicit assumptions**. It is not the review's underspecified three-property claim and not an assertion that all impredicative probabilistic type theories are impossible.

**Relevance to Kakutani.** A proposed convex compactification can introduce nonatomic laws and thereby destroy the desired universal-typing interpretation. A fixed finite set of actual programs avoids this problem: the simplex of laws on those programs is compact and convex, while every law is atomic. A continuum of probability parameters is not a continuum of distinct program outcomes.

**Novelty disposition.** These proofs combine elementary measure/filter facts with the chosen typing semantics. The exact combined formulation is new to this packet, but the search did not establish priority or an independently recognised breakthrough. It must be compared with probabilistic logical relations, intersections of conull filters, probabilistic powerdomains and compactness restrictions. No theorem title should be used as evidence of novelty.

## 3. Exact representation boundaries

### Theorem T05 — relation extension preserves exactly the old components

For a relation R on any set X, the R-invariant subsets are exactly unions of components of its equivalence closure ≈_R. An added relation S preserves **every** old invariant subset iff

    S ⊆ ≈_R.

**Proof.** Sufficiency follows because each old code is constant on a component. For necessity, if x S y but x≉_R y, the principal class [x]_R is an old invariant subset containing x but not y; S fails its invariance. ∎

This gives an exact, general test for whether a new computation rule preserves the old code universe. The finite census checks all 512×512 relation pairs on a three-element carrier; that is an exhaustive check of that finite scope, not the proof of the general theorem.

### Corollary T06 — naïve nondeterministic conversion collapses the model

If choice(x,y) reduces both to x and to y, and both branches are treated as bidirectional type-membership equalities, then for every code A,

    x∈A iff choice(x,y)∈A iff y∈A.

Since x and y are arbitrary, only ∅ and T remain. The repair is to treat branching as an effect with a law/support semantics, not as equations equating its outcomes. This does not say nondeterminism itself is inconsistent.

### Theorem T07 — the review's bare trilemma is false

Take C=Unit, El(*)=Bool, All(B)=*, pack(f)=f(*), and app(b,*)=b. There is an internal universal code, two computational values, and abstraction/application for every ambient family over C with beta. All three informal properties coexist.

This is deliberately a small countermodel. It lacks the rich closure and self-universe conditions of Girard's construction; that is precisely why those hypotheses cannot be silently omitted. The theorem resolves the proposed blanket claim, not the stronger and well-specified Girard theorem.

### Theorem T08 — observation factorisation and the corrected erasure bound

Let o:I→Q be surjective. A family f:I→V factors as g∘o exactly when it is constant on each fibre of o. If V has at least two values, **every** family factors iff o is injective.

**Proof.** Factoring families are fibre-constant. Conversely define g(q)=f(i) for any i with o(i)=q; fibre constancy makes it independent of the choice. If o identifies distinct a,b, choose f(a)≠f(b), preventing factorisation. If o is injective as well as surjective, use its inverse. ∎

Full type erasure corresponds to one observable fibre. It can represent precisely constant underlying-program sections, not arbitrary ambient families. In finite cases there are |V|^|Q| representable families, rather than |V|^|I|. For real-valued finite families, the optimal sup-norm fibre-constant approximation has error

    (1/2) max_fibre(max f − min f),

attained by the midpoint of each fibre's range. The lower bound follows from the triangle inequality applied to the fibre's extrema; the midpoint attains it.

These are standard factorisation/approximation facts. Their purpose is to replace a one-example slogan with an exact capacity statement, not to claim a new version of Reynolds' theorem.

## 4. Finite syntax, substitution and semantic soundness

### Theorem T09 — renaming and substitution commute with interpretation

Finite type syntax is var(n), arrow(A,B), all(A), and bottom. An environment ρ:N→C interprets variables. A binder extends ρ at zero and shifts old variables. Define capture-avoiding simultaneous substitution structurally, lifting the substitution under a binder.

Induction on the type expression proves:

    [[rename r A]]_ρ = [[A]]_(ρ∘r),
    [[subst σ A]]_ρ = [[A]]_(n↦[[σ(n)]]_ρ).

The arrow case follows componentwise. In the binder case, the lifted substitution maps the newly bound variable to itself and shifts substituted older expressions; the environment-extension equation yields the induction hypothesis under every code argument. This is the part the earlier prose bridge omitted.

### Theorem T10 — finite universal introduction is sound

Use `FiniteDerives t A` with i/k/s/application, one-premise type generalisation, type instantiation, and reduction. Its universal-introduction constructor takes **one finite derivation of the body**, not one proof per semantic code. There are no open term assumptions in this particular combinatory certificate language, so there is no omitted term-context generalisation side condition.

Induction on the finite derivation, using T09 for instantiation and the environment-extension identity for generalisation, gives

    FiniteDerives(t,A) ⇒ for every ρ, t∈[[A]]_ρ.

It also constructs the corresponding semantic `Derives` proof. This removes infinitary proof search from the source certificate language while retaining the semantic model as the target.

### Proof-carrying checker design

`FiniteBridge.lean` defines a `Checked` result carrying the inferred type, the raw program and a `FiniteDerives` proof. `check` returns `Option Checked`. Its reduction routine constructs a target together with an actual `Step` proof. Thus any accepted result comes with the precise theorem to which T10 applies; bottom is not accepted as inhabited.

**Actual verification limit.** This code is authored but uncompiled. The separate Python checker uses direct de Bruijn cutoff substitution; the Lean proposal uses simultaneous substitution. The packet compares Python substitution with an independent fresh-name reference and finite interpretation tests, but does not yet contain a machine-checked refinement proof of the Python implementation or JSON parser. A structurally proof-carrying design is not equivalent to an observed successful kernel check.

## 5. Completeness and normalisation: positive answers and counterexamples

### Theorem T11 — exact-term completeness fails

Let z=K I 0. It reduces to I, so invariance puts z in the semantic identity type. Every term derived in the supplied infinitary or finite source calculus is marker-free: I/K/S are marker-free; application preserves the property; type rules do not change the term; reduction cannot introduce a marker absent from its source. Induction proves this invariant.

But z contains 0. Therefore semantic membership does not imply derivability in either supplied calculus. This is a natural counterexample exploiting a real source/semantics mismatch, not a failure to search for a derivation long enough. `BoundaryResults.lean` contains the corresponding proof proposal.

The counterexample alone does **not** refute completeness modulo conversion, because I is a typable representative of z's class.

### Theorem T12 — every inhabited invariant code contains a non-SN term

Let D=S I I and Ω=D D. There is a compatible three-step cycle

    D D → (I D)(I D) → D(I D) → D D.

For any t∈A, the term K t Ω reduces to t and therefore belongs to A. Compatible reduction can instead cycle forever inside Ω. Consequently K t Ω is not strongly normalising.

Thus no nonempty code in the current universe consists exclusively of strongly normalising programs. This is stronger than saying that a normalisation proof was omitted: that semantic claim is false and requires different candidates/closure conditions. The cycle and the code statement are represented in `NormalisationAndNumerals.lean`, uncompiled.

### Theorem T13 — the finite source calculus is nevertheless strongly normalising

Translate I,K,S into λx.x, λx y.x, λf g x.f x(g x), and application homomorphically. Translate the finite type rules into Curry-style System F rules, treating the primitive bottom symbol as a fixed fresh type constant. Type generalisation and instantiation are the usual ones. The source reduction rule is justified by System F subject reduction. Each compatible SKI step translates into a **nonempty** compatible beta sequence (one, two or three root contractions, with context closure).

By the standard strong-normalisation theorem for typable System F erasures [S05], an infinite source reduction would give an impossible infinite beta reduction. Hence all finite-source typable terms are strongly normalising. ∎

This is a relative mathematical transfer using established metatheory, not a new normalisation theorem and not a kernel-checked bridge in this packet. T12 and T13 are compatible because the finite source is strictly narrower than semantic membership.

### Theorem T14 — even conversion-completeness fails for this primitive-bottom language

The semantic type bottom⇒bottom is all raw terms, by vacuous quantification. Hence Ω belongs to it. But Ω has no normal form. More explicitly, all its reducts have the shape

    (I^m D)(I^n D), m,n≥0.

A step decrements an available outer I stack, or, when m=0, the S contraction produces (I^(n+1) D)(I^(n+1) D). Every such term has a redex. This proves absence of a normal form by induction over reduction sequences.

For completeness, confluence of this concrete SKI relation follows by the following parallel-reduction proof. Define t⇛u using reflexivity on atoms, componentwise parallel application, and parallel root I/K/S contractions whose retained arguments reduce in parallel. A term's complete development t* contracts its original root redex, if any, using complete developments of its arguments; otherwise it develops the two application components. This recursion is on strict subterms.

Induction over t and the parallel derivation proves the triangle property: if t⇛u, then u⇛t*. For a non-redex application only the componentwise rule is possible, and the component induction hypotheses give the result. At an original I root, a componentwise step leaves I x' and can then contract using x'⇛x*; a contracting step already gives x', handled by the same hypothesis. At K, the same argument retains the developed first argument and discards the second. At S, a contracting step gives (f' x')(g' x'); the three induction hypotheses develop this to (f* x*)(g* x*), with the two copies of x' developed identically. A componentwise step at that root can make the root contraction in its next parallel step. The partial K and S application prefixes are not themselves root redexes, so there is no omitted overlap case.

The triangle property makes parallel reduction diamond: any two one-step parallel targets reduce to t*. Its reflexive transitive closure is confluent by the finite diamond-grid induction. Every original step is parallel, and every parallel step can be sequentialised by developing arguments then contracting the root, so the two reflexive transitive closures coincide. Therefore the original compatible SKI reduction is confluent.

A term convertible to a normalising term consequently has a normal form. Ω cannot be convertible to any finite-source typable term, by T13. Completeness modulo conversion fails as well, for this language and this interpretation of primitive bottom.

**Dependencies and scope.** Confluence is proved above by the standard parallel-development method; this proof is not supplied in Lean. T13 still invokes established System F normalisation. The counterexample does not contradict positive-type completeness results for other candidate models or differently restricted source languages [S01].

## 6. Nontrivial free theorems directly from codes

For any raw x the principal class [x]={t|t≈x} is a code; unions of classes are codes.

### Theorem T15 — identity characterisation

    f∈All(A↦A⇒A) iff for every x, f x≈x.

For necessity instantiate at A=[x]. For sufficiency use invariance of any A containing x. This is equality of observable behaviour modulo the declared conversion relation, not syntactic equality f=I.

### Theorem T16 — global Boolean selector characterisation

If f is marker-free and f∈Bool, then at least one of the following global behaviours holds up to conversion:

    for every x,y, f x y≈x;
    or for every x,y, f x y≈y.

First instantiate at [0]∪[1] to obtain f 0 1≈0 or ≈1. Substitute arbitrary x and y for the inert markers in the conversion derivation. Each SKI rule and context step is preserved by this substitution; f is unchanged because it is marker-free. The same chosen branch thus works for every x,y. Conversely, either global projection behaviour implies Bool membership by invariance.

The proof establishes a disjunction of the two global laws; uniqueness of the branch uses separation of the two markers and, if expressed through arbitrary conversion proofs, the usual confluence fact. The Lean source does not claim an unproved uniqueness theorem. Neither law asserts that f's syntax must literally be K or K I.

### Theorem T17 — Church numeral representation

Let NatCode=All(A↦(A⇒A)⇒A⇒A). For every marker-free f∈NatCode there exists one n≥0 such that

    for every s,x, f s x≈s^n x.

Use the code O={t|there exists n, t≈0^n 1}. It contains 1 and is stable under application of the raw marker 0. Membership of f gives f 0 1∈O and hence a witness n. Marker substitution as in T16 transports that fixed witness to every s,x. No iteration count is selected afresh after observing the type or arguments.

These are genuine consequences beyond definition unfolding, but they are not a full Reynolds abstraction theorem. They are also not novel claims of priority: much stronger realisability parametricity and positive-type representation theorems are known [S01].

## 7. A symbolic-certificate compiler with a proved use case

### Theorem T24 — symbolic trace specialisation

Suppose f is marker-free and a checked **head-reduction trace** gives

    f 0 1 →^k 0   or   f 0 1 →^k 1.

The trace consists of actual SKI contractions, not approximate executions or statistical tests. Substituting any raw x,y preserves the steps and their heads until the selected argument is returned. Therefore the very same certificate establishes f x y→^k x or →^k y for all raw inputs, including divergent ones.

`certify_selector` checks freshness and constructs this trace; `replay_selector` verifies it. The compiler can replace the two-argument dispatch behaviour by the appropriate primitive selector, with its exact head-prefix cost. The freshness check is essential: K(K 0) consumes both test arguments and returns marker 0 even though it is not a uniform first projection on arbitrary inputs.

The packet records the initial faulty mutation-test fixture K 0, which was still rejected by the endpoint check. It was repaired to K(K 0), making removal of the freshness guard genuinely detectable. Both evidence states are retained.

A semantic type alone gives no uniform operational budget: I^n K has the same Boolean type for all n but requires n extra head steps. Thus the certificate records a property not supplied merely by the type. This is a concrete compiler use, not a benchmark claim of superiority. General contextual equivalence, evaluation under every effect strategy, a native backend and WebAssembly correctness remain separate obligations.

## 8. Term-dependent semantics with explicit coherence

### Theorem T23 — coherent tracked dependency

A family D:T→C is conversion-coherent when x≈y implies D(x) and D(y) have the same members. This cannot be omitted. For example, a raw-syntax-sensitive family selecting top at I and bottom at I I is not coherent even though I≈I I.

Define the tracked dependent product

    Π(A,D)={f | for every x∈A, f x∈D(x)}.

It is a code because conversion of f is preserved in each application and each D(x) is a code. Argument conversion is sound because coherence transfers the fibre. Thus simultaneous conversion of function, argument and dependent output is accounted for.

Use the Church pair pair(x,y)=S(S I(K x))(K y), which satisfies pair(x,y) f→*f x y. Define Σ(A,D) as the union of conversion classes of these pairs with x∈A and y∈D(x). Projection by K yields an A-member. Projection by K I yields a member of D(first), because first≈x and D is coherent.

An identity code can contain exactly those p≈I for which x,y∈A and x≈y. It is invariant, has reflexivity witnesses and supports transport along conversion through coherent families. `EffectsAndDependence.lean` states these semantic results.

This is a useful semantic extension, not a completed flat dependent proof assistant. A full syntax with contexts, contextual substitution, universe rules, the general identity eliminator J, decidable conversion and executable checking for all admissibility conditions is not provided. General semantic membership is not made decidable. The ambient set theory/Lean universe hierarchy is also not eliminated. Calling these familiar constructions a new full dependent type theory would overstate the result; compare [S02], [S04], [S12].

## 9. Effects live in the calculus rather than beside it

The new finite effect syntax has pure certified terms, rational weighted mixture, application, and type abstraction/instantiation. Both mixture branches must have the same type, including at weights zero or one. Its compiler erases type operations completely and preserves actual effect operations.

A finite distribution is normalised and has nonnegative rational weights. Application independently samples the function computation and argument computation **once each**, then returns their raw application. It does not duplicate an effectful expression and silently resample it after a K/S contraction. Sharing and independence are explicit parts of the intended evaluation strategy.

For a pure code A define

    Lift(A)={µ | µ is normalised and supp+(µ)⊆A}.

### Theorem T18 — support soundness and internal lifted universals

Induction on source derivations proves that every positive-weight output has the declared pure type. Pure terms use T10; mixtures use their same-type premises; application uses the realised arrow; erased universal rules use intersections and T09. Because mass is one, a distribution inhabiting Lift(bottom) would need a positive-weight outcome in the empty set. No such distribution exists.

Furthermore,

    Lift(intersection_i A_i)=intersection_i Lift(A_i).

This support identity alone is elementary. Its substantive application is justified by the explicit choice of atomic, normalised laws and T01: almost-sure membership on this countable program carrier really has a common full-support witness. There is no uncountable-null-set quantifier error.

Dirac laws show Lift is injective. Therefore Cprob={Lift(A)|A∈C} is isomorphic to C. Transport All through this isomorphism. The probabilistic universal code is itself in Cprob and can be instantiated at itself. The same computation/distribution is used at every instance; a family of separately chosen laws is not a polymorphic realiser.

`EffectsAndDependence.lean` contains support-level soundness, outcome existence and no-empty results for the proposed effect calculus. Exact mass arithmetic and the Python parser are executable-tested, not yet part of the kernel proof. The literature already treats much richer combinations of polymorphism, nondeterminism, probability and relational reasoning [S03].

### Theorem T19 — compact typed selection

Fix actual proved/certified programs t1,...,tm in All(B). Their real probability simplex Δm is nonempty, compact and convex. A closed-graph correspondence R:Δm⇉Δm with nonempty convex closed values has an equilibrium by Kakutani. Every equilibrium law is supported on the same valid programs; all type instances, including the universal type, remain valid.

This is the safe instance of T04, not a new proof of Kakutani. Its hypotheses cannot be dropped. The full simplex over a countably infinite program set need not be compact: the right-shift map on laws over N has no fixed point, since stationarity would force each coordinate to zero. Existence also does not by itself give a computable or convergent policy.

## 10. A nonrational reflective equilibrium with an executable exact sampler

Let a response use one fair bit and two independent calls to a p-biased oracle and return one precisely when the fair bit is one and the two oracle calls are not both one. Its response probability is

    g(p)=(1−p²)/2.

### Theorem T20 — unique equilibrium and rational representation gap

A coherent law satisfies p=g(p), hence p²+2p−1=0. Its unique solution in [0,1] is

    p*=sqrt(2)−1.

It is irrational. Every finite rational-mixture syntax tree has rational outcome probabilities by structural induction over finite sums and products. Therefore no such finite tree represents this equilibrium exactly. A real convex completion alone has introduced a realisability obligation, not an executable program.

### Theorem T21 — exact fair-bit sampler, almost-sure termination and cost

Draw U by successive fair binary digits. After n digits, its prefix determines one dyadic interval [k/2^n,(k+1)/2^n). The polynomial H(x)=x²+2x−1 is strictly increasing on [0,1]. Compare both endpoints with its unique root:

- if H(upper)<0, return K;
- if H(lower)>0, return K I;
- otherwise draw the next fair bit.

The sign is computed exactly by the integer expression k²+2kd−d² for endpoint k/d. No primitive irrational constant or floating-point comparison is required.

Since p* is irrational, exactly one dyadic cell remains unresolved at every depth. Consequently

    Pr(N>n)=2^(-n),
    Pr(return K by depth n)=floor(2^n p*)/2^n,
    E[N]=Σ_{n≥0}Pr(N>n)=2.

Taking limits proves almost-sure termination and output probability exactly p*. The equality-of-binary-expansions exceptional paths have probability zero; no sure-termination claim is made. Every terminating leaf returns a sound internally polymorphic Boolean selector. Its own code remains an admissible type argument. This is a countably branching productive extension, not a finite rational-mixture term.

`irrational_selector_prefix` is an executable exact finite-prefix transition. The census verifies every bit string through length 12 and checks deeper exact brackets. That finite census does not prove the almost-sure limit or expected value; those follow from the preceding argument. A full recursive probabilistic typing judgement with almost-sure termination certificates has not yet been encoded in Lean.

### Theorem T22 — convergence for this response, not for Kakutani generally

For p∈[0,1], g(p)∈[0,1/2]. On [0,1/2],

    |g(x)−g(y)|=|x−y|(x+y)/2 ≤ |x−y|/2.

For p_(n+1)=g(p_n) and arbitrary p0∈[0,1], induction gives |p_n−p*|≤2^(-n) for n≥1. This supplies a contraction-based approximation method in addition to existence and the exact sampler. It is not an inference of convergence from Kakutani alone.

Exact sampling from fair bits and reflective-oracle semantics have extensive prior art [S10], [S11]. The concrete coupled certificate/cost construction is a worked application, not a claimed new sampling principle.

The achieved resolution is a narrower, defensible one: internally coded self-instantiating polymorphism is retained; its finite source/semantics boundary is made explicit; genuine incompleteness and normalisation obstructions are proved; effect extensions respect computational observations rather than equating branches; and exact atomicity/compactness conditions prevent unsound probabilistic universal reasoning. This is progress on the intended foundations, not evidence that every requested breakthrough has already occurred.
