## Main finding

The chapter's intended function is to add scientific confirmation to an already asserted rational proof of the temporal origination of the cosmos. It is not presented as the sole ground of that proof. The opening explicitly makes modern cosmology a confirmation of the philosophical argument for created time and space (p. 87); the closing celebrates convergence of rational and scientific proof (p. 130); the final fallback says the rational argument remains conclusive and independent of scientific developments (p. 132).

The chapter's strong thesis is that the cosmos, including time, began. Its evidential presentation is cumulative and its theistic fallback is broader: even an attempted eternal-world alternative, if it required the finely adjusted structure Ameri attributes to it, would still point to design (p. 129). That fallback supports a different route to a designer; it does not itself establish temporal origination. Keeping these two routes distinct is essential.

## Continuous structural context

### 1. Introduction and history, pp. 87–92

P. 87 sets an apologetic contrast between earlier philosophical/materialist eternity and the twentieth-century scientific case for origination. P. 88 identifies the Big Bang with the beginning of both space and time, rather than an explosion in pre-existing space or time; the footnote acknowledges that explosion language is figurative. It also introduces the heat-death argument before the expansion history.

Pp. 88–90 narrate Einstein, Friedmann, Slipher, Hubble, Lemaître, Gamow, the microwave background, and subsequent confirmation. This narrative is used to make temporal origination appear an empirical discovery independently converging with theology. Pp. 91–92 introduce steady-state continuous matter creation as the main historical rival, reject it by observational and explanatory claims, and conclude with a scientist's reported preference for the Big Bang's comparatively economical fit.

This historical sequence is not merely background: the alleged defeat of a particular continuous-creation cosmology supplies the initial pattern for later claims that eternal alternatives are scientifically unsuccessful. It does not yet discuss divine eternal activity or distinguish theological varieties of such activity.

### 2. Positive evidence and testimony, pp. 93–103

Pp. 93–99 summarize 27 numbered evidential items, introduced as most of Hugh Ross's list of thirty. These include microwave-background properties, expansion, light elements, stellar/galactic histories, and other physical features. The summary cites Ross's appendix at its end. It is therefore inaccurate to call this list 27 independently checked scientific proofs assembled afresh in the present investigation.

Two conditional formulations are especially worth preserving. P. 96's item 15 states the Hawking–Penrose result with assumptions about cosmic mass and general relativity governing the dynamics. Several other entries are instead worded absolutely or exclusively. The list's rhetorical destination is one general conclusion, but not every entry is expressed with the same logical form or evidential strength.

Pp. 99–103 shift from observations/theorems to testimony and the alleged philosophical resistance of scientists. Ameri explicitly explains in a p. 103 footnote that the length of the testimony section is meant to rebut the denial that the Big Bang implies a creator. Reported testimony about the theological significance of a beginning is part of his apologetic strategy; it is not an additional independent measurement of that beginning. Some testimonies are themselves mediated through Heeren, Jastrow, or other secondary publications.

### 3. The hypothetical failure of the Big Bang, pp. 104–114

Ameri responds through several layers rather than staking everything on one model:

1. Pp. 104–105 distinguish a scientific theory from an unsupported guess.
2. Pp. 105–110 offer other grounds for a beginning: the second law, relativity, expansion/BGV, and Olbers' paradox.
3. Pp. 110–114 argue that evidential accumulation and successful correction within the broad Big Bang framework make abandonment unlikely. P. 111 distinguishes the broad model from theories nested within it and treats inflation as an internal repair for the horizon, flatness, and monopole problems.
4. From p. 114 onward he challenges proposed eternal alternatives individually.

The second-law presentation is explicitly tied on p. 106 to a materially closed cosmos and to a particular description of its energy use. Pp. 107–108 then claim that quantum cosmology cannot evade the generalized second law except by reversal of time. These are the author's scientific and philosophical claims, not independently verified results of this reading.

BGV appears twice in this stretch. P. 108 introduces it under relativity, in broad language about incompatibility between temporal infinity and Einstein's theory. P. 109 describes a positive-expansion condition and then an incompleteness result before concluding against eternity. The text subsequently compresses this to a blanket characterization of expanding universes (p. 127). Preserve this movement of scope: the chapter contains conditional/theorem language as well as much stronger universal paraphrases. This report does not decide whether any paraphrase faithfully captures the original theorem.

Olbers' paradox is introduced in a quotation with assumptions of spatial and temporal infinity (p. 110); Ameri's following simplification emphasizes beginningless time. Those are not word-for-word equivalent premise sets and should not be merged in a philosophical reconstruction.

### 4. Proposed eternal cosmologies, pp. 114–123

Ameri's objections are layered: lack of observational support, alleged physical/mechanistic problems, an independently assumed rational prohibition of an infinite past, and occasional design inferences.

- **Oscillating/cyclic universe, pp. 114–116.** A beginningless sequence of expansions and contractions is first characterized as rationally defective independently of physics. Additional objections concern evidence, thermodynamics, density, mechanism, a reported bound on cycles, and fine adjustment. The conclusion is expressly conditional: even if an oscillating history were correct, it could not be past-eternal. P. 116 nevertheless allows an infinite future in the reported model. Cyclicity itself is not identified with beginninglessness.
- **Eternal inflation, pp. 116–118.** The branching production of further universes is described separately from whether the overall process has an infinite past. Ameri questions empirical testability and cites alleged beginning results. Do not turn his rejection of detailed eternal-inflation scenarios into rejection of the general inflationary repair he endorsed on p. 111; those have different stated targets, although the rhetoric is broad.
- **String-related scenarios, pp. 118–120.** A pre-Big-Bang black-hole/vacuum scenario is rejected by arguments about old/merged products and equilibrium. The ekpyrotic/cyclic brane scenario is attacked through rational infinity, internal physical issues, design, and BGV. P. 120 then reports Steinhardt as conceding that past-infinite cycles are not required and a beginning is plausible. Ameri's target remains the claim that such scenarios establish eternity, not merely their inclusion of earlier stages.
- **Vacuum fluctuations, pp. 120–121.** This is the clearest recognition that a beginning of *our* universe need not, by itself, be the absolute beginning. The proposed timeless-looking background is actually an active energy domain producing universes. Ameri objects that beginningless production would yield infinitely old/numerous products and mergers unlike our young cosmos; he then states that the eternity of the initial energy would make what arises from it eternal. The proposed escape through inflation is said to restore an absolute beginning. This latter step is important for the agency investigation but must be treated as a local argument about a physical generative model, not silently generalized to every possible agent.
- **Hartle–Hawking, pp. 121–123.** Ameri distinguishes imaginary-time mathematical description from real time, and removal of a sharp singularity from removal of a beginning. P. 122 describes a smooth base rather than a first point; p. 123 nevertheless insists that the model has a beginning even without a singularity. The distinction is substantive: in his treatment, a beginning need not mean a singular point in the mathematical representation.

### 5. The motionless-eternal case, pp. 123–124

The heading marks a new objection rather than another active succession model. Ameri recalls time as the measure of existence between events; without motion of any kind there is no time. He then considers a cosmos eternally motionless that later moves.

His rational response is a causal dilemma: either the first change has a cause or it is causeless. The first alternative is said to require a nonmaterial entity beyond time and the inert cosmos, whose precedence is explicitly ontological; the second is rejected as an uncaused transition. His physical response invokes the need for energy/heat from outside. He explicitly distinguishes an active quantum vacuum from genuine immobility.

For T20, this proves that his causal language does not uniformly mean temporal succession of cause before effect. It does not, in these pages, establish whether an agent can have an eternal succession of distinct acts. The target is the initiation of movement in an otherwise motionless physical cosmos.

### 6. Recapitulation and last fallback, pp. 124–132

The nine-part conclusion claims scientific support for origination, the durability of the Big Bang, the failure and speculative character of alternatives, their lack of verified history/mechanism, and the design requirements of allegedly eternal models.

P. 126 states the crucial distinction explicitly: the dispute is generally not whether our cosmos developed from a Big Bang, but whether that event is the beginning of everything or a later stage of an eternal scenario. This is the strongest protection against attributing to Ameri a simple, unqualified inference from any Big Bang history to absolute beginning. He recognizes the gap and tries to close it with the later arguments, however forcefully the earlier exposition compresses the claims.

P. 129's design fallback is genuine: if the proposed eternal structures are carefully balanced enough to survive and function, that structure would require wise design on his account. This is compatible with denying that those structures actually exist. It is not a concession that past eternity has been established, or a proof that the designer must have a first act.

P. 131 frames two alternatives, an eternal/self-sufficient cosmos or a created one, but expressly acknowledges that eternity does not logically deny a creator for many ancient philosophers. It then identifies a conflict with the Islamic claim that everything in the world is created by God. The sentence juxtaposes eternity and self-sufficiency yet preserves their historical/conceptual non-equivalence; a summary should retain the parenthetical qualification. This unit does not analyze whether “each member is created” differs from “the succession has a first member.”

Pp. 131–132 resist deferral to unknown future science. The final reason is decisive for the chapter's architecture: the rational argument survives irrespective of scientific developments. Scientific reversibility is therefore not presented as a defeater of the underlying metaphysical conclusion.

## Relevance and limits for eternal successive activity

1. **Past infinity is the principal target.** The unit rejects beginningless physical sequences while allowing future-unending sequences in reported models (pp. 116, 123, 128). Do not report a blanket rejection of every kind of infinity.
2. **A particular cosmos and all reality are distinguished.** Pp. 120–121 and 126 show awareness that a locally young universe can be embedded in a purportedly older or eternal process. The further rejection of the latter is a distinct argumentative burden.
3. **Ontological and temporal precedence are distinguished.** P. 124 expressly uses ontological precedence for a cause beyond time.
4. **A beginning and a singular first point are not simply synonymous.** Pp. 121–123 treat the alleged elimination of a singularity as insufficient to eliminate a beginning.
5. **Eternal production versus eternal products is an unresolved transfer risk.** The movement on pp. 120–121 from eternal generative energy to eternal outcomes is relevant to questions of ever-renewed finite effects. Here it is embedded in a concrete vacuum-collision model. These pages do not establish a general analysis of beginningless voluntary agency or of the distinction between eternity of a kind and eternity of individual members.
6. **The chapter does not adjudicate all theistic eternal-succession positions.** It is directed at materialist cosmologies and occasionally ancient philosophical eternity. There is no explicit treatment in this range of Ibn Taymiyya, a beginningless succession of freely chosen divine acts, or a taxonomy separating those positions from an independently eternal material world. Absence here is a scope limit, not a whole-book claim.
7. **Design is a separate route.** The fallback of p. 129 cannot by itself answer whether divine action must start, whether the world is co-eternal, or whether only particular worlds are temporally originated.

## Textual and argumentative cautions

- **Printed wording anomaly, p. 130:** The boxed recap's clause `وكل أمل في إنكار الأزلية` literally concerns denying eternity, although the surrounding argument concerns attempted escape from a beginning and securing eternity. The render confirms the words occur in the PDF; this is not an extraction artifact. It is plausibly a local wording error. Do not silently emend it or treat it as a deliberate reversal of the chapter's position.
- **Polemical rhetoric is not additional evidence.** The chapter repeatedly attributes resistance to emotional/materialist motives. Record that as Ameri's assessment; the attributed motives have not been established by this reading.
- **Acknowledged limits coexist with categorical conclusions.** Pp. 127–129 discuss limits on access to pre-Planck conditions and missing mechanisms; elsewhere the chapter speaks categorically about all evidence and all alternatives. This is an important evidential tension, not a licence to rewrite the author as merely agnostic or as independently scientifically verified.
- **Citation layers matter.** Ross is a major source for the evidence inventory; Craig is expressly acknowledged for the alternative-model discussion on p. 114; several claims are cited through collections or personal reports rather than original experiments. BGV, Hawking–Penrose, Wall, Mithani–Vilenkin and other papers receive source references in the book, but none was independently read in this subtask.

## Compact formulation suitable for the larger reconstruction

Ameri presents modern cosmology as cumulative confirmation of an independently conclusive rational argument for the beginning of the material cosmos and time. He recognizes that a Big Bang beginning of our universe alone does not exclude an eternal wider process, and therefore adds attacks on past-eternal models, a separate treatment of an eternally motionless cosmos, and a design fallback. His distinctions between past and future infinity, local and absolute beginnings, ontological and temporal precedence, and beginning versus singularity must be retained. The cosmology unit itself does not expressly settle the distinct thesis of eternally successive voluntary divine acts. (Ameri, pp. 87, 104–124, 126, 129–132.)
