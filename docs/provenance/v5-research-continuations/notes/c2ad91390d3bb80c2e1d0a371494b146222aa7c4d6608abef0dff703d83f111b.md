## Exact binary weighted-Brier reduction
Let truth vertices be v0=(0,1), v1=(1,0); weights (theta,1-theta), 0<theta<1; incoherent x=(x1,x2) with delta=x1+x2-1≠0. A coherent action is c=(q,1-q). Define r_theta=x1-(1-theta)delta and k_theta=theta(1-theta).

Then L_theta(c,v_i)=(q-i)^2 and L_theta(x,v_i)=(r_theta-i)^2+k_theta delta^2 for i=0,1. Therefore the exact set of all-state weakly improving coherent repairs is

I_theta(x)=[1-sqrt((1-r_theta)^2+k_theta delta^2), sqrt(r_theta^2+k_theta delta^2)] intersect [0,1].

When r_theta is in [a,1-a] for a>0, any q in this interval obeys |q-r_theta|≤k_theta delta^2/(2a). The repair decodes theta through theta_hat=(q-x1+delta)/delta, hence |theta_hat-theta|≤k_theta |delta|/(2a)≤|delta|/(8a).

Thus small-disturbance all-state repair is a statistically demanding identification task, even though the output is only one coherent credence. This concerns performance under a supplied normative family, not independent justification of that family.

## Candidate information theorem
For theta0,theta1 whose admissible repair intervals are disjoint, any algorithm choosing q from a transcript produces a hypothesis test on the two parameters. Its worst-case repair-failure probability is at least (1-TV(P0,P1))/2, where P_i are full transcript laws including observable provenance. For mutually absolutely continuous adaptive observation kernels, KL chain-rule bounds use only fresh informative observations; copied data adds zero conditional information.

With Bernoulli(theta) observation kernels and theta in a fixed interior interval, choosing |theta1-theta0|=O(|delta|) yields a local-minimax obstruction on the order exp(-C N delta^2) for N fresh samples. Precise constants and quantifiers remain to verify. This is not yet a pointwise lower bound for every fixed theta.

## Candidate persistent-restoration theorem
Given deterministic nonzero disturbances delta_t→0, eventual all-state dominance implies that decoded theta_hat_t→theta. If all informative probes violate the target and all nonprobe observations are parameter-independent, persistent restoration forces only finitely many informative probes. Under a non-atomic prior with full-support finite-history likelihoods, a finite-information posterior remains non-atomic; a parameter-blind randomised output sequence cannot identify theta exactly. Need a proof that does not incorrectly treat the last probe as a stopping time.
