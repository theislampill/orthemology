# The particular-explanans bridge

## Result and scope

**Read as the explicitly displayed relational and classificatory axioms, the restricted package does not, by itself, entail a supernatural fact.** Its externality condition excludes an explainer that is a member or part of a member of its target plurality. It does not exclude a target member being part of the explainer. A three-fact relational control preserves every listed restricted explanatory principle while exploiting precisely that unexcluded direction.

**The exact repair is substantive but small:** exclude basic natural facts from being proper parts of their own complete explainers, or require the chosen explainer to contain no basic-natural target member. Either addition yields the supernatural conclusion, provided an actual wholly particular/basic natural target exists and the total basic-natural plurality is available. Ordinary part-minimality of full explainers does not suffice.

This is explicit-axiom independence, not a metaphysically adequate counterexample. No claim is made that a mixed conjunction containing its explanandum genuinely explains that explanandum. The control does not establish the possibility of a natural world satisfying the authors' intended explanatory relation, refute their supernatural conclusion, or settle its epistemic warrant.

## 1. What the text actually supplies

Let E(y,S) mean complete explanation of a nonempty plurality S, and let p≤y be inclusive fact-parthood. The source's partial explanation is PE(p,S) iff some z has p≤z and E(z,S) (L88–92). Its separately named partially self-explanatory condition uses **proper** parthood: p is a proper part of a fact explaining p (L61–63). These must not be confused with p explaining a part of its explanandum.

Write P(p) for wholly particular and H(p) for supernatural. The basic-natural predicate is B(p) iff P(p) and not H(p) (L163–167). Universal-containing mixed facts can therefore be non-basic without being supernatural.

The target principles are:

- Restricted PSR (L168–170): a nonempty plurality of B-facts has a complete explainer y with y≠x and not y≤x for every target member x.
- Particular-constituent requirement (L171–173): explanations of particular facts include a particular fact. The audit grants the stronger useful reading that the constituent is **wholly** particular; weakening that reading would not rescue the bridge.
- No basic full self-explanation (L148–158).
- Both complete and partial transitivity clauses (L95–97).
- Distributivity to members and their parts (L132–133).
- No Extended Circles (L93–94): PE(x,S), y∈S, y≠x and not y≤x entail not PE(y,{x}).

No displayed principle in these passages bans basic proper-part self-explanation. The opening expressly allows partially self-explanatory facts in general. Nor does No Extended Circles ban them: identity and target-part-of-partial-explainer cases are excluded from its antecedent.

The later epistemic formulation repeats the same externality direction at L304–305. Its L327–328 restatement is weaker still, excluding only membership. Neither passage supplies the missing reverse-parthood exclusion.

## 2. The exact failed inference

Assume B is nonempty and the total plurality N of all B-facts exists. Restricted PSR gives E(y,N) and an explainer outside N. Hence y is **not basic natural**. This part of the reasoning is valid.

To infer a supernatural fact, use an actual member b₀ of N. Distributivity gives E(y,{b₀}); the particular-constituent condition supplies a wholly particular p≤y. This singleton step avoids applying a condition for entirely particular targets to an arbitrary mixed target plurality.

If p is not supernatural, the definition makes p basic natural, so p∈N. Distributivity then gives E(y,{p}). Restricted PSR excludes y≤p and y=p, but we already have p≤y. Those facts are compatible: p is a proper part of its complete explainer y.

The proposed contradiction is therefore unavailable. The inference would need a reason why p cannot be a target member **contained in** y. Non-basic y alone is insufficient because y may contain universal generalizations alongside p.

Nonemptiness matters. An entirely universal fact-domain can make the restricted PSR and particular-target condition vacuous. The positive arguments presuppose at least one actual wholly particular fact; if that fact is already supernatural, the existence conclusion is immediate, and otherwise it supplies b₀.

## 3. Three-fact control

There are exactly three obtaining fact-labels:

- b: wholly particular, basic natural;
- u: universal, not wholly particular;
- c: contains b and u, hence not wholly particular.

No fact is supernatural. Parthood is reflexive plus b≤c and u≤c. The atoms b and u are disjoint. Admit all seven nonempty subsets as pluralities, including the total basic-natural plurality {b}.

The only complete explanation is E(c,{b}). An optional extension adds E(u,{u}); both versions are checked. Partial explanation is derived from parthood and E, never assigned independently.

### Verification of each premise

1. **Restricted PSR:** the only nonempty all-basic plurality is {b}. Its explainer c is neither b nor a part of b.
2. **Particular constituent:** the only explained target containing a particular fact is {b}. Its explainer c contains the wholly particular b.
3. **No basic full self-explanation:** E(b,{b}) is false.
4. **Partial self-explanation:** PE(b,{b}) is true because b≤c and E(c,{b}); moreover b≠c. This is the precise feature the displayed package permits.
5. **Complete transitivity:** for E(c,{b}), the intermediate b completely explains nothing, so no further edge is required. In the optional extension, the u-to-u composition reproduces E(u,{u}).
6. **Partial transitivity:** the same intermediate-target check suffices. All partial explainers of {b} are b, u and c; b has no complete outgoing edge. The optional u case closes on itself.
7. **Distributivity:** the only member and only part of a member of {b} is b. For optional {u}, it is u. The required singleton edge is already present.
8. **No Extended Circles:** for target {b}, x=b is exempt by identity, and x=c is exempt because b≤c. For x=u, its antecedent holds, but the forbidden reverse PE(b,{u}) is indeed absent. The optional u self-edge introduces only an identity-exempt case.
9. **Mereology:** parthood is reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric. It also satisfies strong supplementation and fusion for every nonempty plurality. This is the three nonzero elements of a two-atom Boolean mereology, not a pathological nonsupplemented chain.
10. **Part-minimal full explanation:** no proper part of a full explainer completely explains the same target. Thus adding this ordinary minimality condition still leaves the control intact.

The control fails unrestricted PSR: for example {c} has no complete explainer. It is therefore not a counterinterpretation of a package that silently retains unrestricted PSR.

## 4. What the control does and does not interpret

The source's L144–147 explains a **being's** inclusion in a fact by relevant entailment of its existence. It does not supply a fully axiomatized fact mereology or a semantics for the complete explanation relation. The finite check models fact-parthood structurally; it does not silently equate entity inclusion with fact-parthood.

The reverse-parthood arrangement has a familiar syntactic illustration: a conjunction b∧u contains both conjuncts while neither conjunct contains the conjunction. Conjunction elimination in the cited kinds of logic does not itself prohibit that arrangement. This illustration is not a complete model of relevance entailment, all actual truths, or the authors' ontology of concrete beings.

Most importantly, E(c,{b}) is a stipulated edge. Calling c a mixed fact does not show that it removes the mystery of b, has an appropriate causal role, or meets the authors' intended standard of explanation (compare L75–87). The source's semantic motivations might prohibit the edge, but the prohibition must do explanatory work beyond the enumerated relational axioms.

“Relevance” must accordingly be specified. Relevance of a being's existence to a containing fact is not already a prohibition on explanandum-containing explainers. An explanation-specific adequacy principle excluding such circular dependence would close the gap. Part-minimality alone demonstrably does not. Nothing here proves that an adequate explanation can be manufactured by padding a fact with a universal generalization.

## 5. Exact restricted repairs

These are sufficient repairs, not proved necessary, unique, or weakest; the positive repair theorems assume a wholly particular constituent, whereas the source’s phrase “particular fact” is less explicit.

### Repair A: exclude basic proper-part self-explanation

Add: no basic natural p is a proper part of a fact that completely explains p. This is exactly the source's proper-part self-explanation definition, now prohibited for the basic-natural class.

Proof: obtain E(y,N) and a wholly particular p≤y as in §2. If p were not supernatural, p would belong to N. Distributivity gives E(y,{p}), and the original restricted PSR already gives y≠p. Thus p is a proper part of a complete explainer of itself, contradicting the added prohibition. Therefore p is supernatural. The source's definition then supplies the existence of the associated concrete supernatural being.

Only member projection is needed from distributivity. Neither transitivity nor No Extended Circles does additional work in this repair. It is unnecessary to forbid partial self-explanation of every fact, or to impose a universal disjointness condition.

### Repair B: strengthen the chosen explainer's externality

Require additionally that no member p of N is a part of the selected explainer y. Given a wholly particular p≤y, p cannot be basic natural, so p is supernatural. This repair does not need distributivity after the particular constituent has been obtained.

Disjointness of y from every target member is a sufficient stronger condition. Simply reversing the displayed parthood direction would change the principle rather than interpret an equivalence; adding the reverse condition is also a substantive strengthening. Either repair needs its own justification if used in the epistemic route.

## 6. What retaining unrestricted PSR changes

The source's footnote at L108–110 warns that theorems use earlier displayed axioms. That creates a reading worth checking, but the later abstract, summary, and epistemic development expressly target restricted basic-natural PSR. Unrestricted PSR is introduced as a stronger comparison, and the L129–139 route is a conditional derivation for a specially defined ordinary class. It should not be imported without notice into the later advertised restricted result.

If nevertheless unrestricted PSR and comprehension of all actual facts are retained, there is a valid stronger route. Let F be the plurality of all actual facts and let E(Q,F). From an actual wholly particular target b₀, distributivity and particularity yield a wholly particular p≤Q. Suppose p≠Q. Antisymmetry gives not Q≤p. But:

- PE(p,F), since p≤Q and E(Q,F);
- Q∈F;
- PE(Q,{p}), since distributivity gives E(Q,{p}) and Q≤Q.

These contradict No Extended Circles with x=p and target member Q. Hence p=Q, so Q is wholly particular. Distributivity gives E(Q,{Q}). If Q were not supernatural, it would be a basic full self-explainer, contrary to the source's prohibition. Hence Q is supernatural.

This proof requires no transitivity, but it does require the unrestricted total-explainer premise absent from the finite control. It is valid conditional reasoning, not a rescue of an argument advertised to require only restricted PSR and the weaker basic-natural comprehension. The proof also makes the initial actual particular fact explicit.

## 7. Formal evidence

`RestrictedBridgeControl.lean` encodes the three facts as Fin 3 and all subsets as bit masks in Fin 8. Zero is the empty subset and is excluded where the PSR requires a nonempty plurality. The singleton encoding and nonempty/member equivalence are separately checked. All stated finite checks quantify over every fact, plurality and Boolean choice of the optional universal self-edge. This is verification of one proposed control and its stated harmless extension, not a search over arbitrary models.

The file uses Lean 4.19.0, `import Std`, and kernel-reduced `decide`, with no Mathlib, installed dependencies, `native_decide`, admitted proof or new axiom. Printed dependencies are only Lean's standard propositional extensionality. The mereological strengthening and part-minimality checks are included.

`BridgeRepairs.lean` kernel-checks the two local repair proofs and the stronger unrestricted route. Its local hypotheses separate the supply of an explainer and of a particular constituent from the subsequent inference. The prose above explains how restricted/unrestricted PSR, nonemptiness, singleton projection and the particularity premise supply those hypotheses. Standard classical Lean dependencies are printed; no additional axiom or `sorryAx` is present.

Formal validity is relative to these predicates. It neither validates the stipulated explanation edge as metaphysically genuine nor verifies the truth of the positive repair premises.

## Retained judgment

Credit the authors with a valid move from restricted totality explanation to an explainer outside the basic-natural class, and with explicitly identifying the need for a particular constituent. The remaining source-relative gap is that an outside mixed explainer may contain an inside particular constituent. Exact displayed axioms leave that possibility open; basic proper-part noncircularity closes it. The epistemic defense must justify the strengthened principle or explain why adequate explanation already satisfies it.
