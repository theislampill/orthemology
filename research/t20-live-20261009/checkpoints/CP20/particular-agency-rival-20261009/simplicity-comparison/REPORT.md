# Purposive creation without a distinct intrinsic intention

9 October 2026 UTC. Independent bounded comparison for the continuing T20 inquiry.

## Result

**O’Connor supplies a genuine original-scope rival to the requirement of a distinct intrinsic executive intention for each possible created order. His proposal includes actual personal causal exercise and rational purpose. It is not merely a renamed nonpersonal cause or a denial of all divine activity.** The stronger claim that the proposal establishes adequate complete particular determination does not follow.

His argument has substantial force against inserting an otherwise unexplained intention between agent and effect. It has less force against the strongest Ibn Taymiyyan account, which requires an adequate particular basis for the intrinsic willing itself and permits fulfilled intrinsic succession. Moving from that account to O’Connor’s comparison changes an explanatory premise, not just the location of one node.

The bounded conclusion is therefore two-sided. A source defender cannot establish the indispensability of intrinsic particular willing merely by repeating its preferred ontology or by citing the role of executive intentions in embodied action. A rival cannot establish the dispensability of every intrinsic particular determination merely by showing that an ungrounded intention-token would leave an explanatory question unanswered.

## Source synopsis

O’Connor challenges one objection to simplicity without adopting simplicity. His “naive” model has God freely cause a reason-bearing intrinsic intention, which produces creation. The explanation need not assume reasons necessitate the act or make it probable. The modification offered for a simplicity theorist removes that executive intermediary: the agent directly produces an order fitting an apprehended purpose. Contingency belongs to the agent–creation relation while God remains intrinsically invariant across possible worlds. His parity argument asks why direct agent-to-world production is mysterious if direct agent-to-intention production is acceptable. He locates agency in exercising causal power; bodily guidance explains a familiar use of separate intentions. Willing this world becomes purposive creation rather than a distinct intrinsic state. Agent causation’s coherence is assumed. The compatibility of rich reasons with simplicity remains unresolved. He inclines toward necessary creation of something, while leaving the independence question unfinished. Complete divine self-knowledge is not developed into a full model. See pp. 405–412, especially 407–410.

Timothy O’Connor, “Simplicity and Creation,” *Faith and Philosophy* 16(3), 1999, 405–412. [Author’s paper](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OConnor_SimplicityCreation99.pdf); [publisher record](https://place.asburyseminary.edu/faithandphilosophy/vol16/iss3/8/); DOI 10.5840/faithphil199916339. The synopsis is under 200 words. The analysis below is this review’s comparative assessment.

## What the proposal successfully puts in question

The relevant target is an alleged universal entailment: rationally purposive production requires a separate content-bearing executive state intrinsic to the producer. A proposed counterexample can challenge that entailment without accepting every element of the source’s alternative ontology. Requiring it to preserve the disputed entailment would prevent the disagreement from being heard.

Conversely, a substantive alternative is not a countermodel preserving an ontology it deliberately revises. The proposal retains a distinction between producer and dependent product, and between power and actual exercise. It does **not** thereby retain the source’s distinction between a particular intrinsic divine act and its external effect. A real causal relation can differ while its source has no intrinsic difference, on the proposal’s own account. Calling that relation an exercise does not secretly reintroduce the missing intrinsic act.

Cross-world intrinsic invariance must also be distinguished from temporal inactivity. The proposal denies neither that the world is actually produced nor that production belongs to the agent. It changes where variation in the productive situation is located. Criticism must address the adequacy of that location, rather than treating unchanging intrinsic nature as equivalent to doing nothing.

This makes the rival relevant to the existing practical-perfection argument in a different way from a blind producer. An agent alleged to understand and produce for fitting reasons already claims the excellence of practical self-governance. The remaining questions concern the realization and explanatory adequacy of that claim. The familiar comparison between informed action and useful but uncomprehending output does not, without a further argument, decide between these two purposive accounts.

## The exact force of the middleman argument

Within the stipulated indeterministic agent-causal framework, an executive intention cannot be the ultimate explanation of agency merely because it is an internal state. Its own occurrence must already be attributable to the agent. If immediate agent production of that state is intelligible, demanding another prior intention to authenticate that production threatens either repetition or an unargued privilege for one kind of effect.

This is a strong objection to the following insufficient answer: “The world is selected because the agent forms the intention to select it,” where nothing further is supplied about that intention’s particular occurrence. Adding a description of what was selected does not explain the selection. The source’s own refusal to exempt divine willing from adequate determination supports that diagnosis.

The comparison does not establish three stronger conclusions:

1. That an intrinsic commitment could never contribute an indispensable practical or productive condition.
2. That every demand for an explanation of particular selection is answered by locating its production in a substance rather than in a prior event.
3. That all ontologies of intrinsic willing reduce to an indeterministic production of an otherwise unexplained intention.

The exact difference from the strongest source reconstruction is the third point. O’Connor’s parity comparison keeps the pre-product intrinsic agent condition fixed while comparing an intention as immediate product with an external order as immediate product. The source reconstruction does not take complete relevant intrinsic conditions to remain indifferent. Its particular act has adequate conditions, potentially including prior internally originated acts. An intention under that description carries more explanatory structure than the bare intermediary under attack.

This observation does not itself vindicate those conditions. If a defender merely appends “adequately grounded” to the intention while providing no reason to believe in that grounding, the rival’s challenge remains. But the existence and sufficiency of such intrinsic conditions must be assessed; their explanatory role cannot be deleted by a parity argument that has abstracted them away. Nor does a structural account of permissible succession exhibit the entire actual divine history.

## A source-faithful response and its limits

### Particular differentiation

[Majmūʿ, item 805](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/805/), retained L311–312, supplies the crucial authorial principle. The criticized position exempts voluntary divine specification from the complete-determiner demand it employs elsewhere. Its force is not that all production must be mechanical or externally coerced. Its force is that actual specification requires an adequate basis for this specification, rather than only a standing capacity equally related to alternatives.

The substantive argument against the rival is thus: underived personal ownership answers **whose production this is**; fit with a known good answers **what makes it rationally eligible**; neither description automatically answers **why this particular exercise occurs**. If the complete relevant basis remains nonselective, calling the exercise personal does not show that the stronger explanatory demand has been met.

That principle is philosophically contestable. An agent-causal theorist may reject the demand for a necessitating particular differentiator while preserving causation and rational explanation. The source should not classify that disagreement as absence of all causation by definition. Equally, asserting that personal production is basic does not by itself give an argument that it satisfies the source’s stronger criterion. The disagreement concerns how far adequate explanation must go.

### Actual commitment and informed agency

The retained [Shifāʾ e176–177 discussion](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/shfa-alalil-002.html) gives an intelligible reason to distinguish knowledge of benefit and capacity from actual willing: a person can possess the first two and refrain because of another concern. [Akmaliyya](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/591/), canonical L235–240, adds a positive comparison: unimpeded efficacy joined to informed wise discrimination is more excellent than undirected efficacy or externally supplied control.

These considerations support actual practical commitment. They do not by themselves prove that every possible divine practical commitment must be a separate internal executive representation. The rival can agree that merely knowing an outcome is good and possessing power do not produce it, then identify commitment with directly producing it for the good apprehended. Rejecting that answer requires more than repeating the human example.

The strongest remaining objection concerns **basing**. An effect can fit a reason without having been produced for that reason. Preserving common reason–effect content does not by itself preserve the reason-reference role assigned to intention in the initial model (pp. 407–409). If the result is useful in several respects, its useful structure alone need not identify the consideration for which the agent acted. What makes the particular causal exercise guided by this understood end, rather than an exercise whose outcome happens to satisfy it? An explicit intrinsic commitment is one possible answer; a primitive or otherwise grounded reason-directed exercise is a proposed alternative. Establishing that the latter is inadequate requires an argument about practical explanation, not an assumption that bodily control machinery is necessary for divinity.

This is a pressure point rather than a demonstrated incoherence. The proposal claims genuine purpose-directed exercise, not just accidental conformity. It would be unfair to remove that claim and refute the resulting weaker model. The burden is to give the claimed reason-directed relation sufficient positive content. The source faces the corresponding burden when its own particular intrinsic determination is asserted but not fully articulated.

### Succession and regress

[Darʾ, subsection 016](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/dr-taardh-alaql-walnql-016.html), retained L0112–0159, prevents a false dilemma between a primitive unexplained intention and an endlessly deferred explanation of the same act. Its comparison distinguishes fulfilled successive acts from failure ever to complete the very agency or one particular effect. L0145–0148 supplies the relevant alternative to wholly unchanged specification: particular intrinsic volitions and acts can matter to different productive occasions.

This gives the source a way to resist the claim that every requirement of intrinsic particular conditions necessarily starts a vicious sequence of intending to intend. It does not prove that any proposed infinite chain is acceptable, that a first particular divine act needs an external donor, or that one complete history has been uniquely derived.

The Shifāʾ e175 created-subject regress argument expressly relies on the subject’s beginning to exist. That premise cannot be carried over automatically to an everlasting original. Its e178 general-will discussion also blocks requiring a separate divine micro-intention for every created motion. Neither concession eliminates intrinsic willing altogether or licenses unexplained particular specification.

## Limits that matter to this comparison

**Agent causation remains a substantive assumption.** The bounded result is a conditional challenge to an objection against simplicity. It is not an independent demonstration that a causally basic personal substance can exercise the requisite power without further determining conditions. A defender should engage that causal proposal at its own level, rather than treating nonnecessitation as a proof of irrationality.

**The rich-simple nature remains unestablished.** A rival needs enough cognitive and practical structure to distinguish apprehended ends, but simplicity constrains how that structure can be intrinsic. This is a real unresolved integration problem. It is not a license to say that the author has adopted a featureless divine scalar or proved the impossibility of differentiated attributes.

**Necessary creation of something is weaker than necessary creation of this order.** Even if the former were granted, it would not select the latter. Nor would it by itself establish the source’s stronger account of perpetual fulfilled activity. The paper’s treatment of noncreation therefore cannot be used as proof of unrestricted modal alternatives or as proof that the actual total order is necessary.

**Complete divine self-knowledge requires a further integration.** Knowing which production is actual must be included in a complete account. One cannot simply assert that different known contingent truths entail different intrinsic mental states; that would assume a contested theory of divine cognition. Neither can one use complete factive self-knowledge as a selector by importing the actual effect into its content. Awareness of the actual relation and production for a reason are distinct explanatory claims. This paper does not supply the full reconciliation, and its omission is not a demonstrated contradiction.

## Bounded judgement

The rival makes a genuine advance over an empty “free choice” coordinate: it locates ownership in actual agent production and contests an alleged necessity of a distinct executive intermediary at original scope. The most persuasive outcome is a **burden correction**. Intrinsic willing needs an argument for its explanatory role; its name and its location inside the agent are insufficient.

The middleman argument does not overturn the strongest source account. Its direct parity holds between two stipulated indeterministic agent-causal models, not automatically between one of those models and intrinsic wise production with adequate particular conditions. The source’s positive reason for those conditions is its complete-determiner principle, reinforced by the distinction between standing ability and actual production and by its permitted account of fulfilled succession. That reason retains force, but the inspected materials do not display a complete determination of every actual divine choice.

The remaining discriminator is therefore precise: **can actual production for an apprehended reason be adequately explained by a basic reason-directed agent–effect exercise while the producer has no distinct intrinsic particular determination, or does adequate original production require such determination?** The paper makes the first proposal intelligible enough to challenge a cheap dismissal; it does not certify its complete metaphysical adequacy. The source gives substantive reasons for the second; the present comparison neither turns those reasons into a definition nor settles them by a new formal consistency test.
