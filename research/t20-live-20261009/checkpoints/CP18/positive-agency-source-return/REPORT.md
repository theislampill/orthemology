# Positive agency: a bounded return to four proposed locators

9 October 2026. Source comparison only. This does not integrate or close T20, certify research duration, or revise a confidence figure.

## Result

**Darʾ 10:141 is relevant, but its useful argument is the connected discussion at 10:141–146. It gives a positive, conditional bridge between knowing production and willing, not a new proof that an otherwise noncognitive original possesses knowledge.** The author expressly says that self-knowledge has already been established for the discussion at hand. His reply therefore cannot be substituted for that prior establishment.

The return also corrects a potentially consequential attribution: the argument at 10:141–143 from knowledge of opposite alternatives to an additional selecting attribute is **Ibn Rushd quoted by Ibn Taymiyya**. The latter's own substantive assessment follows. It would be mistaken to cite the whole passage indiscriminately as Ibn Taymiyya speaking, or to treat Ibn Rushd's opening reference to knowledge and wisdom as a proof of those attributes from sourcehood alone.

The other candidate locators add no new positive-agency warrant in this bounded check. Darʾ 7:219 concerns inference from observed origination; 8:116 concerns the evidential superiority of particular origination/specification over a disputed modal detour. Jāmiʿ VI:199 is the conclusion of an already studied final-causation treatise. Its supporting argument is on the preceding pages, not a newly discovered ascent on p. 199.

**Assessment:** the existing affirmative case retains its cumulative force from intention, proficient production, pure perfection and appropriately qualified giver-priority. This return sharpens its inferential dependencies and prevents a false new-proof claim. It does not independently resolve the strongest structured nonpersonal-original proposal or supply a new wisdom theorem.

## 2. The four locators

### Darʾ 7:219: observed origination, not the requested ascent

The page is Ibn Taymiyya's own commentary after quoted material. Its relevant wording is: “فحدوث الإنسان يستدل به على المحدث” and “والفرق بين الاستدلال بحدوثه، والاستدلال على حدوثه بين”. He differentiates using the witnessed origination of humans and other particular things to infer an originator from first proving those things originated by an accidents argument and the finitude of past events. The page explicitly makes the latter route unnecessary.

This supports the user's temporal safeguard; it does not establish knowing, willing or wise agency simply by identifying an originator. Only this candidate page was newly print-locator-checked. Adjacent Shamela requests did not return their bodies, so no continuous 7:218–221 reading is claimed. [Electronic print locator](https://shamela.ws/book/21506/2504).

### Darʾ 8:116: particularizer and the asymmetric need of existence

The opening finishes a quotation from al-Rāzī. Ibn Taymiyya's response begins “قلت”. He says taking a detour from origination through possibility is longer and weaker, then challenges that route as formulated. The positive comparison is that specification of one of two similar times is more evident than the unrestricted claim that neither existence nor absence obtains without a determiner. His next continuous paragraphs distinguish the absence of a productive cause as an indicator/entailment of nonexistence from a positive efficient production of absence.

This is not a rejection of every contingency argument, nor an identification of every particularizer with conscious choice. No step here independently converts particular causal differentiation into cognition. See [8:116](https://shamela.ws/book/21506/2863) and the bounded retained context `sources/dar8-116-bounded-context.txt`.

### Darʾ 10:141–146: the selected positive argument

The voices and steps matter:

1. **10:140–141, Ibn Taymiyya:** he credits what he regards as Ibn Sīnā's sound argument for knowledge of effects from self-knowledge, while disputing the claim that philosophers uniformly deny divine will. He praises partial truth rather than treating an opponent's other errors as invalidating every argument.
2. **10:141–143, quoted Ibn Rushd:** knowledge concerns opposed alternatives; actual production of one requires something beyond knowledge considered merely as equally concerning both. At 10:142: “فصدور أحد الضدين عنه، يدل على صفة زائدة على العلم، وهي الإرادة”. The quotation also rejects modelling divine willing as needy human acquisition and presents its own view of the relation between knowledge, will and necessity.
3. **10:143, Ibn Taymiyya's reply:** he rejects attributing this view to all philosophers or all Peripatetics. It belongs to some; he distinguishes Abū al-Barakāt, Ibn Rushd and Ibn Sīnā. Agreement that some affirm knowledge and will is not blanket endorsement of all wording in Ibn Rushd's account.
4. **10:144–145, Ibn Taymiyya:** voluntary action entails knowing the action. He then invokes the stronger exclusion of knowledge from the relevant nonvoluntary/natural action, and concludes a mutual implication of knowledge and will in the action. He defends a sound knowledge argument even where its proponent fails to accept the will he takes it to imply.
5. **10:145–146, Ibn Taymiyya:** an argument can disclose something before a learner explicitly knows its necessary conditions; the order of a learner's discovery is not the order of ontological dependence. His decisive limitation is explicit at 10:146: “والكلام في هذا المقام بعد تقرير أنه عالم نفسه” and “وأما تقرير كونه عالماً بنفسه، فهذا مذكور في موضعه”. The proof of self-knowledge is not furnished merely by this paragraph.

The immediately preceding electronic context also distinguishes the mutual implication of self-knowledge and knowledge of effects from one piece of divine knowledge efficiently producing the other. Neither divine inference from a previously unknown datum nor an external completion of knowledge is required. In particular, the source is not positing separately subsisting epistemic parts to repair the source's ignorance.

### Jāmiʿ VI:197–199: a useful mapping, not new positive credit

Printed 199 says the just-completed principle helps establish God as worshipped for himself and as intrinsically loved ultimate end. It is a short conclusion. The preceding printed 197–198 argues that an end's suitability to be sought cannot be manufactured solely by the very intention it is to explain; pure absence cannot provide differentiating intentional content. It distinguishes a prior desired object from subsequent attainment, illustrated by Mecca versus arrival and rites.

This is a substantive final-cause argument, already represented in the prior research with its limitations. It begins inside an account of intending agents and concerns the ultimate object of purposive action. It does not supply a fresh passage deriving the original's cognition from an independently noncognitive productive source. At p. 197 the editor marks a one-word manuscript lacuna; it is not silently repaired here. Printed/PDF 200 is blank. The scan images 197–200 were all viewed.

## 3. What the selected argument establishes, and what it needs

### Positive contribution

The passage prevents treating divine will as an arbitrary label pasted onto an already established intellect. Knowledge of alternatives, considered only as knowledge, does not yet explain the producer's preference for this opposed realization. The source thus distinguishes cognitive presentation from practical determination. Its further argument connects a source's complete self-knowledge with knowing its own productive acts and effects, and refuses the proposal of a knowing producer wholly devoid of willing.

It also explicitly permits discovering one attribute before articulating the others. A defender need not arrange every valid proof in one compulsory chronological sequence. Nor does beginningless activity make a source unknowingly natural by definition. This contextual point corroborates the established temporal safeguards; it is not a newly discovered timeless-cause refutation.

### Formal limit, without declaring a textual error

Let W mean the relevant voluntary action and K the relevant knowing action. W implies K validates its contrapositive: absence of K implies absence of W. **It does not alone validate K implies W.** The stronger claim that non-W entails non-K supplies that converse. The source explicitly employs the stronger claim; it must be stated and assessed, not smuggled in as mere contraposition of the first implication.

There is a further semantic restriction. Mere propositional awareness that one's body is undergoing an involuntary process does not make that process voluntary. A defensible application therefore needs the relevant relation between cognition and original production, or an independently warranted exclusion of involuntary processes in the original. Defining K as already intentional production makes the implication easier but cannot then prove intentionality from bare sourcehood.

Even granting the source's W/K equivalence and its complete-self-knowledge-to-effects inference leaves the antecedent question: why does the actual original have complete self-knowledge? The text announces that as a prior result. A thin formal interpretation with an original and differentiated effects but with both K and W false satisfies the equivalence. This is only a logical non-entailment diagnostic. It is not a metaphysically possible original, a causal ontology, or a countermodel to the full pure-perfection/giver case.

### The strongest relevant rival

The live alternative should be an actual, intrinsically structured nonpersonal productive reality, with its own powers or obtaining qualitative organization and an account of changing states/effects. Its differentiation need not be an externally appended selector applied to a featureless scalar. It may contest whether original sufficiency requires cognitive or practical perfections; it may also contest the stronger complete-determiner demand. Those are distinct disputes.

The selected passage does not rule out this alternative merely because it is nonpersonal. The Ibn Rushd argument begins with cognition of alternatives; the Taymiyyan reply's self-knowledge route begins with self-knowledge. Conversely, the rival gains no independent warrant simply by being describable, labelling its structure intrinsic, or denying the perfection comparison. It still owes an account of actual original adequacy, the status of its determining structure and why its proposed noncognitive ontology deserves belief. The existing positive cumulative case may therefore retain defeasible evidential preference without these pages yielding a deduction from nonreceipt alone.

## 4. Wisdom and the stopping decision

No selected passage newly derives wise purpose from bare differentiation. Ibn Rushd's mention of wisdom is part of the quoted starting characterization. The nearby return to the perfection of knowledge and the claim that freedom from outside completion does not mean an attribute-free essence confirms arguments already represented by T17, T19 and the recent T20 scope return. It earns corroboration, not another independent proof count.

Accordingly, stop this secondary source test here. There is no reason to begin another general attribute campaign merely because four new page numbers were proposed. The narrow useful increment is the authenticated conditional bridge, the speaker correction, the self-knowledge dependency, and the precise formal distinction between a one-way condition and the stronger biconditional used in the source. The earlier affirmative appraisal and its remaining eligibility dispute both survive.
