## Final disposition after author revisions

**Accept the revised affirmative appraisal. No further substantive correction is required.** The author has explicitly named the modal bridge as the affirmed foundational judgment, allowed further intrinsic content to repair an incomplete account, distinguished incomplete description from incomplete reality, and limited the exercise argument's claim against nonproductive explanations. Those revisions address the concerns below. The optional local wording and effects-possibility refinement remain improvements in precision, not barriers to the conclusion.

The revision also correctly states that §4 tests a proposed transfer from an explanation of effects; it does not attribute efficient self-production to the strongest brute rival or a modern source. The new contribution is the explicit bearer-presupposition reason and affirmative appraisal. The underlying distinction of explanatory targets is inherited.

## Verdict

**Retain the affirmative philosophical assessment.** The report supplies a real clarification of the positive argument and a valid restricted answer to the nonnecessitating agent-act analogy. It does not manufacture an instance from a definition, swap original bearers across alternatives, or entail necessitation of every effect. Assent need not be suspended because its foundational judgment cannot be derived from weaker premises accepted by every skeptic.

The important limit is internal to that assessment: the exercise discriminator supports the foundational judgment; it does not independently derive its modal strength. The initial draft generally acknowledged this, and the revisions now make the allocation explicit.

## Initial findings and their disposition

Line numbers below refer to the preserved initially reviewed draft. The author's later wording need not duplicate the proposed wording verbatim.

### 1. Name the modal reach of the affirmed foundation

**Resolved in revised §4:** the modal bridge is explicitly affirmed as a substantive rational judgment, and its force is not attributed to anti-self-production alone.

At REPORT.md lines 11 and 92–97, the formula “an insufficient reality cannot have its deficiency discharged by bare actuality” is weaker than the judgment required at line 95 unless “insufficient” already includes the relevant existential openness. Ordinary adequate explanations can be nonnecessitating; the report itself correctly permits that. Eliminating a bearer-presupposing exercise does not by itself eliminate every nonproductive, nonnecessitating account.

Add, near the initial foundational statement:

> The affirmed foundational judgment includes the claim that complete intrinsic adequacy for this bearer's own existence excludes a genuinely open possibility of its nonbeing. The exercise and conditional-instantiation analyses clarify and support that judgment; they do not obtain its modal force solely by eliminating circular production.

This is an allocation of warrant, not a demand for a new neutral proof or a retreat to suspension. The source itself makes that stronger judgment explicit at L184. The report's reasons for accepting it remain the distinction between possibility and actuality, completion of the actual existential target, and a principled self-sufficient terminus. Calling this bridge foundational should neither conceal its strength nor erase its philosophical weight.

### 2. Limit the exercise discriminator to the argument it actually defeats

**Substantively resolved in revised §4:** the author explicitly leaves a defended nonproductive intrinsic proposal to assessment at the foundational judgment. The following additional sentence near the bearer-presupposition argument would be useful but is no longer necessary.

Lines 67–71 successfully defeat the proposed explanation in which an operation of an existentially available g supplies g's entire original actuality. The explanation would borrow the bearer it purports wholly to supply. Timelessness does not repair that explanatory order. This is the report's clearest substantive strengthening.

However, “an exercise belongs to an actual bearer” alone establishes neither productive priority nor a general ban on constitutive accounts. An account identifying a bearer with its concrete activity does not automatically postulate an already subsisting subject that then produces itself. Whether that account is intrinsically existentially adequate remains to be shown; merely naming it does not defeat the affirmative judgment.

Add after line 69:

> The defect concerns an exercise invoked as productive provision by its bearer. It is not inferred merely from co-occurrence or from the claim that an activity constitutes a bearer. A nonproductive constitutive proposal must independently establish complete adequacy for the bearer's obtaining; the agent-act analogy does not establish that adequacy.

This makes the already-present limitation at lines 71, 77, and 143 govern the discriminator locally, avoiding an unintended claim that it exhausts all explanations. It also keeps ordinary maintenance of an already actual subject distinct from the target of supplying the original's whole actuality.

### 3. Correct the local exhaustiveness wording

**Resolved in revised §3:** further intrinsic content can repair the account; only an explicitly exhaustive conditional basis is subjected to the external-or-unprovided conclusion. The new distinction between an incomplete account and an incomplete reality strengthens this correction.

At line 51, “some further existential provision” does not by itself imply external dependence. A proposed further intrinsic basis would instead deny that the stipulated conditional account exhausted the intrinsic resources.

Replace the two-options passage with:

> If the further provision is external, complete independence is relinquished. If it is genuinely intrinsic, the account is no longer exhausted by the conditional proposal and must establish its adequacy for g's obtaining. Leaving the obtaining altogether unprovided rejects the foundational no-brute judgment. The conditional proposal by itself therefore does not preserve the asserted complete self-adequacy.

This preserves the argument's useful result without letting the word “further” perform an unsupported externality inference.

## Checks passed and real strengthening

- **Same bearer:** Once the positive intrinsic-sufficiency judgment is warranted for actual g, the proposed necessity concerns g. No move from “necessarily some original” to “some necessarily existing original” occurs. Fixing the witness prevents witness-switching but does not itself establish necessity; the report assigns the latter to existential character.
- **No definition-generated existence:** Actuality is independently inherited. The report infers a character of that actual bearer rather than creating its instance from an essence-description. Its distinction between an epistemic starting point and unexplained existential provision is useful and sound.
- **No instantiated-nature shortcut:** L184's identity sentence cannot independently establish necessity. The report correctly relies on the preceding positive sufficiency judgment and notes that concrete essence/existence identity also applies to creatures.
- **No effects determinism:** The exercise argument distinguishes an available producer explaining a distinct effect from an alleged operation supplying its own original bearer. Consequently it gives a principled reason not to extend the local existential judgment to all choices or effects. Line 123 would be slightly more exact as “does not by itself exclude contingent received effects,” rather than claiming that their metaphysical possibility has independently been established.
- **No exhaustive taxonomy established:** The report acknowledges this at lines 77 and 143. Preserve those qualifications. A defended nonproductive nonnecessitating self-account could bear on the judgment; a bare category label does not yet provide such an account.

The strongest final description is therefore: **an affirmative, source-grounded appraisal of the modal existential-sufficiency judgment, strengthened by a target-specific objection to conditional instantiation and bearer-presupposing exercise.** It is more than a restatement that skeptics can disagree, and less than a new derivation of modal necessity from purely nonmodal explanatory completeness.
