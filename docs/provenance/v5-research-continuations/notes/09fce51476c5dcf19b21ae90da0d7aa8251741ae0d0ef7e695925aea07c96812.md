# Two finite-budget priors with no finite-budget proper mixture

New standalone successor, ordinary theorem. The literal tolerance is the SAME fixed0<e<=1/4 throughout. All priors and policies use the same real-parameter EndpointProcess experiment, passive Bernoulli receipts, full observed prefixes, positive report clock and fixed independent probability seed.

## 1. Priors and exact normalization

Let a_i>0 be half separated, a_(i+1)<=a_i/2, and assume

    a_0<=1/[2(1+e)], Z=sum_i a_i,
    S=sum_i r_i log(1/r_i)<infinity,
    r_i=a_(i+1)/a_i.

Define b_i^-=(1-e)a_i, b_i^+=(1+e)a_i and

    mu^- =sum_i(a_i/Z) delta_(b_i^-),
    mu^+ =sum_i(a_i/Z) delta_(b_i^+).

Each is a normalized positive-parameter prior, supported in(0,1/2]. The support ratios are unchanged. Their own normalizers are Z^-=(1-e)Z and Z^+=(1+e)Z, and exactly

    b_i^-/Z^- = b_i^+/Z^+ = a_i/Z.

Thus EACH side belongs to the already proved p=1 half-separated point-prior class. Each separately admits a common measurable coherent finite-expected-count policy, for this same literal e. No transformation of the loss tolerance is used. The predecessor's quantitative constant gives separate upper budgets

    C(mu^-)<=256S/[(1-e)Z],
    C(mu^+)<=256S/[(1+e)Z].

One explicit eligible support is a_i=exp(-(i+1)^2)/2: the accepted original support is merely halved, preserving S and ensuring the upper support promise uniformly for every e<=1/4. A rational alternative is a_i=2^(-(i+1)^2-1). The two examples are distinct priors.

## 2. Every proper mixture is universally infinite

For0<theta<1 let mu_theta=theta mu^-+(1-theta)mu^+. The critical-pair geometry proves that no real report is accepted at both b_i^- and b_i^+.

For each all-zero word of length n and each fixed seed, at least one pair member is missed. Its joint mass is at least

    min(theta,1-theta)(a_i/Z)(1-b_i^+)^n.

This holds for EVERY i simultaneously, so the nonnegative component masses can be summed. After n=1,2,..., the exact geometric sum yields a lifetime contribution at least

    min(theta,1-theta)(a_i/Z)(1-b_i^+)/b_i^+
      = min(theta,1-theta)(1-b_i^+)/[(1+e)Z]
      >= 3 min(theta,1-theta)/(10Z)

per component. The last conservative bound only needs a_i<=1/2 and e<=1/4; the actual stated upper-support assumption is stronger. There are infinitely many components. Tonelli therefore gives

    E_(mu_theta) count(pi)=infinity

for EVERY measurable real full-prefix policy pi and every fixed independent probability seed law, including a policy redesigned for theta. The endpoint priors theta0 and theta1 have finite budgets, while EVERY interior mixing coefficient is bad. This is not merely the failure of one inherited policy.

## 3. Precise nonconvexity and local total-variation consequences

Fix any independent seed law. Let F_e be the probability priors for which some admissible common policy has finite actual expected literal count. Then

    mu^-,mu^+ in F_e,
    theta mu^-+(1-theta)mu^+ not in F_e for every0<theta<1.

In particular F_e is nonconvex. No convexity theorem for Bayes risk is contradicted: the optimum of linear-in-prior policy costs is an infimum of linear functions, hence concave, not convex. The displayed failure of finite-domain convexity is compatible with that fact.

The two side supports are disjoint. Within each pair b_i^-<b_i^+, and b_(i+1)^+<b_i^- follows from half separation and e<=1/4. Thus the two priors are mutually singular. Under the sup-event convention,

    TV(mu_theta,mu_phi)=|theta-phi|.

Consequently every total-variation neighbourhood of either finite-budget endpoint contains a bad proper mixture. For every finite horizon N, the optimum count still obeys

    |C_N(mu_theta)-C_N(mu_phi)|<=N|theta-phi|,

by bounded policy loss and an epsilon-minimizer argument. The pathological lifetime interior is not visible as a discontinuity of any fixed finite-horizon objective.

## 4. Why successful policies cannot be mixed naively

Let pi^- and pi^+ be any successful policies for the respective side priors. A meta-policy may use an independent seed to choose between them, randomize afresh, switch adaptively based on receipts, or ignore them and redesign its reports. All these remain inside the universal common-policy class. Under a proper mixture, EVERY such policy has infinite prior-averaged expected count.

This does not say the parameter or side cannot eventually be learned, or that infinitely many errors occur almost surely. The obstruction is the expected total count after averaging over the infinitely many signal scales. Nor does it say a fixed component's conditional expected error count must be infinite.

The distinction from a supplied side tag is exact. If the model-choice bit is revealed before reporting, the controller may run pi^- on minus runs and pi^+ on plus runs. Its expected count is then

    theta E_(mu^-)count(pi^-)+(1-theta)E_(mu^+)count(pi^+)<infinity.

That experiment provides an extra observation correlated with the latent component. It is outside the fixed independent-seed interface. Calling the tag a private seed would violate the independence promise. A free model oracle is therefore a substantive interface change, not an implementation detail.

## 5. Scope and significance

Both successful priors are interior-supported p=1 priors with identical gap geometry and normalized weights, and each may have infinite inverse moments. Their failure to combine is about common inference across critical pairs, not endpoint atoms, a changed loss or a wrong normalizer. Computable rational examples give the same mathematical nonclosure; the existing rational reference's declared support family is not silently expanded to those rescaled priors without a separate implementation.

This supplies an obstruction to a general black-box rule claiming that a finite menu of individually finite-budget inference procedures always admits a finite-budget combined procedure under unknown model choice. It does not rule out combination under additional separation, finite inverse moments, paid side information or another explicitly changed reporting contract.
