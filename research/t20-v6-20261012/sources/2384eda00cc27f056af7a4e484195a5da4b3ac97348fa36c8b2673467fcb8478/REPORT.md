# Local correction and surviving cognitive capacity

10 October 2026 UTC. Bounded T20 source and inference test. No protected edit, integration, T21, contemporary diagnosis, or programme closure.

## Result

**The apparent circle in rational restoration fails on the strongest source-supported reading.** Dependence on a sound native cognitive constitution does not imply that one local impairment removes every usable cognitive ground until a person's whole condition has been restored. Corrective reasoning can exercise a surviving truth-directed capacity. It remains dependent on native cognition and does not certify that cognition from an entirely external foundation.

This result is stronger than saying that a restoration is merely possible: the inspected primary argument positively retains direct cognition, recognizes particular sources of error, and identifies known premises as grounds of competent inference. The source also permits limited correction inside an otherwise defective intellectual practice. It does not establish that any particular current person meets those conditions.

The new credit is a direct source-grounded resolution of a specific quantifier and correction-order objection to the saved account. The general local-defeater and operative-basing architecture is inherited. No new independent verification of basic Creator-recognition, new proper-function theory, or actual DAEE runtime defect was obtained.

## Primary evidence and its limits

The received [Intiṣār li-ahl al-athar, published under the name Naqḍ al-manṭiq](https://islamcontent.com/ar/content/19669), edited by ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Ḥasan Qāʾid, was checked in the hosted transcription and linked page-image PDF. The PDF identifies the third edition, 1440/2019, Dār ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm and Dār Ibn Ḥazm. They are presentations of the same edition, not independent textual witnesses. No manuscript collation was performed.

At 44–47 Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes knowledgeable conviction from unsupported conviction, permits mistaken self-appraisal in both directions, and locates errors in particular causes while retaining a presumption of healthy cognition. The epistemic use of inference presupposes known premises. At 265–266, in a concessive interpretation within his polemic against making formal logic obligatory, he allows correct material to remove some error, explicitly without guaranteeing beneficial truth or avoiding further error. At 271–272 he distinguishes the grounds on which the same premise is accepted: knowledge, popularity, or an interlocutor's concession. These are his own argumentative comments around reported classifications. [Page-image witness](https://islamcontent.com/storage/contents/19669/ar-alaintisar-liahl-alathr.pdf#page=100)

The direct continuous scopes are 44–49 and 265–276; 37–40 and the opening of 50 were contextual checks. PDF images at printed 45, 47, 265–266 and 271–276 were checked against the transcription. At 47 an affirmative clause is editorially emended from a negative in the stated base witness. The source's polemical historical and religious diagnoses are not adopted as contemporary assessments. Its bodily-error examples are historical analogies, not current medical explanations. Quoted reports and the editor's authentication notes were not independently researched.

### The adjoining foundational priority argument

The context at 273–274 is essential. The author affirms the genuinely basic standing of selected rational cognitions that opponents classify as mere popularity or imagination. He then rejects using a syllogism dependent on basic premises as a general warrant for discarding those foundations. At 275–276 the discussion moves to reported accounts of prophecy; those opponent accounts are not his adopted psychology of correction.

The argument is not neutral between a genuine basic cognition and every felt seeming. Read with 45, it protects the former while allowing error to be identified through a particular defect. A competent corrective inference does not turn a genuinely known proposition into a falsehood. It may expose a mistaken claim to knowledge, correct an interpretation or application, or remove a later defective explanation. Calling something “fiṭrī” settles none of these disputed applications by itself.

There is a local textual tension at 274. Both the HTML and page image print “بل الغلط فيما تقل مقدماته أولى”, apparently favoring error where premises are fewer; the next sentence expressly places syllogistic cognition nearer error than immediate fiṭrah. No apparatus note resolves that clause. It is not silently emended, and no premise-count theorem or numerical reliability rule is extracted. The preceding dependence argument can be assessed without that comparative clause.

## Executed inference test

Let an alleged impairment d concern the present operation of route A. Let B be a genuinely usable cognitive route, perhaps perception of a relevant fact, a remembered known content, or competent reasoning from known premises. The disputed global reading would infer from d alone that B cannot have warrant because both A and B belong to one native constitution.

That inference requires an extra premise: every local defect disables every relevant exercise of that constitution. Neither common ownership nor constitutive dependence supplies it. The primary argument's distinction between particular error and retained direct cognition rejects the needed universal promotion. Its adjoining foundational-priority argument also prevents recasting this as theoretical reason overthrowing its own genuinely known basic grounds. The conclusion is therefore not that cognition has escaped its native foundation; it is that a defective exercise and the entire constitution are different objects of appraisal.

The constructive case has content, rather than an unexplained restoration label. Suppose A supports a claim through a disputed report, while B includes an actually known comparison fact and a competent content-using inference exposing a specific inconsistency in that report. If the comparison fact and inference survive d, B can warrant the local correction while other errors remain. B need not first warrant an assertion that the whole person is epistemically healthy. Nor does this local correction authenticate A, every premise of the encompassing practice, or the person's prior acquisition story.

This is an application of the already earned operative-ground result. No synthetic person has been inserted into a table and called knowledgeable. The new primary classification makes an important guard explicit: a premise can be socially accepted and also known; its availability under several descriptions does not determine the actual ground on which this subject accepts it. Withdrawing popularity or source endorsement leaves a knowledge-grounded use intact only when that latter use really obtains. Relabelling the premise “demonstrative” is not a replacement for it.

The correction can also stop short of a warranted replacement belief. The source's limited-error-removal passage expressly admits that possibility. Thus three transitions remain distinct: abandoning a defective support, rejecting an error for a good reason, and obtaining warranted positive recognition. Only the third supplies the requested positive recognition; the first two are not its surrogates.

## The strongest rival and the exact surviving result

A rival may grant local fallibility but argue that this particular d attacks a shared basic capacity used by both A and B. That is a serious challenge when independently supported. The present result does not immunize B against it. It rejects only the inference from unspecified or local impairment to universal defeat. Where the defect actually reaches B's interpretation, premises, memory or competent use, the surviving-route application fails at that identified point.

A second rival says the argument assumes the very warrant it needs to explain. This is right against a claim to derive all first-order warrant from a neutral metatheory. No such derivation is offered. The task here is narrower: whether an account that already warrants basic and competent cognition renders its own correction impossible. It does not. The independently warranted ground is independent of the defeated support, not independent of all natural cognitive capacity. Ordinary knowing need not wait for reflective proof of this entire account.

The source's first-person cognition discussion also does not make every confident avowal incorrigible. Its admission of erroneous affirmation and erroneous denial blocks that reading. Conversely, a bad retrospective explanation does not by itself rebut an otherwise warranted first-order recognition. Psychological origin, present epistemic basis, and reflective self-appraisal remain different questions.
