# Additive strengthening: an exponential bad-count tail

This addendum preserves candidate v2 and its complete ordinary proof. It sharpens its deliberately loose per-segment mismatch count by using a property of the **actual** controller that v2 did not exploit fully: row statistics never reset. There is no new controller, exploration schedule, sample test or target selector.

## Main conclusion

For the same literal generated coBüchi controller and fixed finite input, the total actual bad count C has a **positive exponential moment**. It has an explicit exponential tail, hence an O(log(1/δ)) high-probability bad-visit budget. The result is pointwise in the input parameters, not uniform as row gaps or positive probabilities vanish.

Retain s,a,m,q,p,D,r,η,A,c from THEOREM_AND_PROOF.md, with A≥1,c>0 and the genuine all-deterministic-prefix tail estimate `P(G_nᶜ)≤A exp(−cn)` for n≥1. The sharper Hoeffding choice is an ordinary option. The separately developed literal product-law formal bound can use `c=η²/2`, `A=2qs/(1−exp(−c))`, after replacing η by min(η,1) if necessary. Shrinking η preserves every phase/test argument.

Define

- `α=q+2m`
- `β=q(m−1)+2m(m+1)`
- `λ=−log(1−r/2)>0`
- `B=(D/λ){[α(1+log(2A)/c)+β]log 2+log 2}`
- `V=(D/λ){α log 2/c+1}>0`

Then, for every u≥0,

`P(C>B+Vu) ≤ exp(−u)`.

Consequently

- `P(C>B+V log(1/δ))≤δ` for 0<δ≤1
- `E C≤B+V`
- `E exp(tC)≤exp(tB)/(1−tV)<∞` for 0≤t<1/V

These are ordinary stochastic bounds. The new Lean module `GlobalCountBudget.lean` kernel-checks the global cumulative-count mechanism; it does not claim that a conditional-MGF or final concentration theorem has been proved end to end in Lean.

## 1. The global charge budget is linear

Let n≥1, τ_n and the inclusive stopped bad count C_n be exactly as in v2. Every action time before τ_n has phase index less than n+m. Write `L=n+m`.

Separate each dangerous segment's progress transitions into:

1. nonterminating mismatching executions, after whose receipt the same segment continues
2. a possible segment-terminating transition, whether caused by mismatch rejection, another stale-row rejection, support change, target entry, component exit, or τ_n detection

A nonterminating mismatch has an accurate completed history, a false empirical test and current phase less than L. Its completed cumulative count must therefore be less than L. If it were at least L, the source's strict phase gate and genuine ε+η row gap would force rejection. This implication is kernel-checked as `nonrejecting_mismatch_count_lt`.

Each execution of a fixed pair increments its global count by exactly one. No support reset, phase change, target entry or exit decreases any row count. Thus, **over the entire stopped run**, a given pair can contribute at most L−1 such nonterminating mismatch executions. Matching executions at that same pair also increment the count and can only use up this budget sooner. The argument does not require a pair to mismatch every candidate.

The injection `time ↦ (executed pair, its consumed tape index)` is proved for every actual adaptive stack policy in `consumed_pair_index_injective`. The finite-set theorem `global_postcount_budget` consequently bounds the total nonterminating mismatches by `q(L−1)` across arbitrarily many epochs. It is not a per-phase bound.

A high-count mismatch can occur again after a phase/support change, but it must terminate that segment if the post-history is accurate; the τ_n-detecting transition is terminal by definition. Each dangerous segment has at most one terminal transition. There are at most `H_n=2m(n+m+1)` segments. Therefore the global charged-interval budget sharpens to

`J*_n=q(n+m−1)+H_n=αn+β`.

This includes terminal mismatch executions exactly once. A target-entry edge that ends navigation is terminal even though its phase need not change. Fully matching operating segments may be arbitrarily long or later interrupted; they still contribute zero bad cost, and their later dangerous descendants remain within H_n.

## 2. Conditional geometric MGFs need no interval independence

Every charged interval has the same uniform bound from the v2 finite augmented-chain argument. If T_i<∞ is its actual history stopping-time start and L_i its length through the next mismatch/terminal event, then conditional on that history

`P(L_i>kD | F_(T_i))≤(1−r)^k`.

The predecessor interval lengths are measurable at the next interval's start. The skipped zero-cost periods need not have bounded duration. If no further interval starts, set its start to∞ and its length to0. On the event of an infinite start, its exponential factor is1, and the same MGF upper bound holds. The start and finish times are nondecreasing stopping times; their completed lengths are known by every later finite start. Thus conditional tower arguments apply to this padded chronological sequence. No independence between intervals, no conditioning on G_n, and no iid-at-random-count assertion is used.

For G~Geom(r) on {1,2,...},

`E exp(λG)=r exp(λ)/(1−(1−r)exp(λ))=2`

at `λ=−log(1−r/2)`. Hence `E[exp(λL_i/D)|F_(T_i)]≤2`. Apply the tower rule successively from the last padded interval to the first; the already accumulated product is measurable at the relevant start. This proves

`E exp(λ C_n/D) ≤ E exp((λ/D)Σ_(i≤J*_n)L_i) ≤ 2^(J*_n)`.

The finiteness of every interval on its finite-start event follows from the geometric tail. Padding makes the number of factors deterministic even if the real number of intervals is random. All calculations can equivalently be performed for finite truncations and passed to the limit monotonically.

## 3. Optimize a deterministic cutoff in the two-term tail bound

On G_n, C=C_n. Markov's inequality for the stopped exponential gives, for every n≥1 and t≥0,

`P(C>t) ≤ A exp(−cn) + 2^(αn+β) exp(−λt/D)`.

Do **not** insert the fixed factor2 directly into the old shell-series argument. That would require `c>α log 2`, which need not hold for small row gaps. The following optimization avoids that error.

For a requested tail level u≥0 choose the deterministic integer

`n(u)=ceil((log(2A)+u)/c)`.

Since A≥1 and c>0, n(u)≥1. It satisfies

`A exp(−cn(u))≤(1/2)exp(−u)`

and

`αn(u)+β ≤ α(1+log(2A)/c)+β+(α/c)u`.

Substitute `t=B+Vu` using the constants above. The second term is also at most `(1/2)exp(−u)`. Adding establishes the displayed exponential tail for every c>0, including arbitrarily small c.

## 4. Mean and positive exponential moment

The tail bound says C is stochastically dominated by `B+V Z`, where Z has the mean-one exponential law. For thresholds below B the comparator's survival probability is1, and for thresholds B+Vu it is exp(−u), exactly the derived bound. The comparison applies to every increasing nonnegative measurable function.

Integrating the survival function gives `E C≤B+V`. Directly integrating `exp(t(B+VZ))` gives `exp(tB)/(1−tV)` when tV<1. Thus the total bad count has a positive exponential moment. No assertion is made that the physical time of its last bad action has such a moment.

For finitely many true models, take the minimum positive exponential parameter across models; alternatively use a common row margin and the minimum positive transition probability over the whole known family. Hence one positive parameter works simultaneously for every σ∈B0 and for every finite prior mixture on B0.

## 5. Strengthened existence characterization

The computed coBüchi region also equals the region admitting a common lawful policy whose total bad count has some positive exponential moment under every live model. The actual generated policy witnesses the forward direction. Exponential integrability implies finite mean, then a.s. coBüchi, so the reverse direction is inherited from the accepted necessity theorem. This does not assert that every a.s.-winning policy has an exponential moment.
