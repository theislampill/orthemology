## The question in simple terms

### An explanation for a young reader

Imagine a child learns a way to solve a puzzle, then shows another child how to do it. The first child can leave, yet the useful way of solving the puzzle can keep going. The table, pieces and teacher may still help. We should ask what the children really learnt and what help they still need. If the puzzle changes, they may need to learn something new.

Now imagine a rule book for checking recipes. Two recipes can always give the same result when used, while the rule book treats their written forms differently. A checker can examine a complete explanation of why a recipe passes. That does not mean it can always find an explanation, or always tell us that none exists.

### An explanation for an older student

There are several separate achievements. A recipient may perform a task, recognise the strategy, produce an example that teaches someone else, or judge whether a new recipient and setting are suitable. Evidence for one achievement need not establish all four. A supported ability can be genuine: using a well-prepared laboratory or library does not make every success accidental. The important question is whether the support and the claimed contribution have been described accurately.

In the formal work, a semantics explains what expressions mean, while typing rules specify which certificates can be constructed. Soundness says that accepted certificates have the promised meaning. Completeness would say that every relevant semantic success can be certified. The new example separates these claims. Its proof checks every typing rule, so the conclusion cannot be evaded by proposing a less obvious route through the rule book.

## Continuing a task through new carriers

### The capacity and its support

A useful continuation claim begins with the object being continued. That might be a policy for selecting actions, a particular explanatory competence, a complete controller, or an organisation with pending obligations. These objects differ. Preserving a policy at the boundary between episodes need not preserve an interrupted computation. Preserving an organisation may require timing, authority and consequential records in addition to successful task performance. The criterion should be fixed before inspecting the outcome.

The Apoautosis Companion permits a contribution to be assessed within an honestly declared supported arrangement. An ability need not manufacture its laboratory, invent its learning mechanism or independently qualify every material it uses. A prepared route can supply justified but fallible opportunities for re-expression. What must not happen is an unnoticed change of subject: counting support as part of the organisation to establish a faculty, then excluding it when claiming independence. The same boundary must govern the positive attribution and its limitations.

Brinkmann and colleagues trace machine-to-four-human transmission of a game strategy taking losses to unlock higher rewards: every member has the maximum reported mean on independent demonstration trials, and all four humans receive positive strategy coding. Later humans generate the demonstrations used by successors. A contrasting population retains machine ancestry after successful strategy performance is lost. \[E1\] The programme application credits an actual execution-and-demonstration contribution and a defensible minimal faculty using a prepared, fallible route. Designed support and its finite renewed use matter; qualification competence need not itself be copied to preserve access. Stronger component-owned qualification, recipient guarantees and whole-AI continuation remain unproved. Generation four has no observed subsequent recipient. The outcome-selected path supports existence without estimating transmission reliability. New-instance use rests on the reported procedure; readable figure data verify the selected links and scores, while full raw-data reproduction and historical training provenance remain unresolved.

Three judgements should therefore be available in any future application of the framework. First, identify the actual contribution: what did the proposed component do? Second, identify the warranted route: what makes the next use a reasonable exercise of a capacity rather than a coincidence? Third, state the strength of the service claim: an observed finite sequence, a probability of success within a domain, or a guarantee over a specified family of requests. These judgements concern different propositions and need not receive the same verdict.

A route can remain usable without its user acquiring the provider's expertise. Conversely, access that worked once may fail under a changed interface or resource condition. This is why a stronger claim of preserved qualification should name the discrimination involved: recognising acceptable inputs, assessing a receiving configuration, selecting a remedy, or rejecting an unsuitable case. General intelligence, consciousness and unlimited autonomy are unnecessary additions unless the claim itself requires them. A narrow attribution should not be defeated by requirements belonging only to a broader one.

## Questions that would change a stronger judgement

For continuation, fix the stronger request first. A reliability claim needs an appropriate success domain and failure accounting. A transferred qualification claim needs evidence that the relevant discrimination is actually available to the successor. A whole-organisation claim needs its consequential state and obligations included in the continuation criterion. Stationary external support can remain part of a legitimate supported faculty; the question is whether it meets the declared request and remains available.

For restoration and correction, the most discriminating next history would join a specified acquired competence, the same recipient's response to a changed condition, a justified repair, and renewed useful contribution. Protection of correct commitments should be measured alongside correction of mistakes. For the foundational inquiry, the strongest next addition would concern the exact fixed-bearer or productive-dependence bridge, rather than another general statement that truth, agency or directedness needs explanation.

For mathematics, further work should choose explicitly among mechanising the written proofs, studying a restricted decidable fragment, or assessing another stated rule extension. These are different projects. The present results already exclude two easy shortcuts: treating old semantic validity as a sufficient typing certificate, and treating exhaustive certificate search as a total decision service. They leave substantial constructive work possible inside precisely declared limits.
