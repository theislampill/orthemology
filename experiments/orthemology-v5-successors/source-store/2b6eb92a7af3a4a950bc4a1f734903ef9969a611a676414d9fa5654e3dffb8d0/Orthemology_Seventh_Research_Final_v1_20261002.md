## Repair through changing versions and permissions

### The family accounts for its supports

The dynamic plant has a two-input table and an absorbing unsafe state. A current descriptor binds target identity, target version, control epoch, recipient, operation scope and target table. The adequate repair replaces the whole table and is idempotent. Permission-only changes advance the control epoch even when the target bytes do not change. An unauthorised write remains unsafe even if its payload is correct. \[D1, §2\]

Each of n roots jointly supports its labelled commitments, local permission state, durable revocation and cancellation memory, command binding, live gate and relevant addressing and dispatch. At most B distinct roots ever lose a relevant duty during the declared horizon. This lifetime-union bound charges a root once even if its faults move among roles or it later appears correct. It is stronger than a bound on faults at any single instant.

A repair path contains q distinct live serial gates. At landing each gate checks the same complete command; any untainted gate can still veto it. Old signed votes do not replace this continuing causal power. All plant mutations remain mediated, including those requested by faulty dispatchers. Active shared selectors, control channels or downstream writers must be included in the actual support map. Counting copied gate occurrences as new independent roots would invalidate the premise.

### Revocation needs an institutional and a causal connection

A legitimate owner requests a new descriptor. An untainted acknowledging root first closes old commitments and durably records their revocation. A certificate with r distinct acknowledgers makes the new control epoch effective. The old permission must remain institutionally valid until that completion, or every old path must be closed earlier. This reservation condition concerns actual permission; it cannot be manufactured by a convenient definition of a certificate.

The actor need not learn every intervening change. Its old evidence can remain accurate as a record of preparation while a live gate prevents an obsolete command from landing. The commitment warrants this protected action envelope, rather than claiming that the world stays unchanged.

### Sharp support thresholds

Every q-path and r-revocation set intersect in at least q+r−n roots. An overlap larger than B contains an untainted root whose durable revocation continues to veto the old action. Repair availability and revocation availability separately require that q and r not exceed n−B. The exact feasible threshold conditions for this symmetric interface are therefore:

$$
\mathrm{q}\ +\ \mathrm{r}\ >\ \mathrm{n}\ +\ \mathrm{B},\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{q}\ \le{}\ \mathrm{n}\ -\ \mathrm{B},\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{r}\ \le{}\ \mathrm{n}\ -\ \mathrm{B}
$$

The inequalities imply n≥3B+1. At the minimum, both q and r must equal 2B+1. Conversely those values meet the conditions. The same family supplies necessity witnesses when intersection is too small or either threshold exceeds the surviving roots. This is a sharp result for the declared interface, not a universal lower bound for every distributed repair architecture. \[D1, §3\]

$$
\mathrm{B}\ +\ 1\ \le{}\ \mathrm{c}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{l}\_\mathrm{t}\mathrm{h}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{h}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{l}\mathrm{d}\ \le{}\ \mathrm{q}\ -\ \mathrm{B}
