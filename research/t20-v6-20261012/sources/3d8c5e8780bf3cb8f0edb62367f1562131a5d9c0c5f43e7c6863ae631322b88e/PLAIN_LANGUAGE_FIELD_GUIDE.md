# Orthemology T20 — plain-language guide through the mathematical forest

**A guide for the owner, not a replacement for the proof archive.** New threads should read this before diving into individual ZIPs. Technical terms are explained by analogy; analogies are not formal correspondences until a typed map and the relevant proof are provided.

## The entire programme in one image

Imagine an investigation that must continue for years. It has original observations, sources, conclusions, governing rules, proofs, uncertainties, potential corrections and future questions. A short summary is convenient, but can be dangerously incomplete. Orthemology asks: **What must we keep so the investigation can remain right, recognise when it goes wrong, and change course without losing the reasons for earlier conclusions?**

The latest T20 research is a mathematical exploration of that question in seven interlocking domains.

### 1. Evidence and trust — a discredited witness can still contain information

Imagine a witness gives a number and is later exposed as a random-number generator. That discredits the witness's claim to know the answer. But if an audit records the relationship between that number and another verifier, the observed number may still be essential for interpreting the audit. In one exact finite example, the posterior is **1/5** with report-plus-audit and **1/2** if the report is incorrectly deleted.

**Lesson:** withdrawing someone's authority is not necessarily deleting their observed report. There are separate questions about what happened, whether the source was credible, how a later test depends on the report and whether the underlying conclusion remains warranted. The historical source research uses analogous distinctions: defeating one inferential ground doesn't erase independently valid grounds.

**Not proved:** the credibility of arbitrary real witnesses, or a theology of divine guidance from a finite Bayesian toy model.

### 2. Tensors and information — the summary can lose the relationship

Two inspection reports each say “safe”. They look like two reasons. Both may be copied from the same third report. A summary counting two votes loses the shared ancestor. A properly typed multiway representation can retain that relation, but calling the object a tensor requires real algebraic structure.

The inherited **parity** theorem is a particularly sharp demonstration: every pair, triple or other proper subset of binary variables may have identical distributions while one full joint property is completely different. Later T20 proved similar limits for future evidence correction and tensor moments.

**Lesson:** separately accurate local facts do not always determine a global judgement or its behaviour after future updates. A finite tensor hierarchy sometimes repairs this under a *specified finite support and update family*. There is no universal licence to keep only low-order marginals.

### 3. Geometry — making numbers coherent is not learning

Say probabilities for three mutually exclusive hypotheses total 130%. Fixing them to sum to 100% is worthwhile bookkeeping. Riemannian geometry can rigorously describe a downhill path for the chosen numerical objective. But the fix could change relative beliefs despite receiving evidence that tells us nothing. T20 produced such counterexamples and then stronger Bayesian-compatible geometric corrections.

The original separable-Hessian classification selected entropy-like curvature from *all-start safety plus original-clock sum closure*. Later nonseparable and stochastic-channel research proved there are **different valid geometric families under different contracts**, so “entropy is always unique” is false. Invariant Gaussian families give another exact Riemannian model, while arbitrary distributions usually cannot be represented by a small fixed polynomial summary with exact future evolution.

**Lesson:** geometric consistency, Bayesian fidelity, actual probability-law validity, truth and epistemic warrant are five different claims.

### 4. Choosing the next question — the shortest proof is not always cheapest

Suppose checking the currently available proof costs 10 units. Obtaining one more piece of evidence allows a simpler valid proof whose combined acquisition and verification cost is 2. Fewest missing facts doesn't mean least effort. Likewise, multiple proofs can share steps, so pricing them as separate trees can overcount the true cost—and aggressive local pruning can discard a globally cheaper shared proof.

The T20 compiler work developed finite proof catalogues, ordered source-support guards, Lean-checked algorithmic fragments, witness extractors, hardness constructions and controls. Later audit-value work proved that even if two models agree on answers *after* each possible test, differing outcome probabilities can reverse whether the test is worth doing.

**Lesson:** to optimise an investigation you may need the actual costs, permissible operations, information dependencies and possible outcome probabilities, not just the current conclusion.

### 5. Type theory — a true identity may be unprovable with available rules

Think of a calculator that can correctly represent a fact but lacks the operation needed to establish a general identity. Dot's long-running HasE investigation became a precise study of this difference. The original compiler identity is semantically valid in a specified interpretation yet **independent of the unchanged HasE proof rules**, according to the Lean-checked countermodels. The positive identity has proofs in **explicitly strengthened** calculi with extra dependent induction and rigidity principles.

The research also developed source-level substitution, recursor uniqueness, numeric extraction, serialised proof-certificate checking and formal limitations on computing all semantic truths.

**Lesson:** “true in this model”, “provable in the original calculus”, “provable after adopting an additional principle”, “algorithmically checkable proof” and “decidable truth” are distinct. An external derivation history is not automatically retained in a proof-irrelevant typed equality.

### 6. Infinite information — every moment can still mislead

Moments are statistics such as an average, the average of squares and higher-power aggregates. Knowing many moments may identify an unknown finite collection of points. But infinite probability distributions can be different despite sharing *every algebraic moment*. Worse, a sequence of numerical states can obey a valid-looking differential equation yet cease to correspond to *any* probability distribution.

T20 built both impossibility examples and positive repair theorems under compact support, determinacy, effective tail bounds, full square positivity, coercive polynomial dynamics and other stated conditions. It can produce a negative-expectation square as a certificate that the apparent state is impossible. Some positive laws can be computed from appropriate complete named moments; uniqueness alone under an arbitrary non-effective representation is insufficient.

Later full-law and finite-atomic dynamics connect these moments to true geometric flows. One explicit Euler step can produce an impossible negative-variance state even when the continuous flow remains valid. A positive-particle update is a conditional constructive repair.

**Lesson:** mathematical solvability, statistical identifiability, realisability as a genuine probability law, effective computability and stable numerical implementation must all be checked separately.

### 7. Knowledge, Will, Wisdom and original grounding — philosophy is part of the research

The underlying positive philosophical inquiry is about *actual obtaining, objective rightness and their adequate original explanation*. The inherited arguments distinguish an actual productive ground from a proposition about one; an original bearer from its abstract attributes; standing capacity from an exercised act; an intrinsic divine act from a created effect; a beneficial accident from a knowingly intended wise purpose; and source authority from the kind or scope of a particular utterance.

Dot's Arabic-source work repeatedly revisits these joints rather than treating a phrase in Ibn Taymiyyah or Ibn al-Qayyim as a theorem without interpretation. The nuanced affirmative scope includes a **defeasible rational appraisal of intrinsic necessity and one complete common original Creator for the relevant productively connected created order**, while retaining the actual premise-level obligations.

**Lesson:** a mathematical proof can help track which premises, source meanings or corroborative grounds support a conclusion, and how the warrant survives a challenge. It cannot simply *supply* actual existence, independent cognitive warrant or divine self-sufficiency by symbol manipulation.

## The point where the seven stories meet

```
Real claim, source, bearer, governing standard
         ↓  precisely typed representation
What must a summary retain for future questions?
         ↓  fibre / tensor / continuation tests
Which evidence and proofs legitimately support an answer?
         ↓  provenance / warrants / proof catalogue
What would a permissible correction or inquiry do?
         ↓  Bayesian, stochastic, geometric and audit semantics
Is the new state realisable, computable, and stable?
         ↓  positivity, moments, geometry, numerical controls
Can the formal rules certify the claimed relationship?
         ↓  typed proof certificates and semantic residuals
Retain results, witnesses, failed maps, provenance and next obligations
         ↺ repeat, with new discoveries changing earlier priorities
```

**This is a conceptual map, not one already deployed Orthemology/DAEE engine.** It shows where research interfaces might become fully formal and testable.

## What a genuinely good cross-front result looks like

It proves—not merely suggests—that a *particular* mapping preserves exactly the relations that a theorem uses. Or it supplies a counterexample where a proposed mapping loses a vital source, identity, time or evidential distinction. It records classical ancestry, exact assumptions, needed data, verified controls and remaining meaning/warrant. A simple counterexample can be worth more than a hundred untested “theories of everything”.
