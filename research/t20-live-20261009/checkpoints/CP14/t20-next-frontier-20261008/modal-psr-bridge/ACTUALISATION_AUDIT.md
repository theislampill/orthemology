# Actualisation audit: what the complete target does

9 October 2026. Analytical reconstruction of the actualisation step, not a new cosmological theorem. The original Gale–Pruss argument identifies the possible explanatory world with actuality using the complete actual contingent fact (1999, pp. 462–464). The following isolates less structure sufficient for its existential conclusion. It does not replace their stated proof, authenticate a modal ontology, or settle the warrant for W-PSR.

## 1. Fixed interpretation and exact assumptions

Let a be the actual member of a nonempty domain W of admissible possibilities. Let P be **one fixed proposition**, not a rule that returns a different world-totality at each world. Let H_P be the fixed proposition that P has an explanation, with the intended explanation relation and quantification understood consistently across W.

Write w ⊨ S for truth under that interpretation. Require:

1. **Classical truth at the points used:** bivalence at a; negation and consistency at the witness world.
2. **Explanandum factivity:** w ⊨ H_P implies w ⊨ P. No rule that an explanans necessitates its explanandum is needed.
3. **Negative-explanation completeness for P:** if ¬H_P is contingent across W and true at a, then every P-world satisfies ¬H_P. This follows if P really contains every actual contingent truth, including truths about explanation. It is strictly less than requiring P to individuate a world.
4. **Actual application of weak PSR:** some v in W satisfies H_P.

“Contingent” here means true somewhere in the given global modal domain and false somewhere in it. The domain and interpretation are substantive background. No accessibility relation, S5 inference, counterpart identification, or constant-domain quantifier interchange is being inferred from the definitions.

## 2. Derivation

Assume for contradiction that a does not satisfy H_P. By bivalence a satisfies ¬H_P. The possible witness v satisfies H_P, so ¬H_P is contingent. By negative-explanation completeness, P at v entails ¬H_P at v. Factivity gives P at v. Thus both H_P and ¬H_P hold at v, a contradiction. Hence H_P is actually true.

For this existential conclusion **no particular explanans q must be carried between worlds**: H_P can be an existential proposition interpreted world by world. Its actual truth supplies an actual explanatory witness under that same interpretation. There is no exchange of a modal operator and an individual existential quantifier.

A stronger, fixed-q variant takes the proposition E(q,P) instead of H_P. If P includes the actual contingent negatives of each such explanatory proposition, the same proof transports E(q,P) for that same proposition q. That variant requires fixed cross-world proposition identity for q. Gale–Pruss's original identity-of-worlds proof supports that stronger result in its own possible-world ontology.

The two arguments should not be conflated. The authors' proof works by maximality and world identity. This audit shows why its **existential output** survives without using world identity: the complete target already decides the relevant explanation-status proposition.

## 3. What this does not prove

- Possible explanation of an arbitrary true proposition need not be actual explanation. Assumption 3 is the decisive extra condition.
- The inference does not establish that W-PSR is true. It identifies what accepting its application to this fully comprehensive target commits one to.
- Logical strength does not settle epistemic priority. A metaphysical premise may have basic or defeasible warrant even when, against substantive background assumptions, it entails a controversial conclusion. No demand for ontology-neutral derivability is imposed here.
- The proof establishes actual explanation, not its efficient-causal character, an intrinsic productive resource, a bearer, or a de re necessary particular.
- It does not distribute explanation to every conjunct. Only **truth** of P is used to obtain truth of an included negative explanation proposition. No claim E(q,P) ⇒ E(q,S) is used.

## 4. Restricted-target loss control

A minimal logical scope diagnostic has three worlds a,b,c. Keep a fixed target F and a fixed explanation-status proposition H_F. Put F true at a and b, false at c; put H_F true only at b. Thus F is genuinely contingent, its explanation is factive wherever available, and F possibly has an explanation. Yet F has no actual explanation at a.

This diagnostic omits exactly negative-explanation completeness: ¬H_F is an actual contingent truth, but F does not contain it. One can read F merely as the stipulated full description of a chosen created-order fragment, with explanation-status outside that fragment. That reading explains the representational risk, **not** an assertion that these assignments constitute a metaphysically possible or actual cosmos.

If the actual complete fact P_a includes both F and ¬H_F, b is not a P_a-world. The original complete-target inference is untouched. Restricting to a created or received field therefore requires an independent reason why the omitted explanation, agency, and intrinsic-act facts can be omitted without losing the closure needed for transport. Calling the restricted field “total” does not supply that reason.

## 5. Consequence for the divine-act question

A contingent truth about an intrinsic act of a necessary agent is eligible for the complete contingent target because of its modal truth status. This does not classify the act as a created substance, an externally received perfection, or a beginning of its bearer. Removing such truths merely because they are not created would change the target to which the actualisation proof applies.

Conversely, leaving them in the target does not make the act's own explanation a productive cause of the agent. Whether their explanatory questions are answered by agents, reasons, collective grounds, contextual regress termination, or a further adequate particular determination remains a separate issue. It is not settled by the actualisation lemma.
