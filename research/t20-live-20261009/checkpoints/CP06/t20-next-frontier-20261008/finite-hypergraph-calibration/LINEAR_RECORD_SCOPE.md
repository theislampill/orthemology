# Linear-record inheritance without a false probability constraint

The archived *Information, Composition, and Self-Revision* Proposition 6 concerns a linear record on signed probability laws. Its statement includes mass retention; on a nonempty probability fibre it identifies feasible difference directions, with an additional feasible-support restriction. A full-support feasible law removes that support restriction. The review-side SCOPE_ADDENDUM for historical-mathematical-transport explicitly warns that full support alone does not remove an omitted mass constraint.

The present log calibration vector y_i=log u_i is not a probability law on root labels. Its coordinates are negative and have no normalization condition. In particular, neither sum_i u_i=1 nor sum_i y_i=1 is a premise, and valid perturbations need not sum to zero. The probability-law proposition must not be instantiated literally with y in place of a probability distribution.

What transports is its elementary linear-fibre reasoning, proved anew on the correct domain. If F={y in U:Ay=b} is nonempty and U is the open calibration box, every feasible difference lies in ker A. Conversely, for any v in ker A and any feasible y, sufficiently small y+epsilon v and y-epsilon v remain in U. Their difference spans v. Thus span(F-F)=ker A without any mass row. A linear target h^T y is constant exactly when h annihilates ker A, equivalently h lies in the row space of A.

The general set-theoretic fibre criterion also applies: a target is recoverable from the retained record precisely when it is constant on that record's fibres. Neither criterion establishes that a mathematical calibration record is a faithful physical measurement. The new work is the explicit product/ratio representation and the admissible homeomorphic realization of its kernel fibres, not invention of a general rank or fibre theorem.

## Exact failed probability transport

Take positive interactions AB and AC, with known counts and no unary anchors. Their incidence matrix has rows (1,1,0) and (1,0,1). The vector v=(1,-1,-1) lies in the kernel but has nonzero coordinate sum.

For example base values (1/5,1/4,1/3) may be scaled by factors (6/5,5/6,5/6). Both interaction products stay unchanged, and the same factors may be applied to sufficiently interior higher sampled levels. Piecewise-linear endpoint-fixed homeomorphisms realize the changed values. A fictitious mass-zero condition would discard this genuine gauge. Adding the row (1,1,1) would make the matrix full rank, but that row is a new observation of the total product, not automatic probability normalization.

The interaction graph is likewise an observation-constraint graph. Its unsigned row (1,1) imposes a sum of log calibration values. A difference/offset calibration graph would instead have a row (1,-1) and a different kernel; connectedness results cannot be transported across that sign change.
