## Accepted result

**Accept the bounded application-level bridge and limit in research-v2.** Under independently fair formation, real recipient and third-party interests, warranted access, actual coordination dependence and the stated balance, a protective arrangement can give its recipient a new reason to cooperate. The care history explains G's planning responsibility; the arrangement's actual effects explain R's action reason. This does not grant G a power to bind simply by choosing.

The two-sided-alternatives limit also survives review. G cannot derive an otherwise unwarranted control condition from its own unjustified refusal of an equally adequate, reasonably available alternative without relevant offsetting loss. The corrected version assesses alternatives at selection and at the recipient's later decision point. Deliberately creating sunk coordination costs does not retrospectively establish fair selection. Innocent participants' resulting interests may nevertheless warrant R's temporary cooperation, while fitting remedy and reconfiguration responsibilities remain subject to present feasibility and competing reasons.

This changes recipient decisions without silently deriving command power: cooperate in the fair interdependent case; allow a genuinely harmless alternative absent another binding ground; reject a gratuitous control condition without automatically waiving care; and adjust unsafe reliance even when the source acts wrongly. These are conditional normative judgments, not an actual divine-address finding or a new general moral theory.
