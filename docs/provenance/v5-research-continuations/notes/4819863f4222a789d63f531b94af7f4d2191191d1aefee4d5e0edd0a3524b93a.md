# Always-issued continuum repair and the price near the endpoints

## One actual-data procedure

At opportunity n≥1, acquire the first n fresh selector bits, use their empirical mean as the estimated weight, and issue q=1/2+epsilon times that estimate. Here the multiplication applies only to epsilon and the estimate. The complementary coordinate is 1−q. Epsilon is fixed, positive and at most1/4. The executable Lean procedure uses rational epsilon and exact rational empirical counts. Its only data argument is the acquired finite word.

The procedure is coherent on every word. Under the constructed iid Bernoulli(alpha) source law, the strong law proves eventual strict improvement at both truth states for every fixed real alpha in(0,1). This is a separate always-emitting procedure from the confidence-certified procedure that can abstain. It has no asserted uniformly small probability of ever making an error.

`ContinuumAuditRestoration.lean` proves these claims against the actual source law. Its frozen source is author-compiled with standard axioms, with independent review requested. It does not invoke the finite-model control equivalence.

## Exact lower error cost

For an interior alpha, an all-zero prefix yields q=1/2, which fails literal repair. An all-one prefix yields q=1/2+epsilon, which also fails. The two prefix events are disjoint for n≥1 and have actual probabilities(1−alpha)^n and alpha^n. Therefore the probability of an incorrect report at opportunity n is at least their sum.

Tonelli's theorem and the geometric sums give the expected total number of incorrect reports at least

    (1−alpha)/alpha + alpha/(1−alpha).

`EmpiricalRepairCost.lean` proves the actual-prefix probability bound and the expected-count bound in extended-nonnegative-real geometric form. This is a lower bound for this particular empirical procedure, not for every possible repair policy. It is independent of the value of fixed positive epsilon because the extreme empirical reports are excluded for every positive epsilon.

The bound diverges near both endpoints. Thus no finite parameter-uniform expected error budget exists for this procedure, even though every fixed interior parameter has almost-sure eventual restoration.

## A finite upper cost at every fixed interior parameter

The following ordinary proof strengthens almost-sure finiteness to finite expectation. It is not silently included in the kernel result above.

Set v=alpha(1−alpha) and r=v epsilon/4. The previously proved approximate-parameter repair lemma says that an empirical error of at most r implies strict repair at both truths. For n independent Bernoulli draws, the fourth central moment of the empirical mean is exactly

    [n v(1−3v) + 3n(n−1)v²] / n⁴.

To see the identity, expand the fourth power of the sum of centred independent draws. Terms having a factor with exponent one have expectation zero. The surviving terms are the n fourth moments v(1−3v), and the 6 times choose(n,2) pairs of second moments v². Dividing by n⁴ gives the display.

For n≥1 this is at most(v+3v²)/n². Markov's inequality applied to the fourth power then gives

    P(report n is incorrect) ≤ min(1, (v+3v²)/(n² r⁴)).

Summing the bound establishes finite expected total errors, with the coarse bound(v+3v²)π²/(6r⁴). This constant is not optimal; the point is finiteness under exactly the same actual iid source and always-issued policy. No external concentration or stopping-time assumption is inserted.

At alpha=0 and alpha=1 the actual stream is respectively all zeros and all ones almost surely, and the report is the unique weak repair at every opportunity. Those endpoint assertions are ordinary here pending the separate actual-law kernel closure. Strict repair at an endpoint is impossible because the undisturbed coordinate already has its optimum.

Thus this single policy has finite expected error count for each fixed alpha in[0,1], but there is no common finite bound over that closed continuum. The endpoints themselves have zero error; the expected-count function is unbounded as they are approached through interior models. The difference concerns arbitrarily long finite misleading prefixes, not discontinuity of any fixed-prefix probability.

## Relation to finite issuance and finite-model control

The separate finite-issuance obstruction explains why one cannot generally replace this eventual guarantee with a uniformly small probability of any error while retaining endpoint issuance. For this empirical policy the first report is always wrong at every interior parameter: after one bit the estimate is either zero or one. This is fully compatible with finite expected total errors and eventual correctness.

For a finite restriction of the parameter family, the maximum of finitely many per-model finite constants is finite. That arithmetic observation does not itself identify this policy with the finite-model canonical controller, whose action type and support-menu interface must be matched separately. The empirical report space here is countably infinite, and the parameter family is a continuum.

The operational contract remains one of expected selector charges at each truth. Correctness is not improvement of every realised charge. No argument here authenticates an external data source or derives normative authority from a sample mean.
