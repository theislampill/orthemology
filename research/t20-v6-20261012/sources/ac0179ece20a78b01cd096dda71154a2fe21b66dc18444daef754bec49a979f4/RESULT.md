# Identity evidence cannot distinguish provenance paths

## Result and consequence

The unchanged original HasE grammar already proves uniqueness of identity evidence. The same source argument works for arbitrary recursively newly formed types in the unadopted Joint extension. For any actual context Γ, endpoints x,y:A and actual proof terms e,f:Id_A(x,y), the new theorem derives:

    I : Id_(Id_A(x,y))(e,f).

This is an actual source derivation, not an inference from semantic proof irrelevance. It is not definitional equality and not Raw conversion of open proof variables. A checked control has the literal variables 0 and 1 typed-identical in their proof context while rejecting their unrestricted PolyConvPlus conversion.

The consequential boundary is representational. A source-typed observer from one identity type to a fixed carrier cannot distinguish its proof inputs up to that carrier's typed identity. In particular, given a closed typed numerical observer, two supplied proofs of the same identity, and finite observation-conversion certificates for its two outputs at counts j and k, numeric_observer_same_count proves j = k. This theorem consumes those certificates; it does not itself construct or run their extraction. The broader availability of extraction for closed typed naturals belongs to the separately retained extractor theorem. Thus distinct evidential routes, provenance paths, or certificates must not be represented solely by inhabitants of that Id type if their distinctions are intended to remain observable.

An external auditor can still inspect and distinguish polynomial syntax, derivation trees, and separately retained records. The theorem does not delete those records or identify them as external data. Its observation restriction concerns source-typed observers in this calculus under their actual well-formedness premises. Proof correctness and external pathway evidence can therefore coexist.

The source interface already provides a separate data channel. The controls construct ordinary Sigma packages pairing a natural-number label with identical I evidence. Packages carrying labels 0 and 1 provably have no identity witness in HasJ. The label's distinctness survives even though the proof field is identical. This demonstrates one sufficient source-level representation of distinguishable labels: separately retained, typed data alongside identity evidence. Sigma labels are not claimed to be a uniquely necessary architecture; other distinguishable data representations or external records can serve different purposes. It does not by itself authenticate labels, establish provenance truth, or implement a provenance system.

## The exact rule responsible

1. Source J transports a reflexive identity proof to the reversed identity. Its polynomial is the unchanged jPoly, whose two K contractions erase to the branch evidence I.
2. Two such symmetries produce I at any already inhabited identity, even in an arbitrary open context. No closed-term semantic normalization is needed.
3. In the actual J telescope, form the genuinely proof-dependent motive

       Id_(Id_A(x,y))(e,I).

   The bound e has its lookup typing; the preceding source canonicalization types I at the same inner identity. At the reflexive base, both endpoints are literally I, so ordinary identity introduction suffices.
4. Apply J and its unchanged K erasure to obtain contraction of e to I. Compose two contractions to identify e and f.

The decisive feature is this erased J computation together with a canonical reflexivity representative and source conversion. The proofErase constructor is not used as an extra equality-reflection premise. The result is not obtained by merely reading a PER definition that identifies proof realizers. It is a derivable consequence of the source interface itself. Structural metatheory, naturally, is established over all source constructors; dependency closure should not be confused with a claim that the source grammar contains only J.
