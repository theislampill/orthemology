## 2. Exact gradient contract

Let I be a fixed nonempty finite label set. Each X_i is a nonempty connected open subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. Zero-dimensional factors are allowed. Work on the entire labelled domain

    M = Δ_(|I|−1)° × ∏_i X_i,
    g = (1/α) Σ_i dw_i²/w_i + Σ_i w_i ||dx_i||²,  α>0,

with tangent constraint Σ_i dw_i=0. Let E∈C²(M). The same objective, metric rule, labels and product domain are used in every comparison; no position, context, partition, or source catalogue is changed by an audit.

For a partition P of I, its audit family A(P) consists of every strictly positive multiplier constant on each P-block:

    T_a(w,x) = (B_a w,x),   (B_a w)_i = a_i w_i / Σ_j a_j w_j.

“Every” is the entire continuous family, not merely one instrument's finite outcomes, finitely many observed source ratios, or the abstract real span of integer-generated log factors.

For P_1,…,P_k, k≥1, let R be their common refinement: its blocks are all nonempty intersections G_1∩…∩G_k, with G_l∈P_l. For A∈R put

    m_A=Σ_(i∈A)w_i,  r_A=(w_i/m_A)_(i∈A),  x_A=(x_i)_(i∈A).

The negative gradient is C¹, hence has unique maximal local flows. As in the source, equivariance and maximal-flow commutation are equivalent, without any assertion of forward completeness.

## 3. Exact classification for every finite family of partitions

### Theorem A: refinement rigidity of the gradient class

The following are equivalent:

1. −grad_g E is equivariant under every T in every A(P_l).
2. −grad_g E is equivariant under every T in A(R).
3. For C² functions V_A on Δ_(|A|−1)°×∏_(i∈A)X_i,

       E(w,x) = C + Σ_(A∈R) m_A V_A(r_A,x_A).

The same equivalences hold with vector-field equivariance replaced by commutation of maximal local flows.

The implication 2⇒1 holds because every original block is a union of refinement cells. The equivalence 2⇔3 is the inherited fixed-partition theorem. The substantive missing implication 1⇒3 follows from the next two elementary lemmas; no action-generation assertion is used.

### Lemma A1: intersections of additive product decompositions

Let Ω=∏_(i∈I)Ω_i be a product of nonempty connected open Euclidean sets, and let F∈C²(Ω). If F admits an additive decomposition over every P_l,

    F(z)=Σ_(G∈P_l) F_(l,G)(z_G),

then F admits an additive C² decomposition over R.

**Proof.** A differential of F in a coordinate of Ω_i depends only on z_(P_l(i)) for every l. If j is not in the refinement cell R(i), at least one P_l separates i and j. That decomposition makes the differential in Ω_i independent of Ω_j. Change outside coordinates one at a time; connectedness and the full product domain ensure the differential depends only on z_(R(i)). This can also be read from vanishing cross-block second derivatives.

Choose a basepoint z*. For each A∈R define

    f_A(z_A)=F(z_A,z*_(−A))−F(z*).

The differential of f_A in any coordinate of A is exactly that of F, because the latter is independent of all outside coordinates. Therefore F−Σ_A f_A has zero differential. Ω is connected, so this difference is the constant c=F(z*). This yields F=c+Σ_A f_A, all f_A C². A constant can be placed into any one summand. No simply connectedness or extension to a boundary is used. □

### Lemma A2: compatible additive homogeneous decompositions

In Lemma A1 take Ω_i=(0,∞)×X_i with coordinate z_i=(q_i,x_i). Suppose also F(tq,x)=tF(q,x) for every t>0. Then the refinement decomposition may be chosen with every summand H_A(q_A,x_A) positively 1-homogeneous in q_A.

**Proof.** Use the anchored C² decomposition F=c+Σ_A f_A above. With L_A=Σ_(i∈A)q_i∂_(q_i), Euler's identity L F=F gives

    Σ_A [L_A f_A−f_A]=c.

Each bracket depends only on z_A. Since those variables range independently over a nonempty product, each bracket is a constant k_A and Σ_A k_A=c. Set H_A=f_A+k_A. Then Σ_A H_A=F and L_A H_A=H_A. For fixed (q_A,x_A), differentiating H_A(tq_A,x_A)/t shows its derivative is zero, so H_A(tq_A,x_A)=tH_A(q_A,x_A). All H_A are C² because only constants were added to the anchored C² functions. This avoids losing a derivative by treating Euler derivatives as the definition of H_A. □

### Proof of Theorem A, 1⇒3

Define the canonical homogeneous extension to independent, unnormalized positive weights:

    S=Σ_i q_i,   F(q,x)=S E(q/S,x).

This extension is C² and positively 1-homogeneous. For each l, the inherited fixed-partition classification gives

    E=C_l+Σ_(G∈P_l) m_G V_(l,G)(r_G,x_G).

Absorb C_l into each V_(l,G), using Σ_G m_G=1. Then

    F(q,x)=Σ_(G∈P_l) Q_G V_(l,G)(q_G/Q_G,x_G),
    Q_G=Σ_(i∈G)q_i.

Each term depends only on (q_G,x_G), so Lemmas A1–A2 provide a 1-homogeneous C² decomposition F=Σ_(A∈R)H_A(q_A,x_A). Restrict H_A to total mass one to define V_A(r_A,x_A)=H_A(r_A,x_A). Homogeneity gives H_A(q_A,x_A)=Q_A V_A(q_A/Q_A,x_A). Restrict S=1 to obtain conclusion 3 (with C=0, or with an arbitrary common constant redistributed). The inherited ODE argument gives the flow statements. □

### Consequences and the exact interaction boundary

- Crossing 2×2 rows and columns have singleton refinement. The only permitted energies are C+Σ_ij w_ij V_ij(x_ij). Arbitrary row interaction is not simultaneously column-compatible.
- If refinement cells contain several labels, arbitrary nonlinear conditional-weight and position interaction survives **within each such cell**. For example P={123|456}, Q={124|356} has refinement {12|3|4|56}; E=`[w₁w₂/(w₁+w₂)](x₁−x₂)²` satisfies both families.
- For nested partitions, the theorem returns the finer partition, as it must. A one-block partition imposes no additional constraint. Singleton and zero-dimensional factors cause no exception.
- This is an emergent enlargement of the gradient's symmetry, not an enlargement of the generated audit subgroup or a license to apply unapproved audits.

## 4. Row/column symmetries retain odds ratio for general fields

Write the four labels as 11,12,21,22, or 1,2,3,4. In log odds relative to label 4,

    u=(log(w₁/w₄), log(w₂/w₄), log(w₃/w₄)),
    θ=u₁−u₂−u₃=log(w₁w₄/(w₂w₃)).

Row audits translate u by s(1,1,0); column audits translate u by t(1,0,1). Their generated group is exactly the plane ker(u↦u₁−u₂−u₃), equivalently factors (a₁b₁,a₁b₂,a₂b₁,a₂b₂). It preserves ρ=exp θ and does not contain the singleton factor (2,1,1,1).

### Proposition B: full C¹ field classification for this restricted group

A C¹ field commutes with every row and every column audit iff, in these coordinates,

    udot=f(θ,x),   xdot=v(θ,x),

for C¹ functions f and v. Equivalently in weights,

    wdot_i=w_i[c_i(θ,x)−Σ_j w_j c_j(θ,x)],

where c_1,c_2,c_3=f_1,f_2,f_3 and c_4=0; a common addition to every c_i is irrelevant.

**Proof.** Audit differentials are the identity in (u,x), so equivariance is translation invariance along the displayed plane. Its cosets are exactly the θ level sets. Any invariant C¹ coefficient factors through (θ,x), for example by evaluating it on the smooth section u=(θ,0,0). Conversely, dependence only on θ and x is invariant. The weight formula is the inverse log-odds differential. □

### Countercontrol B1: odds-ratio-dependent equivariant field, not a gradient

Take

    udot=(θ,0,0),   xdot=0.

It is smooth and has the explicit all-time flow θ(t)=θ(0)e^t, u₂(t)=u₂(0), u₃(t)=u₃(0), u₁(t)=u₁(0)+θ(0)(e^t−1). It commutes with row/column audits and fails singleton equivariance: (2,1,1,1) increases θ by log 2 and hence changes udot₁ by log 2.

It is not −grad_g E for any C² energy in the stated contract. Directly, in intrinsic simplex coordinates w₁,w₂,w₃ with w₄=1−w₁−w₂−w₃, the Fisher equations would require

    ∂₁E=−θ/α,   ∂₂E=∂₃E=0.

But ∂₂∂₁E=(1/w₂+1/w₄)/α is nonzero, whereas ∂₁∂₂E=0. This violates equality of mixed derivatives. Theorem A reaches the same obstruction without assuming this special field.

### Countercontrol B2: an audit-invariant energy need not have an equivariant gradient

Take the positive smooth energy E=ρ=w₁w₄/(w₂w₃), with positions unused. It is invariant under every row/column audit. Its homogeneous-degree-zero ambient derivative is ∂_iE=ρ ε_i/w_i, ε=(1,−1,−1,1), whose weighted mean is zero. Thus

    wdot=−α ρ (1,−1,−1,1),   xdot=0.

At α=1 and w=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4), a row audit (2,2,1,1) gives weights (1/3,1/3,1/6,1/6). The actual gradient remains (−1,1,1,−1), whereas the pushed old gradient is (−4/3,4/3,2/3,−2/3). The equivariance residual is

    (1/3,−1/3,1/3,−1/3).

The audit is not an isometry of the state-dependent Fisher metric. Invariance of a scalar energy is insufficient for gradient covariance. No nonconstant odds-ratio-only energy satisfies the simultaneous gradient contract.

### Countercontrol B3: a permitted row interaction fails the column audit

Let E=`[w₁w₂/(w₁+w₂)](x₁−x₂)²` on four labels, with no contribution from labels 3 and 4. It has the inherited row-block form and hence commutes with every row audit. At equal weights, x=(0,1,2,3), α=1, its position velocity is (1,−1,0,0). A column audit (2,1,2,1) gives (2/3,−4/3,0,0), so the position residual is (−1/3,−1/3,0,0). No action-generation argument can rescue this interaction. The common-cell example in §3 shows exactly when interaction can survive.

## 5. Exact ET-23 consequence for several fixed cone instruments

This theorem uses ET-23's cone and operation hypotheses, with no metric assumed.

Let K⊂V be nonzero, finite-dimensional, closed, convex, pointed and generating. Let u be strictly positive on K\{0}; B={z∈K:u(z)=1}. Write the canonical indecomposable direct sum as K=⊕_(a∈A)K_a, with positive projections P_a. For each l=1,…,k fix a complete finite positive instrument

    T_(l,y)=Σ_a c_(l,y,a)P_a,   c_(l,y,a)≥0,   Σ_y c_(l,y,a)=1.

The representation is inherited from ET-23. Let P_l partition A by equality of the **entire outcome column** (c_(l,y,a))_y. Let R refine those partitions, Q_H=Σ_(a∈H)P_a, and B_H=B∩range(Q_H).

### Corollary C: common-refinement stability and least joint stable hull

For every nonempty closed convex C⊆B, the following are equivalent:

1. C is stable under every defined outcome of every fixed instrument l.
2. Every extreme point of C lies in one B_H, H∈R.
3. C=conv(⋃_(H∈R)(C∩B_H)).
4. For every z∈C and H∈R with u(Q_Hz)>0, Q_Hz/u(Q_Hz)∈C.

Consequently, the smallest closed convex family containing a specified z∈B and stable under all those instruments is

    conv{Q_H z/u(Q_H z) : H∈R, u(Q_H z)>0}.

**Shortest proof by a tagged instrument (independent reviewer contribution).** Choose positive β_l with Σ_l β_l=1 and define the single instrument S_(l,y)=β_l T_(l,y), whose outcome records both the selected instrument and its outcome. Its sum is I. Two canonical summands have the same full S-column exactly when their columns agree for every original instrument, because every β_l is positive. Thus its equal-column partition is exactly R. A tagged branch has positive mass exactly when the corresponding original branch does, and its normalized posterior is identical: the factor β_l cancels. Consequently stability under the tagged instrument is precisely simultaneous stability under the originals. Apply the inherited ET-23 Theorem 5 and its least-hull consequence. This proves every conclusion, including all zero-mass cases. The tagging is a mathematical reduction, not a claim that the combined instrument is an available physical experiment. □

It makes the contribution level explicit: Corollary C is a direct application of an inherited theorem, not a new general cone rigidity theorem. The following proofs make its concrete refinement and zero-normalizer mechanics visible.

**Alternative proof by extreme points.** ET-23 Theorem 5 says that 1 holds iff, for every l, each extreme point e of C is supported in one P_l-block. Conversely, support in one H is support in a block of every P_l. This proves 1⇔2. Conditioning a finite convex mixture of points in the separate B_H onto an occupied H remains a convex mixture of its H-points, proving 3⇒4. Every branch reweights refinement masses and leaves its conditionals unchanged; hence 4 and convexity imply 1. For the least-hull statement, every stable closed convex family containing z contains the displayed conditionals by 4. Their finite convex hull is compact, contains z by its own summand-mass decomposition, and is stable because each branch rescales each of those finitely many vertices. This proves minimality and existence. □

### Independent conditioning proof, including the zero-mass guard

For two original blocks G∈P_l and H∈P_m, the projections commute and Q_G Q_H=Q_(G∩H). If u(Q_(G∩H)z)>0, then u(Q_Gz)>0, because Q_Gz is the sum of that positive component and other cone components. Therefore the first conditional z_G exists. Its H-mass is

    u(Q_H z_G)=u(Q_(G∩H)z)/u(Q_Gz)>0.

ET-23's equivalence gives closure under the first and second group conditionings, so the result Q_(G∩H)z/u(Q_(G∩H)z) lies in C. Iterate over the finite partitions. At every step, positive final intersection mass guarantees positive prefix mass. If final intersection mass is zero there is no conditional to require. This is a derivation of set membership from the inherited closure theorem, not an assertion that group-conditioning projections are outcomes available in the original noisy instruments.

### Why this does not erase the odds-ratio action guard

On K=R⁴_+, use a row instrument with first-outcome coefficients (3/4,3/4,1/4,1/4) and its complement, and a column instrument with (3/4,1/4,3/4,1/4) and its complement. Each instrument sums to identity and has exactly its declared full-column partition. Every finite branch sequence preserves positive support and odds ratio. Yet the least jointly stable **closed convex** family from an interior p is the entire simplex, by Corollary C.

Already at the uniform p, row and column first outcomes are

    p_R=(3/8,3/8,1/8,1/8),  p_C=(3/8,1/8,3/8,1/8).

Each has odds ratio one, but their midpoint (3/8,1/4,1/4,1/8) has odds ratio 3/4. Thus an orbit invariant does not survive taking arbitrary convex mixtures of orbit points. Boundary vertices additionally require closure or a separate limiting argument; no finite perfect disclosure follows. On nonsimplicial cones, the least hull retains each original within-summand conditional and need not equal all of B.

Corollary C does not need reciprocal branches to be physically available or every nonzero group conditioning to be an admitted observation. It uses precisely closed-convex stability under every defined outcome of the fixed complete instruments. Positivity or one selected outcome alone is insufficient for the inherited barycenter argument.

## 6. A genuine but explicitly augmented cone-gradient application

ET-23 alone does not choose a metric, differentiable correction law, or energy. There is therefore no automatic implication from Corollary C to Theorem A. The following application is valid only after providing the missing geometry explicitly.

For each canonical K_a let B_a° be the relative interior of its normalized base. Choose an affine Euclidean coordinate system x_a on aff(B_a); its coordinate domain X_a is a nonempty connected open convex Euclidean set (a point in dimension zero). The normalized relative interior of K has the smooth global coordinates

    Ψ(w,x)=Σ_a w_a s_a(x_a),
    w_a>0, Σ_a w_a=1, s_a(x_a)∈B_a°.

The inverse is w_a=u(P_a z), s_a=P_a z/w_a. Define, rather than infer, its Riemannian metric by transporting the inherited metric through Ψ:

    Ψ* g_B = (1/α)Σ_a dw_a²/w_a + Σ_a w_a ||dx_a||².

For a partition P of the canonical summands, a positive grouped rescaling R_λ=Σ_a λ_aP_a with λ constant on P-blocks preserves K, and its normalized action is exactly a P-audit in these coordinates. Every such normalized action is a branch of some identity-summing binary positive instrument: choose 0<ε<1/max_a λ_a, take T₁=εR_λ and T₀=I−εR_λ. Both branches are positive, sum to I, and the first normalizes to the required audit. This says “some mathematically permitted instrument,” not “one outcome of a previously fixed instrument.”

Thus, if covariance is required for **all** strictly positive branches from all P_l-block-constant identity-summing instruments, Theorem A applies verbatim with canonical summands as labels. The energy must separate over their common-refinement cells. If the refinement isolates every canonical summand, it has form C+Σ_a w_a V_a(x_a), with arbitrary internal V_a.
