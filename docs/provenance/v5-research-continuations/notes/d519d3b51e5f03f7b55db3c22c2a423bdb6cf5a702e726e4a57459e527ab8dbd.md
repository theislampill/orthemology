## 2. Distribution-free strict-width theorem

The quantifiers are valid as written: for fixed centres, tolerance ε and relative width δ<ε with δ≤1/16, **one explicit policy independent of the component shapes** works for every supported component family whenever S is finite. Conversely S=∞ forces infinite cost for every family and every policy. A uniform-in-δ cost bound as δ↑ε is not asserted.

The shifted report q*=1/2+εa(1−δε) gives exactly

D0((1−δ)a,ε,q*) = D1((1+δ)a,ε,q*)
= εa[δ−ε+εa(1−δε)²].

The coefficient signs make these the two worst endpoint excesses over the interval. Since 0≤δε≤1/64, the squared factor is at most one, and a≤(ε−δ)/(2ε) gives excess at most −εa(ε−δ)/2. Coherence follows directly from 0<1−δε≤1 and a≤1/2. Geometric decay supplies a finite tail cutoff H.

The head/tail switch is correctly driven by the observed selector J_n. If the true component is i and J_n=i, the selected branch is the appropriate empirical head or accepted shifted tail report. Thus the failure indicator is bounded by the classifier error plus the empirical failure indicator on the finite head. The proof does not need the policy to know i.

The classifier analysis in the predecessor only uses δ≤1/16. In particular its protected boundaries, positive 1/n floor, and concentration estimates are independent of the smaller δ≤ε/4 geometry assumption. Reusing its **classification error** bound is legitimate even though its previously accepted centre-report theorem had the narrower width hypothesis. This distinction must remain explicit in any formal claim: the narrower theorem cannot simply be applied at the wider width.

Integrating the inherited empirical bound over that restricted finite mixture gives exactly the added 32 M_head/ε² term. The empty head has M_head=0. ## 3. Worst-case critical and supercritical obstruction

For ε≤δ≤1/16, the critical pair ((1−ε)a,(1+ε)a) lies in each allowed δ-cluster. Its two relevant excesses sum to 2(q−1/2)². If both parameters were accepted, q=1/2; the upper parameter then has strictly positive D1. Thus critical equality does not allow a common accepted report at a positive centre.

The mixture-risk summation is valid. For each fixed zero-word report and seed, at least one half-component is missed in every component. The identity of the missed half can change across components, stages and seeds. No measurable choice of that identity is needed: both half-component likelihoods have the same deterministic lower bound. Consequently all component lower bounds may be added as nonnegative terms under the same policy.

An independent simpler lifetime calculation confirms the lower conclusion directly. Put u_i=(1+ε)a_i. At stage n≥1 the i-th weighted component contributes at least

(a_i/(2Z))(1−u_i)^n.

Summing exactly over positive times gives

(1−u_i)/(2(1+ε)Z) ≥ 3/(20Z),

using ε≤1/4 and a_i≤1/2. Every component therefore contributes a uniform positive amount, and nonnegative Tonelli makes the lifetime count infinite. This calculation even works for ε≤1/4 without intercluster separation; the stricter width theorem embeds it in its own ε≤δ≤1/16 regime. The source’s coarser shifted-window constant exp(−1)/(18Z) is also valid.

For the absolutely continuous construction, each edge strip has width h=εa²/16. The inner upper endpoint is at least a, and all parameters stay inside (0,1). If one report accepted any point of both strips, setting t=q−1/2 would give

t≥(3/4)εa, while t²≤εh=ε²a²/16.

These contradict one another strictly. Hence an accepted set can intersect at most one strip, so one entire strip of component mass 1/2 is missed. The preceding likelihood/geometric argument still applies. This does not rely on an arbitrary-q grid, a singular density, or an atom at zero. Countably summing the positive-width uniform strip measures produces a Borel absolutely continuous probability prior.

The sharpness claim is correctly existential on the bad side. It says the allowed class contains bad component families when δ≥ε, not that every such family is bad. Centre atoms remain valid finite-budget examples when S is finite. Both quantifiers are necessary to the conclusion.

## 4. Critical whole-cluster uniform law

The positive critical claim is limited to ε=δ≤1/16 and uniformly distributed components. The tail cutoff a≤1/8 is sufficient, including equality.

With s=εa(1−ε²), h=2ε²a² and W=4εa², the two reports q_L=1/2+s−h and q_R=1/2+s+h are coherent. Their four endpoint excess expansions in the source are exact. For an independent check, write E=ε²a². Then s²≤E and

2sh+h² ≤ E(4εa+4ε²a²) ≤ E.

Also Wε(1−ε)≥15E/4 and Wε(1+ε)≥4E. These inequalities give nonpositive excesses at the stated outer and inner endpoints (the minus-2sh case is even easier). Monotonicity extends acceptance over [l,u−W] and [l+W,u]. There is a nonempty central overlap because W≤εa/2.

Only the two edge strips can contribute local literal failure, and their total uniform mass is exactly 4a. In the lower strip α≤a(1−ε/2); in the upper strip α≥a(1+ε/2). The empirical comparison to a therefore incurs at least εa/2 one-sided deviation.
