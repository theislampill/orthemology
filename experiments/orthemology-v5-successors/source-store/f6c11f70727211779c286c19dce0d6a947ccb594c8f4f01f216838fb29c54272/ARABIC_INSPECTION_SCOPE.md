## Same-E tests applied after direct inspection

These are inference tests in the programme's vocabulary, not additional historical exposition.

1. A complete producer whose actual efficacy covers every relevant prerequisite would support the original-possession route at its strong scope. The question remains whether an E-explanation of the declared inventory must be such a complete producer. The transfer cannot acquire that strength only at the exclusion step.

2. If one bearer is the productive source of every member of an inventory containing that very bearer, strict productive priority gives the prohibited self-dependence. The active plural configuration does not assign that universal productive role to one of its members. Applying the same exclusion to that configuration requires the collective principle identified in CE-1 §2. It is not a consequence of the individual argument's validity.

3. A received cause considered without its prerequisites is insufficient. A theory adding all of its actual prerequisites is not thereby certified adequate, but its inadequacy does not follow solely from the insufficiency of each cause considered alone. Treating every received prerequisite as unfulfilled merely because no member has original possession would settle the very point under examination. The rootless account's ability to call each prerequisite fulfilled likewise needs substantive warrant; the rival does not win by stipulation.

4. Prohibiting a whole from deriving original sufficiency from merely arranging individually insufficient items can have genuine force. It does not turn the disputed non-well-founded productive relation into a passive arrangement. The active interpretation, the target's full scope, and the adequacy standard still have to remain fixed together.
