# Targeted source control: reasons, creation, and desired consequences

10 October 2026. Received electronic *Shifāʾ* markers 179–193 read continuously by root, including the objection at 184–186 and its subsequent principles. This is inherited-source rereading, not independent source confirmation. The chapter and fifth principle continue; no complete-chapter reading is claimed.

The actual source prevents a collapse of three evaluative objects:

1. A creaturely action considered in itself, approved or disapproved in that respect.
2. Its occurrence together with its consequential relations to other ends.
3. The Creator's own production, with its complete purpose and placement.

At e180 the author explicitly allows approved subordinate action to be unrealised because its occurrence would forfeit a more loved end. Conversely, a disapproved subordinate action can lead to another more loved end. The conclusion about the goodness of God's own production therefore cannot be tested by silently replacing its object with the subordinate act considered alone. This point was sent to the source investigator before finalisation.

The strong phrase at e181 that an unrealised object would have been realised if it contained good occurs inside this qualified comparison. The following text expressly rejects the unqualified propositions that every consequence of existence is good and every consequence of absence is evil. It does not establish joint realisability of all separately attractive goods, a unique complete history, or an externally supplied perfection.

The source is not content merely to assume fixed natural means. At e184–186 it reports the objection that an omnipotent chooser could create favourable effects while omitting harmful ones, including al-Rāzī's formulation concerning fire. The response appeals to the larger architecture of knowledge, voluntary agency, real secondary causes, wisdom and ends. Merely citing the local fire-benefit example as if this strongest objection were absent would misrepresent the unit. This reading neither supplies an independent numerical comparison of global goods nor reopens the earned general Wisdom appraisal.

At e191–193, the distinction between a desired end in itself and an end sought for another end governs the purposive reading. Reported human purposes and original providential purposes can differ for the same history. The text also distinguishes uncertainty or inability behind merely consequential expressions from knowledgeable, powerful divine purpose. None of this alone identifies every caused event as adoption of an expressed claim, or every subordinate outcome as approved in itself. A source-owned attestation inference still needs its particular communicative content and role.

Retained potentially orthogonal observation: e192–193 distinguishes several senses of naskh, including removal of a mistaken interpretation rather than repeal of an established norm. The example separates accounting from punishment. This is relevant to faithful correction and occurrence-sensitive normative interpretation; it is recorded as a source distinction, not yet a new theorem, new application, or proof that a particular modern correction implements it.

No contemporary legal judgement, diagnosis of a person's cognition, new historical authentication, or permission for harmful action follows from these source reports. Root's role here is testing the exact inference and the scope of the investigator's proposed contribution.
