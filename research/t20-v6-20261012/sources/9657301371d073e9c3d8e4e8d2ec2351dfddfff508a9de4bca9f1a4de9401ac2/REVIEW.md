## Exact contextual receipts

1. **Printed550–561, Heraclius inquiry.** The unit begins at550 after Waraqah's report. Questions and commentary concern ancestry, earlier claimants, truthfulness, followers, defections, war, fidelity and teaching. Printed560 reports Heraclius's wish to reach the prophet and his kingship as the stated obstacle. It then shifts explicitly to Abu Sufyan, described as hostile at the time. His certainty clause is “بأن أمر رسول الله ... سيظهر”: that the messenger's cause will prevail. The following authorial “قلت” appraises the question and investigation as yielding “علما جازما بأن هذا هو النبي الذي ينتظره”: firm knowledge that this is the prophet the inquirer expects. Printed561 answers the reported objection attributed to al-Maziri and explains cumulative acquisition. The two subjects, propositions and source voices cannot be collapsed. Nor does the victory clause establish that Abu Sufyan lacked every stronger belief; it only fails to establish that stronger content by itself.

2. **Printed657–664, framing.** The continuous authorial discussion concerns faith, its parts and variation, praise/blame in different respects, received obligations, capacity and deficiency. It does not begin as a general behavioral psychology of all propositional knowledge. Printed662 expressly distinguishes deficiencies that are blameworthy from deficiencies for which the subject is not blamed. This framing blocks an unqualified inference from less outward performance to culpability.

3. **Printed665–668, operative distinction.** Printed665 specifies assent to what God deserves of divinity and the messenger of messengership. It relates this to love, magnification and obedience, allows intervening pride/envy and discusses possible later loss of assent. Printed666 ends by stating that neglecting what knowledge calls for can lead to its removal. Printed667's clear final paragraph says firm assent is followed by some work of the heart, with feasible bodily effects, and compares settled willing plus ability with performance. The sentence crosses into668: if no heart-action follows, the assent is not firm. Printed668 then says heart-action may fail to follow **in its entirety**, due to intervening passions. Thus full incompletion and total inner absence are not interchangeable, and practical conduct is not declared evidentially irrelevant.
