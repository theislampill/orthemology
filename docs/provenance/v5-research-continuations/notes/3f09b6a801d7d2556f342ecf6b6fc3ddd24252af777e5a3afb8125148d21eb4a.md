## External termination and origin completeness

The supplement makes a useful discriminating advance in the objection's formulation. A real B-origin may matter to whole actual production even if it is represented as a primitive relation rather than as a new mode object. Refusing reification therefore cannot by itself escape an independently justified target that includes actual origination. Conversely, merely showing that B's intrinsic act externally terminates in x does not establish that a source complete for x's existence must supply every real actual origin of x.

The strongest endpoint-total rival can grant that A is sufficient for every dependent endpoint, that B genuinely contributes through an unborrowed act, and that the B-to-x relation is primitive. It then denies origin closure rather than denying the relation. There is no contradiction with endpoint sufficiency until the stronger coverage premise is independently warranted. Changing x into x-as-received-from-B inside an explanatory necessity claim changes the respect. Actual reception from B needs B; this does not show that x cannot exist without B.

The affirmative side is nevertheless substantive. If the explanatory promise really concerns the full actual coming-to-be, an account omitting one of its actual efficacies is incomplete in that respect. This identifies why origin closure matters and why no extra mode object is required. What remains unproved is that the earlier existence/source inquiry independently earns this full target and a single complete bearer over it. Naming the stronger condition precisely does not derive it. The supplement acknowledges this, credits the recovered third-tranche anticipation, and claims no new theorem.
