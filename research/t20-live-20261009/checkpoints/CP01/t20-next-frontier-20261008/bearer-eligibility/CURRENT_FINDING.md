# Bearer eligibility: the source has a comparative answer

8 October 2026 UTC. Successor research to the earlier efficacy-warrant assessment and concentration addendum. All earlier reports remain unchanged. T20 remains open; no protected integration or global closure is claimed.

## Changed judgement

The earlier concentration addendum correctly separated possible consolidated work-capacity from each existing bearer's eligibility. It did not yet give the strongest source answer to the latter. The new continuous primary reading materially changes that assessment: **a mere identity-preserving acquisition objection is answered by a comparative-disqualification route**.

For a genuinely pure perfection, the source argues that being inherently incapable of receiving it is worse than lacking it while capable. A proposed absolutely perfect original cannot escape the comparison merely by being assigned an essentially restricted kind. That argument need not make A become B, merge two numerical bearers, or demonstrate a temporal transformation of A into a new more powerful individual.

The remaining disagreement must address the source's unrestricted perfection hierarchy or the classification of the relevant independence as a pure perfection. It must not be presented as though the source has no answer to an essential-receptivity restriction.

## 2. What the source means by eligible perfection

The Akmaliyya's question already presses the central objection: a property can be good for one kind and bad for another, so why transfer created perfections to the creator? The answer does not indiscriminately transfer whatever someone values. It distinguishes a perfection of an existent as such from a kind-relative advantage carrying deficiency. Its concluding comparison rejects an alleged absolute perfection if it improves one respect while worsening another. Eating, for example, remedies the need of an animal but cannot be promoted into self-sufficient divine perfection. Contradictory tasks are also excluded. [Canonical L001–014, L069–080, L258–274;6:68–71,85–88,137–140.]

This gives a three-way distinction:

- **Logical possibility:** the description must be coherent; simultaneous incompatible states or a created self-necessary being are not perfections waiting to be acquired.
- **Pure comparative worth:** the perfection does not owe its value merely to a defect or restricted kind.
- **Attribution to this bearer:** once the relevant necessary being is independently shown to have unrestricted perfection, an essential refusal of such a pure perfection is itself disqualifying.

The source does not claim that every actual being can possess every perfection while preserving all its individuating conditions. It can regard self-sufficiency as superior even though a particular creature cannot become uncreated. Comparative superiority and identity-preserving acquisition are different claims. That is why the nonreceptivity response matters.

## 3. The direct answer to nonreceptivity

At6:88–90, the author reports the objection that lack of a capacity is a privation only in a receptive subject: a stone is neither blind nor sighted in the relevant technical sense. His reply is not merely to insist on a missing predicate. A receptive living being is already superior to a wholly unreceptive thing; possession of the perfection is superior still. Denying receptivity therefore does not protect the proposed divine subject's perfection. [L081–090.]

Dar9:36–39 explicitly gives the charge of begging the question about divine receptivity, then answers it through the same comparison. The author's endorsement of the substance of the comparative argument follows a quotation from al-Amidi; the quotation is not silently treated as independently authored by Ibn Taymiyyah. His further claim that God can animate apparently inanimate things is an additional answer about created objects, not the only basis for comparing perfections. [section707 L001–011.]

Applied to concentration, the strongest reconstruction is:

1. Independent adequate agency is a coherent, pure excellence.
2. A proposed necessary original is not permitted to fall below that pure excellence by an essential limitation.
3. If the proposed A is receptive to it, its constitutive perfection cannot await an outside donor.
4. If A is essentially unreceptive, that is a greater limitation, not an exemption from2.
5. Therefore the proposed restricted A cannot qualify as the independently perfected original at issue.

This is a genuine argument. It does not depend on a literal merger producing the same numerical A. The earlier addendum's insistence that a consolidated profile alone does not prove de re eligibility remains correct; what changes is that consolidation is not the only available warrant.

## 4. Does necessary existence itself establish unrestricted perfection?

The source does argue this; it does not leave maximality as an unannounced definition. Three grounds appear in the Akmaliyya:

- An a-fortiori comparison: necessary, ancient, self-subsistent existence is superior to dependent, originated existence; a pure perfection possible to the latter is therefore not to be barred from the former. The author expressly appeals to their common existence and to the superiority in question. [L035–038;6:76–77.]
- A source-of-perfection argument: what originates and gives a perfection is prior to, and more entitled to, its pure excellence. [L039–046;6:77–79.]
- Direct comparative judgements and the rational content of scriptural examples, alongside the claimed natural recognition of the creator's perfection. [L016–034, L047–068.]

These are philosophical warrants with substantial intuitive and explanatory force. The first is not a theorem of the thin statement that A has no efficient producer. Moving from superiority in existential independence to unrestricted priority in pure excellence is an evaluative metaphysical principle. The source argues for its force rather than deriving it from modal notation alone.

The strongest positive reason is that a wholly underived ground ought not require a more derivative being to supply or exclusively exemplify a basic pure excellence that makes genuine agency possible. Once a pure perfection is eligible, external supplementation would undermine the claimed explanatory self-sufficiency. This is particularly compelling for life, cognition, and productive capacity; it is not merely an aesthetic preference for a bigger property list.

The limitation is exact: a supposed independent source can deny that all higher excellence elsewhere is a perfection missing from its own nature. The source replies that pure perfections are not kind-relative and that essential incapacity is inferior. That reply deserves direct appraisal of the hierarchy and purity classification. Repeating “different essence” does not answer it, but repeating “necessary” does not by itself prove the hierarchy either.

## 5. Executed dependency test: the donor argument's quantifiers

No new finite world was invented. The following inference audit applies directly to the source's donor rationale.

Suppose every realised created pure perfection has some underived source, and any source that genuinely gives that perfection must possess its appropriate perfection at least eminently. From these premises one can conclude that **for each realised created perfection there is an appropriately perfected source**. One cannot infer that **each proposed source possesses every such perfection**. The source witnessing one perfection need not be the source witnessing another.

Adding that A is the original source of all those perfections would validate the A-specific conclusion, but that comprehensive attribution cannot be borrowed without argument when the current target is whether several independent originals can divide or jointly supply original efficacy. Nor does possible consolidation by itself establish that either present bearer originally gives the other bearer's perfection.

This test does not show that the entire source argument is circular. The a-fortiori necessary-being comparison is a separate route. It identifies what that route must do: support pure-perfection eligibility for each proposed necessary original without assuming comprehensive creation by that original in advance. The anti-receptivity reply then blocks a merely stipulated essential-role exemption.

The Akmaliyya itself cautions at6:83 that establishing attributes does not, without additional explanation, refute polytheism. This is an important internal scope control, not a retraction of its unity arguments later in the epistle. [L065.]

## 6. Individual essence and numerical identity

Dar4's continuous discussion rejects the inference that shared necessary existence plus individual distinctness automatically introduces externally caused composition. Each putative bearer has its own particular existence and essence; an abstract common concept is not a numerically shared component requiring individuation from outside. The author insists on the truth of unity while criticizing that attempted proof. He does not thereby endorse plural necessary beings. [sections790–793, especially791 L006–016 and792 L002–014.]

Consequently the source's positive perfection argument must not be replaced by a different claim that all individuating features are defects, that two qualitative equals are numerically identical, or that all necessary beings instantiate one interchangeable concrete essence. Numerical distinctness is not itself the inferior property. The alleged restriction of a pure excellence is what the comparative route challenges.

This also clarifies the scope of “possible for this bearer.” The relevant question is whether the proposed bearer can qualify as underived perfect agency with that restriction. It is not always whether an already fully specified hypothetical individual can survive removing one of its stipulated essential properties.

## 7. Optional activity, relative goods, and actual independence

The full Akmaliyya does not require maximal actualisation of every external good. It positively makes the perfection of particular acts depend on fitting time, wisdom, and willing. Sometimes the absence of the act is the appropriate perfection. It also distinguishes necessary attributes from particular acts and effects; the essence is not the efficient maker of its own necessary attributes. [L071–077, L103–109, L120–134, L147–160.]

Its positive discussion of love, mercy, emotion, and wise discrimination prevents an impoverished conception of perfection as bare causal domination. It rejects projecting the involuntary passivity or need accompanying created emotions onto original agency. [L181–215.] At6:125–130 it nevertheless explicitly ranks self-sufficient whole production above assisted partial production and combines unimpeded efficacy with wise selection. It closes by requiring both wisdom and effective power, not choosing between them. [L216–240.]

A participant-valued cooperation end can therefore be a relative good worth discussing without being a pure perfection that overrides unrestricted agency. Classifying it as relative does **not** prove that choosing it is forbidden: the source itself allows wise choices among particular goods. Conversely its value does not establish that borrowed original efficacy is an acceptable mode of original agency.

The strongest source application says that the relevant perfection is the wise agent's actual effective willing, which must not need an independently original helper to become adequate. Created means do not violate that when their relevant original basis comes from the same original. A peer's unreceived contribution is different precisely at the origin of that operative basis.

The refined rival must meet this actual-agency claim. It cannot survive solely by denying acquisition of a new intrinsic entity or by appealing to the optionality of the particular act. Its best remaining challenge is that nonexclusive manifestation may express two already adequate activities rather than complete either one's agency. Whether that is genuine unborrowed efficacy is the nonabsorption/actual-production question being investigated separately.

For the current full-Q rival, both bearers already have represented solo constructors. Its principal disagreement therefore is not a lack of standing work-capacity that consolidation would repair. An essential-role objection to acquiring solo capacity is weaker than the actual rival and cannot be used to preserve it. The live issue is the adequacy of the coactive undertaking and the source-level status of its complete volition.

## 9. Independent comparative judgement

The affirmative case is stronger after this reading, not merely better documented. I withdraw a generic essential-role or identity-preserving-acquisition objection as a standalone defeater of the eligibility argument. The source has a substantive alternative: essential incapacity for a genuinely pure excellence is itself inferior. A rival must contest the relevant purity or comparative hierarchy, rather than assign an incapacity by type and call the problem solved.

This positive assessment is epistemic as well as deductive. Direct comparative recognition, the a-fortiori priority of self-sufficient agency, and the requirement that original explanation not borrow its effective basis supply real warrant. They are not discounted because they are philosophical rather than mechanically checked. The source's capacity to distinguish genuine pure excellences from dependence-remedying goods and contradictory tasks also improves the argument: it answers a predictable overgeneralisation instead of adopting every apparent advantage.

The donor-quantifier test limits one route without cancelling these others. It is not a reason to declare the entire argument circular. Conversely, the separate a-fortiori route should not be reported as a logical consequence of efficient nonreceipt alone. Its metaphysical valuation is an intelligible positive premise open to substantive assessment.

I therefore strengthen the qualified preference for one comprehensive original source. The strongest surviving coalescent interpretation still has a focused challenge: explain genuinely operative nonexclusive manifestation without completing either agent's actual efficacy from an independent original. Ordinary outcome sufficiency and unreceived intrinsic records are not enough. Yet source assertion of the norm and its philosophical attractiveness are not the same as having demonstrated that every proposed primitive joint manifestation violates it. I do not convert this strengthened preference into a new universal impossibility theorem.

The companion wise-determination report was directly reviewed, without new test-replay credit. Its conditional derivation of the diagonal means under equivalent practical roles is a real improvement in the rival's instrumental explanation. It does not show the richer end is a pure perfection, and it does not change the actual-production issue. This prevents using a now-weakened missing-means objection to inflate the present source-based preference.

The companion nonabsorption report was also directly reviewed. Its exact positive-local-response counterexample does not authenticate a complete original-agent world, and the report correctly says so. It identifies why local responsiveness is not the source's finite productive-augmentation norm. This leaves the two source lanes with the same focused application question: whether genuine peer participation can occur without diminishing or externally completing the first original's actual efficacy. It does not create a further independent eligibility objection.

## Current disposition

The previous review requires a positive correction: **the source has a substantive answer to mere essential nonreceptivity and numerical-acquisition objections.** That answer strengthens the eligibility bridge and removes the claim that only a bare consolidation assertion is available.

The donor quantifier audit also earns a negative discrimination: no permission exists to distribute all created perfections to every original merely from there being an original source for each. The independent comparative hierarchy must justify that broader application.

The remaining philosophical appraisal concerns whether actual independent whole efficacy is a pure perfection at the relevant scope and whether the proposed coactive relation supplies foreign completion. Those questions have become more specific; they are not a reason to repeat the claim that the source overlooked receptivity, optional acts, relative goods, or individual essence. Research remains active while these source/model interfaces are being reconciled.
