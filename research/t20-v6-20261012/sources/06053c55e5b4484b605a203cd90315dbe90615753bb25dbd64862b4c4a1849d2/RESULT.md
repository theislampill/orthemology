## 1. The completed operation

The inherited forward predictive quotient can be extended faithfully to revisions of **named historical evidence factors**, including revisions made after the modeled world has changed. Its correct extension is a current-state/history joint record, not division of a historical likelihood into the current marginal.

Let B be the inherited forward predictive quotient on a finite current latent state X. Let H=(H_1,...,H_m) be the finite historical attributes to which m identified source occurrences attach. The lifted record

    z_h = B r(·,h), for every historical tuple h,

intertwines future action–observation instruments and replacement of those old likelihood factors. If the allowed factor revisions independently separate each historical coordinate, equality of this lifted record is exactly equality of all mixed-continuation mass ratios and target answers over the full joint-law domain. Its admissible cone is a product of copies of the inherited predictive cone.

A decisive negative control uses two historical roots. Both laws have the same current target, the same complete root–root marginal, and the same target/root joint marginal for **each** root. Every isolated single-root revision consequently gives the same target answer. After revising both roots, the answers are instead **9/16 and 7/16**. Thus a collection of individually adequate retrospective summaries need not compose. The parity mechanism is inherited; the new-to-this-packet work is its use as a historical-revision discriminator and the exact mixed-operation interface.

A retained finite Markov factorization computes these revisions by replacing a likelihood at its original time slot and contracting the chain. It need not materialize the full history tensor. A separate zero-support control shows why the existing posterior, even with its original normalizer and the whole posterior path law, cannot in general restore paths previously assigned zero likelihood; the original factorization or another sufficiently rich preconditioning record is then required.

## 3. What is being revised

Fix a finite current latent state X and a finite historical tuple space

    H = H_1 × ... × H_m.

Each H_i is the modeled historical attribute on which an identified occurrence i's likelihood depends. It is not itself the source's identity; occurrence IDs and the map from each ID to its historical slot are fixed metadata. Equal numerical likelihoods or equal historical values do not identify two occurrences. Copies of the same occurrence address its existing slot, rather than creating another factor.

A law r on X×H describes the current joint posterior under the currently accepted model. Its origin may be a finite path model

    r = N(base law × product_i L_i(H_i)),

possibly after marginalizing other historical coordinates. Dependence between different H_i is allowed. The product factorization is a premise about observation likelihoods conditional on the modeled history, not a claim that sources or historical states are marginally independent. Shared reliability or dependence must be included in the base state/factors if the model requires it.

For now each current L_i is strictly positive on its historical alphabet. Reassessing that same occurrence with L_i'≥0, while preserving the base path law and all other factors, gives the ratio

    ρ_i(h_i)=L_i'(h_i)/L_i(h_i).

The revised joint law is

    R_i(r)=N(ρ_i(H_i)r),                              (1)

on positive total mass. This is a change in a stipulated model/accepted factor, not a fresh observation, physical undoing of a transition, erasure of an experienced event, or automatic rational permission to decondition it. The scalar E_r[ρ_i] is the **new-to-old evidence normalizer ratio**, not generally the probability of a newly observed event; ratios may exceed one. All original occurrences and prior model versions remain historical records.

A same-slot replacement is idempotent when implemented as assignment of L_i': after the first assignment, reassigning that same table has ratio one. Blindly multiplying the original ratio again would count the reassessment twice. Successive versions use the ratio of the next table to the current table. Different slots multiply their ratios; a repeated ID still names one slot. This is compatible with the earlier root-identity discipline, but extends its immutable-likelihood model explicitly rather than pretending that model already admitted calibration changes.

**Retained version frame.** The numerical record E(r) is the state for **supplied ratio actions**, not the entire state for literal commands assigning a new likelihood table. Assignment requires the current L_i and its version in order to form L_i'/L_i. The catalogue, occurrence-to-slot map and current tables are therefore retained alongside E(r). Theorems 1–2 compare joint laws in the **same** such frame; under the same successive assignments their table updates and ratios coincide. They do not assert an equivalence between states whose hidden current factor tables differ. This explicit qualification was added following independent-review feedback.

For a concrete guard, (π,L)=((2/3,1/3),(1/2,1)) and (π',L')=((1/3,2/3),(1,1/2)) both yield posterior (1/2,1/2) and original normalizer 2/3. Assigning the named factor to the constant one instead returns π and π', respectively. Thus the posterior record and normalizer cannot replace the current-version table. This is a countercontrol outside the fixed-frame comparison, not a defect of supplied-ratio transport.

The exact quotient theorem below uses strictly positive ratio tests. Nonnegative ratios are supported by its sufficient construction with a zero-mass guard. Restoring support excluded by an old zero likelihood is a different operation, treated in §8.

## 4. Exact composition with the inherited forward quotient

Let the finite permitted future alphabet Σ have nonnegative column-substochastic matrices M_a on X. Let C be a column-stochastic target readout and u the all-ones row. As in the inherited construction, let

    W = span{u M_w, c M_w : w∈Σ*, c a row of C}.

Choose a full-row-rank basis matrix B of W, and retain the inherited identities

    B M_a=T_a B,       u=νB,       C=LB.

Future actions are conditionally functions of current X at this interface: the joint action is M_a⊗I_H. If future dynamics also depend on historical tags beyond X, enlarge X or supply another justified forward state before invoking this model. No hidden dependence is silently dropped.

Write r_h=r(·,h), and define

    E(r)=(z_h)_h,       z_h=B r_h.                   (2)

For a historical weight ρ(h)=product_i ρ_i(h_i), the descended operations are

    future a:       z_h ↦ T_a z_h,
    historical ρ:   z_h ↦ ρ(h)z_h.                  (3)

Their mass and target numerators are

    mass(z)=sum_h νz_h,
    target(z)=sum_h Lz_h.                           (4)

Normalize only when mass is positive. Both maps in (3) preserve

    K_H = product_(h∈H) K,       K=B R_+^X.         (5)

This is a cone in the reduced coordinates, not necessarily an entrywise nonnegative orthant. Signed entries in B or T_a do not invalidate it. Every element of K_H has a joint nonnegative representative by selecting a representative independently in each h-slice. It is exactly (B⊗I_H)R_+^(X×H), with a harmless coordinate-order permutation if slices are stored first.

### Theorem 1: the mixed-operation transport

Equations (2)–(4) compute exactly every finite sequence of the admitted future operations and historical multipliers, including its cumulative unnormalized mass ratio, possible/impossible status and defined target posterior. A multiplier represents a factor replacement only while its declared ratio is defined; support restoration after an old zero is excluded here and handled in §8.

**Proof.** At each h, B M_a r_h=T_a B r_h. Also B(ρ(h)r_h)=ρ(h)Br_h. These identities give the lifted intertwining

    (B⊗I_H)(M_a⊗D_ρ)=(T_a⊗D_ρ)(B⊗I_H).

The target and mass identities follow slice by slice from C=LB and u=νB. Induction proves the assertion for arbitrary finite words. Normalizing after each positive step yields the same normalized endpoint as normalizing the product once; the product of step normalizers recovers the total mass ratio. If total mass vanishes, nonnegativity makes every fine slice zero, hence the descended vector is zero. Conversely zero descended mass on K_H implies zero fine mass in every slice. No posterior is assigned on that branch. □

Future operators and historical multipliers commute **at this joint interface** because they act on different slots:

    (M_a⊗I)(I⊗D_ρ) = M_a⊗D_ρ = (I⊗D_ρ)(M_a⊗I).

This does not say that likelihoods at two time positions commute with physical transitions in a forward matrix product. It says that an already modeled historical variable stays fixed while the current state advances. Nor does it identify workflow composition with tensor concatenation: the displayed intertwining, on explicitly typed factors, is the claim.

### Theorem 2: exact information content on the full joint-law domain

Within one fixed retained catalogue/current-table frame, suppose the permitted positive ratio tests at each historical slot i contain a set R_i spanning every real function on H_i, and include the constant one. Each slot can be independently left unchanged or given one such test. Then for normalized joint laws r,s, the following are equivalent:

1. E(r)=E(s).
2. Every admitted mixed continuation has equal cumulative mass ratio and, when possible, equal target posterior from r and s.

Thus W⊗R^H is the exact required linear observable space for this contract. This is not a literal storage lower bound or a posterior-only classification.

**Proof.** Theorem 1 gives 1⇒2. For the converse, first apply any independent product of historical tests ρ=product_iρ_i, then any forward word w. Equality of mass ratios and posteriors gives equality of target numerators; both vanish if the word is impossible. Thus r−s annihilates

    (u M_w)⊗ρ,       (c M_w)⊗ρ.

The first factors span W. Products of independently chosen spanning functions on the individual H_i span R^H by the elementary tensor-product basis property. Therefore these observables span W⊗R^H, whose coordinate record is (2). Hence E(r)=E(s). □

A finite positive spanning test set is sufficient. For H_i={1,...,d}, use one and the d−1 functions 1−ε1_{h=j}, 0<ε<1, j<d. Their differences from one span d−1 indicators; the final indicator is one minus their sum. These are downward revisions of a positive likelihood, so they do not require a factor greater than one. Only one test per historical slot is needed for the separating family; an infinite sequence of observations is not required.

Equivalently, the mixed invariant span is precisely W⊗R^H: it contains the output rows, is invariant under forward operators and all historical multipliers, and the preceding generating argument shows minimality. That reasoning is a direct specialization of inherited observable closure. Adding history labels alone, without their joint relation to the forward state, does not supply this record.

The full-domain condition matters. A known Markov factorization, a finite reachable family or structural relations among H_i may encode the same record much more compactly. If only a restricted revision family is permitted, replace R^H by the span of its reachable product weight functions; no unrestricted-history lower bound follows. If only normalized target outputs are demanded and mass ratios are omitted, equivalence may be coarser, exactly as the inherited posterior-only examples warn.

## 5. Two separately adequate one-root summaries fail to compose

Let current target Y and historical attributes U,V take signs ±1. With σ∈{+1,−1}, set

    p_σ(y,u,v)=(1+σ yuv/2)/8.                       (6)

All eight atoms are strictly positive. Every proper marginal is uniform, including the **entire** (U,V) joint marginal and both (Y,U) and (Y,V) smoothing tables. This is the inherited parity construction moved to the interior; no new parity theorem is claimed.

Use original likelihood tables L_U=L_V=1/2, and revised tables

    L_U'(u)=(1+u/2)/2,       L_V'(v)=(1+v/2)/2.

These are valid strictly positive binary-event likelihoods, with complementary outcomes available. The ratios for reassessing the identified positive event are ρ_U=1+u/2 and ρ_V=1+v/2. They are not observations of two new events. The historical variables U,V remain unchanged.

Every single-root test f(U)>0, not just this chosen test, gives target law (1/2,1/2) under both p_σ, because Y and U are independent in both. The same holds for every g(V)>0. Its mass ratio is the same in the two laws as well. Consequently even the **complete isolated single-root response functions** fail to distinguish the states.

After both displayed revisions, the common mass ratio is

    E[(1+U/2)(1+V/2)]=1,

whereas

    E_revised[Y]=E[Y(1+U/2)(1+V/2)]=σ/8.

Therefore

    P_+(Y=+1 after both)=9/16,
    P_−(Y=+1 after both)=7/16.                       (7)

The precise continuation failure appears after the first revision: the other smoothing table has changed to

    p_σ^{new}(y,v)=(1+σ yv/4)/4.

Its correlation is +1/4 or −1/4, although the retained pairwise inputs before the revision agreed. A deterministic update of the pairwise-summary record therefore cannot be correct for both. Recomputing the second summary from retained full factors repairs this; keeping its old value does not.

A joint record is sufficient by §4; with no state-changing future operation and C=I_Y, its eight unnormalized entries give the probability-plus-target response tensor. This control also separates normalized target answers, so the failure is not an artifact of requiring mass ratios.

The same pattern occurs for m historical binary roots: an interior interaction proportional to Y product_i H_i is invisible to revisions supported on any proper subset of root slots, and detected when all slots are revised nontrivially. This is a scoped consequence of the inherited parity argument, not a claim that all real histories need unbounded tensor order.

## 6. A factorized realization that does not store an exponential array

For a standard finite nonstationary Markov model, retain the normalized initial law π, column-stochastic transitions P_t, and occurrence-indexed local likelihood factors. Write D_t for the diagonal product of the **current** factor tables at time t. Then the terminal unnormalized law is

    α_T = D_T P_T ... D_1 P_1 D_0 π.                (8)

Each factor is attached to its original modeled state X_t. The ordinary path expansion proves (8): summing path products over the intermediate states is exactly matrix multiplication. For any specified set of historical replacements, substitute the revised tables at those same D_t and contract again. The result equals revising the full posterior path law by the corresponding ratio and marginalizing, whenever all replaced old likelihoods are positive. The equality follows term by term before normalization, including Z_new/Z_old for the normalizers.

This is the classical forward calculation of a hidden Markov model, used here for source-versioned retrospective replacement. Rabiner's 1989 tutorial §III-A, printed pp. 262–263, gives the forward/backward recursions; Murphy's thesis §3.2.1–3.2.2 gives the diagonal evidence matrices and normalization accounting. Only those targeted passages were checked. This packet adds no new forward/backward algorithm. Sources: [Rabiner](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cga/behavior/rabiner1.pdf), [Murphy](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~murphyk/Thesis/thesis.pdf).

For n states and T≥1 transitions, once the diagonal D_t tables are formed, a full forward recomputation uses O(T n²) arithmetic operations and may keep only an n-vector in addition to the retained model/factors. Forming all D_t from m occurrence factors adds O(m n) arithmetic operations. One may instead cache ordered interval products in a balanced tree: an interval stores the product of its right chronological part times its left part. Replacing one factor at a slot containing k_t occurrence factors costs O(k_t n) to rebuild that D_t from its ledger, then O(log(T+1)) ordered matrix products, for O(k_t n+n³ log(T+1)) operations with naive dense multiplication and O(T n²) cached entries. These are arithmetic-operation counts; exact rational bit lengths may grow, and no empirical efficiency comparison is claimed. The direct forward pass can be preferable for small T or sparse models.

Multiple roots at one time retain separate IDs; D_t is rebuilt from their current tables. Rebuilding avoids division by a zero factor. A repeated request assigning the same table leaves D_t unchanged. An audit trail records old and new table versions rather than silently modifying occurrence identity. The model still requires substantive justification of its source-to-state association and likelihoods.

A cached terminal matrix product alone is not the whole revision-ready record: it does not identify every original leaf. A current posterior alone retains still less. The retained factorization is a compact, compositional way to recover the joint revision responses for this restricted model family. It does not contradict §4's exact information equivalence on arbitrary joint laws, and no demand to store |X|×product_i|H_i| floating-point entries follows.

## 7. Why terminal likelihood division can be wrong

Even a single historical revision must act at the correct time. Let an old binary state U have law (1/2,1/2), historical likelihood L=(3/4,1/4), and let a deterministic transition swap its two states before the terminal target Y is read. The current law is (1/4,3/4). Removing that historical factor should return the original law propagated through the swap, namely (1/2,1/2).

Dividing the current target coordinates by the old, untransported vector L instead gives

    N((1/4,3/4)/(3/4,1/4))=(1/10,9/10).

Using the correctly transported old factor (1/4,3/4) happens to work for this invertible deterministic transition. The lifted theorem never asserts an inverse physical transition.

### Strictly positive, noninvertible transition control

Now use three historical states and two current states, with the same fixed transition and old likelihood in both cases:

    A = [[3/4, 1/4, 1/2], [1/4, 3/4, 1/2]],
    L = (1/2, 1/4, 1/3),
    π = (4/11, 4/11, 3/11),
    π' = (9/22, 5/11, 3/22).

Every transition entry, likelihood and prior entry is positive. The transition is noninvertible: its kernel contains (1,1,−2). The old posterior laws at the historical time are

    N(Lπ)=(1/2,1/4,1/4),
    N(Lπ')=(9/16,5/16,1/8).

Both old evidence normalizers are 4/11. Applying A gives the **same** current target law (9/16,7/16). Nevertheless removing L at its original slot gives

    Aπ=(1/2,1/2),
    Aπ'=(43/88,45/88).

Hence no function of the current target law and old evidence normalizer, even with the same known A and L, performs historical removal for both priors. This is an all-prior information obstruction to a current-target-only shortcut, not just an incorrectly oriented vector division. A retained prior/path factorization or sufficient historical/current joint record distinguishes the inputs and computes the correct outputs. The script checks this control with both direct contraction and inverse-likelihood weighting of the joint historical/current law.

## 8. Zero-support revision needs preconditioning information

Let three paths a,b,c have base laws

    π=(1/2,1/3,1/6),       π'=(1/2,1/6,1/3).

Use the same old factor L=(1,0,0). Both models give **the same complete posterior path law** δ_a and the same original normalizer 1/2. Replace that old factor by the constant one. The revised path laws are π and π', which differ, and a terminal indicator of b gives 1/3 versus 1/6.

No decoder from the old posterior law, old factor and original normalizer can perform this support-restoring replacement on both inputs. There is no defined ratio 1/0 on the excluded paths, and assigning one cannot recover their forgotten relative masses. This is an information obstruction, not a numerical division inconvenience. Keeping a finite original factorization such as (8) supports a fresh contraction after leaf replacement, including this case, provided the base model itself was retained. It does not recover an unspecified or lost base model.

In contrast, a positive old factor can be replaced by a zero-inclusive new factor using a well-defined nonnegative ratio; the lifted construction handles it and reports impossibility if every path is killed. A later restoration from that newly zeroed posterior again needs the preserved preconditioning model/record. A sequence of arbitrary support-restoring revisions is therefore not covered merely by a normalized-current-tensor state; the retained base factorization has a strictly stronger revision contract.
