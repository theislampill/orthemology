# Expected score improvement can coexist with certain report failure

This ordinary corollary clarifies the success criterion of the frozen empirical-policy results. It adds no new source-law assumption or kernel claim.

Fix0<epsilon≤1/4 and alpha in[0,1]. Let the policy use the empirical mean of n≥1 iid Bernoulli(alpha) selector observations and issue q=1/2+epsilon times that mean, with multiplication applying only to epsilon and the mean. Write v=alpha(1−alpha). The first two already-proved central-moment identities give, for each fixed truth separately,

    E_data[excessZero(alpha,epsilon,q)]
      = E_data[excessOne(alpha,epsilon,q)]
      = −v epsilon²(1−1/n).

Indeed E[mean]=alpha and E[mean²]=alpha²+v/n. Expanding either literal excess loss leaves the same expression; its linear estimation-error term has zero expectation. The excess loss already averages the independent next selector charge under the constructed contract. The new expectation here is over the acquired training data, a different layer of randomness.

For n>1 and an interior alpha, both expected excesses are strictly negative. This does not say that a realised issued report satisfies both inequalities, and it does not supply an all-issued confidence certificate.

A sharp finite example is alpha=1/4, epsilon=1/4, n=2. The possible reports are1/2,5/8,3/4, with probabilities9/16,6/16,1/16. Every one fails at least one truth's repair inequality, so the probability of literal common repair is zero. Nevertheless the expected excess at each fixed truth is−3/512. Exact rational enumeration of all four acquired words confirms both facts.

There is no conflict with the earlier theorem that randomisation adds variance to quadratic loss. Here the data-dependent report distribution itself depends on the true source law. Its mean is the source-specific quadratic projection and minimax common-repair optimiser and its variance penalty is v epsilon²/n, below the available margin v epsilon² when n>1. A fixed, source-independent lottery cannot borrow this dependence as evidence.

Thus the order of evaluation matters. Averaging the loss for each truth over acquired data is weaker than requiring each issued report to improve every truth. Counting incorrect reports is a further criterion. The three should not be exchanged when comparing the safe abstaining certificate, the always-emitting empirical policy and the finite-model target-good controller.

This corollary is an ordinary algebraic interpretation of the checked first and second moments. It is not an additional historical novelty claim or an independently kernel-checked theorem endpoint.
