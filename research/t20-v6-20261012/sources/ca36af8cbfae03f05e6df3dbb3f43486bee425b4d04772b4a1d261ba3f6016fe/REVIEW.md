## Universal proof checks

1. **Law existence and descent.** For fixed measurable V finite and nonnegative almost surely, 1/P_m(hV) is strictly positive and at most one, so normalization is positive for every finite h≥0. The updated law is equivalent to the original law. For h>0 the multiplier is strictly decreasing, and the covariance identity gives strict descent exactly for nonconstant V when the original mean is finite. If the original mean is infinite, V/P_m(hV)≤1/h gives a finite new mean; an extended-real inequality is legitimate but subtraction is not. Polynomial loss moments through degree m follow from retaining a polynomial denominator term. No coordinate coercivity is implicit.

2. **Audit square.** With one fixed L≥0 satisfying 0<∫L dμ<∞, both compositions have density proportional to L/P_m(hV). Its integral is strictly positive and finite. Unbounded L and zero-likelihood deletion are allowed; an infinite L-integral is not. The same loss, degree, step and baseline must appear on both paths. Recomputed baselines or losses invalidate this multiplication proof. Root’s final fixed-ambient-infimum convention is valid if that finite infimum and ambient space are supplied independently: subtracting it makes additive shifts cancel without consulting the audited support. It does not rescue posterior-support recentering. The supplied three-point zero-likelihood counterexample is exact. Predetermined time-varying scalar factors still commute with the same audit, but this supplies no order theorem for their time discretization.

3. **Local rate.** Taylor's positive remainder yields 0≤a(z)−e^(−z)≤z^(m+1)/((m+1)!P_m(z)). Under E V^(m+1)<∞, dominated convergence proves the stated L1 leading coefficient, including its normalization correction. The coefficient has positive L1 norm for nonconstant V. Merely having m moments does not give the additional order: the atom-plus-Pareto calculation has error bounded below by a positive multiple of h^α for m<α<m+1. Its change of variables, convergence at zero and infinity, and atom normalization lower bound are correct. The one-sided smoothness statements at zero are appropriately distinguished from positive-time smoothness.

4. **Repeated steps and endpoint estimate.** Intermediate normalizers cancel only because the factors are fixed. For a≥b>0, a^n−b^n=(a−b)Σ a^(n−1−j)b^j≤n(a−b)a^(n−1). Combining this with the retained Taylor denominator gives n z^q/(q!P_m(z)^n), q=m+1. Since P_m≥1+z, the supremum for n>q is (q/n)^q(1−q/n)^(n−q), attained at q/(n−q); n=q has supremum one. This proves the n^(−m) uniform scalar bound. Ordered-density mixture normalization gives TV≤(A−B)/A≤(A−B)/B, with B=E e^(−tV)>0. No initial moment is needed. The constant is not uniform over laws, since B may be arbitrarily small.

5. **Initial layer.** An equal n-step recomputation for every terminal t and one common h-trajectory are different procedures. The former can have a uniform n-rate on [0,T] using B_T. In the latter the first grid time uses only one step, and Pareto α<m refutes uniform O(h^m) absent moments. The delayed interval τ≤nh≤T is covered by replacing n^(−m) with h^m/τ^m. There is no contradiction.

6. **Observables and tensors.** The endpoint uniform scalar estimate integrates against any initially integrable |Q|, requiring no mixed V moment. For V^s, multiplying by (nz/t)^s and maximizing z^(q+s)/(1+z)^n gives the supplied constant for n>q+s (also n=q+s when this is an integer). The exact weighted numerator is finite by exponential damping and the approximate one by the error bound. Normalization adds exactly the stated exact-law expectation term. Observables controlled by D(1+V^s) follow; arbitrary coordinate polynomials do not. For V=||x||², the denominator degree mn makes all state monomials through total degree 2mn integrable, and the weighted endpoint result covers each fixed polynomial for sufficiently large n. This is not reconstruction from divergent or discarded moments.

7. **Determinacy.** If ν≤Cμ and μ is polynomial-moment determinate with all necessary moments, then Cμ−ν+ν' is a positive finite measure with the same moments as Cμ whenever ν' shares ν's moments. Determinacy forces ν'=ν. Equivalently the normalized version in the author report is a probability. This argument is valid in R^d. Bounded-density updates therefore preserve the conditional premise; they neither prove that an indeterminate input is determinate nor give an effective reconstruction. Constant V is an immediate countercontrol. Rational damping is polynomial, so the Pareto tail and missing exponential moments are correctly retained. The compactly supported finite-summary examples correctly distinguish full-sequence determinacy from finite-summary sufficiency.
