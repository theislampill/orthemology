# Zero-probability audit: an exact finite boundary

10 October 2026 UTC. New local derivation/application check; classical finite conditional-probability mathematics, not a claim of field novelty. No source-veracity conclusion, protected edit or T20 closure follows.

Let Theta be finite with at least two elements. Fix p(theta)>0, summing to one. Let P0(theta,A=0)=p(theta) and P0(theta,A=1)=0. Thus the declared baseline excludes the audit event A=1.

For any strictly positive probability vector q and any epsilon satisfying 0<epsilon<min_theta p(theta)/q(theta), define

P_epsilon(theta,1)=epsilon q(theta),
P_epsilon(theta,0)=p(theta)-epsilon q(theta).

Both cells are strictly positive, the theta marginal is exactly p, P_epsilon(A=1)=epsilon, and conditioning gives P_epsilon(theta|A=1)=q. The total-variation distance to P0 is exactly epsilon, since the absolute differences in the two audit columns sum to 2 epsilon.

Choose two distinct positive q vectors. The corresponding positive models approach the same baseline, retaining exactly the same prior, yet the audit-conditioned posteriors approach different limits. Therefore conditionalisation on this impossible event has no unique continuous extension selected by the baseline alone. An arbitrary smoothing or small-error permission does not determine a revision policy; the new conditional law needs additional substantive modelling support.

The result is not that a fixed well-specified positive audit model is ambiguous. Its posterior is unique. Nor does it say a subject can never rationally revise a probability-zero assessment. It locates what the original model fails to supply when model revision is required.

If p is instead an already-observed retained-history posterior, the same proof applies on its full positive support. No inference is made about hypotheses excluded by that support. One must not silently change the target proposition, the source's claim of exceptionless veracity, or the audit's authentication to force a positive likelihood. A hypothetical fallible-source model gives no defeater credit against the intended stronger claim without a warranted mapping.

This boundary sharpens the operational distinction between inverse removal of an accepted factor, lawful positive-probability audit conditioning, and revision of the model's support. It does not provide a full epistemology or an actual source assessment.
