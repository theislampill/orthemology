# Original actuality and existential necessity

## Result

The strongest positive route is not that an actual root has no incoming arrow and is therefore necessary. It is that received actuality calls for an adequate actual supplier, and that an ultimately adequate supplier cannot leave its own whole existence both genuinely contingent and explanatorily unaccounted for. A genuinely intrinsic **existential** sufficiency would close that latter demand. Conditional possession of a nature, even essential independence whenever a bearer exists, would not.

This inquiry strengthens that route at a particular point. A contingent root cannot explain its whole existence by an exercise of its own already instantiated power: that explanation presupposes some of the very actuality it promises to supply. This is a reason to distinguish intrinsic existential sufficiency from self-production. It also explains why denying self-production need not deny a necessary source's contingent acts. The acts are exercises of an actual bearer, not a means by which an otherwise absent bearer makes itself actual.

That result has a strict limit. A candidly brute contingent root does not offer the impossible self-production. A genuinely non-well-founded productive theory does not offer a first powerless instrument. A holistic or noncausal account need not accept the source advocate's whole-existence explanation type. None is refuted by the self-production argument alone. The necessary-source argument therefore retains two substantive warrants: ultimate productive completion against wholly received being, and existential explanatory adequacy against autonomous contingency. They must be defended, not hidden in the word *independent*.

The positive discrimination is nevertheless substantial. A particular's real need can be recognized before adopting an abstract principle about every possible fact. That recognitional starting point makes source inquiry a response to actual received being rather than an imposed demand that everything have a cause. Original existential sufficiency then offers a kind of closure that a brute contingent basis deliberately withholds; a real non-well-founded account offers distributive supply that an inert set of relay rules lacks; and a developed holistic essence may supply genuine global actuality while moving, rather than eliminating, the question of its modal status. Those are different explanatory achievements. Neither a blanket parity verdict nor an unearned contradiction claim is justified.

This is a continuation of the same Sixth foundational inquiry. It adds no canonical status, no new R5 defeat, no mathematical-foundation theorem, and no proof that a proposed rival is metaphysically possible. It does not derive one common bearer, Knowledge, Will, or the revealed divine identity from necessity alone.

## 1. Begin with the real episode and keep its dependence exact

Take the admitted actual case: a thinker carries out a genuinely warranted correction. The episode is not merely an allowed sentence in a calculus. Its bearer, powers, evidence and exercise are actual. If the exercise is productively received, that reception must be established for the respect being explained. Neither the episode's truth nor its conformity to an objective standard is itself an efficient-causal relation.

The previous world-bridge inquiry already distinguishes truth, warrant and productive supply. Its disappearance control also matters here: a possible situation in which the entire basis and every thinker are absent is not thereby a situation in which a thinker uses the same method and believes falsely. We do not obtain existential necessity merely by invoking the actuality of knowledge.

The first genuine advance beyond bare occurrence comes when the thinker or the relevant exercise is shown to receive its actuality. A supplier must be actual to supply anything. A conditional rule saying what would happen if powers were instantiated does not instantiate them. This secures an actual explanatory demand. It does not yet secure a first source, a singular common source, or a necessary source.

The reception must also be distinguished from passivity. A dependent organism can possess genuine intrinsic powers; an actual agent can exercise a power received through its origin without being a tool that merely channels another agent's token exercise. The source argument need not deny this. Indeed, making created causes causally idle would weaken its account of the actual episode. What is received can be an active nature. The question is how that nature is actual, not whether its effects are fictitious.

Consequently, the following argument is too quick: every dependent is a passive conduit; conduits cannot originate power; therefore every actual dependent requires an original source. The first premise is not given by dependence. A stronger argument must concern the existential possession of active powers, not replace every active recipient with an inert relay. This distinction becomes decisive against the serious infinite rival.

## 2. What the primary rationale supplies

In the Aṣfahāniyya discussion, Ibn Taymiyya separates proving a necessary existent from proving the intended creator. He starts with observed origination, then argues that existence through oneself is sufficient through the existent itself: «إذ نفسه كافية في وجوده». He considers both an essence/existence distinction and their identity; the argument is not restricted to the former. Its next step concerns the source of all possibles. Its subsequent critique refuses indiscriminate composition-based attacks on real attributes. These are distinct argumentative moves, not one definition that already contains the entire theological conclusion. [Sharḥ al-ʿaqīda al-Aṣfahāniyya, hosted section 001, paragraphs corresponding to lines 61–76 and 82–99](https://www.islamicbook.ws/ageda/alaqidt-alasfhanit-001.html).

The complementary Darʾ passage gives a crucial methodological guard: proof of having no efficient producer must not acquire every additional meaning associated with a term for intrinsic necessity. Inseparable attributes and their bearer can be mutually conditioned without being each other's efficient producers. [Darʾ taʿāruḍ al-ʿaql wa-l-naql, displayed 3:110–112](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/496/تعليق-ابن-تيمية).

These are verified electronic primary-text passages, not manuscript collation. The Arabic author's rational-immediacy judgments are part of his position; they do not establish a contemporary consensus. The argument below is our reconstruction and evaluation. It preserves his positive idea while making its disputed existential commitment answerable to its strongest opponents.

The strongest reading is this: an existent which is actual through itself has a positive mode of actuality sufficient for its own being. It is not merely something whose producer happens to be missing from our records. Such intrinsic sufficiency also does not mean that it performs an efficient act upon itself before it exists. The explanation is in what the reality is, not in a self-manufacturing event.

This reading is worth defending because it offers an intelligible difference between two stopping points. At a brute contingent stopping point, the actuality is admitted but the possibility of its complete absence remains an unexplained alternative. At an intrinsically existentially sufficient stopping point, the proposed reality itself excludes that alternative. The second is meant to explain why the source does not need an actualizer, not to exempt an arbitrarily selected favorite from a rule applied to everything else.

Yet the positive reading cannot be recovered from identity alone. Every actual thing is itself. If positing its identity means positing that very thing as actual, then its nonexistence is excluded only under that supposition. If the phrase instead means that what the thing is unconditionally suffices for its existence, it expresses the stronger existential thesis under examination. It would be unfair to dismiss the source merely by choosing the weaker reading; it would be equally unfair to regard the stronger reading as established by the weaker one.

### 2.1 Faqr and ghinā: recognition before an abstract PSR

A further primary passage sharpens the positive starting point. In Majmūʿ al-fatāwā 2:9–11, Ibn Taymiyya describes need (*faqr*) and sufficiency (*ghinā*) as intrinsic to their respective realities, expressly denies that every matter needs a cause, and allows particular knowledge of dependence before thoughts of contingency or origination. His example even allows uncertainty about the soul's eternity. He claims fitrī recognition of the particular unique creator, not only an indefinite supplier. His metaphysical statement speaks of created substances; createdness cannot serve as our neutral premise for proving itself. [Majmūʿ al-fatāwā, displayed 2:9–11](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/131/?idfrom=&idto=&start=).

The intended object of need is therefore the concrete self as existentially received, not merely its appetite for food, its lack of expertise, or its emotional desire for help. Ordinary needs may occasion reflection, but they do not deductively establish whole-existence dependence. The passage's positive epistemic proposal is that this stronger received manner of one's actuality can itself be recognized. That proposal must be assessed as a claim of basic rational knowledge. It is neither a deduction of createdness from a premise already labeling everything created nor an impartial proof that every thinker already knows the complete theological conclusion.

There is a philosophically important idea here independent of accepting the passage's full theological identification. In a particular case, we can understand a real inability to supply the very actuality being exercised. A newly living individual did not exercise prior agency to originate its own existence. That observation rules out self-origination; positive reception must still be known from its actual productive history, not merely from the absence of self-production. An acquired cognitive exercise can genuinely use a capacity and evidence whose availability it does not itself provide. Understanding these concrete dependencies need not await a theory quantifying over every contingent fact. Nor does it require thinking that the recipient is unreal or its exercised power passive.

The epistemic claim is not that every feeling of dependence is infallible. Hunger, vulnerability or a desire for support can occur without revealing a complete existential source. What matters is warranted recognition of a particular dependence in the stated respect. Evidence of origination, actual support and the limits of the recipient's productive contribution can support that recognition. The ordinary legitimacy of such causal understanding is not annulled because one has not yet settled the metaphysics of all possible ultimate grounds.

This gives the shared source inquiry a positive world-directed foothold when the recognition is warranted: **this actual received possession requires the actuality on which it really depends**. That is stronger than a merely hypothetical demand for a possible source. It can be immediate in the sense of not being inferred from a prior universal proposition, while still admitting articulation, checking and correction. With only the occurrence of a thought conceded, the stronger receivedness premise is not yet secured. With receivedness secured, an actual supplier follows, but identifying it as the ultimate common bearer or as Allah requires more than that shared local premise. This reconstructive minimum is deliberately narrower than the primary author's full recognitional claim. The Third and Sixth local-causal results already support the general method; the new passage gives the intrinsic-need/intrinsic-sufficiency contrast its proper source context.

The metaphysical claim is correspondingly stronger than *we happen not to have made ourselves*. Faqr, on the proposed reading, concerns how the bearer has its actuality: it does not become existentially autonomous merely because its power is genuinely its own or because every currently needed condition has been met. A supplied actual power can be complete as a power for its local exercise while remaining received as to its existence. There is no need for another event which first manufactures the abstract property of being needy; dependence is the manner in which the concrete recipient is actual.

Ghinā must be read with equal discipline. It is not merely prosperity, local operational independence, or the absence of an observed benefactor. Existential ghinā means that the actual source does not stand in need of further existential supply. If a particular candidate still requires an outside actualizer for the very efficacy it contributes, the recognized need has been relocated rather than fully discharged. This is the positive reason to seek an original adequate source; it is not a desire to avoid long explanations.

Can such ghinā be independently recognized? Sometimes local sufficiency can be recognized directly in an actual producer relative to a specific task. Absolute existential sufficiency is a stronger claim. The local case alone does not make the ultimate source's entire intrinsic nature perceptually accessible. Here the positive route is an explanatory inference from the received manner of this reality to a source adequate in the same respect, followed by a rational assessment of what genuinely complete adequacy would mean. A non-inferential rational judgment of the faqr/ghinā contrast can be offered as a warrant for that assessment, subject to clarification and defeating reasons; confidence alone is insufficient, and citing the source's assurance does not force the judgment on every interlocutor.

This is a real evidential position between two extremes. The source advocate need not suspend every particular causal judgment until a universal PSR has been proved. It also may not extend the scope of a particular judgment merely because the full divine conclusion feels immediate. Its rational claim is that an account which explains received possession through a positive non-received mode answers the recognized need in a way a description of endlessly relocated need does not. That is a defeasible explanatory reason for original actuality, answerable to the actual infinitist's account of completion. For necessity, the additional claim is that adequate intrinsic **existence**, unlike locally adequate action, cannot be merely permitted by a nature whose instantiation remains unaccounted for. This is where the Aṣfahāniyya rationale has force beyond a bare modal label, and where the brutalist or noncausal rival must meet it.

## 3. Four questions that must not collapse into one

First, **is the bearer actual?** An actual supplier must be more than a specification. The admitted episode can lead to actual antecedent resources when its reception is warranted.

Second, **is its actuality unreceived?** This concerns whether another actually supplies the being under discussion. A demonstrated absence of external reception is stronger than a failure to observe a producer. Still, it is a causal or existential-dependence claim, not yet a modal conclusion.

Third, **is its unreceived mode essential?** One may claim that, wherever this very bearer exists, it is unreceived. This rules out its being manufactured elsewhere. It still leaves open a situation in which it does not exist at all. Strengthening actual autonomy into essential autonomy therefore does not by itself repair the necessity inference.

Fourth, **is the bearer's nature existentially sufficient?** This says more than that an existing bearer necessarily has its intrinsic character. It says that what the bearer is excludes its complete nonexistence. Combined with the actual witness already obtained, it yields necessity of that witness. Without the actual witness, a description of an allegedly necessary being remains a proposed description; without the existential force, essential autonomy remains conditional.

There is a further converse mistake. Necessary existence by itself need not be unreceived existence. A bearer might exist in every relevant possibility because another source necessarily supplies it. Hence neither the modal frequency of its existence nor the permanence of its effects proves original possession. What the proposed necessary source needs is a defended mode of existence, not just a modal status word.

Nor does having every actual existential condition fulfilled settle the issue. Every actual contingent bearer has whatever conditions are actually required for its existence fulfilled. The distinctive claim is that this bearer's complete existential sufficiency is intrinsic and not contingent upon another supply, not merely that nothing is currently missing.

## 4. Received efficacy and the actual infinite rival

The strongest infinitist grants actual members, actual powers and genuine reception at every link. Each supplier already has what it supplies through another actual supplier. There is no first member waiting to receive power from nothing, and no temporal task of constructing infinitely many links one after another. Oberle directly challenges the inference from removing a first cause in a finite instrumental example to rejecting such a genuinely infinite series. His discussion also treats grounding; that relation must not be silently identified with efficient production. [Oberle, “Grounding, infinite regress, and the thomistic cosmological argument,” §§2–3, author PDF, zero-based pages 6–9](https://thomasoberle.weebly.com/uploads/3/8/1/8/38187281/thomist_paper.pdf).

Grant that starting point. The source advocate's best objection is then not that none of these bearers is actual. It is that the proposed account has repeatedly located received possession without identifying what makes possession available in the first place. If the question is only which actual supplier explains this local receipt, the rival answers it. If the question is complete ultimate provision of the actuality employed throughout, the source advocate argues that the answer keeps presupposing the same kind of supplied actuality.

This objection becomes demonstrative only with a further doctrine: a completely received field cannot itself be the exhaustive source of the actuality received. That doctrine is not irreflexivity, transitivity, or the maxim that causes must exist. Nor is it established by counting links. It is a substantive account of productive completion. The previous tranche already identifies this obligation; we do not rename it as a new theorem.

There are positive reasons for accepting it. A route of supply and the possession of the resource supplied are different explanatory roles. Extending a route can explain more of the actual origin while leaving its existential enablement conditional. An originally actual source can exercise a power without first borrowing the very possession involved in the explanation. This changes the manner of explanation, rather than merely shortening its length. Moreover, the principle has the same force against a finite circle of pure reception and an infinite line; its motivation is not hostility to infinity.

But these reasons have to meet the rival's reply: each dependency is real production, not merely a conditional instruction. The infinitist claims that being actually supplied by another actual recipient is a complete local possession, and that an infinity of such real supplies is an admissible concrete order. Nothing requires the rival to regard those existents as less real. To refute it, one must justify the ultimate-completion requirement, or show that its claimed productive relation cannot have the specified global realization. Neither follows from a diagram.

This is also why the source argument should avoid using *borrowed* in two senses. In one sense, a recipient really has its own power because another supplied it. In a stronger sense, the recipient's exercise is an instrument of another original exercise. The stronger description already brings an original possessor into the kind of relation asserted. A demonstration that the actual target has that stronger structure would be significant evidence. It is not supplied by calling any existential dependence borrowing.

## 5. A substantive infinitary reply, rather than more relay equations

Morganti develops an alternative to an all-or-nothing transmission picture: an infinite dependence structure can provide a global constitutive account. His convergence analogy is not a literal claim that things are fractionally existent; he separates reality from the finite process of analyzing it. He also expressly stops short of claiming to answer why there is anything at all. This is a developed metaphysical proposal, not an assertion that an empty relay diagram activates itself. [Morganti, “Dependence, Justification and Explanation: Must Reality Be Well-Founded?”, §§3–4, institutional postprint, zero-based pages 5–10](https://iris.uniroma3.it/retrieve/e397d80e-d5d2-b0de-e053-6605fe0a1c76/ERKE%2010.1007_s10670-014-9655-4.pdf).

For the present inquiry, distinguish two uses of this proposal. A constitutive explanation can make sense of a fully actual continuum or hierarchy without requiring our analysis to terminate. That defeats an argument from finite human comprehension to finite metaphysical structure. It does not by itself show what productively supplies a particular received exercise, or why the concrete hierarchical reality is actual. The objection is about explanatory type and target, not a demand that the rival finish an impossible calculation.

The rival can strengthen itself. It may say that the global order is an actual productive field, with intrinsic capacities that genuinely produce the thinkers and their exercises. Then the account has supplied more than transmission: it has offered original actuality at the level of the field. This is a real concession to the source inquiry if the field is unreceived, but no concession to a separate creator, a single simple bearer, or necessity. Its holistic nature may be complex and its existence may still be claimed contingent.

Alternatively, the rival can keep every concrete member existentially received and deny that any extra field supplies them. Then the issue remains genuine non-well-founded production, not a tacitly admitted original whole. A proposition about all the members is not automatically one more concrete entity. The source advocate may demand an account of all their actual received being without reifying an aggregate, but may not award itself a new independent whole by putting the members in a collective sentence.

These alternatives are not evasions by definition. They make different claims about the world. The substantive pressure is to identify where positive actuality resides, what receives it, and whether the relation asserted really answers the target. An infinitist who supplies a constitutive hierarchy has answered more than the passive-law objection; one who claims efficient ultimate completion still owes the additional bridge.

## 6. Could original actuality be contingent without reception?

Yes in the limited but important sense that a candid proposal of this sort has not been shown contradictory by the accepted local causal premises. No in the sense of being an established actual or metaphysically possible world. The distinction is essential to fair comparison.

The strong proposal says that a concrete basis B exists, really has its own active nature, receives no existence from anything else, and could altogether have failed to exist. Its downstream powers may be essential; its origin field may be closed; its thinkers may genuinely understand. It does not need to posit an event at which B appeared out of nothing. It need not hold that B's possible absence includes a false believer. It may even hold that B is essentially unreceived whenever it exists. The residual primitive is B's being actual at all.

This rival refuses the inference from modal contingency to reception. It need not refuse every cause, every explanation, or objective truth. In particular, the commonplace success of causal explanations within the field does not logically settle whether that field's own whole existence is explicable. The positive source advocate must defend a scope generalization, not accuse the rival of denying the local data it accepts.

The candid rival therefore cannot be refuted by saying that B would otherwise have to produce itself. It says precisely that B is unproduced. The self-production contradiction rules out one attempted explanation; it does not rule out declining that explanation.

Nevertheless the rival's concession is substantial. Its intrinsically active nature explains how B acts once actual. It does not yet explain B's actual existence rather than its possible complete absence. Calling the nature *intrinsic*, *fundamental* or *autonomous* does not supply that missing existential force. The source advocate is entitled to press this question provided the same question is pressed against its own proposed source.

### Why a power cannot manufacture its own whole bearer

Consider a proposed repair: B's own active power makes B exist contingently. If this is efficient production by an actual intrinsic exercise of B, then some actuality of B is already required for that exercise. An explanation which promises to supply B's **whole existence** cannot leave that presupposed actuality outside its supply. Producing it by the same exercise would make the exercise dependent upon what it is supposed originally to actualize. The repair has not supplied an original account.

The premises are independently intelligible: an intrinsic exercised power requires an actual possessor; a purported efficient origin of that possessor's whole existence cannot merely use the same actuality as an already operative productive prerequisite; and a circular presupposition does not discharge that promised origin. The last two state a noncircularity requirement for **whole-existence efficient origination**, not a ban on every mutual condition. No premise says that B exists necessarily. Thus the objection is not a disguised inference from necessity to necessity. A coherentist who rejects that requirement contests the explanation standard itself; bare logical contradiction has not been extracted from the mere coexistence of bearer and act.

Its scope is equally modest. It does not forbid a living thing maintaining a later state through an earlier exercise. It does not refute beginningless self-maintenance merely by calling all stages one bearer. That proposal reinstates a non-well-founded history and must face the completion question there. Nor does it refute a process ontology in which the alleged bearer's acts constitute it rather than being exercises of an already supported bearer. That ontology changes the explanatory relation and needs a separate argument.

The genuine increment is an asymmetry: a source's exercise can account for a contingent effect without accounting for the source's entire actuality. Hence the reason for rejecting causal self-origination does not require every original source to be changeless or every exercise necessary.

## 7. The strongest positive necessity argument, with its burden visible

The source advocate can now give a connected argument rather than a modal slogan.

1. There is actual received productive being in the admitted episode.
2. An ultimately adequate productive account of its received being requires actual supply, not merely an uninstantiated rule.
3. A wholly received account does not ultimately discharge the requirement of actual supply; an original actual basis is needed. This is the defended productive-completion premise from §§4–5, not a theorem of local causality.
4. Suppose a particular original bearer g has been independently identified within that account. Its whole existence is not externally received.
5. If g's whole existence were genuinely contingent, it would remain an appropriate target of existential explanation. This is an explanatory claim about contingency, not the claim that absence causes presence.
6. Its actual existence cannot be adequately explained by a merely conditional nature or by an intrinsic causal exercise which already requires its actuality.
7. An adequate account of such a bearer must therefore either exhibit intrinsic existential sufficiency or identify a further supplier. This is an additional explanatory-exhaustiveness claim, evaluated below.
8. The latter conflicts with the established original status in the same whole-existence respect. The former excludes g's absence. Therefore this same actual bearer exists necessarily.

Premises 5 and 7 jointly rule out brute original contingency. They are not derived from premises 1–4. This does not make the argument logically circular: neither premise names God, asserts the desired source's existence, or exempts a privileged bearer. They state general explanatory commitments which also constrain an impersonal or holistic ultimate basis. But a premise can be noncircular and still be disputed. Their independent rational force is the central modal question.

The best defense of premise 5 is that permitting complete absence while affirming actuality leaves a real existential contrast. It is not answered by detailing what the bearer would be like if present. The defense of premise 7 is that actuality cannot be supplied by a mere uninstantiated possibility, and that a bearer which does not owe its being to another must have its existential sufficiency intrinsically if it is to have such sufficiency at all. This favors a positive ultimate nature over an unexplained occurrence of that nature.

There are two candid limits. First, judging a question appropriate does not prove that it has an answer. The brutalist denies that reality must satisfy this explanatory demand. Second, premise 7 is stronger if *supplier* means an efficient concrete source than if it means any metaphysical explanation. A noncausal account may claim to explain contingent concrete actuality without a further efficient producer and without an intrinsically necessary concrete bearer. It must be evaluated on that stronger formulation rather than excluded by a terminological choice.

Thus the honest deductive endpoint is precise: **given ultimate productive completion, a particular original bearer, and exhaustive nonbrute existential adequacy in the specified sense, the bearer is necessary**. The research contribution is not this conditional deduction itself. It is the defense and exact discrimination of what those premises demand, why self-production cannot substitute for intrinsic sufficiency, and why the strongest rivals contest different parts of the argument.

## 8. Noncausal essence and indeterministic explanation are genuine tests

Dasgupta distinguishes facts which lack a ground despite being apt for one from essential facts for which that request is inappropriate. His argument for essential existence additionally assumes that existence can be explained only through existence facts. His §13 expressly contrasts conditional, “Kantian” essence with existence-entailing, “Anselmian” essence using free logic and factivity. That distinction is relevant prior work, not an invention of this paper. His full rationalism entails necessitarianism, which this inquiry does not adopt. [Dasgupta, “Metaphysical Rationalism,” §§2–4, 9 and 13, printed pp. 382–388, 396–398 and 402–403](https://shamik.net/papers/dasgupta%20metaphysical%20rationalism.pdf).

This distinction helps the positive argument: an ultimate account need not make the source efficiently cause itself. It can appeal to what the source is. There is no demonstrated general prohibition on an essence having existential content; a true, factive existential essence would do more than an invented description. But there is an important test. An essential truth such as *whatever is B has character F* can be true without settling whether B is instantiated. To do the required existential work, an essence must carry an unconditional existential commitment, or another bridge must connect it to actuality. The question is the truth and warrant of that content, not a rule forbidding it merely because it is existential.

A less familiar rival rejects necessitation in the explanatory relation. Pearce argues that rationalists who accept necessary autonomous grounds and genuine contingency should permit total grounds which do not necessitate what they ground. He distinguishes this from merely omitting enabling conditions. His view concerns metaphysical grounding; it is not a ready-made efficient production mechanism. [Pearce, “Metaphysical Rationalism Requires Grounding Indeterminism,” §§1–4 and 7](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-american-philosophical-association/article/metaphysical-rationalism-requires-grounding-indeterminism/8DF300A3BD3E5F891F457108BDEFA47E).

Applied here, the rival might propose a necessary autonomous essential fact that non-necessitatingly grounds a contingent concrete basis. That would be neither a bare contingent brute nor a concrete bearer efficiently manufacturing itself. The source advocate cannot reject it simply by announcing that all sufficient explanations necessitate, while preserving non-necessitated divine acts elsewhere.

This proposal must not be misclassified as a counterexample to the user's strong complete-unborrowed-ground claim. If *received* includes every derivative mode of existential actuality, the grounded concrete basis is received in that broader sense. It has not granted complete unborrowed whole-existence sourcehood and then defeated the modal conclusion. Instead, it contests identifying the ultimate autonomous ground with a concrete productive bearer. If *received* is restricted to efficient reception, it challenges the passage from absence of efficient reception to broader existential autonomy. Either way, the relation and target must remain fixed. A further ground cannot be quietly acknowledged in the rival and excluded from the author's definition of its independence.

Two discriminations remain. If the alleged ground is only a condition governing already actual things, it has not reached the whole-existence target. If it genuinely explains the basis's existence, the account must specify how a noncausal existential explanation supplies the actuality on which the productive episode depends, and whether it ultimately posits a concrete existent, an abstract essential truth, or a plural structure. An insistence that only concrete actuality can efficiently produce is sound as a restriction on efficient production; it does not itself establish that every ultimate existential explanation must be efficient.

This is an outstanding bridge, not a vindication of noncausal spontaneous instantiation. The rival must furnish real explanatory content and independent warrant. Still, once it is admitted as a serious proposal, the positive necessity argument must defend its exhaustiveness premise rather than assume that every alternative is either a cause or nothing.

## 9. Holistic actuality does not settle its own modality

Schaffer's priority monism is a useful concrete resource: the material whole is prior to its dependent parts, which remain real. His account invokes entanglement and possible atomless complexity; it is not the thesis that no parts exist. His stated object domain also excludes deities and spirits. The thesis that whole-priority holds necessarily is distinct from the claim that this numerical cosmos exists necessarily. [Schaffer, “Monism: The Priority of the Whole,” §§1.1–1.4 and 2.2–2.3, printed pp. 31–41 and 50–63](https://www.jonathanschaffer.org/monism.pdf).

The source inquiry can grant the proposal for the sake of argument without certifying its metaphysical possibility or treating contemporary physics as proving it. On that proposal, a holistic concrete basis has genuine intrinsic powers not obtainable by treating isolated parts as self-sufficient. Its internal relations need not be external productive causes of the whole. The source advocate's rejection of indiscriminate composition arguments is therefore important: complexity alone cannot dispose of this rival.

The next question is existential. If the whole is contingently actual and unreceived, it faces the same adequacy demand as B. If it has a genuinely existentially sufficient nature, it grants a major original-necessary endpoint, while leaving the kind of source in dispute. If only *some* total realization must exist, the account has supplied a necessary existential generalization, not yet the necessity of this actual bearer. If its parts can be replaced across worlds, that identity issue must be answered rather than hidden in the term *the whole*.

An irreducibly plural basis requires equal care. The claim that the plurality is jointly sufficient need not identify one member as independently sufficient for all. Nor does the necessity of a structural pattern prove the necessary existence of every actual token that realizes it. These are not objections from ignorance: they specify the precise additional claim needed to reach the source conclusion under examination. The previous single-bearer coverage and unity results remain available only at their earned scope.

## 10. How to compare explanations without declaring victory by stipulation

Welchance's recent challenge is directly relevant. He argues that foundationalists must account for their fundamental existents just as infinitists must account for their chains; merely positing a foundation does not accomplish that. He also credits infinitism with explaining concrete existence in a uniform manner and resists an automatic unification advantage for foundationalism. His claim concerns metaphysical explanation and comparative virtues, not the demonstrated actuality of an infinite efficient-causal world. [Welchance, “Metaphysical Infinitism and Theoretical Virtue,” §§1–2, published 11 March 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-026-01084-1).

The first challenge should be accepted. A source introduced because it would solve a problem has not thereby had its own existence explained. A theory cannot demand an ultimate explanation from its rival while licensing its own bare necessary-source label to end every question. If the source's purported intrinsic nature has no content beyond *whatever is needed to be necessary*, the explanatory advantage has not been earned.

But this does not force parity. The relevant distinction is between kinds of warranted explanation, not between equal counts of unexplained nouns. A developed intrinsic nature could explain why existential reception is inapplicable to the source and why it can supply dependent actuality. A brute basis deliberately lacks the former explanation. If the intrinsic account is independently justified, this is a genuine advantage. It is not canceled merely because its ultimate nature is itself a primitive in the theory. Conversely, the word *essence* is not evidence of that justification.

The uniformity challenge also needs discrimination. The original-source proposal need not say that dependents have a lesser grade of existence. They are fully real. What differs is their mode of receipt. A single account of why some actualities are received and an original actuality is not may be a principled theory, rather than an arbitrary mixture of unrelated explanations. Yet whether the unreceived case has been earned is exactly what the infinitist disputes. The source advocate cannot win by presupposing the difference, and the infinitist cannot win merely by treating explanatory uniformity as invariably more important than discharging an ultimate dependency.

Three concrete questions therefore organize the comparison.

**Does the account identify real productive resources?** An instantiated active basis or a real productive chain answers more than conditional laws alone. Either side that fails here fails before the modal inquiry.

**Does the account explain its own existential stopping point, or explicitly leave it primitive?** A contingent brute and a brute necessity label both fall short of an intrinsic existential account. An infinite local account also cannot claim a global existential answer merely by repeating its local relation. None loses its genuine local explanations for that reason.

**Does the additional metaphysics earn its cost?** A necessary-source nature should derive existential sufficiency and relevant productive capacity from an independently defensible characterization, not append one bespoke clause per challenge. A holistic or infinitary nature should likewise explain its global productive scope, rather than simply stipulate that its entire actual configuration is admissible. Neither is entitled to count the other's open questions while hiding its own.

No arbitrary numerical score follows. The positive preference for intrinsic existential sufficiency is a reasoned preference for explanatory closure where that closure is substantively supported. The live rivals identify where support is missing; their survival does not establish that every explanatory virtue balances exactly.

## 11. Why this does not collapse contingent acts into necessity

The modal conclusion concerns the source's existence, not every exercise of its power. A standing necessary bearer can support a particular contingent exercise. The exercise's being grounded in its bearer is not the same relation as its being efficiently created by another being. The bearer/attribute distinction from §2 prevents an illicit conversion of every intrinsic condition into an external supplier.

An actual complete production may include an actual determination or volition which is contingent. The conditional sufficiency of that full actual production does not make every item included in it necessary. Thus even if the full actual cause suffices for its effect, necessity of the source's existence alone does not yield necessity of the effect. One must not quietly replace *the source exists necessarily* with *the total actual productive state exists necessarily*.

This is not an independent proof of freedom, renewed acts, or the adequacy of every proposed agent-causal explanation. Those require the attribute inquiry. It is a scope guard against importing pure-act doctrine or universal necessitation into the original-actuality argument. The source advocate also owes symmetry: if non-necessitating explanation is allowed for its own acts, it cannot reject every rival explanation solely because an alternative outcome remains possible. The targeted challenge is the explanation of the original bearer's whole existence, for which the bearer cannot simply be assumed as the acting background.

## 12. Exact increment and remaining verdict

The Third actuality/infinite-enablement essay already distinguishes passive equations from an actual infinite chain, recognizes global actuality and unconditional essence alternatives, and refuses a finite-source-removal refutation of infinitism. The accepted Sixth upstream argument already supplies the complete-coverage result, an actual-root-to-necessity conditional, the fixed-plurality analysis, and the core Aṣfahāniyya rationale. The reviewed world bridge already separates disappearance from false belief and identifies the anti-skeptical generalization burdens. Those achievements are retained, not claimed again.

The present increment is narrower and deeper:

1. The modal gap survives even a strengthening from actual independence to essential unreceivedness. Conditional autonomy and unconditional existential sufficiency are different claims.
2. Received **active** powers must not be replaced by passive conduits. The relevant foundation premise concerns their existential supply, not a supposed absence of intrinsic efficacy in recipients.
3. The self-actualization test distinguishes an impossible bearer-first causal repair of contingent originality from candid bruteness, temporal self-maintenance, process constitution and noncausal essential grounding. This prevents both an overbroad refutation and a modal-collapse repair.
4. The source argument's explanatory-exhaustiveness premise is tested against a developed noncausal, non-necessitating rival. Rejecting brute facts is not alone a proof that the remaining explanation must be a necessary concrete efficient source. The grounded concrete basis is not a counterexample granting broad complete unborrowed sourcehood; the dispute concerns ultimate-ground type or a shift in the meaning of reception.
5. Real published infinitary and holistic proposals replace a generic rival label. Their constitutive achievements, modal limits and additional productive obligations are separately evaluated.
6. The comparative argument now charges both sides for existential-content and explanatory-type commitments. It explains what would count as a real source advantage without treating either parity or superiority as automatic.
7. A newly checked primary faqr/ghinā passage grounds the positive route in particular recognition of actual need, expressly avoiding an unrestricted all-facts causal principle. Its source-specific application explains why intrinsic sufficiency can be a rational explanatory warrant rather than a mere demand for a preferred conclusion.

The strongest affirmative thesis is therefore an argued metaphysical claim: complete received actuality calls for original positive actuality, and original actuality is ultimately adequate only if its whole existence is intrinsically sufficient rather than brute. This thesis has a coherent, source-sensitive rationale and survives the removal of several bad arguments made on its behalf. Its comparative advantage is earned where those claims have independent support; it is not an all-things-considered ranking licensed by a label. It is not yet an unconditional deduction from actual rational correction. The precise remaining work is to vindicate the productive-completion and existential-exhaustiveness premises against genuine non-well-founded and noncausal competitors, and independently to characterize the intrinsic nature alleged to do that work.
