## 3 Attribution and dialectical force

1. At 2:180, reply 9 answers an opponent's admitted eternal will by parity with wisdom. The attribution of specification to wisdom, will, knowledge, and power has genuine argumentative significance. Its immediate setting does not independently supply a constructive account of every particular determination. The report keeps both facts.

2. At 2:181 before the section break, the author argues that denying purpose removes willing and leads to a will-less complete necessitator with its effects coeval. This is an endorsed polemic, not the opponent's premise. It does not reject effect-necessity conditional on actual complete voluntary production.

3. After that break, reduction of all ends to pleasure and removal of distress, followed by the claim that intermediaries must be futile, belongs to the reported deniers of wisdom. The answers that follow defend wisdom. The candidate does not attribute that reductive psychology to Ibn al-Qayyim.

4. At 2:183–184, reply 6 entertains acts without further purposive explanation only as an argument binding the opponent on the opponent's concessions. The explicit qualification is followed by the author's affirmation that all divine acts and law have wisdoms and ends, whether or not creatures know them in detail. This prevents reading the reply as endorsement of arbitrary unexplained willing. It does not erase the distinct thesis that an end can be sought for itself rather than for an endless further end.

5. Reply 7 grants direct attainment for the argument and rejects equality in every respect. Reply 10 presents a conditional comparison of kinds of activity and then positively affirms the significance of varied divine activity. The candidate fairly preserves its positive force while declining to derive a particular schedule, an actual token assignment, or identical full grounds with divergent outcomes from modal availability alone.

6. At 2:188, the distinctions between necessity and permissibility of purposive explanation again occur in a dialectical response. The text's positive appeal to continuing activity and intrinsically sought ends is not a declaration that a wholly indifferent complete situation can specify itself.

This is argument-to-concessions usage of “ad hominem,” not a personal attack. “Dialectical” also does not mean all neighboring replies are merely hypothetical or devoid of authorial commitment.

## 4 Positive premises and their exact explanatory reach

### Constitutive means

Replies 1–3, 2:181–182, deny the coherence of preserving a relation or a specified occurrence while deleting what its realization requires. The son-as-son example supports the relation claim, not a claim that every customary biological route is uniquely necessary for producing a person. The positive achievement is a principled restriction on what counts as the same intended end. It does not independently establish that every such relational end is worth pursuing or that one exact realization is uniquely good.

The report recognizes this restriction. It neither uses omnipotence to erase constitutive dependence nor inflates dependence into a sufficient particular reason.

### Additional goods

Reply 4 supplies another positive premise: mediation can bring benefits for the mediating participants themselves. The trade illustration makes this concrete. The report's four-step application explains why a buyer-only replacement description has not preserved the full displayed practical comparison.

This is stronger than saying that an undisclosed reason might exist. It identifies an actual type of relevant benefit asserted by the source and explains how the proposed simplification omits it. It is appropriately called sufficient to rebut the specific inference to futility. That use of “sufficient” is not productive sufficiency or a complete all-things-considered determinant.

The application is appropriately sensitive to altered premises. If the alternative also provides for sellers, the two-beneficiary argument alone no longer discriminates the arrangements. Additional relevant features would have to be established. If the proposed act has burdens, the existence of one benefit does not by itself establish that those burdens are outweighed. These are constraints on the strength of the explanation, not grounds for denying its bounded positive content.

Reply 7's formulation must consequently remain contextual. The bare fact that two options differ cannot justify selecting either one; differences can be irrelevant or disfavored. The candidate explicitly restricts its positive argument to independently warranted favorable differences supplied by the surrounding discussion.

### Diversity and divine names

Reply 10 supplies a distinct reason concerning activity of both kinds rather than confinement to one. Credit for that positive type-level rationale is warranted; reducing it to mere absence of contradiction would underread the text. However, the text does not infer that every conceivable variant must occur, that one effect-token has both incompatible causal descriptions, or that one exact instance has an independently disclosed actual intention merely because both kinds are within power.

Reply 11 contains a stronger necessity claim about effects or correlates appropriate to the divine names. It is not merely the claim that such effects could exist. The report does not deny that force. But this locus still does not individuate each beneficiary, circumstance, or temporal token, or establish one total history as uniquely eligible. Its attribution should remain distinct from reply 10's diversity argument.

The wording about wisdoms for the agent at 2:182 should also be read with 2:185, which denies that God obtains perfection from creatures. Additional goods in creation are not a claim that the divine bearer receives essential completion from an outside provider. No contrary inference occurs in the candidate.

## 5 Particular determination and uniqueness

The reviewer reread the retained *Majmūʿ* 16:300–305 and *Miftāḥ* 2:919–924 controls. These are respectively Ibn Taymiyya's account and Ibn al-Qayyim's account; the authors are not interchangeable evidence labels.

*Majmūʿ* 16:304–305 relates particular willing to apprehended differentiating content and places the relevant grounds within the agent, without treating the future object as an already actual external cause. It also says the cognitive-volitional representation originates. The means argument adds substantive examples of relevant content; it does not discharge the origin or exact occasion of every particular representation. Mere standing capacity remains too coarse a differentiator.

*Miftāḥ* 2:921–923 distinguishes what is necessary through choice from what is involuntary. Its determinant need not exclude actual willing. Consequently a reason's failure to force the act through a separately subsisting prior mechanism is not a defect by itself. Equally, merely appending an occurred choice to a premise does not independently explain its content.

The candidate correctly distinguishes at least four questions: effect given complete actual production; relevant discrimination against a stated alternative; uniquely permissible alternative derivable from a separately enumerated reason set; and human demonstration of a unique total history. These are not equivalent. Its positive result answers the bounded non-futility contrast without pretending to have answered them all.

No general exception for tied full reasons is licensed. “Within power” is modal ability language; it does not establish that complete wisdom, actual intention, and operative conditions are identical for two differently specified outcomes. Nor does the absence of a demonstrated global uniqueness theorem defeat the earned local explanation.
