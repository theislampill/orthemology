# Complete causes, received power, and the mode of production

9 October 2026 UTC. Targeted Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl rereading under the continuing T20 research mandate. No outreach, integration, closure, new model, or formal-proof claim.

## Result

**The received source expressly combines necessary occurrence, real created efficacy, and one complete original provider. It does so by distinguishing modes and levels of production. Necessary occurrence from a completed causal determination is not, in this text, an unindexed attribution of sole original production to whichever agent exercises power and will.**

The source also **positively excludes independently powerful coequals**. Its exclusion cannot be dismissed as an opponent's claim: the Sunni respondent affirms it at e146 and e150. But the complete chapter 19 exchange supplies no additional independent derivation of “complete power plus decisive will entails sole actual production” beyond the inherited nonduplication argument and the broader original-provision commitments. E50's explicit contradiction uses independence that excludes the other's actual production. The attempted inference to that exclusive meaning remains a substantive issue when the rival proposes redundant, nonexclusive complete processes.

This is a **mode-of-production transport test**, not a discovery of subordinate causality and not a reversal of the historical common-source judgment. The inherited whole-received-body appraisal already records the key passages and their limits. The new return checks exactly what survives a translation into an unindexed causal predicate and how the current interface handles that translation.

## 2. Four claims that the source requires us to distinguish

1. **A complete sufficient process conditional on received actuality.** A route can be complete as an operating sequence while its agent, power, recipient, instruments, enabling circumstances, and persistence are supplied. Completing such a route does not turn its operator into their original provider.
2. **The entire actual productive basis.** This asks what actually supplies all the productive requisites of the specified effect in the relevant respect. E143 deliberately expands attention beyond the creature's voluntary exercise to such requisites. A process diagram that begins after they are available does not answer this larger question.
3. **Exclusive actual attribution.** This adds that no distinct independent original supplies the same entire production in the same respect. A source-only sufficient route and the exclusion of every other actual original contribution are different claims. E50's final prohibited case expressly adopts the latter sense of independence.
4. **Necessary consequence or concomitance.** Given the complete causal determination, the effect follows. This specifies a connection, not by itself the productive ownership of all the antecedent's components. An unindexed implication cannot show that a named agent originally supplies them all.

The complete-cause claim is not merely a statement of regular succession: the source insists on real productive powers. Conversely, the inference to occurrence is not a production-mode declaration. Both distinctions matter.

## 3. The complete chapter 19 exchange

### Necessary occurrence is compatible with voluntary action

At e140 the Jabri interlocutor argues that power and motive either necessitate action or require another determinant, and proposes that necessitation amounts to coercion. At e140–142 the Sunni reply distinguishes coerced or involuntary motion from action that follows necessarily through the agent's own will and capacity. E150–151 revisits the same argument. A fully determining ground can be conceded without identifying voluntary action with a falling body or a forced limb.

This is important positive content. The reply does not retreat to idle power, deny actual action, or treat action as unrelated to will. Its point is that **necessitation and coercion are different relations**. It also uses divine voluntary action to challenge the interlocutor's classification. None of these arguments concludes that every subject with an actual effective will becomes the sole original producer of its effect.

### Why a created agent is not independent

E143 gives a substantive, concrete reason. The creature's power, decisive will, and motives are parts of the complete cause. Seeing requires the visible object, its suitability for vision, the organ and its health, and removal of obstacles. Striking requires an instrument, a receptive target, the intervening space and lack of obstruction, strength and bodily connections, and other circumstances not supplied by the striker.

The argument therefore differs from “another agent is present, so this agent is incomplete.” It identifies **actual received requisites** that the creature does not originate. E144 adds that even the creature's own relevant power and volition are received. This is genuine positive warrant against independent created production, within the source's theology and ordinary causal examples. It is not a bare assertion that the creature's activity has no effect: the opposite claim is expressly rejected.

These examples do not, without further premises, establish that a proposed unreceived redundant original also receives a requisite from its peer. The proposed original is precisely not the creature whose organ, power, and surrounding world are already admitted to be created. Transferring the observed deficit requires the stronger original-efficacy argument.

### The same produced act, different production modes

E145 raises the problem of one effect between two powers. The Sunni reply surveys and criticizes several solutions. It rejects merely relocating creaturely efficacy to an act's description while denying influence on its existence. It also rejects limiting divine capacity to cases in which the creature is not acting.

E146–147 supplies its affirmative solution. The produced motion really occurs through the creature's received power and will. It is attributed to divine power as creation and bringing-into-being, and to the creature's power as causal and direct performance. The creature's power is itself an effect of divine power and a partial cause. Both that created power and its effect fall within the original creative provision.

This concerns **one produced motion q under different relations**. It does not make God's intrinsic creative act numerically identical with the creature's motion or make God the subject performing the creature's morally qualified deed. The act–product distinction at e51–54 and the continuation at e152 prevent that collapse.

E150 makes the hierarchy explicit: two independent, coequal powers are distinguished from two powers one of which is received from the other. The source affirms the first configuration's exclusion while allowing the second causal structure. Real creaturely causation therefore does not contradict sole original provision. Nor does that allowed hierarchy supply a counterexample containing two independent originals.

## 4. What supports nonduplication here, and what remains disputed

### The source's positive case must be preserved

E50 divides apparently shared production into different cases: cooperation where neither can do the whole; alternative production; joint carrying although either could carry alone; and each actually performing the whole independently. The final case is rejected because each agent's independence excludes the other's doing it. The standing ability of each carrier is not confused with two complete independent actual performances.

At e145 the interlocutor frames the problem in terms of an effect being both dependent and independent when two complete producers are proposed. At e146 the Sunni respondent independently **endorses** the impossibility of one numerically identical effect between independent complete producers, placing it alongside causal principles he regards as rationally evident. At e150 he endorses the mutual-hindrance argument against coequals whose powers belong intrinsically to them rather than being received from one another. He explicitly says the argument can be sound even when his opponents fail to defend its premises.

Thus it would be false to report the inspected text as merely agnostic about coequal exclusion, or to attribute all exclusionary content to a rejected interlocutor. The positive assertions have philosophical evidential weight; they are not erased because they are contestable.

### Why this is not a new independent bridge

The e50 contradiction is secure for **exclusive actual whole-production**. Its wording does not separately demonstrate that every nonexclusive complete process is secretly partial, preempted, or impossible. E145's dependence/independence formulation likewise needs the relevant dependence relation held fixed: indispensability of each particular producer does not follow merely from its being an actual cause if genuine redundancy is admitted.

A strong source defence can make a further claim: an effect's determinate actual receipt individuates its entire production, and an independently original agent's complete actual efficacy cannot leave any of that productive adequacy supplied by a peer. This is an intelligible productive-ownership and perfection rationale, not necessarily an arbitrary verbal preference. But the current chapter's assertions do not independently resolve its application to the strongest nonexclusive redundant proposal. Merely substituting “entire basis” or “independent” for the conclusion would not supply the missing reason.

The inherited Majmūʿ/Oneness nonabsorption, augmentation, and perfected-efficacy arguments remain separately owned evidence. This return neither rereads nor downgrades them by pretending that Shifāʾ contains their whole defence. Its bounded result is that **no additional independent nonduplication rationale was found in this complete received exchange**. The original common-source appraisal is not thereby mechanically confirmed or reversed.

## 5. Two supplementary source controls sharpen the distinction

### Powers can persist while their effects are blocked

The complete fourth principle at e188–190 attacks denial of natural powers and causes. At e189 it distinguishes abolishing a cause's efficacy, installing a blocker while its power remains, and permitting its normal effect. This is stronger than mere event coincidence.

It also shows why possession of a power and a completed effective cause must not be identified. A blocker can prevent manifestation without removing the standing power. When the cause is complete, occurrence follows; identifying the complete cause still requires the relevant circumstances. This does not establish that a perfected original can be blocked by a peer. That would transport a created-cause example beyond its scope.

### Necessitating local causes remain within original provision

In the final rational-fitra subsection, e305 distinguishes the existence of the receptive subject, its preparation, and its ongoing supply. E306's thirteenth argument distinguishes an implanted cause sufficient for its result from one needing another factor. Both are inside a received natural order; the distinction is not between a sole original creator and no cause at all.

This is a useful internal semantic control: the vocabulary of a cause entailing its effect does not invariably designate an unreceived original producer. The broader fitra conclusion depends on further substantive premises; this report does not newly establish them. The e306 continuation's marginal intrusion remains uncorrected and is not needed for this causal point.

### Complete original provision has affirmative perfection content

Numbered replies 11–16 at e219–222 argue that perfect attributes have fitting actual manifestations and that complete kingship involves varied wise action. E220's thirteenth reply says the conditions required for its exercise are themselves from the original source, so no external provider completes that kingship. E221 also ranks purposive good agency over mere unpurposive capability.

These passages provide positive reasons for caring about actual adequate provision, rather than treating exclusive sourcehood as a desire for sole credit. Their immediate argument already employs the comprehensive-source account. They do not by themselves prove that a redundant peer necessarily supplies a missing completion, or that every proposed unreceived original must individually originate every requisite. The latter is the extension at issue.

## 6. The strongest redundant rival and the proper evidential balance

The strongest relevant rival is **parallel nonexclusive production**, not the source's allowed hierarchy and not complementary half-production. Each proposed process is actually operative and complete at its designated causal interface; neither borrows a missing stage from the other; one effect terminates both. To answer the original-specific challenge, the rival further needs warranted unreceived powers and actual adequacy for all relevant resources and conditions. Calling the powers primitive does not establish that further claim, but neither does primitiveness itself refute it.

The inherited Schaffer reading supplies genuine prima facie support for redundant actual causation, including the noncompositional spells proposal and a process rationale. The conditional macro/micro case also prevents treating every case as two summed physical contributions. These are affirmative, defeasible reasons to challenge an unrestricted conceptual prohibition. They are not an independently established pair of perfected original suppliers. No new Schaffer reading or new modal possibility is claimed here.

The fair balance is therefore two-sided:

- The source gives substantive reasons for original productive adequacy and directly endorses coequal exclusion. Its created-cause examples genuinely show received completion.
- The rival gives reasons not to identify actual causal contribution with indispensability or exclusive attribution at every causal scope. Those reasons do not automatically authenticate the stronger original-resource interpretation.
- The missing bridge is not repaired by moving between these meanings. Neither a failed deductive shortcut nor the absence of a full rival settles the cumulative defeasible original-source judgment.

## 7. Transport into the actual current interface

A faithful conceptual transcription distinguishes “G originally creates q,” “c directly performs/causes q,” and “c's effective power is received from G.” The last relation makes this a hierarchy. Forgetting the modes yields the true but poorer summary “G causes q and c causes q.” **The error arises if one then infers that both are coequal entire original producers.** The forgetful summary contains no such information.

The current effect-production report already calls its route coordinate **positive original roots**, distinguishes operative routes from occurrence certificates, and disclaims metaphysical authentication of its causal interpretation. Its `OperativeRoute` record separates a route, birth, root set, and guards; `exhaustive_positive_sources` classifies a singleton root set. The adapter rejects created-input rules rather than inventing their lineage. The richer reactive wrapper retains its separate original/efficient-edge interpretation.

These are real safeguards. No inspected report or code step currently imports the source's creature as an additional original merely because the creature directly acts. **No implementation defect or failed test is reported.**

There is nevertheless an application obligation: the raw root field is a `frozenset`, with no intrinsic creation-versus-direct-performance mode or automatic metaphysical originality check. If someone supplied an unindexed list of every causal participant as the original-root set, the singleton classifier would faithfully compute a semantically inappropriate result. The source controls prohibit that input interpretation. The present report already recognizes the interpretation burden, so this is a source-grounded transport guard rather than a new toy theorem or a new defect discovery.
