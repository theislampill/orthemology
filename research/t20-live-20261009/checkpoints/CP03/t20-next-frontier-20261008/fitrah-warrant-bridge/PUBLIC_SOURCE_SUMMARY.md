# Fitrah recognition and warrant

Ameri’s complete fitrah chapter, pp. 157–204, presents a developing, defeasible cognitive disposition rather than explicit newborn theology. Its strongest warrant argument appeals to parity with ordinary reason and perception. It rejects popularity as proof and recognises that a psychological cause can coexist with religious truth. The contested step is identifying this particular recognition as a truth-directed deliverance. Primary Ibn Taymiyyah context also separates teacher-independent learning from genuinely non-discursive cognition.

Direct study checks narrow the empirical support: mortality effects reverse under some conditions; adult speeded judgments concern broadly specified agency; the clinical affiliation study cannot classify atheism as illness.

My assessment permits relatively basic Creator belief to have prima facie rational standing without a prior scientific theory of its mechanism. That does not certify a particular believer’s factive knowledge or make every religious inclination reliable. This reading adds no independent verification of factive basic recognition. Common origin alone does not transfer warrant between cognitive domains. Nor does explaining a belief’s production automatically defeat it. The next useful test concerns the content, epistemic role and defeaters of a specific recognitional experience.
