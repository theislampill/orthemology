# The direct rejection of brute original existence

9 October 2026 UTC. Focused T20 source and inference return. This report supplements, and does not alter, the completed brute-contingent source return.

## Result

Darʾ 3:287–289 directly considers the proposed third option: an existent whose existence is neither through itself nor through another. Ibn Taymiyyah rejects it as an immediately evident rational truth, explains existence through oneself without self-production, and expressly connects complete existential independence with necessity through oneself. Its intended scope includes a beginningless brute original. The comparison with an originated thing's inability to produce itself does not restrict the thesis to originated things.

This supplies stronger direct textual evidence and a more exact classification of the positive warrant. It does not disclose a further deduction from premises the strongest brute rival already accepts. Nor does the absence of that deduction make the source's foundational warrant defective: the surrounding argument expressly rejects the demand that a clearer starting judgment always be proved by more obscure premises.

The decisive distinction is this. If “through itself” means only absence of external existential need, a fully unreceived brute already belongs to that side of the division; the substantive commitment is that such independence entails intrinsically sufficient necessary existence. If “through itself” means that positive sufficiency, excluding a third option is itself a substantive foundational principle. The sophisticated brute rival can keep these meanings distinct and deny that principle. It therefore presents a real philosophical disagreement, although its own alleged metaphysical possibility has not thereby been established.

## The continuous argument

### Before the focal reply

At 282–286, the discussion tests explanations of an entire possible collection, including the role of its arrangement. The collection's arrangement is not simply confused with each member, and the whole is not required to be an additional independently existing object. A purported complete cause drawn from the dependent collection has not thereby supplied its own needed actuality. This is the familiar productive totality question, not yet a separate proof that an explicitly unreceived brute is impossible.

The new section at 286–287 explains the order of warrant. Knowledge that this originated thing has an originator can suffice; the reasoner need not first construct an increasingly elaborate proof through time-selection, possibility, determinants, regress, and circularity. Some premises require explanation because they are obscure; others are clearer than those proposed to prove them. Listing every imaginable objection would never end. That methodological argument governs the reply to al-Rāzī which follows. [Electronic context](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/592/).

### The objection and reply

At 287, al-Rāzī is quoted objecting that Avicenna's denial of existence from a thing's own essence does not yet show existence from another: neither source might apply. The objection demands either a declaration that the third option is evidently impossible or a proof of its impossibility. This is a challenge to completeness of the argument; the quotation alone does not establish that al-Rāzī endorses a brute ontology.

At 288, Ibn Taymiyyah takes the first route. He treats the third option's impossibility as basic rational knowledge, comparable in clarity to an originated thing's not originating itself. He nevertheless says a person who doubts it needs clarification. The foundational stance is therefore not a prohibition on addressing a serious doubt.

At 288–289, he illustrates how one can make a simple starting point hostage to harder disputes: require proof that a producer exists, then proof that the nonexistent is not a thing, and so on. His point is that these proposed demonstrations are less evident than the original judgment. At 289 he clarifies the intended positive alternative: «بل يعنى أنه لا يحتاج في وجوده إلى غيره، بل وجوده واجب بنفسه». Existence through oneself means having no need of another for existence and being necessary through oneself, not bringing oneself into existence.

The author regards the principle and the falsity of its contrary as clearer than the attempted proofs. That is an explicit foundational warrant claim. It must not be paraphrased as a concealed deduction from anti-self-production, or dismissed as though only derivation from weaker premises could provide philosophical warrant.

### The continuation constrains the reconstruction

At 289–292, the answer to a further al-Rāzī objection distinguishes a sequence of existentially derivative producers from successive conditions of occurrence. The discussion is not a simple inference that every beginningless sequence is impossible. Earlier and later locations of alleged producers do not remove the explanatory problem if the entire productive basis remains derivative. Conversely, a previous event's role as a condition does not make it the independent source of a later thing's being. [Continuation](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/594/), [following unit](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/595/).

At 293, the author again groups existence neither through self nor another with the other claims he regards as immediately impossible. At 294–295, the self's necessary attributes and conditions such as life in relation to knowledge are expressly distinguished from effect-to-producer relations. Their inseparability is not mutual production. At 296–298 the same distinction is applied to examples of motion and to the difference between indefinitely continuing activity and one particular eternal effect. These surrounding pages prevent turning the direct anti-brute principle into either a denial of intrinsic qualification or an argument for a first moment of all activity.

Textual cautions matter. The electronic unit at 291 omits the restrictive phrase shown in print as “except at the time of its existence”; the printed passage is about a producer existing then, not a producer being absent then. The fourth reason at 292 ends incompletely, and the editor notes a gap of about two words in a witness. The preceding third reason also contains an awkward final clause. No new theorem of continuous or cross-world dependence is extracted from either ending. At 288 the enumeration label appears mismatched with the alternatives preceding it; no repair of that label is needed for the substantive reply. The direct 287–289 wording on the third option, self-production, and necessity is clear in the scan.

## What necessity and selfhood mean here

There are two different uses of necessity in these pages. Calling a judgment necessary or immediately evident classifies its **epistemic warrant**. Calling an existent necessary through itself classifies its **mode of existence**. Neither usage is a numerical probability, and the first is not itself proof of the second merely because both use necessity vocabulary.

The ontological order at 289 is independence and necessity through oneself, followed by existence without beginning or end. It does not infer intrinsic necessity merely from existence throughout one actual history. Iṣfahāniyya 34–35 makes the intended exclusion of possible nonbeing explicit, so the combined argument is stronger than a temporal persistence claim. Its interpretation in a possible-worlds formalism still requires a domain of genuine metaphysical alternatives, not arbitrary listed histories.

The self is the concrete existent under discussion. It is not a pre-existing maker of itself, a bare essence stripped of all attributes, a mental description whose external instance is generated by definition, or automatically a personal mind. The subsequent attribute discussion excludes those first two readings particularly clearly. “Its own reality is sufficient” is an existential claim; it is not the claim that the thing has a causal operation directed at its own existence.

The comparison with Iṣfahāniyya is exact. At 35 the reason supplied is «إذ نفسه كافية في وجوده»: its own reality suffices for its existence. The text then considers both a hypothetical essence/existence distinction and the author's preferred concrete identity. The identity alternative clarifies what the subject is; it does not do the necessity work. Creatures can also have concrete essence/existence identity. Likewise, “where its identity is posited it cannot be absent” cannot be isolated as a proof from “if it exists, it exists.” The preceding intrinsic-sufficiency claim is essential to the strong reading. [Iṣfahāniyya electronic text](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/شرح_العقيدة_الأصفهانية), [retained edition scan](https://archive.org/download/waq13116/13116.pdf).

## The affirmative argument and its precise commitments

The following reconstruction distinguishes the logical steps from the source's substantive starting judgment.

1. Some concrete reality is actual. The comparative Iṣfahāniyya argument supplies observed originated cases when a productive target is needed.
2. An actual existent either needs another for its existence or does not. This division by external existential need is exhaustive.
3. Complete absence of that need is not bare unexplained actuality. It is existence sufficiently through the concrete self, and such sufficiency excludes possible nonbeing through that self.
4. Therefore an actual existent which genuinely admits nonbeing is not fully independent in that respect; its actual existence requires another.
5. Separately, a complete productive basis cannot remain wholly within the same derivative field. The totality argument supplies the original basis to which the independence and necessity principle applies.

Step 3 is the substantive anti-brute commitment. It is not produced by excluded middle in step 2, and step 5 does not replace it. A beginningless unreceived original already avoids an all-received productive field; its challenge is to step 3. Conversely, denying step 3 does not refute the independent argument against an entirely borrowed productive basis.

Two formulations make the exact disagreement visible:

- **Thin through-self:** it means absence of every external existential requisite. The exhaustive division is secure, and the brute candidate is classified as through-self. The affirmative thesis is then that this status has the positive intrinsic sufficiency and necessity the candidate denies.
- **Thick through-self:** it already means positive intrinsic existential sufficiency. The brute candidate denies both that status and external receipt. The exclusion of that third combination is the foundational no-brute principle, not a tautology generated by the two words “self” and “other.”

These are two ways of locating the same substantive commitment. They do not require accusing Ibn Taymiyyah of offering only a definition. In particular, Iṣfahāniyya supplies a positive explanation of the commitment, while Darʾ makes explicit its status as a basic rational judgment and its intended application to the third option.

## The strongest rival and fair adjudication

The strongest relevant rival proposes an actual, concrete, intrinsically qualified B with unreceived productive powers. B has no outside producer or existential requisite; it never produced itself; it may have no temporal beginning. It adequately supplies the received effects under discussion. Nevertheless, B could have been absent, and there is no sufficient intrinsic or external explanation of why it obtains. This is a proposed position, not a certified possible reality.

It is important not to weaken that proposal into the production of something by nothing. B's existence is precisely what the rival declines to characterize as an episode of production. Nor does its lack of an explanation of its own obtaining imply a lack of powers sufficient for producing something else. Sufficiency for the effects and sufficiency for the source's own existence are different questions.

The rival can accept the thin through-self terminology and reject the necessity implication. Alternatively, it can reserve through-self for intrinsic explanatory sufficiency and deny the exhaustiveness of that thicker division. If it states the interpretation consistently, neither response is merely a verbal mistake. It denies exactly the source's substantive anti-brute premise. A weaker rival that equates through-self with self-production, or calls a conditionally realized description an original existent, would instead miss the argument.

The affirmative case has an intelligible advantage: it preserves a uniform connection between actual being and existential adequacy. If the concrete self is not sufficient for its own actuality, merely granting possibility supplies no actual being. An external source would then be required; if none is required, the existent is adequate through itself. This is why the source regards necessity as a principled terminus rather than an exception to “everything must have a producer.” Its force is directed at actual existential insufficiency, not at an imagined equal-probability balance between being and nonbeing.

The strong rival's reply is a principled refusal to infer an unsatisfied existential requirement from lack of intrinsic necessitation. B is primitive actual being; its contingency need not be an unrealized potential waiting for a producer. This avoids self-creation and a temporal leap from nothing, but leaves the very obtaining of B without a sufficient account. It is not another sufficient explanation answering the source's demand. Whether that explanatory stopping point is rationally acceptable is the disputed issue.

Neither side wins by a report of its own commitments. The source's assertion of immediate evidence is not, by itself, a demonstration that every rational dissenter fails. But a rule permitting only proofs from weaker agreed premises would equally misassess the source: rational first principles need not have that kind of proof. Proper appraisal asks whether the clarified sufficiency judgment is independently credible and whether the alleged third case gives a reason to reject it. Merely describing B as absent elsewhere, or drawing a modal table, supplies no such possibility warrant. Equally, repeating the word “insufficient” is not an additional argument against someone who disputes that modal contingency creates an existential debt.

The resulting assessment is asymmetric but bounded. The source has a positive rational account and now a directly verified response to the exact third option. The brute rival has not established a counterexample under the intended meanings and independently supported surrounding commitments. Such a counterexample would falsify the disputed premise, not preserve it. The rival identifies and rejects that premise, and so requires a substantive defense rather than semantic relabeling. The new passage does not furnish a separate deduction establishing its denial to be inconsistent with premises the rival retains. Philosophical assent to the positive account need not wait for such a deduction.

## Application to ContingencyNeed

The unchanged interface states that an actual bearer which is not necessary is wholly received. Its `Necessary` predicate quantifies existence over the represented worlds. `ContingencyNeed` is explicitly a premise, not a theorem derived from the original-resource construction. The subsequent necessity inference uses it by contraposition on the already obtained original bearer.

Darʾ 287–289 and Iṣfahāniyya 34–35 provide a source-grounded rationale for that premise under the intended reading. They do not prove the formula for every interpretation of its types and predicates. Three conditions remain explicit:

1. B ranges over the relevant concrete bearers. It must not silently expand to every act-token, proposition, necessary attribute, or merely describable item. The source's several usages of necessary and possible do not all coincide with this typed modal predicate.
2. W represents genuine alternatives for that same bearer, and the claimed nonnecessity is warranted. Temporal beginninglessness alone gives neither its necessity nor its contingency.
3. `wholeReceived` expresses the relevant external existential dependence, rather than mere intrinsic inherence or concomitance. The source's allowance that a dependent thing exists through another or together with another is not a claim of universal causal passivity. Identifying that existential dependence with the declared whole-reception relation is part of the application, not a new consequence of a binary word choice.

Under that interpretation, the rival B is exactly a denial of ContingencyNeed's relevant instance: actual B, genuine possible absence, and no whole-existence receipt. The productive original-bearer result can remain intact while this further necessity step is disputed. No new formal model is needed to identify the disagreement, and no such model would establish B's metaphysical possibility merely by satisfying weaker predicates.

The continued-source and attribute controls also prevent importing the separate `ConstitutiveReception` premise from a claim about existence at every time. Essential nonreceipt across worlds and intrinsic necessitation are distinct tasks in the existing interface. The incomplete wording at printed 292 cannot discharge either one.

## What changes and what remains

The completed earlier return already credited positive intrinsic-sufficiency reasoning and explicitly allowed foundational warrant. Its negative result concerned the three then-tested Darʾ locators, not a claim that Ibn Taymiyyah never considered the third option. The new passage corroborates and sharpens that account rather than replacing it with the opposite verdict.

The material gain is that the source directly targets the beginningless brute rival's proposed existential status and deliberately grounds its rejection in a basic rational judgment. The live disagreement is therefore not an overlooked alternative, a missing temporal first cause, or a mere definition of contingency. It is whether fully unreceived actuality is necessarily intrinsically sufficient, with the corresponding modal consequence.

This reading supplies no new uniqueness theorem, personal-attribute ascent, or closure of T20. It establishes the exact affirmative premise, its foundational warrant claim, and the strongest rival's real point of denial. A next substantive advance would assess that premise or the rival's modal warrant directly; another wholly received chain, self-production objection, or arbitrary world-frame would not decide it.
