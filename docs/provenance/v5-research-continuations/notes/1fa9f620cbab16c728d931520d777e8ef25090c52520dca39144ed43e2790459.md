## Why the load-bearing argument works

The potential trap is genuine: a barycentric quadratic regret estimate alone would yield only a square-root displacement bound, which is insufficient for eta between epsilon squared and epsilon. This proof does not make that substitution.

First, sublinear eta/epsilon together with epsilon→0 implies eta→0, and the actual approximate all-state inequalities imply localization through the earlier scalar-divergence theorem. Next, on the coherent tangent space the sum of the positive parts of all truth-state first derivatives is bounded below by a positive constant times the norm. The proof uses the positive barycentric identity, positivity of the Hessian and compactness of the tangent unit sphere. It also handles a zero-dimensional tangent without needing a nonempty sphere premise.

After localization, differentiability bounds each remainder by a/(2N) times the displacement. Every statewise score comparison bounds its positive first derivative by O(epsilon)+eta plus that remainder. Summing and absorbing half the lower bound yields O(epsilon), using eta≤epsilon eventually. The normalized repair sequence is therefore bounded as a proved conclusion. A subsequence and the eta/epsilon→0 first-order limit produce the same simultaneous Hessian-normal condition as exact repair.

The n≥3 ratio-connectivity and endpoint-integration arguments are then reused transparently. Thus allowing the existence of a shared nonnegative sublinear additive tolerance does not enlarge the universal compatible additive-generator classes. The reverse implication chooses eta=0, using the already-proved exact local construction.
