# Ameri on worship and practical authority

## Result

This is a positive source for an argument that worship is owed, with practical implications, rather than merely advantageous. It is not a completed argument for the separate power to make otherwise-underdetermined conduct obligatory through a commanding act. The latter distinction is my assessment, not Ameri's terminology or a claim that he aimed to solve this exact problem.

## Source summary

Ameri rejects the inference from requesting worship to divine need (pp. 14–21). Worship is owed through intrinsic perfection and benefaction, not only useful to worshippers (pp. 32–38). Page 34 distinguishes scriptural external obligation from internal recognition of perfection. A house-owner analogy challenges beneficence without acknowledging the provider (pp. 47–48). Differentiated excellence, gifts, dependence, and divine ownership support unequal standing and submission (pp. 67–71). Cosmic harmony and human benefits supply additional considerations (pp. 38–63). The conclusion explicitly includes duty (p. 71).

The passage on love and worship extending beyond command, reward, and punishment is quoted from Ibn al-Qayyim (pp. 32–33), not invented by Ameri. The no-questioning passage is quoted from Sayyid Qutb (pp. 22–23). Ameri's own prose provides the decisive external/internal distinction and the ownership/status analogies.

## Independent analysis of the bridge

The following is my conceptual assessment. It is not a further paraphrase of the booklet.

### Three conclusions must be kept apart

1. A fitting response is warranted: a creature has reason to acknowledge and thank its perfectly good source.
2. A response is owed: deliberately withholding appropriate acknowledgement can wrong that source, rather than merely deprive the creature of benefits.
3. A normative power exists: within a defensible domain, the source can intentionally select an otherwise-underdetermined action and make it obligatory by directing the creature to perform it.

The second conclusion genuinely adds something beyond advice. It can ground a directed obligation: something is owed to someone. But a claim-right to an independently appropriate response and a power to settle what shall be obligatory are different normative relations. Their connection must be argued, not obtained by changing the vocabulary from gratitude to obedience.

A defensible bridge might be narrow. If someone has a prior claim to an appropriate response, a reasonable request can identify how that response should be given. Once the request is known, intentional refusal may be a relational wrong. That would make the utterance practically relevant rather than mere information. It would still leave open why this selector has jurisdiction over every proposed domain, what kinds of specification are permissible, and why an alternative sincere response does not suffice.

### The strongest charitable reconstruction

The most promising premise package is original and continuing creation, perfect goodness, unsurpassable wisdom, benefaction, and a relationship admitting truthful mutual recognition. It is unfair to reduce that package to superior physical force. Original creation is also not equivalent to an ordinary person's contributing a small causal benefit. Simple comparisons with a parent, employer, or successful technician do not disprove the richer package.

Nevertheless, even granting the whole package, the specifically normative step remains: why does the appropriate acknowledgement include allowing this person to settle otherwise-open practical questions? A possible answer is that practical responsiveness constitutes the truthful acknowledgement required by this unique relationship. That answer needs a justification of its scope. Otherwise it can mean only that reasonable requests connected to appreciation deserve a response, which is a local relational principle.

The ultimate conclusion might be true while this particular inference remains incomplete. Failure to derive a power is not proof that no such power exists.

### Wisdom and dependence do different work

Wisdom can support trust in an instruction's assessment of what is beneficial or independently right. Perfect honesty can support confidence that the instruction represents that assessment accurately. Neither feature, taken by itself, explains why the act of instructing changes what is owed. A flawless adviser may disclose every antecedent reason without acquiring a general authority to legislate.

Dependence adds a personal relationship and can strengthen gratitude. But the transition from being the cause of another's existence to having a right over that other's deliberation is normative. Describing dependence more comprehensively does not eliminate this step. A defender must articulate the moral principle connecting the two, perhaps through rightful benefaction, constitutive recognition, or another account.

The ownership route faces a related question. If morally rightful ownership and its relevant scope have already been established, powers concerning use can follow. Calling causal authorship ownership, however, cannot establish that normative title. Rights concerning a house, benefits available in it, and autonomous occupants are distinguishable. Extending an analogy from objects or premises to persons requires an account of what authorizes the extension. The point is not that divine and human ownership must be identical; it is that their difference must do explanatory work rather than silently remove the difficulty.

### A discriminating test

Consider two otherwise closely matched cases. In both, the creator is perfectly good, the creature has the same dependence and received benefits, and several forms of acknowledgement are independently appropriate. In one case no public selection occurs. In the other, the creator expressly selects one form.

Ask separately:

- Has the creature merely learned which action would best express gratitude?
- Has a particular relationship-dependent duty arisen because an entitled person has made a reasonable request?
- Has a legitimate power selected one option as binding even though the other options remain independently good?

A positive answer to the second question may be enough for the local case without establishing the third as a general jurisdiction. This test does not require a morally bad divine command or deny perfect wisdom. It isolates whether and why the commanding act itself makes a practical difference.

### Result relative to the prior frontier

On the supplied description of T17/T19, this reading strengthens the case for directed, non-instrumental reasons and illustrates how a request may matter relationally. It does not independently close the outstanding jurisdiction question. If the needed premise is that the creator is already entitled to binding selection, the local-duty result remains conditional upon that entitlement.

A useful next theoretical target is therefore a bounded recognition-to-specification principle: explain why an independently owed acknowledgement grants its rightful recipient authority to select its permissible realization, then determine whether and how that domain extends. Its premises, exceptions, and range should be explicit. Merely expanding the definition of worship to include all obedience would not establish the principle.

Nothing in this report authenticates an actual revelation, identifies an actual divine command, or establishes that a purported command meets the conditions. Those questions are outside this reading's result.
