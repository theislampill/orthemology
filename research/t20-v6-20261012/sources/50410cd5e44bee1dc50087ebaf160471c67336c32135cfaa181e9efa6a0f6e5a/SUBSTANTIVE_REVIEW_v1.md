Under the reductio the combined claim is actual. The accepted E3 argument applies the relevant nonactual-possibility theory at a comparison where that claim is false but still possible. The omission was a consequential compression risk: a reader could apply a theory about nonactual possibilities directly to the actual combined claim. It did not invalidate the accepted component argument, which states the modal premise openly.

Draft-v4 line 81 now explicitly requires the separately warranted nonactual-but-possible comparison before domain coverage and achievement preservation are invoked. The target-specific contingency gain and inherited qualified-fact ownership remain intact. R1 is resolved.

Draft-v3 line 129 used a broad intrinsic-ground description immediately before deriving a necessary particular. Intrinsic grounding in general is not yet the stronger existential status used by the accepted E4 argument. A bearer can have an intrinsically grounded property without thereby possessing an existence-entailing essence.

Draft-v4 line 129 now states the stronger existential antecedent required by the accepted E4 argument. This retains the stronger premise on which the necessary-particular consequence is assessed. It does not derive that premise from ordinary productive power, bare necessary existence, or membership in a resource base. R2 is resolved.

The same revision expands the previously undefined reduced-target and possibility labels. Those were reader-facing notation corrections, not additional philosophical results.
