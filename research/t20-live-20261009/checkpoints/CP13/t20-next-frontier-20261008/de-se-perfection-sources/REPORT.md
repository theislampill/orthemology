# De se self-knowledge and original sovereignty: source-bound discrimination

8 October 2026 UTC. Bounded research, not integration, closure, an actual metaphysical countermodel, or research-duration certification. Earlier source readings retain their credit.

## Result

Complete self-knowledge makes an original agent accurately recognize **its own actual agency and the actual extent of its authority**. It does not, without a further perfection or authority premise, make that extent exclusive. Even if genuinely self-ascriptive, action-guiding cognition is additionally granted, that requirement does not contain exclusive authority. The existing policy code does not itself establish this stronger epistemic-basing interpretation.

The important affirmative qualification is that the inherited source does offer the further premises. It argues both for the original adequacy of a completed undertaking and, separately, for universal actual supremacy over distinct external bearers. Those are substantive comparative arguments, not simply definitions or demands for exclusive credit. If the stronger universal-supremacy norm is independently warranted at that domain and dependence-respect, two original bearers are excluded without needing de se knowledge at all. The present literature does not independently settle that norm's disputed application.

The epistemic increment is consequently discriminating but bounded: a rival cannot preserve perfect cognition by being confused about which agent it is, overlooking a peer's actual productive role, falsely claiming sole provision, or mistaking a common reason for its own exercise. A sufficiently strengthened rival instead recognizes its own exercise and the joint basis truthfully. Whether that state is **perfect original agency** remains the substantive original-efficacy/supremacy question. Recognition cannot supply the truth of the norm whose application it recognizes.

## 1. Primary literature, read in argument and reply

### Lewis: self-location and the repair of the two gods

David Lewis, “Attitudes De Dicto and De Se,” *Philosophical Review* 88 (1979), 513–543. Full paper read in continuous overlapping blocks; printed 520–521 visually checked. [Primary paper](https://andrewmbailey.com/dkl/Attitudes_De_Dicto_and_De_Se.pdf).

The gods example, pp.520–521, withholds self-location despite complete knowledge of world-propositions. Its explicitly described repair is each god correctly self-ascribing its own properties. It neither merges their identities nor requires each to possess the other's properties. The haecceitist reply is considered at pp.522–524; causal/action-explanatory motivation and alternative formulations continue through pp.534–543. The relevant contrast is not simply information quantity: knowing a proposition involving oneself can fail to be knowing it as oneself. These are epistemic toy gods, not demonstrated bearers of unrestricted original productive efficacy over a common created order. Their repair is therefore a control against a **de-se-alone** uniqueness inference, not a counterexample to the full theological conjunction.

### Perry: an index is not yet the appropriate cognitive state

John Perry, “The Problem of the Essential Indexical,” *Noûs* 13 (1979), 3–21. Full paper and endnotes read. [Primary paper](https://www.uvm.edu/~lderosse/courses/lang/Perry%281979%29.pdf).

Perry's pp.13–15 distinguish the context of belief from the context at which a content is evaluated. Their merely coinciding does not produce the self-recognition that explains action. His positive account, pp.17–20, distinguishes belief-states from objects believed. This permits shared subject matter without identical first-person modes. Our application to the retained architecture is stricter than a paraphrase of Perry: accurate centered annotations and a successful response policy cannot, by themselves, establish an actual subject's intentional ownership. Perry himself disclaims a full theory on p.20. Neither his preferred account nor Lewis's property account is treated here as an uncontroversial complete theory of divine cognition.

### Grim: the strongest local objection must concern knowledge, not feelings

Patrick Grim, “The Being That Knew Too Much,” *International Journal for Philosophy of Religion* 47 (2000), 141–154, author version. Introduction and the complete §2, author-PDF pp.1–6, read continuously; the separate liar, knower and Cantorian arguments are outside this operation. [Author PDF](https://pgrim.org/pgrim/Being.pdf).

Grim argues that self-recognition supplies something known that mere third-person or de re cognition lacks. He rejects an answer that excludes feelings from knowledge: his challenge survives unemotional examples. He also objects that a belief-state-only analysis fails to acknowledge genuinely acquired information. This is a serious challenge to an overly thin account of maximal knowledge. Yet its unrestricted form challenges any omniscient being coexisting with another self-knowing subject, including a created one; it does not by itself isolate a second **original** agent as the problem. The 1985 article was located but not directly recovered; its quoted argument in the following reply is not credited as a separate full reading.

### Torre: other-ascription need not impersonate the other subject

Stephan Torre, “De Se Knowledge and the Possibility of an Omniscient Being,” *Faith and Philosophy* 23 (2006), 191–200. Complete published paper and notes read; pp.197–198 visually checked. [Journal record and download](https://place.asburyseminary.edu/faithandphilosophy/vol23/iss2/5/).

Torre rejects a simple haecceity-based reduction, pp.192–195, while retaining a substantive de se difference. His properties account, pp.195–198, distinguishes another person's self-ascribing the same property from accurately ascribing that property to the original subject. He favors the second sense for omniscience and denies that omniscience requires an impossible identity-mediated self-ascription to someone numerically distinct. The strongest contextual reading concerns that same ascription, not a blanket inability to have separate self- and other-directed cognitions. If both subjects have F, both may know themselves as F and know the other as F. This is an export-scope guard, not a new defect attributed to his argument. The reply offers a substantive epistemic interpretation; it does not demonstrate an actual omniscient being or settle every phenomenological knowledge question.

### Ganya: a contemporary authorship condition, with conditional scope

Wandile Ganya, “God and the View From Nowhere: De Se Knowledge and Divine Omniscience,” *Sophia*, published 14 August 2026, DOI 10.1007/s11841-026-01169-y. Full publisher HTML article, objections, notes and references read. Publication metadata and article body were directly verified; the cited works were not all independently checked. [Publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11841-026-01169-y).

The Bridge Principle/Self-Location Constraint requires self-ascribed authorship of the actual outcome, conditional on libertarian agency and robust contrastive responsibility. The paper considers reductionism, simplicity with differentiated cognitive aspects, and necessitarianism. It assumes a unique divine agent rather than proving uniqueness. Its denial of modal alternatives is stronger than necessary agreement between agents across different possible outcomes. Its stronger claim that adding an explicit first-person identity to third-person cognition still fails needs more than a substitution slogan: that identity may already supply self-location. We need not settle that dispute here. Even a strengthened concession of direct, non-inferential authorship cognition would not supply exclusive authority; non-inferentiality is our additional concession, not Ganya's independently established condition.

The papers are not five independent confirmations. Torre uses Lewis; Ganya explicitly develops the essential-indexical tradition. Their different replies and qualifications matter more than their count.

## 2. The strongest proposed sovereignty inference

The user's conjunction deserves this strengthened reconstruction:

1. An eligible original possesses complete pure perfection, not merely bare necessary existence or freedom from efficient production.
2. Its cognition includes full truthful self-recognition of its intrinsic powers, actual acts, practical reasons, and their original productive scope.
3. Its will appropriately conforms to the truths about fitting perfected agency that it knows.
4. An appropriate completed original undertaking obtains its complete productive adequacy from that original's own resources; alternatively, original perfection requires universal actual supremacy over every distinct external bearer.
5. A genuinely independent peer would violate the relevant completeness or supremacy condition.
6. The agent must recognize that fact and cannot coherently regard the violating state as its fully perfect exercise.

This is considerably stronger than “both know everything, so each knows that it alone is God.” It keeps practical conformance and actual authorship. But its contested work lies in 4 and the classification in 5. Premises 2–3 remove ignorance or irrational nonconformance as evasions; they do not derive 4.

Conversely, stating the contested condition explicitly does not make its argument circular. A separately warranted pure-perfection principle may entail uniqueness. The proper question is whether its comparative and explanatory grounds justify its **scope and application**, rather than whether it can be obtained from thinner premises alone.

## 3. What the inherited source actually contributes

### A. A real universal-supremacy argument

The retained Akmaliyya, *Majmuʿ*6:125–130, canonical `akmaliyya-canonical-working.txt` L216–240, ranks a bearer on whom everything depends above one whose dependence-domain is partial. L217 expressly considers something outside the supposed realm, so the passage cannot fairly be reduced to a large inventory of created outputs. L218–223 connect the comparison to full creation, power and accomplished willing. L235–240 combine unimpeded efficacy with wise discrimination; arbitrary indifference is not the proposed perfection. [Primary epistle](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/591/).

The inherited `bearer-eligibility/domain-addendum/ADDENDUM.md` and `eligibility-review/REPORT.md` already separate this route from local nonabsorption. Their elementary consequence remains valid: if every original excludes receiving the relevant original completion from another, and every original supplies that same completion to every distinct external actual bearer, A and B cannot both be originals. The second premise must include B and the dependence-respect must match. Mere agreement, object-dependence of a description, or joint contribution to Q is not automatically the prohibited reception relation.

The source's comparative reasons remain positive evidence for universal original sufficiency. This operation neither downgrades them to arbitrary stipulation nor counts their rereading as new support. The remaining substantive dissent denies that **universal relational supremacy** adds a pure excellence beyond already complete wise unborrowed agency. That dissent needs argument; de se truthfulness does not answer it on either side.

### B. Self-description is itself assessed through already true perfection

The same epistle6:137–140, L258–274, explicitly evaluates truthful divine self-description and self-revelation as perfection. L264 includes supremacy over everything other than the bearer, followed by the statement that these are exclusive lordship perfections. L261–262 distinguish false appropriation from truthful self-report by a creature.

This is directly relevant to recognition and conformance, but its argumentative direction is important. The self-description is commendable **because it truthfully expresses the bearer’s warranted perfections**. It does not make an exclusivity claim true merely because the cognition is first-personal. It supplies no license for two subjects to truthfully claim a mutually exclusive attribute.

### C. The source answers the easy logical-impossibility escape

Canonical L069–090 distinguish a coherent pure excellence from contradictory tasks and kind-relative remedies for deficiency. L073 expressly excludes producing something while it remains necessary through itself. L082–090 answer essential nonreceptivity through comparative disqualification: inability to possess a genuine pure excellence need not excuse a supposedly absolutely perfect candidate.

Thus the strongest rival cannot stop at “A cannot make the necessarily original B become dependent while B remains original.” The source need not demand that transformation. It can challenge the eligibility of the entire two-original hypothesis. But this reply remains conditional on the disputed scope really constituting a coherent pure excellence. It cannot promote numerical identity with B, possession of B's first-person identity, or every conceivable exclusivity relation into a perfection. **Different numerical identity is not itself an epistemic deficiency.**

### D. Actual efficacy and object-conditioned knowing remain different

`majmu20-item2032.txt` L001–020, especially L003–008 and L013, addresses actual individual whole provision, not merely disagreement. `minhaj2-item156.txt` L034–041, especially L040–041, makes relational actual efficacy a perfection. A lack of donated intrinsic power does not therefore defeat the source's objection. [Majmuʿ20](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/2032/), [Minhaj2](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/108/156/).

The recovered Dar016 L1012–1054 adds the important distinction between an object conditioning knowledge-of-it and that object giving the knower a perfection. L1052 remains hypothetical. Its surrounding argument recognizes actual knowing as perfection, yet neither authenticates an original peer nor turns an epistemic object into a productive co-supplier. [Dar electronic subsection016](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/dr-taardh-alaql-walnql-016.html).

It would consequently be a mistake to argue either “knowing B depends on B, therefore A is deficient” or “knowing B is harmless, therefore B's irreducibly original productive contribution is harmless.” The kinds of dependence differ at exactly the point still in dispute.

## 4. Concrete tests against the strengthened rival

### Truthful own-centre cognition

Allow A to recognize: it is A; this intrinsic exercise is its own; B is a distinct original; the represented created effect has both original contributors; A is not its sole actual whole provider. Give B the corresponding properly indexed awareness. For the stronger philosophical test, additionally assume awareness of the actual reasons and appropriate epistemic basing of each subject's execution, rather than merely their verbal descriptions. That is a further intensional interpretation, not a result extracted from the existing policy implementation.

No inference from truthfulness requires A to self-ascribe B's numerical identity. Nor does knowing that B knows something as B require A to mistake that as its own centre. A complete epistemic account should specify both own-centre cognition and how other-centre truths and their self-ascriptive roles are grasped; it should not call a missing cognitive interpretation complete by label.

This is a **conditional analysis of added epistemic requirements**, not certification of actual omniscience, intentional uptake or a metaphysically possible two-original world. Semantic centered completeness has its reduced-signature interpretation; it does not by itself verify the stronger action-guiding basing condition. The old `own_policy(original,q)` does not take centered knowledge or the forecast as input, and `joint_end` attaches its end record separately. Owned act indices and the retained conditional means rationale therefore do not mechanically establish that self-knowledge is a reason through which the choice is made. The inherited H8a–H8f architecture still separates first-person significance, uptake, same-subject ownership, because-of control, centre stability and source applicability. A positive realization of this stronger basing relation is an additional model-level obligation, before the further question of actual metaphysical instantiation.

### Standing ability is not actual entire execution

Truthfully knowing a full represented solo repertoire does not entail presently willing every possible output, presently executing every available capacity, or singly supplying the actual joint undertaking. It also does not refute the source's actual-adequacy objection. The subject must know which of these claims is true. Replacing one with another inside a first-person sentence does not close the inferential gap.

### Necessary concordance is not a hidden common controller

Two reason-responsive subjects may, for purposes of the test, necessarily reach the same fitting choice because each knows the same sufficient reasons. From coextensive decisions alone one cannot infer that an additional bearer issues their choices or that one donates the other's intrinsic act. Conversely, correct forecasts do not establish that the reasons are sufficient or that the acts are independently owned.

Necessary concordance may hold across histories with different targets. It neither gives a Cartesian product of individually described choices nor entails that only one total world is possible. Any attempt to force conflict by holding all perfect reasons and willing fixed while changing one output needs an independent admissibility argument.

### Recognition and conformance

Suppose it is independently established that no perfect original may rely on another original for the actual adequacy of its undertaking. A and B cannot truthfully deny the rule or rationally choose a state they know violates it while retaining perfect practical agency. This improves the dialectical demand: the rival must defend the adequacy of the mode, not merely claim the agents are unaware of its cost.

But if the relevant normative classification has not been settled, knowledge of all true norms does not select the affirmative norm by itself. Truth-tracking cognition cannot decide which disputed proposition is true without a warranted account of the object of cognition. “A knows it is perfect” is equally unhelpful if perfection has merely been assigned as a flag.

## 5. Exactly what warrant changes

New bounded gain:

- The chosen literature provides direct controls for de se self-recognition, cognitive/action-guiding ownership, and the difference between knowing another's truth and falsely taking its centre as one's own.
- The strongest examined contemporary authorship condition can be granted without obtaining exclusivity. This makes the test stronger than a bare omniscience extension.
- Applying the inherited self-description passage fixes the direction of argument: truthful recognition of independently warranted authority, then fitting conformance. It does not supply the missing authority norm.

Unchanged:

- The inherited affirmative comparative appraisal of one comprehensive original remains a substantive defeasible judgment.
- The strengthened rival has not earned metaphysical possibility, original-perfection eligibility, a fully adequate divine cognition theory, or independent actual whole provision.
- No new generic uniqueness result follows from de se knowledge alone. No external agent's private perspective is assumed to be a fact that everyone must know as its own.

The next discriminating warrant is narrowly identifiable: an argument that **universal actual supremacy** is a coherent pure perfection of every eligible original, including its domain over a proposed peer; or an independent account showing why genuinely joint original production fully realizes each subject's appropriate agency without foreign completion. A stronger account of owned self-knowledge is useful only if it supplies one of those bridges. The selected literature supplies no such bridge, and the present result makes no claim of exhaustive literature or source-corpus absence.
