## Dretske 2004: later primary argument

[“Psychological vs. Biological Explanations of Behavior”](https://store.behavior.org/httpdocs/resources/157.pdf), *Behavior and Philosophy* 32, 167–177. A current trigger and the events establishing its efficacy answer different questions. Dretske accepts describing a structuring cause as a cause of persisting background conditions (170–171), and footnote 7 marks a presentational change from his earlier book. His animal example makes an already available appearance-indicating state acquire an avoidance-producing role through experience (174–176). The same state participates through different properties: intrinsic properties in immediate triggering, relational indication in recruitment. The crucial claim at 176 is that its indicator role explains why this state was recruited. This gives a developed historical alternative to demanding another simultaneous trigger, but whether recruitment bestows the relevant representational indicator function still needs scrutiny. These are findings from the complete later article, not verified details of the 1993 chapter.

## Reppert 1998: targeted comparison

[“The Argument from Reason”](https://www.theistic.net/papers/V.Reppert/ReppertAFR.pdf), displayed pp.19–25. Reppert argues that merely conventional properties lack the needed causal relevance, challenges medium-independent functional roles, and uses Davidson/Kim to distinguish event causation from efficacy of mental properties. Rational inference requires beliefs to cause beliefs through their propositional contents. He separately challenges a physical account of apprehending universally necessary logical relations. His target is therefore stronger than either a correct output or an explanation of acquired behavior. ## Baker 1991: a specific objection

[Lynne Rudder Baker, “Dretske on the Explanatory Role of Belief”](https://uh.edu/~garson/BakeronDretske.pdf), *Philosophical Studies* 63, 99–111. Baker distinguishes mere indication from having the function of indicating. She objects that if recruitment confers representational function, that already-conferred function cannot explain the same initial recruitment without circularity; indication’s efficacy alone does not prove meaning’s efficacy (107–109). She also distinguishes structuring a response type from explaining an actual reason-guided token. Footnote 17 links an earlier version of her paper to a response to Dretske’s 1990 presentation. This is a verified objection, not a demonstrated final refutation. The complete reply below must be assessed with it.

## Dretske 1991: the actual response

[“How Beliefs Explain: Reply to Baker”](https://www.uh.edu/~garson/Dretske%27s%20Reply%20to%20Baker.pdf), *Philosophical Studies* 63, 113–117. Dretske denies that the causal roles in Baker’s circle are identical. Earlier token recruitment constitutes a later token’s indicator function; that historical property explains its current efficacy, even when it misrepresents. The function is to indicate, not to produce a fixed movement. He also defends structuring explanations of particular tokens while distinguishing response identity from present timing. This is a positive constitutive account of content’s explanatory role, not merely an unanswered appeal to indication. The entire response was read.
