# Independent narrow assessment: O'Connor 2013, section 8

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: the modal transport in section 8 (printed pp. 42–43), read with printed pp. 27, 32–37 and section 7. This is a source-semantic assessment, not a ruling on metaphysical possibility, a new theorem, or a revision of the productive-ground argument.

## Result

Bare de re necessary existence does not suffice for section 8's conclusion. Nevertheless, the strongest source-supported reconstruction is richer: the proposed explanation includes an invariant, exhaustive ontic-dependence structure and an adequately self-explanatory necessary ground. On that reconstruction the transport is valid in S5. The coverage-switch control below does not preserve this richer premise and therefore does not defeat it.

The chapter explicitly supplies the invariant-dependence conception on p. 33; this is not an assumption invented solely to rescue section 8. It also argues for noncontrastive explanatory adequacy in pp. 34–37. It does not establish, against every opponent, that every possible complete explanation must instantiate such a profile. Accordingly, the defensible criticism is a conditionality/exhaustiveness qualification to section 8's dialectical reach, rather than a demonstrated equivocation from necessary existence to necessary explanatory success.

## Target and vocabulary

- C(w): reality at w has a complete explanation under the chapter's standards. These standards permit nonnecessitating productive causes. PCE II permits a nonvacuous explanation of why a contingent truth lacks a true explanation.
- E(g,w): candidate ground g exists at w.
- N(w): there is a de re necessarily existing candidate, represented in the small control by existence of g at every accessible world.
- A(g,w): g supplies an adequate exhaustive grounding profile for reality at w. This includes the necessary ground's nonbrute nature and the appropriate explanation of whatever contingent concreta/events occur; it does not require a necessitating explanation of every contrastive fact.
- H(w): there is a candidate g with both necessary existence and an essentially adequate exhaustive profile, schematically: some g satisfies box E(g) and box A(g). Equivalently for this purpose, a world-invariant dependency structure may supply the requisite coverage. The premise need not be formulated as a single intrinsic property of g.

H is much stronger than N. It is also stronger than g's actually causing one universe, having necessarily existing reasons, or having an essentially fixed intrinsic nature.

## Explicit sufficient premises

1. Modal framework: S5 for absolute necessity, with the transworld identity/quantificational resources needed for de re necessity. O'Connor explicitly assumes S5 in p. 42 n. 20.
2. Adequacy: an instantiated rich profile H supplies C. This is substantive explanatory adequacy, understood noncontrastively, not a modal theorem.
3. Essential coverage: the profile is adequate for whatever reality obtains at each accessible world, not just for the world's actual products. In the H formulation, S5 makes H invariant across the relevant accessibility class.
4. Exhaustiveness: any possible C is supported by some such rich profile. This is needed to reach the unrestricted conclusion that no complete explanation is possible, rather than merely excluding this family of explanation schemas.

With (2)–(4), C and the existence of a rich profile coincide throughout the relevant possibilities. If C held at another accessible world, a rich profile would exist there; its necessary existence and invariant adequacy would make C hold here. Thus not-C here entails box not-C. In a compressed formulation, the substantive bridge is that every possible complete explanation would secure completeness here. S5 transports necessary status; it does not manufacture that bridge from the word 'necessary'.

Another concise reconstruction lets S mean that a candidate complete-explanation schema is instantiated. Assume, globally, C implies S and S implies box C. Then possibility of C implies possibility of box C; S5 yields box C and hence actual C. Again, the work is done jointly by the explanatory and essential-coverage premises, not S5 alone.

## Where the thin reconstruction fails

Even granting throughout the frame that C implies N, not-C does not entail not-N. A necessarily existing object may be present without explaining all reality. Conversely, if a particular necessarily existing object is impossible, complete explanation has not thereby been excluded unless the possible complete-explanation options are exhausted by impossible candidates of the relevant kind.

The description on p. 42 already includes the candidate's explanatory role: it constitutes all reality or is the source of contingent reality. If that role is read as an accidental, world-relative relation, the move from absence of such a role-qualified being to impossibility of one is invalid. An object could remain in existence while ceasing to bear the role. If the description carries the chapter's p. 33 necessary dependency structure, however, this objection misses its intended target. The local paragraph is compressed; the preceding text supplies a principled richer reading.

## Source-semantic findings

### Printed p. 27

The necessary/contingent distinction is a substantive ontological distinction: self-existence versus intrinsic ontological and explanatory incompleteness, with contingent things ultimately depending on a necessary being. This already exceeds the bare modal claim that some individual happens to exist in every world. It motivates the ground-based framework. It does not independently demonstrate that a bare necessarily existing individual's presence guarantees complete explanation.

### Printed pp. 32–33

Page 32 explicitly distinguishes a barely specified necessary cause from sufficiently informative explanation schemas. It considers several mechanisms and ontologies, including indeterministic agency, impersonal generation, and wholly necessary reality. Bare existence is plainly not being treated as a full explanation.

Page 33 is the decisive support for the strongest reconstruction. Tentatively endorsing the relevant existence-explaining schemas is characterized as positing necessary ontic dependence of contingent physical things on a necessary being. The proposed structure is conceived as obtaining for any possible reality. This supplies the invariant coverage conception rather than merely necessary existence.

The same page distinguishes a schema's working, its competitors' problems, and exhaustiveness of the alternatives as separate considerations. It expressly allows tentative preference with reduced confidence when exhaustiveness is lacking. That limits how categorically section 8 can bind an opponent who has not accepted the whole explanatory framework.

### Printed pp. 34–37 and notes 14–15

The positive explanatory work is the productive-ground argument: actual concreta and events may be fully explained by the actual activation of dispositions even when the outcome was not necessitated. The distinction between caused-but-undetermined and wholly uncaused remains intact.

Page 35 n. 14 is especially important. Necessary motivations can explain different alternative choices. Which motivation actually explains an action is allowed to be contingent. Essential coverage therefore must not be misrepresented as every actual causal/explanatory relation being essential.

Page 35 n. 15 adds the essential interdependence of the necessary being's features to avoid brute constitution of its nature. This supports ultimacy but, by itself, does not guarantee that there cannot be an unrelated brute contingent object. The p. 33 global dependency claim supplies the crucial additional coverage. It would overstate the evidence to cite n. 15 alone as a proof of an essentially complete Creator profile.

Pages 35 and 37 state explanatory success conditionally on the schema's truth. PCE II includes substantive explanations of why certain facts lack explanations. The reader should not replace C with unrestricted contrastive PSR or demand determinate contrastive selection among every alternative.

### Section 7, printed pp. 40–42

The Basic Question concerns why the particular contingent objects and events there are exist/occur. Section 7 explicitly distinguishes this from why anything contingent exists. A necessary agent may or may not be required to produce some contingent reality. If production is optional, the existential question need not have the relevant first-order explanation.

This does not itself undermine essential coverage. A necessary ground can be the adequate source of every contingent product in every world while producing different things, or producing no contingent things. In a world without contingent products, totality must still be adequately grounded through the necessary being's nature. Completeness in this sense does not entail necessary creation or necessity of the actual output. An alleged countermodel that allows an independent brute contingent reality while retaining g has instead removed the global dependency premise.

### Section 8, printed pp. 42–43

The impossibility of a genuinely necessary being's contingent absence is sound under the assumed modal framework. Extending that point to complete explanation needs the role/coverage and exhaustiveness premises above. They have contextual support, especially p. 33, but are not consequences of S5 or of the minimal definition of necessary existence.

The argument concerns a categorical metaphysical denial of complete explanation. Merely declining to accept an explanatory schema, or suspending judgment about C, does not assert not-C and so does not incur this modal conclusion. The chapter itself distinguishes objective intelligibility from humans' ability to lay out a full explanation; nothing here reverses that distinction.

## Assessment of warrant and residual issue

- **Explicitly supplied:** S5; the envisaged invariant ontic-dependence structure; the noncontrastive standard; the additional no-brute-constitution condition on the theistic ground.
- **Substantively defended in the chapter:** the legitimacy of nonnecessitating productive explanation and the difference between explained indeterminacy and brute contingency; reasons to prefer explanatory schemas, conditionally on coherence and comparative virtues.
- **Assumed or conditional rather than settled here:** the full metaphysical coherence and adequacy of each rich schema; the completeness of the space of candidate explanations. The p. 29 concession that the nonagential principle-based approach has not been conclusively rebutted reinforces this last limitation. Page 39 identifies a major anti-Humean position as assumed, and p. 40 retains epistemic possibility of failures in completing the favored schemas.
- **Residual issue:** section 8's unrestricted anti-intelligibility reversal binds a critic only given the rich-profile and exhaustiveness assumptions. A critic can dispute those assumptions without having been refuted by S5 alone. The strongest reading is a valid conditional burden-shift inside the chapter's modal explanatory framework, not an independent proof of intelligibility.
- **Not established:** that the author actually equivocates between bare necessary existence and an essential total-explainer role. The p. 33 passage makes the charitable, logically valid reading too well supported to award that accusation on the thin countermodel alone.

## Minimal mechanical control

Files: `modal-premise-independence.py` and its checked result `modal-premise-independence-results.json`.

The control uses two universally accessible worlds and one candidate g. It enumerates only existence and coverage valuations. The witness has g existing at both worlds, adequate coverage false at w0 and true at w1. Complete explanation requires a necessary ground throughout the model, yet not-C at w0 coexists with possibility of C. This checks the thin reconstruction's missing premise.

The same witness fails H: g lacks necessarily adequate coverage. Imposing both rich-profile sufficiency and rich-profile exhaustiveness eliminates every counterexample in this tiny finite check. This verifies what the control does and does not preserve; it is not a proof of the metaphysical premises, a model of the author's full theory, or an assertion that either valuation is metaphysically possible.
