# Collective explanation and the apt existential question

8 October 2026. Bounded T20 source investigation. T20 remains ACTIVE. Earlier reports and the original bearer interface are unchanged. This is neither an integration nor a closure claim.

## Result

Dasgupta 2014 offers **positive, defeasible warrant** for taking an irreducibly collective explanation to be adequate. Its strongest argument is not merely that no contradiction has been exhibited, or that singleton explanations encounter difficulties. Section 9 connects the proposed explanation to what coordinated numerical and individual discourse does: express a system of facts by means whose usefulness depends on their relations to one another. On that interpretation, requiring each item to possess a separate fundamental explanation can impose an explanatory division that the subject matter does not support.

That is a substantive answer to the charge of evasion. It can change which **fundamental** question is apt. It does not make every ordinary question about one person's existence meaningless, nor does it turn the collective explanation into a singleton qualitative ground. The paper expressly distinguishes grounding from **accounting for** a consequence of something plurally grounded (p. 11). A reader must not erase that distinction in either direction: the weaker consequence relation is not a singleton explanation, but its weakness does not by itself discredit the collective explanation.

The strongest remaining objection concerns the transition from the coordinated inferential role of expressions to the metaphysical explanation of the nonsemantic facts they express. The transition is argued abductively, not derived from semantics. It is especially compressed for individuals. This leaves room for a critic to accept the representational story while disputing its explanatory sufficiency, without assuming distributivity or primitive individuality. Conversely, insisting that every adequate explanation must already be an isolated ground for each individual would beg the very question at issue.

For the actual Necessary Being inquiry, the distinction between **which particular** and **whether anything exists** is decisive. The paper's structuralist proposals presuppose an obtaining qualitative or relational basis. They give no autonomous explanation of that basis's actual obtaining. Neither their proposed adequacy nor their preservation of individuals supplies an actual unreceived concrete resource, its intrinsic bearer, or whole-existence reception. They also establish no absence of such resources. The inherited whole-field use of productive Completion therefore remains available, with its substantive application premises still to be defended. Its availability is an inherited result, not a new theorem from this investigation.

## 2. What has to be granted before the positive argument begins

The paper does not purport to establish either that reality is fundamentally qualitative or that mass is fundamentally relational. It argues for what explanatory form is attractive **if** these ontological theses hold. The brief Occamist motivations in §§2 and 6 point back to other work. This prevents the present paper from functioning as an additional independent demonstration of the same qualitative ontology.

The relevant alternative is also not the bare replacement of names by quantifiers. Several qualitative ontologies are admitted. The algebraic account is referenced rather than rebuilt; existential generalizations are allowed as another theorist's candidate basis. Note 47 explicitly separates the author's view from treating those generalizations as fundamental. Thus no argument here licenses silently identifying the author's algebraic obtaining facts with ordinary existential facts that have the same truth conditions.

Individuals are retained in the intended derived discourse. The Max Black discussion preserves two distinct objects rather than identifying qualitative indiscernibility with numerical identity. Its displayed existential formula does not, by itself, exclude additional objects. The retention of objects and their existence truths is compatible with disputing their fundamental priority. It does not settle whether actual resources are intrinsic, unreceived, or productive.

There is a further qualification often obscured by the simplest global presentation. The paper permits a proper subset of individualistic facts to be directly plurally grounded, with other individualistic facts subsequently grounded singly in members of that subset (pp. 8–10, note 22). Consequently, structuralism as a family need not deny every singular explanation of every person. The paper accepts the intelligibility of explaining a person's existence through particular constituents or origins; those explanations are unavailable only as **purely qualitative** explanations of the bottom-level individualistic facts (p. 9, note 23).

This matters to the question under investigation. The ordinary request for an explanation of an individual's existence may remain perfectly apt. The disputed demand is that the fundamental qualitative explanation must divide into individual qualitative explanations all the way down.

## 3. The strongest affirmative argument, reconstructed fairly

The argument has three different jobs. Treating any one as doing all three would distort its strength.

### 3.1 A reason to reject the singleton requirement

First, §§3–4 create pressure against qualitative singleton explanations. A qualitatively specified local region can have a duplicate without the original individual existing. Expanding to the complete qualitative cosmos can meet the necessitation requirement conditional on qualitativism, but creates a relevance problem if the explanandum remains just this person's existence. The objection concerns explanatory relevance, not mere discernibility.

Moving to the whole plurality changes that relevance assessment: a qualitative feature of a distant region can matter to the individualistic facts about that region, even when it is irrelevant to this person's existence considered alone. The argument has some force because matching explanatory scope to the scope of the facts is a familiar explanatory virtue. It need not be read as manipulating a formal relation to evade a counterexample.

However, this step initially removes a difficulty. It does not establish that the proposed collective explanation is actually sufficient. The author recognizes exactly that gap at the opening of §9, p. 18.

### 3.2 A positive explanation of why the collective account can suffice

Section 9 supplies the more consequential response. In the fictional measurement community, numerical predicates coordinate applications so that the ratios of assigned numbers recover underlying mass ratios. There need not be a uniquely privileged material standard. Once the practice is established, the content is expressed through a coordinated system; examining one coordinate in isolation misrepresents the role of the system.

The positive inference is best reconstructed as follows:

1. The underlying relational facts provide the worldly information that the coordinated discourse is organized to express.
2. Its successful use does not require an additional intrinsic magnitude attached to each object independently of that structure.
3. The collective explanation identifies what makes the coordinated assertions true as a system, while respecting the inferential role that gives the discourse its point.
4. Requiring a separate fundamental ground for each numerical attribution would therefore demand additional structure for which that role supplies no need.

This is more than a statement of formal permissibility. It is an explanatory-economy argument with an account of the explananda. It seeks to show why nothing relevant has been omitted. Its force is conditional and comparative, but that does not make it evidentially empty.

The analogous proposal for individuals is that singular terms help organize reasoning about qualitative reality through their coordinated quantificational roles (p. 22). If that account correctly characterizes the relevant factual content, the collective grounding hypothesis becomes attractive for a reason independent of the bare possibility of plural syntax. There need not be a further primitive identity contribution for each name to record.

But the individual case is not developed here at anything like the length of the measurement case. Its main development is referred to the earlier work. This is a limitation of the present paper's positive support, not a proof that the analogy fails or that the earlier material contains nothing helpful.

### 3.3 Why changing the apt question is not necessarily evasion

The best structuralist reply is not: “Your singleton question was apt, but I will answer a weaker question instead.” It is: the apparently independent fundamental explanandum may be an artifact of expressing an interdependent system in separately usable sentences. At the relevant level, explanatory adequacy should be assessed for the complete system whose facts are being expressed.

That response deserves consideration on its merits. Ordinary separately evaluable sentences can coexist with a fundamental explanation that is not separately parcelled out; pp. 14–15 and 22 explicitly permit compositional truth conditions. Separate grammar does not prove separate metaphysical grounds. Nor does the ability to alter one ordinary object's condition, with other conditions fixed, prove that its fundamental ground varies independently. Section 10 addresses that modal inference.

Nevertheless, **this account of aptness is an interpretation of the best positive argument, not a theorem or an explicit 2014 autonomy criterion**. The paper does not establish that every ungrounded singleton is unfit for any explanatory question. Its permitted higher-level singular explanations already rule out that blanket interpretation. In particular, absence of a singleton qualitative ground cannot simply be reclassified as autonomy in the later rationalist sense.

## 4. The strongest objection after granting the positive reply

The serious objection survives without saying that grounding must distribute:

> Does the demonstrated role of the representational system explain the nonsemantic facts it expresses, or only why that system is an economical way to record independently given facts?

This is the investigation's question, not a quotation from the source.

The concern is substantial because an inferentially useful representation can be complete for a specified range of information without determining metaphysical priority. A user of the same numerical system might think intrinsic magnitudes explain why the ratio relations hold. A user of ordinary names and quantifiers might retain primitive individual facts. Functional success alone does not decide between them. Those opponents must, of course, defend their additional ontology; its possibility is not a refutation of structuralism.

Dasgupta anticipates the relevant limitation. The linguistic practice does not deductively entail the grounding thesis (p. 20). The argument is offered as a compelling best explanation, with the ontological antecedent already in play. Likewise, p. 18 mentions a possible sufficiency principle combining relevance with necessitation but does not establish or adopt it as a general theorem that settles the issue. The substantive explanatory appraisal cannot be replaced by checking those two formal boxes.

The objection is strongest when stated under a **settled interpretation** of the expressions. A purely mathematical rescaling supplies another numerical encoding; it does not by itself supply another metaphysically possible world in which the same interpreted assertions have different truth values. Conversely, if the defended ontology and semantics show that the apparent remainder is merely alternative coordination, demanding a worldly explanation of that remainder would indeed be misplaced. The question is which interpretation is warranted, not whether an array of numbers can be rescaled.

For individuals, the same caution cuts both ways. Arbitrarily relabelling a model neither destroys its individuals nor explains their actuality. But the structuralist need not construct a privileged qualitative name for each individual, and the opponent cannot require that as a condition of adequacy. What remains to be warranted is that the full qualitative basis actually explains the actual individualistic plurality, rather than merely sharing its qualitative information. The paper offers an abductive case for that warrant, but no neutral deduction forcing an opponent who accepts the representational role to accept the dependence direction.

This yields a discriminating assessment rather than parity. **The collective proposal earns more than coherence-level standing. Its support is strongest as an internally motivated account of explanatory form within qualitative/relational ontology, and weaker as an independent demonstration of that ontology or of actual existential sufficiency.** The relevant source passages therefore improve the rival without settling its most demanding application.

## 5. Accounting, substitution, and the existential target

`RULE_AND_TRANSPORT_AUDIT.md` gives the exact source-bound rule inventory. Three results matter here.

First, p. 11 defines accounting through logical consequences of a plurally grounded body of facts. The paper explicitly declines to identify this with a relevance-preserving explanation of each consequence. It therefore does not claim to have supplied the very singleton qualitative ground that its earlier arguments deny. A fair assessment must ask whether the collective explanation is appropriate, not credit it with a different form of success.

Second, p. 5 supplies two context-preserving substitution patterns to define derivative grounding. It **does not assume that primitive ground is transitive** under either pattern. They do not license discarding unconsumed explananda, replacing grounded facts by arbitrary logical equivalents, or identifying a plural list with its conjunction. The source argues separately that conjunctions are grounded in their conjuncts. Its rejection of a conjunction-only alternative rests on its explanatory cost, not a deduction that every collective ground must distribute. Nor is this a general prohibition of multiple complete grounds: notes 4 and 12 explicitly permit alternative full grounds of a disjunction. The conjunction objection cannot be transported into a causal nonduplication or common-source principle.

Third, changing the target from this particular individual's existence to the assertion that **something exists** does not automatically fix the projection. Even if one grants that an individual-existence fact grounds an ordinary existential generalization, the second substitution pattern replaces that member while retaining the rest of the grounded plurality. A singleton existential ground would need a further explanatory premise. This is a rule-level limitation; it is not an objection to choosing the whole plurality as the relevant explanandum.

The actual Necessary Being inquiry should not be recast as a demand to identify which qualitatively indiscernible object is which. A successfully explained nonempty whole can address an existential concern without privileged qualitative names. The relevant unresolved issue is whether the obtaining basis of that whole has an adequate existential explanation, and whether the required productive premises follow. The naming problem alone does not answer either question.

## 6. Why the modal objection does not settle the issue

The standard measurement representation/uniqueness result in note 38 is an attributed background result, not a new mathematical contribution of this investigation. It connects a coherent full system of positive ratios with numerical assignments unique up to common positive rescaling. It secures the possibility of coordination, not a preferred normalization, a physical ontology, or metaphysical grounding.

Section 10 responds to the apparent modal underdetermination through mass-counterpart relations based on relational roles. Its interpretation permits ordinary claims of local mass change while preserving relationalism, and recognizes context sensitivity. A rescaled encoding therefore cannot be promoted directly into a countermodel to the author's necessitation claim. Neither can the existence of counterpart interpretations be promoted into authenticated numerical identity across worlds.

Note 45 leaves open which notion of necessity validates the grounding principle. It does not choose a uniform ordinary S5 interpretation, settle the relevant contexts of reduced modality, or construct the inherited global bearer semantics. Its reference to essential necessity is a candidate, not an actual essence with demonstrated existential force. The paper consequently supplies neither a modal contradiction nor the cross-world premises required by the original bearer extension.

No separate executable gauge calculation, tensor construction, sampling extension, or fabricated grounding model was performed. The mathematics already present in the source is sufficient to locate the disputed inference. An additional easy representation calculation would not decide whether the represented nonsemantic facts are adequately explained.

## 7. Effect on whole-field productive Completion

The unchanged interface interprets resources as concrete intrinsic instantiations in the same existential respect. It separates:

- an actual received target;
- an actual relevant resource that is not received;
- an actual intrinsic bearer of the resource;
- reception transport from bearer to intrinsic resource.

Dasgupta's grounding syntax supplies none of those identifications simply by its arity. A complete plurality can be treated as the target, but an explanatory plurality is not automatically a concrete intrinsic resource, an efficient act, one bearer, or one agent. Conversely, qualitative ontology does not automatically exclude all bearers or a coexisting original productive source.

The already established affirmative response remains the strongest one: apply Completion to one received whole-field target, with Realisation and SupportTransport independently warranted. No singleton-distribution premise is required. The present paper does not defeat that conditional inference. It adds a better-motivated reason why **adequate explanation as such** should not simply be defined as the production of the required resource. An exhaustive application of Completion still needs its own substantive argument.

Nor does the paper furnish an actual counterexample to Completion. To do so, one would need an actual received target and the relevant resource failure, not merely a different offered explanation. A qualitative explanation could coexist with an original producer. A description of fundamental obtaining could also remain unexplained or receive further explanation. This paper does not select among those options.

Most importantly, the positive language-role argument begins with a world whose qualitative or relational facts obtain. It does not establish an explanation of that basis's actual obtaining, or show that this further explanatory question is inapt. This assessment does not assume that an empty world is metaphysically possible. Asking about actual obtaining is not a disguised demand for a separate ground of every named individual. The actual-obtaining question remains after the strongest structuralist reply is granted.

## 8. Changed knowledge, surviving alternatives, precise obstruction

**Changed knowledge.** The 2014 paper supplies the missing positive adequacy argument: explanatory scope is supported by a coordinated representational role, not just by plural logical form. It expressly distinguishes consequence-accounting from grounding, states two derivative-ground substitution patterns without assuming primitive transitivity, and marks both the abductive and modal limits of its own argument. It also permits structurally grounded bases with higher-level singleton explanations. These are new source findings for this bounded task, not new independent evidence that actual qualitative ontology is true.

**Surviving alternatives.** An adequate collective qualitative explanation remains a serious option; it can coexist with derived individual facts, ordinary singular explanations, and an original productive source. A singularist qualitative theory can instead revise relevance judgments, although it owes the explanatory defense the paper challenges. An individualist may retain primitive individual grounds but must address the independently motivated economy argument. An eliminativist could reject the relevant derived facts rather than accept an inadequate explanation; the paper acknowledges that option (p. 8 and note 34), without recommending it. None is excluded solely by the displayed plural calculus. Autonomy, bruteness, and further dependence of the actual qualitative basis also remain distinct possibilities; none is established here.

**Precise obstruction.** The source gives defeasible warrant for replacing a demand for separately parceled fundamental explanations with a collective explanatory demand. It does not give an actual autonomous qualitative basis or an argument that collective adequacy entails, excludes, or exhaustively replaces an actual unreceived concrete intrinsic resource. Thus the surviving application question is not simply “Why this primitive identity?” It is: **what warrants the actuality and explanatory status of the whole obtaining basis, and what independently connects that explanation to the productive resource, bearer, and reception premises?**

This completes the bounded reading and adequacy appraisal. It does not certify corpus exhaustion, integrate a conclusion into a protected text, assign a numerical confidence revision, or close T20.
