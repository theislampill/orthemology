## Semantic assessment

- `state_measurable` and `output_measurable` derive finite-input-prefix factorization. Concrete states need not be finite or countable, and synchronous Boolean output requires no measurable concrete-state structure. This is for a fixed machine and fixed supplied initial state. An initial state or valuation selected nonmeasurably from the same future input is outside the theorem.
- `Simulation` is a same-input relation with two substantive local obligations: equal output and preservation by the next-state transitions. It need not be functional, injective, or invertible. Whole streams and then their actual pushforward laws are equal by induction. The finite solver is applied only on the abstract finite carrier, with its inherited finite/decidable/nonempty hypotheses.
- Same-input simulation is distinct from the predecessor's independent-input `infiniteRel`, which determines input-independent states in the atomic-mass solver. A finite echo can simulate itself while failing that independent-input relation. The new proofs do not substitute one relation for the other.
- In `GuardedSimulation`, both states advance along the actual same input. `badAt` is defined on these evolving states, and `badRun` is the countable union of all such events. Its measurability is derived by a finite-prefix Boolean monitor, even for arbitrary state carriers and an arbitrary guard predicate. Outside this whole-run event, the relation is preserved and outputs coincide.
- The TV/defect estimate is therefore a valid common-source coupling bound. The summable epsilon premises concern the actual bad-at-n events. They are not automatically supplied by local transition checks, independently sampled state marginals, an average per-step error, or long finite-prefix agreement. An always-true guard makes the local conditions vacuous and gives the appropriately vacuous whole-run bound.
- `ObserverFamily.State` genuinely depends on the typed valuation. Its initial/out/next fields are explicit compatibility obligations. The substitution square uses the literal checked `eval (psub ...)` identity and the substitution's tracking field; related substitutions then transport observation laws under contextual PER compatibility.
- `observe` syntactically initializes from the evaluated term, and `candidate_source_observation` retains the evaluated source polynomial. However, `start` is an arbitrary supplied function and may ignore that term. Thus this core is an operational interface, not a proof that an actual parser or evaluator is source-faithful. Equal observed laws or rational defect values do not imply source-term equality.

The later Q8/parser/runtime implementation successor is excluded from this core seal and will require a separate audit. No connection to the separate scored-repair controller is asserted here.
