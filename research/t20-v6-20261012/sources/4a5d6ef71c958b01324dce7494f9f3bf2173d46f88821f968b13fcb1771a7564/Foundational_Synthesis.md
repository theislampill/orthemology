# T20 foundational synthesis: preserving affirmative arguments while joining the mathematics

## 1. What this document contributes

The foundational programme must not be reduced to a collection of warnings about what mathematics cannot prove. The supplied T20 record includes affirmative philosophical and source-based investigations, revisions of earlier appraisals, and increasingly explicit accounts of what follows from the premises actually defended. Those arguments deserve their own evidential treatment.

This document therefore has two tasks. It explains how the cross-front mathematics can **carry an affirmative argument through correction without distorting it**, and it provides a new, explicit local-to-common-source bridge for further foundational investigation. It does not replace a source argument with a toy model or promote a finite formal theorem into a metaphysical conclusion whose premises have not been supplied.

The packet atlas and source register preserve dated original reports. In particular, earlier provisional restraint and later affirmative target-local sourcehood appraisals are not collapsed into a single timeless status. A local countermodel defeats exactly the inference it models; it is not automatically a refutation of every developed argument in the same philosophical neighbourhood.

## 2. Five questions that must remain distinct

An argument may involve at least five different questions:

**What is the conclusion?** A source may be necessary, uniquely adequate for a target, common to a whole domain, knowledgeable, willing, wise, authoritative, or the rightful object of a response. These are not synonyms.

**Which premises establish it?** An argument may draw on productive dependence, essential perfection, disclosed intention, a scriptural report, the meaning of an utterance, recipient conditions, or a previously defended philosophical result.

**Which inferential bridge is used?** A conclusion about one effect may require a bridge to a whole domain. A conclusion about a beneficial outcome may require a bridge to an operative reason. A conclusion about authority may require a bridge to the force of a particular communication.

**What was checked?** A finite countermodel, a Lean derivation, a verified arithmetic sign, an interpretation of a passage, and an independent philosophical appraisal check different things. Their strengths should be retained without exchanging their jurisdictions.

**What changed?** A report can be withdrawn, an interpretation refined, a hidden shared premise discovered, or an earlier counterexample shown to target an incomplete account. Correct revision depends on identifying that change rather than merely adjusting the confidence displayed next to a conclusion.

The cross-front contribution makes these distinctions operational. The provenance of an objective, interpretation rule, and admissible response is part of the state that a source-sensitive correction may need to preserve.

## 3. Positive retention: a local defeat need not destroy a surviving ground

Suppose a conclusion has two adequately checked routes, one using source A and one using source B. If A is defeated while B and its complete supporting context remain intact, the B-grounded argument survives. This is not a numerical average between the defeated and undefeated arguments. It is the continued existence of a warrant.

The formal support result in `Cross_Front_Theorems.md`, Theorem H, expresses this by a family of complete support witnesses. A claim loses all represented warrants only when every witness is hit by the revision. Conversely, different report names do not establish independence: a common original source, interpretative rule, or unexamined assumption must appear in the dependency record.

This supplies a positive account of preservation through criticism. The appropriate response to a failed subargument is not necessarily “the entire conclusion is now open”. It is to identify whether the failure reaches each material support route. Likewise, the appropriate response to a new successful argument is not to rewrite an earlier failed argument as successful; preserve both the failure and the new route.

This matters especially in T20, where an early discriminator and a later developed appraisal may have different premises. Chronology is part of the evidence. A later source-based recovery can legitimately restore an affirmative conclusion without making the original, narrower objection irrational at its earlier scope.

## 4. From target-local exclusivity to a common source

### 4.1 The quantifier transition

There is a real difference between

\[
\forall e\ \exists!s\ A(s,e)
\]

and

\[
\exists!s\ \forall e\ A(s,e).
\]

The first says that each effect has a unique source of the specified kind. The second says that the same unique source is adequate for every effect. A two-effect, two-source model separates them unless additional premises connect the source assignments.

This observation is not a blanket rejection of an affirmative sourcehood argument. It identifies the exact interface at which its target-local conclusion can contribute to the common-source programme.

### 4.2 The constructive bridge

Theorem G proves a positive result. Give the effect domain a connected relation. For adjacent effects, establish that a source adequate for both exists. Local uniqueness then forces the source labels to agree across every edge; connectedness propagates that equality through the entire domain.

The research task becomes concrete: **which justified relation connects the targets, and why does each connection require common source adequacy?**

A sourcehood argument establishing local exclusivity can now discharge one premise of a larger theorem. An argument about interactions, common productive dependence, or joint adequacy can discharge another. A coverage argument establishes that the connected domain is the intended domain, not merely one subsystem.

This is a way for multiple foundational fronts to contribute to one another without declaring them already identical.

### 4.3 Aggregation is not a shortcut

One possible route uses composite effects. Suppose an effect domain admits a joint object for two targets; suppose a source adequate for that joint object must be adequate for each component in the relevant original sense. Then a common adequate source for the joint object supplies an overlap edge.

But the crucial premises must be defended. Merely forming the set \(\{e,f\}\) does not establish that it has one individual productive source. Allowing a collection of independent sources to count as one “source” can make a common-source statement true by a change of vocabulary while leaving the intended question unanswered.

Thus the bridge requires an adequate account of source individuation and the inheritance of the relevant adequacy from a whole to its components. It cannot infer a single Creator by packaging several creators into a dependent pair or a set.

### 4.4 Necessity and attributes remain additional content

Even a proved common-source theorem does not, by itself, establish intrinsic necessity, original productive efficacy, or divine attributes. Those require the substantive meanings and arguments of their respective fronts. This handoff does not downgrade any independently established T20 argument on those questions; it does not borrow its conclusion without checking the relevant premise either.

The constructive benefit is a modular proof obligation: sourcehood work, common-domain work, necessity work, and attribute work can supply different parts of an integrated argument while retaining their own scope.

## 5. Wisdom: operative reasons are not interchangeable with beneficial outputs

A beneficial outcome and an action performed for a wise reason are different descriptions. The latter includes a relation among knowledge, relevant circumstances, a means, an intended end, and the agent’s reason for acting.

This does not imply that reasons can never be warranted. The T20 source-based work concerning the ship, wall, mediation, and self-praise is important precisely because disclosed circumstances and intention can add information absent from the bare outcome. The argument must preserve what those disclosures establish rather than discard it in a compressed label such as “useful result”.

The report–audit example in the mathematical contribution gives a structural parallel. Two states can agree on each separately retained expectation while differing in the joint relationship needed for the next inference. Likewise, separately recording “the action occurred” and “someone benefited” does not necessarily preserve “this benefit, in these circumstances, was an operative reason for this action”.

The parallel is a representation requirement, not an identification of Wisdom with a statistical correlation. The substantive warrant for a disclosed reason comes from the source argument, the meaning of the communication, and the grounds for accepting it.

### A positive inference pattern

An affirmative account can have the following shape:

- The relevant communication is adequately established and correctly attributed.
- Its contextual meaning discloses a reason for a particular act, not merely an incidental consequence.
- The reason relates the act to an appropriate end under the circumstances identified.
- The argument’s account of the agent and the source rules out the relevant defeating interpretations.

When these premises are defended, preserving them preserves an affirmative Wisdom argument. A counterexample containing only an unintended beneficial outcome does not refute this richer inference, because it omits material premises.

The role of the new audit interface is to identify what must be reconsidered when one of those premises changes. Revising a source can change the interpretation or justificatory objective, not merely a final belief weight. The dependency-directed replay theorem then specifies a safe recomputation frontier.

## 6. Recipient conditions and essential perfection

The repentance and responsive-receptivity sequence brings another important distinction into view: **a condition for a particular response is not automatically a source from which an essential perfection is received**.

A minimal logical model makes the separation explicit. Let a faculty or perfection be present independently, while a particular responsive act occurs when an appropriate occasion and volitional condition obtain. Changing the recipient’s condition changes whether that act is appropriate or realised. It does not follow merely from that change that the faculty itself was acquired from the recipient.

Such a model only establishes non-entailment: the inference from “this response has a condition” to “the responder lacked an essential perfection” is not valid without further premises. It is not a replacement for the developed theological argument about the nature of the response, the source’s perfection, or the meaning of the relevant passages.

The affirmative contribution is to preserve the distinct dependencies. A record should distinguish:

\[
\text{condition of the particular act}
\quad\text{from}\quad
\text{ground of the relevant faculty or perfection}.
\]

A revision to the first does not automatically invalidate the second. Conversely, calling something a recipient condition cannot make a genuine dependence objection disappear; the source argument must actually establish the distinction.

The same discipline applies to self-praise. An account of an immanent reason for praise need not be equivalent to a need for an external audience’s approval. The presence of one motive in an ordinary example does not prove that every instance of the same verbal act has that motive. Source-specific affirmative reasoning must retain its actual account of the act and reason.

## 7. Knowledge, Will, and a shared-source conclusion

A tensor record can describe patterns of dependence, and a dynamics can describe a transition toward an objective. Neither mathematical form alone entails knowledge or volition. Yet this does not make the mathematical work irrelevant to attribute arguments.

It can clarify what must be represented for an existing argument to remain faithful: the alternative possibilities considered, the relevant distinctions recognised, the dependency between a chosen act and an end, and the source-specific premises connecting such an act to Knowledge or Will.

The useful cross-front question is not “does a gradient resemble a decision?” It is: **does the representation preserve the features that the defended attribute argument actually uses?**

A posterior describing uncertainty about a source is also not the source’s own knowledge. A mathematical parameter identifying a preferred state is not, without more, the source’s will. Preserving this distinction lets the numerical and philosophical work contribute without smuggling a change of subject into the proof.

A common-source bridge can help unify attribute inferences only when the premises concern the same source in the same relevant sense. If distinct target-local arguments establish different attributes, their aggregation requires justified identity of their source referents, not merely reuse of one variable name.

## 8. Objective norm-fixing and corrective objectives

An optimisation problem requires an objective, admissible domain, and constraints. The fact that a dynamics decreases its objective establishes a relationship between that dynamics and that objective. It does not independently establish that the objective expresses the correct norm.

The positive connection runs in the other direction as well. Once an objective norm and its application conditions have been adequately defended, a faithful mathematical representation can help implement corrections toward it, test whether the intended constraints survive, and identify which apparent improvements were produced by changing the standard rather than satisfying it.

This is why the new contribution records the provenance of the objective and metric. If a source revision changes what the objective should be, passive reweighting of an old endpoint may not meet the revised retrospective contract. The one-atom gradient example shows this without any ambiguity about numerical precision.

A moving-objective argument needs an explicit distinction between improvement relative to one standard and change of the standard. For a differentiable path,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}E_t(z_t)=\partial_tE_t(z_t)+dE_t(z_t)[\dot z_t].
\]

Even when the second term is nonpositive for a gradient descent, the first can increase the displayed energy. This does not refute correction; it explains why its claim must name the objective and its currentness.

The relevant role for GDOPL is analogous at the governed project level: an improvement is an admissible, evidence-backed transition under the declared project obligations, not a large amount of activity or a lower number obtained by dropping a constraint. This packet does not alter an RLGWO, project requirement, merge authority, or release status.

## 9. Authority, attestation, communication, and response

The late source-bound response work should be represented as more than “the speaker is authoritative”. An inference to a particular response can depend on several distinct relations:

- the source’s rightful authority;
- the authenticity and attribution of this communication;
- whether the source actually adopts or attests the relevant content;
- the communicative force of this particular utterance;
- the recipient, circumstances, and continuing applicability conditions.

These are conceptual fields, not a compulsory Bayesian factorisation. They can be related without being numerically independent.

A source may possess authority while a particular utterance expresses permission, recommendation, praise, a conditional assurance, or an obligation. The authority premise alone does not choose among these speech acts. Once the force and conditions are adequately established, however, the argument can have a positive, determinate conclusion about the fitting response.

A finite source certificate can verify that the record contains the declared evidence and the permitted inferential steps. Its checker does not turn an uninterpreted string into an authenticated communication by itself. The source-facing warrant and the finite certificate play complementary roles.

Historical revision is particularly important here. Retaining what was actually said and previously believed can coexist with revising the current interpretation. The record should not erase the historical utterance merely because the current reconstruction changes, nor treat an obsolete interpretation as still current because its original file hash is unchanged.

## 10. Identity, copying, and sufficient representations

T20’s identity and completion work can be connected to representation sufficiency without equating every notion of identity.

Two encodings may preserve all the declared observations and future operations. That supplies an appropriate behavioural or semantic equivalence for that contract. It does not automatically establish numerical identity of persons, subjects, sources, or historical events.

Likewise, a copy can preserve a theorem’s meaning while having a different provenance, cost history, or authority status. A synchronised state can match current outputs while differing under a future withdrawal. This is the concrete reason the earlier sidecar theorem and the new audit-profile construction matter.

The type-theoretic distinction between the original calculus and an explicitly extended calculus is another instance of this discipline. Adding sound rules can provide derivations that were not available before. It should not be described as proving that the original rule system already possessed them. Conversely, a proved gap in the original system should not be repeated as a permanent obstacle after an explicitly sound extension has supplied the required capability.

Finite effective certificates and a broad semantic interpretation also remain distinct. A witness for one statement can be mechanically checked without requiring a universal procedure deciding every semantic statement. This is a positive design pattern for warranted research, not merely a limitation.

## 11. A compositional interpretation contract

A useful formal interface has three layers:

\[
\text{source history and warranted interpretation}
\longrightarrow
\text{declared mathematical state and operations}
\longrightarrow
\text{checked outputs and permitted conclusions}.
\]

Each arrow has an obligation. The first must explain why the mathematical variables and operations represent the intended objects. The second must preserve the relevant invariants and provide the claimed numerical or proof evidence. A source-sensitive revision must update both arrows when their premises change.

For example, suppose a conclusion depends on a current interpretation and on a numerical comparison. If the source revision leaves the interpretation intact but changes a likelihood slightly, the sharp relative-error bound may preserve the comparison. If it changes the meaning of the relevant communication, a numerical stability bound does not establish that the conclusion is unchanged. The interpretation dependency must be reappraised.

This provides a route to selective, justified repair: retain the parts whose warrants remain intact; recompute the affected numerical or formal parts; reopen only the source-facing premises actually reached by the revision. It avoids both indiscriminate scepticism and automatic promotion of a computational result into a foundational conclusion.

## 12. The contribution to take forward

The strongest constructive foundational addition is the **overlap-and-coverage bridge for source unity**. It gives the affirmative target-local work a precise role in a larger argument and identifies the substantive premises that further research should try to establish.

The strongest methodological addition is **source-revision-aware preservation of affirmative warrants**. It combines the proof-support result with the replay theorem: a source revision can affect a likelihood, an objective, an interpretation, a rule, or a permission, and each affected dependency has a different repair obligation.

The strongest numerical addition is a **two-part decision margin**. A bounded relative evidence error and a bounded geometric replay error can jointly preserve a declared decision when its value gap is large enough. This is a positive safeguard, not a licence to replace sourcehood, authority, or Wisdom with a scalar score.

These contributions should be reviewed alongside the original affirmative T20 reports, not used as a substitute for them. The atlas preserves those reports and their historical sequence so that a later recovery is not silently erased by an earlier objection, and an earlier failure is not silently rewritten as a proof.
