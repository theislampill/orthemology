# The first-order price of an unresolved scoring standard

Status: ordinary mathematical candidate. This derives a quantitative obstruction coefficient from the common-repair geometry rather than treating every incompatible pair as a separate theorem. No historical novelty or meniscus claim.

## 1. The exact variational object

Let V⊂R^n be finite nonempty, C=conv(V), p∈relint(C), T=span(C−p), and let W_1,...,W_m be a finite nonempty family of positive-definite symmetric matrices. Put B_i(x,y)=xᵀW_i y. Fix h outside T. For small epsilon>0, b_epsilon=p+epsilon h is incoherent. Define

    R(epsilon)= min_{c∈C} max_{i,v∈V} [L_i(c,v)−L_i(b_epsilon,v)].

Negative R means a strict common improvement is available. Positive R is the smallest unavoidable worst-case worsening. If the tolerance parameter is constrained to be nonnegative, its minimal required value is max(0,R), not the signed R itself. This is a common-action adversarial-standard objective, not an average over an assumed prior on standards.

Define the first-order obstruction coefficient

    kappa(h)= min_{z∈T} max_{i,v∈V} 2 B_i(p−v,z−h).

The minimisation is a finite linear programme on T, after introducing an epigraph variable. It has a minimiser. Its value is nonnegative.

## 2. Quantitative characterization

For some finite K and all sufficiently small positive epsilon,

    |R(epsilon)−epsilon kappa(h)| <= K epsilon^2.

Moreover:

- kappa(h)=0 iff every W_i has the same tangent projection of h
- if kappa(h)>0, every common repair suffers worst-case worsening at least (kappa(h)/2)epsilon for all sufficiently small epsilon
- if kappa(h)=0, the common projected repair supplies a strictly negative worst-case regret of order epsilon²; because the metric family is finite, min_i ||Q_i h||²_Wi>0

Thus incompatible normative geometries have a LINEAR leading robust-repair cost, while the improvement supplied by a known score is QUADRATIC. Absolute costs still tend to zero; the ratio of unavoidable common-action harm to oracle improvement can diverge. This is not a statement that ordinary approximate repair gets absolutely worse as the disturbance shrinks.

### Proof that kappa is attained and nonnegative

Take one metric W_0. Since p is relative-interior, C contains a tangent Euclidean ball B_T(p,r), r>0. Positive definiteness gives m_0>0 with B_0(u,u)>=m_0||u||². For u∈T,

    max_{v∈V} 2 B_0(p−v,u) >= 2 r m_0 ||u||.

The maximum over V equals the maximum over C, and the bound follows by testing an opposite tangent point or by the dual norm of W_0 restricted to T. Therefore the finite maximum defining kappa is coercive in z and attains a minimum. Its lambda-weighted average over truth vertices for any one metric is zero, where lambda_v>0 and sum lambda_v v=p. Its maximum is consequently nonnegative.

It equals zero exactly when every constituent linear form is <=0 and their positive barycentric averages are zero. Thus all forms vanish, giving B_i(t,z−h)=0 for every t∈T and every i. Positive definiteness makes z the corresponding metric projection; equivalence follows.

### Proof of the expansion, including minimiser control

For c=p+u with u∈T, the exact excess loss is

    2 B_i(p−v,u−epsilon h)+B_i(u,u)−epsilon² B_i(h,h).

The minimiser u_epsilon exists since C is compact. Testing u=0 bounds R(epsilon) above by a constant times epsilon for epsilon<=1. The W_0 tangent-ball bound gives a lower bound of the form

    max excess loss >= 2r m_0 ||u|| − H epsilon − J epsilon²,

with finite H,J independent of u. Hence ||u_epsilon||<=M epsilon for some M at all small scales. No O(epsilon) localisation of a minimiser is assumed.

Write z_epsilon=u_epsilon/epsilon. These vectors lie in a fixed compact ball of T. On that ball the quadratic remainder B_i(z,z)−B_i(h,h) is uniformly bounded over finite i. Therefore

    R(epsilon)/epsilon >= kappa(h)−K_1 epsilon.

Conversely choose a minimiser z_* of the first-order problem. Relative-interiority ensures p+epsilon z_*∈C for all small epsilon. Evaluating there gives

    R(epsilon)/epsilon <= kappa(h)+K_2 epsilon.

Take K=max(K_1,K_2). For kappa=0, use the exact common projection identity, not merely the O(epsilon²) estimate, to obtain strict common improvement. QED.

## 3. Exact binary interval formula

Let b_epsilon=(1/2+epsilon,1/2), 0<epsilon<=1/4, and let the unresolved normalised weight be alpha∈[l,u]⊂[0,1]. Set m=(l+u)/2 and d=(u−l)/2. Among coherent q∈[0,1], the exact minimax solution is

    q_*=1/2+m epsilon−d epsilon²,
    R(epsilon)=d epsilon−m(1−m)epsilon²−2md epsilon³+d² epsilon⁴.

In particular kappa=d, half the unresolved weight interval's diameter.

Proof: the two worst-case losses are attained at opposite interval endpoints:

    f_0(q)=q²−1/4−l epsilon(1+epsilon),
    f_1(q)=(1−q)²−1/4+u epsilon(1−epsilon).

Their crossing is q_*. It lies in [0,1]. f_0 is increasing and f_1 decreasing there, so their maximum is uniquely minimised at the crossing. Substitution gives the polynomial formula. The same lower bound holds for real q outside [0,1], so inadmissible reports cannot improve it.

This gives an exact tolerance criterion: a common coherent repair with worst-case worsening <=eta exists iff R(epsilon)<=eta. For a singleton norm d=0, the value is negative and equals the familiar oracle gain with a minus sign. For fixed unresolved width d>0, any eta=o(epsilon) ultimately fails. The degenerate norm endpoints 0 or1 are permitted by the binary optimisation formula but not by the general positive-definite geometric theorem; that distinction is explicit.

## Normalisation and prior-art qualification

The zero set of kappa is the common-normal compatibility condition, and adding a
tangent vector to h leaves kappa unchanged by translating the optimisation
variable. Its numeric value is NOT invariant under independent positive
rescalings of candidate scores: the max compares their numerical loss units.
Any use as a quantitative cross-domain diagnostic must specify those units.

The general first-order minimax differentiation mechanism is classical. The
independent prior-art comparison locates Meitz–Shapiro (2025), Proposition 2.3,
as a relevant multiplier/directional-derivative result. The exact local score
formulation and explicit O(epsilon²) remainder are the work checked here; they
are not a claim to invent minimax sensitivity analysis.
