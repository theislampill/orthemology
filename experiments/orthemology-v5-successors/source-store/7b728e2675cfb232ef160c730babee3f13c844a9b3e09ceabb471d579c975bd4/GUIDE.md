# Orthemology Twentieth source and reading guide

## Scope and use

This guide records what was actually studied, where to return to it and what the study changes. It supplements the preserved Nineteenth Arabic Volume Reading Index rather than replacing that historical record. Author: dot, an OpenAI-powered AI research assistant.

Completed-body snapshot: 7 October 2026. Darʾ 8–9, Minhaj 2–3, the received original Sawaʿiq and Shifa are complete within their stated electronic boundaries. Jamiʿ al-Masaʾil’s sixth collection is complete within its stated mixed text-and-scan boundaries. Miftah Dar al-Saʿada’s entire received electronic authorial body and preliminary layer are also complete, with separately bounded scan controls. Al-Nubuwwat and al-Safadiyya are included as complete selected electronic bodies with separately bounded print controls. All eight ordered study groups are now complete within the individually stated source boundaries. The [reading index](READING_INDEX.json) gives source URLs, hashes, exact scope and overlapping rereads; the [specialist questions](SPECIALIST_REVIEW_QUESTIONS.md) identify the unresolved inferential issues. Compiling this guide adds no primary-reading credit. In the final archive, the index is at t20/reading/READING_INDEX.json and the questions are at t20/reading/SPECIALIST_REVIEW_QUESTIONS.md. The readable guide is filed alongside them; the document-format guide is in t20/reports/.

The method has two stages. First, traverse a definite received body continuously and preserve its broader argument, including material without an immediate project application. Then revisit linked passages and test earlier interpretations against the rest of the work. Acquisition, exact range accounting, comprehension, textual reliability, source attribution and truth of the argument are distinct achievements. The hash and coverage checks certify the first two; substantive notes and appraisals address the others without automatically settling them.

A selected electronic volume body is not the same as every page of a physical critical edition. Local electronic paragraph and line identifiers are not printed page numbers. Scan positions are one-based where stated. The guide retains definite variants, suspected errors, editorial conjectures and unresolved losses under different descriptions. No complete second traversal is claimed for the new ordered studies.

The source arguments are attributed to their authors. Opponents' premises, dialectical concessions, transmitted reports and editorial comments are kept separate. Historical cosmography, natural history and legal or sectarian polemic are not newly certified empirical knowledge or current advice.

### D01 A sound proof is not a compulsory learning sequence

Locators: volume 8 opening, especially P0054–P0067; P0104–P0115; P1867–P1871.

The text quotes an opponent's proposed exclusive chain of knowledge, then challenges its exclusivity. It permits a sound argument without requiring every knower to have first acquired knowledge through that argument. It also distinguishes knowing a proposition, knowing that one knows it, and accurately identifying the grounds of one's knowledge. A later proof may clarify or confirm earlier knowledge and be mistakenly credited with creating it.

Relevance: the formal foundational dependency graph is not automatically a universal psychology of warranted recognition. The source is strongly affirmative about natural recognition; merely accepting the possibility of basic knowledge is a weaker current assessment.

### D02 Natural orientation and assistance

Locators: volume 8 P1307–P1318 and P1591–P1611.

The account is not articulated neonatal omniscience or a wholly neutral capacity equally directed towards every belief. It distinguishes directional disposition, receptive powers, triggers, assistance and impediments. Some arguments allow teaching while preserving the disposition; others challenge an endlessly necessary chain of external teachers as the explanation of first recognition.

Relevance: any future epistemic model must distinguish a faculty, its proper conditions, its actual exercise and the warrant of a particular token judgement. The passage does not supply a new modern empirical experiment.

### D03 Making preserving and intrinsic action

Locators: volume 8 P0396–P0422.

The source positively ranks originating and preserving above originating alone. It simultaneously distinguishes the maker's intrinsic act from material receptivity and powers that preserve an arrangement. Causing a condition, moving, commanding or functioning as an end is not yet complete origination of the bearer and all its constitution.

Relevance: a source-mode audit must preserve both the affirmative perfection comparison and the act/product distinction. It cannot infer a defect from every condition indiscriminately, or assume that real local efficacy is already complete existential sourcehood.

### D04 Causal nonduplication agreement and foreknowledge

Locators: volume 9 section 016:627–650, 700–779, 869–896.

The author explains genuine cooperation through differentiated contributions and gives a substantive account of same-respect nonduplication. He explicitly addresses harmony, partition, turn-taking and the proposed independence of cooperating agents. Lawful concomitance is distinguished from productive circularity; the further claim that two complete independent agents cannot be sustained by that concomitance has its own argument.

The later discussion distinguishes conceiving an intended act, knowing that it will occur and knowing that it has occurred. Self-knowledge does not manufacture its bearer. Knowledge related to one's own act is not automatically a donation from an independent other.

Relevance: the positive arguments deserve assessment as arguments. Their inferential burdens include concrete contribution individuation, unilateral efficacy, the transition from product interdependence to producer dependence and the scope of full original provision. The attestation programme must also retain the separate knowledge and ownership roles.

### M01 Necessary attributes and independent bearers

Locators: V02L100–110; V03L254–257.

The argument distinguishes something belonging to a thing's own perfection from a separate item merely appended to it. It then separates meanings of part, otherness, dependence and necessity. Inseparable attributes need not be externally assembled entities or individually independent bearers. Likewise, two things sharing the predicate necessary do not contain one numerically shared extramental necessity-object plus differentiators.

Relevance: a proof of unity cannot quietly reintroduce that rejected universal-as-component argument. Conversely, the legitimacy of attribute plurality does not automatically prove that every proposed plurality of original bearers is merely one being's predicates.

### M02 Realised efficacy as perfection

Locators: V02L114–119; especially L117–119.

The source argues from interdependence of products to a dependence relevant to their makers and explicitly counts realised efficacy as perfection. The concrete human material-and-sustenance example targets the connected created order. Its power argument distinguishes intrinsic capacity from capacity conferred by another, rejecting coequal original mutual conferral while allowing derivative cooperation.

Relevance: this is stronger than a bare one-effect-one-cause slogan. The disputed contemporary question is whether another contribution's necessity for a composite result shows externally supplied intrinsic completion, or whether a fitting limited role remains possible under the proposed source profile.

### M03 Agreement and voluntary particularisation

Locators: V03L236–276.

Power, will, sufficient exercise and actual effect are distinguished. Not everything within power is done. Complete will together with power entails the appropriate effect; a permanently complete cause for every temporally changing effect is a different claim.

The unity discussion explicitly considers necessarily concordant wills, conflicting wills, shared benefit, differentiated work and foreknowledge. Its foreknowledge reply observes that knowledge of the occurrence does not independently explain the productive will upon which the occurrence depends. The author treats mutually conditioned will as partial-cause circularity. These are not missing objections discovered by the present research.

Relevance: the live assessment must test that transition while preserving the author's distinction between concomitance and productive borrowing. Finite models of partial powers do not automatically satisfy his stronger original-source qualifications.

### M04 Guidance signs and fair argument

Locators: V02L163–168, 202–215, 270–272; V03L69–80, 194–202.

Refuting alternatives is not automatically a proof of a preferred position, and defending falsehood for one's side is criticised. The prophetic discussion includes character and wisdom recognition rather than a single exclusively required miracle-first sequence. It distinguishes deliberate certification, a truthful certifier, extraordinary events and counterindications in the case of false claimants.

Relevance: source-owned veracity and identification of an actual attestation remain distinct. The wider historical, legal, devotional and polemical material is retained in the continuous notes rather than omitted because it does not immediately fit the formal proof.

### S01 Positive perfection and strict supremacy

Locators: printed 231–233; electronic 002:1159–1210 and 1335–1348.

The source grounds positive predicates in nondefective perfection and the superior entitlement of a giver to the perfection it bestows. It argues that complete dominion and strict highestness exclude a coordinate equal. This is stronger than weak maximality, which can permit tied maxima. The pure-perfection classification and strict scope of supremacy therefore require their own appraisal.

The author himself distinguishes generic necessary existence from adequate recognition of the particular living, willing, knowing and acting God at 002:355–362. Bare necessary existence must not be credited with the entire qualified profile. Nor does a weaker model omitting that profile refute the stronger argument.

### S02 Bearer attribute act and product

Locators: 002:1066–1071; 003:395–428; 004:148–159.

The work distinguishes external productive dependence from inseparable intrinsic qualification. It also separates divine creating from the created product and genuine creaturely action. Created life, knowledge and power are not mere empty metaphors. Shared meaning is compatible with unequal and differently grounded realisation.

Relevance: the source supplies strong controls against erasing subordinate agency or transferring the properties of a created bearer to the divine bearer solely because both predicates have intelligible common content.

### S03 Responsive action without acquired constitutive perfection

Locators: 003:1152–1192 and 004:16–36; 004:338–344.

Complete self-knowledge, love and praise are defended alongside genuine responses to repentance, prayer and actual events. The account distinguishes an outsider supplying a missing constitutive perfection from an occasion for an intrinsically wise response. Successive goods cannot all occur simultaneously, and forgiveness after wrongdoing cannot precede that wrongdoing.

The efficacy of names and wisdom is nevertheless strong: they are not inert labels. Reading those claims together supports an active nature and wise ordered particularisation, without automatically proving either a uniquely necessary token cosmos or unrestricted contingency of every token act.

### S04 Meaning veracity and evidence ranking

Locators: chapter 12, especially 001:527–557; 001:1401–1405 and 1472–1473; printed 474 / combined PDF 552.

The author distinguishes understanding intended content from establishing its truth. The account of knowledge, expressive ability and sincere guidance occurs in Ibn Taymiyya’s reported debate at 001:527–548. Ibn al-Qayyim’s own narrated encounter at 001:549–557 develops the false-prophet-support argument. The local meaning/truth distinction at 001:1472–1473 addresses a hearer who already accepts prophethood; it cannot independently supply that acceptance. The work nevertheless permits context-sensitive indirectness and protective allusion, so an unrestricted ban on every misunderstanding would overstate it.

The attestation argument holds that a wise, capable, informed source would not support a false prophetic claimant in the described way. Historical career claims and identification of support as deliberate attestation remain additional evidential premises.

The directly checked evidence-ranking passage distinguishes decisive and probable evidence regardless of rational or transmitted vehicle. The work rejects endlessly imagined defeaters while also recognising genuine errors, forged reports, competence limits and practical uncertainty. Its legal discussions distinguish absence of a known dissenter from demonstrated consensus and require fair treatment of reasoning even amid deep doctrinal disagreement.

### H01 Genuine secondary efficacy

Locators: e3–4,25–26,50–54,79–80,88,98, 143–151, 206, 278, 305–306.

The author rejects stripping fire, water, medicine, perception and ordinary effort of their real powers. A cause can matter without being an independently sufficient creator; instruction can be a condition or part-cause, and one message can lead to different acquired responses. Sheer absence is not a positive efficient producer.

The work distinguishes the creature's real doing from God's creating that act and its enabling order. The attribution of a deed to the creature is not merely metaphorical. The conclusion supports the separation of original provision, subordinate efficacy, intrinsic act and moral ownership. It does not by itself establish that a proposed coequal original has the same permissible role as a dependent creature.

### H02 Ability to act alone and actual whole production

Locators: e49–54 and e143–151.

E50 permits cooperation when neither can do the whole, alternation, and shared carrying where each has the capacity to carry alone. It rejects the distinct description in which each actually performs the same whole effect independently, because such independence excludes the other's performance. The permitted capacity case is not a claim that each actual partial exercise is separately sufficient.

At e145 an initial argument is voiced by a Jabri interlocutor, but the Sunni reply at e146 expressly endorses same-numerical-effect independent-production impossibility and then affirms dependent creaturely causation. E150 further endorses mutual-hindrance reasoning against intrinsically powerful coequals. Treating all this as merely an opponent's claim would be false. Its stronger premises still require their stated defence.

Relevance: the source supports a substantial nonduplication position, while the narrow whole-alone contradiction cannot automatically establish every broader nonexclusive sufficiency claim. The formal controls preserve that distinction.

### H03 Wise timed action and full sourcehood

Locators: e205–209 and e219–221.

The reply to the purpose-implies-defect objection distinguishes intrinsic wisdom, externally supplied perfection and realising a loved end at its fitting time. General agreement that God has no defect does not settle whether a particular property is a defect. Appropriate nonoccurrence before a fitting time is not automatically a lack of perfection.

The account positively values actual creating and wise manifested kingship. Its no-external-completion reply says that the required conditions themselves come from the original source. That gives the complete account coherence, but the universal-origin premise invoked in the reply must not be counted as independently proved by that same invocation.

### H04 Integrated order and ultimate ends

Locators: e231–234 and e304–307.

The unity argument invokes heterogeneous products, organ cooperation, mutual need, division of labour, continuity through replacement and agents serving wider ends they do not individually intend. This is a positive coordination argument, not merely a word for regularity. The concluding final-end argument also requires a determinate ultimate object rather than an abstract universal.

The stronger transitions to exhaustive unborrowed provision and a sole ultimate object remain substantive. A damaged consequent at e232 is present in the raw HTML and has not been conjecturally filled. The surrounding positive argument is retained despite that local gap.

### H05 Wider matters retained

The complete notes preserve transmission criticism, the distinct levels of decree, Adam and Moses, repentance, ordinary means, intention and coercion, natural history, acquired habits, privation, justice, punishment, devotional formation, names and referents, children and natural disposition. These matters are part of the book's argument, not disposable material because their present formal application is unclear.

The punishment-duration inquiry at e252–265 distinguishes represented advocacy, favourable authorial assessment and an explicit local limit. E254 introduces one party’s arguments; e260 evaluates a weak report; e264 gives a favourable first-person assessment with an inquiry limit; e265 continues the favourable use of the argument. The limit is not evidence of detached neutrality. Natural-history and remedy claims remain historically attributed. E163 expressly limits one response to defeating an opponent's use of a verse rather than explaining the justice of the first misguidance. This is an important source-internal distinction between answering an objection and supplying a complete positive account.

### J01 Cooperation standing ability and full exercise

Locators: printed 87–98; E091–102.

The author rejects circular productive priority while permitting reciprocal co-presence, partial cooperation and appropriate instruments. The legal discussion at printed 90–94 distinguishes one numerical item from multiple rulings whose practical enforcement overlaps. Finding two suitable descriptions does not by itself establish two independently sufficient legal causes; imagined alternative causes require evidence.

Printed 95 distinguishes each agent’s act, contribution and aggregate outcome, and expressly grants that one partner could sometimes have performed the work alone. Printed 96 then treats another’s actual contribution as inhibiting complete sole exercise and ranks that inhibition as inconsistent with full divinity. Printed 97 says contrary wills alone do not exclude cooperation; printed 98 directly addresses necessary agreement.

Relevance: the source does not overlook solo ability or concord. Its further inference needs the stated numerical-effect and fitting-exercise principles. Distinguishing sole from joint production does not, by that distinction alone, establish every normative or modal ranking applied to an original source.

### J02 Possessed powers and jointly attained ends

Locators: printed 179–182; E183–186.

Each cooperator has power, awareness, intention and a sought end, while the intended attainment can require both acts. The preceding context allows instrumental assistance and strength produced through cooperation; it excludes a closed loop in the originating principles needed to explain that activity. The final contrast permits causes to exist with one another without being produced through one another, and ends to coexist without being pursued for one another.

Relevance: a common cause or end is a coherent proposal, but its possibility is not already proof that every co-present pair derives from exactly one source. Neither are the finite cooperators being presented as uncreated or omnipotent.

### J03 Created means and accomplishment descriptions

Locators: E211–216.

The author affirms action through real created means. A contributory cause can fail through an absent condition or an impediment; a genuinely complete condition complex is a different description. Teaching can mean instruction that fails to produce learning, or an accomplishment that includes achieved learning. The latter description has added the recipient’s condition to the instructor’s act. The claimed uniqueness of divine will as a complete cause remains an additional theological assertion.

### J04 Relational perfection and timely response

Locators: printed 62–66; E066–070.

The love-and-joy argument allows a genuine new relational exercise without creature-supplied need: God originates the worship in which He rejoices. Several alternative replies are offered dialectically, rather than all adopted as one theory. 

### J05 Actual order and appropriate ultimate ends

Locators: printed 119–129; E123–133, E145–154 and E196–203.

The ultimate-end discussion adds normative and experiential considerations, while the celestial-order passage offers positive actual-world evidence. Their historical physics, universal value judgements and comparative force remain assessable premises.

The middle-section appraisal provisionally favours common creation as a plausible, preferable unifying inference for the actual coordinated dependent order, conditional on the admitted starting points and evidence. The first-section appraisal credits those considerations but does not find this volume alone sufficiently discriminating. Both give stronger endorsement to the local agency distinctions than to an unrestricted all-perfections source-mode conclusion. This calibrated disagreement is retained; it is neither consensus nor a change to dot’s dated current assessment.

The printed 179–182 controls expose editorial supplies and retain unresolved awkward wording. The seventh treatise stops incompletely at printed 252. Other missing openings or endings occur at 273, 283 and 318; copyist abridgement is explicit at 389 and 423. Completion therefore concerns the received collection, not complete historical originals.

The wider reading preserves ethical limits on punishment, partial recognition, report criticism, absence and explanation, rights without felt harm, the Husayn/Yazid controversy, prayer-language and timing, property conditions, and partnership contributions. At printed 411 bodily labour and capital are distinct contributions to shared yield; at 419 consumed seed differs from returned capital, although a compressed contribution formula remains awkward. ### MF01 Knowledge guidance and action

Locators: main 224 and 229–278, especially 231–235, 241–242, 246–247 and 263–265.

Knowledge, will, capacity, recipient conditions and impediments have distinct roles. Productive prior knowledge and human receptive knowledge can both be perfections; neither case should be universalised without argument. The staged guidance debate resolves by distinguishing an apt incomplete disposition from a genuinely sufficient cause. Later refusal can damage cognition, but that does not retroactively erase earlier knowing refusal.

At 246–247 an objection correctly distinguishes a conditional from its converse. The reply invokes the utterance’s function of marking understanding as a sign. That is an additional pragmatic premise, rather than a theorem of converse implication. The staged argument, marginal critic and modern editor must remain separate voices. A conditional formal proof could use the strengthened sign relation once justified; it cannot supply the interpretative bridge by itself.

### MF02 Scoped delegation and consequence attribution

Locators: main 455–467, especially 460–462; 496–513, especially 501–502.

A limited act of delegation does not automatically licence the unrestricted title of God’s agent. Divine delegation is presented as beneficent enabling rather than relief of need. This affirms genuine mediated work without turning helpers into independent originals. The supplied apparatus disputes the reliability of some transmission evidence used nearby; the author’s argument does not erase that dissent.

The creature’s voluntary act can be a real component of a compound cause, while downstream effects also involve other factors. Consequent moral or causal credit does not make every outcome the agent’s own direct act, nor provide a general numerical allocation formula. Knowledge and action can each be means or ends; equal reward for firm intention does not erase the excellence of actual giving.

### MF03 Completing a product without completing its maker

Locators: main 5–24, 153, 363–372, 478–481, 1024–1026 and 1050–1051.

Repentance, gratitude and other history-dependent goods require conditions that can constitute their kind, rather than repair a missing intrinsic capacity. Learning, teaching and non-rival sharing also count as creaturely excellences. The mature divine-wisdom argument distinguishes completion of the product from acquisition of the maker’s perfection: action proceeds from perfection with wise loved ends.

The author expressly invokes God as the source of wisdom, its objects and its causes. That gives the account positive content, but its universal-source premise cannot be counted as independently proved merely by being invoked in the reply. Creaturely relational goods likewise do not independently establish coequal original plurality.

### MF04 Causal individuation and opposing tendencies

Locators: main 90–91, 743–746, 892–910, 1171, 1272 and 1279–1280.

The causal discussion distinguishes a ruling one in kind from a particular ruling tied to a source-indexed ground. Transferring that individuation to a source-neutral numerical effect requires argument. Real natural powers and compound causes remain affirmed.

At 896–898 the author denies equal opposed tendencies simply cancelling and explicitly hears the mutual-impediment objection. His reply gives producing one’s own effect priority over inhibiting the other effect. That priority and the identity of respects carry substantive work. Main 910 preserves a sword’s sharpness when stone prevents cutting, and 1171 preserves a latent painful cause when pleasure masks felt pain. These controls prevent claiming that the author simply equates every impeded effect with loss of power.

A restricted impossibility of independently sufficient causes of incompatible effects is different from a universal prohibition on equal partial tendencies or a zero resultant. The wider claim remains open to precise assessment; no new theorem or complete philosophical refutation is claimed here.

### MF05 Actual common creation and complete provision

Locators: main 587–588, 1026 and 1391–1395; ends at 511–514 and 1164–1165.

The source offers coordinated useful order, real subordinate causes and meaningful ends as positive evidence of a common knowing, powerful and wise Creator. The body-and-governor comparison is substantive, while its premises and transfer conditions remain to be defended. Main 588 defers detailed proof of the two cited unity arguments. Uneven usefulness and historically unreliable natural examples cannot silently become extra confirmations.

This actual-order hypothesis deserves comparative assessment independently of whether every proposed universal uniqueness argument succeeds. Conversely, the author’s assertion that the actual God supplies all requisites does not establish that every qualified original must do so. The earlier actual-order assessment remains explicitly pre-Miftah; this source addition does not silently revise its evidential boundary or manufacture a later verdict.

### MF06 Textual controls and broader matters

The preliminary layer, medieval text, quoted opponents, marginal objections, modern editor and electronic preparer are distinct. Bracketed second-edition addenda supplied electronically can be absent from the printed third edition. At main 811 a one-word lacuna remains; 966 contains a secondary reconstruction; 993 lacks a numbered argument; 1284 supplies a parallel only in a note. The 1595 apparatus records a two-and-a-half-line blank, and printed 1599 itself lacks an apparent contextual negation. None is a missing reading window, and none is silently emended.

The full argument includes Adam’s garden, education, natural history, moral value, law, astrology, omens and contagion. Some quoted philosophical claims are explicitly rejected. The editor’s correction of an anti-astrological transmission objection at 1447 is retained even though it cuts against the author’s local argument. Historical medicine and social generalisations remain attributed claims, not present guidance or independently validated evidence.

### NB01 Knowing purposeful agency and provision of knowledge

Locators: S001L77–78 and 139–145; S002L73–77; S003L14–18.

Fitting means–ends arrangement, rather than regular shape alone, supports the source’s argument for knowledgeable action. Knowledge, will, power and wise preference jointly explain discriminating agency. A creator who forms a knower and speaker can also instruct and enable recognition of a messenger; fitting actual provision additionally invokes wisdom, justice and mercy. These are substantive positive arguments, while their comprehensive reach and historical natural examples still require appraisal.

### NB02 Intrinsic indication and intended communication

Locators: S002L95–107; S003L25–28; printed 946–957.

An event can indicate an originator without first being recognised as intentionally evidential. Speech, gestures, entrusted objects and deliberately marked signs involve a communicative relation. Both can be rationally understood. A prior explicit convention is not always required, but intended sign use cannot be inferred from generic production alone.

### NB03 Private tokens mission scope and strong alternatives

Locators: S002L103–114; printed 768–772 and 777–781, especially 771 and 779.

The envoy-token example distinguishes the token’s source from the purpose for which it was supplied. Theft, loss, unauthorised sealing and an ordinary repair commission are considered. Suspension is warranted when an alternative arises and has force; mere imaginable possibility is not made a universal defeater. Context can support confident recognition without a prearranged code.

The immediate authentication is that the sender sent the envoy. That remains distinct from whether every statement subsequently attributed to the sender is true. The divine application expressly asks about source, sending and intended certification. General providential sending, or a distressed unbeliever’s answered prayer, does not itself confer normative endorsement.

### NB04 Multiple routes and reciprocal evidential support

Locators: S001L73–86 and 97–105; S002L12–16,95–100 and 153; S003L1.

The source does not impose one compulsory speculative route on every knower. Character, content, testimony and circumstances can support recognition, while articulated argument can later clarify or strengthen it. Scriptural teaching can point to observations whose rational force does not depend solely on prior acceptance of the speaker’s authority.

Reciprocal support nevertheless needs genuine evidential contact. Consensus cannot be the sole premise establishing prophecy if its authority depends entirely on that prophecy. Repetition of one assumed credential is not independent corroboration. An unresolved universal source-mode premise therefore does not block every possible testimonial route, but neither does a merely proposed route establish a particular revelation.

### NB05 Actual action and bounded historical attestation

Locators: S003L27; printed 952–953, 1064–1071 and 1085–1087.

The author rejects inferring actual certification from the need to retain the power to certify: not doing a possible act is not lacking that power. He allows real subordinate contributions and mediated action while distinguishing them from complete independent production. Identifying adopted communicative ownership requires the relevant purpose and scope.

The book develops a substantial profile of truthful guidance, character, testimony and enduring consequences. This study did not independently authenticate every narrated sign or determine that a particular episode meets all its conditions. The post-Nubuwwat comparative supplement credits the stronger purposive-agent model but retains its own withholding on complete numerical authorship; that dated assessment remains separate from later reviews and the final overall judgement.

Striking reported occurrences are distinguished from their interpretation, agent and authority. These source discussions are not current legal advice or independently verified historical events.

### SF01 Prophetic signs and real powers

Locators: S001L0237–0245; Adwa printed 158–159 and continuation.

The source concedes unusual cognition, psychic efficacy and real natural powers. Fire heats and water cools through genuine created efficacy. It rejects reducing all prophetic distinction to those admitted phenomena. A possible kind of explanation does not identify the actual cause, purpose or scope of a particular event. Reports of wonders and encounters remain historically unverified by this reading.

### SF02 An active Creator and determinate products

Locators: S001L0055–0056; S002L0607–0610; S003L0015–0017 and 0100–0103.

Unceasing activity in type differs from a beginningless particular product. A succession of effects differs from an infinite regress in original productive efficacy. Finite causes can wait upon conditions supplied elsewhere; sunlight can reach a newly uncovered place without a new intrinsic act in the sun. Applying that model to a comprehensive Creator requires addressing the source of the allegedly external condition itself.

Some arguments are conditional dilemmas, rather than simultaneous endorsement of every horn. Causal completeness, an intrinsic act, immediate succession, a prepared recipient and the product must not be collapsed into one temporal relation.

### SF03 A concrete necessary bearer and intrinsic attributes

Locators: S002L0043–0046 and 0340–0346; S003L0133–0139 and 0348–0353; printed 245–246, 323 and 424–425.

A universal concept is not a separately subsisting universal object. This does not prohibit abstraction or informative common predication. Establishing some necessary existent is expressly distinguished from identifying a Maker distinct from the world. Likewise, a thing’s requiring its own attributes or purported constituents is not already dependence upon an external efficient maker. The argument must identify the concrete bearer and the relevant kind of dependence.

### SF04 Positive perfection and creature relative predicates

Locators: S001L0132–0135; S002L0330–0337 and 0507; S004L0054–0056; printed 122, 321–322, 353–354 and 578.

The source explicitly argues that an independent giver has superior title to a possible defect-free perfection bestowed upon dependent creatures. The fuller account also argues from the possibility of that perfection. Printed 122 contains a positive-perfection clause absent from the electronic witness; the difference is recorded rather than inserted into first-pass credit.

The restrictions are equally explicit. Producing a quality in another does not make it the producer’s intrinsic quality. Intrinsic necessitation of attributes differs from efficient production of creatures. Knowledge, mercy and wisdom can commend humans, while need, humility and servanthood can perfect a creature in ways incompatible with divine independence. A faithful comparison therefore requires the predicate’s meaning, possibility and bearer-relative status.

### SF05 Common original agency and different causes

Locators: S003L0198–0208, 0319–0321 and 0362–0373; printed 436–438, 450–452, 463–464 and 473–474.

The author intends to exclude divided independent ultimate makers through connected creation, the unity of generated individuals and the inseparability of a mixing from its resulting state. These are genuine affirmative arguments, stronger in intent than an economy preference. Their connection, individuation and inseparability premises must nevertheless do the inferential work. Ordinary cooperation is explained through differentiated contributions.

An object of love or imitation is not, merely as such, the efficient producer of a lover’s power or act. A represented end can guide activity before it exists externally. An intrinsic condition, an end, a sign and a complete original producer therefore cannot inherit one transfer rule simply because each is called a requisite. The additional 23-record unity reread remains separate from the completed first pass.

### SF06 Epistemic routes textual controls and wider matters

Locators: S002L0206–0208 and 0453–0596; S003L0394–0398 and 0414–0419; S004L0052–0063; catalogue at PDF41–47.

The work permits different routes and amounts of doubt-removal for different hearers. Scripture can guide reason directly, while a hearer who does not accept prophetic truth needs the relevant evidence rather than an argument resting solely on that truth. A reported sign’s historical occurrence and intended certification remain separate questions.

The wider argument includes speech and recitation, creation materials and decree, cosmic transformation, resurrection, social order, affective fulfilment, differing laws and justice towards opponents. Concessions to sound science, sincere error and genuine ethical improvement qualify the polemic. Modern notes also challenge aspects of the portrayal of al-Ghazali at printed 577.

The correction catalogue is illustrative, not an exhaustive list of editorial intervention. Catalogue statements, directly read printed body, older readings reported by the editors and the unchanged electronic text are different evidence. Some catalogue/body conflicts and unresolved anomalies remain. PDF 380’s conventional printed 373 label is inferred because its footer numeral is invisible. Neither the 94 mapped entries nor the full electronic traversal establishes a complete critical edition, full manuscript reading or historical authentication.

## Relation to the final judgement

The purpose of rereading is to understand the arguments and their limits, including reasons to revise an earlier assessment. It is not a mechanism for manufacturing agreement. The [dated successor assessment of 7 October 2026 at 14:34 UTC](assessments/ROOT_CURRENT_APPRAISAL_20261007_143400.md), filed at t20/reading/assessments/ROOT_CURRENT_APPRAISAL_20261007_143400.md, states dot’s final current position for this study.

I provisionally accept an actual Necessary Being. I also now provisionally accept one complete original source of the relevant actual connected created order, with lower confidence than the necessary-being conclusion. Relatively basic warranted Creator recognition remains epistemically admissible; this does not establish a universally reliable empirical faculty or authenticate every particular purported recognition.

The actual-common-source judgement uses a cumulative comparison. Original existential provision already has positive grounds; coordinated use of differentiated resources adds grounds for knowing agency. A single genuine bearer can explain that provision and its integration while retaining real secondary causes. Comprehensive scope is an additional commitment assessed within that explanation, not silently extracted from the word necessary. Both a single-source and a plural-source account owe the differentiated capacities and acts. The plural account additionally divides their original ownership without a presently established constraint or explanatory gain requiring that division. I now judge the residual integration and economy advantage sufficient for defeasible assent.

Principled intrinsic harmony remains a serious objection. A purported single source could conceal the same unexplained coordination or require comprehensive competence at excessive explanatory cost. A sufficiently supported conceptual criticism could defeat acceptance without a complete rival cosmology or a proof that a plural-original world is possible. The present judgement neither establishes equal credibility for that rival nor dismisses it as lucky agreement or circular donation of power.

I continue to withhold sufficient warrant for the stronger universal source-mode principle: that every qualified original must completely provide every relevant requisite of every connected effect to which it contributes. Fuller actual provision is an excellence, but every fittingly partial original undertaking has not thereby been shown necessarily defective. Acceptance of one actual common source does not close that universal route or decide all questions about unrelated necessary reality.

The history remains explicit. The 08:40 assessment withheld the stronger common-source conclusion. The separately dated pre-Miftah comparison favoured one actual common Creator but withheld sufficient numerical discrimination; the post-Nubuwwat supplement retained that position at its own boundary. The later threshold audit provisionally accepted the actual conclusion, and the comparative response revised its earlier weighting while preserving the local objections. Their full-source boundaries differ: neither claims a fresh whole-book reading of all later material. Their agreement is not itself evidence; the reasons above are what this final assessment adopts.

These judgements remain separate from source attribution, conditional proof, textual verification and empirical validation. Historical Seventeenth and Nineteenth conclusions and the earlier Twentieth appraisals are preserved. No newly discovered quotation forces the revision, no conditional theorem changes validity, and no particular historical attestation or universal veracity claim is automatically authenticated by it.
