## Asbahani, printed 389–391

The text contrasts a messenger's conveying speech with originating it. Attribution to a human messenger and an angelic messenger is used against making either the author of the conveyed Quran. At 391 the carrier's voice, movement and act of recitation are distinguished from the speech recited. This supports retaining carrier occurrence, source occurrence and source authorship separately. It does not settle the source of arbitrary matching words by a modern label, nor claim that every faithful translation is a new source assertion.

## Asbahani, printed 725–728

At 725–726 the argument is specifically from established messengership and divine attestation to truthfulness in what the messenger reports from God, including particular reports within that mission. It is not a counterexample to the Asbahani argument with its full established-divine-attestation premises supplied, and it is not a model of revelation.
