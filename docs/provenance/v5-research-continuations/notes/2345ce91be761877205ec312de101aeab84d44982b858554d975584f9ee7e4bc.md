## Fixed-codebook proofs

F1's linear-programming argument is valid for the continuous simplex. The feasible uniform vector has total N=b−M+1. An optimal vertex has at most M fractional coordinates, since more would admit a nonzero two-sided perturbation preserving active row constraints and fixed bounds. If fractional coordinates occur, the number z of ones is at most N−1; rounding every positive coordinate upward therefore uses at most N−1+M=b roots and preserves all lower mass constraints. This proves the bound N/u. A finite probability grid would not prove it, and the author does not use one as a substitute.

F2's integer thresholds are exact. When d=gcd(u,N)≥M, positive block sizes divisible by u/d sum to u. Making every uniform block's corruption fraction strictly exceed N/u needs N+M=b+1 corrupt roots. Conversely, allocating exactly the integral threshold in every block, then padding, supplies the opposing bound. Together with F1 this gives equality. N=0 is separately handled by the zero-error menu, and M=1 gives the ordinary uniform b/u value.

The u=8,b=3,M=2 example therefore has exact fixed-codebook risk 1/4. It is a genuine counterexample to treating the M−1 singleton plus uniform remainder upper bound 2/7 as a universal optimum, and it lies within the inherited restoration parameters f=4,k=1. No overlapping supports are needed for this counterexample; their potential advantage elsewhere remains open.

F3's induction correctly gives V_fixed(u,b,b)=1/(u−b+1). At threshold t=1/(u−M+1), a coordinate of one row at least t satisfies that row. Delete it, normalize the others on u−1 roots, and induct on M−1 with the same t. A remaining row with selected-root mass a<1 then has union mass at least a+(1−a)t≥t; a=1 is already satisfied. If no coordinate reaches t, every M-set exceeds t because its complement contains u−M coordinates each smaller than t. The matching singleton/uniform construction closes the boundary. This does not close intermediate 1<M<b cases.

## Shared-random frontier

Their distinct set has r≤M roots, and a uniform b-set contains it with probability choose(b,r)/choose(u,r), at least the M-root expression for M≤b<u.
