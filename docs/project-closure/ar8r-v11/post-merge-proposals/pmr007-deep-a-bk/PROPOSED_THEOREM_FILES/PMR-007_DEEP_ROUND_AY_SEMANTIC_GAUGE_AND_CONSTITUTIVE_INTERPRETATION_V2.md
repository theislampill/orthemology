## 1. Two independent symmetry layers

Let `S` be a finite neutral structural system with carrier `X`.  Let
`C=(C,Λ)` be a finite content algebra whose signature `Λ` contains exactly the
content relations, operations, and designated elements independently admitted
into the neutral comparison.  An interpretation is

\[
\mu:X\to C.
\]

Two groups are kept separate:

```text
Aut(S): structural-position automorphisms;
Aut(C,Λ): content-algebra automorphisms.
```

Deep AM addresses the first group.  Deep AY addresses the second.

Let `B` be the set of independently warranted semantic, referential, source, or
experimental anchors currently admitted.  Define the residual content gauge

\[
\Gamma_B=Aut(C,\Lambda\cup B).
\]

No anchor is accepted merely because it favors one architecture.

## 2. Eligible neutral evidence

Let

\[
E:\operatorname{Int}(S,C)\to Z
\]

be the complete finite **eligible evidence map** for the declared comparison.
It may include registered structural, causal, behavioral, source-compatible,
or representation facts, but it must use one candidate-independent experiment
and coding contract.

The evidence is content-gauge invariant when

\[
E(\gamma\circ\mu)=E(\mu)
\qquad
(\gamma\in\Gamma_B).
\]

A target `T`—for example literal reference, truth-value name, source-role name,
intrinsic notionality, or mentality—is exactly identifiable from `E` only if
there exists `d` with `T=d\circ E`.

## 3. SGCI-1A — semantic-gauge nonidentifiability

If `E` is `Γ_B`-invariant and there exist `gamma in Γ_B` and `mu` such that

\[
T(\gamma\circ\mu)\neq T(\mu),
\]

then `T` is not exactly identifiable from `E`.

### Proof

Gauge invariance gives

\[
E(\gamma\circ\mu)=E(\mu).
\]

Any exact decoder `d` would therefore assign the same target to both
interpretations, contradicting their different `T` values. ∎

Thus eligible neutral evidence identifies at most gauge-invariant properties of
the residual orbit

\[
\Gamma_B\cdot\mu.
\]

This is evidence-relative nonidentifiability, not metaphysical indeterminacy.

## 4. SGCI-1B — exact anchor/stabilizer condition

The literal interpretation `mu` is free of residual content-gauge ambiguity
relative to `B` exactly when

\[
|\Gamma_B\cdot\mu|=1,
\]

equivalently, every residual content automorphism fixes `mu` pointwise on its
image.

This condition says that the **declared anchors** remove the relevant formal
recoding ambiguity.  It does not establish that the anchors are true,
source-authoritative, world-adequate, constitutive, or mental.

## 5. SGCI-1C — intervention and representation firewall

A causal or behavioral experiment breaks the gauge only if the common
candidate-independent evidence map it defines is not `Γ_B`-invariant.  If the
content labels, interventions, and output coding are recoded together, the
experiment remains gauge-equivalent.

Choosing different label maps, target predicates, representations, or
interventions for competing architectures is not one common experiment and
cannot supply neutral discrimination.  This applies the Deep AQ, AN, AO, and
AT gates at the content-semantic level.

## 8. Empirical and source effects

### PRH and OSM

Kernel alignment, trajectory fit, and state-machine geometry may constrain
shared relational structure.  They do not identify literal reference when the
registered empirical evidence is invariant under content recoding.  A new
sensor or intervention can break the gauge only by defining a genuinely
expanded common experiment.

A translated source predicate or source co-reference can reduce the residual
source-expanded gauge under its own authority contract.  Deep AP and AX still
require an independently warranted source-to-world map and reliability model.
The source anchor does not automatically migrate into neutral H7a2.
