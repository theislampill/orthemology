# Independent review: owed-response specification

10 October 2026 UTC. Independent reading against the exact inherited residual; not a manuscript collation, historical authentication, contemporary diagnosis, integration decision, or closure.

## Finding

**The selected al-ʿUbūdiyya passages support a specific performance inadequacy and source-sensitive disclosure constraints. They do not add a new constitutive title for selecting one of two otherwise adequate responses.** The positive increment is a worked discriminator: replacing content-bearing exclusive acknowledgement with an isolated name does not, merely by preserving a referent or producing an intense experience, preserve what was acknowledged. This is narrower than prescribing one uniquely obligatory verbal form.

This finding retains the accepted necessity, sourcehood, Knowledge, Will, Wisdom and due practical allegiance conclusions. It does not downgrade allegiance to admiration or require norm-free derivation of every normative premise. Nor does it demand unlimited jurisdiction to establish one local obligation. It asks only whether these additional passages discriminate the exact disputed selection.

## Independent source checks

### 149–158: subjection and worship

149–152 locate worship in an extensive range of outward and inward acts. At 153 the author's own account requires love together with submission; either alone is inadequate to the stated worship relation. At 154–158 universal creaturely subjection to providential governance is distinguished from the intentional worship, obedience and exclusive divine orientation he commends. Recognition of origination or need is not presented as sufficient performance of worship.

This gives substantive performance constraints. It does not argue that causal subjection alone confers every particular command title. Conversely, its distinction must not be used to retract the inherited normative force of actual sourcehood: the conclusion concerns what counts as rendering the already owed response, not whether sourcehood matters. The explicit target includes the claim that awareness of universal determination removes command and prohibition. That target differs from a recipient who accepts practical allegiance while disputing one particular form's exclusivity.

### 172–174, controlled by 190–194 and 209–218: love and force

172 expressly distinguishes leaving recommended acts from leaving obligatory ones. 173 includes both obligatory and recommended commands among loved good actions. The attributed al-Fuḍayl account at 173–174 requires sincerity and correctness together; correctness is conformity to the Sunna. It is endorsed within the author's exposition, not an independent authentication performed by this review.

190–194 explain practical conformity to what the beloved loves, the relation between complete love and effective willing, capability limits, and the importance of means that actually attain the end. They resist reducing love to a private feeling. They do not erase the force distinctions. 212–213 again name obligations and recommendations, and 218 applies the same distinction within the account of perfected willing. Therefore the inference “perfect love pursues a preferable form; every preferable form is required” is not licensed by these units.

209–218 also reject confusing comprehensive providential willing with normative approval. The author's polemical diagnoses and reported rival sayings are not independently verified facts about contemporary groups or individuals. His 209 qualification that some reported sayings may be falsely attributed or mistaken is material, as is 212's distinction between a report's veracity and its speaker's authority.

These passages positively authorize substantive practical comparison within the author's framework. But “correctness means following what is commanded” does not independently establish the exact selection entitlement at issue. Calling a form prescribed assumes that its prescription has already been warranted. Where attribution and force are independently authenticated, disclosure can settle the obligation without an additional constitutive derivation; this is the inherited route, not a newly established authentication result here.

### 224–236: the strongest new discriminator

224–225 provide the immediate setting: unification of attention is to retain, rather than erase, discrimination between creator and created and the content of exclusive worship. 225–227 contrast the content-bearing affirmation/negation of exclusive divinity with a name taken merely in isolation. On the author's argument, an isolated referential item by itself does not affirm or deny that content. A hearer can therefore preserve the same putative referent while failing to perform the same acknowledgement. This is an intelligible non-equivalence argument, not simply the assertion that the selected form binds.

The limits are explicit and essential:

1. At 226 and 228, the apparently one-word answer to “who sent down the book?” is contextually complete. The question supplies the predicate. Token length is not the criterion.
2. At 226–227 the author allows that accompanying knowledge or a state of the heart can supply benefit from outside the isolated name. His contention is about what the expression contributes by itself, and why this does not establish it as a superior authorized substitution. It would be too strong to report that every utterance of a name is psychologically empty or wholly incapable of beneficial association.
3. At 227 the alleged danger of dying between the negative and affirmative portions of the confession is answered through the person's intention. An accidentally truncated sequence is not treated as a deliberate contrary commitment. Intention does real interpretive work; it does not thereby make every proposed ritual substitution authorized.
4. 230–231 treat “in the name of God” as a complete contextual performance through an understood action. Grammatical omission is compatible with semantic completion.
5. 229–233 supply several meaningful remembrance formulas, while 234–236 conclude with sincerity and revealed practice. The argument does not select a single phonological string as the only adequate performance in every setting.

Accordingly, the passage bears on the inherited report's second route: demonstrate that a proposed alternative does not perform the same relevant act. It does not establish the first route: a power to make one otherwise adequate alternative alone obligatory. It also does not establish that a specific formula is presently due from a specific contemporary addressee. That further application needs the actual duty, occasion, conditions, attribution and force.

## Source status and claim boundaries

Primary electronic witness: Ibn Taymiyya, al-ʿUbūdiyya, displayed Majmūʿ al-fatāwā 10:149–236, https://quranislam.github.io/fatwa/ibn-taymiyyah/fatwa147.html . The URL was independently opened. Substantive reading used the parent's locally received page extracts, whose exact hashes are recorded separately. The complete requested extracts were inspected in bounded chunks; a first oversized output was truncated, and the missing middle was reread rather than counted as read.

Read in this review: received displayed 149–158, 169–174, 188–194, 209–218, and 224–236. The beginnings/ends of some extracts cross incomplete argument boundaries: 158 ends mid-discussion, 174 begins a new lexical question that continues beyond the extract, 194 ends mid-sentence, 209 opens a continuing polemic, 218 begins the further account of annihilation that continues, and 224 opens mid-discussion. This review claims no completion of those larger neighboring units. 224–236 covers the bare-name argument and the work's received concluding unit. No new scan pagination, imprint identification, manuscript collation, whole-work reading or transmission authentication is claimed.

The inherited scoped-original-authority and token-force reports were read to fix the earned scope and avoid treating their accepted conclusions as open. Neither this review nor the textual findings independently reauthenticate every cited Qurʾānic or hadith report. This is an evidential-credit limit, not a withdrawal of prior warranted acceptance.

## Review disposition

The source-level conclusions above were reached before receipt of the author's new report. Final comparison with its frozen wording is recorded in a separate addendum. Suitable outcome: accept a bounded performance-discriminator contribution, retain the authenticated-disclosure route at its inherited conditions, and leave the local constitutive selection-title residual open. Repetition of due-love and source-correctness formulas alone should not be credited as its solution.
