# Objective Normativity and Corrective Cognition

## Abstract

Correction is intelligible only where a change can answer to a standard that distinguishes improvement from mere alteration. The standard may concern truth, adequate reasoning, the function of a capacity, a fitting response to another person, or a rightful practical requirement. These are related questions, but an answer to one does not automatically answer the others. This work develops an account of their relations. It establishes a conditional obstruction to wholly self-authorizing radical correction; states the additional premises needed for an argument from evaluability to an intelligent ground; separates proper functioning, accuracy, and warranted exercise; and proves exact results about truth-calibrated revision and finite discrimination among functional explanations. It then reconstructs affirmative arguments for intrinsic normative reasons, owed acknowledgment, wise direction, and source-owned undertakings. The resulting account distinguishes the ground of an obligation from a person's knowledge of it, present permission from relinquished authority, and foreknowledge of a response from its actual achievement. A sustained comparison with James, relational moral naturalism, and the ethics of agency preserves their strongest contributions and identifies the precise outstanding explanatory burdens. Objective correction, successful learning, moral categoricity, and legitimate authority emerge as connected achievements with different warrants.

**Scientific scope.** This is a synthesis of public philosophical arguments and bounded mathematical results. Its mathematical propositions have the stated hypotheses; its philosophical appraisals retain the premises and qualifications of the arguments that support them. It neither silently revises an accepted edition of an Orthemology paper nor attributes new discovery or owner acceptance to its exposition. The metaphysics of original being is developed separately in [Received Actuality and Intrinsic Necessity](received-actuality-and-intrinsic-necessity.md); the epistemology of actual recognition, warrant, and defeat is developed in [Recognition, Warrant and Restoration](../epistemology/recognition-warrant-and-restoration.md). The present subject is the normative order to which correction answers and the relations through which that order can guide cognition and action.

## 1. What correction commits us to

A change of belief can remove an error, exchange one error for another, or replace a true belief with a false one. A change of practical policy can repair a wrong, improve efficiency in pursuing a bad end, or merely accommodate a new preference. A successful account of correction must therefore identify both the transition and the respect in which the transition is better. The causal fact that a subject changed is insufficient. So is the subject's satisfaction with the result. A later judgment may itself need correction. [S1–S4]

This starting point is modest in one way and substantial in another. It does not assume that every ordinary assessment is true, that people possess an infallible faculty, or that all correct judgments share one decision procedure. It does assume, whenever a particular correction is asserted to be objectively warranted, that there is a truth about the relevant superiority that is not constituted solely by the approval being corrected. Someone can contest that assumption. What they cannot do is reject it and then claim to have supplied the same account of objective correction without explaining the change of subject.

The term **orthability** designates the possibility of an adequate, fitting resolution under an appropriate analysis. Its philosophical use requires three distinctions.

1. **Local evaluability.** A specified analysis supplies a domain, cases, obligations, and standards of successful resolution. A procedure can be assessed against that specification.
2. **Objective conditions of evaluability.** There really are the relevant differences between identity and nonidentity, truth and error, possibility and impossibility, adequate and inadequate response. A specification can represent these differences accurately or inaccurately.
3. **Created representations and applications.** A person, language, institution, or program embodies a particular account of those conditions and attempts to apply it.

An analysis can be locally coherent while misrepresenting its subject. Agreement between its implementation and its specification establishes fidelity to the specification; further evidence is needed to establish that the specification itself is correct. Conversely, the fallibility of representations does not entail that there is nothing for them to get right. This is the central distinction between correcting a representation and manufacturing its truth by changing it.

No entity called “Orthability” is introduced alongside persons, facts, acts, and relations. The objective claim concerns the reality of the conditions expressed by particular modal and normative judgments. A proposition about adequate inference need not designate a separate abstract object that issues commands. Whether such truths ultimately require a personal ground is a further argument, not a grammatical consequence of using a noun.

### 1.1 Six questions about normativity

The following questions should be asked separately throughout the discussion:

| Question | What would answer it? |
|---|---|
| Is the assessment true or false? | A truth condition for the particular judgment. |
| Can its truth exceed present approval? | An account under which mistaken approval or disapproval is possible. |
| What grounds that truth? | An explanation of the relevant normative fact or relation. |
| How can a subject know it? | An adequate epistemic route, including applicable defeaters. |
| Does it give the subject a reason independently of current wants? | A warranted account of categorical practical reasons. |
| Who may specify, enforce, or otherwise alter what is owed? | A relevant title and a valid exercise of that title. |

Actual motivation is an additional issue. Someone may recognize a reason and fail to act; someone else may act under threat without recognizing a legitimate reason. A categorical reason can be pro tanto: its independence from contingent desire does not make it decisive against every competing consideration. Nor does having a decisive reason to perform an act automatically authorize someone else to compel or punish its performance. These distinctions are needed by the affirmative account as well as by its rivals. [S14–S20]

### 1.2 Concrete reasoning and sound reasoning

Concreteness and soundness answer different questions. A particular inference has a material and temporal occurrence. The rule under which it is assessed has a type. The subject represents that rule in some way, binds it to a particular case, performs some operation, and arrives at or accepts a conclusion. The episode can succeed at some of these tasks and fail at others.

For example, a valid rule can be misremembered; a correctly remembered rule can be applied to a case outside its scope; a correct application can produce a conclusion that is later transmitted incorrectly. A delivery failure unrelated to the reasoning does not retroactively make the reasoning invalid. The relevant obligations must be fixed by the claim and its evidential dependencies, before its outcome is known. One cannot protect a preferred conclusion by retrospectively excluding the very step that supplied it. [S1–S3; R1]

Strict soundness of an actual reasoning claim therefore concerns an adequate relevant pathway and an actual link to truth. This is more demanding than the statistical reliability of a process type. A generally reliable process can fail on an occasion; a badly grounded guess can be true. It is also more precise than requiring every event in a large surrounding episode to succeed. The distinction permits improvement at the right level: faculty, rule representation, case identification, operation, or uptake.

A faculty can operate before its possessor can articulate a theory of that operation. A child need not solve the philosophy of perception before seeing a nearby object. Equally, the faculty with which someone evaluates a proposed standard is not automatically infallible because it is evaluating standards. These observations create room for ordinary knowledge and for reflection without making reflective self-endorsement its own sufficient warrant.

## 2. Correction through time

An adequate state description is not yet an account of a subject's development. A dynamic account must distinguish the conditions under which a better state exists, the subject can reach it, the subject can discover a route, and a particular learning process actually follows that route. **Dynamic orthability** concerns lawful, evidence-sensitive, correction-capable trajectories under a declared analysis. It is a proposal about how improvement can be represented and assessed, not a demonstrated universal learning law. [S2]

Let a state space be $`X`$, let $`R\subseteq X\times X`$ be an admissible transition relation, and let $`A\subseteq X`$ be a target set whose adequacy has independent warrant. If the initial state is $`x`$, existence of an $`R`$-path from $`x`$ to $`A`$ says that the target is reachable under this model. It does not say that the actual subject knows the path or that a chosen policy finds it. Nor does a formal definition of $`A`$ prove that its members are objectively adequate.

A claim of corrective availability concerns a more adequate admissible continuation, not the empty reflexive fact that a state is reachable from itself. An account with no better admissible continuation cannot promise correction in that respect merely by allowing the subject to remain where it is.

### 2.1 Eight distinct modalities

The scientific meaning of a learning claim depends on which of the following is established.

| Modality | Content | Why a neighboring claim can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphysical possibility | The proposed state or transition is genuinely possible. | Possibility need not preserve the actual laws or boundary conditions. |
| Possibility under actual laws | The actual laws permit it in a suitable situation. | The present state may have no admissible path to that situation. |
| Objective reachability | A target can be reached from the specified state under the admitted transitions. | An available path can depend on disturbances that no policy controls. |
| Policy controllability | An admissible policy can secure the target under the stated disturbances and information conditions. | An ideal controller's access or resources may exceed the actual actor's. |
| Actor accessibility | The actor possesses the relevant actions, resources, and opportunities. | The actor may lack evidence identifying which accessible route is correct. |
| Evidence-based discoverability | The available evidence can identify an adequate route or target. | A fixed representation may be unable to express what the evidence supports. |
| Learnability in a representation class | A suitable learner can recover an adequate representation under specified data assumptions. | A particular update rule can fail despite another learner's success. |
| Convergence of the chosen process | The actual specified update has the claimed limiting or finite success. | Its assumptions can fail, and convergence to its target need not establish the target's truth. |

These distinctions do not constitute an unconditional linear implication chain. “Policy” can mean a fully informed controller or a policy restricted to the actor's observations; those are different domains. Learnability can mean identification in the limit, statistical consistency, or successful finite discrimination; those are different quantifiers. A claim about one must be restated before it is transported to another.

Simple examples make the separations concrete. A permitted transition outside the initial reachable component cannot repair the initial state. A target achievable on one favorable disturbance history need not be robustly controllable. A usable route can remain undiscoverable when two candidate environments produce the same observations but require different actions. A hypothesis class that excludes the true target cannot recover it exactly merely by adding samples. An identifiable target may still defeat an update rule with an absorbing error state. A decreasing declared loss can approach a suboptimal value if the rule repeatedly chooses insufficient improvements while a better route remains available.

These examples establish logical distinctions between claims. They do not show that a particular person lacks a path to correction or that a real learning system is incapable of improvement. Empirical evidence can identify mechanisms, estimate reliability, or expose missing cases. What it cannot do by sheer increase of sample size is answer a question that the measurement and model class have deliberately left unrepresented.

### 2.2 The subject is not its diagnostic record

An utterance, questionnaire response, or computational state can be evidence about a subject's cognition. It is not the whole subject. Several mechanisms can produce the same discourse; the same mechanism can produce different discourse under different contexts. A noetic model must therefore preserve the distinction between a person, the person's capacities, the available evidence, and the analyst's representation.

The same applies to testimony. Ten copies of one statement are ten occurrences but may convey one evidential origin. An increased number of repetitions does not itself provide independent warrant. Conversely, genuinely independent competent testimony can add information. The issue is the origin, dependence, content, and reliability of the testimony, rather than its raw count. The fuller information-theoretic treatment belongs in [Collective Inquiry and Strategic Coordination](../mathematics/collective-inquiry-and-strategic-coordination.md).

Within an Atharī account, **fiṭrah** names an original normative disposition and its proper orientation. It is not identified with one measured coordinate, an entropy minimum, or the convergence of a diagnostic algorithm. A model can represent selected aspects of learning and correction without claiming to measure the whole disposition. Its agreement with a theological interpretation is not independent evidence for that interpretation when the interpretation was already used to construct the model.

## 3. Radical correction and the limits of self-authorization

Ordinary correction often uses an accepted higher-order standard. A calculation is repaired by recalculating under the same arithmetic; a rule is amended under a retained constitutional procedure. Such cases need not overturn every basis of authority available to the agent. **Radical correction in a specified respect** is a stronger case: the entire currently operative basis of authority in that respect is itself subject to defeat. [S4]

Let $`B_s`$ be the current token basis of authority at state $`s`$, and let $`\mathrm{Auth}(a,r)`$ mean that $`a`$ adequately warrants the correction in respect $`r`$. The basis includes the higher-order rules actually relied upon; it is not artificially restricted to whichever first-order rule is being criticized.

**Proposition 1 (the local authority obstruction).** The following claims cannot all hold for the same correction and the same respect:

- The correction is objectively warranted and therefore has an adequate authority.
- Every authority wholly contained in $`B_s`$ is defeated for this correction.
- Every adequate authority for the correction is wholly contained in $`B_s`$.

**Proof.** By the first claim, choose an adequate authority $`a`$. By the third, $`a`$ is wholly contained in $`B_s`$. The second therefore defeats $`a`$ as an adequate authority for this correction, contradicting its selection. The contradiction depends on holding fixed the correction, respect, and meaning of adequate authority. $`\square`$

The result says something substantive about a proposed explanation of radical correction: a basis cannot supply all of the adequate authority that its own genuinely radical defeat removes. The logical form is elementary. It does not prove that every actual correction is radical, that authority must be a separate object, that all normative truths are personal commands, or that a deity exists.

### 3.1 Ways of answering the obstruction

Several responses are available, and their costs differ.

**Retain a higher-order standard.** A rule can authorize its own amendment through a higher-order procedure. If that procedure retains adequate authority, the event was not radical relative to the entire basis. This is often a perfectly good explanation of ordinary self-correction. It becomes evasive only when the explanation continues to claim that every such authority was defeated.

**Appeal to a standard that exceeds this token basis.** The relevant norm may be a trans-state type, an objective truth, a constitutive norm of inquiry, or a fact to which the present representation fails to conform. This answers the local containment problem if the proposed standard really has the requisite authority. It leaves the nature, grounding, and epistemic accessibility of that authority to be explained.

**Give a holistic account.** Adequacy may belong to a wider pattern rather than to one isolated current representation. Such a proposal must explain why the whole is authoritative rather than merely coherent. A self-consistent network can still systematically misrepresent its subject. The local proposition does not refute holistic grounding; it prevents the whole from being silently reduced to an entirely defeated token basis.

**Take the relevant normative fact as primitive.** A primitive truth can be sufficient at the level of the local argument. Whether this is an adequate ultimate explanation is a further metaphysical question. The objection that primitivism explains too little is not a contradiction proof.

**Abandon objective radical correction in this case.** A theorist may describe the change as preference, convention, error, or relative improvement. That rejects a premise of Proposition 1. It is not another way of satisfying all three premises.

These are diagnostic responses, not a theorem that five mutually exclusive metaphysical systems exhaust every possibility. In particular, retaining a metarule and rejecting objectivity are premise-rejecting answers. They must not be counted as examples of all the original radical-correction assumptions jointly holding.

### 3.2 Regress and recursive repair

An indefinitely continuing sequence of revisions need not be an indefinite deferral of authority. A fixed norm can assess every stage; a global constitutive account can apply to an infinite pattern; or a prior independently warranted source can ground the sequence. Temporal succession and explanatory dependence are different relations.

If, instead, each stage receives all its authority only from a later ungrounded stage, the proponent owes an account of how the whole has authority. A well-foundedness principle would exclude the relevant endless dependence, but that principle must be defended. The local obstruction does not by itself establish it. One cannot derive universal foundationalism by labeling every recursive procedure a vicious regress.

The proper conclusion is therefore bounded. Objective radical correction excludes complete containment of its adequate authority within the very token basis defeated in that correction. Which wider account is correct remains open to argument. The original public presentation of this radical-correction proposal was not independently adopted as a complete metaphysical proof; the present proof states its valid local content without retrospectively changing that historical status.

## 4. Intelligibility and its ground

The argument from intelligibility asks why evaluable thought and action are possible at all. Its strongest starting point is not that a particular program runs, nor that a community uses normative language. It is that some judgments about identity, truth, implication, possibility, and fitting response are true, and that their truth is not produced merely by whichever practice currently endorses them. That realist premise can be warranted without being neutral between every philosophical position. [S1–S3]

### 4.1 The objection to wholesale construction

Consider an explanation according to which a process constructs every condition that makes its own operation determinate, possible, and successful. To describe the process as constructing rather than failing, one must distinguish what it does, what would count as producing the result, and what separates that result from alternatives. On the realist reading, these distinctions are conditions of the purported operation's being the operation it is. They are not merely optional words used by an observer.

The argument can be put carefully:

1. The proposed construction has determinate identity and a genuine success condition.
2. These are among the relevant objective conditions of evaluability.
3. The construction depends on those conditions in the same respect in which its success is identified.
4. An explanation cannot make that very dependent success the sole noncircular source of those same conditions.
5. Therefore this construction does not wholly originate all of its own conditions in that respect.

The fourth step expresses the noncircular explanatory requirement. The conclusion concerns dependence, not necessarily chronological priority. Conditions can be constitutive of an operation without occurring earlier in time. Nor does the argument say that every representation, language, law, institution, or local standard must be eternal. Such things can be created and changed while answering to conditions under which their creation and change are intelligible.

This account of evaluability does not require the stronger thesis that every concrete token has an essential counterfactual modal profile of its own. What the argument needs is the relevant distinction between adequate and inadequate possibilities under which an operation is assessed. Replacing that requirement with a universal token-essential-modality thesis would add a premise not established here.

The argument leaves real alternatives. A theorist may reject the realist move from the requirements of a description to the conditions of reality. Another may take the relevant order as primitive. Another may deny that its origin is a construction-like process at all. An account of brute emergence can therefore resist this particular reductio, although it still owes whatever explanation its own claims require. A global conclusion that every relevant condition has an underived bearer requires more than this same-operation, same-respect obstruction: one must justify the scope of the dependence relation and, where used, its well-foundedness. Stroud's criticism of transcendental arguments makes the representation-to-world issue especially important. [P1]

### 4.2 From objective order to an intelligent ground

An impersonal order can be acknowledged without yet attributing understanding, intention, or agency to its ground. The positive case for an intelligent ground therefore introduces substantive premises.

The first is **irreducible directedness**: at least some genuine functions and normative orientations cannot be exhausted by present regularity, reproductive success, causal contribution, or convention. A representation can be directed toward truth even when it is false; a capacity can malfunction even when the malfunction is advantageous in some setting. A complete theory must explain which target is proper and why that target fixes the relevant assessment.

The second is **adequacy of the source to such directedness**: a sufficient ground of this order must adequately account for its determinate targets and their relation to means. On the intellectual version of this premise, knowledge of those targets and relations belongs to the explanation. If objective truth-directed functioning requires this kind of grounding, an explanation that excludes all understanding from its source is incomplete.

The inference is conditional, but it is not empty. It identifies the explanatory work that understanding would do: it unifies the target, the suitability of the means, and the institution or preservation of their directed relation. Its opponent must address that work rather than merely observe that many reliable processes operate without conscious reflection. Equally, the affirmative must defend irreducible directedness and the relevant adequacy principle against selected-effect, intrinsic-form, and structural accounts. Reliability by itself does not imply a designer. A design story by itself does not imply a truthful or good designer. [P2–P5; S5]

Further attributes require further argument. Contingent determination of one among alternatives supports a willing selector only given a warranted selection premise; bare contingency does not eliminate brute fact. Actual institution or maintenance of an order requires efficacy, but efficacy is not itself wisdom. Wisdom requires a fitting end, adequate understanding, proportionate means, and direction because of the fittingness. The common-premise argument from evaluability does not establish that whole account merely by adding “wise” to its conclusion.

This last qualification resolves an inconsistency in the inherited exposition: its argument map explicitly withheld the common-premise Wisdom step, although a concluding sentence included Wisdom among the results. The coherent reconstruction preserves the positive intellectual-ground argument and keeps that particular Wisdom inference conditional. The later affirmative arguments about actual fitting ends and source-owned fidelity in §§9–11 have additional premises and source-specific warrant. They are not annulled by the narrower argument's incomplete step.

### 4.3 Rival resting points

A serious comparison grants each rival what it actually explains.

**Modal primitivism** can affirm objective possibilities and necessities without a further ground. It avoids the contradiction of a process creating conditions already required for that same process. The dispute concerns whether primitive order is explanatorily satisfactory, not whether the word “primitive” is formally inconsistent.

**Platonism and structural realism** can locate objective relations beyond present agreement. Their challenge is to explain applicability to concrete cognition and action, and, where needed, the authority of one relation rather than another. The affirmative cannot simply assume that every impersonal relation lacks explanatory force.

**Nominalism and conventionalism** can explain representations, classifications, and learned practices without reifying universals. Their more demanding task is to account for objective similarity, constraint, and error when those are among the admitted phenomena. A sophisticated nominalist need not identify correctness with whatever a group currently votes for.

**Selected-effect and causal-role accounts** explain why capacities persist and how they contribute to systems. Their ability to explain malfunction is a real contribution. Whether their relevant histories and roles fix truth-directed epistemic function is the sharper issue; an adaptation can support deception, and a stable causal role can serve a bad end. An account that independently identifies a truth-directed role deserves assessment under that richer description.

**Intrinsic-form or value realism** can supply normative structure without personal design. It must explain the relevant form or value and how it bears on the agent. The local correction argument does not eliminate this option.

**Divine conceptualism** offers a personal intellectual locus for logical or normative order. It still must explain why the identified contents are necessarily true or fitting, how they apply, and how the account avoids arbitrary choice and self-dependent grounding. Merely relocating a relation inside a mind does not finish those tasks. [P6]

**Infinitism** contests the demand for a well-founded terminus. Its viability depends on the kind of explanatory dependence involved, rather than on the mere length of a temporal sequence.

**Deflationary or expressivist treatments** may reject the realist starting point while preserving much evaluative practice. They change the explanatory target. The existence of that option does not invalidate a conditional realist argument, but it limits what the argument establishes against someone who does not accept its datum.

Generic theism is also a legitimate terminus of a philosophical argument whose premises do not identify a particular revelation. Additional school-internal or testimonial premises must be displayed when the exposition moves beyond it.

### 4.4 Self-undermining claims and the Hume correction

An assertion offered as rationally authoritative that all reasoning lacks authority threatens its own standing. That conditional objection does not dispose of every causal or naturalistic account of belief. Hume himself anticipates the objection in *Treatise* 1.4.1.12, after distinguishing his causal account and the persistence of belief in paragraphs 7–11. His later mitigated skepticism recommends restrained inquiry and correction rather than permanent universal suspension (*Enquiry* 12.24–25). The public source's reference to paragraph 8 for the explicit objection requires this more precise paragraph-12 locator. The intelligibility argument therefore cannot use “Hume is self-refuting” as an established premise. It must address the actual normative or descriptive claim under consideration. [P7]

### 4.5 Capacity for disclosure

Suppose an intelligent, willing, efficacious ground has been established under the relevant premises. Communicative capacities then present a further explanatory question: determinate meaning, fitting expression, and reception are among the capacities actually instantiated in created speakers. If the ground is adequate to such perfection-relevant capacities, and if that adequacy excludes its being intrinsically incapable of intentional disclosure, a capacity for disclosure follows.

The qualification matters. The premise is not that every cause possesses every property of its effects. Nor does the conclusion establish an actual speech act, particular words, a language, a transmitted text, or the authenticity of a claimant. Those require additional evidence. Within an Atharī theological account, distinctions among divine knowing, willing, speaking, created vocal media, and recipient uptake remain substantive commitments. A learning model does not establish or refute them by representing only created messages and cognitive states.

The strongest warranted structure is consequently a set of connected arguments: objective evaluability; a bounded obstruction to self-originating authority; a further intellectual-ground proposal; separately supported accounts of will, efficacy, wisdom, and disclosure; and distinct evidence for any actual communication. Their connections are philosophical work, not interchangeable labels.

## 5. Proper function, accuracy, and warranted exercise

A capacity's regular operation, its proper function, its truth-directed orientation, and a person's warranted exercise of it are four distinguishable achievements. A stable process can reliably produce what it was selected to produce without being selected for truth. A properly functioning truth-directed process can err on an occasion. A true conclusion can be unwarranted because its operative basis is defective or defeated. These separations are central to a source-faithful use of proper-functionalism. [P4–P5; S5–S7]

### 5.1 What a structural profile can determine

Let $`\mathcal W`$ be a finite class of cases, let $`D:\mathcal W\to Q`$ record a declared descriptive profile, and let $`T:\mathcal W\to\{0,1\}`$ record the independently specified target judgment. The profile may include behavior, internal organization, environment, selected history, or an entire modeled trajectory. Its exact domain must be declared. For structured descriptions, $`D`$ can take values in their declared isomorphism classes, so an invariant structural classifier is covered by the same result.

**Proposition 2 (identification through a profile).** There is a function $`f:Q\to\{0,1\}`$ with $`T=f\circ D`$ on $`\mathcal W`$ if and only if

```math
D(w)=D(w')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T(w)=T(w')
\qquad (w,w'\in\mathcal W).
\quad\text{(1)}\quad
```

**Proof.** If $`T=f\circ D`$, equal profile values have equal images under $`f`$, so the displayed implication holds. Conversely, if the implication holds, assign to each value in $`D(\mathcal W)`$ the target value of any case producing it. The implication makes this assignment well-defined. Extend the assignment arbitrarily to unused values of $`Q`$. The resulting $`f`$ has the required factorization. $`\square`$

The theorem does not prove that any particular target is objective, that its values are known, or that every theoretically described case is a genuine metaphysical possibility. It identifies the information needed for exact classification within the stated class. A claim that a neutral profile fixes a source-dependent target must show that the target is constant on the profile's fibres.

Suppose two admissible cases have the same structural profile but differ in the independently fixed truth linkage or intended function. No classifier of that profile alone can distinguish them correctly. Adding a complete history helps only if the enriched history actually separates the relevant cases. A pair stipulated to agree throughout that history remains indistinguishable by it. This is a conditional result about the reduct, not a prohibition on empirical evidence identifying function.

The choice of descriptive profile also bears on the generality problem for reliability. One actual operation falls under many process types with different reference classes and error rates. A declared engineering classification can make a local test precise; it cannot, by declaration alone, establish that this is the epistemically privileged process type. The relevant classification and environmental range need their own causal and epistemic justification. [P2–P3]

Even an underived modal order, exact additive revision increments, nonproduct constraints among domains, complete finite responsiveness, or successful trajectories can be represented without fixing the intended truth relation. Where the admitted case class permits target variation while these achievements remain fixed, their conjunction still fails exact identification. A proposed normative account restricts those profiles substantively only when its target is not left as another freely assignable coordinate. This is a statement about that case class, not a claim that every richer account admits the variation.

A logical qualification is necessary. Failure of exact identification does **not** mean that every profile-invariant predicate fails to entail the target. A predicate that is false in both cases may entail the target vacuously; a predicate true only on other, uniformly positive fibres may genuinely be sufficient. To refute the implication $`G(D(w))\Rightarrow T(w)`$ using the differing pair, the proposed structural achievement $`G`$ must actually hold on that pair, including its negative member. The stronger unqualified nonentailment formulation in the inherited target-relabeling note therefore needs this restriction. [S7]

### 5.2 Why different accounts of function require different tests

Theories of function make different substantive claims. Their explanatory gains and remaining bridges should be assessed at their actual scope.

| Account | What it can explain | What still needs warrant for epistemic proper function |
|---|---|---|
| A retained higher-order rule | Why one operation corrects another under a governing procedure. | Why that procedure is true or fitting in this case; a harmful metarule can also govern consistently. |
| A trans-state or constitutive norm of truth | Why an error remains an error when current approval changes. | Its applicability and the subject's access; constitution of inquiry does not supply an infallible faculty. |
| Selected-effect function | Why a capacity has a history of producing a selected effect and can malfunction relative to it. | Why the selected effect is truth-directed; advantageous deception is a relevant control. |
| Causal or organizational role | How a component contributes to an organized system. | Why this role is epistemically proper rather than merely useful to a harmful or mistaken system. |
| Personal design | Why means are arranged toward an intended end. | The designer's competence, the truth-directed character of the end, and the applicability of the intended function. |
| Practice or convention | How participants coordinate and correct local deviations. | Why a shared standard remains correct when the practice itself is mistaken. |
| Holistic coherence or equilibrium | How mutually supporting states fit together. | Why the coherent or uniquely stable state answers to truth rather than a mutually reinforcing error. |
| Primitive truth norm or impersonal value | A candidate objective standard beyond present approval. | Its relation to the actual capacity, its explanatory sufficiency, and the subject's warranted access. |
| Original truth-directed disposition | A source-based account of a capacity's intended orientation and fitting environment. | The source, design, end, suitability, actual operation, and absence of relevant defeat in the application. |

These are not nine empirically refuted mechanisms. Several can coexist. For example, a truth-directed capacity may have a selected history and an organizational role, and a theist may regard those as part of its created means. The question is whether the additional normative and truth-related relations have been supplied and warranted, rather than inferred from a thinner description that leaves them open.

A malicious or incompetent designer is a counterexample to the inference from design alone to epistemic goodness. It is not a counterexample to the fuller hypothesis of a good, knowledgeable, truth-directing source. Similarly, selected deception tests a survival-only explanation; it does not refute a naturalistic theory that independently identifies selection for accuracy under the relevant conditions. The discipline is symmetrical without presuming that all theories have equal evidence.

### 5.3 A source-relative functioning contract

A source-neutral proper-function proposal can require an independently fixed truth target, a function-fixing relation aimed at that target, an appropriate environment, proper operation, favorable objective truth probability, and the absence or resolution of relevant defeaters. The conjunction can support a truth-oriented process assessment at its declared scope. Favorable probability is still not the actual truth of each output. The following richer source-relative contract makes that correction explicit rather than inheriting an ambiguous use of “successful function.” [S5–S6]

A useful conditional analysis names the elements that a source-based proper-function judgment needs. For a capacity $`F`$, subject $`s`$, environment $`e`$, and task $`q`$, write:

- $`\mathrm{Src}`$: the relevant source relation is actual and adequately identified;
- $`\mathrm{Des}`$: the claimed design or institution belongs to that source;
- $`\mathrm{AimT}`$: the relevant function is directed toward truth for this task;
- $`\mathrm{Fit}`$: the means and capacity are suited to that function;
- $`\mathrm{Op}`$: the capacity operates in accordance with that function;
- $`\mathrm{Env}`$: the task and environment meet the pertinent conditions.

With the arguments suppressed for readability, define the following contract predicates:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Func}&=\mathrm{Src}\land\mathrm{Des}\land\mathrm{AimT}
                 \land\mathrm{Fit}\land\mathrm{Op}\land\mathrm{Env},\\
\mathrm{AccFunc}&=\mathrm{Func}\land\mathrm{Acc},\\
\mathrm{WarrAcc}&=\mathrm{AccFunc}\land\mathrm{Und},
\end{aligned}
\quad\text{(2)}\quad
```

where $`\mathrm{Acc}`$ records actual accuracy and $`\mathrm{Und}`$ records the relevant absence of undefeated undercutting or rebutting conditions. The notation is a declared source-relative contract, not a theorem that every possible kind of warrant has this analysis. [S6]

The conjunctions establish immediate implications in one direction. Their converses require additional evidence. Proper functioning under a favorable truth-producing design does not eliminate every error. Accuracy does not establish functioning: a lucky true result can arise through a defective process. Functioning and accuracy do not by themselves settle defeat: a subject can acquire a genuine undercutter concerning the operative basis.

This repairs a tempting conflation in simpler “successful function” formulas. A high probability of truth is a property of a process and environment; actual truth is a property of this result. Treating the first as identical to the second obscures both ordinary fallibility and the possibility of lucky correctness.

Nor do accuracy, reliability, corrigibility, and coherence alone identify the source relation. A public comparison can assign the same neutral performance profile to a personally instituted truth tracker and an impersonal truth tracker while changing source predicates. Such a comparison establishes nonidentification relative to the admitted models. It does not establish that both are actual or metaphysically realizable. The affirmative source claim and the rival's admissibility need their own warrants.

### 5.4 What the positive proper-function argument adds

The positive source account is not exhausted by the foregoing negative controls. It proposes that a faculty has a determinate proper end because its origination and continuing orientation answer to knowledge and goodness; that the relevant environment is fitted to its exercise; and that error and restoration are intelligible as deviations and repairs relative to that end. This is more explanatory than merely attaching a favorable label to a successful output.

Its strongest case joins several considerations: the directedness of representation toward truth; the distinction between a function and whatever a system happens to do; the possibility of correcting even an entrenched disposition; and a source adequate to the relation between true target, suitable means, and actual operation. The argument's intellectual and teleological premises were made explicit in §4.2. If those premises are warranted, the resulting explanation can exceed an agnostic structural description.

A demonstration that structure alone fails to identify that explanation does not withdraw its independent evidence. Conversely, possessing the explanation does not certify each contemporary judgment as properly produced. Particular functioning and warrant still require the relevant application of equation (2). That is how the metaphysical account and the epistemology of actual inquiry can support one another without becoming circular substitutes.

## 6. Wise direction, effective performance, and stable character

Wisdom cannot be reduced to reaching a goal. An efficient harmful optimizer has goal-directed success without the fittingness required by wisdom. Neither is wisdom identical to actual success: a person can choose the fitting end and proportionate means but be prevented from completing the act. The corrected public argument distinguishes wise direction, its effective performance, and its stable possession. [S8]

Let $`\mathrm{Ag}`$ mean agency; $`\mathrm{K}`$, adequate understanding of the end and relevant means; $`\mathrm{FitEnd}`$, the fittingness of the end; $`\mathrm{ChooseFit}`$, choosing it because of that fittingness; $`\mathrm{Means}`$, proportionate means; and $`\mathrm{Own}`$, the attribution of the relevant direction to the same bearer. Set

```math
\mathrm{WiseDir}
 =\mathrm{Ag}\land\mathrm{K}\land\mathrm{FitEnd}
  \land\mathrm{ChooseFit}\land\mathrm{Means}\land\mathrm{Own}.
\quad\text{(3)}\quad
```

For the effective exercise, add the relevant capacity and nonaccidental execution:

```math
\mathrm{WiseExec}
 =\mathrm{WiseDir}\land\mathrm{Cap}\land\mathrm{Exec}.
\quad\text{(4)}\quad
```

A stable characterization further requires an appropriately stable disposition and wise direction across the relevant admitted occasions. One successful act cannot establish that whole claim. The domain of occasions must be warranted, rather than selected to exclude counterevidence.

These formulas articulate sufficient assessment contracts. They do not settle every philosophical analysis of wisdom or prove that a bearer satisfying them exists. Their value lies in displaying which omission a counterexample exploits.

- A knowledgeable but vicious agent lacks the fitting orientation even when it knows how to achieve its harmful purpose.
- A benevolent but incompetent agent may have a fitting aim without adequately understanding or selecting the means.
- Accidental beneficence can reach a good outcome without wise direction.
- An impersonal truth-tracking process can be epistemically useful without being an agent that chooses because an end is fitting.
- A good plan blocked by an external obstacle can satisfy wise direction while failing effective execution. Including actual success inside the definition of every wise direction would erase this case.
- Dividing knowledge, intention, and execution among several uncoordinated bearers does not establish that one bearer owns the complete direction. A genuine collective agent may do so, but its integration must be supplied.
- A selected function aimed at deception, a merely unique causal root, or a mechanically optimal policy does not add the missing fitting agency by itself.
- A single wise episode does not establish stable wisdom; stable efficiency in a defective end does not establish either.

The same distinctions clarify source predication. A conditional model can show what a source would have to satisfy for wise direction, but it does not authenticate that source. When a richer affirmative argument independently warrants actual sourcehood, knowledge, goodness, and fitting direction, the poorer model's unidentifiability result is no reason to erase that appraisal. The later arguments below are assessed under their richer premises.

## 7. Truth-calibrated revision on a finite network

A scalar improvement score can make revision appear orderly while pointing away from truth. The following result states exactly what must be checked to identify a finite revision score with a previously warranted truth-loss difference. It preserves the difference between mathematical integration of increments and epistemic warrant for their calibration. [S9]

Let $`G=(V,E)`$ be a finite undirected graph. For each edge, consider both orientations and give an integer increment $`d(u,v)`$ satisfying

```math
d(v,u)=-d(u,v).
\quad\text{(5)}\quad
```

Let $`L:V\to\mathbb Z`$ be an independently fixed and warranted truth-loss function. Lower values of $`L`$ are better in the specified respect. Choose a spanning forest $`T`$ of $`G`$. A fundamental cycle is the cycle formed by an edge outside $`T`$ together with the unique tree path joining its endpoints, with a consistent orientation when its increments are summed.

**Proposition 3 (finite calibration criterion).** The following statements are equivalent:

1. On every oriented edge, $`d(u,v)=L(u)-L(v)`$.
2. The same equality holds on the forest edges, and the sum of $`d`$ around every fundamental cycle is zero.
3. There is a potential $`N:V\to\mathbb Z`$ with $`d(u,v)=N(v)-N(u)`$ on every edge and, on each connected component $`C`$, a constant $`c_C`$ such that $`N(v)=c_C-L(v)`$.

**Proof.** The first statement implies the second because the loss differences telescope around every closed cycle. It implies the third by choosing any integer $`c_C`$ for each component and defining $`N(v)=c_C-L(v)`$.

Now assume the second statement. On a component choose a root $`r`$ and set $`N(r)=0`$. For any vertex $`v`$, define $`N(v)`$ by summing $`d`$ along the unique forest path from $`r`$ to $`v`$. The forest calibration gives

```math
N(v)=L(r)-L(v).
\quad\text{(6)}\quad
```

For a nonforest edge $`(u,v)`$, take the fundamental cycle consisting of the forest path from $`u`$ to $`v`$ followed by the edge from $`v`$ back to $`u`$. Its zero sum says

```math
N(v)-N(u)+d(v,u)=0.
\quad\text{(7)}\quad
```

Antisymmetry therefore gives $`d(u,v)=N(v)-N(u)=L(u)-L(v)`$. This proves the first statement on all edges. Finally, the third statement immediately yields the first by substitution. $`\square`$

If two potentials have the same edge differences on a connected component, their difference is constant there: traverse a path between any two vertices and telescope. The component constants in the proposition are therefore the whole freedom, not an omitted global calibration parameter.

### 7.1 Consequences and controls

Every sequence of accepted edges with $`d(u,v)>0`$ strictly decreases $`L`$. It cannot form a directed cycle. In a finite graph it cannot revisit a vertex and hence contains at most $`|V|-1`$ edges. With integer loss it also has length at most the difference between its initial loss and the minimum loss in the component. Neither bound establishes that an improving outgoing edge always exists or that a terminal point has globally minimal loss.

Acyclicity alone is weaker than calibration. Any finite directed acyclic graph has an ordinal rank increasing along its arrows, but that does not realize arbitrarily prescribed numerical increments. For example, increments of one on $`a\to b`$ and $`b\to c`$, together with an increment of three on $`a\to c`$, form no directed cycle but cannot be exact differences of a single potential. The two routes from $`a`$ to $`c`$ have different totals.

Even exact differences need not be calibrated to truth. Choosing $`N=L`$ instead of $`N=-L`$ gives an exact potential whose increases worsen loss. A forest can be perfectly calibrated while a newly admitted nonforest edge violates the required cycle equality. A coherent potential unrelated to the true target supplies order without truth orientation. Accidental correct conclusions can occur within such an order without converting the mechanism's function into truth-directed correction.

These controls explain the importance of a complete, fixed edge registry. Adding a new revision edge changes the object being certified. The previous forest-and-cycle checks need not cover it. Likewise, changing the intended truth-loss function changes the calibration claim even when every stored increment remains identical.

The proposition is consequently a finite, exact result conditional on the declared graph and independently warranted $`L`$. It does not discover truth-loss from the increments, certify a metaphysical source, or prove that a real subject's learning is globally sound. Its constructive content is that the whole finite calibration claim can be verified using a forest and the associated fundamental cycles.

## 8. What interventions can distinguish

A functional explanation may have consequences that ordinary observation cannot distinguish. A useful intervention must discriminate the competing explanations through the complete response relevant to the inquiry. Merely distinguishing a surface action can leave its target, source attribution, or normative mode unsettled. [S10]

Let $`H`$ be a finite nonempty hypothesis set and $`I`$ a finite set of authorized, feasible interventions. Let $`O`$ be the response domain, and suppose

```math
r:H\times I\longrightarrow O
\quad\text{(8)}\quad
```

is deterministic and static for the experiment in question. A response can be a structured object rather than a single bit. The model assumes that each hypothesis and each intervention retain their meaning during the inquiry; state-changing tests require a richer transition model.

For distinct hypotheses define the disagreement set

```math
\Delta(h,h')=\{i\in I:r(h,i)\ne r(h',i)\}.
\quad\text{(9)}\quad
```

**Proposition 4 (nonadaptive discrimination).** A set $`J\subseteq I`$ identifies every hypothesis from its response vector if and only if $`J`$ meets every disagreement set $`\Delta(h,h')`$ for distinct $`h,h'`$.

**Proof.** If $`J`$ misses a disagreement set, the two associated hypotheses have the same response at every selected intervention, so their response vectors coincide. They are not identified. Conversely, if every disagreement set is met, every pair of hypotheses differs in at least one selected coordinate. All response vectors are distinct, so observing the complete vector identifies its hypothesis within $`H`$. $`\square`$

Thus the minimum number of nonadaptive tests is the minimum size of a hitting set for the pairwise disagreement sets. If a disagreement set is empty, no permitted nonadaptive test set distinguishes that pair. Static determinism also means that adaptivity cannot distinguish an observationally identical pair.

### 8.1 The exact adaptive recurrence

For a current nonempty candidate set $`S\subseteq H`$ and unused interventions $`J\subseteq I`$, let $`V(S,J)`$ be the minimum possible worst-case number of further interventions needed for exact identification. Set $`V(S,J)=0`$ when $`|S|=1`$. For an intervention $`i`$, its response classes are

```math
S_{i,o}=\{h\in S:r(h,i)=o\}.
\quad\text{(10)}\quad
```

Call $`i`$ splitting when at least two of these classes are nonempty. For $`|S|>1`$,

```math
V(S,J)=
1+\min_{\substack{i\in J\\ i\ \mathrm{splits}\ S}}
\ \max_{\substack{o\in O\\S_{i,o}\ne\varnothing}}
V\bigl(S_{i,o},J\setminus\{i\}\bigr),
\quad\text{(11)}\quad
```

where a minimum over no splitting intervention is interpreted as $`\infty`$ and $`1+\infty=\infty`$.

**Proposition 5 (adaptive optimality).** Under the stated finite, deterministic, static model, equation (11) gives the optimal worst-case number of tests.

**Proof.** A test that does not split the current candidate set conveys no identifying information and can be removed from a decision tree without worsening its performance. Repeating a previously used test is likewise useless because its response is already fixed on the current candidate set.

Any successful nontrivial decision tree therefore begins with a splitting intervention $`i`$. After observing $`o`$, it must identify the actual hypothesis within $`S_{i,o}`$ using the remaining interventions. Its worst-case depth is at least one plus the largest optimal depth among those nonempty branches. Minimizing over possible first tests gives the lower bound in equation (11).

Conversely, choose a minimizing first intervention and an optimal subtree for each nonempty response class. The resulting decision tree attains that value. The argument is an induction on the number of remaining interventions; terminal singleton sets have value zero, and a nonsingleton set without a possible split cannot be identified. Finite minima are attained because the intervention set is finite. $`\square`$

### 8.2 A strict adaptive advantage

Consider four hypotheses and three binary interventions:

| Hypothesis | $`i_0`$ | $`i_1`$ | $`i_2`$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $`h_0`$ | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $`h_1`$ | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| $`h_2`$ | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| $`h_3`$ | 1 | 0 | 1 |

All three interventions are needed nonadaptively. Omitting $`i_0`$ leaves $`h_1`$ and $`h_3`$ identical; omitting $`i_1`$ leaves $`h_0`$ and $`h_2`$ identical; omitting $`i_2`$ leaves $`h_0`$ and $`h_1`$ identical.

Two tests suffice adaptively. First use $`i_2`$. If its result is zero, the candidates are $`h_0,h_2`$ and $`i_1`$ distinguishes them. If its result is one, the candidates are $`h_1,h_3`$ and $`i_0`$ distinguishes them. A binary tree of depth one has at most two leaves, so one test cannot identify four hypotheses. The adaptive optimum is therefore exactly two, while the nonadaptive optimum is exactly three.

Adding permitted interventions cannot increase the optimum, because the old procedure is still available. Removing interventions cannot improve it. These monotonicity claims hold only when the hypotheses, response semantics, authorization, and target remain fixed. Changing what a response means changes the experiment.

### 8.3 The epistemic boundary

The finite results do not choose the hypothesis class, warrant the assumed response map, or prove that an authorized intervention is ethically or practically permissible in a real setting. Those are input conditions, not consequences of the combinatorics. A finite class may omit the actual mechanism or a hybrid of its members. Stochastic responses require probabilistic identification criteria, not a deterministic decision-tree certificate relabeled as empirical certainty.

A richer response may include the operative target, the claimed source, reasons offered, and the force of an address. Projecting that response to a surface action loses whatever distinctions are not constant on the projection's fibres, by Proposition 2. A benign and a malicious mechanism can agree in an aligned setting; a version change can alter a source's response contract; an intervention can leak the disputed target into the result by construction. These are reasons to specify the experiment accurately, not to abandon intervention as evidence.

The constructive conclusion is exact: once an eligible finite response model is warranted, the proof supplies both the nonadaptive criterion and an optimal adaptive procedure. The philosophical conclusion is equally exact: discriminatory behavior establishes only the distinctions the response model genuinely carries.

### 8.4 Identification under independently eligible evidence

The preceding discrimination results leave a further question: which distinctions may the inquiry legitimately use? A description can assign an informative coordinate to every candidate without supplying any independently warranted way to obtain or interpret it. Writing “the true architecture” in a table would separate the alternatives perfectly. It would do no evidential work. Conversely, independently warranted testimony can add a distinction that a deliberately restricted behavioral experiment omits. The formal question of separation and the epistemic question of eligibility must therefore be joined without identifying them. [S23–S28]

Fix a finite nonempty class of candidates $`M`$, a target map $`Q:M\to C`$, and a finite registry $`A`$ of proposed evidential coordinates, here called **anchors**. An evidence context $`k`$ supplies a subset $`A_k\subseteq A`$ whose members are independently eligible for the comparison. Each eligible anchor $`a`$ is a total map $`a:M\to V_a`$ with a fixed type, interpretation, and version. The mathematical model assumes these maps and their eligibility; it does not establish either. An observed value must have the meaning assigned by the map, and missing information must not silently be filled with the disputed target's value.

For $`S\subseteq A_k`$, write $`P_S(m)=(a(m))_{a\in S}`$ for the complete joint profile. Identification of $`Q`$ means that a function $`f:P_S(M)\to C`$ satisfies $`Q=f\circ P_S`$. The target can be coarser than complete candidate identity: different candidates may require the same action or share the same relevant property. Define the set of target-conflict pairs and the pairs covered by one anchor as

```math
E_Q=\{\{m,m'\}\subseteq M:Q(m)\ne Q(m')\},
\qquad
C_a=\{e=\{m,m'\}\in E_Q:a(m)\ne a(m')\}.
```

The context-relative minimum is

```math
\kappa_Q(k)=\min\bigl\{|S|:S\subseteq A_k,
             \ \bigcup_{a\in S}C_a=E_Q\bigr\},
```

with value $`\infty`$ when no cover exists. The empty family is allowed. Thus a constant target has minimum zero, including when no anchor is eligible.

**Proposition 5A (eligible target-conflict cover).** A family $`S\subseteq A_k`$ identifies $`Q`$ exactly if and only if it covers every member of $`E_Q`$. Consequently, some eligible family identifies the target if and only if $`\kappa_Q(k)<\infty`$.

**Proof.** Suppose $`Q=f\circ P_S`$. If a conflict pair were uncovered, its two members would have equal values at every selected anchor and hence the same joint profile. Applying $`f`$ would give equal target values, contrary to the definition of a conflict pair. Thus every conflict pair is covered.

Conversely, suppose every conflict pair is covered. If $`P_S(m)=P_S(m')`$, no selected anchor distinguishes the two candidates. They therefore cannot form a target-conflict pair, so $`Q(m)=Q(m')`$. Assign to each attained profile the target value of any candidate producing it. The preceding implication makes the assignment well-defined, and it gives the required $`f`$. The finite registry has finitely many subsets, so any nonempty collection of covering subsets contains one of minimum cardinality. If none exists, some conflict pair is uncovered even by $`A_k`$ itself, and no eligible subfamily can identify the target. $`\square`$

This combines the fibre criterion in Proposition 2 with the finite discrimination argument of Proposition 4; it is not an independent new general theorem. What changes is the explicit eligibility condition and the possibility that several candidates have the same target value. [S26–S27]

The direction of refinement is essential. Let three candidates have target values $`(0,0,1)`$ and let an eligible anchor have values $`(x,y,z)`$, all distinct. The anchor identifies the target by sending both $`x`$ and $`y`$ to zero and $`z`$ to one. There are three attained profiles and only two target values. Requiring the numbers to be equal would reject this valid identifier. Identification requires each profile fibre to lie inside a target fibre; it does not require the two partitions to coincide. This example also shows why extra information within one target class is harmless to exact identification. [S26]

### 8.5 What additional evidence can improve

**Proposition 5B (fixed-map eligibility expansion).** Suppose the candidate class, target, and value maps remain fixed and $`A_k\subseteq A_d`$. Then

```math
\kappa_Q(d)\leq\kappa_Q(k)
```

in the extended order containing $`\infty`$.

**Proof.** Every eligible covering family in $`k`$ remains an eligible covering family in $`d`$. If the old minimum is finite, the new minimum cannot exceed the cardinality of an old minimizing family. If the old value is infinite, the inequality holds automatically in the extended order. This includes a constant target, for which both minima are zero. $`\square`$

The map-preservation hypothesis cannot be replaced by preservation of a name. On two candidates with different targets, an old anchor with values $`(0,1)`$ identifies the target. A later same-named instrument with values $`(0,0)`$ does not. Counting the latter as the former would falsely infer monotonicity from a change of semantics. A new candidate population can likewise introduce a previously absent conflict. Eligibility expansion, instrument revision, and enlargement of the comparison class are different operations. [S26]

The minimum counts entries in the declared registry. It is not a measure of epistemic strength, acquisition effort, proof length, or explanatory simplicity. If two binary anchors are repackaged as one ordered-pair anchor, the same information can have registry cardinality one instead of two. A weighted variant may minimize $`\sum_{a\in S}w(a)`$ for independently specified nonnegative costs, but the choice and additivity of those costs require their own justification. A source package containing many conditions must not be advertised as one cheap observation because the table gives it one column. Actual inquiry, possible failed checks, and warranted human uptake remain separate questions.

A small architecture comparison illustrates both the constructive result and its limitation. Let the target distinguish a unified personal candidate $`u`$, a single-bearer impersonal candidate $`i`$, and a plural coordinated candidate $`p`$. These are stipulated comparison descriptions, not a proof that any description is actual or metaphysically realizable. The columns below are the complete value maps used by this example. [S26–S28]

| Candidate | Declared neutral profile | Common-bearer coordinate | Intentional-uptake coordinate | Guarded source package |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $`u`$ | $`n`$ | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| $`i`$ | $`n`$ | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| $`p`$ | $`n`$ | 0 | 0 | 0 |

With only the neutral column eligible, no target-conflict pair is covered and the minimum is infinite. If the two formal coordinates are admitted, they jointly give profiles $`(1,1)`$, $`(1,0)`$, and $`(0,0)`$, so they identify the three candidates. Neither alone suffices: common bearer leaves $`u,i`$ together; uptake leaves $`i,p`$ together. The minimum is exactly two in that registry. The common-bearer values are input to this calculation. No bearer-identity or property-ontology theorem is being inferred from them; those questions belong to the separate treatment in [Received Actuality and Intrinsic Necessity, §10.10](received-actuality-and-intrinsic-necessity.md#1010-what-isand-is-notunified-by-the-construction).

The guarded source column induces the same partition as uptake in this table. If that package and the common-bearer coordinate are independently eligible in a source-conditioned comparison, their minimum is also two. Equality of partitions does not make their warrants interchangeable. In particular, the formal-coordinate calculation supplies no operational procedure for measuring uptake, and the source-conditioned calculation supplies no neutral measurement of it.

Adding a column with values $`(u,i,p)`$ would reduce the minimum to one. If the column merely reads the answer under dispute, its exclusion is an eligibility judgment, not a combinatorial defect. If an independently warranted experiment genuinely has those three responses, its admission would instead be a substantive improvement. The result leaves that possibility open. Likewise, if independently warranted premises reduce the candidate class to $`\{u\}`$, the residual identification minimum becomes zero. That does not mean that establishing those premises required no evidence.

### 8.6 Source-conditioned exclusion and its exact burden

A source-based argument needs to distinguish the truth of a claim, its attribution to a source, and its application to the target being assessed. Consider a contract $`(b,\Pi,\mathrm{Auth},\mathrm{Ident})`$. Here $`b`$ is the source's intended referent, $`\Pi`$ is the relevant predicate bundle, $`\mathrm{Auth}`$ supplies the requisite warrant for accepting that attributed content, and $`\mathrm{Ident}`$ identifies its referent with the target bearer. Authentication in this sense is more than possessing a document or matching its checksum. Its sufficient epistemic conditions must be warranted for the kind of claim being made. Referent identification is equally substantive: a correct statement about one object need not be a correct statement about another. [S23–S24]

The same distinction applies to a school-internal theological reconstruction. Its source predicates may have real argumentative force under that reconstruction's warranted premises. They do not become conclusions of a deliberately source-neutral argument merely by being included in a formal model. Nor does the failure of the restricted argument cancel an independently warranted source argument. The source-conditioned comparison studied here uses an Atharī interpretation; it does not claim a neutral consensus about the theology or authenticate its source-to-world application.

**Proposition 5C (finite conditional rival filtering).** Let $`R`$ be a finite declared family of complete rival assignments for a finite predicate registry $`\Pi`$. Define

```math
D(r)=\{q\in\Pi:r\text{ assigns }q\text{ false}}.
```

For a selected set $`S\subseteq\Pi`$, every rival conflicts with at least one asserted member of $`S`$ if and only if $`S\cap D(r)\ne\varnothing`$ for every $`r\in R`$. Under warranted attribution, truth acceptance, referent identification, and applicability of those assertions, every such rival is excluded from the corresponding source-expanded class.

**Proof.** A rival conflicts with a selected assertion exactly when some selected predicate is false in that rival, which is exactly membership in $`S\cap D(r)`$. Universal quantification over the finite rival family gives the equivalence. When the selected assertions are warranted of the identified target, a rival falsifying one of them cannot satisfy the expanded premises. The conclusion depends on those premises; the intersection condition alone supplies no warrant for asserting them. $`\square`$

The predicates in this statement have truth values in the candidate assignments. Mere silence about a predicate or absence of evidence for it is not its falsity. An incompletely specified rival cannot be discarded just because the record omits a positive assertion. Nor does filtering an inconsistent source theory supply a credible winner: the expanded class must itself have an eligible interpretation. The practical comparison requires these semantic checks before applying the finite result.

One inherited example uses abstract order, impersonal origination, plural realization, a silent personal ground, a ground whose speech is only created, and a personal creator without the proposed revelational identification. Its minimum counts can be proved without inventing an objective explanatory score. In its exact assignment table, the created-speech-only rival lacks only the uncreated-Speech predicate; the plural rival lacks only oneness or the same-referent binding; and the last rival lacks only identification of the revealed referent with the neutral target. These three defect sets are pairwise disjoint. Every separator of the full six-rival portfolio therefore needs at least three selected commitments. The set consisting of uncreated Speech, oneness, and referent identification meets every defect set, so three is attained. [S24]

For the restricted first three rivals, knowing and oneness suffice. Knowing excludes the abstract and impersonal assignments; oneness excludes the plural assignment. No single registered predicate suffices, because the impersonal rival's defect set and the plural rival's defect set are disjoint. The minimum there is exactly two. These are results about the displayed predicate assignments, not neutral proofs of the attributed predicates or of the rivals' metaphysical possibility.

The full-portfolio count requires a qualification. Referent identification occurs in that counted commitment set, while it also serves as a standing condition of source application. Once identification is already fixed in the background, the rival that denies it has already been excluded. Among the remaining five rivals, uncreated Speech and oneness suffice; the disjoint created-speech-only and plural defect sets show that two additional predicates are still necessary. Thus the raw full-portfolio minimum of three and the residual minimum of two answer different questions. Authentication remains separately assumed in both calculations. Neither number counts all the work required to establish the source contract.

This clarification also prevents a source-critical error. Referent identification is an additional bridge, not a predicate established merely by the cited passage's using one name. The inherited reconstruction distinguishes true volition or speech attributes from unrestricted use of corresponding Divine Names, and distinguishes a necessary existent from a Creator conclusion. Its recorded access is to an English translation, not a fresh verification of the Arabic primary text. Those source qualifications remain part of its authority boundary. The mathematical filtering argument requires none of them to be silently strengthened. [S23–S24]

### 8.7 Why a source-dependent distinction does not become a neutral entailment

**Proposition 5D (premise-relative nonentailment).** Let $`\Gamma`$ be a set of premises in a declared semantics and $`C`$ a conclusion. If an admissible interpretation satisfies $`\Gamma`$ and falsifies $`C`$, then $`\Gamma\not\models C`$. A derivation of $`C`$ from a stronger set $`\Gamma\cup\Sigma`$ does not remove that countermodel to the original entailment.

**Proof.** Semantic entailment requires every admissible interpretation satisfying the premises to satisfy the conclusion. The stated interpretation violates that requirement for $`\Gamma`$. Additional premises may exclude it from the models of $`\Gamma\cup\Sigma`$; they do not change its satisfaction of the original premises. $`\square`$

A related language distinction explains the scope of this result. Suppose two expanded structures have the same reduct to a language $`L`$: the same domains, interpretations of its symbols, and any semantic relations required by its operators. Every $`L`$-sentence then has the same truth value in the two structures. For an ordinary first-order language this follows by induction: terms have the same values, atomic formulas have the same interpretations, Boolean operations preserve agreement, and quantifiers range over the same domains. Thus a difference confined to extra source predicates cannot itself be detected by an $`L`$-sentence. This pairwise observation does not assert that the entire expanded theory is a conservative extension; adding source premises can restrict which reducts remain admissible. [S23–S25]

There are two ways to exceed this boundary legitimately. One can independently warrant the additional premises or their sufficient equivalents at the intended comparison scope. Alternatively, one can give a different argument for the conclusion. The restriction concerns the dependence of this argument; it is not a demand that every possible argument establish every premise used by its predecessor. No permanent inability of reason, observation, or testimony follows.

The historical architecture comparison has this limited force. Its three candidates—a unified personal source, impersonal powers with plural realization, and abstract order with a concrete actualizer—agree on one selected neutral profile. That profile records underived order, formal cross-domain coupling, an additive revision field, finite articulability, token responsiveness, concrete realization, and availability of knowledge-like, selection-like, and efficacy roles. The target commitments concerning personal sourcehood and the richer attributed properties vary. Any target that varies within this attained profile fibre fails exact profile-only identification by Proposition 2. [S25]

This is not the assertion that the profile contains every neutral discriminator. An independently warranted coordinate that separates the conflicting pair would defeat the particular nonidentification witness. It is also not a claim that adding the desired properties to a candidate derives them from the profile. A formally described impersonal candidate can show insufficiency of the registered formal premises without thereby establishing its metaphysical possibility. The comparison is nonexhaustive, and a unique survivor in it need not be the only source-compatible architecture outside it.

The explanatory comparison requires an additional discipline. Counting bearers, grouping primitive features under one name, or listing unresolved premises does not yield an objective cross-kind cost metric. Shared derivation, restrictions across cases, and counterfactual dependence can be explanatory achievements; merely placing freely variable features in one bearer need not add them. The inherited comparison expressly supplies no warranted scalar aggregation, prior probability, or truth-conducive ordering. Its source-compatible survivor therefore cannot be promoted to a neutral explanatory winner just by counting its entries. [S23; S25]

The packaging result can be made exact. Let a plural explanatory profile have primitive space $`\Theta=\prod_{j=1}^n\Theta_j`$, explanandum space $`E`$, and output map $`F:\Theta\to E`$. Its declared primitive variations and represented dependencies are part of the comparison. Replace the primitive space by $`\widehat\Theta=\{g\}\times\Theta`$, and let $`p(g,\theta)=\theta`$. Assume that the new output map is $`\widehat F=F\circ p`$, that $`p`$ carries exactly the same admissible primitive variations in both directions, and that the new presentation adds no further cross-coordinate law, restriction or derivation. The carrier label is fixed; it does not restrict the values of the primitive coordinates. [S29]

**Proposition 5E (carrier-label invariance).** Under those hypotheses, the two presentations permit the same primitive profiles, determine the same explanandum profiles, and preserve every dependence expressed by their registered primitive variations. They are derivationally equivalent at this represented level.

**Proof.** The inverse of $`p`$ sends $`\theta`$ to $`(g,\theta)`$, so profiles correspond bijectively. The equation $`\widehat F(g,\theta)=F(\theta)`$ gives the same output at each corresponding profile. For any admissible variation $`i`$, its transported variation is $`\widehat i=p^{-1}\circ i\circ p`$ on the corresponding domain. Hence $`\widehat F\circ\widehat i=F\circ i\circ p`$. The same correspondence in the reverse direction preserves all registered variation/output comparisons and their dependencies. No extra restriction or derivation has been added, so the fixed carrier label supplies no further represented explanatory contrast. This is an isomorphism of the specified explanatory profiles, not a proof of equivalence between complete metaphysical theories. $`\square`$

If, in addition, $`E=\prod_jE_j`$ and $`F(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)=(F_1(\theta_1),\ldots,F_n(\theta_n))`$, with independent coordinate variation still permitted, each output depends only on its own primitive coordinate. The account therefore remains a product of independent explanatory modules even when all features are attributed to one bearer. The argument would fail if a new law restricted the admissible joint profiles, if one primitive variation constrained a different domain, or if a shared derivation supplied a relation omitted by the original representation.

For example, two freely variable binary coordinates admit all four pairs. Giving them a common fixed carrier leaves all four pairs available. Requiring the coordinates to agree leaves only $`(0,0)`$ and $`(1,1)`$. This is a genuine new joint restriction despite retaining both marginal values; the one-coordinate flips of the unrestricted product are no longer always admissible. The example distinguishes a new relation from a new label. It does not establish the restriction's truth or the personal character of anything satisfying it.

The inherited application concerns modal identity, concrete actuality and efficacy, determinate content, and normative correctness. Assigning one bearer four freely recombinable primitive features for those domains does not yet derive their coordination. Shared derivation, restriction of joint possibilities, cross-domain counterfactual dependence, reduction of independently posited primitives, or exclusion of a previously permitted configuration could supply additional explanatory content. Rational agency is one proposed architecture for linking representation, evaluation, selection and efficacy; its links still require argument. The result neither denies ontological unity's possible value nor makes plural primitives preferable. It preserves the predecessor's exact limit: a reduction in bearer count alone is not a reduction in represented primitive explanatory freedom. [S29]

The architecture comparison also makes a narrower descriptive judgment. Write $`H_a`$ for an architecture's registered unresolved premises, $`K_a`$ for its admitted consequences, and $`B_a`$ for its unexplained stopping points. Its declared set-inclusion diagnostic requires no larger premise or stopping-point set, no smaller consequence set, no worsening of the registered surviving-countermodel condition, and at least one strict improvement. On the frozen three-row table no architecture meets these requirements against another. Already the premise-set test fails in every direction: each row contains a declared premise token absent from each other row. For the personal-source row one such token is the required common-bearer bridge; the impersonal-powers row has its powers-realizer premise; the abstract-order row has its necessary-abstract-order premise. Thus no row's full $`H_a`$ is a subset of either competitor's. [S25]

This verifies **no strict dominance in that declared diagnostic**. It does not prove that the premise tokens have equal costs, that differently named premises are semantically independent, or that every reasonable explanatory ordering leaves the alternatives incomparable. Identifying two tokens, discharging a premise, or changing the consequence and countermodel assignments would require a new justified comparison. In particular, the richer affirmative arguments elsewhere in these works must be assessed before transporting this historical table to their conclusions. The table is not an objective cost metric, and source compatibility still does not become neutral dominance.

The conclusion also runs in the other direction: the restricted comparison cannot erase a richer positive argument. Sections 4–6 defend additional intellectual, teleological, and normative premises, while §§9–11 develop actual source relations and truthful disclosure. [Received Actuality and Intrinsic Necessity, §§10.4 and 12.4](received-actuality-and-intrinsic-necessity.md) gives affirmative, defeasible arguments for actual connected-field source unity and informed practical agency. Those appraisals must be assessed at their own premises. A historical profile deliberately omitting their disputed explanatory relations cannot supply a global verdict against them. Their warrant does not, in turn, authorize treating every older bridge as an unconditional formal theorem.

### 8.8 Transport between evidence contexts

An anchor can legitimately move between contexts only when its intended use survives the move. This requires identification of the originating and receiving contexts, the anchor itself, its type, value semantics, version, authority, and applicability. Some cases preserve these directly; others need fresh justification. Extensional similarity of two finite partitions establishes an informational comparison, not the truth or entitlement of either input. [S26]

A positive sufficient case is transparent. Suppose an eligible anchor $`a`$ is replaced by $`a'=t\circ a`$, where $`t`$ is injective on its attained values. Then $`a(m)=a(m')`$ if and only if $`a'(m)=a'(m')`$, so the two anchors cover exactly the same target-conflict pairs. Replacing one by the other in a family preserves coverage and cardinality. The calculation establishes the information-preservation part of transport. It does not authenticate the recoding or warrant the receiving use. Those conditions must accompany the equality of maps.

The version example has two candidates: the same received bytes apply to an earlier recipient version and are stale for a later version. The required actions are respectively apply and hold. Receipt bytes are equal in both candidates, so receipt alone leaves the conflict uncovered. A properly interpreted version-compatibility coordinate with values true and false identifies the required action, giving minimum one in the version-aware registry. A separate authority coordinate, though required by the application contract, is true in both candidates and covers no conflict. Its failure to reduce the cardinality does not make it dispensable to warranted action. Capability, relevant invalidators, adoption, and actual execution likewise have their own roles beyond the profile calculation. [S26]

The source-world example has a different target. Two candidate interpretations share the same translated role profile but differ between personal and impersonal classification. Role profile alone gives an infinite minimum. The frozen table gives intensional predicate preservation the values true and false, and gives actual-world selection the values actual and nonactual. Either coordinate would separate the pair if independently eligible, so each is a one-anchor cover. The two coordinates nevertheless have different types and warrants. Preserving a predicate's intended strength is not selecting which candidate is actual; formally writing an actual-world selector is not possessing evidence for its value. [S26]

The stronger source package used in the architecture table makes the obligations explicit. Its source-to-world conditions include acceptance of the source regime; exact custody and source version; adequate translation; identification of the source bearer; an admissible interpretation family; unique candidate denotation under that family; actual-world selection; preservation of intensional content and predicate strength; identity with the proposed target; adequacy to the world; and applicability to this target. Its practical-role conditions add objective fittingness, apprehension of the relevant end or reasons, intentional uptake, selection because of fittingness, reliable realization, and co-instantiation of the required roles by the intended bearer. [S28]

These nineteen conditions constitute a declared sufficient contract. Their being conjoined makes every one necessary for satisfying that particular conjunction; a truth-table check of single-condition deletions does not independently prove that each is philosophically necessary for every possible source attribution or wise act. In particular, reliable realization makes this package stronger than the wise direction of §6. A fitting plan can be wisely chosen and fail in execution. The package must not turn that existing distinction into a contradiction by redefining all wisdom as successful performance.

Architecture classification, recipient applicability, and source-world predication thus share one finite conflict-cover structure while retaining different substantive targets. No theorem of the common structure makes a version certificate evidence for personal agency, or turns a theological predicate into a common intervention. The useful transfer is the separation criterion with its assumptions intact.

### 8.9 Scope of the resulting comparison

The new formal layer identifies the exact remaining question for a fixed comparison: which independently eligible anchors cover its remaining target conflicts? It allows additional evidence to improve an inquiry, recognizes a source argument's conditional force, and makes illegitimate changes of meaning visible. It does not choose the true target, establish the actual model class, or manufacture a source's authority from its own classification.

The results assume finite declared candidates, stable total maps, and exact complete anchor values. No noisy or statistical identification theorem, unknown-class guarantee, adaptive acquisition bound, or actual-world selection follows. Existing §8.1 supplies an adaptive result under its different explicit conditions. The logical nonentailment argument likewise requires an admissible counterinterpretation, not merely a contradictory list labeled a rival.

The mathematical proofs and finite cases above stand independently of historical checking receipts. The corrected refinement condition is essential; historical finite trials cannot replace the general proof or authenticate the philosophical input. These results extend the existing account of cognition by locating precisely where warranted source content, new observation, or a substantive bridge can change a conclusion. They leave the positive arguments and their genuine unresolved premises available for direct assessment.

## 9. Intrinsic reasons, fitting response, and the indifferent agent

The preceding results leave room for substantive normative reasoning. They show where a descriptive or computational account has not supplied a target; they do not establish that normative judgments are unwarranted unless deduced from wholly norm-free premises. A reason can have independent prima facie warrant through what it is about: another person's actual good, an actual relation, a fitting acknowledgment, or a valid undertaking. The remaining work is to articulate that warrant and test its scope. [S11; S13–S17]

### 9.1 Why present indifference is not a general defeater

Consider a capable agent who recognizes what another person suffers but does not care. Three questions remain distinct: whether the other is harmed, whether the treatment is wrong, and whether the agent has a reason to desist independently of current desire. The agent's indifference does not cancel the first. It also does not by itself defeat an independently warranted affirmative answer to either of the other two.

The positive argument is that a person's significance, a fitting relation, or what is due is not constituted by the wrongdoer's present appetite. If a relationship genuinely warrants a response, the question whether the response is owed can remain fixed while motivation changes. A reason addressed to an unwilling agent is not incoherent merely because it fails to produce compliance. Occurrence conditions for an act and normative conditions for its being due are different.

This is not a complete refutation of the informed amoralist. Such an interlocutor can contest the normative force of the alleged good or relation. The affirmative then owes substantive support for that appraisal. But “I do not want to respond” is not, without a further premise connecting all reasons to present wants, an answer to a warranted claim that a response is due. The dispute concerns the authority of the reason, rather than the empirical existence of a desire.

Ibn Taymiyya's *Minhāj al-sunna* 3:104–106 makes the pertinent distinction between willing as a condition of an act's occurrence and willing as a condition of obligation. The passage presupposes a valid obligation; it does not establish the legitimacy of every proposed obligation by itself. Its contribution is to block the inference from lack of willingness to absence of an otherwise warranted requirement. [P17]

### 9.2 Real goodness, contextual fittingness, and command

The relevant discussion in *Majmūʿ al-fatāwā* 8:428–436 defends real evaluative features while rejecting two simplifications: fixed moral classifications indifferent to every circumstance, and an account in which an act's goodness means nothing beyond the occurrence of a command. The positive account allows an act to have benefits or harms of its own, an address to add a real relation, and the purpose of a directive to be fulfilled through a response that differs from its ostensible outward object. [P14; S11–S12]

These are three distinct sources of explanatory relevance.

**An independently good or harmful act.** The act has features that matter before the particular directive is introduced. Their created character does not make them unreal, and their normative significance is not exhausted by the evaluator's feeling.

**A directive-added relation.** An actual rightful address can make a further kind of response fitting. An action may now answer a command, demonstrate fidelity, or discharge a relation that did not have that specification before. This additional goodness does not imply that the act previously had no value.

**A response-mediated end.** The directive can make possible an actual act of willingness or acknowledgment through which a good is achieved. The point need not be the source's acquiring information, nor need it require completion of every outward step initially contemplated. Section 11 develops this case.

Neither the existence of goodness nor an increase in goodness fixes obligatory grade. An excellent act can be recommended while a less extensive performance meets the requirement. Calling an added relation good therefore does not, without the relevant authority and mode, show that every failure to realize it is a violation of an obligation.

### 9.3 Due acknowledgment and the limits of injury-based accounts

The argument from a genuine benefit is directed. If this benefactor supplied this benefit, acknowledgment answers to that benefactor rather than to a substitute who did not supply it. The relevant relation can persist even when the recipient ceases to enjoy acknowledging it. Acknowledgment also need not be a payment that benefits the benefactor. It can be a fitting recognition of what is already true of the relation.

The discussion in *Jāmiʿ al-masāʾil* VI, 234–252, uses gratitude and due right to challenge an account under which injustice always consists in injuring its object. Its internal argument is that an opponent who already accepts a rational requirement of gratitude has admitted a case in which failure of due response matters without being measured solely by the benefactor's loss. That does not independently establish every alleged right. The account must still identify who is owed what and why. [P16; S11]

The same source acknowledges that describing justice as putting a thing in its appropriate place requires specification. This is a useful virtue of the argument. A general fittingness account is not useless because its applications need knowledge, but neither does the general phrase settle the application. A later good consequence does not automatically erase an earlier wrong, and lack of injury does not automatically erase a warranted right.

The source illustrates fittingness through proportions in buildings, garments, food, and cultivation: appropriate treatment can concern a good that does not itself feel pain, while a failure can harm its beneficiary. These examples establish that fittingness is not exhausted by suffering in the immediate bearer. They do not establish that every artifact function generates a categorical duty for a person; that further practical relation still needs warrant.

For the theological application, the source's claim is that divine worship can be due for the source's own worth and relation to the creature, while its beneficial effects accrue to the creature. God's lack of need is thus not a reason that acknowledgment must be pointless. This is an argument about fitting relation, not a claim that the recipient supplies the source's essential perfection.

The source's healthy and disordered desire analogy, in VI, 186–193, concerns the possibility of a mistaken evaluative appetite. It should not be converted into a contemporary clinical diagnosis. Nor can a disputed standard be proved merely by defining dissent from it as disorder. The analogy has force only where the relevant account of healthy orientation already has warrant. The distinct final-cause argument beginning later in that unit is outside the complete argument received here.

### 9.4 Original benefaction and practical allegiance

The affirmative account of the actual wise and good original is stronger than a recommendation to admire an impressive cause. It joins origination and continuing provision with intrinsic excellence and the fittingness of directed love and acknowledgment. On that appraisal, the response is practical: it concerns worship, allegiance, and obedience, not only the spectator's favorable judgment. The public argument treats this as a substantive, defeasibly warranted conclusion. It is preserved here at that scope. [S13–S16]

The explanatory force comes from the conjunction. Causal production alone does not grant unlimited jurisdiction over every action of what is produced. A maker can have a bad end, can act without knowledge, or can lack the relevant title. The actual perfect original is not characterized by those omissions. Its benefaction identifies the source to whom acknowledgment is directed; its goodness and wisdom make the response fitting; its original relation distinguishes it from dependent substitutes.

Ibn al-Qayyim develops these considerations in *Miftāḥ dār al-saʿāda*, especially 2:878–881 and 2:1076–1089. In the latter unit, lack of divine benefit from obedience is answered through intrinsic worth and benefaction rather than a wage bargain. The argument supports a real practical orientation toward the source. It also qualifies the response by the creature's finite powers. Worth beyond the recipient's capacity does not establish a requirement to perform the impossible or to maximize every conceivable improvement. [P19]

The distinction between obligatory and more complete performances is explicit elsewhere in the same source. The treatment of fasting and charity at 2:939 distinguishes a required performance from a more complete combination; the following discussion distinguishes removal of an obligation from the continuing recommendation of its object. These controls prevent the stronger original-worth argument from being flattened into either optional appreciation or unrestricted maximizing duty.

The result is a positive relation-sensitive appraisal. It does not by itself answer every later question about which form of acknowledgment is exclusively required, the scope of a particular directive, or its communication to this recipient. Those are legitimate further questions, developed in §10. They do not annul the owed practical response already warranted.

### 9.5 Wrongness, desert, and actual punishment

Theology can distinguish evaluative defect, a basis for blame, and the fulfilled conditions for punishment. The corrected printed reading of *Darʾ taʿāruḍ al-ʿaql wa-l-naql* 8:490–494 attributes to the endorsed account real good and bad features while maintaining nonpunishment until the relevant message reaches the recipient. Calling an act a basis for blame or punishment is therefore not a claim that all conditions of actual liability have been met. [P15; S11]

The source-critical repairs matter to the inference. At printed 492, the account allowing punishment of those who have not sinned is reported as an opposed view; an electronic extra negative changes that description. At printed 494, omission of the restrictive particle in an electronic witness alters the stated condition on punishment. The retained print-control report corrects both and identifies the authorial preference separately from the reported schools. No new manuscript collation or transmission judgment is made by this exposition.

The philosophical lesson is broader than the particular theological conclusion: recognition of a real normative defect does not by itself settle every question of imputability, adequate notice, enforcement, or sanction. Conversely, a condition delaying or excluding punishment does not necessarily eliminate the underlying evaluative difference. These are distinctions in an account of normativity, not an adjudication of any contemporary person's duties or liability.

## 10. Authority, disclosure, and undertakings

A reason to acknowledge a source, a source's power to specify a response, and a recipient's knowledge that a duty obtains are different but connected relations. Confusing them can either inflate authority or withhold warranted practical conclusions. A satisfactory account must avoid both errors. [S13–S16]

### 10.1 Three ways a particular response can be warranted

Suppose a source is already owed a practical response, and two outward performances appear to the recipient to satisfy it. Why should an actual specification make one required?

**A relevant selection title.** The owed relation may confer control over this aspect of its discharge. An admissible, feasible, adequately conveyed exercise of that control can determine which performance is due. The substantive premise is that the actual relation grants that particular control. Stating “a rightful command binds” displays the conditional consequence but does not independently establish the contested title.

**An actual performance difference.** One candidate may fail to realize the owed good in the actual circumstances. If that difference is established, a determinate duty need not await a theory of unrestricted control. The explanation is that the supposedly equivalent alternative was not actually adequate. Calling the source's chosen performance obligatory cannot itself be the evidence that its competitor fails.

**Truthful normative disclosure.** A properly authenticated, knowledgeable, truthful, source-owned assertion can warrant that a particular obligation obtains. Such testimony need not provide the recipient with a second independent derivation of the constitutive title. Knowledge of a fact does not always require an independent explanation of everything that grounds it.

The third route is especially important. An unresolved explanatory comparison about title is not an additional condition imposed on an obligation already warranted by adequate normative testimony. The testimony still requires actual attribution, content, normative mode, scope, time, application, and treatment of relevant defeaters. A bare quotation, a sincere wish, or a claimant's assertion of authority does not automatically satisfy those conditions.

A source can speak truthfully about an obligation; it can also request, recommend, permit, intercede, or perform a purported act of specification. These have different truth and success conditions. Truthfulness concerning a proposition must not be confused with automatic constitutive success of every attempted directive. In particular, sincerely intending to impose a duty does not establish that the intended duty was within the speaker's authority.

### 10.2 Why general governance does not settle every local title

*Miftāḥ* 1:17–18 and 2:1067–1076 presents wise direction as opposed to abandoning humanity to unregulated appetite and mutual predation. It also treats creaturely choice as relevant to accountability. This supplies positive support for moral governance and real agency. It does not show that every rival to one disputed specification is a rival to all moral direction. [P19; S14]

The narrower interlocutor can accept practical allegiance, worship, and normative restraint while asking why one otherwise adequate form of response is excluded. To answer that interlocutor solely by showing the costs of lawlessness changes the comparison. A local title would suffice; no universal theory of jurisdiction is required. Nor does describing the narrower question prove that an adequate alternative actually exists in any particular case.

The ownership and justice discussion at *Miftāḥ* 2:1121–1135 strengthens the point in a different way. The endorsed account refuses to justify punishment without desert merely by invoking power or ownership. It also positively maintains the due worship relation. The punitive restriction is not a blanket argument against command authority; it blocks a shortcut that would make the word “ownership” do all the normative work.

Thus the affirmative remains substantial. The original's actual goodness and benefaction ground practical acknowledgment, and a warranted in-scope exercise of retained control can specify it. The residual explanation asks what warrants that control in the contested relation. It neither converts the positive appraisal to an unsupported hypothesis nor treats the opponent's merely possible alternative as an established defeater.

### 10.3 Informed choice and merely apparent equivalence

An informed, wise source can know relevant differences that a recipient does not see. Unexplained aversion or the appearance that alternatives are equally good does not establish their actual equivalence. The discussions of election in *Zād al-maʿād* 1:13–17 and the received *Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl* electronic unit 31–34 connect selection with knowledge of fitness, rather than bare arbitrary power. The latter expressly extends the argument to commitment to the actual command despite aversion. [P20–P21; S15]

The primary examples include the suitability of those elected to a commission. Their rationale can rebut a preference for a candidate based on irrelevant worldly prestige. Transporting that rationale to exclusive control of an otherwise adequate form of a single recipient's response requires attention to the change of object. Suitability for appointment and title to exclude another adequate discharge are not identical relations.

Two conclusions must therefore remain separate. Where adequate source-owned disclosure establishes an in-scope obligatory requirement, the recipient's lack of access to its whole wisdom need not defeat the obligation. Within a different explanatory comparison that deliberately leaves the selection title unsettled, possible hidden benefit does not prove that an alternative is actually inadequate. Greater benefit alone also does not settle obligatory grade. This qualification preserves the source's affirmative practical argument without converting epistemic humility into a universal title proof.

### 10.4 Permission, promised nonexercise, and relinquished title

Three situations can all leave a person presently free to perform an action, yet have different implications for a later specification.

| Situation | What is established | What does not follow merely from it |
|---|---|---|
| Present permission or nonprohibition | The action is currently left open under the applicable normative mode. | An irrevocable grant of immunity against every later in-scope specification. |
| A valid undertaking not to exercise control | The source has constrained its own later conduct under the undertaking's actual terms. | Extinction or transfer of the underlying normative power. |
| A constitutively effective relinquishing grant | The recipient has the warranted immunity or transferred control the grant creates. | Anything outside the grant's actual domain, duration, conditions, or valid effect. |

The distinctions are symmetric. Prior nonprohibition is not proof of surrendered control. Absence of evidence of surrender is not proof of universal retained control. Lack of evidence that a prohibition exists is not identical to evidence that no prohibition exists. Each conclusion needs the corresponding evidence. [S13–S16]

The source distinguishes default nonprohibition from positive legislation. Ending a default state therefore need not be the repeal of an earlier positive command. That case differs from a change in the normative grade of a previously required performance. Both differ from a recognized speaker's intercession: the Barīra example distinguishes a request in the mode of intercession from an obligatory command. Declining the former does not become defiance merely because the speaker could have authority in another mode. This is an interpretation of the inherited argument, not a new transmission authentication.

### 10.5 Relations and valid agreements can specify duties

Ibn Taymiyya's *Qāʿida fī al-maḥabba* 120–132 offers a positive account of normative relations and agreements. Shared benefits, protection against shared harms, justice, truthfulness, and fidelity do substantive work. Kinship and neighborhood can matter without a verbal agreement; other relations arise through the parties' acts. Agreement can specify a performance that was otherwise unowed, provided the undertaking is valid under the applicable norms. [P22; S16]

This gives deliberate commitment a real normative role without requiring that a contract manufacture all of its own validity conditions. A bare request lacks the addressee's corresponding undertaking. An invalid condition does not become valid simply because the parties pronounce it. Equally, prohibitions on illicit conditions cannot mean that every contractual narrowing of a previously open choice is invalid: the same argument recognizes such specification in permitted transactions.

The source also distinguishes cooperation by agreement from obedience to an existing authority, and rightful from wrongful obedience. A pledge can confirm an already existing duty rather than create a temporally new one. Affiliation with a teacher does not confer unconditional authority, and agreement does not by itself create a source-authorized form of worship. These controls retain the account's positive mechanism while limiting its transport.

The wider discussion at 33–39 connects shared normative order, love, submission, and the ultimate object of practical orientation. It offers real reasons for ordered cooperation and for the distinctive divine relation; it is not merely an account of coercion. Yet the need for cooperation does not establish the jurisdiction of every particular directive. Moving from a contractual mechanism to the original's control over another agent requires the actual relevant title or undertaking, not a shared word such as “obligation-making.”

### 10.6 A conditional right need not make its beneficiary a legislator

The self-obligation argument in *Miftāḥ* 2:1135–1143 gives a particularly useful constructive case. The source owns an undertaking concerning its treatment of a qualifying recipient. The recipient's actual response matters to satisfaction of the condition. Fulfillment answers to the source's intrinsic praiseworthiness; it is not a reward that supplies the source with its essential perfection. [P19; S14; S16]

The inference can be stated in full:

1. A particular source-owned undertaking is warranted, including its content and conditions.
2. The recipient actually fulfills the relevant condition.
3. Under that valid undertaking, the qualifying recipient has the stated claim.
4. Satisfying the condition does not make the recipient the author of the undertaking or a superior commander of the source.
5. The actual condition-dependence of the claim therefore does not, by itself, entail external legislation, existential receipt by the source, or acquisition of the source's essential perfection.

The dependence is real. Eliminating it would erase the source's conditional account. Knowing that the recipient responds is not a substitute for the response. But dependence of one particular beneficiary claim and origination of the source's essential character are different explanations.

This argument does not establish a general theory in which every promise creates a temporally new obligation. An utterance may disclose, enact, or specify different aspects of a relation. Nor does the passage settle the number or temporal individuation of intrinsic volitions. The right of a beneficiary under an undertaking is also not the reverse of an unlimited right to command the beneficiary. Undertaking one's own performance and imposing another's performance are different powers.

### 10.7 Fidelity gives a content-sensitive constraint

An independently warranted undertaking can support a more discriminating inference than an appeal to perfection without a specified relation.

Fix its actual beneficiary, domain, duration, conditions, reserved changes, and release terms. Establish that it remains applicable. Suppose a proposed interpretation of a later address attributes to the source precisely the exercise that the continuing undertaking excludes. The earlier commitment is a real practical undertaking, not merely an expression of current preference. Contravening it in those same circumstances would conflict with the fidelity and intrinsic praiseworthiness that warrant its fulfillment. On the affirmative account of the perfect source, both fully specified attributions cannot stand together. [S16]

The conclusion is an obligation to re-examine the conflicting attribution or interpretation. It does not license discarding whichever evidence is inconvenient. One must assess the undertaking's terms and continuing applicability, the later address's authorship, its scope, and its alleged normative force. A request consistent with the recipient's liberty need not conflict with it. A change allowed by a genuine reservation need not conflict either.

This supplies the missing respect-specific premise that a generic perfection shortcut lacked. The earlier faulty shortcut simply assumed that every alleged unauthorized specification was a defect, without independently establishing the relevant title. Here the valid undertaking identifies what would make the particular contrary exercise unfitting. A promise not to exercise a power can therefore constrain conduct even where the power's constitutive extinction is unproved. If a valid relinquishing grant is independently established, it additionally constrains the title itself.

The philosophical conclusion is positive and bounded: actual relations and valid source-owned commitments can add normative structure; their effect depends on their content. They neither require a creature to become the source's legislator nor turn every declaration into a successful imposition of duty.

## 11. Wise ends and actual achieved response

A source can know that a response will occur while the good of that response still requires its occurrence. Foreknowledge and achievement have different explanatory roles. Confusing them can make response-mediated goods appear idle or can mistakenly portray an original source as acquiring its essential perfection from its creatures. [S12]

### 11.1 A determinate end, suitable means, and recognizable completion

The almsgiving test discussed in *Majmūʿ al-fatāwā* 8:436 provides a bounded example. The outwardly proposed transfer is associated with a test of actual approving readiness. The response is realized; the subsequent direction leaves the property with the respondent. The account can identify the end, the means that occasion it, and the achievement that completes that stage.

The philosophical reconstruction is as follows:

1. The end is independently specified as the actual fitting response, rather than defined after the fact as whatever the whole sequence happened to contain.
2. The solicitation provides an occasion for that response.
3. The respondent actually makes the response; its achievement is not identical to a prior representation or knowledge of it.
4. Once that response is achieved, the literal transfer is not still needed to make that same response occur.
5. The subsequent direction can therefore be wise in relation to the completed end, while allowing the respondent to retain the goods.

This is a positive practical explanation. It does not merely say that the source may have an unknown reason. It identifies a recognizable end and a reason why one later step is no longer needed for that end.

The exclusion is exact. Completion defeats the claim that the literal transfer is still required because this already achieved response remains unachieved. It does not show that no other good could ever justify a transfer, that every other end has been completed, or that the entire initial sequence was the only wise one. It also does not derive the legitimacy of every test from the fact that a test can have an end.

### 11.2 Factive knowledge and explanatory roles

One cannot construct a coherent counterfactual by fixing factive knowledge that the response occurs while deleting its occurrence. Factivity already includes the truth of what is known. The legitimate distinction is explanatory: knowledge represents the response, while the response instantiates the good at issue. A knower's understanding that a person will acknowledge a benefit does not itself constitute the person's act of acknowledgment.

The example accordingly does not portray testing as information gathering by an ignorant divine source. Its purpose can concern the actual respondent and the real relation enacted. Nor does using an actual created response as a means or end make that response the donor of the source's original essential excellence. The source's standing character, the fittingness of a particular act, and the actual completion of a relational good are different respects.

### 11.3 Completion without an inappropriate model of defect

A good can be fitting at a particular time without its earlier nonoccurrence being a defect in the original's essential character. The absence of a completed creaturely response before the response occurs need not be an absence of the source's knowledge, will, capacity, or wisdom. The account must identify which bearer, capacity, and respect the alleged lack concerns.

This also distinguishes a succession of actual completed ends from a process that indefinitely postpones every end. Real finite achievements need not become meaningless because further good acts remain possible. Equally, more activity is not automatically greater essential perfection. The positive appraisal concerns fitting activity, appropriate means, and real completion, not a numerical maximization of acts.

Omnipotence does not make all means purposeless. If the end includes a person's actual voluntary response, replacing the person with a simulation or merely representing the outcome changes the end. The argument does not establish that every possible end requires the same means, or that a particular entire history is uniquely necessary. It identifies why one response-mediated good has real conditions.

Finally, successive directives conveyed by a messenger do not by themselves settle how intrinsic divine volitions are individuated. The bounded source unit supports the practical discrimination described above; it does not prove one comprehensive volition or a particular collection of separate volition tokens. The fuller ontology of succession and original agency belongs to its own argument. The earned source-specific appraisals of knowledge, will, wisdom, and efficacy remain in place without being redescribed as conclusions of this one example.

## 12. Objective assessment and its strongest inspected rivals

An argument for objective normativity must engage alternatives at the strength of their actual claims. Rejection of current appetite or local convention does not refute every non-theistic account of objective value. A naturalistic account, in turn, must explain why the relation it identifies supplies the normativity at issue, rather than merely a useful descriptive ranking. The comparison is about reasons and explanatory adequacy, not the moral character of people who accept different theories. [S17–S21]

### 12.1 James: ethical reality and an encompassing order

William James's “The Moral Philosopher and the Moral Life” distinguishes the origin of moral judgments, the meaning of ethical relations, and the ordering of competing demands. His account is not simply a theory of the present evaluator's preference. It treats actual conscious claims as ethically significant, including the claims of weak beings whose demands are easily ignored. Its practical problem is how to seek an inclusive realizable arrangement when the claims conflict. [P8; S19]

The no-sentience example must be read under its actual conditions. James imagines physical facts without conscious life, God, or an interested spectator. He then introduces sentience and restores the possibility of goods and evils. A solitary thinker has no outward obligation to another claimant, but the later discussion allows conflicts and obligations between the same thinker's ideals at different times. A world with two loving souls has real goods, claims, refusals, and obligations even when other beings, including gods, are removed from the example.

Consequently, his no-sentience case cannot support an inference from the absence of God alone to the absence of every ethical reality. The modern theistic source that uses the passage accurately preserves its exclusions in the quotation; the problem lies in extending its conclusion. The correction does not accuse the quotation of being fabricated. It requires an additional argument excluding or disqualifying the finite ethical relations that James explicitly admits. [P13; S17; S19]

James also rejects treating every demand as an all-things-considered requirement that must be satisfied whatever its competitors. Each demand has significance so far as it goes; conflict produces the problem of practical ordering. His direction is toward an inclusive whole with fewer defeated claims. Existing settlements can embody accumulated practical insight, yet suppressed interests and better arrangements can expose their defects. Neither the private philosopher's favorite nor current social equilibrium is simply enthroned as final truth.

This gives his account a constructive relation to correction. An existing arrangement can be judged inadequate because it excludes claims that a better arrangement can accommodate. The corrective force depends on the substantive inclusive criterion. It is not supplied by the fact that a social change succeeds or that the new dominant party approves. The account's own assertions about historical progress and psychological development do not become independently established empirical claims in the present exposition.

James's ending remains strongly theistic. A final stable ordering of all demands, he argues, requires an encompassing divine thinker whose ordering supplies the most inclusive realizable whole. That claim exceeds the motivational suggestion that religious belief can evoke a more strenuous life. It proposes a locus for systematically unified moral truth. It also leaves epistemic limits: even certainty that such a thinker exists would not disclose every concrete judgment to a human philosopher.

The source therefore supports neither “without God there is no ethical reality whatever” nor “James establishes a fully complete secular moral order.” It separates finite ethical relations, practical ordering, religious motivation, final encompassing order, and human access to that order. Whether his demand-based metaphysics supplies the objectivity intended by the stronger Orthemological argument remains a philosophical question. Correct attribution is a condition of that comparison, not a victory for either theory.

### 12.2 Relational naturalism and the powerless sufferer

Railton's *Moral Realism* proposes objective, relational truth conditions: an individual's good is connected with informed self-advice, and moral assessment with impartial consideration of those affected. Such a relation can be objectively instantiated even though its relata include persons, capacities, and interests. Its truth need not depend on what a current observer likes. The proposal appeals to explanatory and corrective roles rather than simply equating rightness with actual desire. It does not claim to provide a desire-independent compliance reason for every conceivable rational agent. [P9; S18]

A powerless sufferer is a useful discrimination case. The sufferer cannot bargain, retaliate, or reward; the inflictor's current preferences remain unchanged. Four questions arise: whether the sufferer is harmed; whether the treatment is wrong; whether the inflictor has a reason to desist independently of contingent ends; and whether a third party can know the answers without identifying their ultimate metaphysical ground.

The relational proposal has a substantive response to the first two. A person's capacities, projects, and conditions of flourishing can be damaged despite the inflictor's indifference or prevailing social approval. Impartial consideration can assess that damage without giving the powerful person's preference decisive weight. This is stronger than moral sentiment as a sociological fact. It does not yet prove that the proposed natural relation is the rightness in question. The normative identification still answers to counterexamples, explanatory adequacy, and the weight given to each person's interests.

The categorical-reason question is a further burden, which this particular account expressly does not fully discharge. That limit matters when the target is a reason binding every rational agent independently of contingent ends. It does not entail that the account has supplied no objective assessment. Equally, retaining the word “ought” cannot confer a stronger categoricity that the account has not established.

Epistemic correction introduces another distinction. A powerless person's interests may have little causal influence on bargaining or social learning. That threatens the reliability of an actual correction mechanism, not the reality of the interests. An adequate moral epistemology must explain how inquiry can become responsive to those interests without using influence as the measure of worth. Survival, stability, consensus, and confidence are evidence only under a relevant truth-sensitive connection. The same demand applies to appeals to innate moral awareness: naturalness or felt certainty alone does not authenticate every judgment or identify its ultimate source.

The remaining naturalistic questions are concrete. Is the idealized self-advice sufficiently determinate? Does idealization preserve the person whose good it is meant to identify? What supports interpersonal comparison and impartiality rather than domination? Does explanatory success support the normative identity or only a neighboring descriptive theory? A reasoned objection should address those questions. Their being unresolved is neither a proof of inconsistency nor an identification of the unique correct theistic alternative.

### 12.3 Why agency is a stronger categorical-reason proposal

An account of categorical reasons must permit criticism of an agent's ends, rather than only of misinformation and inefficient means. Smith's revised ethics of agency attempts this by connecting ideal advice with robust capacities for knowledge-acquisition and desire-realization. Protective concern for agency, together with self-control and a supportive social setting, is proposed as necessary to that robustness. The newer formulation directly assesses capacities; it cannot fairly be treated as merely scoring agents by whichever acts a theory has already labeled right. [P10–P12; S20–S21]

The positive rationale is substantial. A person's present action can damage the capacities needed for later action. Other persons can obstruct, deceive, assist, and sustain that person. Harmony of current projects is fragile if it disappears whenever circumstances change. Concern directed at agency itself is less dependent on one temporary bargain, and self-control can restore the relevant orientation when motivation weakens. A community of such agents offers a serious model of secure finite agency.

This rationale need not be reduced to a cardinal score to have philosophical warrant. Nor is one fortunate exploiter a decisive counterexample: local success says little about robustness under relevant disturbances. The stronger account earns comparative consideration precisely because it addresses temporally extended and interpersonal vulnerability.

The outstanding questions are narrower than a generic is–ought complaint. First, why is the ideal adviser whose advice determines a nonideal subject's reasons identical with the agent who robustly possesses and exercises maximal capacities throughout life? Second, even granting that idealization, why must every qualifying realization contain the specified intrinsic, universally protective concerns?

### 12.4 Fixed-standard correction and the correction of an end

Suppose an agent knows a justified means to an independently warranted end but is disrupted by a competing impulse. Hold fixed the end, the relevant information, and the circumstances. A capacity to detect and correct the disruption improves performance under that fixed standard. This is an intelligible excellence. It explains why true belief, motivation, self-control, and successful action can come apart.

Now remove an intrinsic concern for another person's agency while preserving information and instrumental organization. What would count as repair? If the correct standard follows idealized contingent ends, loss of the concern may change what the agent has reason to do. If concern for others belongs to the correct idealization, the reason remains and the lost concern is a defect. An argument for correction under a fixed standard does not by itself choose between those standards.

The newer direct-capacity benchmark improves this comparison by providing an outcome more independent than theory-relative moral compliance. Nevertheless, moving from good capacity protection to the normative authority of all ideal advice remains substantive. The criticism is not that any use of a normative premise is circular. It is that the proposed identification needs warrant at the very point where it assesses the subject's ends.

Accurate advice can concern the nonideal subject's costs and limitations; it is not advice to imitate the ideal person's behavior regardless of circumstances. Nor must every agential reason be overriding. These features block easy objections based on imitation or absolutism. They do not make the ideal-adviser identification a mere verbal equivalence.

### 12.5 Effective protection and necessary intrinsic concern

The mechanism argument initially permits protective resources within the agent or in the environment. It then proposes intrinsic desires and self-control as a particularly direct explanation of protection, and advances the stronger claim that the capacities cannot be maximally robust without them. The transition from a good mechanism to a necessary mechanism is the precise point at issue.

A successful alternative would need to preserve genuine agency, feasible actions and goals, and the same relevant perturbation range. It would also need comparably specified repair resources. One cannot count failures of a rival's infrastructure as nearby while holding the preferred psychology's support invulnerable without a reason. Conversely, granting the rival an indestructible environment while allowing arbitrary disruptions of the preferred package would be equally misleading.

Several proposed counterexamples fail these conditions.

- A hardwired rule that represents agency as an end, integrates means–end beliefs, and motivates action may already be the relevant desire on a functional account. Renaming it an algorithm supplies no difference in kind.
- A shield that makes troublesome goals impossible can protect agents by shrinking their feasible domain. That does not establish equal capacity under the original comparison.
- Instrumental aid serving a genuine non-agential end is a substantive psychological alternative. It can cease when the means–end relation or intrinsic project changes, so agreement over convenient cases does not establish the required robustness.
- Nonmotivational repair in memory, perception, physiology, or infrastructure can sustain genuine goal-directed agents without itself representing agency as an end. This is a legitimate mechanism class, but no complete member has been shown here to satisfy every intended diachronic and interpersonal requirement.

The last possibility is not foreign to Smith's own treatment: the earlier account allows reflex-like as well as action-based self-control. That preserves the distinction between protection and a particular motivational implementation. It does not supply a ready countermodel to the later complete ideal.

The warranted assessment is consequently neither a demonstrated necessity theorem nor a fully controlled counterexample. There is serious comparative support for protective agency, and an unresolved exclusion of all relevant alternatives. A qualitative argument could close that comparison; demanding an exhaustive numerical simulation would add an unnecessary requirement.

### 12.6 Universal concern and the finite-agent domain

Reliable protection of each person's capacities does not immediately imply that every protector intrinsically desires the capacities of every possible beneficiary. A network can distribute roles; support can overlap; instrumental cooperation and nonagential resources can contribute. These alternatives are not established maximally robust models. Perturbations of roles, partners, and projects may favor universal intrinsic concern. The issue is whether the argument actually establishes that universal requirement under a common, warranted robustness range.

The premise that another person's effort developed or maintains an agent's capacities is also conditional. Where it applies, reliable assistance matters. It cannot by itself exclude every solitary but otherwise suitably equipped agent. Still less can it be transferred unchanged to a proposed unreceived, complete original whose powers are not acquired from helpers. A finite-vulnerability argument needs an independently applicable vulnerability before it can require original peers.

This domain restriction does not refute the finite-agent ethic or establish an amoral original. Nor does it negate an independently warranted affirmative appraisal of original perfection. It identifies the assumptions that would have to travel with the derivation.

### 12.7 Conditional transport to categorical reasons

The positive conditional can be stated without assuming agreement on every personal end.

**Proposition 6 (categoricity under warranted ideal advice).** Fix the relevant circumstances, capacities, and competing considerations. Suppose that:

1. For each admitted variation in the reason-capable subject's contingent preference, an applicable and nonempty class of ideal advisers is independently warranted.
2. Every eligible adviser supports the relevant reason independently of that contingent preference.
3. Correct advice from that ideal standpoint determines a genuine reason for the nonideal subject in those circumstances.
4. The application identifies the reason correctly, retaining the subject's actual costs and limitations.

Then the subject's ceasing to have the contingent preference does not, by that change alone, remove the reason.

**Proof.** Take an admitted preference variation. By the first premise there is an eligible adviser. By the second, the adviser supports the relevant reason under that variation. By the third and fourth, the support determines the correctly applied reason for the actual nonideal subject. The reasoning holds for each admitted variation, so the reason does not depend on the contingent preference within that domain. $`\square`$

The nonemptiness and applicability conditions matter. A merely coherent description of an ideal is not an independently warranted reference. Shared concern also need not recommend the same outward act in different circumstances. The result is a genuine conditional route to categoricity, with its weight carried by the idealization and advice premises. It does not establish that every actual agent will be motivated, or that the resulting reason always defeats every competing one.

Nor does it retroactively add categorical reasons to Railton's different criterion of moral assessment. The two theories propose different identifications. An equivalence argument would be needed to transfer a result from one to the other. Finally, a naturalistic identification of a reason and an ultimate metaphysical explanation of the relevant nature are different questions. Neither successful identification nor incomplete identification settles God's existence by itself.

### 12.8 Moral reasons, personal ends, and sanctions

The revised ethics allows moral reasons to conflict with each other and nonmoral reasons to outweigh moral reasons. Its noninterference principle excludes protection of an exercise that itself constitutes interference. It also distinguishes capable reason-holders from the broader class of patients whose agency can matter. A selfish permissible choice or justified defensive interference is therefore not an immediate counterexample to its conditional pro tanto conclusions.

Some contents and weights remain indeterminate. The proposal allows further reasoned specification and practical coordination, while constraining their inputs through the proposed underlying account. This is more demanding than unconstrained agreement; it is also not a complete first-order code derived by definition. Partial indeterminacy need not eliminate every objective constraint.

Wrongness, permission to sanction, and an obligation to sanction remain separate. The source explicitly treats the authority of particular sanctioning persons and the permissibility of particular sanctions as further questions. Its speculation about radically degraded social conditions preserves some obligations rather than eliminating all of them. These qualifications prevent practical noncompliance, lack of enforcement, or one morally permissible pursuit of personal ends from being used as false refutations of the theory.

They also mark the limit of the categoricity argument. Establishing a reason does not by itself confer a legislative title or a right of coercion. That is the same distinction required by the affirmative account of original authority in §10, reached here from a different theoretical route.

## 13. Wisdom, explanation, and the scope of a judgment

A complete assessment must preserve positive warrant without turning it into immunity from criticism. It must also preserve unanswered questions without treating them as the cancellation of everything already supported. Three distinctions bring the preceding arguments together.

### 13.1 The actuality of a reason and access to it

Confidence in a source's character, understanding of genuine kinds of good, and reasons to expect limited access to particular justifications are different resources. If the first two are independently warranted, ignorance of a particular reason need not defeat the positive account. Requiring exhaustive explanation of every token only from one side would be an unequal standard. Ignorance alone, however, does not establish the actuality of a sufficient reason. The warrant belongs to the positive premises, not merely to a lack of information. [S17]

An absence-of-evidence inference should therefore consider detectability. If a relevant reason were present, how likely is the inquirer to recognize it under the actual conditions? Generic human finitude does not answer every such question; neither does confidence in one local judgment establish exhaustive access. The appropriate assessment is case-sensitive without being arbitrary.

A system-relative explanation of means also presupposes an eligible end. Holding a test-purpose, agency, or a stable order fixed can explain why certain costs arise. It does not by itself prove that every alternative end or order is inadmissible. Conversely, describing a painless alternative does not show that it preserves the same real goods and their conditions. Actual interpersonal participation and a simulation, for example, need not instantiate the same end.

### 13.2 Harm, justification, and ultimate ground

A critic can temporarily grant a hypothesis's commitments to perfect goodness, knowledge, and power and ask what they imply concerning suffering. Such an internal criticism need not first establish a cosmic norm binding every conceivable rational agent. It must still identify the relevant shared normative content and the proper differences of knowledge, standing, circumstances, and action. A human welfare criterion cannot simply be imposed on a different subject without an applicability argument. [S17–S18]

Real harm also differs from gratuitous harm. Establishing the first does not establish that sufficient justification is absent or unlikely. The critic owes that further comparison; the defender's mere statement that an unknown justification may exist is not a complete positive explanation. The strongest account preserves both the reality of the good or harm and the evidential questions about its relation to the claimed wisdom.

Likewise, unredressed wrong and absence of any truth about wrongness are different. An unmet claim to justice can remain real. Final recompense matters if independently warranted, but the fittingness of complete justice does not by itself establish its actuality. Psychological need or consolation is not the missing metaphysical or testimonial premise.

### 13.3 Pure perfection and the correct bearer

A benefit, a larger quantity of activity, or success at a stipulated end is not automatically an unmixed perfection appropriate to an original source. That classification needs argument. Conversely, an account cannot exempt a proposed unrestricted original from comparison merely by stipulating that it is essentially unable to receive the perfection at issue. Essential nonreceptivity and eligibility as an adequate original are distinct assessments; the detailed metaphysical argument belongs in [Received Actuality and Intrinsic Necessity](received-actuality-and-intrinsic-necessity.md).

The normative arguments here retain the source-specific affirmative appraisals on which they depend. They do not infer all perfections from efficient nonreceipt alone, establish a numerical merger of sources, or replace the separate explanation of original agency. A distinction between standing capacity and wise exercise also does not create a newly admitted perfected-source injustice or falsehood. The character appraisal, the respect-specific judgment about a defective act, and the actual conformity of an exercise must be kept distinct.

Similarly, a model of finite agents who require support cannot settle whether an independently original peer is necessary for a particular divine end or whether that peer's contribution would count as foreign completion. Real interpersonal goods can be retained while that additional original-peer application remains outside the present result. It deserves its own source-bound argument.

## 14. The resulting account and its open questions

Correction is more than change when it answers to a warranted distinction between a state and a better one. An operative representation can embody that distinction without creating its truth. A whole token basis cannot supply all the authority that a genuinely radical defeat removes; a wider standard, a revised account of the basis, or a change in the claimed objectivity is required. This local result leaves the ultimate grounding question open to substantive philosophical arguments.

The positive account proceeds by connecting objective evaluability with directed function, intelligent adequacy, fitting ends, and actual source relations. Each connection has an intelligible role. Their different premises prevent an engineering success from becoming a proof of metaphysical authority and prevent a sparse structural countermodel from erasing richer affirmative evidence.

The mathematical results make part of this discipline exact. A profile identifies a target precisely when the target is constant on its fibres. A revision score agrees with a warranted truth-loss exactly when its forest calibration and fundamental cycles satisfy the stated conditions. A finite intervention family identifies its hypotheses precisely when it separates their response vectors; adaptivity has an exact decision-tree recurrence and can strictly reduce worst-case testing. None of these results supplies the independently warranted target or model by itself.

The philosophical treatment gives that normative work real content. Reasons can address an indifferent agent. Intrinsic goodness, directed acknowledgment, and valid undertakings can support practical claims beyond appetite or coercion. Owed allegiance is more than admiration, while its particular specifications remain sensitive to actual title, performance, communication, and truthful disclosure. A source-owned conditional right can depend on a real response without making the recipient a superior legislator or a donor of original essential perfection. Wise achievement can be real even when foreknown.

The strongest inspected rivals sharpen rather than dissolve these questions. James supplies finite ethical reality and a separate argument for final encompassing order. Relational naturalism addresses objective assessment while leaving categorical compliance reasons as a further task. The revised ethics of agency offers a serious positive robustness rationale and a conditional route to categoricity, with the equivalence of ideal advice and robust agency, and the necessity of universal intrinsic protective concern, still requiring the stated comparisons.

The remaining questions are therefore determinate. How should the objective order of evaluability be grounded, and which bridge premises warrant an intellectual or personal explanation? Which accounts fix truth-directed function rather than only regularity or advantage? When does an owed relation confer the particular selection title under discussion, independently of the already sufficient route of warranted normative testimony? Can the strongest account of ideal agency establish the universal implementation claim without narrowing the comparison by definition? How are actual finite inquirers warranted in applying any of these standards under their real capacities and defeaters?

Progress on those questions should change their coherent scholarly treatment. It should not be measured by the number of new accounts of having asked them.
