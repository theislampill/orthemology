## 2. Proof-sensitive memory to constructive proof witnesses

### PT-01: what a canonical residual frontier remembers

For a fixed admitted finite proof catalogue, each proof has support `Sπ` and fixed check price `κπ`; a persistent inventory `K` leaves residual pair `(Sπ\K, κπ)`. Keeping Pareto-minimal pairs gives exactly the future completion-price function, over **all** finite further acquisition sets. Conversely that function determines the frontier, and residualization followed by normalization is a congruence. This is the coarsest quotient for that full menu of possible acquisitions; a restricted action menu can identify more states.

The kernel supplement proves the finite-order statement for a decidable-equality token type and linearly ordered cost type, using `WithTop` to separate infeasibility from finite prices. It does not require finite ambient token space or nonnegative prices for this static theorem. The operational inquiry application does require its own nonnegative-cost and persistence assumptions. The frontier is a sufficient summary of completion cost, not a replacement for a proof object, its authenticity or the act of checking it.

### PT-02: completion and acquisition

In the finite scenario/test model, tests are persistent, one-use, order-independent and remain available. Full-transcript attainability is equivalent to each feasible scenario's total returned inventory containing a goal support. Bellman recursion takes the minimum of an available terminal check price and `test cost + worst feasible successor value`; finite optimal policies are attained. This is worst-path accounting, including random choices when present, not an expected-cost optimization theorem. Testing can be optimal even after a certificate becomes available if it reduces later check cost. Expiring or destructive tokens invalidate the union-of-full-transcript argument.

An all-token goal can have `2^n` inequivalent future residual states despite only two present availability values. This is a finite-controller distinction bound, not a lower bound on every physical machine with a free external archive.

### PT-03–PT-05: ordinary trees, guards, finite minima and executable extraction

The unguarded finite grounded theorem assumes that a judgement identity includes context, type, version and all substitution-relevant restrictions. Repeated ancestor judgements can then be pruned without increasing support or nonnegative occurrence cost, giving branch height at most `|J|` and finite frontier saturation.

Source-sensitive guards change the state. A rule may inspect the **ordered exact child-support tuple**, even nonmonotonically. Pruning only on judgement or on Pareto-dominated support is unsound before those guards have been discharged. The safe state is `(judgement, exact support)`. Along a branch, supports only grow through the finite grammar token union `B`; equal-state pruning gives height `H = |J|(|B|+1)` and a minimum-cost original tree for every attainable exact state. Finiteness of the grammar suffices even when ambient `E` is infinite. Negative costs or infinitely many rules can destroy attainment.

The later Lean layers are genuinely additive:

1. finite guarded attainment supplies a finite catalogue and original-tree witnesses;
2. guarded saturation proves soundness/completeness of the actual numeric recurrence at every bounded height;
3. executable guarded tables refine that recurrence under explicit rule/judgement enumerations, equality tests and decidable guards;
4. constructive witness extraction exhaustively enumerates bounded ordered trees, checks validity/support/cost and returns a tree attaining the computed optimum.

The bounded-height correspondence needs neither global nonnegative costs nor finite judgement space. Those hypotheses enter the global cutoff/attainment theorem. Full **numeric table equality** permits early stopping; equality of keys alone does not, since an existing state's price can fall from 10 to 1. Witnesses need not stabilize with values: later rounds may return a larger or different equally priced tree. No canonical, smallest-node or scalable witness algorithm is claimed. The witness checker establishes validity and price; optimality comes from the separate enumeration/correspondence theorem.

### PT-06–PT-07: shared cost, overlap and a second attainment theorem

Shared DAG cost counts a node once. Choosing a individually cheaper proof of one child can destroy useful overlap with another: the retained example has scalar/tree composition cost 9 and a shared proof cost 5. Globally aligned node identities must agree in rule, ordered references, scope and price and yield an acyclic union. Weighted full incidence vectors suffice to compute the union cost. Scalar individual/pairwise intersection totals do not suffice in general; the three-way parity example has identical proper intersection data but union costs 3 and 4. Full inclusion–exclusion or equivalent higher-order intersection information repairs that loss. Special encodings such as known powers-of-two weights have additional recovery power and are not excluded.

The explicit acyclic empty-support 0/1 shared-DAG budget problem is NP-complete by Set Cover, with polynomial witnesses in that representation. A separate exact-support take/skip chain establishes hardness without sharing. Neither theorem means that ordinary availability queries inherit exact-support hardness, or that an expanded exponential support table is polynomial in the original compact input. Classical shared-AND/OR and Set Cover ancestry is retained; the missing full internal RP manuscript prevents claiming first internal priority.

There were real narrow validation repairs: a binary witness builder initially used an n-ary rule, the inherited structural traversal did not test admitted-rule membership, and an all-unit tree formula lacked its feasibility qualifier. The corrected report, admission wrappers and matching binary witnesses pass the final digest-bound review. The written reductions and original nonbinary witness census were not refuted; the old validation overclaim is not silently retained.

For guarded DAGs, choose the earliest node of each `(judgement, exact support)` and redirect children to their earlier representatives. Ordered supports and guards are preserved by topological induction, root support is exact, cost does not increase, and at most `|J| 2^|E|` nodes remain. A finite whole-graph catalogue therefore attains the optimum. This is a written, reviewed theorem with exact finite controls; it has not become a Lean DAG theorem. The normalizer is not itself an optimizer and need not preserve the root rule or witness identity.

## 4. From proof identity to source-realized inquiry

### PT-19–PT-20: explicit state, explicit randomness

The source-observable tag lower bound is within a fixed coarse fibre and fixed observer contract: `n` distinguishable histories require at least `n` tags; proof terms cannot covertly evade the bound because of typed proof uniqueness. It is not a minimization theorem over arbitrary encodings or a replacement for the external D1 partition computation.

For a finite rational **joint** outcome/next-state kernel, aggregate duplicate replies and take the least common multiple of the reduced denominators. Conditional uniformity of each seed given the full operating history and chosen action suffices for probabilistic transport. Equal per-time seed marginals or separately correct outcome and successor marginals do not supply the joint adaptive-history law.

### PT-21–PT-22: exact stopping and lawful transport

For finite immutable answer labels, finite observation/action alphabets, a fixed time-homogeneous Markov joint kernel, robust legal action menus and always-permitted correct terminal reports:

- sure finite stopping on every supported path is equivalent to the least support attractor; finite branching gives a uniform finite depth;
- almost-sure exact stopping under arbitrary observation-history policies is equivalent to the greatest retained support region in which every actual-state/support pair has a goal path using region-safe actions;
- uniform randomization over those safe actions supplies a support-memory winner. The resulting finite product chain gives a geometric block bound and finite expected stopping time for that constructed controller.

The actual-state coordinate is used in the offline proof, never revealed to the policy. A support graph with arbitrary fixed edge probabilities is insufficient: one-sided revelation can have a visible goal edge but failure probability 1/2. Two-sided revelation can stop almost surely while its all-wait supported branch defeats sure stopping. Limiting posterior concentration never supplies a finite zero-error terminal event by itself.

The review records 2,930 independent finite model instances, 10,202 exact policy matrices and 459 separately checked candidate controllers. Universal arbitrary-policy completeness rests on the written product-chain argument, with qualitative-POMDP ancestry credited. This result removes a named positive-determinism restriction for the narrower exact-stopping objective; it does not subsume general hidden-parity control or solve cost optimization.

Evidence/posterior quotients must preserve the appropriate joint target-and-response law and legal action structure. Posterior ratios can conceal different predictive normalizers and survival probabilities.
