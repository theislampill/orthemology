# Intrinsic wise willing and the regress of determination

9 October 2026 UTC. Bounded source and inference assessment for the continuing T20 research. No integration, closure, protected edit, new formal result, upload, or new source-acquisition credit.

## Result

**The source permits wisdom to explain why an undertaking is fitting without making a separately stated normative ranking the entire determinant of its particular realization. The agent's actual willing is productive, rather than an idle report of that ranking. But the source does not permit particular willing to be an unexplained selector when the whole relevant determining situation is otherwise unchanged.** These positions are compatible because normative justification, actual willing, and the complete particular determining basis are different explananda and explanatory roles.

The proposed regress objection also divides. The authors deny an indefinitely deferred completion of the very agency, or of one particular effect; they permit successive actual acts and achieved ends of an already active original. Shifāʾ's argument against a beginningless history of a creature's volitions expressly depends on that subject having begun to exist. Its denial of a separate special divine will for every created volition does not discard the motives and causes through which the creature acts.

**No live inherited verdict needs revision.** All of these principal doctrines already occur in the inherited readings. This return's narrow executed operation is to test two proposed transports together: from the creature's volition-regress argument to alien origination of every original act, and from permission for successive intrinsic acts to unexplained particular selection. Both fail at identifiable premises. This neither discovers an unread doctrine nor supplies a new positive solution to the still-unspecified complete determination of a particular divine act.

## What actually determines the particular act

The strongest source-faithful reconstruction has four components.

First, the original has intrinsic life, knowledge, power, willing and wisdom. Their reality is not a series of gifts from another producer. Second, an act is directed toward a loved end through apprehended fittingness; known useful outcomes do not by themselves constitute willing. Third, actual particular willing and the relevant conditions complete the particular production. Fourth, the source's own prior acts can matter to those conditions without making a peer the original provider of its agency.

This is substantive, although it is not a complete calculus for every act. Shifāʾ e176–177 rejects reducing will either to absence of coercion or to power plus knowledge of benefit. One can know a benefit and possess power while a conflicting preference prevents undertaking it. E209 makes a known fitting end integral to rational willing. E213 attributes specification to wisdom, will, knowledge and power together. Its immediate argument is partly a parity response to opponents who already accept eternal will with temporally specified objects; that response should not be advertised as an independently sufficient derivation of the complete positive account. The direct positive account of real powers, causes and wise ends at e206 prevents reducing the whole discussion to parity alone.

Miftāḥ 953–955 similarly argues that necessary attachment to what is fitting can be the effect of the subject's own wise choice. Intrinsic determination does not establish external compulsion. But its conclusion is not that merely writing down a selected will supplies the explanation of that will. Majmūʿ L311–312 directly rejects using an unchanged will equally related to times and alternatives as an exception to the demand for a complete determiner. L342's more particular objection is reported dialectic; L312 supplies the unambiguous authorial endorsement of the general requirement.

Three readings of “reasons underdetermine the act” therefore need different verdicts.

1. **The reasons stated by us do not select one outcome.** This is compatible with the sources. A coarse generic end or a partial description can leave out relevant practical and productive conditions. Our ignorance is not an ontological tie.
2. **Normative fittingness permits more than one outcome, while a particular intrinsic willing and its adequate determining basis settle the occurrence.** No inspected source establishes that this must be impossible. Miftāḥ 908 explicitly permits choice among certain equally weighted creaturely options. That supports the distinction between permission and actual determination. It does not establish a divine case with completely equal alternatives, or show what ultimately determines God's choice in such a case. The earlier Darʾ availability example is likewise a genuine particularization account whose transfer to an unconstrained original remains limited.
3. **All relevant particular determining conditions are unchanged and indifferent, but an otherwise unexplained act of choosing makes one option actual.** This conflicts with the stated complete-determiner principle. Calling the occurrence the agent's own exercise does not answer why that particular exercise occurs. Requiring no external producer of the original does not grant that exemption.

The second reading is the strongest open position. It distinguishes a sufficient basis of occurrence from an independently antecedent normative reason that uniquely ranks one feasible outcome. The text does not prove that every complete normative reason-set contains a single best result. Nor does it establish that genuinely complete normative reasons actually tie in a divine case. Those two missing claims cannot be exchanged.

## The regress arguments and their different targets

### A created subject cannot contain a beginningless personal history

At e174 the Jabri presents the dilemma that a soul either originates its will by another will, leading to regress, or without will, which he rejects. At e175 the Sunni respondent does not merely endorse every formulation of that dilemma. He restates the argument and expressly specifies why the relevant endless sequence is impossible here: the servant exists after not existing, so it cannot bear beginningless events.

This is already in the historical notes. The present inference check follows that particular premise. Token novelty can remain true when moving to an everlasting original, while the finite-bearer premise becomes false or unestablished. Therefore the creaturely argument cannot, by itself, show that every new original volition must be donated by an external producer. Such a transfer would need a separate argument that the original's particular act cannot have its sufficient conditions intrinsically.

Conversely, the finite-bearer qualification does not establish that every proposed infinite dependence is harmless. Eternity of the bearer is not a universal answer to regress.

### Completion cannot be perpetually deferred for the same act

Darʾ L0112–0127 provides the important counterweight. Ibn Taymiyya criticizes al-Rāzī's bare eternal-will answer, permits a sequence in which this actual effect follows that actual effect, then expressly rejects two other structures: the very capacity to act never becomes actual without a further unactualized completion, and the completion of one particular occurrence is repeatedly postponed to another completion of that same occurrence. L0126–0127 illustrates this with a supposedly created creative command requiring another simultaneously efficacious created command, without an actual originating basis.

The passage's terminology at L0120–0121 is terse and partly awkward. Its explicit contrast, reinforced at L0135–0139, is sufficient for the limited conclusion: **a series of distinct accomplished acts is not the same structure as an act that never receives its completed conditions.** It is unsafe to paraphrase the allowed structure as “any infinite volition chain is permitted.”

At L0145–0154 the author also refuses a wholly unchanged actor's unexplained successive outcomes. Intrinsic volitions and acts can specify different occasions; their succession answers the proposed inference from an eternal bearer to an eternal particular product. This is a positive source account of permissible intrinsic conditional activity. It remains more abstract than a fully articulated history of the actual selected occasions.

### A separate divine micro-volition is not required for every created motion

Shifāʾ e178 directly rejects the requirement that every creaturely will, breath, water-droplet movement or letter needs its own separately individuated special divine will. General creative willing can establish a genuinely active creaturely nature. Particular guidance adds support and removes impediments; withholding leaves the supplied creature acting through its own inclinations and circumstances.

This is a productive account, not merely a modal permission. Its causal organization has explanatory content. Yet it does not say that a generic standing will, stripped of that organization, is the sufficient explanation of each particular event. It also does not explicitly give an individuation theory for God's intrinsic volitions or establish that none can depend on any earlier intrinsic act. The passage concerns the relationship between divine general willing and many created movements; applying it directly to a divine meta-volition problem requires additional premises.

### Instrumental reasons terminate while fulfilled goods can continue

Shifāʾ e210 and e216 distinguish what is wanted for something else from what is loved for itself. An intrinsically loved end need not gain its value from another, further end. This terminates a regress of merely instrumental justification. The e210 phrase denying the need for another “cause” occurs in this final-cause discussion; it does not revoke the efficient-causal demand on new occurrences.

E211–212 then allows continually achieved ends followed by further achieved ends. The intended good is repeatedly obtained, rather than always postponed. This can be an endless future sequence; the text also contests the prohibition of past succession. It does not turn every desired end into a means whose value depends on an unattained final event. That difference gives the reply its substantive force.

Together with e207, this blocks the inference from achieving a loved end at its fitting time to receiving perfection from another. The original's wisdom is intrinsic, and its own provision can realize its intended result. The e207 universal-source reply invokes the theological claim that no other creator exists; that part is not an independent proof of that claim or of every proposed original's comprehensive provision.

## The strongest rival and the remaining comparison

A serious rival can accept purposive, knowledgeable agency and intrinsic ownership while denying that its sufficient exercise requires a necessitating differentiator for every particular preference. It can say that the agent acts for an adequate but nonunique reason and directly brings about this choice; the reason explains the intentional character, the agent's exercise explains its actual occurrence, and no further prior act of choosing to choose is required.

This is stronger than a blank choice coordinate. It distinguishes rational justification from production and does not inherently invoke an external donor or a featureless subject. But precisely where it makes the particular exercise ultimate without a further adequate differentiating basis, it disputes the source's complete-determiner demand. It cannot claim to preserve that demand merely because including the already occurred exercise in the antecedent makes the chosen result follow.

The source's strongest reply also must not be overstated. It offers real reasons against treating a completely unchanged indifferent basis as explaining a new specification, a substantive account of informed purposive activity, and an explicit alternative to a vicious regress: intrinsic conditional succession with accomplished effects. It does **not** exhibit all the particular practical and causal grounds of the actual divine history. E212 expressly limits human knowledge of wisdom, and e214 expressly calls a concession to some unpurposed acts dialectical before reaffirming wise ends for all acts.

Accordingly, the correct assessment has two levels. At the structural level the account positively explains how intrinsic willing, fitting reasons, operative conditions and achieved ends can play different roles without alien completion. At the level of why this complete particular divine history rather than each alternative, the inspected sources affirm an adequate wise determining basis whose detail they do not supply. Ignorance of that detail is not evidence that it is absent. It equally is not a new source-based demonstration of one specific proposed determination.

## What changes and what survives

The executed test rejects a specific overextension of e175: novelty of a volition does not preserve the finite-bearer premise needed in that creaturely regress argument. It also rejects an overextension of e178 and of allowed succession: neither gives arbitrary particular will an exemption from explanation. The same-effect completion control in Darʾ prevents the correction becoming an unrestricted infinite-regress permission.

These conclusions reinforce rather than revise the present productive-sufficiency and particular-agency judgements. The practical-authority account likewise remains separate: a rightful directive can specify a recipient's duty without an antecedently unique best permitted option, but that normative specification is not an explanation of the directing original's own volition. No authority argument fills the present causal gap.

The current original-bearer **Completion** premise remains an actual unreceived relevant resource. It does not mean deterministic sufficiency, exhaustive original production, or unique wise selection. No change to that interface is warranted.

The precise unresolved question is whether an intrinsic act's complete causal determination can be independently warranted while the fully articulated normative reasons leave more than one fitting possibility, and what supplies that particular determination at original scope. These sources neither prohibit that combination merely through the absence of a unique optimum nor substantiate a primitive tie-breaking divine act. A useful further advance would require an actual argument at that relation, not another table labelled agent causation, a repeat of the already read regress distinctions, or a claim of metaphysical possibility from formal consistency.
