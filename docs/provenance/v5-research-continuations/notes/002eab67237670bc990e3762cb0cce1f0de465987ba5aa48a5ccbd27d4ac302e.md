# Finite-information cost of unknown-standard repair

Status: candidate ordinary mathematical theorem and integrated crosswalk. Not a historical novelty or kernel-check claim. Source geometry is independently developed in `source-bridges/LOCAL_REPAIR_RIGIDITY.md`.

## Definitions

Let V={(0,1),(1,0)} and L_alpha(x,v)=alpha(x1-v1)^2+(1-alpha)(x2-v2)^2. The unknown but fixed supplied standard is alpha in (0,1). For 0<epsilon<=1/4, the disturbed credence is b_epsilon=(1/2+epsilon,1/2). A coherent repair has form c=(q,1-q). Success means L_alpha(c,v)<=L_alpha(b_epsilon,v) for BOTH v in V. A repair may randomise using parameter-independent private randomness. Only genuine fresh probe outcomes carry unknown-standard information; a copied or transformed old outcome is not a fresh outcome.

## G1. Repair decodes the standard

Define r_alpha=1/2+alpha epsilon and D_alpha=alpha(1-alpha)epsilon^2. Completing the square gives

L_alpha(b_epsilon,v_i)=(r_alpha-i)^2+D_alpha,
L_alpha(c,v_i)=(q-i)^2.

If q>=r_alpha, success at i=0 entails 2 r_alpha(q-r_alpha)+(q-r_alpha)^2<=D_alpha. If q<r_alpha, success at i=1 entails 2(1-r_alpha)(r_alpha-q)+(r_alpha-q)^2<=D_alpha. Because r_alpha in [1/2,3/4], both linear coefficients are >=1/2. Hence |q-r_alpha|<=2D_alpha<=epsilon^2/2, and decoded alpha_hat=(q-1/2)/epsilon obeys |alpha_hat-alpha|<=epsilon/2.

Consequently, two standards separated by more than epsilon have DISJOINT successful-repair sets. This is a necessary estimate tolerance; it is not sufficient on its own.

## I1. Exact two-standard lower bound with an N-sample budget

Suppose each fresh probe gives an independent Bernoulli(alpha) outcome. At most N fresh probes are available before the repair; their times may be adaptive. All remaining input and private randomisation are parameter-independent. Put alpha_minus=1/2-epsilon and alpha_plus=1/2+epsilon. Both lie in [1/4,3/4]. Their success sets are disjoint by G1.

Any repair algorithm defines a test: decide minus when q lies in the minus success set, plus otherwise. Its minus error equals minus repair failure; its plus error is at most plus repair failure. Therefore maximum repair-failure probability is at least (1-TV(P_minus,P_plus))/2, where the transcript includes all the algorithm's actual information.

Pre-generate N independent Bernoulli outcomes and independent private randomness. Every adaptive at-most-N transcript and output is a parameter-independent measurable postprocessing of these variables, so TV contracts. The Hellinger affinity of the two N-sample product laws is

F_N=(sqrt(1-4epsilon^2))^N.

Cauchy-Schwarz gives TV<=sqrt(1-F_N^2). Thus the following exact expression is a universal lower bound:

max_failure >= [1-sqrt(1-(1-4epsilon^2)^N)]/2
             >= (1-4epsilon^2)^N/4.

Using -log(1-x)<=x/(1-x) and 4epsilon^2<=1/4 gives

max_failure >= (1/4) exp(-(16/3) N epsilon^2).

In particular, a uniform guarantee of failure at most delta<1/4 over alpha in [1/4,3/4] needs

N >= [3/(16epsilon^2)] log(1/(4delta)).

This is a lower bound in a declared observation model, not a universal cognitive sample complexity law. The two alternatives depend on epsilon: it is LOCAL-MINIMAX, not a pointwise rate assertion for every fixed alpha. The exact optimal testing risk can be checked using rational binomial probabilities without a floating-point solver.

## I2. Provenance matters to the sample count

If k copied records are measurable functions of N original fresh probe outcomes and independent randomness, they remain within the same postprocessing experiment. I1 counts N, not k. Additional genuinely conditionally informative observations require their actual conditional observation model. A provenance label alone does not establish independence or authenticity.

## P1. A positive eventual-correct-repair construction

Fix 0<a<1/2, alpha in [a,1-a], and set A=a(1-a), c=A/4. Estimate alpha using n independent Bernoulli(alpha) observations; clip the sample mean to [a,1-a], obtaining alpha_tilde. Return q=1/2+epsilon alpha_tilde.

On |alpha_tilde-alpha|<=c epsilon, put d=q-r_alpha. Then |d|<=c epsilon^2. For either world, the improvement in loss is at least

D_alpha -2|d|-d^2
 >= epsilon^2 [A-2c-c^2 epsilon^2]
 >= epsilon^2 [A/2-A^2/256] >0.

Clipping cannot increase estimation error for alpha in [a,1-a]. Hoeffding yields failure probability at most 2 exp(-2 n c^2 epsilon^2). Thus

n >= log(2/delta)/(2 c^2 epsilon^2)

suffices for strict improvement at both worlds with probability at least 1-delta.

For a deterministic disturbance sequence epsilon_t in (0,1/4] and summable delta_t>0, use the maximum of the preceding required sample budgets through t. If those samples can be supplied through licensed, target-preserving actions, the first Borel-Cantelli lemma gives almost-sure eventual strict improvement at every issued repair opportunity. The target here is correctness of the issued action, not uninterrupted coherence of a physical state between opportunities. If each recurring disturbance itself counts as a bad state, infinitely many such disturbances already defeat physical co-Buchi persistence. Whether sampling and interstage latency are target-preserving must be part of the supplied evaluation contract. Reused growing samples create dependence between failure events but do not invalidate the summable union/BC argument. The sample-availability premise is essential: this result does not make harmful exploration disappear or grant access to observations.
