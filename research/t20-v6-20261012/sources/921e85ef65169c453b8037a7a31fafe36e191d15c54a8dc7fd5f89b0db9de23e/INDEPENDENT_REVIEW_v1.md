# Kind priority: a narrower target, a distinct exclusion question

Independent internal philosophical review, 3 October 2026 UTC. This review concerns the developing Tenth kind-priority investigation. It is separate from the reviewed null-preservation paper. It is not external specialist validation, a new cosmology, or programme closure. No code or canonical materials were changed.

## Result first

**The smaller existential target earns a real strategic gain. The common-basis test establishes a precise inference limit. Neither establishes an actual necessary participant yet.**

The gain is that possible production of one suitably classified contingent witness can answer the declared modal question; there is no need to reproduce every actual individual, exact multiplicity, or whole relational inventory. The limit is that a cause's kind membership can be prior to its exercise and can also ground existential nonemptiness without existential nonemptiness itself being prior to that exercise. Producing another witness therefore does not create a cycle just by existential generalization.

This does not make the proposed endogenous explanation adequate. A further, independently intelligible consideration favours the affirmative: propagation that starts with a kind already instantiated does not by itself account for that kind's existential availability. Section 4 endorses a bounded, rationally warranted relevance judgement for an explicit existential-supply question. It does not require a second derivation before a basic rational judgement can count. The outstanding issue is whether that reason excludes every explanation adequate to the precise existential question, including joint explanations, at the same explanation relation the transfer promises.

The investigation is worth retaining as a target-abstraction and priority diagnosis. Report neither a refutation of the cosmological route nor a new necessary-being result.

## 2. The gain is real and exactly delimited

Let Q be a fixed family of classifications selected by reference to the actual situation. Let F(x) abbreviate that x contingently exists and instantiates at least one classification in Q. Let p mean that there is an F.

Compare two possible-explanation tasks:

1. Produce witnesses meeting every entry, count and relevant relation of a complete actual inventory.
2. Produce an F-witness.

An adequate witness to the first may supply a witness to the second, but the second does not require the first. In particular, failure to preserve exact infinite counts or all actual individual origins does not defeat a possibility witness deliberately offered only for p. The target is not an illicit retreat if p is declared at the start and the conclusion stays at its scope.

Fixing Q matters. A possible outside producer must be outside the fixed target classification or otherwise exempt from F for a reason independently stated. Selecting a new Q in the possible case changes the proposition. Nor does actual coverage imply coverage at every possible world. Keeping that distinction allows a contingent possible outsider without presupposing a necessary producer.

The improvement is a smaller modal task, not an independently established possible outsider. It also comes with a structural cost: p does not identify or exhaust its witnesses. What made full-inventory self-production look immediate cannot be imported unchanged into p.

No appeal to kind realism is needed merely to form F. Conversely, forming the disjunctive predicate F does not make it an explanatory nature. In particular, the property of being contingent must not silently become a natural kind. Actual coverage, origin-neutral classification and kind-to-power priority are distinct commitments. A finite actual population secures finite collection bookkeeping; that alone does not establish the other commitments.

In particular, the separate condition (52)(a) at P-KIND is not discharged by the single-instance modal reduction or by substituting general descriptions for individual names. It remains a separate application premise. The review does not universalize Q's actual coverage to possible outsiders or collapse this condition into causal participation.

## 3. The common-basis fork is not a dependency cycle

Use four facts:

- b: source s instantiates a relevant kind
- a: s exercises the relevant productive power
- c: a different recipient t instantiates a relevant kind
- p: at least one relevant kind is contingently instantiated

Grant all of the following for this test: b is explanatorily prior to a; a genuinely produces c; b and c each independently supply a truthmaking witness for p. Then a possible ordering is:

    b → a → c → p
    b ─────────→ p

The arrow here marks the admitted direction of explanatory dependence or witness-grounding for the test; it does not identify grounding with efficient causation. Even if one provisionally treats the transitive closure as a single strict precedence relation, the structure is acyclic. No edge p → a follows. Adding the direct b → p edge does not reverse a route from b through a and c.

Consequently, two different moves require distinction:

- Sound local restriction: a does not explain b by producing its own prior basis.
- Further restriction: a cannot adequately explain p through producing c.

The first does not entail the second. This result arises with one productive source and one further witness; it does not depend on an infinite regress. It is also stronger than merely saying that an alternative can be described: it identifies an invalid inference from the specific premises offered.

One proposed repair is priority under existential abstraction: whenever a prior productive basis directly witnesses p, p itself is prior to the activity. That is an additional metaphysical principle. It is not ordinary transitivity. Another repair is to deny the adequacy of the a-to-c-to-p explanatory route even without asserting p's priority to a. The second may be philosophically preferable because it makes the exact target-sensitive relevance judgement explicit.

The fork does not establish that a explains p. That would need an explanation-preserving move from producing c to explaining its existential generalization. Simple logical entailment is insufficient. Both sides therefore face a precise question: does producing a further witness explain this nonemptiness fact, or only the produced instance?

## 4. The affirmative reason has independent content

Consider a copying mechanism whose operation requires an existing template of the very type under discussion. Its operation really produces another template. There remains an intelligible difference between explaining that additional token and accounting for the availability of templates on which the operation depends. The latter is not achieved merely by repeating the copying operation across more tokens.

The reason is not temporal firstness. A timeless or beginningless account can have the same dependence of each exercise on already-instantiated productive character. It is not modal necessitation either: an independent producer could explain a contingent instance indeterministically. Nor does it demand that every fact about the producer be explained. A contingent outside producer can meet the limited target without an ultimate account of its own existence.

This supports a target-relative **antecedent-witness nonpresupposition** norm: an account of existential availability cannot discharge that very availability solely through productive activity whose antecedent resources already instantiate the target field.

The strongest version concerns dependence of the productive channel, not merely redundant truthmakers. Its output is obtainable only by using a target-instance already present in its antecedent productive resources. Two independently sufficient causes of another event need not have this structure. The availability supplied by the second cause is not thereby an input into its own productive channel. This difference gives the existential objection more content than the observation that p would hold anyway.

I endorse the bounded judgement: **an activity whose productive availability depends on an antecedent F-witness does not, solely through its output, supply the availability of F-instantiation on which it operates.** For the explicitly stated supply question, this is a rationally warranted assessment of explanatory relevance, not just a coherent stipulation. The appraisal rests on the input/output dependence just identified; it does not assume that p grounds the activity. It also does not certify the wider claim that every explanation of the proposition p must answer that supply question, or that every joint internal account has the relevant antecedent dependence.

The norm is not merely a disguised necessary-being conclusion. It applies to finite, mundane target classes, permits contingent outsiders and plural contributors, and leaves the outsider's own existential explanation open. Its positive motivation is the difference between supplying existential availability and using it. One can rationally appreciate that distinction without a neutral decision algorithm or a theorem ruling out every rival.

Its limitation is equally exact. The norm is compelling for an answer promising nonpresupposing existential supply. Whether the same requirement belongs to every adequate, ordinary explanation of the proposition p remains the issue. An answer accounting for a produced witness may be a genuine, limited reason why p is true. The affirmative needs to show why that answer misses the particular explanatory demand, rather than merely showing it leaves other witnesses or a deeper history unexplained.

Thus there is an undercutting result against deriving target-level exclusion from the fork alone. There is no rebutting defeater of an independently warranted nonpresupposition judgement. The active rival's bare description supplies neither.

## 5. Four explanation distinctions must remain independent

The review does not treat the following contrasts as interchangeable:

1. Adequate explanation of the stated target versus a merely contributory consideration.
2. Proximate explanation versus an ultimate or fully historical account.
3. Nonnecessitating explanation versus a logically sufficient determinant.
4. Noncontrastive explanation versus an answer selecting one outcome over a specified alternative.

An explanation can be adequate, proximate, indeterministic and noncontrastive at once. A target-sensitive nonpresupposition requirement therefore need not be the whole-origin E* rejected as a silent strengthening in Ninth. Equally, the mere phrase existential nonemptiness does not establish that the transferred relation E contains the requirement.

Several shortcuts fail:

- Factivity is not vicious circularity. A true report that an agent produced an F entails p; this does not invalidate ordinary productive explanation.
- An alternative witness's availability is not automatically a defeater of explanatory relevance. Overdetermination prevents a general inference from p's holding without one contribution to that contribution's explanatory uselessness.
- Conversely, genuine production of t does not automatically explain every existential abstraction satisfied by t.
- A conceptual or grounding answer cannot be excluded merely because the proposition is existential. Its adequacy and any demand for eventual causal participation must be defended at the actual target.

For this task, the causal-participation premise is a substantive restriction on what counts as an adequate answer. The central source location is P-KIND; relevant source controls are P-CONCEPT and A-EKIND. Those locators should be used rather than turning this observation into additional historical exposition. Their existence prevents treating the restriction as an analytic truth about the word explanation.

There is an exact conditional check here. If the actual witness-ground b → p already qualifies as an adequate E-explanation, then p already has an E-explanation before the possible-to-actual transfer is applied. That particular transfer application adds no productive participant. To obtain productive participation, the argument must exclude the conceptual answer at this target or require that answer to extend into an appropriate productive account. This does not make the restricted principle globally redundant: other applications may matter. It identifies why existential abstraction can make the selected application too weak if E is very permissive. Nor does the conditional certify all witness-based grounds as explanations; precisely that target-relative adequacy must be assessed.

## 6. Joint activity and the weaker necessary-participant conclusion

The scalar target avoids an all-instance inventory lift. It does not license the general inference that no member explains p, therefore no collection explains p. Cooperative powers routinely have effects that no member can produce alone.

For the proposed exclusion, the account must instead warrant that the relevant joint productive activity still starts from target-instantiating antecedent resources, and that this excludes an adequate E-explanation of p. Kind priority relative to each member's own exercise is not automatically priority relative to an entire joint history.

A finite mixed control makes the point without infinitism. Let outside producer n create s, and let s then create t. The kind fact about s precedes s's exercise but is produced within the combined history. It is not an antecedent condition of the whole history merely because it is antecedent to one stage. The history can explain a target first instantiated by s while including s's later genuine contribution. This is an inference control, not a claim that gratuitous detail always improves an explanation.

For the desired intermediate conclusion, a weaker norm suffices: **no wholly internal productive account adequately explains p.** If actual causal participation has been established, the explanatory participants are actual substances, and every actual contingent participant is covered by F, that norm excludes the case in which all participants are contingent. At least one actual participant must therefore be necessary.

That inference is valid at its stated premises. It does not infer necessity from a necessarily occupied role or from the necessity of p. It identifies a member of the actual explanatory participants. No common-bearer unity, intrinsic explanation of the necessary member, N2/N3 or practical perfection is required to credit it.

The weaker norm does not show that every participant in an adequate explanation must be necessary. Exclusion of all wholly internal explanations and elimination of every internal contribution from a mixed explanation are different claims. Retain the necessary-participant target and do not burden its assessment with the stronger purification claim.

## 7. Disposition and the genuinely discriminating next step

Retain these positive results now:

- The fixed-kind existential target reduces the modal production burden while preserving a possible route to an actual necessary participant.
- A bounded, rationally warranted target-relative judgement distinguishes existential supply from propagation dependent on an antecedent target-instance. Its wider application remains open.
- The common-basis test locates a definite non-entailment before any appeal to infinity.
- A properly warranted no-wholly-internal-account norm would suffice for participant extraction; all-necessary explanatory membership and later unity are unnecessary.

Retain these limits without downgrading the positive reasons:

- The current record does not establish the exclusion norm for the precise ordinary E used in the transfer.
- No adequate wholly internal E-explanation has been established either.
- Actual kind coverage and a possible outside witness are not supplied by class notation alone.
- Necessary-participant actuality remains unestablished; this is not a verdict that such belief is irrational or unsupported by any reason.

The next inquiry should therefore present and assess one strongest target-sensitive judgement: **why an account that genuinely produces an additional instance nevertheless fails to explain p's nonemptiness at the already declared E.** It should confront witness-based grounding and joint activity directly, allowing a basic rational justification if that is the proposed warrant. Repeating no-self-causation, requiring a complete history by default, or demanding an impossible rival before crediting the positive reason would each miss the present result.

This is a precise relocation of the live foundational issue, with a smaller possibility burden and a distinct existential-priority test. It does not reopen the separate null-preservation paper's accepted technical findings and does not close the overall programme.
