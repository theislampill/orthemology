# Informed election and the scope of a response-selection title

11 October 2026 UTC. Bounded source test. No protected edits, integration, historical authentication, present duty adjudication, or closure.

## Finding

**Shifāʾ e33 positively argues for commitment to the actual command despite aversion.** Its practical conclusion is not merely reduced confidence in alternatives. Where authenticated source-owned disclosure establishes an in-scope obligatory requirement, that requirement is warranted without a second independent constitutive-title derivation. The already earned owed obedience and practical allegiance are not reduced to optional reliance.

The proposed Qurʾān 28:68 route does not supply an additional constitutive selection-title derivation for the separate explanatory comparison that deliberately leaves this entitlement unsettled. Its strongest inspected Ibn al-Qayyim rationale combines original provision with knowledgeable election, but its discriminating object is the fitness of those elected, particularly for prophetic commission. Transferring that rationale to control of an otherwise adequate form of an owed response requires another premise. This is a precise local obstruction, not a denial of original rightful authority or a demand for universal jurisdiction.

Within that separate comparison, a narrower epistemic application is also available: **a recipient's unexplained dislike or apparent equivalence of alternatives does not establish their actual equivalence when the relevant selection is independently warranted as informed and wise.** Conversely, that epistemic limitation does not prove that the selected option alone is required. The present application cannot turn a possible unrecognized difference into an established necessary difference.

## Primary source units

In the site's attributed extract of *Zād al-maʿād*, ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm 1:13–17, Ibn al-Qayyim distinguishes election among created things from the more general willing of their creation. He defends the negative reading of the verse's final clause through six arguments. The contextual comparisons concern appointment of messengers and distribution of favor according to knowledge of fitness; the concluding argument connects election with wisdom. This is not bare arbitrary power. The modern webpage supplies the edition locator; no print scan or apparatus was collated. [Attributed primary extract](https://www.ibnalqayem.net/articles/774).

The corresponding *Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl* discussion, received electronic e31–34, distinguishes the same election from general will. Qurʾān 33:36 occurs in its lexical illustration. The subsequent subsection explicitly extends informed selection to legislation and urges commitment to the command despite aversion, grounding this in benefits known to the source but not the recipient. The following supplication discussion separates knowledge, ability, facilitation, continuing blessing and contentment. These are the author's endorsed arguments, apart from expressly attributed quotations. No new authenticity judgment about the transmitted reports is made. [Received primary transcription](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/shfa-alalil-001.html).

## Strongest reconstruction and discriminating test

The candidate can be reconstructed without reducing original authority to force:

1. The actual original supplies the creatures and the relevant benefaction.
2. The original knows their capacities and the relevant ends and consequences.
3. Its election therefore answers a real, informed suitability rather than the challenger's uninformed preference.
4. The challenger cannot replace that election merely by proposing its own favored recipient.

This supports source-owned wise appointment and rebuts an unsupported assumption that worldly prestige establishes superior fitness. But the separate constitutive explanation being tested has a different object: two performances by the same recipient, each otherwise adequate to the already owed relation, with the disputed selection entitlement deliberately left unsettled. This comparison is not a further warrant requirement imposed on an already authenticated obligation. Step 4 cannot be transported simply by substituting “response form” for “commissioned recipient.” The text's reason for discrimination is not an established power to make either equally adequate discharge uniquely due at will.

Two readings of the residual must therefore remain separate:

- **Merely apparent adequacy:** the recipient sees no relevant difference. Informed wise guidance can defeat confidence in that assessment, conditional on its actual attribution and normative mode. Where the guidance is established as an in-scope obligatory requirement, obedience is owed despite the recipient's incomplete understanding; where its mode is weaker, informed selection alone does not upgrade that mode.
- **Genuinely adequate alternative:** the comparison holds fixed adequacy apart from the disputed specification. Possible undisclosed benefit does not refute this comparison. Greater benefit alone also does not establish obligatory grade, as the inherited obligatory/recommended distinction already shows.

The second is an explanatory comparison under unsettled entitlement, never a missing obligation-warrant once actual authenticated in-scope obligatory disclosure is established. The first identifies an epistemic limitation without proving that every alternative is secretly defective. The source-faithful interpretation preserves the passage's positive commitment to actual command: inaccessible wisdom does not license refusal. Its practical argument need not independently derive the title presupposed in that guidance.
