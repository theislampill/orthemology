# Optimal finite-horizon growth under Beta uncertainty

Status: fresh ordinary theorem, pending independent review. This is a companion to BETA_PRIOR_BUDGET_BOUNDARY.md, not a replacement for the recovered third-tranche empirical learner. No kernel proof, compiled optimal controller or historical priority is claimed.

## Contract and result

Use exactly the companion's contract: fixed 0<epsilon<=1/4; one alpha drawn from Beta(a,b), a,b>0, then held fixed; passive iid Bernoulli(alpha) receipts; a common independent seed; one coherent always-issued report after exactly n receipts. Literal failure is positivity of either inherited excess loss. Index n=0 is the initial report and n>=1 is post-acquisition.

Write p_n(pi) for the prior-averaged probability of failure at index n, and V_N for the infimum over policies of expected failures at indices 1,...,N. Put H_N(s)=sum_{n=1}^N n^(-s). Constants below may depend on a,b,epsilon but never on n,N or the policy.

There are finite strictly positive constants c,C such that, for every n>=1,

  every policy pi has p_n(pi) >= c [n^(-a)+n^(-b)],
  the retained empirical learner has p_n(emp) <= C [n^(-a)+n^(-b)].

Consequently

  c [H_N(a)+H_N(b)] <= V_N <= C [H_N(a)+H_N(b)].

The same bounds hold for the retained empirical learner's cumulative cost, so it attains the optimal order without knowing the prior shapes. This is order optimality for this passive statistical decision problem, not exact optimality of that learner or a statement about leading constants.

Let m=min(a,b). The optimal cumulative growth is therefore:

- Theta(N^(1-m)) if m<1;
- Theta(log(N+1)) if m=1;
- Theta(1), with a finite lifetime budget, if m>1.

When a,b>1 the analogous optimal expected future count over indices n>N has order N^(1-a)+N^(1-b). No such finite tail budget exists when either shape is <=1. The initial report adds at most one and does not change these growth classifications.

## 1. Retained empirical repair radius, checked directly

The empirical report is q_n=1/2+epsilon h_n, where h_n is the empirical mean. At h=alpha both excesses equal -epsilon^2 v, where v=alpha(1-alpha). For h in[0,1], q_h and q_alpha are in[1/2,3/4]. Thus

  |D0(h)-D0(alpha)| = epsilon |h-alpha| (q_h+q_alpha)
                        <= (3/2) epsilon |h-alpha|,
  |D1(h)-D1(alpha)| = epsilon |h-alpha| (2-q_h-q_alpha)
                        <= epsilon |h-alpha|.

If |h-alpha|<=r=v epsilon/4, both excesses remain strictly negative for interior alpha. Therefore empirical failure implies |h_n-alpha|>r. This is the same conservative radius already used in the third tranche; this calculation supplies a short independent check of that inherited hypothesis.

## 2. A self-contained concentration bound

For a centred Bernoulli draw X=Y-alpha, E X=0, |X|<=1 and E X^2=v. Hence E|X|^k<=v for every integer k>=2. Since k!>=2*3^(k-2), for 0<=lambda<3,

  E exp(lambda X)
    <= 1 + v sum_{k>=2} lambda^k/k!
    <= 1 + v lambda^2/[2(1-lambda/3)]
    <= exp(v lambda^2/[2(1-lambda/3)]).

The same holds with -X. Independence, exponential Markov inequality and the admissible choice lambda=r/(v+r/3)<3 give

  P(|h_n-alpha|>=r) <= 2 exp(-n r^2/[2(v+r/3)]).

With r=v epsilon/4, the exponent's coefficient is

  r^2/[2(v+r/3)] = v epsilon^2/[32(1+epsilon/12)]
                         >= v epsilon^2/33,

because epsilon<=1/4. Consequently

  p_n(emp | alpha) <= 2 exp(-n alpha(1-alpha) epsilon^2/33).

The bound may exceed one for small n; that is harmless. It is ordinary mathematics, not an inference from a finite simulation. It is a standard bounded-variable concentration argument proved here in full, not claimed as novel.

## 3. Integrating the upper bound

Set t=epsilon^2/66 and K(s)=max(1,2^(1-s)). On alpha<=1/2, alpha(1-alpha)>=alpha/2 and (1-alpha)^(b-1)<=K(b). On alpha>=1/2 use beta=1-alpha symmetrically. Extending each nonnegative integral from[0,1/2] to[0,infinity) gives

  p_n(emp) <= [2/B(a,b)] *
       [K(b) Gamma(a)/(t n)^a + K(a) Gamma(b)/(t n)^b].

This is the asserted upper bound with an explicit finite constant. No endpoint correctness assumption enters the integration, and the prior has no endpoint atoms.

## 4. Integrating the universal lower bound

The companion theorem proves, for every policy and n>=1,

  p_n(pi) >= R0^(-a) M0(n) + R1^(-b) M1(n),
  M0(n)=E(1-alpha)^n, M1(n)=E alpha^n,
  R0=(1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon),
  R1=(1+2epsilon)(1-epsilon)/[(1-2epsilon)(1+epsilon)].

This follows from the all-zero and all-one posterior events, which are disjoint when n>=1. It covers arbitrary measurable real reports and independent randomisation.

Let k(s)=min(1,2^(1-s)). Restrict the integral for M0 to 0<=alpha<=1/(n+1). On this interval (1-alpha)^(b-1)>=k(b) and

  (1-alpha)^n >= [n/(n+1)]^n >= exp(-1),

the last inequality following from log(1+1/n)<=1/n. Therefore

  M0(n) >= exp(-1) k(b)/[a B(a,b)] * (n+1)^(-a)
         >= exp(-1) k(b) 2^(-a)/[a B(a,b)] * n^(-a).

Symmetrically

  M1(n) >= exp(-1) k(a) 2^(-b)/[b B(a,b)] * n^(-b).

Both coefficients are strictly positive. Taking the smaller after multiplying by R0^(-a),R1^(-b) proves the asserted universal lower bound. It requires no asymptotic exchange or gamma-ratio approximation.

Summation proves the finite-horizon claims. The elementary integral test proves the stated powers, logarithm and summable-tail orders. Every lower bound holds for every policy, including horizon-dependent policies, while one horizon-independent empirical policy supplies all upper bounds.

## 5. One exact Bayes policy exists for all horizons

The passive sampling contract also permits an ordinary decision-theoretic existence statement. After n receipts containing k ones, the posterior is Beta(a+k,b+n-k). For a candidate report q in[1/2,1/2+epsilon], its acceptance interval in alpha is

  l(q)=(q^2-1/4)/[epsilon(1+epsilon)],
  u(q)=[1/4-(1-q)^2]/[epsilon(1-epsilon)].

Both endpoints lie in[0,1] and l<=u. Its conditional success probability is the continuous function F(u(q))-F(l(q)), where F is the posterior CDF. A maximum exists on the compact report interval. Select the least maximiser; it exists because the maximiser set is nonempty compact. Outputs outside the interval never improve success. Randomisation cannot exceed its maximum.

There are only countably many finite receipt histories, so this deterministic selection is measurable without any parameter-dependent seed. Reporting that maximiser after each history simultaneously minimises every per-step Bayes failure probability. Since reports do not affect future receipts, the same policy minimises every finite-horizon cumulative count and the extended lifetime count, by conditional expectation and Tonelli. It likewise minimises any prescribed future tail of the count.

This is an existence characterisation via a one-dimensional compact optimisation. It does not provide a certified numerical argmax algorithm or assert that approximate maximisers preserve exact literal correctness. When the optimal lifetime expectation is infinite, calling it a minimiser of that extended quantity is vacuous; the simultaneous finite-horizon statement remains substantive.

## Scope and crosswalk

The new result links literal repair geometry to an ordinary statistical decision problem with a sharp prior-dependent count-growth rate. It strengthens the Beta finite-budget iff and distinguishes almost-sure eventual repair from integrable repair cost. The same retained empirical learner is used throughout; the new contribution is the all-policy comparison and resulting rate characterisation, subject to independent review and a future novelty search.

The result does not extend automatically to control-dependent data, extra samples per report, stopping/abstention, excess-loss magnitude, priors with atoms or singular support, hidden changing alpha, or a supplied alpha-dependent seed. It is not a humanity-frontier or meniscus-break claim.
