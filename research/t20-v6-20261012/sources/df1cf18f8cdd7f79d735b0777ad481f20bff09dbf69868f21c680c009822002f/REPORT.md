The ordinary typing transfer accounts for every constructor of the existing `FiniteDerives`, including forward reduction. The normalisation conclusion is explicitly relative to subject reduction and strong normalisation for the exact Curry-style System F target; importing the standard explicit formulation without the appropriate bridge would not discharge that dependency.

The supplied ordinary parallel-development proof establishes confluence for the exact I/K/S relation. With `D=S I I` and `Omega=D D`, every reduct has a classified tower form and still reduces, so Omega has no normal form. It inhabits the primitive-bottom arrow. This is distinct from the preserved `K t Omega` obstruction: the latter is semantically accepted yet not strongly normalising, while it can still be convertible to a finite-source term.

Finally, I and S K K are distinct source normal forms, although their lambda macro translations beta-convert. A future model comparison must not treat the simulation as conversion-reflecting.

### U11: beta on application is not enough for a dependent calculus

The finite tracked substitution core has a literal substitution/evaluation square. The examined bracket compilers also satisfy an application beta law. Nevertheless the first compiler maps convertible bodies `I x` and `x` to the distinct normal forms `S (K I) I` and I. Adding an eta shortcut fixes that witness but fails on `K x I -> x`, whose abstractions are `S K (K I)` and I.

Thus **both specified compilers fail body-conversion congruence**. This is a precise falsification of the proposed direct full-calculus bridge, not a claim that every possible dependent calculus is impossible.
