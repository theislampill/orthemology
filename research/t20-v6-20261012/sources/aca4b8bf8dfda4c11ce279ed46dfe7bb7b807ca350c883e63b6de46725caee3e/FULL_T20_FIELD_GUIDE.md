## 1. The whole thing in one everyday story

Imagine a group trying to find out whether a bridge is safe.

They collect inspections, identify defects, compare standards, estimate risk and decide what to examine next. One report is copied from another. A laboratory later withdraws a certificate. A compressed summary forgets which component was inspected. An apparently sound calculation relies on a probability model that cannot describe any possible bridge. A computer can verify an individual calculation but cannot derive the general rule someone wants to use.

The group must distinguish several questions that sound similar but are not:

**What is actually true? What does the evidence support? What does the current representation retain? What will remain answerable after new evidence arrives? What can be computed? What can the declared proof rules certify? Which response is warranted?**

Most of T20 is investigating relationships among these questions. The mathematical branches do not merely add impressive notation to a review process. They establish conditions under which one of these properties follows from another—and construct cases in which it does not.

The philosophical branches ask something deeper as well: what makes objective truth, fitting judgement, genuine agency and warranted recognition possible? What would an original source of actuality have to be? What supports Knowledge, Will, Wisdom and rightful authority? Those are substantive philosophical investigations, not properties that a file checker can manufacture.

The shortest useful description is:

> **Orthemology studies warranted resolution of determinate realities, including what must be preserved when an investigation learns, corrects itself, changes representation or passes its conclusions to a successor.**

That description includes both the objects investigated and the processes of investigating them. It does not reduce Orthemology to assurance engineering, a theory of machine learning, or a single theological argument.

### Why there are so many archives

The archives are often successive stages of one investigation: a conjecture, a written proof, an adverse review, a corrected proof, a Lean formalisation, an executable implementation and a source-bound application. Some packages are cumulative. Some preserve a narrow successor and require an earlier package. Some recover an already earned result rather than discover another one.

An archive count therefore measures preservation objects, not independent discoveries. The register uses content hashes to distinguish identical copies from genuinely different deliveries.

## 2. The central discovery across the research: preservation has several meanings

A summary can preserve today's answer without preserving tomorrow's answer. It can preserve every conditional answer without preserving the probability that each observation occurs. It can preserve probabilities without preserving which investigations are authorised. It can describe formally evolving numbers without describing any possible probability distribution. It can uniquely determine a mathematical object without providing an algorithm that computes it. A statement can hold in an intended interpretation without being derivable from the current proof rules.

These are not six ways of saying the same thing. T20 has produced concrete examples separating them.

| What is preserved? | What that actually gives | What it does not automatically give |
|---|---|---|
| A present judgement | The same answer now | The same response to later evidence |
| Future conditional answers | The answer after each admitted observation | The observation's probability or value |
| Observation probabilities and update dynamics | A faithful stochastic account at the declared scope | Source authenticity or justified likelihoods |
| A valid model class | States correspond to possible mathematical objects | The model represents the actual case |
| A uniquely determined object | No other admissible object fits the full record | Effective recovery from that record |
| A sound interpretation | The rules preserve meaning in that model | Completeness of the proof calculus |
| A checked proof | The formal conclusion follows under the encoded rules | Actual-world warrant for every premise |
| An authorised procedure | The operation is permitted under a declared regime | Objective fittingness of that regime |

There is no single scalar measuring all of this. In particular, lowering an error, reducing the number of open obligations, or accumulating more agreeing reports cannot serve as a universal substitute.

This is the main reason the branches are starting to fit together. Each discovers a different way in which a tempting shortcut loses something consequential.

## 3. Story one: evidence, sources and what survives a correction

### 3.1 Losing trust is not the same as losing information

Suppose an instrument issues a report. Later, an audit reveals that the report was generated by a random coin. It would be natural to throw it away.

But suppose the audit also tells you whether the random report matched an independent, informative verifier. The report itself is now needed to interpret that comparison. A random zero that matched the verifier tells you something different from a random one that matched it.

T20 constructs an exact example in which keeping both the report and the audit yields a posterior of **1/5**, whereas deleting the report while retaining the audit gives **1/2**. This does not restore the report's credibility. It changes its evidential role: no longer trusted testimony, it is an observed item in a relation to another source.

This is a direct connection between epistemology and higher-order information. All the pairwise summaries can conceal the informative three-way relation among the hypothesis, report and audit.

**Practical lesson:** withdraw the unsupported warrant, not automatically every datum associated with it.

Sources: `T20_Source_Audit_Revision_Intermediate_20261010.zip`; `T20_Audit_Continuation_Transport_Intermediate_20261010.zip`.

### 3.2 A defeated argument does not necessarily erase knowledge

The Arabic investigations distinguish a failed argument from the grounds by which someone actually knows a proposition. Discovering that one certificate is defective does not establish that the proposition is false, or that every independent route to it has failed.

There can also be genuinely wider consequences. Defeating a universal premise about a source's truthfulness is not the same event as invalidating one local proof. The scope of the defeat matters.

This distinction helps explain why evidence-withdrawal models need more than a list of accepted conclusions. They need to record which conclusions depend on which grounds and in what way.

**Practical lesson:** a correction must identify what was defeated—one inference, one certificate, a source-wide reliability premise, or the conclusion itself.

Sources: the evidence-revision and source-successor checkpoints; the later local-correction and responsive-receptivity packets.

### 3.3 Basic cognition can help correct a local impairment

A local failure does not imply that every cognitive capacity is disabled. One can use surviving perception, memory, inference or testimony to identify and correct a particular impairment. T20's source investigation connects this with the priority of genuine basic knowledge.

The corresponding limit is important: a blanket statement that restoration works “in all cases” needs a scoped interpretation. A system cannot make an unrestricted recovery guarantee merely by invoking basic faculties.

**Practical lesson:** distinguish local damage with surviving corrective resources from total loss of the resources needed for that particular correction.

Source: `T20_Structured_Context_And_Local_Correction_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 3.4 The record of a correction is not its semantic truth

The assurance investigation found that the existing machinery already distinguishes repairing an interpretation from replacing a governing norm. That is retained capability, not a new theorem.

It also identified a narrower boundary: a generic validator can check declared revision permissions, but cannot reliably detect an undeclared change of meaning solely from declarations that omit it. If the recorded bytes conceal the consequential distinction, a structural checker cannot recover that distinction by inspecting the same bytes more insistently.

**Practical lesson:** machine enforcement can protect an explicit semantic contract; it cannot establish that all relevant semantics were faithfully declared.

Source: `Interpretation_Repair_Assurance_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

## 4. Story two: the cheapest warranted investigation is not the smallest-looking proof

### 4.1 Additional evidence can make a proof cheaper

Imagine that a claim can be checked now, but its proof is expensive. Acquiring one extra piece of evidence may permit a much simpler proof. T20 gives the comparison of a currently available proof costing **10** and a route involving additional evidence costing **2 altogether**.

Counting missing premises would prefer the wrong route. The relevant quantity is total warranted acquisition and checking cost under the declared contract.

The early warranted-discrimination work retains the evidence requirements and prices of admissible proofs, then calculates which acquisitions can complete them. It is finite and scoped. It does not interpret arbitrary natural-language arguments automatically.

Source: `T20_Warranted_Discrimination_Research_and_Lean_20261009.zip`.

### 4.2 Why source support must stay attached to a proof

Two proofs of the same claim can use different sources. A cheap proof may depend on source A, while a more expensive proof depends on source B. If the next step requires independent corroboration with A, the more expensive proof may be indispensable.

Discarding it solely because its price is higher loses an admissible future operation.

T20 therefore refines proof states by their exact evidence support and scoped judgement. Where the rules examine ordered premise supports, replacement must preserve those ordered checks too.

**Practical lesson:** “same conclusion, lower price” is not a sufficient criterion for substituting one proof for another.

Sources: Checkpoint 24; `T20_Guarded_Proof_Compilation_Verification_Supplement_20261009.zip`.

### 4.3 From an existence argument to an actual returned proof

The work proceeded through several distinct accomplishments: a finite shortening bound, existence of minimum-price proof witnesses, correctness of a saturation calculation, executable numeric output, and extraction of an actual valid proof tree matching that output.

Each closes a different gap. A proof that a minimum exists does not itself supply an efficient program. A program that prints a price does not itself return a valid proof. A returned proof with the right price still needs a correspondence theorem to justify minimality.

The implementation is an exhaustive reference construction. Its correctness is not a scalability claim.

A useful stopping counterexample also survives: the set of reachable claims can stop changing while their best prices fall from 10 to 1. Stable reachability keys do not prove stable costs.

Source: `T20_Constructive_Proof_Compiler_and_Knowledge_Update_20261009.zip`.

### 4.4 Shared proofs are not proof trees

A tree charges repeated uses separately. A shared graph can check a lemma once and reuse it.

This changes optimisation. The cheapest separate proof of each subclaim need not form the cheapest shared proof of their conjunction. Dependencies and overlapping paid nodes matter.

T20 shows that three proof bundles can have the same individual costs and the same pairwise overlaps, yet total **3** or **4** when each shared node is charged once. The missing information is the triple overlap. This is a genuine higher-order information issue, although retaining aligned node identities already solves that particular representation problem without imposing a tensor implementation.

The shared-DAG normalisation result then supplies a finite catalogue containing a minimum under its exact-support guards and nonnegative once-per-node prices. Its tested normaliser reduces a supplied witness; it is not an efficient global optimiser.

Sources: `T20_Shared_Proof_Cost_and_Information_Update_20261009.zip`; `T20_Guarded_Shared_Proof_Attainment_Supplement_20261009.zip`.

### 4.5 Knowing all the possible answers is not enough to choose the test

Suppose two research models agree about the conclusion you would draw after every possible outcome of an audit. They can still disagree about how likely those outcomes are.

T20 gives a three-outcome example in which this changes the value of the audit from **+1/32 to −1/32** after its cost. One model says perform it; the other says skip it.

**Practical lesson:** selecting an inquiry requires its outcome probabilities and costs, not only a menu of conditional answers.

Source: `T20_Posterior_Inquiry_Value_Intermediate_20261010.zip`.

### 4.6 Finite agreement does not automatically preserve eventual success

The stochastic-inquiry work examines noisy investigations that change the state while revealing information. It separates agreement on finite conditional beliefs from the stronger guarantee of eventually obtaining an error-free answer with probability one.

One system can have a positive probability of remaining unresolved forever despite looking equivalent under a weaker finite-answer record.

This is not a demand that human knowledge always require error-free certainty. It is an exact boundary for a specified decision procedure.

Source: `stochastic-inquiry-cumulative-evidence-20261010.zip`.

## 5. Story three: what tensors and continuation actually contribute

### 5.1 A tensor is useful when the relationship is the information

Imagine three records: a source report, an audit, and an independent check. Looking at each separately, or even at all pairs, may miss a relation that only the triple determines.

In Orthemology, typed tensor representations can preserve selected multi-way interactions. Their factors have declared roles; swapping source, claim and standard is not automatically meaningful. Tensor contraction, marginalisation and projection perform actual algebraic operations, but the interpretation of those operations must be specified.

The familiar parity construction is a clean warning: two distributions can agree on all lower-order marginals and disagree on a higher-order fact. The contribution is not that Orthemology invented parity. It is the way such obstructions constrain its actual representation and inquiry contracts.

### 5.2 A summary must be judged against what comes next

The inherited fibre criterion says that a retained record determines a target only when indistinguishable records never require different answers.

Continuation theory asks the harder question: will that remain true after every action and observation in a declared future inquiry?

A summary that suffices for today's query can fail after a new observation exposes a previously hidden interaction. T20 repeatedly turns this into positive constructions: retain the additional observables generated by the permitted operations, rather than simply accumulating every available datum.

The finite-dimensional memory and augmentation formulas belong to declared finite models with explicit encoder restrictions. They are not universal estimates of human memory or the time required for arbitrary refutation.

### 5.3 Evidence can make low-order summaries stop being closed

Suppose a model stores all marginals involving at most two variables. It may seem reasonable that it could keep updating those pairwise records without retaining the whole distribution.

T20 shows why informative evidence can defeat that idea. The updated pairwise answer may depend on a three-way or higher interaction that the old summary discarded. This can obstruct every update rule on the summary, including nonlinear ones; the problem is missing information, not a poor choice of formula.

There are precise exceptions and constructive repairs. The required extra interactions depend on the actual observation channel. In some cases the repaired representation is still smaller than the full joint distribution.

**Practical lesson:** choose retained interactions from the update contract, not from a fixed preference for low-order tensors.

Source: `T20_Channel_Tensor_Research_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 5.4 Event possibility is weaker than event probability

A compressed record may determine that an event is possible without determining its probability. It may know its probability without preserving every posterior effect.

These are different targets. T20 characterises a feasibility question separately rather than treating it as a failed attempt to reconstruct the whole law.

**Practical lesson:** sometimes a genuinely useful smaller representation exists because the question is weaker. Not every operation needs full recovery.

Source: `T20_Hierarchical_Marginal_and_Event_Feasibility_20261010.zip`.

### 5.5 Local consistency does not guarantee a global state

Several pairwise binary probability records can each be valid while no joint distribution has all of them as marginals. Even a positive-definite proposed covariance matrix does not automatically repair that failure.

This is the difference between numbers satisfying selected local checks and numbers describing a possible global object.

T20's later moment-hierarchy results extend that lesson into infinite-dimensional dynamics: positive coordinates and valid initial data do not by themselves keep an evolving record inside the set of actual probability laws.

**Practical lesson:** a representation needs a realisability condition, not only arithmetic consistency.

## 6. Story four: geometry, learning and correction

### 6.1 Going downhill is a mathematical claim with a specified hill

Gradient descent reduces a declared objective. A Riemannian metric specifies how lengths and steepest directions are measured in a chosen state space.

Orthemology already had actual Hessian/Bregman mathematics, not merely gradient language. Its canonical nudge can simultaneously improve every categorical truth-state scoring loss at an incoherent interior credence assignment.

That is a meaningful dominance result. It is not the same as discovering new evidence, correcting every relative belief, or acquiring epistemic warrant.

### 6.2 Normalising beliefs can accidentally distort learning

Consider an observation that contains no information about which hypothesis is true. Properly interpreted, it should not change the relative hypothesis weights.

T20 constructs a geometrically legitimate repair that nevertheless changes the forecast after such evidence. A correction can improve scores relative to its unnormalised input while making the overall evidence update worse.

This prompted a stronger research question: which geometries make repair compatible with the intended evidence update?

**Practical lesson:** assess the composed operation—evidence update followed by repair—not merely the repair in isolation.

### 6.3 The entropy result became a hierarchy of scoped results

The early separable theorem studied a particular correction on the interior cube. For at least three coordinates, all-start forward safety plus autonomous evolution of the coherence sum selected a scaled logarithmic-curvature family. The two-coordinate exception was explicit.

That did not prove that every appropriate noetic geometry must be Shannon geometry.

The later nonseparable result allows interactions among coordinates. A perspective family separates the coherent probability shape from total mass. It admits normalisation-compatible correction with non-entropy coherent scores, including a quadratic/Brier example.

Still later, requiring contraction under the specified family of finite stochastic channels selects KL geometry on normalised states, while leaving freedom in the total-mass penalty. Additional coordinate-additivity restricts that remaining freedom.

| Requirement | Consequence in its declared setting |
|---|---|
| Separable canonical correction, scalar closure and all-start safety | Logarithmic curvature |
| Nonseparable normalisation-compatible projection | A broader perspective family |
| The stated all-channel information monotonicity | KL coherent divergence |
| Further coordinate-additivity | Stronger restriction on the positive-cone generator |

These are compatible theorems with different hypotheses. Their progression is an example of the programme correcting an overbroad interpretation by identifying exactly which assumptions did the selecting.

Sources: Hessian Admissibility; Nonseparable Bayes Repair; Channel-Natural Repair packets.

### 6.4 The clock is part of the prediction contract

Two descriptions can trace the same curve at different speeds. That may be harmless for a question about the eventual normalised endpoint, but not for a question about what happens by a deadline, how much time correction costs, or when an audit occurs.

The binary-log investigation finds that the original-clock trajectory requires more retained information than a normalised-clock description. A single signed coherence sum can suffice for the latter while all continuous coordinates are required for the former under the theorem's assumptions.

**Practical lesson:** changing the clock changes the question. It is not a free compression operation.

### 6.5 Lower error is not the same as remaining admissible

A trajectory can reduce its objective while approaching or reaching a forbidden boundary. Two states can show the same coherence trajectory while only one hits that boundary.

The safety theorem and its counterexamples distinguish valid interior evolution from an attractive numerical trace. They do not establish geodesic completeness of the entire metric.

**Practical lesson:** optimise within a justified domain and monitor the properties that make a state admissible.

### 6.6 Fixed hidden interactions can support exact lower-dimensional geometry

A low-dimensional moment record may fail to determine its future because hidden interactions change the effective geometry. T20 then obtains a positive result by fixing those interaction structures explicitly.

Within the resulting family or leaf, the retained moments admit an exact conditional Riemannian description. Different leaves may require different motion from the same visible starting summary.

This is not pretending the missing information disappeared. It is supplying it as part of the modelling contract.

**Practical lesson:** the real state may be “these numerical coordinates, under this retained interaction context”, not the coordinates alone.

Source: `T20_Leafwise_Geometry_and_Wise_Means_20261010.zip`.

### 6.7 Evidence can change support, not merely move a point

An observation may rule out possibilities entirely. That changes the face of the probability simplex on which the state lives.

The facial-geometry work extends the model to such changes and identifies when support information must be retained. It also separates two propositions:

A chosen model family remains valid after an observation.

Replacing an arbitrary actual distribution with that model before observing produces the same result as observing first.

The first does not imply the second. The commutation results give exact conditions, including zero-probability branches.

Source: `T20_Facial_Geometry_and_Evidence_Commutation_20261010.zip`.

### 6.8 Geometry still needs a noetic interpretation

An exact geometric representation of probability updating is already mathematics. To become an operational account of a DAEE correction, it needs a justified representation map, actual update semantics, admissible operations, sources, authority and preserved evidence.

The proposed hybrid state has two parts: a smooth numerical state and an exact context retaining identities, proofs, permissions and history. T20 makes the interface increasingly concrete, but does not establish one universal geometry of human cognition.

## 7. Story five: infinite information is not automatically usable information

### 7.1 Finite moments can be enough when the support is known

If a distribution is supported on a known finite set of distinct scalar points, sufficiently many exact moments recover its weights. T20 formalises the algebraic core in Lean.

It then removes an earlier assumed projection: a finite set of distinct vectors over an infinite field admits a scalar projection separating those vectors. Combined with finite moment recovery, that gives a constructive algebraic route from a vector catalogue to recovered weights. Finite fields have a genuine obstruction.

These are exact algebraic results. They do not guarantee numerical stability under noise or discover an unknown support catalogue automatically.

Sources: finite-moment-recovery and Separating Projection packets.

### 7.2 All moments can still leave more than one law

On unbounded domains, different probability laws can have identical algebraic moments. They can consequently agree on every initial derivative of a moment-based trajectory while disagreeing on its later values.

The problem is not that someone forgot to include “one more moment”. The entire chosen summary can be insufficient.

T20 connects this classical phenomenon to its actual correction dynamics and identifies a constructive alternative: retain a transform, such as the appropriate Laplace information, on which the future operation acts faithfully.

Source: `T20_Source_Measure_Continuation_Intermediate_20261009.zip`.

### 7.3 Determining a law and computing it are different achievements

An infinite record can uniquely identify a hidden distribution without making its indexed weights uniformly computable in the chosen representation. Effective bounds on tails or hidden mass can restore recovery.

Conversely, some bounded observables can be computed without reconstructing the entire hidden indexed law.

**Practical lesson:** ask what the algorithm is given, what it must output, and in which representation. “The law is determined” is not a complete computational specification.

Source: `Capped_Tensor_Recovery_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 7.4 Tail certificates buy computable continuation—but boundaries matter

A certified exponential-tail bound permits effective continuation in a strict interior domain. At an endpoint, normalisation can be computable while a stronger mean-recovery task has no uniform procedure under the weaker data contract.

This does not contradict finite exact recovery. The information supplied, uniformity requirement and operation being computed differ.

Source: `Exponential_Tail_Effectivity_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 7.5 Determinacy supplies a broader positive route

The later determinate-moment result does not require a positive exponential-tail bound for damped continuation. Given a valid, uniformly computable full moment record that uniquely determines its law on the specified nonnegative domain, it supplies an effective route to that law and its damped future observables.

The promises are substantive. A finite prefix does not verify that the entire sequence is valid and determinate. The result also does not say that every Taylor series converges; a useful continuation can be computable by a different method.

Source: `Determinate_Moment_Effectivity_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 7.6 A regular Fisher boundary can still conceal continuation information

The Fisher-endpoint result strengthens the warning against identifying smooth local data with a complete state. Distinct probability states can reach finite-distance, regular boundaries with identical derivatives of every order, yet have different continuations.

A local geometric record can therefore be arbitrarily rich and still omit essential provenance about which underlying law is being continued.

Source: `Fisher_Endpoint_Geometry_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 7.7 The hierarchy of equations is not the state space

T20 studies a moment evolution in which each derivative depends on the next moment and a normalisation term. Within its admissible probability-law class, the solutions are precisely damped versions of initial probability distributions. The initial moment record determines a unique admissible trajectory exactly when it determines the initial law under the theorem's contract.

That is a classification of admissible trajectories—not a claim that every positive solution of the coordinate equations is admissible.

The latest counterexamples make the difference decisive. Even with a determinate initial moment record and strictly positive coordinates throughout an evolution, the equations can develop a spurious state represented by no probability law. Every fixed finite-order monitor can initially miss the escape.

The computed witness is particularly intuitive: a polynomial square is assigned a strictly negative expectation. No probability law can do that. The interval calculation certifies the sign with outward bounds rather than relying on rounded samples.

A stronger classical moment-growth condition supplies a positive safeguard. Its exact hypotheses must be retained rather than replaced by “the numbers stay positive”.

Sources: `Realizable_Moment_Dynamics_Evidence_20261010.zip`; `Initial_Validity_Counterexample_Evidence_20261010.zip`; `Determinate_Initial_Positivity_Evidence_20261010.zip`; `Computable_Positive_Hierarchy_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 7.8 A stable distribution may exist without an available selection algorithm

The stationary-law work returns to the measure/computability programme. It distinguishes approximate stationarity, exact computation under effective hypotheses, and families for which no uniform selection algorithm exists.

Its envelope result also distinguishes a strong condition on every transition row from merely preserving an envelope. A corrective process satisfying only the weaker condition cannot inherit the stronger uniqueness guarantee.

A state-dependent policy is another potential change of system: composing or selecting individually well-behaved transitions need not preserve a property whose assumptions were tied to a fixed kernel.

Source: `Effective_Stationary_Selection_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

## 8. Story six: the proof system's missing capabilities became mathematical results

### 8.1 Start with three notions of sameness

Two program expressions can have the same behaviour in an interpretation, be provably equal at a type, or be literally equal/convertible as raw code. These are different targets.

A translation that validates typed equality need not identify the raw expressions. Conversely, raw-code inequality need not refute typed equality.

The HasE work repeatedly checks that one notion is not silently substituted for another.

### 8.2 Closed Church-number programs can reveal a uniform number

Church numerals encode repeated application. An input representing three iterations acts like “apply this operation three times”.

T20 first investigates an intended open-term/PER interpretation, then connects the actual pinned compiler definitions to it. It establishes uniform finite iteration counts at the specified scope and proves closed source instances inside the actual calculus.

Later results identify each closed, derivably Church-typed input with its recovered numeral and characterise equality by the recovered counts. An executable extractor returns certificates for concrete inputs, including terms with a discarded divergent subterm. Failure to finish within a resource budget does not count as a proof that an input is invalid.

### 8.3 Closed instances are not the internal universal theorem

A proof for every closed input, obtained externally one input at a time, does not automatically give the universally quantified formula inside the calculus. That was a real boundary in this investigation, not pedantry.

The distinction becomes even clearer with contexts. Opaque Raw inputs can be replaced without changing the recovered numeral under one theorem. A genuinely Church-typed variable cannot be identified with a single fixed numeral. Structured-context results enlarge the permitted class without discarding the counterexample.

### 8.4 A model had to respect the actual rules

An apparent countermodel is irrelevant if it ignores a typing, identity-elimination or extensionality rule that the calculus actually has.

T20 checked models against all twenty actual HasE rules, including dependent identity elimination, proof erasure and polymorphic instantiation. It also found why a proposed typed-to-raw contradiction failed: the varying endpoint did not have the necessary Raw type.

The finite-Raw investigation proves another limit: a finite summary preserving every application and conversion of the full original untyped calculus collapses in the specified setting. That does not forbid finite typed data or restricted observations.

### 8.5 The exact original identity is independent of the unchanged calculus

The Henkin-model work eventually established that the exact original compiler identity has no derivation under the unchanged HasE rules. Its internal negation is also unprovable there.

The intended full-PER semantics still validates the identity, and the separate HasC certificate system retains its positive result.

This is not “the identity is false”. It locates a gap between intended semantic validity and the certification power of that particular calculus.

Source: `T20_Henkin_Model_And_Source_Independence_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 8.6 Rigidity gives an exact completion principle for induction

The rigidity principle concerns maps preserving zero and successor. Informally, it states the relevant uniqueness of the encoded natural-number structure.

T20 proves that an explicit rigidity hypothesis supplies the compiler identity and then supports dependent induction. The zero and successor laws are checked, including dependent transport. Rigidity and an internal recursor-uniqueness principle are linked in both directions.

The later converse is stronger: for the exact Church-number calculus and stated scope, full dependent induction entails rigidity even without demanding computation laws. Rigidity is therefore not merely a convenient sufficient repair; it marks an exact completion requirement in that result.

The premise remains explicit. The unchanged calculus does not acquire it because the researcher prefers the intended model.

Sources: Generic Rigidity; Conditional Dependent Section; Rigidity Uniqueness; Actual-E Dependent Induction; Induction Rigidity Converse packets.

### 8.7 Pairs require a different missing principle

A pair has projections. But knowing the two projections does not by itself give a derivation that the object equals the pair reconstructed from them.

The Sigma-tag countermodel makes that absence precise. The constructive results establish how the appropriate reconstruction identity supplies dependent elimination, and the full-E result proves the equivalence at the actual extended-calculus scope.

This is a different completion problem from natural-number rigidity. The latest separation packet gives checked interpretations showing that the two specified literal principles cannot substitute for one another in the unchanged calculus. Another checked interpretation validates both.

That is narrower than a soundness theorem for every possible simultaneous extension by full schemes. The last supplied progress message still treated the broader coexistence question as an active investigation.

Sources: Sigma Tag Boundary; Multi-Front Successor; Actual-E Sigma Elimination; Completion Principle Separation packets.

### 8.8 Substitution is the infrastructure that makes the stronger results usable

A derivation with variables is useful only if legitimate terms and type parameters can be substituted into it while preserving typing.

Earlier interfaces were restricted to an older calculus. T20 extends dependent and mixed substitutions through the actual HasE rules, including extensionality. Checked witnesses lie outside the old system, so the stronger interface is not just a renamed old theorem.

That machinery then strengthens induction, fold transport and dependent-pair results to the actual input class.

Sources: Actual HasE Dependent Substitution; Actual HasE Mixed Substitution; Conditional Unit and Fold Transfer packets.

### What this whole branch contributes to Orthemology

It makes the boundary between meaning and certification explicit. It also prevents a common research error: treating a failure of one proof language as a refutation of the intended structure, or treating one favourable model as a completeness theorem.

The branch now contains positive constructions, impossibility results and exact additional requirements. Its open obligations are more sharply specified than they were when the investigation began.

## 9. Story seven: the affirmative philosophical foundations

### 9.1 Actuality, original sourcehood and intrinsic necessity

The philosophical programme does not begin and end with checking whether some abstract symbol is called “necessary”. It investigates actual received existence, productive sourcehood, the bearer of concrete powers and what it means for actuality to be unreceived.

The corpus preserves an affirmative argument, including a later judgement that the same actual original's intrinsic necessity is warranted at its stated philosophical scope. The T17 resource-to-bearer argument was recovered rather than reopened as a missing premise: an originally possessed concrete power cannot borrow the actuality of its bearer while remaining existentially original in the relevant respect.

The Darʾ volume 5 corroboration distinguishes considering an attribute separately in thought from establishing that it could exist independently in reality.

These are philosophical arguments with source and interpretation obligations. Lean can check conditional formulations; it does not turn the intended actual-world premises into formal axioms of reality.

### 9.2 One complete common original Creator

The cumulative record contains a defeasible affirmative appraisal concerning one complete common original Creator of the relevant productively connected created order. The scope matters. The case is not supplied merely by writing “independent” where “exclusive” is needed.

The research distinguishes standing capacity from complete current efficacy, original sourcehood from created means, intrinsic activity from effects in another bearer, and cooperation from two allegedly independent whole producers.

Rival models have evidential force only to the extent that they preserve the intended premises and meanings. A model that omits the disputed bridge can expose non-entailment from a thinner set without defeating the stronger, separately warranted argument.

The guide retains that affirmative appraisal without representing every open application or scope question as closed.

### 9.3 Knowledge has more than one supporting route

Knowing particular production and its determinate features supports one part of the Knowledge argument. Comprehensive knowledge has a separate pure-perfection argument. The source appraisal around Darʾ 10:150–152 maintains this distinction.

A thin causal model lacking the second premise cannot refute the combined argument simply by failing to derive comprehensive knowledge.

At the same time, knowing something and actually adopting a particular communicated utterance are different targets. The latter needs evidence about the occurrence and content of communication.

### 9.4 Wise reasons need not be an external mechanism

A reason for an act, an agent's own willing, the act's timing and the circumstances in which its end is achieved can be distinguished without imposing an external deterministic controller.

The particular-determination research preserves intrinsic reasons and prospective versus executive intention. It also rejects the inference that an undisclosed detail of one particular act automatically defeats the general Wisdom argument.

A sufficient explanation need not always identify a uniquely forced choice among every describable alternative. But neither does a bare assertion of a possible benefit establish the agent's actual reason.

### 9.5 Concrete positive applications matter

The ship and wall accounts provide local explanations linking interventions to specified beneficiaries and disclosed purposes. The wall account expressly attributes the future purpose to the Lord. The force of that application depends on the programme's retained warrant for the disclosure, not on benefit alone.

The wise-means reading supplies another example: intermediaries can secure goods that direct provision would not preserve. Ibn al-Qayyim's trade example supports a reason for mediation without establishing a uniquely best arrangement.

The intrinsic-succession investigation distinguishes adequate production of particular acts from treating the entire succession as one simultaneously completed product. It does not silently impose a first-act requirement.

Sources: Particular Explanation; Wise Reason Application; Intrinsic Succession; Leafwise Geometry and Wise Means packets.

### 9.6 Self-praise is an own-act case, not merely an external effect

The self-praise application identifies the act, its knowingly intended good end and that end's attainment as practical grounds. Accidentally beneficial output does not replace those grounds.

The result leaves the exact wording and occasion of every act-token unresolved. It also avoids imposing a newly acquired cognitive representation or essential perfection on every voluntary act.

Source: `Immanent_Self_Praise_Evidence_20261010.zip`.

### 9.7 A response condition is not necessarily received essential perfection

The repentance study identifies actual repentance as a condition of a specified divine rejoicing. Foreknowledge and standing capacity are not substitutes for that actual condition.

Its first report also corrected an overstatement: questioning the dependence of a particular realisation does not necessarily allege a missing essential faculty.

The responsive-receptivity addendum then recovered the broader developed answer already present in Majmūʿ 8:145–147: realisation through Allah's own activity is distinguished from completion supplied by another, and an occurrence's absence before its fitting occasion need not be a defect.

This matters methodologically. A narrow application limit must not erase the weight of an already argued wider answer. The addendum earns recovered-warrant credit, not a new-discovery count.

Sources: Repentance Response Condition; Responsive Receptivity Recovery packets.

### 9.8 Authority, commands and recipients

The authority research distinguishes rightful direction from bare power, benefaction from arbitrary fiat, and the authority of a source from the force of every particular utterance.

A command can add a good to an act that was already good. Default permission does not by itself establish permanent surrender of authority. Genuine grants and undertaken limits still matter.

The Barīra investigation distinguishes obligatory command from intercession. A person's actual response can qualify them for promised treatment under the source's own undertaking without making that person a superior lawgiver or a supplier of divine perfection.

The undertaking can itself provide a particular reason for fulfilment rather than violation, while leaving the reason for choosing that undertaking a separate question.

### 9.9 Revelation and epistemic availability

The scriptural-recovery investigation asks whether the intended recipient has adequate warranted access to a communication's content. It distinguishes original wording, authorial identity, an earliest recoverable text, a received canonical text, rasm and particular readings.

The mathematical fibre criterion can establish non-recovery within a declared historical model. It does not automatically establish zero warrant, a 50% posterior, the falsity of every doctrine, or a comparative verdict between actual traditions.

The further claim about truthful, wise or maximally great communication needs its own philosophical bridge. Not every beneficial non-disclosure is defective communication. Successful original delivery, later transmission, intended content and recipient obligations must be distinguished.

This remains a substantive application frontier, not a mathematical declaration that one scripture passes and another fails.

Source: `T20_Scriptural_Recovery_Supplement_20261009.zip`.

## 10. The practical DAEE findings

T20 has not merely discussed a future noetic runtime. It found concrete boundaries in existing machinery.

The recognition-guard investigation reproduced a mismatch: the command-line checker accepted advanced alignment when recognition was absent, whereas the governing schema rejected it. The retained candidate guard rejects absent, weak or medium recognition while preserving valid controls. The patch was unapplied at that delivery, and a separate routing inconsistency was not repaired by it.

The prior-probability tactic overstated what an evidential objection requires. An objection's force depends jointly on the prior and likelihood; a near-zero starting prior is not necessary. The corrected treatment preserves the useful demand to justify background assumptions and permits assessment with justified ranges.

The interpretation-repair study distinguishes what declared permissions can enforce from undeclared semantic changes a generic validator cannot recognise.

These findings have different statuses: an implementation mismatch with a candidate repair, a mathematical/probability correction, and an assurance-scope clarification. They should not be collapsed into “DAEE is fixed” or “DAEE failed”.

The richer geometric theorems can eventually support a mathematical interface to these operations. They do not remove the need to establish that the chosen coordinates, likelihoods, authority and proof dependencies match the actual episode.

## 11. Earlier T20, predecessors and external comparisons are not disposable

The newest archive sequence sits on earlier T20 material: exact and noisy reconstruction, finite and countable observation models, source-sensitive qualitative transport, objective norm-fixing, copying and identity, collective diagnosis, source authority and the Necessary Being/attribute investigations.

The early integration packet also contains inherited T16–T19 material. Those predecessors are inputs to T20, not automatically new T20 results. An additive successor can extend a theorem without including all of its source dependencies.

### Earlier source and explanation diagnostics that still matter

The early T20 and companion research did more than establish the vocabulary for later work. It tested whether particular formal pictures actually preserve the philosophical relations they are used to represent.

One useful result concerns harmonious determination. If two allegedly complete determinations agree on an output throughout an admissible compatibility graph, their common output is constant on each connected component. A connected domain and a diagonal-only domain therefore have different consequences. The mathematical question is exact; whether original independence justifies the needed combinations is a separate philosophical premise. Merely saying “they might always agree” neither supplies nor defeats that premise.

Another result concerns explanatory facts. A finite Boolean explanation model can have several local explainers while retaining a unique total explainer. But unrestricted addition of irrelevant fact-content to a total explainer can destroy uniqueness. A formal explanation relation therefore cannot simply be treated as ordinary implication or arbitrary conjunction without checking what those operations preserve.

A modal diagnostic distinguishes a suitable witness existing in each world from one fixed witness serving in every world. A local failure to preserve that distinction is not automatically a refutation of an entire philosophical paper; it identifies the particular inference and the additional condition needed to repair it.

The probability diagnostics likewise separate coherent probabilities, exchangeability, mixture representations and causal interpretation. A likelihood assumption can carry substantial inferential weight even when the arithmetic after that assumption is exact. This earlier result connects directly to the later correction of DAEE's near-zero-prior tactic.

These are examples of formal analysis constraining actual philosophical reasoning. Some use established mathematics, and their particular applications have explicit limits. They should neither be counted as proofs of every theological conclusion nor erased because the mathematical ingredients have ancestry.

### Unknown structure, calibration and noisy recovery

The early restoration programme asks how an observer can reconstruct hidden productive routes rather than merely predict a few outputs. Its variables can include route type, multiplicity, shared gates, the set of affected outputs and the observation protocol.

A total count or a low-order projection can erase differences among those routes. Exact profile-indexed measurements can restore identifiability in the declared finite model. Calibration matters: when two route classes have observationally identical failure behaviour, a distinction that was recoverable under different calibrated bases may disappear.

Noise then creates another problem even when exact identification exists. A mathematically distinct probability can be too close to another to distinguish with a feasible sample. Uniform guarantees need their stated bounds on multiplicity, effect catalogue, trial independence and observation design. Finite, countable and unbounded domains cannot be exchanged without rechecking those hypotheses.

This story feeds the later moment work. “There is exactly one object consistent with the ideal observations” becomes “what representation of the observations does an algorithm receive, and what can it compute reliably?” The capped-tail and determinate-moment results are therefore continuations of an older restoration problem, not unrelated excursions into probability theory.

### Objective standards, collective diagnosis and succession

The programme's norm-fixing question is not solved by declaring an evaluation rule. It asks what makes the rule fitting to the actual case, rather than merely preferred, authorised or widely accepted. Its formal records make that question visible by separating the governing type, the case-bound token, the evidence and the actual truth-link.

Collective diagnosis adds a further distinction: agreement among evaluators need not add independent support when they inherit one distorted upstream source. The CR-9 example already represents this failure while allowing each local reasoning pathway to be adequate relative to its declared information state. That is why independence, blinding, source diversity and independent calculation should not be treated as one binary property.

Copying and succession ask what survives when a record, controller, proof or task moves to a successor. Keeping a name or matching an output does not automatically preserve the constitutive structure. The later typed-identity and continuation theorems make some related preservation questions exact within their own mathematical domains. They do not establish a universal metaphysical theory of personal identity by analogy.

The same discipline governs source-language research: an occurrence of a word, the proposition conveyed, the author's intention and the authority to act on it are not one object. The later command/intercession and source-adoption investigations inherit these distinctions.

These earlier branches are indexed through their unchanged entry reports in the source catalogue. Their existence matters even where the newest packages contain only a narrow successor rather than a reprint of the whole earlier argument.


### The identification results need a precise reading

The much-discussed “three-panel” result is not three ordinary scalar measurements discovering an unrestricted world. It uses complete profile-indexed families of exact observations over an arbitrary finite catalogue, with calibrated route classes and unbounded natural multiplicities in the stated model.

Unique factorisation, exact rational arithmetic and Boolean-lattice inversion do substantive work. Noise, unknown calibration and finite precision change the recovery problem. The computational/statistical results explicitly investigate those costs and limitations.

### The comparative philosophy has retained value without dictating the agenda

The corpus includes source comparisons and formal diagnostics involving causal grounding, necessary agreement, proper function, explanation and probability. Their results should remain available, including failed maps and adverse findings.

Their existence does not create a standing obligation to reopen every rival system. A comparison is useful when it advances a live premise or resolves a serious remaining objection.

### The universal-weight-subspace paper is a research input, not validation of Orthemology

The uploaded external paper studies architecture-specific shared low-rank parameter representations. Its own qualifications distinguish conditional estimation bounds from universal existence, model-wise versus mode-wise structure, and stored LoRA factors from gauge-invariant updates.

It gives a promising test bed: retaining most variance or benchmark performance does not by itself preserve every future adaptation or corrective operation. The linear continuation diagnostic in this synthesis makes that question exact in a deliberately small model. No empirical claim about real networks follows from the toy construction alone.

## 12. Where the parts genuinely connect

### Connection A: hidden information changes the next legitimate operation

Source support changes whether corroboration is licensed. A hidden interaction changes a Bayesian posterior. An omitted moment changes future dynamics. A missing proof principle changes what can be derived.

The shared question is not just “do two states look alike?” but “can they be substituted for one another under the operations we still intend to perform?”

These branches require different mathematical relations. They are not one unproved universal equivalence. Nevertheless, their common demand for operation-preserving representation is specific enough to support the finite construction in the companion synthesis.

### Connection B: improvements have to be evaluated jointly

A lower score may distort evidence. A cheaper local proof may break future guards. A smaller record may lose audit value. An extra source constraint can improve truth-tracking while increasing immediate burden.

A valid correction must therefore preserve the relevant constraints while achieving its specified improvement. It cannot be judged by one convenient local quantity without a bridge to the whole task.

### Connection C: state validity is prior to optimisation

If pairwise records describe no joint law, or a moment trajectory gives a negative expectation to a square, its attractive gradient behaviour is not a successful probabilistic correction.

This supplies a concrete meaning to “admissibility first”: demonstrate that the candidate belongs to the declared state space before treating its numerical improvement as an improvement of that space.

### Connection D: completeness has different objects

Observation completeness concerns information. Computability concerns an algorithm and its representation. Proof completeness concerns a calculus and a semantics. Historical attestation concerns evidence and actual communication.

Confusing these creates false successes and false defeats. The HasE independence and moment-effectivity results are particularly useful reminders that a gap must be assigned to the right level.

### Connection E: the exact context can be smaller than the entire past

Nothing requires every numerical coordinate to store the entire history. A combined record can retain the selected smooth state plus an exact sidecar of the distinctions still needed.

The new finite sidecar theorem characterises a minimum for a declared future action/observation/cost contract. It is a constructive positive answer to a recurring T20 question: how to retain enough without retaining everything.

### Connection F: the philosophical programme changes what the mathematics is asked to preserve

The distinctions between an actual reason and an accidental benefit, source authority and utterance force, or a defective certificate and surviving knowledge are not arbitrary metadata. They determine the targets a mathematical representation must distinguish.

The mathematics can expose a missing distinction or prove a transfer impossible under a chosen record. It does not decide the actual philosophical premise simply by assigning a label.

## 13. What has advanced, what remains open

The supplied record supports a substantially more connected programme than a collection of assurance conventions. There are exact conditional classifications, source-level proof constructions, impossibility results, countermodels, information-preserving interfaces and practical validation findings.

It does not support declaring all foundations complete, every result novel, every written proof kernel-verified, or one fully implemented system governing arbitrary cognition.

The main remaining tasks include the justified noetic interpretation of the mathematical operators; whole-contract integration of evidence, authority, cost and update dynamics; the exact scope and sound coexistence of completion schemes; stronger constructive recovery under usable information contracts; and continued positive philosophical work on original efficacy, particular wise determination, authority and warranted recognition.

Those are genuine research questions. They are not reasons to discard the results already established.

### The key sentence to remember

> **T20 is discovering which things that look equivalent now remain equivalent under learning, correction, proof, computation and further inquiry—and which extra information or principles are needed when they do not.**

That is the emerging intellectual connection. The companion synthesis turns a bounded part of it into explicit theorems and a finite exact reference method, without pretending the entire research programme has already become one universal engine.
