## Segment 1: windows 001–003, NB-S002-L0050–0070

Read every displayed line in sequence, with each END marker visible. The division begins with a new فصل but rests on arguments made elsewhere. It ends mid-sentence at L0070; continuation will be read in window 004.

### Argument, voices and non-obvious material

- L0050–0052: Ibn Taymiyya's programme identifies the Quran's proofs of lordship, unity, attributes, prophecy and resurrection as simultaneously revealed and rational. His claims that revelation is truth and the revealed law is justice are substantive commitments of this programme, not neutral premises established merely by stating them. Scriptural quotations about destroyed peoples, blindness of hearts and the damned are recruited as normative diagnoses. L0051 expressly answers the complacent inference from an opponent's present survival: delayed punishment is compatible with eventual judgement.
- L0052–0054: The author disputes two readings of early condemnations of kalam: restricting them to Qadarite controversy and extending them to every rational inquiry/disputation. He permits and defends Quranic argument and denies that the commands to dispute well were simply abrogated by armed conflict. Diplomatic/security contexts, initial invitation and clarification of doubts are distinguished. This is not an undifferentiated rejection of reasoning. His historical attributions to al-Shafiʿi, Ahmad and named opponents are reports within the source, not checked historical conclusions.
- L0053–0056: Criticism of the body/accident proof and the exclusion of beginningless events leads to the charge that several theological schools fail to establish Creator, prophecy or return. Al-Razi's reported late preference for Quranic routes is quoted as an opponent's admission. The author says ability and will are needed for signs produced to attest messengers; he charges the alternative works with developing negations better than affirmations. These sweeping verdicts exceed what is independently demonstrated in this passage.
- L0056–0058: Polemical group judgements coexist with concessions: named theologians possess merits, effort worth thanking and potentially forgiven ijtihad; schools and individual authors differ in closeness to scripture and acquaintance with rival positions. Al-Ashʿari's survey is called the most comprehensive the author has seen, though still criticised. Hierarchies of reporting competence distinguish al-Ashʿari, al-Shahrastani, al-Ghazali and Abu Talib. Later philosophical/Sufi combinations and claims to supersede the Companions are attacked. A second error is ascribing simple ignorance of scriptural meaning and rational proofs to the Companions. Reports from Ibn Masʿud and prophetic generations supply the author's positive ancestral picture.
- L0059–0061: A messenger cannot reasonably demand acceptance without evidence of truthfulness; a bare report admitting truth or falsity cannot itself be proof. البينات are clear in themselves and make other things clear, analogous to sensory and first/self-evident premises. Guidance explains both the proper end (God as worshipped) and the path (how to worship). It is not to be accepted as unsupported imitation. The author explicitly requires answering an opposing consideration for a proof to be complete. فرقان is the discrimination between valid proof and plausible imposture, illustrated by a route to Mecca, its correct guide and a false guide. This is a strong source-owned epistemic requirement, separate from whether his later applications meet it.
- L0061–0063: Correct conception, evidence for assent, doctrinal content and the means of knowing it are distinguished. Detailed affirmation and general negation in prophetic discourse are contrasted with opponents' supposed reverse practice. The author recounts his own writing occasions: an initial answer addressed admitted believers, subsequent detailed engagement with negators/philosophers, an explanatory work on the Egyptian al-Isfahani's creed, and a response to a Christian book. These are authorial autobiographical/bibliographical comments; this section neither supplies complete titles for each allusion nor establishes a publication chronology externally. He differentiates criticism addressed to believers from proofs needed for a person not yet granting prophecy. L0063 repeats that assent without knowing either the report's truth or its reporter's truthfulness is speech without knowledge.
- L0064–0067: Extended Quranic narratives furnish examples of prophetic proofs, particularly Moses and his opponents. L0065 reads “from your Lord” as qualifying both messenger and sign. Sorcerers' recognition is expert testimony within the narrated event. L0066 notes that Mosaic signs preceded the Torah, distinguishing miraculous evidence from the subsequently given law. Intellectual seeking is distinguished from focused inferential inspection; forgetting evidence differs from denying it. The author ascribes many rejections to desire and pride despite certainty, using scripture for this diagnosis. That diagnosis should not be mistaken for independent access to all historical opponents' beliefs.
- L0067–0069: Both verbal and enacted signs are said to convey information and obligation: God informs that the messenger is his and commands obedience. The author rejects the possibility of acknowledging God's sign while thinking God creates it to certify a liar or pointlessly mislead. He grounds that rejection in necessary natural human knowledge that God does not lie or do evil. There is an explicit difference between general divine creation of every act and a special sign as an intentional attestation. Sorcery can be a created but acquired and recurrent human practice, so its strangeness to an observer does not make it a prophetic sign. The opponents in the biblical/Quranic examples dispute source, calling the event sorcery; the author says they did not grant divine source and then deny evidential intention. This is his taxonomy, not an independently exhaustive survey.
- L0069: Moral/relational contrasts supplement power contrasts: prophets mutually attest, worship God, commend truth/justice and oppose sorcery; sorcerers allegedly quarrel and favour its opposite. The Mosaic promise of a prophet is not simply monopolized for Muhammad: after reporting Jewish, Christian and Muslim particular identifications, Ibn Taymiyya calls the generic reading closer, covering subsequent prophets including Jesus and Muhammad. This concession is important to retain.
- L0070: Recitation, purification and instruction are distinguished. The author interprets Quranic verses as signs of God, commanded norms and messenger truthfulness, joining propositional knowledge with obedient formation. The precise sentence continues into the next window.

### First-pass appraisal questions held open

The author's avowed refusal of bare assertion and demand for counterevidence-discrimination are significant. The live issue is whether fitri knowledge of a nondeceptive God, species-level classification of wonders, moral-character evidence and transmitted scriptural reports supply independent support for the source attribution. The segment asserts a strong attestation bridge, not merely a weak design or generic causality claim. Its universal claim about what humans necessarily know and the diagnosis of resistant opponents need their own warrant. Later windows may refine these points; no verdict is imposed here.

The intended general argument is often recoverable from context, but none of those exact forms is used as a load-bearing proof. ## Segment 2: windows 004–006, NB-S002-L0071–0094

Read continuously, with all END markers. L0071 completes the prior sentence. This segment reaches the chapter on entailment and the semantics of آية; the discussion continues beyond it.

### Argument, voices and non-obvious material

- L0071–0072: Signs have rationally intelligible force; warnings about particular punishment have a revealed-report role. Rival school positions on how divine knowledge is obtained and how its acquisition becomes obligatory are carefully separated. Ibn Taymiyya's preferred view grants actions intrinsic qualities of goodness/badness that can be rationally known, while denying that God punishes anyone before a messenger reaches them. He therefore does not simply choose either “reason alone” or an unrestricted scriptural positivism. “We do not punish until we send a messenger” is deployed against both adversarial poles, including the alleged permissibility of punishing faultless children. The passage reports this as opponents' position, not an approved policy or modern factual report.
- L0073–0075: The opening revelation's creation/teaching sequence supports an a-fortiori capacity argument. Turning an initial embryo into a knowing, articulate, writing human is presented as a greater transformation than informing an existing human of commands/news. Similarly, a God who enables humans to authenticate a human messenger through signs without prior agreement can supply signs of his own messenger. This argues possibility and fittingness of revelation and recognition; it does not on its own identify any specific claimant. The author treats learning, language and writing as gifts and grounds epistemic provision in generosity. His embryological language belongs to the text's explanatory picture; no modern scientific verification is attempted.
- L0074–0075: Prophecy, unity and resurrection provoke unfamiliarity-based surprise; the author distinguishes that surprise from impossibility. A human messenger's common language and nature are fitting means of communication, and an angel would have to appear human for people to receive him. The claim that Muhammad's mission addresses the genus of humans rather than only Arabs is explicit despite the initial Arabic audience.
- L0076–0077: Six stated routes connect miracle and veracity: wisdom, power, knowledge/necessity, divine custom, justice and mercy. God cannot coherently require response to an unrecognizable envoy or make the truthful and false claimant indistinguishable. Divine custom is said never to grant persistent, comparable confirming support to a false claimant. The obvious contrary case of an unjust ruler's success is anticipated: that ruler need not claim to be God's messenger; temporary respite for a liar is distinguished from sustained prophetic confirmation. This is an explicit intentional-attestation bridge with discrimination conditions, not the bare inference that a powerful event occurred. Its assertion of history-wide persistence and eventual exposure is not independently tested here.
- L0077–0079: Etymology grounds the prophetic office in being informed by God, not merely informing others. A divine prophet's reports are entirely trustworthy, whereas a nonprophetic inspired person may mix genuine inspiration with satanic input. General وحي includes bees, heavens, Moses' mother, disciples and Joseph as a child. General revelation/inspiration is therefore not sufficient for prophetic rank. The test for fallible inspirations is what prophets have brought. This is a downstream discernment rule that presumes prophecy already identified; it cannot independently establish that initial identification.
- L0079–0082: The author reconstructs and attacks philosophical prophecy through three powers associated with Ibn Sina: intuitive knowledge without learning, imaginative sensory representation, and extraordinary psychical action on matter. He argues these are nonexclusive of prophets and so fail as distinguishing definitions. He also attacks relabeling philosophical soul/intellect concepts with prophetic words such as preserved tablet, angel and dominion, including inconsistent or ambiguous uses attributed to al-Ghazali. Reports of al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, al-Ghazali, al-Suhrawardi, Ibn Sabʿin and Ibn ʿArabi remain polemical source testimony and were not checked against their own writings. Ibn Sina's explanatory project is nevertheless represented as responding to reported extraordinary facts rather than simply inventing them.
- L0081–0082: The author places real external jinn/angel interaction against an exclusively intrapsychic explanation. The universality of reports of jinn is asserted; the text's certainty about an external ontology must not be confused with its independent authentication here. Moral character, news and commands are contrasted along with messenger identity. Quranic testimony to an external trustworthy angel supplies the positive alternative.
- L0082–0084: A substantive excursus distinguishes Adam's probationary garden from the already-created heavenly garden of reward. The received text favours the first's elevated earthly location, cites multiple ordinary earthly gardens in Quranic usage, and links the distinction to Satan's exclusion from heaven after expulsion. It also preserves differing early positions and says الله أعلم بصحة هذا النقل about a report concerning excretion after the forbidden tree. This is a real evidential qualification, not a categorical endorsement of that report. Heaven's creation is affirmed against its denial; the garden-location question is distinguished from whether reward-heaven already exists. This passage has been flagged for critical-edition scan control before relying on its precise attribution.
- L0083–0084: Present postmortem reward/punishment and final resurrection are distinguished through Quranic citations and reports from al-Mughira, ʿAlqama and Saʿid b. Jubayr. The pedagogic point is not to treat recompense as only remote: one's death is a “lesser resurrection,” though bodily restoration remains future.
- L0084–0085: A prophet need not be a messenger in the narrower sense. A messenger is sent to opponents with God's message; a prophet may guide existing believers under an earlier law. A new law is not necessary for messenger status: Joseph, David and Solomon are offered as counterexamples. The source preserves a broad sending that includes prophets as well as the narrower titular رسول.
- L0086–0087: A major distinction separates universal providential sending/will from approved normative mission. Winds, fire and devils may be “sent” causally, whereas commissioned messengers convey commands/news God loves. Acting within divine will is no defence for sinful action. This blocks a simplistic inference from “God caused/allowed it” to “God commands/endorses it,” crucial for the attestation assessment.
- L0088–0089: An adequate proof/sign must entail its object: it cannot occur both with and without that object and still distinguish it. It may be coextensive with or narrower than what it proves, never broader. Common sun/moon phenomena prove a Creator on the author's view but not a particular claimant's veracity. Multiple creatures may indicate the same divine reality; absence of one sign need not imply absence of the object. Conversely, lack of all evidence where an occurrence would necessarily be widely transmitted can refute an alleged public event. Examples include a ruler's death, public succession, a major city and conspicuous local events; an isolated report in such a case is deemed false. This is a concrete negative-evidence test, not unlimited permission to infer nonoccurrence from silence.
- L0089–0094: The author favours direct signs of concrete realities over a universal syllogism yielding only “some producer.” He does not entirely reject categorical/analogical reasoning; he ranks sign-inference as more complete and disputes the unique privilege of categorical syllogism. An extended philological debate then treats آية as sign rather than a mere textual boundary, cluster of letters, or synonym for marvel. He quotes poetry and earlier exegetes, then gives objections and his own readings. A verse is a sign even recited alone; segmentation describes its boundary rather than its evidential essence. “Group under their sign” can explain an apparently collective sense without lexical equivocation. Marvels are a subclass of signs. Eclipses are signs of warning, expressly not effects of a person's birth/death, and jurisprudence on analogous frightening occurrences/earthquakes is noted. The semantic field includes ordinary created signs, extraordinary signs and Quranic units.

### Interim bearing and cautions

This segment materially strengthens the account of how purpose and testimony are meant to connect: normative commission is distinguished from mere causal production, and evidential specificity is stated as an entailment requirement. It also exposes the pressure points: how one establishes a nondeceptive divine wisdom independently, how one knows an apparently extraordinary event is exclusively divine in the relevant sense, and how an unbounded “eventual exposure” claim becomes a finite historical test. The human-message analogy makes attestation intelligible without conventional prearrangement but does not prove that any particular event carries that intention.

Text is preserved as received. Suspect minor forms include بك for ربك near L0073, “بعلم” near L0073–0074, and several compressed/irregular phrases in the philosophical polemic. No unannounced clean edition substitutes for them. The earthy-garden claim is recorded as an actual received passage pending explicit print control.

## Segment 3: windows 007–009, NB-S002-L0095–0117

Read every displayed line in order with END markers. The discussion proceeds from a taxonomy of signs through a worked human-token model to the theology of prophetic signs. L0117 ends inside an answer continued in window 010.

### Argument, voices and non-obvious material

- L0095–0096: The crucial division is between something whose own existence entails another reality and a sign that works through a sign-maker's communicative intention. A creature indicates a creator without first ascertaining that it was intended as evidence, even though the author thinks a knowing/willing God also intends its evidential role. Created things have multiple purposes, so evidential meaning is not claimed to exhaust them. Both kinds are rationally understood. Some entailments are inseparable; some endure for the relevant stable period only, such as stellar positions and landmarks. A mountain may outlast the town it once located, and that particular evidential relation then ceases. This is a useful acknowledgment of contextual and time-bounded indication.
- L0096–0100: The criticism of logic is specific: allegedly exhaustive general/particular classifications exclude coextensive or concretely linked signs; ignorance of another class is not a proof of exhaustive division. In the disputed-drink example, a universal legal proposition alone cannot identify this particular drink as prohibited; the relevant instance must be identified as intoxicating. A hearer's prior knowledge determines whether one, two, three or more premises must be made explicit. Someone not yet accepting Muhammad's authority needs evidences of prophecy before the scriptural legal premise can function for them. The author thereby acknowledges an order-of-justification requirement.
- L0099–0100: Induction without exhaustive coverage or a known necessitating feature cannot establish a universal. Analogical and syllogistic inference share an inferential core: the common feature must be shown to necessitate the judgement; mere resemblance is insufficient. Certainty versus probability depends on evidential matter, not logical form alone. The discussion retains legal techniques for establishing intoxication's relevance, including text, Quranic harms, congruity, concomitance and elimination. This is not blanket dismissal of formal thought or legal analogy.
- L0101–0102: Signs of illness, emotional sounds, words, roads, mountains, call to prayer and public religious practices diversify the account. A natural cry/laugh indicates a state differently from an intended utterance. Stories of withholding an attack upon hearing the call to prayer or seeing a mosque illustrate operative public signs. Those are narrated legal-historical examples, not fresh authorisation for violence. Qibla direction can be known by sun, stars, winds and routes.
- L0103–0105: Gestures, writing, brands, military tokens and private agreed signals illustrate deliberately instituted signs, while footprints, resemblance and involuntary physiognomic effects illustrate noncommunicative signs. A word's intentional naming can misrepresent its object: naming an idol “god” no more confers divinity than calling a liar a prophet makes him one. Philological disputes over اسم, وسم, سيما and سمو are retained and partly reconciled by levels of derivation. The author also declares a report universalizing his ablution practice to earlier prophets weak, while distinguishing a report concerning prayer times; this is an internal authenticity judgement, not checked hadith criticism here.
- L0105–0107: Intentional signs need not rest on prior conventional agreement. An exclusive garment, ring, turban, sandals or private shared event can reveal the sender through known circumstances. Ibn ʿAbbas is quoted on a household recognising a genuine message through a personal token. Prophetic anecdotes about Saʿd b. ʿUbada's battle standard and Abu Hurayra's sandals serve as examples. The inference has two explicit stages: the sender supplied the token, and he supplied it to certify this envoy. Crucially, alternatives are not ignored: a lost/stolen ring, death of its possessor, unauthorised sealing (attributed to Marwan and ʿUthman), or a repair/sealing task may defeat the inference. If a competing possibility is strong, recipients suspend judgement; if contextual information excludes it, they assent. Only after that comes the distinct possibility that an authorised envoy lies about some content; the passage places the responsibility for sending such a person on the sender. Nonverbal action can function as informing/certifying, sometimes more forcefully than an utterance, while words have greater scope for absent or complex matters.
- L0108–0109: A prophetic sign must entail the truth it signifies, known necessarily or by reasoning reaching necessary premises. “Unusual event” alone is not an adequate definition. “No one countered it here and now” is inadequate because false claimants/sorcerers may also go unopposed; “never countered anywhere” poses a knowledge-of-absence problem. The author explicitly raises this epistemic difficulty rather than leaving it invisible. Extraordinary character and incapacity for contrary reproduction are consequences/requirements of genuine signs, not a sufficient identifier when abstracted from truth-signifying force.
- L0109: A sign need not be exclusive to one prophet. Another true prophet or follower may exhibit a similar sign because that testimony supports the same prophetic truth. The operative exclusion is of a false claimant or opponent as such, not every other person. The author says the moon's division, Moses' staff and the she-camel can produce immediate recognition of divine attestation; these examples rely on received reports and the asserted epistemic character of the events. The argument at this point defines what a true sign must be more easily than it provides an external event-authentication procedure.
- L0110–0112: Divine communication uses public enacted signs and verbal reports, with messengers/books mediating because most cannot hear God directly. Human distance-messaging supplies an analogy. The author again separates questions of source, commissioned transmission and communicative purpose, and admits that some cases require inquiry. Personal acquaintance with a truthful person's character may suffice without an extraordinary event; prophetic assent is not universally conditioned on having a miracle. Conversely, a message can conflict with known facts about its alleged sender, or apparent motives for money, position or marriage can reveal a pretender. Motive/character and content therefore supplement physical performance. The passage claims numerous recognizable signs of truth/falsehood rather than one algorithm.
- L0113–0114: Contradictory tokens cannot both conclusively certify incompatible claims. The second ring for repair illustration makes it clear why shared access defeats apparent exclusive evidential authority. Repeated tokens sent with different genuine envoys, however, are compatible and may constitute the sender's customary authentication practice. The distinction is between compatible multiple missions and contradictory endorsement, not isolated rarity versus repetition.
- L0115–0117: Three positions on extraordinary character are surveyed, including al-Baqillani's reported arguments. Quotes attributed to him allow ordinary death or meteorological events to become signs when tied to a claimant's challenge and withheld from rivals; Ibn Taymiyya responds that the specified occasion is itself extraordinary. He also alleges an internal inconsistency between a universal consensus claim and acknowledgment of dissent. His criticism of Ashʿari-type approaches is that they assimilate prophetic, saintly, magical and natural extraordinary phenomena, leave “lack of opposition” insufficiently specified, and permit untenable implications under unrestricted divine freedom. These are polemical reports, quotations and author's reductio; they are not all concessions the named opponents themselves expressly make. The author says making the prophet's own claim part of the sign risks using the conclusion within its proof. The response to opponents' end-times example continues in window 010.

### Interim bearing

The strongest newly supplied resource is a two-stage, defeater-sensitive account of intentional attestation, together with separation of normative commission from general providence. It would be unfair to attribute to this section an undiscriminating “wonder therefore God therefore this prophet” argument. It would also be unfair to declare the theological application established merely because the human analogy is intelligible: the human examples rely on independently known sender identity, habitual conduct, exclusive possession and finite rival explanations. Whether corresponding divine-source premises are secured remains the principal issue. The author independently recognises several relevant problems (generic marvels, local absence of rivalry, mere shared resemblance, exhaustive classification by ignorance, and premise requirements varying by audience).

## Segment 4: windows 010–012, NB-S002-L0118–0138

Read continuously through the assigned final END marker. The assignment ends at a complete concluding paragraph within an ongoing work. No credit is claimed for NB-S002-L0139 onward.

### Argument, voices and non-obvious material

- L0118–0120: Extraordinary portents may signify divine warning, final judgement, or the truth of a prior prophetic prediction; not every sign must occur simultaneously with a claim/challenge. Ibn Taymiyya says most of Muhammad's signs were not introduced through a formal challenge, and Quranic challenge answered an allegation of fabrication rather than functioning as the initial definition of evidence. Evidence can precede birth/commission or follow death. A claimant's using an event cannot make a nonevidential event evidential merely by declaration. Both local unopposedness and a purported universal absence of opposition remain inadequate; regional unfamiliarity is not species-level exclusivity.
- L0120–0122: Saints' wonders, if genuinely linked to fidelity, belong to the evidence for their prophets. The source distinguishes unique kinds of major signs from smaller/shared kinds and cases differentiated by degree or manner (food multiplication, fire protection and transport). A particular prophet can also have an individually distinctive sign. The account allows revival of the dead among multiple prophets and followers. The distinctions are asserted through exemplars; the reader must not turn them into an independently verified inventory of events or known power-limits of jinn. The source also concedes that al-Juwayni/al-Razi's appeal to immediate recognition of divine attestation may be right in itself even if inconsistent with their other principles. Its criticism is therefore partly intramural consistency criticism, not universal denial of opponents' successful awareness.
- L0122–0124: A sign supports the truth of the proposition, and thus everyone truthfully asserting that proposition, rather than only a self-claimant. An earlier prophet's prediction may strengthen later attestation. Hearing “I am sent” establishes the occurrence of the claim, not its truth. If a contradictory claimant supplies the same supposedly exclusive sign, that particular sign fails; it does not thereby prove the prophet false or defeat every independent indication. The author emphasizes that evidence need not be biconditional. These are important controls on overclaiming and on the accusation of circularity: the relevant conclusion is truth of commission, not merely observed self-ascription.
- L0124–0126: Falsehood, deliberate lying, error and unauthorised assertion receive an extended discussion. The source reports the view that a mistaken but sincere speaker occupies an intermediate category, and cites the Prophet's corrected prayer-memory report as an example considered in that debate. Ibn Taymiyya then argues that one who asserts beyond knowledge/entitlement may be called a liar even without intending deceit. This is a distinction between moral/forensic classification and bare correspondence, although the wording sometimes compresses them. A sexual accusation without the required four witnesses is condemned even if its accuser privately saw the act: it invites a public judgement the speaker has not authorised the audience to make. Disclosure creates additional social harms; confidential advice on repentance, legal confession and complete authorised testimony are distinguished from public rumor. This excursus contributes a norm of responsible assertion, privacy and audience entitlement, not just an abstract two-valued theory of reports. It does not authorise applying the quoted penalties in any present case.
- L0125–0126 textual issue: The received string after the Abu al-Sanabil example runs “كذب من قالها إن له لأجرين أنه جاهد مجاهد” without an intelligible transition, and “المجاهدين/المهاجدة” occurs where the surrounding discussion concerns publicly exposing sin. These irregularities are retained; no fine-grained hadith attribution or ruling is built on this exact corrupted chain.
- L0126–0129: Not only an explicit contradictor but a person withholding assent lies outside the stipulated prophetic/follower sign class. Abu Muslim's protection from al-Aswad's fire and Khalid's reported drinking of poison serve as exemplary follower-attestations. They remain source reports, not historical or practical evidence that poison/fire is safe. The correct classification of a saintly wonder still requires distinguishing it from magical/demonic effects; the author explicitly says that further task is needed and refers to fuller treatment elsewhere.
- L0130–0133: Extraordinary skill, medicine, crafts, poetry, skilled swimming, physiognomic tracking, weaponry and horsemanship show why local wonder is not divine attestation. What one region cannot reproduce may be an acquired, recurrent art elsewhere. Magic and soothsaying are placed in a distinct jinn-mediated class. Superficial resemblances explain mistaken comparisons: verse has breaks like the Quran; the soothsayer sometimes has hidden news; words may affect hearers; prophecy may depart from prevailing custom. The author requires discriminating differences rather than denying all resemblance. His contrasting labels of “sorcerer” and “madman” also expose instability in hostile characterisations.
- L0132–0135: The source presents demonic activity as adapting to its audience's religious expectations, including apparent saintly piety. Moral fruits, manner of worship and fidelity to prophets matter, not performance alone. An esoteric saintly path surpassing prophetic instruction is rejected. The account appeals to Quranic descriptions of trustworthy angels and lying demons and alleges that philosophical psychism and unrestricted occasionalism erase important distinctions. The existence and exhaustive explanatory adequacy of that supernatural classification are not independently established by the continuous reading.
- L0135–0137: Solomon's coerced sovereign command over jinn is distinguished from reciprocal magical service based on indulging their wishes. Muhammad's and other prophets' mission concerns willing obedience to God, and is judged a more complete mode than royal control. Solomon remains a prophet/messenger as well as king; servant-messenger versus prophet-king is a distinction of mode, not wholesale exclusion of Joseph, David or Solomon. Joseph's initially nonconfrontational invitation and Muhammad's escalating oral confrontation are compared; Moses' imposed demand to release Israel supplies another political mode. These passages preserve agency, coercion, purpose and institutional differences within prophecy itself.
- L0138: The assigned portion ends by allowing “extraordinary,” “marvel” and “inimitable” as necessary qualities but refusing them as sufficient unrestricted definitions. “Outside the ordinary” must be relative to all nonprophetic people, not only one locality. What is habitual to prophets alone, including true inaccessible divine news, can be especially diagnostic rather than losing force through repetition. A sign may certify someone's truthful report of another prophet as well as a prophet's self-report. This is a category-level cumulative account, with truth and commission as its target.
