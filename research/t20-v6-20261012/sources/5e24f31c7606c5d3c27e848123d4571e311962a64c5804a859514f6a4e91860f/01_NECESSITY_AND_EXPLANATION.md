# 1. Necessary Being, intrinsic sufficiency, and the structure of explanation

**Claim type:** source-sensitive philosophical reconstruction plus separately identified mathematical analysis.  
**Main sources:** A01–A04, A12–A13, M01–M02. Exact access and pagination limits are in the source ledger.  
**Relationship to Dot:** extends rather than replaces the anti-brute-source return. The existing conditional bearer theorem is not rebranded as a newly proved result.

## 1.1 The source question has a positive answer before the philosophical dispute is settled

The strongest relevant Taymiyyan material is not merely the sentence that a dependent thing depends. The contextual return to *Darʾ* 3:288–289 explicitly confronts the proposed third existential case: a thing whose existence is neither through itself nor through another. Its explanation of existence through oneself excludes self-production. The relevant contrast is between intrinsically sufficient actuality and received actuality, not between a thing producing itself and someone else producing it. [A01]

That makes a material difference to the research. An objection saying that Ibn Taymiyyah never considered an uncaused brute original is textually inadequate. His response can still be challenged, but at the level of its claimed rational warrant. The source's directness does not turn the response into a theorem of unrestricted propositional logic.

The supplementary passage at *Darʾ* 3:153–155 is especially useful because it puts existential dependence and the rejection of reified nonexistent essences into the same argument. It also distinguishes the production of existence from the alleged production of nonexistence. The text does not require a separately subsisting possible essence waiting for an external existence-component. [A02]

**Locator cue:** «لا نريد به أنه في الخارج نفس ثابتة» — “We do not mean that there is an established self outside [the mind].” This is a cue to the anti-reification qualification, not a freestanding critical edition or a complete translation.

## 1.2 The dependence principle needs an explicit subject and respect

Here is a reconstruction in deliberately non-Avicennian language. It is our analytical formulation, not a quotation:

1. Some determinate actuality obtains.
2. If an actuality is received in a specified existential respect, the fact that it obtains calls for an actual adequate source in that respect.
3. A series whose members exercise only received efficacy does not gain unreceived efficacy merely because it is described collectively or extended indefinitely.
4. An adequate complete account of the received actuality therefore includes an actuality that is not received in that respect.
5. If that actuality has no existential supplier, its existence is through itself rather than an unexplained acquisition of existence.
6. Intrinsic sufficiency of this sort is incompatible with existential failure through an intrinsic insufficiency; the source's stronger conclusion is necessity through itself.

Steps 3, 5 and 6 carry philosophical work. Each can be assessed without pretending the text lacks an argument. In particular, step 3 must distinguish an explanatory chain from a time-series, and step 6 must distinguish intrinsic sufficiency from the merely extensional fact that a graph has an empty predecessor set.

There are at least three ways to dispute this reconstruction. A brute-fact critic can deny the demand for complete existential adequacy. An infinitist can argue that distributed received explanations suffice without a first unreceived contributor. An essentialist can accept an original actuality while denying that its intrinsic nature necessitates existence. These are not all the same objection. Answering the first by defining `Received` differently does not answer the third.

Conversely, a purported infinitist does not yet explain the whole order by assigning every vertex a predecessor. The assignment specifies an explanatory architecture. What gives that architecture actual productive adequacy is precisely the issue. An infinite diagram can be a good mathematical object while failing as the proposed original explanation.

### Conditional proof and premise warrant

Let `Actual(g)`, `Received(g)` and `Necessary(g)` be independently interpreted predicates. Assume:

- an actual original witness `g`, so `Actual(g) ∧ ¬Received(g)`;
- `Actual(g) ∧ ¬Necessary(g) → Received(g)`.

Then `Necessary(g)` follows by contradiction. This is the familiar inferential core retained in Orthemology, not a new discovery here. Its significance depends on the second premise having substantive meaning and warrant. Defining `¬Necessary` to mean `Received` would make the inference cheap but would abandon the intended modal question.

A better philosophical test asks what the alleged contingent original has, and what it lacks. If it has sufficient actual intrinsic reality yet could fail to exist, is this merely an unexplained modal annotation, or is there a worked account of existential sufficiency not entailing necessity? If the affirmative account claims this is impossible, what makes its inference rationally compelling rather than a restatement of its conclusion? Both questions belong in the same assessment.

## 1.3 A quotation verification changes the agent's first argument

The latest research agent's first Arabic quotation is located in *Darʾ* 8:116–117. Its immediate context distinguishes the need for an explanation of actual existence from the much less secure assertion that nonexistence needs a symmetrical determinant. The short quotation is genuine; the surrounding balanced-scale gloss is not a safe summary of that context. [A03]

This is a useful correction to our earlier supplement as well. The earlier dossier pointed to nearby 8:120–131 discussions. The new direct locator is closer to the actual quoted sentence. Neither location should be silently substituted for the other as a byte-exact quotation match.

**Consequential point:** absence of an existential producer is not itself an efficient producer of absence. A scale is a physical object with a conservation and equilibrium model. Its behaviour does not supply a metaphysical probability distribution over existence and nonexistence. Calling the two “equally possible” is not the same as demonstrating a probability of one half, and possibility alone is not a causal mechanism.

The argument can therefore be strengthened by dropping the unnecessary symmetry. What needs defending is why this actual dependent occurrence cannot be existentially adequate through nothing, not why a nonexistent thing had equal odds of materialising.

## 1.4 The epistemic order of proof is part of the dispute

The first-principles passage in *Darʾ* 3:309 treats demonstrative knowledge as ultimately resting on non-discursive starting points. Elsewhere in the relevant received text, recognition of the dependence of a particular writing or building is not made to wait for an independently proved universal syllogistic major premise. [A04; A01 contextual return]

This is not an exemption from criticism. It changes the kind of criticism that is appropriate. A principle offered as foundational cannot be assessed solely by demanding an indefinitely longer derivation from still more basic principles. Relevant tests include intelligibility, stability under accurate specification, whether the proposed denial is genuinely understood, cross-case coherence, and whether a competing explanation explains the same actuality rather than substituting a thin predicate.

Nor does failure to persuade a particular interlocutor prove the believer lacks warrant. First-order knowledge, an articulate argument, and a dialectically persuasive demonstration are distinct achievements. This distinction also prevents an overreaction in the other direction: declaring a premise evident does not establish that every audience understands or knows it, and does not license dismissing a developed objection without analysis.

## 1.5 A recent PSR proof exposes an unusually relevant witness problem

**New source:** Stephen Harrop, *Sufficient Reason Vindicated*, first published online in 2025 and assigned to *Analytic Philosophy* 67(3), 276–282 in 2026. The paper's definitions and sections 4–6 were directly inspected. It concerns necessitating conditions, not a proved causal originator or personal God. [M02]

The detailed critique is in `formal/MATHEMATICAL_DIAGNOSTICS.md`, D1–D2. Two observations matter here.

First, if a positive fact and its exhaustive negative alternative both require the same fact `R`, then `R` obtains necessarily. Removing it is not licensed while those requirements and exhaustivity are held fixed. A recombination step must therefore be justified in a way that does not silently discard the necessary-condition premises. The paper's attempt to secure a world containing only selected requisites also needs its own justification; failure of PSR does not immediately give arbitrary world-pruning powers.

Second, the later weaker principle supplies **some** fact implying nonexistence whenever the positive fact is absent. That is a world-dependent existential witness. Deleting the particular witness selected at one world does not show that every alternative witness is absent at another. The packet supplies an exact three-world interpretation of this local gap, and a general conditional repair with a uniform witness or a shared requisite of all relevant witnesses.

These are local analytical findings, not a verdict that the whole published paper has been refuted. They are unusually relevant to Orthemology because they reproduce the exact danger of promoting `for every world, some witness` into `one witness for every world`. A rigorous source-grounded Necessary Being argument must earn that move rather than rely on its notational appearance.

## 1.6 What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled at the inspected source level:** the brute-third-option objection is directly engaged; the quoted existence-determinant sentence has a more precise locator; the source explicitly resists the symmetrical nonexistence argument and the reification of nonexistent essences.

**Settled mathematically:** the common-requisite lemma and the local varying-witness counterinterpretation, at their stated semantics. The finite checks independently exercise the written arguments; no new Lean execution is claimed.

**Still philosophical:** whether all adequate existential explanations must provide intrinsically sufficient original actuality; whether that actuality must be borne by an individual; and whether intrinsic sufficiency entails the intended necessity through oneself.

**Practical next discriminator for Dot:** reconstruct the strongest source argument for step 5–6 above using the same particular witness, then identify whether the rival denies explanatory adequacy, the sufficiency-to-necessity bridge, or only a weaker translation. This is a narrower task than restarting the whole cosmological programme.
