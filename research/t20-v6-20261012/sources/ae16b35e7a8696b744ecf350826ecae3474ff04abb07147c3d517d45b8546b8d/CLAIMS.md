## Narrow proposed increment

The explicit assembly inference from a collectively complete plurality and a real composite to a singular original complete source is tested against preserved member attributions. If the whole's ultimate account covers a member's proper unborrowed productive exercise at the same target and respect, the inherited exclusion identifies the whole with that member. A composition inference cannot retain both distinctness and all those attributions. This is an application of the inherited theorem, not a new theorem.

The application discriminates four responses: a whole with genuinely derivative member agency; a genuine whole with only narrower joint efficacy; intrinsic aspects rather than separate source-bearers; or a defended alteration of joint-possession/productive-accounting semantics. It prevents both automatic promotion of a plurality and indiscriminate rejection of complex sourcehood.

The relevant prior-art positions are identified in the article's cited, charged passages; this application claims no historical priority over them. The programme-specific application concerns original provision of the admitted correction's receiving modes.

## Genuine change to research direction

A future composite-source argument should not merely add a whole to an already original plurality. It must warrant one of the four attribution interpretations above and show why that interpretation applies to the actual target. This is a more specific task than demanding a bearer in the abstract. It does not by itself satisfy that task.
