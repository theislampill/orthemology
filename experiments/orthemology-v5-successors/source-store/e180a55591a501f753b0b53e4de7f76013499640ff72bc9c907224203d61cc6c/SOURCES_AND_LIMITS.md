## How the evidence bears on the verdict

Primary texts establish what arguments and distinctions the authors actually offer. They do not by themselves establish that the arguments are true, that their claims of certainty are universally transferable, or that their historical/scientific illustrations are verified. Several works share authors, passages or traditions; their repetition is not independent confirmation.

The audit's affirmative actual-common-source judgement is an independent comparison of the retained background, source reasons and strongest articulated objections. It disagrees with the prior assessment's final weighting while preserving its local objections and evidence. The sufficiency standard is philosophical and defeasible; no numeric confidence, human review, complete manuscript collation or universal persuasiveness is claimed.
