# Governing criterion repair and contextual transport

## Result and scope

The deliberately constructed C0-to-C1 checker experiment is **warranted retained-standard repair**. Within that experiment it corrects the governing acceptance criterion, not merely a displayed output: C0 admits a provably wrong equivalence class while C1 exactly characterizes the retained target relation. The source file's extra-newline defect and its exact recovery actually occurred in this restart; there is no claim that C0 governed that initial file write or the historical programme. The experiment does not establish radical objective correction relative to the actor's complete authority basis, which contains the retained fidelity standard used to assess the checker.

The immutable source reasons and the proof of the replacement's adequacy can be reused by another recipient. Actual source identification, current recipient permission, the version to which a command applies, and faithful execution must remain warranted under that recipient's circumstances. A copied derivation is not a current grant or a live interlock. This is a substantive limitation even when both recipients agree on the target, the correct replacement criterion, the exact payload, and the independent proof.

The general mathematical result is a sound **cover-and-preserve** rule for recipient evidence classes. Its necessity is asserted only for uniform transfer over an explicitly unrestricted predicate class. For a particular conclusion, an unmatched recipient history can independently satisfy the conclusion, so failure of coverage alone does not prove failure of warrant.

## 1 The actual source-bound correction

Let T be the repository/path/commit triple recorded in `sources/SOURCE_HASH_RECORDS.json`, and let S be its 3,013 retrieved UTF-8 bytes. A source-recovery operation in this restart first wrote S followed by an additional LF byte. Its SHA256 was `c7bf53d6d0fcb6a51e4b31fb0c326137108f158b28398d50929893030d628390`. The exact connector content restored S, with SHA256 `e4ae2e2751535e625f245a1bb2f2e4f2f4c447a55c859e1b1c94dbbf48adf359`, matching the Sixth historical record. The connector's Git blob SHA also agrees with the locally calculated Git blob hash.

The disagreement is about bytes, not about which file should be used, which text looks better, or whether an appended blank line alters the document's argument. The target was fixed before the comparison. No different commit or normalized surrogate is substituted after the failed check.

Now explicitly instantiate an acceptance criterion:

- C0(x): stripping trailing LF bytes from x and S yields equal results.
- C1(x): x equals S byte for byte.

C0 is an experimental weak checker deliberately specified for this audit; it is not asserted to have governed the historical programme. The observed file-creation mismatch makes its failure concrete. C0 accepts S plus one LF even though the retained task requires S. C1 rejects that sequence and accepts S. Replacing C0 with C1 repairs the governing acceptance condition across all candidate strings, not just this one offending output.

### Full token basis at the transition

The actor's basis includes:

1. The declared recovery purpose and retained standard M: recover this source's exact bytes and disclose a historical-digest change rather than normalize it away.
2. The target triple T and connector-backed grounds identifying S.
3. Ordinary finite-string reasoning, exact comparison, and the already supplied historical digest.
4. Local authority to prepare and amend the assigned research artifacts; no authority to mutate GitHub.
5. Source-custody and reporting obligations: preserve the original reference and disclose relevant discrepancies.
6. The ability to replace a subordinate checker when its defect is shown under these retained resources.
7. The disputed subordinate operational criterion C0.

M is not generated by C0's acceptance verdict. The source observation and byte comparison do not obtain their relevant warrant from C0 either. They are independent of the disputed criterion in the respect at issue. They are not independent ultimate causes, independent GitHub witnesses, or guarantees that the document's assertions are true.

Removing C0 does not remove the reasons for C1. Removing M does remove the claim that newline differences are defects of this task, even though the factual byte difference remains. Removing source identity leaves exact equality to an unidentified byte array, not faithful recovery of T. These are distinct deletion tests.

### Why the replacement is legitimate and how far uniqueness goes

For exact recovery of S, an adequate acceptance criterion C must satisfy, for every candidate x, C(x) if and only if x=S. C1 satisfies this directly. Every other adequate criterion has the same acceptance extension; its implementation and syntax may differ. This is extensional uniqueness relative to the fixed task, not proof that exact recovery is a categorical obligation for every person.

A criterion that rejects every candidate is sound in a one-way sense but not complete for this recovery task. It cannot masquerade as a completed repair. A normalized-content task could legitimately use a weaker relation, but changing to that task would change the respect of assessment rather than repair exact recovery under its retained standard.

The actual local file was repaired. Other recipients in the executable illustration are typed model fixtures A and B, not real people whose permissions are inferred. Cross-recipient and multi-version mutation results below are conditional model results; no external recipient's state was changed.

## 2 Lossy representations and version transport

Let f map source representations into a recipient's representation. If the recipient accepts x when f(x)=f(S), that criterion respects exact source fidelity at S precisely when the fiber of f over f(S) contains no other candidate: for every x, f(x)=f(S) implies x=S. The reverse implication x=S implies f(x)=f(S) is automatic.

**Proof.** If the fiber is a singleton, every recipient acceptance entails exact identity. Conversely, any x distinct from S in the same fiber is a false acceptance. This proves the fixed-target equivalence. Requiring it for every S is exactly injectivity of f on the declared candidate domain.

The Lean development checks both equivalences. On the bounded domain of the 40 byte strings of length zero through three over {A, B, LF}, stripping trailing LF produces 36 ordered false-accept pairs; one-byte prefixing produces 468. Identity, byte reversal and base64 encoding have none in that domain. The general injectivity theorem is mathematical; these counts are only finite illustrations. No collision-resistance theorem for SHA256 is inferred from them.

A digest is useful for identifying and checking a source packet under the usual cryptographic and provenance assumptions. It is not logically injective over all byte strings and does not authenticate its own source label. The implementation uses exact payload comparison against the supplied source bytes; hashes are additional audit records, not the deductive basis of exact equality.

There are also two distinct versions. The source version is the immutable commit in T. The recipient's mutable draft has its own revision and authorization epoch. Two draft revisions may contain identical bytes while differing in current permissions or in which outstanding operation is allowed. Consequently payload equality does not erase version identity.

## 3 Three different things that can be copied

### A Derivation

A proof of the conditional statement “if this source reference is authentic and the task is exact recovery, then C1 is adequate” can be copied without making a new argument. A recipient can check its inferential steps. The scope and premise dependencies must travel with it.

### B Warrant for the premises

The recipient needs an entitled route to the actual reference, the operative meaning of exact recovery and any factual premises. A faithful copy of a source witness may transmit testimony or preserve access to the same evidence. It does not create a second independent historical witness. Two receipts descended from the same canonical object remain one source family for any independence claim about that object.

This does not make all copying epistemically useless. For the local serialization defect, inspecting the retained raw source and comparing bytes can defeat C0 even though both copies descend from one source. Independence is claim-relative. What fails is inferring independent authentication or a new witness count from duplication alone.

### C Permission and causal ability

An actor may know exactly which criterion and payload are correct while lacking permission to change a particular recipient's draft. An actor may have permission but lack a mechanism that preserves the source or checks the current epoch. Proof transport, authority delegation and faithful execution are separate relations. An exact same output, a shared label, or consensus does not identify them.

The access-control literature already separates a trusted computing base, authentication and authorization, and analyzes principals and delegation scopes. This work applies that distinction to the declared correction case; it does not claim novelty for it. [Abadi et al., A Calculus for Access Control in Distributed Systems, §§1 and 5](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/gdp/publications/Calculus_for_Access_Control.pdf).

## 4 Evidence classes and the transport theorem

Let E_A be the source assessor's class of evidence-compatible histories and E_B the recipient's. Let P and Q be the source and recipient versions of the judgment. Define robust evidence-relative validity as truth in every history in the relevant class. This is a strong model property, not a definition of all ordinary fallible warrant. To infer actual-world truth from it, the actual history must belong to the declared class. To infer the actor is warranted in relying on it, the class construction and its interpretation require their own warrant.

Let R relate source histories to recipient histories. Assume:

1. Source validity: every a in E_A satisfies P.
2. Backward coverage: for every b in E_B, some a in E_A is related by R to b.
3. Judgment preservation: whenever a and b are in their respective classes and R(a,b), P(a) implies Q(b).

**Theorem.** Q is valid throughout E_B.

**Proof.** Choose any b in E_B. Coverage supplies a in E_A with R(a,b). Source validity gives P(a); preservation gives Q(b). Since b was arbitrary, Q holds throughout E_B.

The relation need not say that the two histories are identical. It can carry a proved conversion, a new recipient's genuine grant, and a changed version when the relevant judgment is preserved. But a source/recipient map invented solely because the desired conclusion would follow provides no independent warrant for coverage or preservation.

### Exact scope of necessity

For this fixed Q, backward coverage is not necessary. An unmatched recipient history might independently satisfy Q. The implementation and Lean proof include that exception.

For **uniform transport over every predicate pair P,Q** satisfying R-preservation, coverage is necessary and sufficient. Sufficiency is the theorem above. For necessity, choose P to be true everywhere and Q(b) to mean “some a in E_A is R-related to b.” This Q is preserved by R. Uniform transport then says that every b in E_B satisfies Q, which is exactly coverage. This uses an explicitly rich predicate class including the relation-image test; it is not a necessity claim for one preselected substantive norm.

### Counterexample that keeps the independent semantic premises fixed

Let E_A contain one history where the source, criterion, payload and current local grant are as audited. Let E_B contain two histories with the same source bytes, the same retained fidelity standard, the same proof of C1, the same payload, and the same received certificate. In b0 the recipient grant is still current at intervention; in b1 it was revoked after observation. R matches the audited source history only to b0. Preservation on every matched pair is true; forward copying of the source proof succeeds. Yet the proposed unconditional judgment “this recipient may now apply this patch” is false in b1.

No semantic premise is withdrawn. No ambiguity about which output is correct is introduced. What fails is the proposal to inherit a present recipient authorization from a source-time proof without covering the newly admissible recipient history. If the proposal already assumes independently warranted current permission in every b, then b1 is excluded and this is not a counterexample to that strengthened conditional theorem. It remains a counterexample to obtaining that premise merely by copying the old certificate.

The generic observation-class obstruction was already available in the Sixth sources. The added discipline here is the exact separation of transportable semantic reasons from nontransported contextual authority, together with the coverage theorem's quantifier boundary and a source-bound executable realization. It is not advertised as a new information-theoretic impossibility.

## 5 Guarded execution and what it adds

The reference model's command binds:

- actor and destination
- source repository, commit and path
- criterion identifier and exact replacement payload
- expected draft revision and authorization epoch
- operation scope
- observation time and lease end

At the atomic step the guard also consults the active grant, its recipient and target bindings, its operation, its validity interval and current revocation state. The source bytes and earlier draft history are retained. A successful mutation installs the exact source payload into the derived draft, advances its revision and appends the prior draft to history. A failed check returns the unchanged state.

The criterion identifier is a protocol discriminator. Its string is not proof that arbitrary code implements C1. The reference model independently performs exact comparison. Likewise the typed Grant record denotes a warranted active grant in the model; merely fabricating that record does not establish a real permission.

**One-step result.** If the trusted guard accepts under its actual current inputs, the step is authorized according to the declared grant relation, satisfies the version and time bindings, installs the exact target bytes, and preserves the source and prior history.

**Mediated-persistence result.** Once the draft equals the source, any finite sequence of further steps through this same faithful guard leaves it equal to the source: a rejected step is the identity, and an accepted step writes the same source. Induction proves the claim. This says nothing about bypassing writers or a corrupted guard.

**Liveness boundary.** If every attempt is rejected because permission is revoked or versions keep changing, safety may hold while repair never happens. Eventual restoration needs an eligible successful attempt during a sufficiently stable interval and a scheduling/delivery condition. It cannot be inferred from the no-bad-write property.

HTTP's strong validators and If-Match preconditions are an established version-sensitive pattern for avoiding stale overwrites. Their use is a relevant design comparison; it does not establish the legitimacy of this grant store or the fidelity of this implementation. [RFC 9110, §§8.8 and 13.1.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-13.1.1).

HTTP Message Signatures likewise requires application-specific choices about covered components and replay defenses; a signature can remain valid when an unsigned part changes. The present model binds the relevant context explicitly rather than treating an authentic old string as present permission. [RFC 9421, §7.2.2 and Appendix B.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9421.html#section-7.2.2).

## 6 Relation to the dynamic repair lane

The dynamic lane's separately frozen family contains n authenticated root modules and a lifetime-union taint set of size at most B. A q-root intervention is a live non-bypassable conjunction of actual gates, and an r-root revocation becomes effective under an independently justified institution that keeps the old lease authorized until completion. Its overlap condition q+r>n+B supplies an untainted shared gate; availability q,r≤n−B gives n≥3B+1 at q=r=2B+1. These are conditional interface facts supplied by that lane, not independently re-proved in this report.

The criterion result can supply the target identity and the justified replacement payload to that mechanism. It cannot supply the institution's permission semantics, a fault commitment, or the physical non-bypass property. If a person's consent is immediately revocable independently of that institution, the old lease may become unauthorized before the gate learns of it. The source-fidelity proof does not resolve that mismatch. The immediate-revocation counterexample therefore remains live under that alternative authority contract.

Conversely, accepting the dynamic institution does not ground objective fidelity or prove Candidate E's full-basis existence premise. The two local results compose only through these explicit typed assumptions. A common predicate or shared command format is not metaphysical integration.

## 7 Defeater discipline

The counterexamples and deletion tests have different logical roles:

1. **C0 false acceptance:** keeps the exact source, task and byte semantics fixed; demonstrates the governing criterion's defect.
2. **Recipient change:** keeps the source, proof and payload fixed; an A-bound grant does not authorize B. This defeats automatic grant copying, not a theorem already supplied with a valid B grant.
3. **Version change with identical bytes:** keeps content truth fixed; a revision-bound old action is rejected. The stale-revision deletion prevents identical content from laundering an obsolete intervention.
4. **Immediate revocation:** keeps current source and normative fidelity reasons fixed; a formerly valid grant ceases to authorize action. Old disclosure alone does not rule this out.
5. **Lossy representation:** keeps exact-recovery obligations fixed; a many-to-one map collapses correct and incorrect candidates. Calling their normalized forms equal does not repair that respect.
6. **Common-root duplication:** keeps the source evidence and its warrant available; duplicate descendants add no independent source root. This does not deny their usefulness for checking local serialization.
7. **Corrupt common gate:** keeps source truth, criterion adequacy and the correct command fixed; an unfaithful executor writes a bad draft. This deletes the mechanism-integrity premise and demonstrates its necessity. It is not a counterexample satisfying the full positive theorem.
8. **No successful window:** keeps every guard faithful and every rejection safe; repair can remain incomplete. This deletes availability/stability, not safety.

No counterexample earns its force by changing the independently warranted fidelity standard after inspecting the answer. No positive result wins by silently changing immediate consent into delayed institutional revocation, assuming a fresh oracle, or excluding a corrupt gate from the support budget.

## 8 Foundational and R5 accounting

The inputs consumed here are the retained target-first norm resources, a source identification route, a specific local authority contract, elementary reasoning, and an explicit execution mechanism. Necessary-source N2, productive P1, bearer identification P2, original cognitive possession, essential truthful agency T0, Wisdom and unity are not premises of the proofs or the implementation. Their deletion leaves the local result intact.

The canonical R5 control retains separately scoped tracks linked through typed interfaces unless independently warranted bridges change the exact world-directed consequence. This case is realizable by a separate source-observation track, a criterion-proof track, and a permission/execution track connected by the declared interface. No common metaphysical bearer follows. The new guard assumptions are not retroactively declared admitted R5 resources, and their engineering benefit is not an R5 defeat. No MEN7 transcendental bridge or MEN8 strongest-union common-model claim is made.

## 9 Present disposition

The result is a concrete and generalizable local improvement: a defective criterion is replaced on reasons not inherited wholly from itself, and the scope of those reasons under copying is made exact. It is **retained-standard repair**, with conditional recipient transport and conditional execution. The stronger full-basis revision claim remains unresolved because this instance does not satisfy its radicality premise. The independent canonical owner and historical packet retain their existing audit/adoption status.
