# Infinitesimal closure versus finite-time sufficiency

The central additional lesson is stronger than the familiar statement that moments can fail to determine a measure.

For unnormalized tilting, the generator acts on polynomial observables by A p(x)=-x p(x), so the vector space of all polynomials is invariant under every finite number of applications of A. Its expectations therefore generate an algebraically closed infinite hierarchy. Under normalization, the explicit additional term m_1 m_r makes the hierarchy nonlinear but still coordinatewise closed on the full moment record.

Neither observation licenses existence of a single-valued finite-time evolution on the moment record. The actual unnormalized pullback is S_u p(x)=exp(-u*x)p(x), and normalized expectations additionally divide by the pullback of the constant one. These are not polynomial functions for positive u. Expanding the exponential as a formal power series does not justify exchanging integration and the sum: the absolute moment sum for the lognormal initial law is sum u^n exp(n^2/2)/n!, which diverges for every positive u. The signed Stieltjes perturbation annihilates all polynomials but need not annihilate these finite-time pullbacks.

The explicit positive-density laws yield two trajectories r(T_t mu_+) and r(T_t mu_-) in the graded product. They have the same entire initial record, the same right derivatives of every order in each coordinate, and solve the same coordinatewise hierarchy, but their first coordinates disagree somewhere in every open positive-time interval. Thus the claimed record evolution is genuinely multivalued at that common initial moment record. No Banach-space uniqueness theorem is contradicted; no function-space hypotheses supporting one were supplied.

By contrast, the initial full measures differ. Their normalized tilt trajectories are individually explicit and obey the semigroup law. The counterexample does not produce two full-law solutions with one and the same initial full measure.

This is fully consistent with inherited H-formalisation Theorem 11.2, which requires r composed with the actual update F to be constant on every representation fibre. It is likewise consistent with H-Continuation Theorem 2, whose predictive span is closed under actual finite action-observation pullbacks and retains normalization data. Neither theorem asserts that formal closure under an infinitesimal generator, absent convergence and uniqueness conditions, already supplies future congruence.

No new general moment-problem theorem or refutation of inherited compact/simplex mathematics is established. The added Orthemology application is the exact demonstration that even the whole formal moment hierarchy and its full boundary jet can fail the declared future-preservation test after an explicit move to unbounded traits.
