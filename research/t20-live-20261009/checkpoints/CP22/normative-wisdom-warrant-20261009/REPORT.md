The expanded reading yields one consequential source-use correction and one substantive comparison. **The cited James passage cannot carry the claim that removing God removes all ethical reality. An independently argued naturalist rival also prevents the rejection of actual feelings from doing the work of excluding every objective moral standard.** Neither result establishes that naturalism supplies categorical reasons for every rational agent, nor disproves a theistic ground of morality.

The book also supports a stronger affirmative position than arbitrary command, irresistible power, or mere ignorance. ## Source capsule: Ameri (161 words)

Sami Ameri's *مشكلة الشر ووجود الله* (Rawasikh, 2023) presents an integrated theodicy: objective moral assessment, divine perfection, purposeful creation, agency, stable order, and final recompense. It rejects identifying justice with unrestricted ownership and rejects both total moral inscrutability and exhaustive human access to wisdom. Its moral argument moves from objective standards to a nonmaterial legislator; the comparison of worldviews predicts no laws or morality without God. Its appeal to James occurs in that comparison. Later discussions defend actual interpersonal goods, distinguish created defects from wise creation, and supplement general reasons with limits on identifying particular reasons. Pure perfection is not equated with a painless product or a unique numerical optimum. The work includes extensive quotations and deliberately polemical cultural and psychological claims. Mustafa Abu Sway's prefatory pages 13–15 are distinct from Ameri's introduction and argument. The new complete reading includes all notes and references; earlier work covered selected passages, especially 153–217. That repeated material supplies no independent witness or new theorem.

## 1. The actual proposition being tested

Six questions are distinct:

1. Does an evaluation have a truth value?
2. Can it be correct independently of its present evaluator's approval?
3. What makes it correct, and is that ground ultimately independent of God?
4. How can a finite subject justifiably know it?
5. Does it give every rational agent a reason independently of that agent's ends?
6. Does anyone have legitimate authority to specify a duty, and does the subject actually respond?

Questions 5 and 6 are not synonyms. A categorical reason need not be someone else's authoritative directive; a legitimate directive need not produce motivation. A threat may motivate without establishing either. The comparison must also distinguish absence of God, physicalism, absence of conscious interests, and elimination of normative facts. Their different meanings do not alone prove that entailments between them are false. The issue is whether the argument establishes those entailments.

The strongest relevant affirmative says that genuine, universally binding moral assessment cannot be exhausted by contingent preferences or impersonal mechanisms, and that the perfect original source explains its truth and authority. This is a substantive candidate, not merely a word “God” attached to preferences. Its comparison with a naturalist must nevertheless address the rival's proposed truth conditions and normative adequacy rather than substitute current liking for them.

### Whole-book dependency check

The opening normativity argument, the worldview comparison, the later surplus-good discussion, and the concluding meaning/justice argument were read in their continuous settings (60–81, 192–197, 218–230). They offer several supplementary routes: moral experience, explanatory demands concerning mind and altruism, order and beauty, human needs, and final justice. They are not just repetitions of the James citation. But this work does not develop an exclusion of the independently specified relational naturalism tested below. Its broader positive-theism programme is expressly outside the book's full argumentative scope (57–59).

This is not a demand that the affirmative derive every norm from norm-free premises. Basic warranted appraisals can have force. Nor is it a finding that the larger source corpus lacks an answer. It identifies what this particular book adds to the retained argument and what it still imports.

## 2. Executed source discriminator: restore James's actual contrast

The complete primary essay, “The Moral Philosopher and the Moral Life,” was read by the designated source checker, printed 184–215, with all three notes and sixteen substantive scan controls. The final report and bindings are in `james-primary/`. I reviewed that complete report and personally returned to primary text 189–190, 197–198 and 213–215. That bounded return is not a second full-essay reading.

Ameri's printed/PDF 79 quotes James's imagined universe without sentient life, God, or an interested spectator. The Arabic accurately retains those exclusions. The consequential problem is the **surrounding application**, not fabrication of a quotation.

The primary sequence distinguishes three claims:

- James 189–190: physical facts considered without conscious demanders do not constitute an ethical world.
- James 197–198: finite human relations can contain genuine ethical claims without God; 198 expressly grants that ethical reality.
- James 213–214: a fully stable, systematic order and finally valid ranking require an all-enveloping divine thinker.

Therefore the first claim cannot supply “without God, no good, evil or obligation” unless a further bridge excludes the finite ethical relations admitted by James himself. His final theistic conclusion cannot silently replace that bridge: **no final encompassing standard** and **no ethical reality whatever** are different conclusions.

The correction preserves the affirmative force of James's ending. An inclusive standpoint that understands every claimant is an intelligible candidate for settling conflicts which a local or partial perspective cannot settle. This is more than a promise to make people feel better or behave more vigorously. Its warrant still depends on why complete ordering needs a single conscious encompassing standpoint, why its ordering is normatively authoritative, and whether that standpoint exists. The present check does not prove or refute those claims. James also explicitly separates an actual divine ordering from our knowledge of its concrete content (214–215).

James's demand-based ethics is not automatically the mind-independent, categorical normativity Ameri intends. His attribution therefore corrects the witness available for the argument; it does not establish his theory's adequacy or turn his theistic essay into an uncomplicated secular victory.

## 3. Executed rival comparison: an indifferent wrongdoer and a powerless sufferer

Railton's independently argued proposal was assessed through a full continuous reading of “Moral Realism,” printed 163–207, notes 1–43, by the rival-source reader. Its separate 135-word source capsule, exact locators and complete original analysis are in `railton-independent-rival-analysis.md`. It is a real philosophical rival, not a newly labelled toy world. Its objectivity is relational and its account expressly leaves a limit concerning categorical compliance reasons.

### The fixed discrimination case

Consider suffering inflicted upon someone powerless to bargain, retaliate or reward, where the inflictor's informed current preferences remain unchanged. Do not decide the case by defining “good” as whichever option our preferred theory selects. Ask each theory four separate questions:

- Can the victim be genuinely harmed despite the inflictor's indifference?
- Can the infliction be morally wrong despite prevailing approval?
- Does the inflictor have a reason to desist regardless of contingent ends?
- Can an evaluator know the answers without already identifying the ultimate metaphysical ground?

The naturalist proposal has a substantive route to the first two: facts about the affected being, informed appraisal and impartial consideration are not identical to the inflictor's present desire. Calling that route a relation does not make its instantiation dependent on the judge's vote. Conversely, objective instantiation alone does not prove that the proposed relation deserves the name “moral rightness.” That normative identification remains answerable to counterexamples and evaluative adequacy.

The paper does not furnish a universal affirmative answer to the third question. This is a serious limit if the target claim is desire-independent reasons for every rational agent. It is not a concession that there are no objective evaluations. A defender who defines the target more strongly must explain why that stronger feature is indispensable to the particular argument under examination. Equally, a naturalist may not claim to have met the stronger target merely by retaining the word “ought.”

The fourth question distinguishes truth from access. A powerless person's interests may fail to influence actual social learning. That would expose a reliability limitation, not demonstrate that the person's harm or moral significance vanished. A successful correction procedure must be responsive to the relevant truth conditions; genealogy, adaptation and consensus are not enough. The same distinction applies to innate moral recognition: naturalness or felt certainty does not independently authenticate every judgment or uniquely identify its ultimate source.

### Why the operation bears on the evil argument

A conditional criticism of perfect benevolence need not first prove a cosmic obligation binding every conceivable rational agent. It may temporarily grant the theistic hypothesis's own claims about goodness and ask what they imply about permitted harm. This does not automatically extend a human welfare criterion to God. The relevant comparison requires shared normative content together with appropriate differences of knowledge, standing, action and circumstance.

The challenge also still owes an argument that sufficient justification is absent or evidentially unlikely. Establishing real harm does not establish gratuitous harm. Thus the naturalist comparison blocks an immediate dismissal based on the critic's alleged lack of any standard, while leaving the strongest wisdom defence fully available.

The discrimination has a definite outcome: attacks on uncorrected feelings or local convention do not alone defeat this rival; the rival's own categoricity limit does not alone establish God's necessity. Its remaining adequacy debts do not make it true, and their existence does not identify the theistic explanation as the sole remaining possibility.

## 4. Reflective test: actuality of wise reasons versus access

The strongest affirmative has three resources which should not be collapsed:

- independently warranted confidence in the source's character;
- positively intelligible kinds of good and their genuine conditions;
- grounds for expecting incomplete access to particular justifications.

If those resources are sound, ignorance of a particular reason is not a defeater by itself. Requiring exhaustive token-level explanations only from this side would be an unequal standard. The inverse is also important: ignorance does not itself establish that an adequate reason exists. The positive warrant must be carried by the source-character and explanatory premises, not smuggled in through the evaluator's limitations.

A discriminating absence-of-evidence inference therefore asks whether the evaluator would probably detect the relevant reason were it present. The book itself recognizes that detectability matters (172–173). This allows a serious, scoped sceptical-theist argument rather than the caricature “anything unknown is justified.” But generic human finitude alone does not settle every proposed detectability judgment. The burden depends on the particular kind of reason and the evidence already available.

Likewise, a system-relative reason can explain a means conditional on an eligible end without proving the end's ultimate wisdom. Holding a test-purpose, agency or a stable causal order fixed can make some costs intelligible; it cannot by itself prove that every alternative purpose or order is inadmissible. This is a scope distinction, not the assumption that a painless duplicate of our actual human life is available.

For T20, this preserves the earned explanation of the diagonal means and the distinction between real interpersonal participation and simulation. It does not establish why the valued participant must be an **independently original** peer, nor settle whether its productive contribution is foreign completion. The added full-book coverage supplies no decisive bridge at that narrower application.

## 5. What positive normative and perfection warrant remains

The resistance to reducing moral judgment to appetite is substantively justified: a sufferer's significance is not cancelled when a wrongdoer stops caring. But both an adequate naturalist and the affirmative can accept that datum. Discriminating ultimate grounds requires more than observing it.

The affirmative also improves on a bare-command account by treating justice as reason-sensitive fittingness rather than ownership alone (46, 83–85, 92). This preserves moral content for the pure-perfection comparison. Recognizing a common core of justice does not impose all human obligations on God or require identical epistemic access. Nor should the prohibition of a univocal equalizing analogy be recast as a ban on every shared predicate or universal proposition.

The perfection inference nevertheless contains a substantive bridge: a feature must be a genuine, unmixed perfection appropriate to original sourcehood, and the source must deserve its fuller possession. Cataloguing a benefit does not settle that classification. Neither increased quantity of activity, maximal pleasure, nor successful execution of a stipulated end supplies it automatically. The retained original-specific appraisal may have defeasible positive warrant without being a neutral formal theorem; this return neither withdraws that warrant nor newly universalizes it.

Final recompense is likewise relevant **if independently warranted**. Unredressed wrongdoing and absence of any truth about wrongness are distinct. An unmet claim to justice can remain a real claim. Turning the fittingness of complete justice into its actuality requires an additional metaphysical or revelatory warrant. The existential need for that outcome may matter in assessing a human worldview, but psychological relief alone is not the missing entailment.
