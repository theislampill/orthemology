# Owned undertakings, open action, and later specification

10 October 2026 UTC. Bounded primary-source continuation of the original-authority inquiry. Separate research evidence, with no protected edit, integration, contemporary ruling, or T20 closure.

## Result

**The selected source gives an articulated account of bounded obligation-making through actual relations and valid undertakings. It does not add an independent argument for the particular still-disputed selection title in the owed-response relation. It does sharpen what a genuine source-owned undertaking could establish: an obligation not to exercise a power is different from a constitutive surrender of that power.** Both differ from present permission.

Ibn Taymiyya's *Qāʿida fī al-maḥabba* supplies the fresh primary control. Its agreements unit distinguishes real shared relations, contractual specification, rightful obedience, and attempts to invent religious requirements. Ibn al-Qayyim's already received self-obligation argument supplies the cross-check for the original source. Together they support a content-sensitive constraint on purported later specification without making a creature a superior legislator. This is an application of inherited source-owned fidelity, not a new general promise theory or a countermodel to the earned allegiance appraisal.

## 2. What the new continuous unit actually argues

### Existing relations and new undertakings: 120–123

Near the end of 120 the author introduces cooperation and agreements. At 121 he begins with human dependence on shared benefits and protection against shared harms. Fidelity to agreements, justice and truthfulness belong to his explicit normative account, rather than being mere predictions of compliance.

Crucially, his account is not pure voluntarism. Kinship and neighbourhood can establish shared concerns even without a verbal agreement. People acquire some relations through their own acts, including contracts, and others through God's creative acts. The duties he identifies answer the real shared good and its preservation. At 122 justice in participation and exchange is part of the account. These are substantive relational considerations, not the inference that anything caused by God is therefore obligatory.

At 123 he distinguishes cooperation by agreement from obedience to an existing authority, and expressly separates rightful from wrongful obedience. Within the revealed legal order he recognizes duties arising through law and through contracts, including vows and agreements of exchange or partnership. The author therefore gives deliberate undertakings a genuine role in specifying what people owe. He does not require that the agreement manufacture all its own validity conditions.

The prior authority reports do not advance a universal principle that no duty can arise without the recipient's voluntary undertaking. The retained owed-source relation already contradicts such a characterization. Consequently 121 is positive source support for a nonconsensual relational mechanism, not a refutation of an invented inherited rival.

### Actual undertaking is not a mere preference: 124–132

The following pages distinguish cooperative agreements directed at justice from conditions contrary to the governing norm. At 127–129, agreements of allegiance do not license disobedience to the source they purport to serve. A pledge can confirm an already existing obligation; not every undertaking creates a temporally new duty.

At 130, mere agreement cannot invent a form of worship and make it source-authorized. At 131, however, the author distinguishes worldly transactions: a permissible provision can become owed through contracting. The mutual undertaking, rather than merely the desirability of the act or a participant's request, does normative work. He explicitly criticizes both unjustified prohibitions of transactions and attempts to make allegiance to a particular teacher unconditional through affiliation alone. At 132 he requires following what is established while treating uncertainty according to the person's epistemic capacity.

Thus the source's case is stronger than an unexplained label of ownership. It joins real shared goods, justice, fidelity and the directed commitments participants actually make. It is also narrower than the thesis that an intention to impose a duty always succeeds. The contract's permissibility, parties, content and governing norms matter. A bare request contains no equivalent undertaking by its addressee. A valid agreement need not make its object an independently prescribed religious practice.

This distinction answers a tempting overreading of the phrase about conditions that forbid the lawful: the same source immediately allows contracts to make an otherwise unowed performance owed. It therefore cannot mean that every contractual narrowing of a previously open choice is illicit. Nor does that establish the validity of every imaginable condition. This is an interpretation of the paired passages, not a contemporary contractual ruling.

## 3. Does the broader argument confer the original's selection title?

The continuous earlier unit at 33–39 gives the strongest selected wider account. It combines love and submission, the need for cooperative normative order, rightful worship, and the ultimate object of practical orientation. At 37 the author expressly reserves unqualified worship and obedience to God, with other obedience derivative. At 39 he offers two arguments: shared human life requires a normative order rightly directed to God; and the final object of practical love can be only the divine original.

These are genuine substantive proposals. They should not be paraphrased as coercion or optional appreciation. The final-object argument connects to the earned source/perfection account; the cooperative argument identifies goods that merely private choice does not secure. The source's own 23–25 distinction between causal governance and responsive worship also prevents reducing its case to mere production.

Nevertheless, the new units do not isolate an additional premise explaining why the original may exclude a particular otherwise adequate form of owed response. The social argument establishes the importance of ordered cooperation, not the jurisdiction of every particular directive. Contractual specification depends on the relation and authorized undertaking that actually obtain. Transferring its result to the Creator's direction of another agent would still require establishing the relevant divine title or a pertinent undertaking by that agent. Neither follows simply because both operations can be called obligation-making.

The positive mechanism is therefore reusable at its actual scope: where a warranted relation and valid undertaking exist, the act can acquire a new owed relational character. It does not independently extend that relation to every creaturely action. The exact local selection-title question remains explanatory and open at the already accepted scope. This is no evidence that a rival possesses equal warrant, that an adequate alternative actually exists in a particular case, or that a truthful authenticated duty report may be disregarded.

## 4. A genuine source-owned undertaking can constrain later action

The cross-check is the already received Miftāḥ 2:1135–1143, reread continuously here. The relevant authorial argument at 1136–1141 affirms beneficiary rights under a source-owned undertaking, distinguishes practical self-demand/restraint from merely assertoric oath, and argues that self-obligation does not require a superior commander. Fulfilment answers the source's intrinsic praiseworthiness. The recipient's actual qualifying response does not supply that essential perfection or turn the recipient into the legislator of the original. These are inherited conclusions, also independently reviewed in the retained conditional-right report.

The following application distinguishes three different cases.

1. **Present permission.** The source leaves an action open now. Without further terms, this does not promise that no later in-scope specification will occur. The original report's Miftāḥ 943 result remains unchanged.
2. **An undertaking not to exercise control.** The source validly undertakes not to restrict this action under specified circumstances. That undertaking establishes a constraint on its own later conduct. It does not, merely by being a promise, prove that the underlying normative power has been transferred or extinguished.
3. **A constitutively effective relinquishing grant.** The source validly grants the recipient immunity from its later selection within the specified domain. If that constitutive effect is warranted, later unilateral selection lacks the surrendered title. Warrant for this stronger kind of grant is additional; it cannot be obtained by renaming case 1 or case 2.

### Executed source-grounded inference

Fix an independently warranted source-owned undertaking in case 2, including its actual beneficiary, action-domain, duration, conditions, reserved changes and any release terms. Establish that it still applies. Suppose a proposed interpretation of a later address would make it the original's own exercise of precisely the restriction that undertaking excludes.

- On the source's 1137–1138 account, the earlier undertaking is a real self-directed commitment, not merely a statement of current preference.
- On 1141's explicit appraisal, contravening such an undertaking would conflict with the intrinsic praiseworthiness that underwrites fulfilment.
- The actual perfect source is therefore not correctly described as owning the contrary exercise while those same terms remain operative.
- Accordingly, the two fully specified attributions cannot both stand. The applicable terms, their continuing conditions, the later address's attribution, or its alleged obligatory force must be re-examined. The argument does not license arbitrarily discarding whichever evidence an interpreter dislikes.

This is a substantive application of an earned fidelity appraisal. The original report's rejected generic perfection shortcut lacked an independently warranted classification of the proposed exercise as defective. Here the identified valid undertaking supplies that classification at a determinate scope. It does **not** supply the classification for every unauthorized purported imposition, nor convert every sincere request into a duty.

For a perfect source, a no-exercise commitment thus has real practical significance even if extinction of the underlying power is unproved. For case 3, an independently warranted constitutive grant would additionally delimit the title itself. The first consequence follows from the inspected self-obligation account; the second still depends on the grant's valid constitutive effect. Neither establishes that such a grant was actually made over the disputed creaturely action.

A request consistent with the recipient's continuing liberty need not contradict either case. A directive after a genuinely reserved condition for alteration is fulfilled also need not do so. Actual normative mode and the undertaking's terms, rather than the speaker's rank alone, determine the comparison.

## 5. Wisdom remains a substantive constraint

Qāʿida 178–183 contains a continuous report of opposing positions followed by the author's endorsed alternative. He rejects both a restriction of divine agency that defeats its intended explanation and a conception on which every possible act is just simply because it is within power. His criticism of arbitrary harmful commands at 179–182 is not his own proposed theory. At 183 he positively locates commands in creaturely good while denying that God needs the service or withholds prohibited things from stinginess.

This reinforces the inherited Wisdom constraint; it does not create a new counterexample to source perfection. The account need not maximize every independently visible good, and the goodness of an act alone does not determine whether it is obligatory or recommended. The valid-undertaking application likewise does not assume that an original could make an intrinsically unwise commitment: validity and warranted source-ownership are among its fixed premises.
