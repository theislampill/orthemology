# Jami sixth collection continuous reading notes

Reader-facing study record, 7 October 2026. The interpretations and reading claims below belong to the recorded study and its bounded rereads. This edition of the notes adds no source-reading or proof credit.

These are notes on direct Arabic readings. Electronic page E numbers belong to Islamarchive book 28157, not automatically printed-page numbers. No automated fetch or extraction counts as reading. Each entry preserves the recorded untruncated reading window and its hash; these are historical coverage records.

## E001–008 editor introduction

Read continuously through END first-001-008 (9df7effac49569807be548a7b0c3b91d9a34eadbb70861b0b1103a8a3adcc6ff), 7 October 2026. This modern introduction identifies 18 collected pieces, not one historically continuous monograph. Some titles are editorial inferences from incipits; not every work is independently listed in medieval bibliographies. Shams describes recovery from difficult handwritten copies, often rereading against other works of Ibn Taymiyya to reconstruct corrupt passages, and says obvious corrections are not all signalled. Accordingly brackets/apparatus are not a complete map of editorial intervention. Attribution of otherwise unnamed pieces partly rests on style, topics and internal references, not always explicit manuscript ascription.

Treatises 1–6 are reported from Jar Allah 1729 (122 leaves, incomplete end; good naskh yet extensive corruption and opening obliterations). T1 leaves 1–18 a; T2 18 a–28 a; T3 28 b–35 a (end possibly missing); T4 Tawhid 35 b–56 b; T5 theological sincerity 56 b–100 b; T6 nonbeing/deprivation 101 a–106 b. T7 Islam and its opposite is from Umm al-Qura 1491,13 b–22 b, end definitely incomplete; copyist identification is conjectural. T8 Husayn/Yazid comes from Alexandria 4 Hanbali law,14 b–25 a, with copyist colophon dated 16 Dhu al-Hijja 728. T9 forgiveness has unknown missing opening; T10 prayer likewise; T11 middle prayer has a missing ending. T12 times/combination has two copies possibly genealogically dependent (second may copy the Kawakib original), so not automatically two independent witnesses. T13 sibling zakat and T14 naming God at slaughter are from Shahid Ali 2751; T14 is avowedly abridged by the copyist. T15 wild animals is a one-page old copy whose prayer for the author's continued life suggests copying in his lifetime. T16 slaughtered animal sale, T17 iqta leasing and T18 orchard guarantee finish the collection. T18's copyist explicitly says he abridged the answer; Majmu 30:220 ff includes an overlapping beginning with additions but lacks this witness's final third. None of these manuscript details is independently inspected here.

Consequence: even completing the received sixth collection does not recover every original historical treatise. Gaps and abridgements predate this reading and cannot be silently repaired from doctrinal expectations.

## E009–020 intention, agency and benefit

Read every supplied word through END first-009-020 (5d78c58f79ab3f504fc510cfcac67d48d8bb9a8480e3fa4e7533826073a806e8). T1 begins with explicit physical manuscript losses, retained as ellipses. Its general argument is descriptive before normative: every voluntary action has an intended object/end and a movement/means. Intention relates to external action as spirit to body. The hadith on intentions is treated as universally descriptive of all action, including ordinary migration for money/marriage, rather than restricted to valid religious acts by an unexpressed qualifier such as acceptance. This is grammatical/methodological economy: avoid both unjustified ellipsis and scope restriction.

The author's criticism of being like a corpse in a washer's hands is directed against abolishing living voluntary movement and choice, not dependence on God as such. He allows entrusted acceptance of events when it does not abandon religious duties, differentiates events to resist, unavoidable affliction to endure, and discretionary acceptance/resistance. Contentment with unavoidable affliction is preferably recommended rather than obligatory. A shaykh cannot receive unrestricted submission equivalent to the prophet. Abu Yazid's reported wish not to will is interpreted charitably as willing only what God normatively approves, or loss of attention to one's still-existing will in devotional absorption. Existing will remains a condition, and sober abiding is better where absorption threatens duties. Neither withdrawal from awareness nor derivative will equals literal nonagency.

E017–020 rejects the claim that sincere divine love eliminates all benefit or end for the worshipper. Divine vision and love are the person's highest satisfaction; relinquishing lower benefits for higher is not ceasing to have a desired good. God is not like an exploiting master who needs servants' output. Command and guidance benefit the creature, whereas God gains no needed benefit. God's pleasure/love remains to be discussed rather than erased. E020 distinguishes universal invitation from granted inner guidance and outward profession; neither general invitation nor outward profession alone is the special gift of believing guidance.

Textual caution: E014 literally says punishment for failure of the recommended action, which is discordant with its immediately preceding distinction of obligatory and recommended; retain as received pending scan comparison, not silently change it. E010 and E012 contain structurally incomplete/manuscript-damaged clauses; they cannot carry fine-grained conclusions about all creature causation unaided. The apparatus hadith assessment at E012 is Shams's modern layer and does not certify the original chain by independent inspection.

## E021–032 divine love and warranted action

Read continuously through END first-021-032 (142f6f13bf357b43fa338685ddf6bf3a00c0804a4334e5645256420ac12a70f8). The author gives two grounds for divine invulnerability: necessary self-grounded attributes and providential control. This is a positive modal/perfection assertion, not merely silence about dependence. The quoted woman's two loves can mean love for benefits and for God's own excellence, or spontaneous experiential love and disciplined commanded devotion. Natural rational recognition may generate genuine love even outside sound worship; that concession is explicit. Love, fear, mercy, and zeal cannot themselves authorise every act they prompt. Moses's requested vision, Noah's petition, and Abraham's intercession serve a prophetic contrast with the strict obedience attributed to Muhammad. Excessive punishment from religious zeal and excessive mercy contrary to justice are parallel departures from normative guidance; rulers' outward politics and mystics' inward states can share the same error.

The normative classification crosses two independent dimensions: sound/right work and sincere end. Sincere misguided worship is genuinely sincere yet not thereby right (including the cited Kharijite praise of Ibn Muljam); sound outward teaching, justice and charity can have a corrupt end; worst is defective action with defective end. The author's own position is that both right means and a right end are needed. Exegetical differences sometimes exemplify rather than exhaustively define a class, illustrated by pointing to a loaf when asked what bread is. E029 imposes evidential responsibility on negative as well as positive assertions; he demands revelation or reason for exclusive salvation claims, rather than accepting identity labels. This is important epistemic material even though he takes his own scriptural commitments as given.

E030–032 gives a comparative religious argument: the highest object, humble exclusive intention, and good action combine into the best religion. That is substantive positive support within its premises, but its identification of uniquely ultimate worth is invoked here rather than independently established against all possible alternatives. The linguistic discussion reads 'face' as direction/intention without an obligatory hidden word; Islam contains both sincere dedication and humble obedience, opposed respectively to association and pride. Textual problems remain in E024, E025 and E029; no syntax is silently regularised.

## E033–046 epistemic reflexivity and justice

Read through END first-033-046 (d0e964664aa4ca54b4fbc58796fbc45071a93c5572f8663e12f01afb522b73b7). Religious forms vary among prophetic dispensations while general submission remains; the author's claim that Muhammad's final dispensation is now obligatory is his confessional argument, not an independently established historical conclusion of this reading. Inner submission needs outward acts, compared to spirit/body and roof/supports. Following scholars/rulers is conditional on what God actually commands, not blanket allegiance to one party; accepting all legitimate evidence is contrasted with selectively accepting only useful texts.

The 'believe in the like of what you believe' discussion (E035–037) is strongly relevant to cognition. Ibn Abbas's variant is reported as meaningful, but the received wording is judged richer. Belief can be an internal likeness corresponding to an external truth; knowing that one knows and affirming one's belief as true are distinguishable reflexive levels. The author canvasses multiple grammatical construals of mithl and its antecedent, not one settled argument. Material blanks at E036 (five words) and E037 (one line) truncate parts of that reasoning. Do not reconstruct a fully determinate theory of second-order knowledge from those gaps.

The major remaining ethical argument distinguishes obligatory beneficence from optional generosity. All unjustified harm is forbidden; necessary harm must be proportional to the need and directed toward protection/benefit rather than harm as an end. Even deserved punishment must be administered justly and mercifully. E042–043 carefully distinguishes justice BETWEEN claimants, where preferential 'kindness' can itself be injustice, from one's own rights AGAINST another, where relinquishment may be better unless it harms oneself or others. A wrongdoer still has rights; guilt does not license additional injustice.

E044–046 explicitly criticizes scholars, ascetics, politicians and ordinary people who punish religious or worldly wrongdoing unjustly while imagining their acts righteous. Both disputing parties can be unjust; sincere interpretation can excuse the agent without licensing imitation. Divisive insults and abandonment count as destructive conflict even short of fighting. This guards against reading polemical labels elsewhere as unconditional permission to harm their bearers. The treatise closes at E046. Apparatus claims about hadith soundness remain editorial reports; none of the many isnads has been separately checked here.

## E047–060 divine satisfaction distinct from creature need

Read through END first-047-060 (ca6074a01aa8e3526f761836a5081a57f9b58a917ff2b9978c7d93d4a2f1ef5e). E048 is a blank/redirect rather than additional received body, excluded by helper. T2 begins by denying God's need for sustenance while affirming that worship is genuinely intended and beloved. The division of al-Fatiha between Lord and servant is used positively: since both halves benefit the servant, something specific to the Lord must explain why only one is called His share. Likewise wrongdoing against God's right cannot be reduced without remainder to the creature's lost benefit.

The central argument sharply separates universal willing/creating from normative love and approval. Universal will equally explains everything that occurs; it cannot by itself account for the difference between morally beloved acts and hated acts, while ordinary hunger/satiety are not classified in that moral way. The author presents this as a rational argument for additional divine attributes, not only quotation of names. He reports and rejects two alternatives: creation exclusively for creature benefit under Mu'tazilite improvement obligations; and creation from mere undifferentiated will without reasons. Philosophical necessary emanation with apparently teleological consequences is also charged with failing to explain the intentional structure it acknowledges.

Objections to creature-benefit-only theory include the real occurrence of harms and punishment when the alleged sole intention was benefit, and the lack of any differentiating relation to the agent. For humans generosity perfects the giver; this prompts rather than simply settles the divine analogy. E058–060 carefully restricts vocabulary: scripture affirms love, approval, joy, anger and being offended, but does not simply apply creaturely pleasure/pain. A creature cannot supply an independently originated benefit to God; its own act occurs through divine power/will. This is the beginning of a source-mode answer to the dependency challenge, not a flat denial that anything relational is real. The author's favoured alternative has not yet been fully developed at the window boundary. E054's 'التكليف الشرير' is received anomalous wording (expected context religious obligation), retained pending scan; no doctrinal argument will rest on the typo.

## E061–072 intention, counterfactual realisation and source-mode perfection

Read all received pages through END first-061-072 (bcb874a8315ae70a924764a5129ec85fb285e867c19dd1ad86625585422d091a). E072 is a blank/redirect; T2 concludesE 071. The opening question fairly states the difficulty: if worship is the creator's purpose and He is able to secure it, why do not all worship? Replies reducing worship to universal innate recognition or restricting the created class to worshippers are rejected. The first is not universal among the mad or deniers and fails to capture the normative stakes. A command interpretation is closer if completed. The Qadari alternative is reported as willing universal worship but failing to obtain voluntary cooperation; the author rejects the constraints it imposes on God's ability to grant free willing.

E063–064 parses lām of purpose/consequence rather than merely labelling the verses: unwanted consequence naturally arises from ignorance or inability, unlike the omniscient and powerful case. Causal and final 'for' are distinguished from the accusative of purpose. The actual solution separates normative loving purpose from all-inclusive effective willing. Not every beloved object must be actualized; a beloved option can be omitted for a weightier reason, and human optional goods are used as a comparison. E065 includes a reported divine-secret story, but the modern note itself calls its chain weak; the argument is not just an appeal to mystery. E066 further distinguishes conditional love of what exists from loving pure nonexistence.

The second objection is expressly the acquisition-of-perfection dilemma: if worship adds something, God was deficient; if it adds nothing, worship seems indifferent. E067 replies that God creates and originates the beloved worship, so its occurring is no poverty toward another source. Renewed love/joy is compared with renewed seeing/hearing: whatever consistent account secures the latter against novelty/contingency objections secures the former. Several alternative replies are offered dialectically (nonpositive renewal, eternal kind-relative knowledge, perfection as responsiveness to each new object); these alternatives should not all be treated as simultaneously endorsed metaphysical descriptions. The core positive comparison is that being such as to hear/see every new object is perfect, and analogously so is fittingly loving/rejoicing in every new good.

E068–070 says unqualified ambiguous pleasure/pain terminology is unsafe. Some philosophical uses of pleasure or passionate love have a true construed meaning; the author's rejection can be terminological etiquette or a prohibition on misleading ambiguity, rather than simple denial of every associated reality. He nevertheless rejects restricting divine love to self alone: relation to servants matters. The claim that such relation entails origination or contingency is said to require proof, rather than following from creature analogy. E071 connects efficient willing to the First and final/normative willing to the Last.

Research bearing: this is affirmative evidence for source-mode perfection and against equating every condition, relation or novelty with defect. The author expressly draws the line at reception from another independent source. Whether each cooperative ultimate agent's own intrinsic efficacy would cross that line is the further question; product dependence cannot simply be substituted for this stronger claim. Suspicious E063 'يبتغي أن لا يكون مريدًا لها' is kept as received for scan checking; the reasoning needs a negation or different verb sense but no emendation is assumed.

## E073–088 mixed recognition, embodiment and purposive predication

Read through END first-073-088 (f7c1267e82add6b93299330e6349e26e6d48618df9e34a6103f19112448dc9ba). E074 absent/redirected. T3 explicitly starts a separate piece; no concluding authorial seal is present, consistent with the editor's possible lost ending. The two Qur'anic hypocrisy images distinguish lost light from intermittent damaged perception and action. The author allows real but incomplete assent, outward affiliation without fully formed inner trust, and the distinction between a positive contrary conviction and simply not having complete assent. His sectarian applications are historical polemic, not this reader's judgments about persons. Knowledge/recognition can be mixed with denial and action can support growth or erosion of belief.

The fire/water exposition links life, perception, desire and light. It contains historically situated natural philosophy (heat/moisture, bodily spirits and blood), not modern science. Embryological passages connect formed or unformed fetal development with contested legal consequences for postpartum bleeding, waiting periods and status; midwives' testimony can matter. These are historical jurisprudential reports, not medical/legal advice. The author distinguishes newly arising concrete bodily substances and forms from mere changes of accidents, rejecting a reductive claim that only properties ever begin. Aristotelian substrate/form/body/intellect/soul categories are reported, with qualified acceptance of body/form and rejection of the separate substrate or separately subsisting nonbody as described there.

E083–084 presents near-to-self evidence for lordship: one's own created life, need, guidance, learning and voluntary motion. Knowledge is the origin of voluntary movement, so teaching rational humans and trained animals belongs within providence; activity's intentional structure is not exempt from creation. These are substantive affirmative claims, not an argument that a creature lacks its own powers. E086 connects inquiry, desire and action to attained understanding. E087 supplies an important semantic control: failure to attain a thing's purpose can license denying its name without denying its bare existence (the tormented is neither dead nor living in the flourishing sense). Legal negations of marriage or sale provide analogies. The physical absence of agency, its incomplete realisation and its failure of proper end must therefore not be interchanged.

Uncertainties include E080 one-word manuscript lacuna; several received verse/hadith numbering errors in modern notes; E086 quoted lover's boast is reported rhetoric, not endorsed comparative measurement of fires. All are retained without normalisation.

## E089–100 Tawhid begins and the exact numerical effect

Read through END first-089-100 (dbff7b88934c178764665afcd8e6f7fe7b2a8333aaab0eb93f30584208ff3944); E090 absent/redirected. T4 titleE 089, bodyE 091. The positive argument begins with a concession that matters: productive reciprocal priority is impossible, but reciprocal co-presence as conditions is not. Efficient causes must precede their effects; final ends follow effects in realisation but precede them in intention. A loop in either explanatory priority gives contradictory self-priority/posteriority. Two correlated results of one agent, or two contributions to one final end, can nevertheless coexist lawfully. Fatherhood/sonship, organs, marital exchange, sale, armies and joint carrying provide distinct examples.

E094 differentiates each cooperating agent's intrinsic source from the other cooperator and each end from the jointly related end; there may be a shared commanding source (one person using two limbs, a director of two workers), or a common further end (offspring). 'May be' should not silently become a demonstrated 'must always be' at this point. The next thesis is categorical: a numerically one act/effect cannot have two complete agents/causes.

E094–098 devotes real argument to juridical 'two causes of one ruling'. It distinguishes numerical token from type and independently documented legal grounds from merely inferred suitable descriptions. Mere suitability does not authorise declaring each factor independently sufficient; analogical difference and absence-of-effect objections would otherwise become unfalsifiable. The alternative cause must be demonstrated rather than invented to rescue the preferred explanation. In simultaneously applicable grounds (impurity, ablution nullifiers, prohibited meat, multiple sanctions), he argues the rulings are themselves multiple even if their practical execution overlaps. A single item cannot literally overlap itself. This is legal evidence/analogy, not an unexplained citation of formal logic.

E098–100 extends that partition to causal cooperation. Each bearer performs its own act; each produces a distinct portion of the jointly borne object's support. The whole is produced by their sum, so neither is actually sole producer in the cooperation event. Crucially E099 explicitly ALLOWS that one could have done it alone in some cases. But then its exercise would differ: producing the whole alone is a fuller exercise than contributing part alongside another. A real partner must affect something; otherwise it is not a real partner. E100 describes the dilemma as inability if the whole lies beyond one's power, or inhibition if one could do the whole but the cooperator's contribution prevents that exercise. Even partial inhibition counts as mutual hindrance, and partnership thereby excludes fully unrestricted exercised power in that event.

This is the author's affirmative causal-ownership/perfection argument, not an opponent quotation. It engages agreement/cooperation rather than only contrary wills. The distinction between standing ability and actual exclusive exercise is recognised and then normatively assessed, not simply overlooked. Its sufficiency still depends on the claim that any other's real contribution limits the first agent's exercise in a way incompatible with ultimate perfection; the definition of sole action alone does not establish that ranking. Record the positive argument before assessing this further premise. Several received clauses (especiallyE 096,E097 and E100's wording on equal power) are syntactically defective; scan comparison is reserved and no quiet repair is adopted.

## E101–112 agreed cooperation and God's own end

Read through END first-101-112 (450023699256dce64adaa370b1e8ea20ffdee1e8a1a05214e32af659249d78ac). E101 expressly criticizes the standard contrary-wills proof: it shows that opposing agents do not realise that action while opposed, but by itself does not show that cooperating agents cannot act together. E101–102 also recognises necessary concord as an option requiring the earlier nonduplication/perfection argument, not a forced appeal to possible contrary willing. The author distinguishes inability to act alone from inability to prevent the other. His 'cooperation itself is hindrance' argument is offered to cover both. The following partition-of-created-realms claim and appeal to purported rational consensus are affirmative moves, but the received wording inE 102 is defective and his report that no rational person denies token nonduplication is not independent evidence of universal consensus.

E103–106 extends efficient-source unity to final-end unity. Created things cannot be their own original cause or their own sufficient ultimate end. God alone is outside the originated/possible field, and acts for Himself without generating Himself. The author explicitly rejects imagining self-existence or self-love as a separate earlier self acting upon a later self. His account preserves wisdom without requiring a separately supplied object on which God depends. Good intended in the first instance and evil intended instrumentally are distinguished; 'evil is not toward You' is read with attention to the preposition, not as a denial that events arise from divine creative willing. Several E103–105 clauses are notably discontinuous; no reconstructed syllogism relying on the missing syntax is credited.

E107–110 gives multiple positive routes to divine self-love. Loving worship and lovers of God presupposes the object loved; love of good creatures either refers to God as final end or to their excellence, which He himself produces. Their originating source is then at least as worthy of love as a lover of them. This is an explicit higher-worth/source-perfection argument, not merely a definition of divinity. E109 distinguishes loving a person for themselves from wanting external benefit (contrasting beauty, virtue and financial assistance); both still involve a lover's own satisfaction. E110's second argument has a reported textual loss and must stay qualified.

E111–112 clarifies context-relative assessment: the same occurrence can be hated under one aspect and wanted for an outweighing good. Conversely an intrinsically beloved act may be prevented when its particular realisation would bring a worse outcome; the expedition of the hypocrites is the scriptural example. Loving goodness does not entail willing every local instance regardless of context. This matters for later veracity research only as a bounded general control; it is not an explicit license for divine falsehood. Apparatus at E111 replaces original 'لا يكون' with editorial 'قد يكون'; the causal/moral distinction thus has an exposed local editorial intervention, which requires visual confirmation before precise use.

## E113–124 self-concern, external goods and final causation

Read through END first-113-124 (53077a1c48c74883611b76bd21bec2a6d3a3b325402ce310c420ed660d12c56f). The author argues from great joy in repentance to great love for the good and thence self-love; the lost-camel comparison concerns magnitude of joy without importing every feature of creature dependence. E114–116 insists that human willing is among originated events and not exempt from causal explanation. Originated things' need for an originator is judged more evident than abstract equipossibility's need for a preponderating cause: actual coming-to-be is sensory, whereas equal modal alternatives are mentally represented. The received clause calling every external thing necessary/impossible 'in itself' at E115 is suspicious and should not be turned into an unqualified denial of created contingency without scan checking.

The final-cause analogy is argued, not just asserted. A self-created act would require another intention ad infinitum or a loop. Likewise a creature cannot make its already-existing bare self the whole achieved end. E117 fairly voices an objection: that proves only the impossibility of being the final explanation of one's existence, whereas our own action can seek our welfare. The reply then distinguishes bare existing self from a new benefit sought through external conditions. Food, clothes, chairs, sensation and learned motivation illustrate dependence on such sources. This supports genuine creature need; the further identification of exactly one sufficient ultimate object is a stronger claim.

E119–120 rejects desire's mere occurrence as sufficient reason for its worth: allowing every desired act as its own justification licenses destructive desires. A valued object must have a feature making pursuit good. The movement from this point to only God as ultimate end invokes already accepted universal lordship; it is not independent evidence for that premise. E121–122 gives a serious account of reciprocal human benefit, exchange and partnership, including spouses, learning, worship and trade. Creaturely beloveds yield situational finite satisfactions and may become unwanted or harmful; the author infers that none is unconditionally loved for its essence in the highest sense.

E122–124's affirmative hierarchy says efficient cause exceeds product and final end exceeds means, even the agent as agent; the end can have its worth whether a particular seeker exists or not, though attaining it requires that seeker. This sharply distinguishes a thing's intrinsic worth from its being attained, a useful parallel to powers versus realised outputs. The hierarchy and claim that the higher cannot act for the lower are appealed to as settled in fitra. They should not be treated as derived solely from Necessary Being. The final regress argument aims at an end loved for itself, then chains a creature's love toward what its beloved itself needs. Preserve its positive explanation of integrated human good while asking how it establishes one globally common terminus rather than several compatible terminal goods.

## E125–134 final Tawhid arguments and boundary

Read through END first-125-134 (a33f595fd678ed9894812992ee2770c1a5b0b6d00ca349486fcdf007525d4ca0). E134 is a blank/redirect; T4 endsE 133. E125–126 argues that prioritizing a derivative beloved over its superior enabling end inverts appropriate love. Healthy fitra is appealed to through analogies of healthy appetite; felt rest in divine remembrance supplies experiential support distinct from inference and distinct from merely requesting benefits. The author explicitly calls on reason, analogy and lived awareness together. This can be defeasible evidence, not only an alleged deduction; it nevertheless raises questions about the reliability and universality of the claimed experience and the comparison class.

E127–129 critiques reducing happiness to abstract knowledge or imitation of an unmoved intellect. A human has will/love as well as cognition; abstract universals are not their own concrete fulfilled object. Ghazali's account of imitating divine attributes is reported, as are objections by Mazari and others. The author does not deny the value of acquired knowledge, mercy and power: these complete flourishing when directed toward worship but do not replace its final object. Ibn Barrajan's language of service/worship is preferred to unqualified imitation. Claims about adversarial philosophers are reports and interpretations, not an independent survey of their systems.

E130 makes a crucial modal/normative contrast explicit: self-creation is impossible to occur, while misdirected worship can occur and be harmful. The verse says the heavens/earth would be corrupted, not nonexistent. E131 then argues from actually ordered celestial motion to correct final direction, conditional on the preceding claim that every alternative ultimate object corrupts the activity. This is positive order/coordination evidence at the actual cosmic scope he describes, but relies on premodern celestial physics (uniform circular motion, ninth sphere) and the already asserted universal final-value hierarchy. It cannot be reported as a modern astronomy proof. E132 distinguishes the different motions rather than treating all coordination as numerical identity; his inference to a source outside the spheres then folds the outermost sphere into the same originated class. E133 repeats agreement as a live alternative and closes with the nonduplication/perfection reply, not contrary wills alone.

First-pass scope is now complete for all actually received electronic E001–134 (including introductionE 001–008 and T1–4). This is direct text reading, not visual certification of every corresponding page or every original historical text. Bounded rereads and scan controls follow. Universal source-mode perfection and a defeasible common explanation of the actual order remain distinct questions in the synthesis.
