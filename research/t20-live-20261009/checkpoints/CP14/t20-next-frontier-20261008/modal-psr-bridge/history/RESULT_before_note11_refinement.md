# Modal possible explanation at the original-bearer interface

9 October 2026. Bounded T20 premise/transport audit. Old work is unchanged. No integration, closure, historical-time credit, new actual Necessary Being witness, or external validation is claimed.

## Result

**The complete-target possible-to-actual inference survives scrutiny without S5 or explanatory necessitation. Its important condition is completeness about explanation itself.** The accompanying audit isolates this condition using the fixed proposition H_P, “P has an explanation”; it needs no transworld identity for a particular explanans. A merely complete description of the created/received fragment lacks that condition unless independently supplied.

**The further inference to an actual necessary particular is a different argument.** It needs substantive constraints on explanatory form and on what explains an agent's existence. Those constraints do not instantiate productive Completion, Realisation, or ContingencyNeed merely because they yield something called an explanation. This is an exact transport boundary, not a refutation of the original-bearer theorem or a demand for a metaphysics-neutral proof of its premises.

The voluntary-act comparison also changes with source version. The 1999 stopping account cannot be charged with asserting that voluntary choice excludes reasons. The later author treatment supplies a stronger, genuinely reason-based proposal, explicitly rejecting explanatory necessitation and admitting a noncontrastive PSR fallback. It remains a live alternative at the particular-determination boundary. It does not, without further interpretation, discharge the inherited stronger obligation.

The 2002 reply's full body remains inaccessible. Its verified abstract settles one attribution, but no other criticism is retained as an objection the authors failed to answer.

## 3. Actualisation: the exact positive result

See `ACTUALISATION_AUDIT.md` for the proof and restricted-target control. Fix one actual total P and one interpreted proposition H_P. Suppose P possibly has an explanation. If H_P were actually false, ¬H_P would be an actual contingent truth. A genuinely complete P includes it. At the explanatory witness, factivity makes P true, and therefore makes ¬H_P true, contradicting H_P there. Hence P actually has an explanation.

This proof needs classical truth, explanandum factivity, fixed propositional interpretation, and inclusion of contingent explanation-status truths. It neither moves an individual witness through a modal operator nor infers that explanations necessitate their effects. The original paper instead establishes identity of the witness world with actuality; that is a legitimate stronger route under its declared world ontology.

The mathematical point is elementary, not a new cosmological theorem. Its decision-relevant application is to the proposed target translation. If “totality” now means only the complete received productive field, its content might no longer decide whether that field has an explanation, how a necessary agent acts, or which explanatory relation obtains. The original actualisation result then cannot simply be copied. Conversely, retaining all these contingent truths does not classify their referents as created beings.

Local W-PSR at the comprehensive P therefore carries actual total-explanation commitment under the stated closure. This does not establish that the commitment is false, epistemically circular, or unwarranted. Recognitional and defeasible metaphysical warrant need not reduce to weaker neutral premises. It does prevent treating the apparent modal weakening as an independently earned weakening at this exact application.

## 4. The three different projections

The following are distinct:

1. P's truth entails truth of each of its conjuncts.
2. An explanation of P provides some explanatory account of each conjunct.
3. The same q separately grounds every conjunct in the same sense and with the same explanatory relevance.

Only the first is used in the H_P proof. Oppy's generic W-PSR-to-S-PSR discussion additionally invokes explanatory dissectivity, including in its special conjunction argument. The original scientific-explanation exclusion and contingent-agent exclusion likewise require more than mere truth elimination. They cannot be reconstructed by inserting logical conjunction elimination inside an unexplained explanatory connective.

The later chapter supplies an explicit answer worth crediting: §2.3.2.1 proposes weakening “explains” to providing enough material for an explanation if irrelevant surplus blocks direct projection, and separately argues that q cannot be irrelevant to itself in the self-explanation step. This is an argued sufficiency response, not silence about relevance. Whether it preserves the required hyperintensional explanatory relation, rather than a weaker accounting relation, remains the comparison to assess.

The inherited Dasgupta alternative targets precisely such a transfer. A full plurality may be qualitatively grounded without its members each having a separate qualitative ground. His “accounting for” logical consequences is not automatically singleton grounding. Therefore a translation into that framework needs either an explicitly warranted weaker accounting-based exclusion argument, or a defended explanatory projection that the framework accepts. Declaring every adequate explanation scientific/personal does not establish that a qualitative ground fits the temporal-law/initial-state pattern used in the exclusion.

The later reasons discussion adds an independent relevance control on the input side: adding a true but unused reason to an explanans need not preserve its explanatory role. That is not the same rule as distribution on the output side, and neither follows from ordinary truth conjunction. Both comparisons require preserving explanatory relevance, but the two theories do not thereby share a grounding calculus. Likewise, the later conceptual explanation of a contrastive proposition through an already obtaining P-and-not-Q is a proposed local explanatory form; it does not, just by that conversion, explain why the complete actual contingent target obtains or fit the original two-way taxonomy without interpretation.

There is also a target-scope limit: Dasgupta’s proposed collective ground explains the individualistic plurality, whereas a genuinely complete P includes the contingent obtaining of the qualitative base itself whenever that obtaining is contingent. Its provision of an explanans for the former is not yet an explanation of the latter. The proposal challenges an unargued projection or exhaustive taxonomy; it is not already a full-totality explanatory countermodel.

This does not settle the metaphysical contest for collective explanation. Nor does it defeat whole-field productive Completion: the saved interface can use one whole received target without distribution. It shows why the Gale–Pruss next step is not a substitute proof of that premise in the face of the already investigated alternative.

## 5. Necessary individual versus necessary proposition

At p. 466 the literal transition to necessity of an existential action claim is awkward; the following purported instantiation instead brackets necessity around g's existence, leaving action outside it. That paragraph was visually checked. Its literal equivalence/instantiation should not be copied as a valid modal derivation.

The faithful intended reading is that some actual g exists at every relevant possibility and actually acts to bring about fixed P. This differs both from necessary existence of *some* occupant and from necessary production of P. The intended conclusion is intelligible without the paragraph's scope slide. It still requires a de re bearer interpretation and the earlier explanatory argument for it.

The later chapter changes the explanans. A necessary broad choice/reason-appreciation state need not entail this P. The 1999 successful-production proposition does entail P. Reusing the earlier q-implies-P modal-collapse argument against the later q would therefore change its meaning. The accepted modal adapter preserves authenticated interpretation; it cannot manufacture either de re identity or explanatory relevance from a necessary proposition.

Likewise, necessary existence is not by definition self-explanation, productive nonreceipt, or an essence-based ontological demonstration. The original connects these through further commitments. The later contingent-only PSR can leave necessary truths outside its demand. Neither move supplies the original bridge's whole-existence reception predicates.

## 6. Voluntary acts and the strongest surviving explanation

An intrinsic voluntary token, an action proposition, the necessary bearer's existence, and the created effect stay separate. Contingency of the first two does not imply external reception of the bearer. Nothing in this audit inserts Emergent→Created, new-token→borrowed-perfection, or action→beginning-of-agent.

The 1999 self-explanation is best read as claimed personal explanatory completion/regress termination. It is not efficient self-production by a previously nonexistent agent. Its contextual permission to supply motives means that “free” does not mean “reasonless.” The exact transfer question is whether that adequacy answers the inherited demand for a particular differentiating basis, rather than merely a generic capacity or an occurrence-labelled will.

The later proposal deserves a stronger appraisal. It identifies reason-appreciation and actual acting-on as relevant, rejects the notion that removing external determination alone constitutes agency, and allows explanations without entailment. Given the actual truth of E(q,P), its explanation status must be contingent whenever P is contingent and E is factive: it is true actually, but at a ¬P world E(q,P) is false, whatever q's necessary truth. Proposing that q explains E(q,P) does not contradict this; that higher explanatory relation may itself be contingent. Factivity alone produces no modal collapse.

The unresolved question is consequently not “How can a necessary q entail a contingent P?” It is whether identifying the appropriate reason-subset explains why that subset was **action-guiding**, and whether this further question is answered or is a **particular contrastive demand** outside the defended PSR. The adequacy of the target programme’s stronger original-determination premise is a further philosophical issue; it cannot be established merely by locating the difference in scope. On a nonnecessitating interpretation, no inconsistency has been shown. On a stronger complete-determiner interpretation, a further adequacy argument is required. Pruss expressly permits a noncontrastive fallback, so the stronger demand cannot be imposed on him merely by the word “sufficient.” His preferred reason-subset account also aims to explain choices contrastively; it must not be reduced to the fallback as though he offered nothing stronger.

The inherited source review also requires fairness here: an unexplained divine selection receives no blanket exemption, but a fully articulated wise determination for every actual act has not been exhibited by the affirmative programme either. Neither side wins by defining its own explanation standard as the other's premise. The later chapter's apparent “God is explained by R” wording is preserved unamended; no finding depends on correcting it.

## 7. Exact OriginalBearerBridge mapping

- **Completion:** actual H_P supplies an explanation, not yet an actual unreceived concrete intrinsic resource relevant to a received target. The target's receipt and the explanation's productive interpretation remain substantive.
- **Realisation:** an actual intentional action report can existentially involve an agent under its interpretation. This is not universal bearer-realisation for every admitted explanatory item, particularly an autonomous qualitative obtaining fact.
- **SupportTransport:** presupposition of an agent's existence by its act is not the saved implication from whole-existence reception to resource reception. The latter needs its own ontological support claim.
- **ContingencyNeed:** excluding a contingent explaining agent through explanatory coverage does not establish that every actual nonnecessary bearer is wholly received. Necessity obtained by that separate route cannot silently supply this universal premise.
- **ConstitutiveReception/essential independence:** neither bare necessity nor the actualisation lemma supplies these. Necessary receipt remains conceptually distinct from necessary existence.

Thus there is no faithful all-premise import from this argument into the saved interface. The old conditional theorem survives unchanged. The new positive gain is a clean transport principle for the explanatory totality and a more exact account of what is lost by restricting its target.
