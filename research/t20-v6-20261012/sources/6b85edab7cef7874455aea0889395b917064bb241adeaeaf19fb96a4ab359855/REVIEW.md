## Focal reading checks

### Printed 44–49

The argument distinguishes knowledgeable conviction from conviction without knowledge. Printed 45 explicitly allows erroneous affirmation and erroneous denial about knowing, and distinguishes particular causes of perceptual or rational error from healthy cognition as the original condition. This supports the report's refusal to equate certainty with knowledge or a local defect with universal defeat. It does not render every person's avowal infallible.

At printed 47 the author rejects requiring an inferential proof that one knows before any premises can count as known: the proof would itself require knowledge of its premises and generate a circle or regress. The following discussion positively permits knowledge through an effective argument whose premises are known. These are authorial comments, not a quoted opponent's view.

The page-47 footnote records an editorial intervention: the base wording contains the negative `لا يحس`, whereas the edited main text is affirmative. Both the saved page image and my independent render of PDF page 102 display it. The report explicitly discloses it and does not rest the conclusion on that edited clause. The succeeding authorial sentence about an argument that yields knowledge is independently present and supports the narrower inference point. This is not an independent manuscript authentication of that succeeding text.

The report correctly distinguishes authorial analysis, reported sayings, and the editor's work. This review neither adopts historical polemical classifications as diagnoses of present people nor verifies the reported sayings.

### Printed 265–270

The chapter opens against making formal logic obligatory and against treating acquaintance with it as necessary for confidence in every item of knowledge. The p265–266 passage is a concessive interpretation within that polemic: some correct material in the practice can remove some error, even if beneficial truth does not result and another error is acquired. The frozen report now gives that concessive setting explicitly.

The passage supports limited error-removal amid substantial error. It does not establish warranted positive replacement, a generally reliable restorative method, or an empirical treatment effect. The report properly separates abandoning support, rejecting an error for a good reason, and attaining warranted positive recognition. Its positive known-premise case depends additionally on p47 and on the inherited operative-ground conditions, rather than extracting positive warrant from p265 alone.

At p269 the author separately distinguishes inference from the formal logical art used to regulate it and rejects an unsupported exclusivity claim about that art. This context fits the report's refusal to identify a particular criticized argumentative practice with all cognition. Reported opponent concessions and the author's own criticism remain distinguishable. The surrounding historical judgments are not evidence about any contemporary individual.

### Printed 271–272

The report accurately identifies the tension in the pinned material. Sound-reason lines 139–145 place all faculties' proper orientation under fiṭrah and include the blanket dependence sentence. Lines 393–409 simultaneously describe sound inference as restorative under significant impairment. Lines 430–436 prescribe filter-loosening and inferential engagement before returning to activation.

Lines 199–206 direct a stable truth-seeking exchange to the appropriate objection route and distinguish a softened filter from recognition or full alignment. These are operational grounds for reading the restoration demand locally. They do not themselves establish an externalist metaphysics of warrant.

The report's inference is valid within its stated target. The global-collapse argument needs a bridge from a defect in route A to the failure of every relevant operation of the native constitution. Common dependence on that constitution supplies no such bridge by itself. A surviving route B would not be external to native cognition merely because it avoids A's defeated support. Nor must B first deliver a reflective certificate of the entire person's epistemic health.

There is no disguised proof that B actually exists in a disputed present episode. The constructive case stipulates genuinely known comparison grounds and competent use, and the report explicitly admits failure where the defeater reaches those grounds, interpretation, memory, or competence. This answers a claim of inevitable internal self-defeat; it does not answer every substantive challenge to the reality of B. If a supported shared-capacity defeater reaches both routes, the application must be revisited.

A literal globally disabling interpretation of the isolated blanket sentence remains in tension with restoration. The report's successful result is therefore conditional on its source-supported local/constitutive interpretation. Its wording about an “actual scope limit” should be understood as the justified interpretive limit established by the whole context, not as evidence that the original sentence is free of ambiguity.
