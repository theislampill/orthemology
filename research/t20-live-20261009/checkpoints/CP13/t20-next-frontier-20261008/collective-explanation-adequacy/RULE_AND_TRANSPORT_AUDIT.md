# Exact rules and the scope of the adequacy inference

This is an analytic source-mapping audit, not a new metaphysical model or a new measurement theorem. Source: Dasgupta 2014, [published PDF](https://mail.shamik.net/papers/dasgupta%20on%20the%20plurality%20of%20grounds.pdf), especially pp. 4–5, 10–11, 18–24 and notes 11–15, 22, 37–38, 45.

## 1. Typed notation without extra ontology

Write G(X,Y) for the source's plural explanatory claim, with X a list/plurality of grounds and Y a list/plurality of grounded facts. This is convenient notation for its official sentential connective. It is not a commitment to independent abstract fact-objects. Logically equivalent contents may remain distinct for explanatory purposes.

Use Q for an appropriate complete qualitative basis and I for the individualistic plurality that it is proposed to ground. No statement here says Q is actual, autonomous, unreceived, or necessarily obtaining merely because G(Q,I) is under discussion. Actual explanatory use would be factive; that does not construct an actual Q.

## 2. Source commitments and their status

1. **Necessitation**, p. 4: G(X,Y) implies that obtaining of all X necessitates obtaining of all Y. The converse is rejected. No entailment from grounded to grounds is assumed.
2. **Relevance**, pp. 4–5: each ground must contribute to the explanation of the grounded plurality. Note 11 explicitly permits collective relevance without requiring relevance to any member taken separately. Relevance is not defined by membership in a successful ground, since that would make the principle empty.
3. **Derivative substitution**, p. 5: the following two patterns specify a transitive closure, but primitive G is not assumed to satisfy them.
4. **Conjunction grounding**, p. 11: conjuncts ground their conjunction. This is a separate substantive grounding judgment, not permission to substitute logical equivalents inside G.
5. **Accounting**, p. 11: grounds account for p if they plurally ground some facts whose logical consequence is p. Accounting need not preserve explanatory relevance to p.
6. **No established general sufficiency axiom**, p. 18: the author considers the strategy of deriving ground from relevance plus necessitation, then develops the more informative linguistic-role argument. This passage is not a proof of that sufficiency principle.

## 3. The two actual substitution patterns

Let concatenation denote collecting the indicated pluralities, and let D be the closure of G under the following patterns:

    G(X,Y), G(Y + U,Z)  ==>  D(X + U,Z)

    G(X,Y + U), G(Y,Z)  ==>  D(X,U + Z)

Closure means the analogous steps can be iterated with derivative claims. The first pattern substitutes an explanation for a complete intermediate input. The second substitutes consequences **grounded by** part of a plural output while retaining the unconsumed output U. These are the source's two patterns in new notation, not extra author-endorsed axioms of primitive ground.

They do not supply:

    G(X,Y + U)  ==>  G(X,Y)

or:

    Y entails Z, G(X,Y)  ==>  G(X,Z).

The latter describes part of accounting, not a grounding rule. Nor is primitive G identified with its derivative closure. Arbitrarily adding irrelevant grounds or discarding explananda to which existing grounds were relevant is not licensed by these patterns.

If I grounds its conjunction C, the first substitution yields D(Q,{C}) from G(Q,I). That explains the paper's derivative-conjunction remark. It does not allow C to ground each conjunct. An alternative that grounds C directly while leaving its conjuncts independently groundless receives an explanatory-overdetermination objection, not a formal inconsistency proof, on p. 11.

This is not a ban on multiple complete grounds. Notes 4 and 12 explicitly allow a true disjunction to be fully grounded in either true disjunct. The p. 11 objection targets two unconnected explanatory bases in that particular conjunction-only reconstruction. It supplies no general causal nonduplication principle, no common-source theorem, and no exclusion of a coexisting productive explanation.

## 4. The ordinary existential consequence test

Let e be one individual-existence member of I, let J be the rest, and let E be an ordinary existential generalization that at least something exists. Assume, additionally and solely for this test, G({e},{E}). This is a familiar existential grounding judgment, but it is not derived from mere material implication.

From:

    G(Q,{e} + J), G({e},{E})

the second pattern yields:

    D(Q,J + {E}).

It does not yield G(Q,{E}), and the two displayed patterns provide no deletion of J. Alternatively, a separate G(I,{E}) premise would license D(Q,{E}) by the first pattern. Membership and existential entailment alone do not supply that premise.

This test prevents exchanging the actual existential question for the primitive-identity question without noticing. The formal distinction survives the change to an existential sentence. But it does **not** show that an adequate collective explanation fails to answer an existential concern. Whether it is adequate is precisely the philosophical question, and the productive theorem can already use the whole received field as one target. Nothing here creates a new need for singleton distribution.

## 5. What measurement mathematics can and cannot transport

Note 38 invokes standard representation and uniqueness results: under suitable constraints, positive mass-ratio data admit numerical representations, unique up to multiplication of the whole assignment by a positive constant. The source explicitly simplifies the underlying measurement theory and refers to Krantz and colleagues for the fuller results. No independent proof of that general theory is claimed here.

This supports the existence of a coordinated numerical language. The inferential rule compares two numerical assignments and recovers the corresponding worldly ratio. Its full-system role is therefore not reducible to treating each numerical value as a self-sufficient report of an extra intrinsic magnitude.

But these three transformations differ:

- **Common rescaling of an encoding:** preserves every ratio. It supplies another representation of the same ratio information. It is not automatically a distinct physical possibility under fixed predicate interpretation.
- **Changing one object's assigned value while holding the others fixed:** changes its ratios to them. It is not common rescaling and is not excluded by the source's structuralism.
- **Choosing a cross-world mass-counterpart interpretation:** determines which ordinary mass claims a relationally described possibility represents. Section 10 makes this context-sensitive; it is not an identity map or a new physical same-mass relation across worlds.

Consequently, a proposed disproof based only on two scaled arrays would not yet engage the source's modal response. A proposed proof of grounding based only on successful ratio reconstruction would equally fail to engage its metaphysical claim. The live bridge is the abductive argument that, under the relevant interpretation of the discourse and ontology, the recovered worldly structure suffices to explain the nonsemantic facts expressed together.

This is why an executable scaling calculation would add no decision-relevant evidence here. It would restate the background mathematics while leaving precisely that bridge untested. No arbitrary assignment of G is presented as a metaphysical countermodel.

## 6. Modal and productive interpretation guards

The applicability of the necessity principle after the counterpart reply remains explicitly unsettled in note 45. Do not treat the preceding rule inventory as a completed S5 model, a fixed global bearer semantics, or a proof that a particular individual exists at every possible world. The suggested connection to essential necessity is not a demonstrated autonomous essence.

Similarly, D, G, and accounting are not definitions of productive receipt, resource actuality, intrinsic realization, or nonreceipt. Enlarging the output target from an individual to a whole field changes no one of those predicates by itself. A successful translation into the actual original-bearer interface would need independent warrant for each relevant interpretation.

The surviving formal result is therefore a faithful boundary: the source licenses specific context-preserving compositions and complete-system representations, but it does not license silent projection, ontology from encoding, actuality from representability, or productive nonreceipt from collective explanation.
