# Original authority, owed response, and a permission that does not surrender control

9 October 2026 UTC. Bounded affirmative T20 continuation. No integration, protected edit, contemporary duty adjudication, or T20 closure.

## Result

**The retained argument establishes more than the goodness of admiring a benefactor: it warrants, defeasibly, an owed practical response to the actual perfect original source. It does not establish every proposed requirement-making title merely from production or gratitude.** The strongest primary argument expressly includes obedience. Its accepted force should neither be erased by a generic “is–ought” objection nor inflated into a proof of unrestricted control over every creaturely choice.

The executed increment is a source-grounded restriction on a live title objection. **Prior nonprohibition does not by itself establish an unconditional grant relinquishing future control.** Ibn al-Qayyim explicitly distinguishes original default nonprohibition from a positively legislated rule at *Miftāḥ* 2:943. Applying that distinction to CP02's prior-grant comparison blocks an unsupported route from “this was previously open” to “the source surrendered the right to specify it.” It does not prove that every domain remains under such control, or refute a separately warranted relinquishing grant.

The same source's positive obedience argument already distinguishes unsurpassable worth from the performance a finite recipient can supply. Its concrete recommended/obligatory distinctions confirm that appropriate responsiveness follows the **actual normative mode** of the source's direction. This is compatible with both command-added relational goodness and a particular nonbinding intercession. It defeats no inherited claim that genuine rightful obligatory commands ought to be obeyed.

## 1. What was already established

The actual T17/T19 paragraphs, CP02 appraisal and quotation check, and the CP22–CP23 normative returns were checked before selecting the operation.

The retained positive argument combines original and continuing provision, real benefaction, complete practical wisdom and goodness, and the fittingness of directed personal acknowledgment. Practical response need not be a payment that benefits God. Indifference does not cancel an independently warranted claim. Such evaluative premises can have substantive, defeasible warrant without being deduced from norm-free propositions.

Three achievements must remain distinct:

1. **An owed orientation and response.** The actual wise, good original giver warrants practical acknowledgment. This is stronger than merely finding acknowledgment attractive.
2. **A valid scoped specification principle.** Where the source possesses continuing rightful control over a use, an admissible, feasible and adequately communicated exercise can determine what is owed. Previously available good alternatives do not defeat that granted control.
3. **A testimonial route to an obligation.** An independently authenticated, relevantly knowledgeable and source-owned truthful assertion that a recipient is obligated can warrant that normative fact. It need not itself create the obligation or await a second independent derivation of the same duty.

The first supplies substantive relational reasons. The second unpacks an operative normative title; it does not independently confer that title upon every producer. The third supplies warranted recognition of a normative fact through testimony. T19 explicitly retained this route. None depends on an arbitrary ruler's ability to force compliance.

The exact open bridge is consequently bounded: **why does the source's standing entitle it to determine this otherwise open action or form of response, with these conditions and recipients?** A purported obligatory address also requires the right content and force. The general truth that a genuine rightful binding command binds does not independently establish those antecedents.

CP23 explicitly preserves the title-and-domain question. The later-history check found no superseding general title proof in the directly relevant CP22–CP23 records. This is a bounded relevant-record check, not a declaration that the entire corpus contains no further argument.

## 2. The strongest developed positive argument

The received *Miftāḥ* argument at 2:1076–1089 is Ibn al-Qayyim's reply to the objection that God gains nothing from obedience and loses nothing through disobedience. He accepts divine sufficiency and denies the anti-obligation inference. The alternatives he criticizes are bare willing, a wage-exchange rationale, and a conception of religion as preparation for philosophy. His own rationale is intrinsic worth together with benefaction.

At 1077–1080, the source is worthy of worship independently of reward, punishment and the recipient's actual worship. Rational and natural recognition precedes detailed revealed instruction; revelation confirms, completes and specifies it. At 1081–1082, perfect loveworthiness grounds worship, obedience and practical concern for what pleases this source; the response is expressly called incumbent on every rational agent. A merely motivational or advisory paraphrase would understate the text.

Its strongest reconstruction is:

- Recognition of complete original perfection is recognition of an appropriately supreme object of practical love, not merely a useful service provider.
- Adequate love answers the object as it actually is. In this relation, fitting response includes practical allegiance and obedience, rather than only detached praise.
- A genuinely rightful obligatory direction can specify that response. Treating such a warranted requirement as optional would fail the acknowledged relation.

The substantive evaluative step is the move from actual perfect loveworthiness and source relation to **owed practical allegiance**. The source supports it through rational/fitra appraisal and the relation between love and action. The programme already accepts its defeasible force. Neither the label “obedience” nor the psychological fact that lovers seek to please independently proves every requirement-making power. This identifies the level at which the argument must be appraised; it is not a reason to withdraw its earned warrant.

There is no need to add a maximization premise. At 1083 the worth of the source exceeds what creatures can perform or comprehend. At 1089 the response sought is according to their powers and capacities, not according to the full measure of what the source deserves. These are the source's own qualifications within the argument. They distinguish intrinsic worth, aspiration and finite performance rather than mechanically turning every higher excellence into a duty.

The fresh concrete control is at 939: fasting is obligatory, while fasting together with charity is more complete and recommended. At 940, the obligation of charity before private consultation is removed while recommendation remains. These cases concern the author's account of divine legislation, not an inference from a creaturely leader's jurisdiction to God's.

Thus “every superior response is obligatory” is an unwarranted strengthening, not the retained conclusion or a premise attributed to the author. The source permits a less complete performance to meet the stated obligation while a more complete one remains recommended. **This does not refute “every genuine rightful obligatory command should be obeyed.”** It helps explain that thesis without the stronger maximization claim: obedience tracks what the source actually requires; optional excellence answers what it recommends.

The passage, its recommendation distinction and the general obedience argument were already read. The new credit is their controlled application to the present authority question, not their discovery.

## 3. Executed discriminator: nonprohibition is not an irrevocable grant

CP02 distinguishes an owner retaining rightful selection control from an owner making an unconditional grant that relinquishes it. Its strongest domain objection is that a particular sphere may have been entrusted to the creature's own discretion. This is a coherent objection when such a grant is warranted. CP02 did not claim that mere prior permissibility proves it.

The present primary return supplies a concrete source test of that tempting additional inference.

### Continuous setting

At *Miftāḥ* 926–927, the author reports an objection that intrinsic good and bad could not change with circumstances or abrogation. His reply from 928 distinguishes a real source of benefit/harm from an invariant classification. The ensuing examples address changes of time, recipient and circumstances. At 938–943 he explains how a benefit can be preserved under a changed legal mode. He then draws the distinction central here:

- Removal of a rule established by a legislative address is one kind of change.
- Ending the status sustained by original default nonprohibition is another.
- A delayed prohibition need not imply that the earlier act was intrinsically beneficial or positively legislated as good.

The author uses historical prohibitions of intoxicants and usury. These are his doctrinal examples; this report neither independently adjudicates their legal histories nor applies them to a present person. His statement of agreement on the technical definition of abrogation is his attributed consensus, not a newly verified consensus.

### The inference tested

Suppose the evidence establishes only that an action was previously unrestricted under the relevant default. That fact is compatible with the source's account of later restriction. It supplies no positive act whose content relinquishes later selection authority. A declaration that control was surrendered therefore requires further evidence about the grant and its terms.

This is not a demand to prove every permission explicitly. Default permission can itself be a genuine governing normative status. The point is that **permission for a present action and immunity from a later in-scope change are different claims**. Establishing the first does not establish the second.

Nor may an interpreter convert ignorance of a prohibition into proof that no prohibition exists. Source-law status, warranted default conduct under limited evidence, and a positively communicated grant remain separate. The passage's analysis of actual delayed prohibition should not be flattened into every case of a recipient's uncertainty.

### Positive consequence and limit

An objection to a warranted in-scope specification cannot rely merely on the act's having been open before. It must identify an unconditional grant, a protected right, a limit of jurisdiction or another reason why this specification exceeds retained control. The new source application therefore removes one inadequate evidential basis for the prior-grant objection and preserves the practical significance of the already warranted scoped control principle.

The inference stops exactly there. Lack of evidence for surrendered control does not prove universal retained authority. An independently established unconditional grant would still matter, and a merely apparent obligating utterance would still require force and attribution checks. The source distinction discriminates the evidence for CP02's cases; it does not settle every case by assigning it to retained control.

The underlying 943 distinction is explicitly present in the inherited middle-section notes. The checked authority appraisals did not execute this particular application. Its novelty is a source-grounded restriction on an inference supporting a live objection, not a new general theory or a correction of a claim CP02 actually made.

## 4. Relation to command-added good and Barīra

An actual rightful direction can make a performance available as a fitting response to that very source and thereby add relational goodness. The value of that relation need not determine its deontic grade. The original source's recommended charity supplies a concrete control: authentic normative guidance and additional excellence can exist without a requirement to perform every extra good.

Barīra supplies a different, occurrence-sensitive control. The acknowledged authoritative speaker identifies his present act as intercession rather than commanding. Her declining that proposal therefore need not be defiance of an obligatory direction. That sequence does not surrender his general standing, and it does not independently establish or limit God's original title.

The examples should not be merged:

- The charity case includes an actual change of normative requirement while a recommendation survives.
- Barīra's exchange clarifies the force of an address; no prior obligation was first established and then repealed.
- The default-permission case need not involve a prior positively enacted rule at all.

Together they give due response definite content: answer the actual source relation and act, including its stated limits, rather than a stronger demand reconstructed from rank or the desirability of the outcome. Conversely, relabeling a warranted obligatory command as advice would not respect that relation.

## 5. The tested perfection shortcut

A possible shortcut was considered: a perfectly good and knowledgeable source would not knowingly issue an unauthorized purported imposition; therefore any such source-owned directive must have rightful force.

This is not independently established by the inspected argument. If the address truthfully asserts that an obligation or relevant standing exists, the inference runs through the already retained testimonial route. If it merely attempts to constitute an obligation, it still needs a warranted premise that this particular unauthorized attempt would be intrinsically defective in the relevant respect. Perfect goodness excludes defects; it does not classify every proposed act as defective without that substantive appraisal.

A sincere nonbinding request is an obvious different case. An intended constitutive exercise is also distinct from its successful normative effect. Calling the act a “genuine binding command” would already build the successful title into the premise. The present operation does not silently convert either intention or an apparent authority claim into its truth.

This leaves an exact obstruction rather than an empty appeal to an “is–ought gap”: the general positive due-response appraisal is earned, but a new title/domain application requires substantive support or a suitably authenticated truthful normative assertion. No available passage inspected here closes that bridge for every conceivable action.
