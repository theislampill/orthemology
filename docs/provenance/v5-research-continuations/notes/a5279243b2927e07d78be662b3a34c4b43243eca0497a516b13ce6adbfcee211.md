## The nested-prefix matroid is genuine

Let f(k)=floor((1−t)k). It is integral, nondecreasing, starts at zero and increases by at most one per step. Prefix-cap independent sets form a matroid. The given exchange proof works: if every element of J outside I is blocked, each belongs to a tight prefix; the largest such prefix contains all those elements. Inside that prefix J cannot exceed I's cardinality, and outside it J is a subset of I. This contradicts |J|>|I|. The empty/no-tight-prefix case is harmless.

The proposed rank formula is exact, not just an upper bound. For a maximum independent subset I of A, each excluded element must lie in a tight prefix. Taking the largest such prefix leaves A and I identical outside it and saturates the cap inside it. If I=A, k=0 gives equality. This proves r(A)=min_k(|A outside P_k|+f(k)) without presuming the desired two-ordering conclusion.

The matroids can have loops, especially among early-ranked roots. That does not invalidate the standard intersection theorem. The inspected Stanford statement and proof cover arbitrary finite matroids; the separate modern primary-paper statement matches the same rank formula. No loopless hypothesis is silently transferred from a neighboring algorithm discussion.

## The two-matroid intersection bound

For every A,k,l, each element outside both prefixes is counted once in |A outside P1_k|+|(E outside A) outside P2_l|. Their sum is consequently at least max(0,u−k−l). Since the two ranks minimize independently over k and l, their sum is bounded below by the corresponding two-index minimum. This is the correct direction of the inequality.

If k+l≥u, reduce the indices to a pair summing to u and use monotonicity of f. If k+l≤u, increase them to such a pair and use the one-step Lipschitz bound for f. Both cases yield a lower bound by min_s[f(s)+f(u−s)], which equals u−max_s[ceil(ts)+ceil(t(u−s))]. Under the threshold hypothesis this is at least u−b.

Edmonds' two-matroid intersection theorem therefore supplies a common independent set of at least u−b elements. Downward closure permits retaining exactly u−b. Its complement C has exactly b elements and satisfies every required prefix quota in both orders. Neither the adversary nor the proof chooses a different C for the two rows.

## Transfer to arbitrary row weights

Sort each probability row in its own descending order. For its cumulative selected counts a_k, the identity p(C)=sum_k(p_k−p_{k+1})a_k holds with p_{u+1}=0. All coefficient differences are nonnegative. Substituting a_k≥ceil(tk)≥tk gives p(C)≥t sum_k k(p_k−p_{k+1})=t. Thus the same C reaches at least B in both arbitrary rows. The construction of a B-attaining partition gives the matching upper bound.

The result is consequently a continuum theorem. It does not assume rational denominators, independent failures, disjoint supports of the input rows, strict rankings or deterministic decoders. An effective certificate algorithm for rational rows follows from standard matroid intersection with the stated independence oracles; the argument does not promise an effective encoding of arbitrary real inputs or a production implementation.
