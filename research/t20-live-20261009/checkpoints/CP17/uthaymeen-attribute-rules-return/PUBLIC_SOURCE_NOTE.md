# Source note: Ibn ʿUthaymīn, al-Qawāʿid al-Muthlā

Work: *القواعد المثلى في صفات الله وأسمائه الحسنى*, Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymin.

## Bounded finding

The book gives rational arguments for generic divine perfection and particular revealed attributes, including mercy. It also reserves divine names and detailed attribution to revelation, and rejects automatically deriving a divine name from an attributed action.

My explanatory interpretation is that rational support for some revealed attributes can coexist with text-governed naming and detailed predication. This is an interpretation, not the author's verbatim formulation or permission to invent further divine attributes.

The reading distinguishes essential qualification from voluntary act-occurrences, shared predication from identical concrete character, and differentiated attributes from separate self-standing bearers. These distinctions constrain how the arguments should be represented; they do not establish the possibility of multiple original producers.

No developed derivation from complete original productive adequacy to sole actual production was identified in the available witness. The perfection reasoning remains relevant to evaluating that transition, but should not be counted as a new, independent resolution of it.
