# Independent countercontrols: stochastic clocks do not preserve nonatomic defect

The retained definition is `P02A2.defect(mu)=1-(mu {x : mu {x}>0}).toReal`. Equality after a measurable receipt projection does not imply equality of the raw source-output defect. Positive finite stuttering preserves recurrent priorities, but that also does not imply defect preservation.

## Constant receipts with random acknowledgment times

Use the admitted two-state fixture-shaped controller a=s, initial state0, with every row returning receipt0 deterministically. The common denominator is D=1 and the specified sampler deliberately uses L=1: bit0 accepts and bit1 rejects. Thus every accepted receipt is0, and infinitely many receipts occur almost surely. The decoded receipt law is the Dirac law on the constant-zero stream, with defect0.

The framed physical output has acknowledgment `not x(4n)` at the first position of frame n and zero in its three remaining positions. Its first k acknowledgment bits have every prescribed pattern with probability2^(-k). Every singleton physical stream therefore has probability at most2^(-k) for every k, hence zero. Its physical Boolean-output law is atomless, with defect1. `DEFECT_CLOCK_COUNTERCONTROL.json` records exact finite checks against the native P02 compiler and codec; the infinite conclusion follows from the explicit cylinder calculation, not extrapolation from those checks.

## Even the held-symbol stream can change defect

Let the logical stream be the fixed alternating sequence0,1,0,1,..., which has defect0. Hold each logical symbol for an independent geometric(1/2) positive number of ticks. Every hold ends almost surely, so this is positive finite stuttering and preserves the set of infinitely recurrent priorities.

A two-state fair-bit Mealy machine realizes the held stream: initial state0, output the current state, and stay if the bit is1 or flip if the bit is0. After the fixed first output0, each next state is conditionally fair, so each compatible k-symbol tail cylinder has probability2^(-k). Every physical singleton has zero mass; its defect is1. This construction fits the finite-table P02 compiler directly.

These controls do not contradict the accepted runtime theorem. Its compiler defect solver applies to the full physical Boolean output. Its rational receipt-law equality applies after the specified measurable decoder. Its parity theorem applies to a positive-stutter interpretation of held priorities. Those are distinct observed laws and must remain distinct in applications.
