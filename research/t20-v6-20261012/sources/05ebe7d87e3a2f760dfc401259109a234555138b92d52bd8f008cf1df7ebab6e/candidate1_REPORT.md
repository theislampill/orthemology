## 6. A conservative rational-grid approximate-Nash estimate

Fix a finite normal-form game with nonempty individual strategy sets and a finite real payoff or cost at every joint profile. Joint hard constraints must already be handled by an admissible strategy-product restriction, allocator/protocol, or a separately justified constrained-game construction.

Write costs as J_i and assume |J_i(s)| ≤ M_i. Let p be an exact mixed Nash equilibrium. Let q be another independent mixed profile and put:

```text
eta_j = ||p_j - q_j||_1.
```

For independent product laws, replacing one marginal at a time gives:

```text
|| product_j p_j - product_j q_j ||_1 ≤ sum_j eta_j.
```

For any bounded cost array, its expectation therefore changes by at most M_i times the corresponding sum. The incumbent cost changes by at most M_i sum_j eta_j. The cost of any fixed unilateral deviation depends only on the opponents' product law and changes by at most M_i sum_(j != i) eta_j.

Because regret at p is zero, these two estimates give:

```text
Reg_i(q) ≤ M_i eta_i + 2 M_i sum_(j != i) eta_j.
```

This is a deliberately conservative sufficient bound. It is not the sharpest available estimate, a proof that every equilibrium moves continuously under a game perturbation, or a claim of novelty.

For a probability vector with m_j coordinates, largest-remainder rounding to denominator K gives a probability vector q_j with:

```text
||p_j - q_j||_1 ≤ m_j / K.
```

To see this, multiply all probabilities by K, take their integer floors, and distribute the missing integer units to the largest fractional remainders. Each coordinate changes by at most 1/K and the total remains one. A zero coordinate is not introduced into the support: when a positive number of units is missing, there are more positive fractional remainders than missing units, so no zero remainder is needed.

Consequently:

```text
Reg_i(q) ≤ (M_i / K) [m_i + 2 sum_(j != i) m_j].
```

For positive epsilon, a denominator making every displayed bound at most epsilon supplies at least one grid epsilon-equilibrium. The assertion is existential: it does not provide an efficient method of finding p, choosing the game, evaluating its costs, or searching the grid.

At epsilon = 0, this argument does not provide a universal finite rational grid containing an exact equilibrium. Rounding strategy probabilities also does not repair an ill-defined joint feasibility model.

### 6.1 What the test establishes

The shipped tests check support-preserving largest-remainder rounding on a declared finite rational family and evaluate the conservative regret inequality around an explicitly verified two-by-two mixed equilibrium. These tests neither exhaust finite games nor prove the general estimate; the preceding inequalities supply the argument.

## 7. Set-valued acceptance, covers, and observation trees

Let W and C be finite nonempty sets. For each w in W, let A(w) be a nonempty subset of C: the candidates acceptable in that situation for one fixed declared decision.

A deterministic retained record r: W → Z is sufficient for a sound choice exactly when every attained record fibre has a common acceptable candidate:

```text
for every z in r(W):  intersection_(w in r^-1(z)) A(w) is nonempty.
```

Necessity follows because the same record must produce the same output candidate. Sufficiency follows by selecting a common candidate on each nonempty fibre. This does not require identifying w.

Define the candidate cover number:

```text
kappa(A) =
min {|D| : D is a subset of C and, for every w, D intersects A(w)}.
```

The minimum number of good record blocks equals kappa(A), assuming an unrestricted deterministic encoder with access to the situation.

**Proof.** A good partition supplies one common candidate per block; the selected candidates cover all worlds. Conversely, a candidate cover lets each world choose one covering candidate; the nonempty fibres of that assignment form a good partition with no more blocks than selected candidates. The two minimum values therefore agree.

### 7.1 This is not merely graph colouring

Pairwise common acceptability is insufficient for a whole record block. For example:

```text
A(w1) = {a,b}
A(w2) = {b,c}
A(w3) = {a,c}
```

Every pair intersects, but the triple intersection is empty. One record value cannot support a uniformly sound output. Two candidate values suffice.

An exact conflict encoding can use the hypergraph of minimal world subsets whose acceptable-set intersection is empty. Good record blocks contain no such hyperedge. A graph retaining only pairwise conflicts loses higher-order obstructions. The candidate-side set-cover formulation and the world-side good-partition formulation are equivalent here; a pairwise graph surrogate need not be.

This is an application of conventional finite-set reasoning, not a new hypergraph theorem.

### 7.2 The fixed-target leaf bound

Suppose a deterministic decision process has at most m retained record values and observes at most d additional binary responses on each run. All controller initialization, source-dependent constants, accessible checkpoints, retained calibration and other pre-existing source information must be included in the record account or fixed independently of w.

For each retained record value, the binary observation tree has at most 2^d leaves. There are therefore at most m·2^d nonempty terminal blocks. Each sound terminal block supplies a candidate acceptable throughout that block. Hence:

```text
kappa(A) ≤ m · 2^d.
```

For b retained bits, m ≤ 2^b gives:

```text
b + d ≥ ceil(log2 kappa(A)).
```

This is a necessary information condition for the specified deterministic, zero-error, fixed-target task. It is not sufficient for physically available observations, affordable state preparation, executable encoding, safe intervention, or actual continuation.

An optimal abstract record need not be implementable through the source's available observation interface. For instance, a record that literally reports w cannot be constructed by an encoder whose permitted observations merge the relevant worlds and whose repertoire cannot separate them.

### 7.3 Why a per-query budget cannot be used as an all-answers budget

Let w be a k-bit string. A later request asks for one specified coordinate. With an available coordinate probe, no source-dependent retained bits are necessary and one response suffices for that request.

Recovering all k coordinates in one shared run is a different task. Issuing the k coordinate probes uses k responses, not one. Thus:

```text
for every query, there is a one-probe answering procedure
```

does not imply:

```text
there is a one-probe procedure returning all query answers.
```

Nor may a per-query d be inserted into a simultaneous-vector lower bound without changing the task and cost accounting.

Repeated requests require a further declaration: whether answers are retained, whether questions repeat, whether the world changes, whether query resources are renewable, and whether the budget resets. Those are not supplied by the word “recursive.”

## 8. Inquiry can identify the answer and destroy its execution

The initial question and the current operative state must be represented separately. In the supplied destructive example, the process is deciding a label of the initial situation, not assigning a label to whatever state happens to remain after an experiment.

A belief therefore contains pairs:

```text
(current_state, immutable_initial_answer_label).
```

A common action updates the first coordinate. Observation filters the resulting pairs. It does not erase the second coordinate when two current states merge.

Terminal acceptance also needs current executability. A candidate can be correct about the initial situation but no longer performable because the inquiry consumed its energy, calibration, memory or interface.

### 8.1 Exact finite decision construction

For a finite deterministic model, enumerate the reachable labelled beliefs under actions legal in every current possibility. A belief is terminally winning when one candidate satisfies the terminal condition for every pair it contains. Then repeatedly add a belief when some common legal action leads, under every possible observed response, to an already winning belief.

The least fixed point gives sure finite decision for that model. The round in which a belief enters supplies a decreasing rank and hence a finite completion certificate. A self-loop that never reaches a valid terminal belief is not made winning merely because the state space is finite.

The implementation checks its returned rank witness against the actual explored transitions. It imposes an explicit exploration limit; exceeding that limit raises an error rather than being called a losing model.

This is the standard finite backward-reachability construction specialised to the relation-valued, current-executability contract. It is not claimed as a separate new theorem family from the supplied manuscript's initial-label decision theorem.

### 8.2 Resource test

In the toy model, an unknown bit requires one probe. Executing the chosen continuation requires one remaining resource unit.

With one unit initially available, probing reveals the bit but leaves zero units: no sound executable continuation is admitted. With two units, the same probe leaves one unit and the resulting decision succeeds.

The comparison keeps the answer criterion fixed. It changes the available initial resource, not the truth of the initial bit. A checker that tests only “the answer is now known” would miss the decisive failure.

## 9. Potential descent requires both structure and an update rule

A finite exact or ordinal potential gives an ordering compatible with specified unilateral improvements. It does not choose which player moves, guarantee that an available improvement is discovered, supply the evaluations, or prevent simultaneous updates from interfering.

For a fixed finite profile space, every accepted strict unilateral potential decrease visits a new profile. Such accepted changes are therefore finite. To conclude arrival at a pure Nash equilibrium, the process also needs a progress condition: it must not stop or stall forever while a profitable unilateral deviation remains.

The tests enumerate all two-by-two common-cost tables with entries in {0,1,2}, start at every pure profile, follow an explicit strict unilateral improvement rule, and check absence of repetitions, strict cost decrease and terminal unilateral optimality.

A different test repeatedly allows only a player with no strict improvement to update while the other player's improvement remains available. The process stalls before equilibrium. “Enough time” matters only together with a process that uses that time to reach the relevant evaluation and update opportunities.

### 9.1 Simultaneous moves

For common cost

```text
         player 2
          0   1
player 1
    0     1   0
    1     0   1
```

both players best-responding simultaneously from (0,0) move to (1,1), then back to (0,0). Each unilateral change would reduce the cost if the opponent stayed fixed; their simultaneous changes do not.

The asynchronous rule instead moves from (0,0) to an off-diagonal profile and stops at a pure equilibrium. Both statements are checked by executing the actual transition rules.

### 9.2 An interior mixed equilibrium can be a saddle

For the same game, both players mixing equally is a Nash equilibrium. Expected cost is:

```text
C(p,q) = p q + (1-p)(1-q).
```

At p=q=1/2, perturbing both probabilities in the same direction increases expected cost by 2t². Perturbing them in opposite directions decreases it by 2t². The equilibrium is therefore a saddle of this joint expected-cost function.

This does not invalidate Nash equilibrium: Nash concerns unilateral deviations holding the other strategy fixed. Nor does it show that every learning rule repels the point. A stability claim must name its dynamics.

## 10. The logit interpretation needs cardinal alignment

Suppose a finite exact weighted-potential representation has costs:

```text
J_i(s_i,s_-i) = a_i Phi(s_i,s_-i) + b_i(s_-i),  a_i > 0.
```

Suppose player i's logit update selects a replacement action with probability proportional to:

```text
exp(-beta_i J_i(action,s_-i)).
```

The opponent-only term cancels in that conditional choice. The conditional law is proportional to exp(-beta_i a_i Phi). Thus a common reversible Gibbs construction with one parameter lambda requires:

```text
beta_i a_i = lambda  for every updating player.
```

“Compatible interests” or an ordinal potential alone does not establish this cardinal alignment. Similar external constraints also do not by themselves establish equal payoff scales or equal learning sensitivity.

With aligned parameters and state-independent random-scan player selection, detailed balance follows by comparing two profiles that differ in one player's action. The conditional normalisation is the same in both directions, and the Gibbs weight supplies the remaining ratio.

This identifies one precise sufficient route for a probabilistic tendency. It does not derive the update law or its effective potential from physical stationary action.

### 10.1 Exact stationary-law counterexample to a product-law inference

Use common cost:

```text
         player 2
          0   1
player 1
    0     0   2
    1     2   1
```

At equal logit bases 2, the rational Gibbs law in order (00,01,10,11) is:

```text
pi = (1/2, 1/8, 1/8, 1/4).
```

Both marginals are (5/8,3/8). Their independent product is:

```text
(25/64, 15/64, 15/64, 9/64),
```

which differs from pi. Under that product, each player's expected cost is 69/64, while switching unilaterally to action 0 gives 48/64. Each player's regret is therefore 21/64, not zero.

The code constructs the random-scan transition matrix, checks stationarity and detailed balance, computes the marginals and their product, and calculates those regrets. The stationary joint law has not become independent mixed Nash play merely because each player has a marginal action frequency.

### 10.2 Heterogeneous sensitivities can break reversibility

Keep the same potential, but use logit base 2 for one player and 4 for the other. Around the profile cycle

```text
00 -> 10 -> 11 -> 01 -> 00,
```

the product of forward transition probabilities divided by the product of reverse transition probabilities is 8.

A reversible law would make that ratio one by cancellation of its state weights around the cycle. The mismatch therefore defeats the common reversible-potential argument in this model. It does not imply that the finite Markov chain has no stationary law.

This is a useful adversarial condition for the proposed explanatory bridge. It is a conventional cycle-balance calculation, not a meniscus result.
