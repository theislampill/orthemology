## Executed dependency test: retain the grounds of confirming descriptions

The test asks what would be lost by reducing the cumulative argument to informed control of an event. It does not assume that a confirming description is unsupported merely because it states an intermediate conclusion. The author explicitly invokes discriminating sign arguments and a public career; those can warrant the description. Three evidential scopes must be distinguished.

### 1. Mere nonprevention

A source knows a claimant acts, could prevent the act, and does not. This does not identify its practical relation to that act. The *Tibyān* reply expressly allows temporary enablement of wrongful actors; the inherited own-act analysis likewise separates willing a created occurrence from loving the creature's wrongdoing. Thus an unrestricted rule that wise knowledge and power require immediately stopping every false claimant is not available from the complete discussion. This restriction is source-owned, not a general rival doctrine imported to cancel Wisdom.

### 2. Independently described evidential profile

Public claims, their specific content, responses to requests, observable performance, and an independently supported career can be described without first declaring the claimant true. Such a profile can support a reasoned inference to a confirming purpose. This is where the positive contextual and cumulative appraisals operate. The comparison can be stronger than any single element without pretending the elements are independent witnesses.

But a property shared by true and false claimants cannot by itself separate them. The source's patience example directly tests this problem. It does not establish that such a shared property is irrelevant in every larger conjunction, or that it has equal statistical likelihood across all populations. No frequency or likelihood is supplied. The proper local conclusion is failure of standalone discrimination, not zero possible evidential contribution.

### 3. Warranted confirming profile

Where the sign arguments or broader profile already warrant commission and source-owned veracity, those achievements can support the interpretation of a further communicative act. The act's occurrence, attribution, content, and relevant link to that warranted commission still matter. Once those links are warranted, no second wholly separate proof of the same confirming status is required. “Independent” here means not borrowed solely from the disputed conclusion; it does not mean statistical independence or absence of all shared grounds.

The source's argument includes both publicly describable comparisons and conclusions supported through further sign arguments. The latter are legitimate premises when their grounds are warranted. Their deletion tests dependence and the loss of argumentative strength; it does not diagnose circularity. Only substituting the bare label for its grounds would fail, and no such substitution is here established as Ibn al-Qayyim's actual mistake. His argument must not be reduced to brute political success or judged as though its cumulative sign evidence had been absent.
