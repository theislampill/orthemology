## 1. Finite deterministic descent setting

Let `X` be a finite nonempty set, let

\[
r:X\to R
\]

be a declared representation, interface, profile, quotient candidate, or
registered observation map, and let

\[
f:X\to Y
\]

be one independently typed target.

Call `f` **exactly recoverable from** `r` when there exists a decoder

\[
d:r(X)\to Y
\]

such that

\[
f=d\circ r.
\]

Write `ker(r)` for equality of representation values and `ker(f)` for equality
of target values.

## 2. TQDC-1A — exact finite descent characterization

\[
\boxed{
f\text{ is exactly recoverable from }r
\iff
\ker(r)\subseteq\ker(f)
}
\]

Equivalently, `f` is constant on every attained `r`-fibre.

The decoder, when it exists, is unique on `r(X)`.

## 3. TQDC-1B — canonical target quotient

The quotient

\[
Q_f=X/{\ker(f)}
\]

is the unique minimal exact deterministic representation of `f` up to
bijection of attained representation values. Every exact representation `r`
factors onto `Q_f`, and

\[
|r(X)|\ge |Q_f|.
\]

## 4. TQDC-1C — finite family and subdirect image

For targets `f_i:X→Y_i`, let

\[
F=(f_1,\ldots,f_k):X\to\prod_iY_i.
\]

Then

\[
\ker(F)=\bigcap_i\ker(f_i).
\]

The attained joint image `F(X)` projects surjectively onto each attained
coordinate image `f_i(X)`, hence is a subdirect subset of their product. It is
the full product exactly when every tuple of attained coordinate values has a
joint realizer in `X`.

## 5. TQDC-1D — coarsening noncreation

If `r'=c∘r`, then `ker(r)⊆ker(r')`. Therefore:

```text
if f is recoverable from r', it is recoverable from r;
if f is not recoverable from r, no coarsening of r recovers f.
```

A common deterministic coarsening cannot create exact deterministic target
information absent from the original representation.
