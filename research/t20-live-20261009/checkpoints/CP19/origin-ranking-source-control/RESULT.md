# Testing an internal origin-ranking objection

9 October 2026. Targeted reflective source return, with no new first-reading, whole-volume, kernel or closure claim.

## Question and outcome

Could Ibn al-Qayyim's rejection of ranking a creature by its material origin undermine the source-of-perfection or necessary-being comparison used in the existing eligibility argument?

The candidate objection fails in its immediate form. The criticised inference orders **materials or ancestry**, then transfers that order to the resulting subjects. The affirmative argument concerns the **complete original giver of a pure perfection**, whose operative actuality is not supplied by another. Those are different relations. The primary text itself places both arguments close together and explicitly gives the distinction needed to prevent this transport.

This is a negative result for a particular proposed objection. It neither proves the giver principle nor closes the more demanding comparative route from necessary existence to all eligible pure perfections.

## The source's actual discrimination

Face68 reconstructs the nested ranking inference. Face69 separately challenges its premise, its transfer, and its use against an accepted directive. Its second objection is decisive for the present test: even if one material were superior, a creature formed from the other could be superior to the creature formed from the first. The source then supplies counterexamples and comparative arguments. Its natural-philosophical claims about earth and fire are historically situated assertions, not independently checked modern science.

The immediately subsequent face70 affirms a different principle: a complete maker and giver is entitled to the appropriate pure perfection, while rejecting the representation of that maker as wholly without effective attributes. It also distinguishes sharing a predicate from being identical in all respects, and real qualification from externally assembled components.

Consequently, the following inference is invalid without an additional bridge:

1. Material-origin superiority does not guarantee superiority of the resulting individual.
2. Therefore an original giver need not possess the appropriate perfection of the actuality it originally supplies.

The first premise does not identify its materials as complete original givers. Indeed, the source's positive account of producing a superior creature from a supposedly inferior material assigns the operative explanation to the maker. Removing that explanation would alter the case offered in support of premise1.

There remains a legitimate broader challenge: why does wholly original provision require the giver to possess a relevant excellence rather than merely the power to produce an excellent effect? The passage gives affirmative perfection reasoning, but the material-ranking counterexample does not independently answer this question. Nor does the source demand that the original copy every creaturely property in its creaturely mode.

## What this does and does not do to the earlier eligibility result

The existing donor-quantifier limitation survives. From a giver for each created perfection one still cannot infer that each putative original gives every perfection. A comprehensive giver premise or the independently appraised a-fortiori route remains necessary. This return neither repairs that quantifier gap nor converts necessary existence into a maximal property list.

The universal-supremacy route also remains separately identifiable. Face79 explicitly argues that two equal candidates cannot each be strictly above the other, and links unrestricted overpowering with uniqueness. That establishes the consequence of strict comparative supremacy. Applying it to a proposed original still requires warrant for that stronger perfection property. It should not be substituted for the less restrictive claim that each candidate is maximal under a comparison that admits ties.

The source presents this as positive rational and exegetical warrant, not as an accidental dictionary observation. Conversely, a formal theorem that strict superiority cannot hold mutually would add no independent evidence for the premise's metaphysical application. No such trivial theorem has been commissioned.
