## Result

**The retained argument warrants Knowledge of the original's particular intentional production, and its separate pure-perfection argument warrants comprehensive knowledge. The latter is not obtained merely by enlarging the list of actual effects.** The source explicitly distinguishes these routes. The existing record already contains their substantive premises; this operation does not discover a missing general attribute proof.

The exact comprehensive comparison is unusually clear at **Darʾ 10:152**, now checked against the retained print scan: knowing everything is more perfect than knowing only some things. Ibn Taymiyya endorses that comparison in his reply to a quoted objection, then addresses the alleged costs of knowing changing particulars and the claim that cognition would complete an otherwise deficient essence. The argument applies a defended standard of pure excellence to the independently established original. It is therefore a substantive positive warrant, not a definition of an omniscient subject or a new theorem from bare causal independence.

The actual-effects route has equally important exactness. Darʾ's later reply rejects a universal representation compatible with many concrete realizations. It requires knowledge of the particular and extends complete knowledge of its particular productive basis to the effect's determinate properties, expressly including length, breadth, depth and quantity. Its conclusion is not merely that the source knows that something exists.

**My appraisal remains affirmative:** successful original intentional determination supplies positive cognitive evidence in its actual respect, and the already defended pure-perfection comparison gives adequate reason to reject a merely partial original intellect. Neither conclusion needs to be reset to suspension because the relevant principles are substantive rather than school-neutral logical axioms. The distinct source-semantic question about nonactual objects is also already answered: production is evidence for being known, not a necessary condition of being known.

## 2 What particular intentional determination establishes

In Darʾ L0790 and L0795, the author's affirmative direction is clear: voluntary action involves cognition of the action. The local complication discussed by the predecessors concerns the converse from knowing to willing, not this direction. No new converse argument is needed when intentional agency has already been positively warranted.

The later passage is more discriminating than a generic “will implies knowledge” slogan. Al-Ṭūsī's reported account at L0915–0916 proposes an overall representation that would also fit other worlds of the same pattern. Ibn Taymiyya's reply begins at L0917. His objection is that a merely universal pattern does not identify the numerically particular external realization. The human examples at L0924–0929 illustrate the required transition: a general intention to help eligible recipients, eat food or use water cannot alone pick out the recipient, food or water actually involved.

These examples also show what is **not** required. Prior special preference for that individual as such is unnecessary. Availability and particular cognition can specify the means of an already apprehended generic end. This is inherited source content, not an invitation to model the original as dependent on an externally supplied opportunity set.

The positive local inference is therefore:

1. The source's activity is genuinely intentional, rather than an occurrence to which an observer merely attaches a purpose.
2. Its actual determination concerns this concrete effect in a relevant respect, rather than only a type equally instantiated by indefinitely many effects.
3. The cognitive content needed for that intentional determination concerns the particular realization in that respect.
4. In the accepted profile of an informed, adequate original, this supports genuine knowledge of its particular product and production.

Step 3 identifies cognitive specificity. It should not, when stripped of the accepted original profile, be presented as a universal epistemology of successful human action. An intention can contain error; a partial agent can accidentally succeed; and someone can intend an object under one description while being ignorant of other properties. The source's stronger original case retains its independently defended cognitive adequacy and actual productive completeness. These are already earned premises, not missing obligations introduced by this report.

Thus **the willing route yields real positive warrant for particular Knowledge at its proper scope**. It is neither an inference from blind causal differentiation alone nor a theorem that any isolated intentional act confers exhaustive knowledge of its object under every description.

## 3 Complete self knowledge reaches the determinate effect and its properties

The self-knowledge route supplies a complementary extension. At L0672–0705 the author distinguishes partial human apprehension from knowledge of the actual source and its states on the complete basis required by the argument. L0692 explicitly extends the source's cognition across its own states; L0693–0703 connect that completeness with its productive consequences. At L0735–0738 he separates knowledge of the productive relation, which includes its relata, from complete knowledge of the source whose actuality includes that relation.

There is no exchange of witnesses here. The source is the same actual bearer; its known productive activity is the particular activity whose effect is being considered. Knowing a standing power to produce effects of type F is weaker. So is knowing a general law compatible with different productive conditions. Neither is the displayed premise of complete knowledge of this actual productive determination.

The later authorial reply makes the range explicit. At L0932, if the productive basis determines changing particulars, adequate knowledge concerns them as changing particulars. L0935–0937 asks whether the alleged knowledge concerns general natures or the particular existing natures. On the second reading, its relevant consequences and effects are included. **L0937 expressly names length, breadth, depth and amount**, and L0938 illustrates enumerated particulars. This is a source-backed property extension, not merely a modern strengthening from existential occurrence to all detail.

The extension still has a fixed target: the properties genuinely included in the complete productive determination and its known consequences. It does not identify the intrinsic act with its external effect or equate the producer with its product. Nor does complete knowledge of a cause-type settle facts that the type leaves undetermined. A proposed example holding only a general source-state fixed while varying the particular completed determination therefore fails to preserve the premise being tested.

This route does not describe the source learning the effect through an inference. L0694–0698 and L0706–0734 repeatedly distinguish our inference between the knowledge claims from one divine cognition efficiently causing or temporally preceding another. They also distinguish understanding the productive relation from making the effect exist through our representation of that relation.

At the accepted common-original scope, the route reaches the actual created field whose complete original production belongs to this same source. A larger field requires the corresponding productive scope; the inference does not silently authenticate that scope. The current affirmative common-source judgement supplies its own warrant and retains its own status.

## 4 Why comprehensive knowledge is a further positive conclusion

The source does not stop at “every effect of which this source is already the complete producer is known.” Nor does it pretend that this proposition has exactly the same range as comprehensive knowledge.

At electronic L0719–0724 it expressly rejects making productive causation the only route or a necessary condition of knowledge. The examples include nonexistents, impossibilities and suppositional cases. At L0722, the direction is stated precisely: being the source's product is evidence that it is known; the converse does not follow. Knowledge of the source itself is another obvious case not established by the source's producing that object.

The affirmative extension is the separate perfection argument. The print sequence **10:150–152** matters:

- Pages 150–151 continue an objection quoted from Abū Ḥāmid. It challenges selective cognitive attribution and reports the claim that knowledge would supply a perfection needed only by deficient creatures.
- At page 152, Ibn Taymiyya replies that the objection exposes inconsistency in selective attribution. He expressly endorses the comparison between comprehensive and partial knowledge.
- The continuation rejects the purported general barrier to knowledge of changing particulars and refers to the separately argued permissibility of real attributes and voluntary acts.
- Electronic L0836–0858 supplies the positive ranking of cognitive excellence and original priority; L0859–0868 denies that possessing an intrinsic attribute means receiving completion from an external donor.

This is not adequately reported as “the source assumes omniscience.” Its proposed warrant is that unrestricted, nondefective cognitive adequacy is a genuine excellence and that the independently established original has priority in eligible pure excellence. Knowledge's benefit is not exhausted by compensating for a creature's ignorance or helping a needy animal survive. Removing the need, organs or labor of acquisition does not remove truth-disclosing apprehension. A source that comprehends the actual and relevant possible order possesses an intelligible cognitive adequacy that a source with unexplained blind spots lacks.

The unrestricted scope can be explained without assuming an actually attained maximum of a finite learning sequence. Take any genuine truth allegedly unknown to this same original. Grasping that truth as true, in an appropriate cognitive mode, removes a cognitive omission; its excellence does not depend on first being ignorant, acquiring information from a teacher, storing a representation in a finite organ, or diverting scarce attention from another truth. Under the defended pure-perfection judgement, an eligible truth-disclosing excellence cannot be excluded from the original merely by assigning it a content boundary. Applied to an arbitrary omitted truth, that judgement excludes the omission. This is an application of a general substantive criterion to the same subject, not a premise announcing in advance that the subject knows every truth.

Eligibility is doing real work here. Truth alone does not produce cognition, and consistency of truths alone is not a theorem that an actual knower comprehends them. The positive warrant comes from the cognitive-perfection argument and its answer to the proposed costs of intrinsic cognition. If an alleged act of knowing requires false self-identification, a contradiction, or an intrinsically defective mode, it is not an eligible instance. Ordinary limits of acquisition, storage and attention explain restrictions in received finite knowers; the record supplies no reason to impose those restrictions on unreceived cognitive adequacy. The source's comparison supports their exclusion without requiring us to design a physical storage architecture for the original.

That assessment also answers the essential-limitation reply at its proper level. Calling a proposed original “the kind that knows only these things” does not explain why its limitation is compatible with the already defended unrestricted pure-perfection standard. The Akmaliyya's retained receptivity response evaluates such restricted kinds rather than demanding that one numerical bearer acquire a new identity. No identity-preserving learning process or merger of subjects is required.

**I find the comprehensive ascription warranted on the present positive record.** The excellence of adequate apprehension, its independence from a foreign donor, and the failure of the inspected creaturely-cost objections provide substantive support. A merely described restriction carries no comparable independent warrant simply because it is labelled intrinsic. This is a defeasible philosophical judgement, not a conclusion certified solely by a logical calculus or by the Arabic author's authority.

The eligible scope remains important. Comprehensive knowing does not include believing contradictions true, performing mutually incompatible activities, numerically becoming every subject, or acquiring a finite creature's deficient mode of learning. Nor is the argument an induction from “one more truth would be better” to an actually attained infinite maximum. It applies the inherited pure-perfection criterion without a privileged exception for a further eligible cognitive content; it does not require separately producing or learning all the objects it knows. A demonstrated incompatibility would matter; none is supplied by the inspected source controls. The earlier centered-knowledge work retains its role of separating knowing another subject's self-ascription from falsely being that other subject.

## 5 Nonactual and impossible objects without falsehood or reification

Darʾ L0720–0722 does not assert that an impossible object exists or that an impossible proposition is true. One can know that an alleged state is impossible, know what a specified supposition would entail, or know a determinate possibility that is not actual. Those are different cognitive contents from affirming the state's actuality.

Likewise, the existence of a cognitive content does not make its intended object a separately subsisting extramental entity. Nothing in this extension requires a new domain of existent impossible objects. The source's own point is that being produced is not the necessary condition of being known. The report neither adopts a general semantics for counterpossibles nor claims that every arbitrary subjunctive sentence has a uniquely determined true answer.

This matters constructively. Comprehensive knowledge can concern the distinction between what is actual, what is genuinely feasible, and what is impossible. It need not actualize every known possibility. The same source may know mutually incompatible possible outcomes without producing them together or believing that both are actual. That respects the already established difference between standing power, actual willing and realized product.
