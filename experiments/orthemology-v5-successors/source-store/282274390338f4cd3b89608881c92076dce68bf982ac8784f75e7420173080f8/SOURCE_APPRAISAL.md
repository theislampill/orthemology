# Al-Nubuwwat: whole selected received-body appraisal

Dated Nubuwwat-stage assessment, preserved with its actual evidence boundary. The later Safadiyya study and final current appraisal are separate records; this text is not retrospectively revised into their verdict.

## Outcome and inferential standard

The complete selected received Arabic authorial body has been read continuously across four contiguous assignments, with subsequent bounded contextual returns. This is 101,419 whitespace words, 436 source records and 51 prepared windows. It is not a claim to have read every page or footnote of the 1198-position critical-edition scan. The source identities, precise credits and remaining limits are set out below and in the verification files.

Ibn Taymiyya supplies a developed, positive theory of knowledgeable purposive agency and intentional divine attestation. Its strongest feature is not a bare claim that wonders prove prophets. It is the differentiation of kinds of indication, contextual recognition of a sender's intention, constraints of wisdom and truthful guidance, and a cumulative profile of testimony, character, message, performance and enduring consequences. The book also contains substantial metaphysics, ethics, epistemology, philology and criticism of rival methods. Its author repeatedly grants opponents real knowledge or a locally sound insight while contesting their proposed foundations.

**Sufficiently warranted by this reading:** the source owns a serious, nontrivial attestation argument; generic causation, unusualness, local inability to imitate, or a claimant's interpretation is insufficient; intentional certification can be rationally recognised without a prior explicit token convention; power, its exercise, a created contribution and the scope of a certified proposition must be kept distinct. These are substantive conceptual results, not merely evidence that certain words appear.

**Positively favoured but not fully settled by this work's displayed case:** a knowing, purposive and morally intelligible ultimate agent as an integrated explanation of order, agency and guidance. The source gives actual reasons for this model, especially through means/ends order and a-fortiori epistemic provision. Those reasons cannot be reduced to labels, but their comprehensive metaphysical and moral reach remains contestable.

**Underdetermined at this study's stopping boundary:** whether a particular narrated episode has independently established occurrence, provenance, intended certification and the exact truth it is said to certify to an assessor not already granting the relevant revelation. The treatise offers many candidates and several routes; this reading did not perform independent historical dossiers for all those candidates. Nor does its integrated picture by itself decisively discriminate one complete original creator from every principled plural original basis.

The standard here is sufficient defeasible warrant for assent after serious, relevant alternatives are addressed, not immunity to every imaginable possibility and not the demand that one neutral deductive proof compel every audience. Lack of deductive closure does not imply lack of evidential force. Conversely, a sound conditional or an attractive unifying account does not automatically establish its application. The book's own permission to suspend when an alternative is strong is especially appropriate to this distinction.

## 1. The source's actual sequence

The opening distinguishes signs of prophets from wonders of adherents and rivals and criticises both denial of all nonprophetic wonders and unrestricted assimilation of them. Relative unusualness and unopposed local performance are inadequate. A follower's wonder does not confer infallibility on everything the follower says (S001L8–33). Comparison with known prophetic kinds, shared testimony and concrete character/content profiles develops before the extended critique of al-Baqillani's proposed tests (L33–80).

The work then broadens into religious knowledge and the body/accident proof, real origination and material transformation, necessary/original reality and its qualifications, love and will, and the difference between producing a means and loving its continued end (L81–165). Wisdom, justice and the criticism of arbitrary willing lead back to prophetic attestation (L166–205). Subsequent passages address knowledge versus assertion, request-response, sincere error, consensus, political/sectarian disputes and the plurality of valid evidential paths (S002L1–49).

The second received volume starts with the Quran's rational/revealed proofs and the requirement to answer counterevidence. It connects human development into a knowing speaker with the possibility of instruction and revelation, then treats prophecy, inspiration, angelic mediation, Adam's garden, and general providential versus approved normative sending (S002L50–87). A detailed theory of signs and inference leads to the private-token example and its divine application (L88–114), followed by limits of challenge criteria, warranted assertion, follower signs and acquired skills (L115–138).

The last part elaborates testimony, necessary/inferential knowing, perfection, wisdom, divine practice, intentionally designated signs, creator/created contributions, and a long set of discriminators between prophetic and rival phenomena. It ends by returning to sense, reason, report and the danger of reifying abstractions (S002L139–S003L78). Broad notes preserve this course instead of recasting the whole book as a list of selected attestation quotations.

## 2. Knowledge, agency and purpose: the positive case

### Knowing and intelligible action

The source argues that fitting arrangement can evidence knowing because parts are specified and placed in relation to an end. The familiar craft examples and natural-function examples are not just collections of regular shapes. Their explanatory relevance is that this is arranged for that. A theologian who relies on this intelligible order to infer knowledge while denying all final purposes therefore owes an alternative account of the very order used as evidence (S001L77–78; S003L14–18).

This is a powerful consistency criticism. The positive extension is that knowledge, preference, power and wisdom jointly explain discriminating action. They need not be separable modules arbitrarily appended to an empty bearer. The account also distinguishes cognition from love of its object and from delight arising in its apprehension; knowing an injurious thing is not itself one's final good (S001L139–145). This prevents an overly intellectualised account of perfected agency.

The objection that purpose necessarily implies an externally supplied deficiency is directly faced. The source argues that the same finite-agent association, applied indiscriminately, would also undermine will and action. It further differentiates fitting new acts and relations from constitutional lack or externally imposed injury (S001L164–170). This does not settle every question about divine perfection, but it answers an actual argument rather than simply refusing the terms.

### Revelation as epistemic provision

A creator who develops a human into a knower, speaker and writer can inform that human and enable recognition of an envoy. Shared human nature and language are appropriate means of communication, not evidence against commission (S002L73–75). Wisdom, justice and mercy then connect capability to fitting provision: requiring obedience to a messenger while eliminating every way to distinguish him would frustrate intelligible guidance (L76–77; S003L1–4).

These are positive a-fortiori and normative arguments. Ability alone is not the conclusion. The author explicitly criticises an argument that would infer actual attestation simply from the need to preserve the power to attest: not performing a possible act is not the same as lacking that power (S003L27, printed 952–953). Actual action and its purpose must still be identified.

### Limits of these arguments

A purposive description cannot be imposed on a neutral pattern and then cited as independent proof of purpose. Nonintentional explanations of functional organisation are not defeated merely by showing that an openly purposive explanation includes ends. The source's stronger claim needs comparative support for applying that explanation to the actual order. Likewise, knowledge of the better and willing the better are distinct in imperfect beings; the claim that they necessarily coincide in the ultimate agent rests on perfection and an account of good willing, not knowledge alone.

The natural examples include premodern physiological and material theories. Their presence neither nullifies all teleological reasoning nor verifies those theories. The book's reply to suffering appeals to wise ends and final recompense; it does not independently resolve every apparently adverse instance. A general confidence in wisdom must have grounds beyond treating every possible outcome as confirmation.

## 3. What intentional attestation requires

The book distinguishes an event that indicates something through its existence from a mark that informs through a sign-maker's intended designation. An originated thing can indicate an originator without the observer first knowing that it was intended as evidence. A word, gesture, seal or entrusted possession signifies a message through a different relation (S002L95–107; S003L25–28, printed 946–957). Both kinds are rationally understood; neither is automatically equivalent to a bare spectacle.

The private-ring/envoy passage is particularly exact. Recipients need grounds both that the sender supplied the token and that he supplied it to certify the mission. The author raises theft or loss, the sender's death, unauthorised sealing, and sending the ring for repair or ordinary sealing. When a rival explanation arises and is strong, they suspend judgement. He does not say any imaginable alternative always defeats knowledge. Where contextual knowledge excludes those alternatives, he permits confident recognition without a previously agreed code (S002L105–107; printed 768–772, especially 771).

The scope of that recognition is important: the immediate token certifies 'he sent me'. Knowing that the messenger was sent remains distinguishable from whether he lies about some content attributed to the sender. One cannot silently turn limited mission-authentication into plenary truthfulness of every later statement. The opening's restriction on followers' infallibility is consistent with this caution.

The divine application is explicit. It asks whether the mark comes from this source or another, whether he sent the envoy with it, and whether he intended informing/certification (S002L111; printed 779 within 777–781). General providential causation cannot settle those questions: the source separately admits causal 'sending' of winds, destructive agents and devils without approved mission (L86–87). Printed 1064 says treating every event as dependent upon God would supply no discrimination between signs and other events. Printed 1085 says a distressed unbeliever's request may be answered; response by itself does not endorse that person's religious claims.

Subordinate causes are also admitted. Angels may mediate, a person may shape material, and an agent may make a real contribution without independently producing the whole result (S003L63–67). A route to intended source-ownership must therefore identify the relevant agent's adopted communicative act and scope, not require an absence of all mediation or equate all causal participation with full authorship.

## 4. Route order and reciprocal support

The source does **not** require every hearer to first construct every speculative metaphysical bridge and only then recognise a truthful messenger. Familiarity with character, content and circumstances can yield warranted recognition; another hearer may require an explicit chain of reasons. The same proposition can be immediately known by one person and inferentially known by another (S002L153–S003L1; also S002L95–100). S001L73–86 rejects a monopoly for one theological method. Scriptural teaching can also point to observations whose rational force does not depend on having already accepted the speaker's authority (S001L97–105).

Thus an unresolved universal SourceMode premise does not block every possible testimonial route. A warranted intentional certification by an identified truthful source could establish its particular content without first proving a general theorem about every conceivable original reality. Whether the authenticated message then bears on numerical common creatorhood depends on its actual content and warrant. Source attribution, truthfulness, mission and reported-content scope must be assessed at the level relevant to that route.

Earlier recognition and later proof can legitimately reinforce each other. An independently well-grounded initial judgement can make a report available as evidence; later observations may strengthen the original judgement or correct its scope. Several partially independent indicators may jointly support a conclusion without one having been conclusively known first. The book's cumulative character/profile/testimony model and its repeated distinction between one failed proof and the remaining proofs support such an order. No single fixed linear method is imposed.

But mutual support does not license circular reuse. If a sign is identified as genuine solely because its bearer follows an already-assumed prophet, then that sign cannot independently establish the prophet whose authority supplied its sole credential. Nor can scriptural authority certify the sole historical premise on which that very authority wholly rests. The book recognises the comparable problem with using consensus as a premise for prophecy when the authority of consensus itself depends on prophecy (S001L73–75; S002L12–16). The correct question is where independent or differently grounded evidential contact enters, and whether the apparently multiple supports are genuinely distinct. This study credits the available routes without assuming that all their proposed historical applications have that independence.

## 5. Strongest objections after the full reading

**The application burden.** The definition of a conclusive sign as entailing truth is sound, but does not identify a historical event as that sign. The book supplies concrete kinds, comparison classes, causes, ends, character and content, so it is unfair to say it only offers a tautology. It is equally unfair to treat its ideal classification as an already-completed external investigation.

**Familiar human sender versus divine sender.** The ring inference draws on known custody, habits and practical purposes. Divine omniscience and sovereignty, if independently warranted, exclude literal accidental loss and an uncontrollable rival. They do not by themselves exclude every non-certifying purpose, especially since misleading subordinate agents and trials are admitted. The source's wisdom/nondeception reasoning is its answer. Its strength depends on warranted divine-nature premises and their relevance to this event; an unexplained attribution of intention cannot substitute for them.

**Honesty versus accuracy.** Good character and a life directed toward truth can be powerful evidence against deliberate fraud. They do not alone exclude sincere misinterpretation, limited cognition, borrowed information or later embellishment. The work itself distinguishes sincere error from lying and recognises fallible inspiration. Its positive response is cumulative, not sincerity alone. Each added strand needs corresponding historical warrant.

**Global exclusivity and relevant comparison classes.** The source correctly rejects 'nobody here did this' as a proof that no relevant creature can. Its own claims about universal human/jinn limits and angelic truthfulness are substantial premises. A classification that defines all successful counterinstances out of the class would not discriminate independently. The account must earn its relevant boundaries through positive grounds.

**Providential custom and lasting success.** Persistent support of a claimant to divine mission differs from an ordinary tyrant's temporary success; the book anticipates that objection. Yet 'eventual exposure' needs intelligible historical criteria and relevant evidence, lest every result be made compatible by an unspecified extension of the observation period. The book's distinctions about worldly and final recompense help prevent crude falsification but also make its strongest empirical generalisations harder to test.

**Transmission and independence.** Numerous repetitions are not automatically independent witnesses. The source itself considers informants' qualities and circumstances, not just number, and recognises when lack of expected widespread reporting is significant (S002L88–89; S003L54–55). The present traversal does not authenticate every flood, healing, prediction, transported object, personal spirit encounter or cited opponent's view. A scan matching an electronic passage establishes textual correspondence, not the narrated event.

**Dialectical versus independent force.** The work often shows that an opponent's admitted knowledge is inconsistent with the opponent's theory. That is a real achievement even if the opponent retains the knowledge by revising the theory. It does not automatically prove the entire positive alternative. Likewise, correcting a distorted account of a school or granting one of its valid routes does not endorse everything else in it.

## 6. Broader and orthogonal findings

The complete reading preserves more than the target arguments:

- Real responsive love, the difference between knowledge/love/delight, and the priority of an end in intention over its realisation.
- Rejection of a single compulsory speculative path, distinctions between mental possibility and external capacity, and between temporal succession and a regress of derivative agents.
- The epistemic role of concrete particulars and stable but time-bounded signs; a landmark can outlast the town it once indicated.
- Philological investigation of prophet, sign, custom and divine names, including refusal to rely on an untraced convenient report.
- Scope of 'Torah' as sometimes wider than the Pentateuch, and suspension of claims about Hebrew stylistic inimitability to linguistic competence.
- Ethical constraints on public accusation and disclosure, separate from private belief or confidential advice.
- Political differences among prophet-kings and servant-messengers, reconciliation and graded judgements in civil conflict, and correction of an inaccurate description of the Karramiyya.
- An earthly probation-garden passage surviving print control, with a modern editor acknowledging an opposed attributed position elsewhere. No harmonisation or chronology is invented.
- The cost of dismissing a witnessed occurrence instead of assessing its interpretation: one can concede that something striking happened while disputing its agent, purpose or authority.

These matters help recover the book's own intellectual shape and prevent a search-only dossier from masquerading as whole-body study.

## 7. Textual controls and stopping boundary

The primary traversal witness consists of three received Islamicbook website sections, not three physical volumes. It is not a diplomatic al-Tuwayyan transcription. The selected scan is al-Tuwayyan's Adwa al-Salaf first 1420/2000 edition, 1198 positions, with authorial body printed 129–1103 and a two-volume seam at 613. The editor reports one manuscript basis and chooses among it and older prints. Historical completeness of the received work is debated in his introduction. Ten announced indexes versus four received indexes concerns modern backmatter, not proof of six absent authorial chapters.

The readers made 67 selected authorial/locator-page scan controls during substantive study, plus 13 modern subject-index locator views. These are separate from 48 bibliographical/acquisition image receipts and from the separate independent second pass. The scan control at 322 reveals a consequential omission in the electronic material-origination sentence. Other discontinuities survive in print; their missing connections have not been invented. The exact issue register and local notes distinguish all voices and controlled versus unresolved readings.

All 51 received authorial windows are covered once in the aggregate first-pass ledger. Bounded electronic rereads and scans supplement that pass and are not counted as additional first-pass coverage. The integrating reader personally read the first and last divisions; the two middle divisions were read by other readers whose full notes/appraisals and receipts were inspected. A truncated integrating reader first display of V1 window 12 was fully repaired. A later combined report-display truncation was repaired at the report level and is not hidden as source coverage.

The conclusion is therefore a completed source-centred study of the entire selected received electronic authorial body with bounded critical-edition controls. It is not a complete critical-edition/apparatus reading, full manuscript collation, independent historical validation of every example, exhaustive comparison with opponents, or a new scientific test. No proof files were altered. The earlier actual-common-source assessment is unchanged; the distinct Nubuwwat supplement below records its additional bearing. Safadiyya remains outside this completed study.
