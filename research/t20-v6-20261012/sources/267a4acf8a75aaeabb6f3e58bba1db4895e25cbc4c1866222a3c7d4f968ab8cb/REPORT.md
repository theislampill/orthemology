# Two recognition contents and the practical effect they do not settle

11 October 2026 UTC. Bounded additive T20 source application. No protected edits, new historical authentication, contemporary personal diagnosis, or closure.

## Result

**The retained Heraclius scene does not contain one interchangeable “recognition” state.** In *Sharḥ al-Aṣbahāniyyah* p.560, Ibn Taymiyyah attributes firm knowledge of the expected prophet's identity to Heraclius. The adjacent Abū Sufyān report attributes conviction that the messenger's cause will prevail to a person still described as hostile. These are different subjects and different propositions. The second cannot be substituted for the first merely because both are expressed in certainty language.

The same commentary's pp.665–668 also blocks an overcorrection: allowing practical impediments does **not** make behavior wholly irrelevant to assent. The author permits failure of the *full* practical response while insisting that the firm religious assent under discussion has some response of the heart. Heraclius's reported wish to reach the prophet is relevant here: the narrative does not depict complete inner indifference. Its admitted practical limitation therefore does not directly contradict the later claim. Conversely, that wish is not the completed obedience or complete faith the later discussion concerns.

The positive increment is this **concrete source-content and response-scope reconciliation**, not the general distinction between knowing and acting, a new cumulative-evidence principle, or an independently verified human case. It protects the affirmative cumulative appraisal from two misapplications while preserving the source's substantive knowledge–action connection. The local reports checked did not already execute this two-subject, two-content comparison against the later some/full-response distinction; that is a bounded comparison, not corpus-wide priority.

## 2. Exactly what p.560 says

The paragraph continues the reported inquiry about teaching and character. It gives Heraclius's speech: the described teaching is that of a prophet; he had expected a prophet, though not from that people; he wishes to reach him, while his kingship prevents his going. It also includes a conditional prediction that, if the report is true, the prophet will possess the place beneath his feet.

Next comes a different speaker and attributed state. Abū Sufyān is described as then an unbeliever and intensely hostile. He reports his reaction to a great king's concern, followed by continued conviction that the messenger's cause would prevail until he entered Islam reluctantly.

Ibn Taymiyyah then gives his own appraisal: this question and investigation afforded the intelligent inquirer firm knowledge that this is the prophet he expected. The following objection by al-Māzirī challenges the adequacy of this evidential route; the reply at p.561 defends it and cumulative acquisition. Thus the certainty verdict is not simply the narrator's loose label on Abū Sufyān's prediction. Its object and epistemic basis are specified.

Three source roles must remain distinct:

1. **Heraclius's attributed speech:** recognition of a prophetic profile, desire to reach its bearer, kingship as the stated impediment, and a conditional prediction.
2. **Abū Sufyān's reported reaction:** conviction about the cause's future ascendancy while hostility remains.
3. **The commentator's appraisal:** the inquiry warranted firm knowledge of the expected prophet's identity.

The reported conditional sentence does not by itself cancel the commentator's explicit appraisal. Nor does the latter give each reported phrase unrestricted factive force. This operation establishes their contextual relationship within the author's argument; it does not authenticate the conversation or independently inspect either historical person's mind.

## 3. The later claim is stronger than behavioral irrelevance

Pages 658–664 distinguish the name and components of faith, increased detail in what has been communicated, circumstances, capacities, and different respects in which a response can be incomplete. These surrounding distinctions prevent equating every use of “faith” with merely knowing one proposition.

At p.665 the author makes apprehension of what is due to God and the messenger practically significant: love, magnification and obedience follow, with pride, envy and other impediments expressly discussed. He then allows nonperformance eventually to contribute to loss of assent. The temporal point matters: later loss is not automatically absence at the earlier recognition.

At pp.667–668 he insists on a finer distinction. Firm assent brings some work of the heart; if nothing of that kind follows, it is not firm assent. Yet the heart's response need not occur *in its entirety* when passions intervene. He also treats external conduct as evidence in the relevant faith appraisal. It would therefore misstate this passage to say that any imaginable total practical vacuum is consistent with unchanged complete assent, or that no pattern of conduct can ever bear evidentially on a cognitive/faith appraisal.

The scope is religiously determinate: p.665 names what is due to God as divine and to the messenger as messenger. Page 670 distinguishes ordinary affirmation of a proposition, including elementary or directly observable truths, from the more specific vocabulary of faith and acknowledgment. The author also reports alternative lexical analyses there; this does not make every reported analysis his sole endorsed definition. Accordingly, the heart-response claim is not here exported to every known proposition. That is another reason not to transfer the entire practical profile from prophetic recognition to a prediction of victory.

One textual caution is material. Page 667, note 6, marks the bracketed incomplete-cause paragraph absent from two witnesses and calls its final words unclear, suggesting a reconstruction. The public Wikisource version also contains an awkward rendering here. The present result does not rest on silently correcting it. The unambiguous some-response/full-response contrast spanning pp.667–668 and the explicit impediment/loss discussion at p.665 suffice for the restricted conclusion. The bracketed love/weakness sentence at p.668 is also absent in two recorded witnesses; it is not a premise of this result. This is edition-level apparatus inspection, not manuscript adjudication.

## 4. The concrete discrimination, executed

### A. Withholding full submission does not erase the inquirer's attributed recognition

The cumulative passage affirms the inquiry's cognitive achievement and itself reports an impeded practical approach. It also supplies a positive practical response, the expressed wish to reach the prophet. Accordingly, it does not instantiate the later antecedent “no work of the heart at all.” Replacing noncompletion of the outward approach with total inner absence would add a stronger fact not supplied by the narrative.

This gives a coherent source reading: a definite cognitive conclusion and a real, incomplete practical response, with a stated competing attachment. Connecting the narrated wish with the later heart-response discussion is our interpretive consistency argument, not an explicit cross-reference by Ibn Taymiyyah. That wish alone is not a sufficient test of firm assent or full faith. It is not an inference that every dissenter has a hidden desire or attachment. The source supplies the reported content here; an observer of another person would need separate grounds.

Nor does the report of wishing prove full obligatory compliance. A practical orientation toward an end, a settled intention sufficient in conjunction with ability, and completed performance are not exchanged by the passage. The later discussion's own capacity and completeness qualifications remain relevant.

### B. The hostile hearer's certainty does not become the inquirer's conclusion

Abū Sufyān's reported conviction has **future ascendancy** as its object. Heraclius's appraised conclusion has **identity as the expected prophet** as its object. Knowing that a movement will prevail does not, just from that proposition's content, identify its source as truthful or establish every normative commitment associated with it. The actual text does not describe Abū Sufyān's clause as a repetition of the full inquiry-based conclusion.

Consequently this adjacent report cannot, without an additional premise, be used as a second instance of the precise prophetic-identity knowledge or as independent corroboration of the first subject's epistemic basis. It can illustrate conviction concerning a consequential outcome coexisting with hostility. The word “conviction” alone does not decide the target.

This is a content-level non-entailment, not a developed alternative history or an assertion that Abū Sufyān lacked any further knowledge. The report may be compatible with stronger claims; it does not establish them by the displayed certainty clause alone.

### C. The combination yields a discriminating application rule

Within this religious recognition/response application, to use an actual refusal against the specified recognition appraisal, identify the proposition allegedly known, the practical consequence claimed to follow, the relevant opportunity/capacity, and whether the evidence concerns absence of that *whole* consequence or of *every* relevant inner response. In this concrete source comparison, the proposed universal inference fails at two separately visible places: the alleged certainty may have a different propositional object, and the observed noncompletion may be weaker than total absence of response.

These are obligations on this proposed interpretive inference, not a new duty for ordinary knowers to report or certify their own inner states. No numerical threshold, fictitious “warranted” coordinate, or general behavioral test is constructed.

## 5. What remains established and what remains unavailable

The cumulative appraisal remains affirmative at its inherited source scope. The present work explains why the reported practical limitation is not by itself an internal refutation of that appraisal. It also rules out using the neighboring hostile hearer's different conviction as if it replicated the whole appraisal. The later some-response requirement remains substantive; it is not weakened into a purely inert description of knowledge.

This supplies no new authentication of the narrated event, no independent warrant verdict about either person's whole life, and no present-day diagnostic or culpability rule. To advance from textual reconciliation to an independently established actual acquisition/retention episode would require evidence of the particular ground, content, uptake and relevant time. The current pages do not supply that new historical evidence. No new external survey is justified merely to rename this limitation.
