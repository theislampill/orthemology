## 3. The source's actual argument and speaker

### Common representation does not become one external constituent

At128–130 **Ibn Sīnā is the quoted speaker**. The proposed inference appeals to one meaning of “human” applying to different sensed individuals, then argues that this universal cannot itself have any one individual's sensible determinations. Do not attribute that inferential package to Ibn Taymiyya.

At133 Ibn Taymiyya asks where the one meaning obtains. A meaning in a knower, a spoken expression in its speaker, and writing on a tablet do not become numerically the same external item in every object they represent. In the unvocalized phrase “قائم بالعالم” here, the context concerns a **knower**, not “the universe.” The shared applicability of an expression or conception does not establish an externally shared universal constituent.

At134 he then gives the mental universal its own ontological standing:

> «وهذه الكليات المعقولة أعراض قائمة بالذات العاقلة، لا توجد إلا بوجودها، وتعدم بعدمها»

Close translation: “These intelligible universals are accidents subsisting in the rational subject; they exist only with its existence and cease with its nonexistence.”

Thus he does not deny that cognition is real. He locates this cognitive actuality in an actual subject. He separately says that external individuals can exist without a **human** conceiving their universals, and that a human can conceive kinds with no external instance. Neither statement permits promoting represented content to autonomous external actuality. Nor does the human-cognition example establish independence from every possible knower or divine knowledge.

### Omitting a requisite in thought differs from existing without it

At135–136 the opponent's “human as such,” considered without attending to either mental or external existence, receives a disambiguating reply. Failing to consider a concomitant is one thing; positively excluding both alternatives is another. The latter incurs contradiction under the author's exhaustive distinction. The former is a legitimate selective act of thought, but does not show external separability.

The crucial print wording at136–137 is:

> «فلا يوجب تصور الملزوم بدون لازمه، إمكان وجوده في الخارج بدون لازمه»

Close translation: “Conceiving that which entails a concomitant without [considering] its concomitant does not entail that it can exist externally without it.”

The bracketed clarification marks the translation's contextual reading; it is not an added Arabic word. The following sentence expressly allows selective conception while denying that unawareness of a concomitant removes it. At137 the concrete example is:

> «وهذا كما أن الإنسان يتصور علما مطلقا وقدرة مطلقة، مع علمه بأن ذلك لا يكون إلا بذات حية عالمة قادرة.»

Close translation: “This is like a person conceiving knowledge absolutely and power absolutely, while knowing that this occurs only in a living, knowing, capable subject.”

This is a positive bearer/qualification claim with an intelligible anti-abstraction rationale. It blocks the inference “I can think about the power without thinking about its bearer, therefore the actual power can subsist without a bearer.” It does not derive the bearer requirement from thought alone: the passage explicitly appeals to the already known relation between actual knowledge/power and a living qualified subject. Its generalization to a disputed new ontological category would still require that category's identification.

### The continuation rules out making attributes separate produced components

At138–141 the source reuses the same mental/external distinction against a composition inference based on common and differentiating predicates. An actual black colour is not externally assembled from an independently subsisting generic colour and a separately subsisting blackness. At142–144 Ibn Taymiyya insists that actual self-standing existence is qualified; on144 an intrinsically living knowing subject is not a bare actual self subsequently combined with detachable life and knowledge.

These are source commitments to qualified actuality and inseparability. They do not make a bearer the **efficient manufacturer** of its necessary attributes. The locally reported five kinds of “composition” on142 are the terminology he is criticizing, not his endorsement of an external assembly ontology. The broader agency and divine-attribute claims on142–143 are contextual controls only; this bounded return does not newly establish them. Likewise the rejection of the shared-predicate composition proof beginning on138 supplies no new proof that two original necessary beings are possible.

Three relations therefore remain separate:

1. **Abstraction:** a thinker omits an attribute, bearer, or necessary concomitant from current attention.
2. **Asserted independent subsistence:** the proposed quality or basis actually obtains without the required qualified reality. This is an ontological claim that abstraction alone cannot establish.
3. **Efficient production or existential receipt:** actuality is supplied in the relevant productive/existential respect. This is not equivalent to either cognitive omission or necessary co-presence.

The target passage supports the first/second distinction. It does not license a production edge from subject to necessary attribute, or from every concomitant to every other concomitant.
