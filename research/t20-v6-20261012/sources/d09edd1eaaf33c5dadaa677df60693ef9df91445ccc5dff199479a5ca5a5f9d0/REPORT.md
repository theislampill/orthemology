# Immanent self-praise: an original own-act content explanation

10 October 2026 UTC. Separate substantive T20 source-and-inference operation. No protected change, integration, T21, closure, new formal result, or withdrawal of an earned foundational judgement.

## Result

**A positive, content-sensitive explanation is available for an original's own activity: truthful self-praise of knowingly intended and achieved beneficence.** The received *Shifāʾ* does not merely say that God praises whatever happens. It identifies the original's immanent self-praise as a way a loved good is realised, distinguishes the grounds of praise into the act, its intended good end, and that end's achievement, and expressly attributes actual self-praise to those grounds. Bare production or accidentally beneficial results do not satisfy the same ground.

The new operation instantiates the current account's practical-content requirement at an **own-act content/type** target. Earlier worked cases concern created means, an achieved creaturely response, and an excluded divine punitive determination. Here the source explicitly identifies positive own activity and supplies its practical basis. No inference from a creature's praise or command to praise is needed to establish the own-act target.

This is not a complete explanation of a temporally individuated self-praise token, a proof that every possible silence is unfitting, or a derivation of one exact verbal form. The source supports why this praise has this content and ground. It does not independently individuate or causally determine an intrinsic act token. If a further account introduces a newly occurring cognitive-volitional representation as its ground, the present passage does not explain that representation’s specified origin. This conditional representation question is distinct from the general particular-act question; a fresh representation is not assumed for every own act.

## 2. The primary argument, with voices and boundaries

The controlling publication is Ibn al-Qayyim, *Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl*, ed. Zāhir b. Sālim Bālfāqīh, Dar ʿAṭāʾāt al-ʿIlm / Dar Ibn Ḥazm, second edition, 1441/2019. The official [catalogue](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/books/shfa-alaalyl-fy-msael-alkda-oalkdr-oal-59) identifies it and links the [Word witness](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/media/download/79) and [page-image PDF](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/media/download/140). These are two presentations of one edited publication, not independent historical witnesses. The already received files, not a fresh download, supplied the current text and images.

### A. The agent's own activity is explicit: 2:184–186

The surrounding argument answers a reported objection: if the good can be produced directly, intermediaries appear pointless. Reply 8 rejects reducing divine wisdom to a creature's acquisition of pleasure or relief from distress; at 185 the author denies that creatures supply the original with perfection. Reply 9 connects purpose to loved ends, retaining the distinction between an intermediate disliked in itself and its better consequence.

Reply 10 at 186 then says the relevant good may be realised through a separately created thing or through what subsists in the original: immanent acts, speech, self-extolling and self-praise. Its decisive wording is:

> بل بما يقوم به من أفعاله اللازمة وكلماته وثنائه على نفسه وحمده لنفسه

Here “immanent” renders the contrast with a separately created effect and **بما يقوم به**. It does not translate **اللازمة** into the assertion that every individual act is metaphysically necessary or eternally actual. The following **فمحبوبه يحصل بهذا وهذا** assigns both kinds a role in realising what is loved.

This is the author's endorsed reply. Reply 11's subsequent discussion of names and their effects is not needed to infer that every named perfection must have one particular recipient. The following chapter presents the opponent's timing/regress objection; that objection is not the author's own explanation of this act's timing.

### B. The ground of praise is not the act's bare occurrence: 2:149–150

After interpreting the original's speech as truthful and just, argument 17 explicitly begins with God's self-praise and His instruction that creatures praise Him. It explains this through the praiseworthy ends and consequences of His acts. It distinguishes three objects of appraisal:

> على نفس الفعل، وعلى قصد الغاية الحميدة به، وعلى حصولها

The act; intending its good end; and that end's realisation. The following challenge rejects praise grounded in a benefit that merely occurred accidentally, without being intended. Its conclusion uses warranted praise as evidence of wisdom. That inferential direction must not be hidden when applying the passage in the reverse explanatory direction.

The source nevertheless expressly offers the practical ground too: **وهذا لما في أفعاله من الغايات والعواقب الحميدة**. It is not only the bare entailment “if praised, then wise.” The wise act and its end are stated as what makes this praise fitting.

The apparatus at 150 marks **ما يجوز قصده** in witness م as an erroneous reading against the printed **ما لا يجوز قصده**. Our argument does not depend solely on that negative: the preceding explicit description of unintended accidental benefit and the separately clear 203 statement carry the criterion. Manuscripts were not inspected. Some Qurʾānic quotations in the Word-derived text are incomplete or irregularly displayed, including at 149 and 204. The page images control those displayed quotations; the present argument rests on the intact authorial clauses and does not silently repair the retained transcription.

### C. Actual own praise is attributed to substantive grounds: 2:200–204

The continuous context distinguishes governance from praise. At 200 the author says that whatever is needed for the source's governance comes from the source; it is not supplied by an independent other. At 201–202 he attributes to opponents accounts sacrificing praise to power or power to creature-centred purpose. Those polemical descriptions are not independently verified histories and are not adopted as new rival campaigns.

The author's positive synthesis and argument 15 at 203 give an especially clear comparison: a person intending good but unable to execute it can deserve more praise in that respect than a powerful producer who does not intend good. This is not a denial of the excellence of effective power. It shows that power and praiseworthy intention are different grounds; effective output cannot automatically replace practical basing.

At 204 the author uses **ولهذا حمد نفسه** and specifies self-praise concerning comprehensive governance, the giving of the book, creation, and complete dominion. He concludes that greater wisdom supplies greater ground of praise, and that the absence of wisdom and its intention removes that ground. The transition supplies **actual attributed own activity plus its reason**, rather than merely a norm telling creatures to praise. The preceding act/intention/achievement taxonomy supplies distinct respects of appraisal; it does not show that every quoted clause individually makes all three claims, or that every intrinsic act of praise temporally follows every mentioned created effect.

The subsequent argument 16 concerns creatures' gratitude and its conditions. It is not silently identified with the original's self-praise. Its stronger claims about differential hardship and gratitude are unnecessary here. No modern welfare calculation, endorsement of harmful action, or fresh authentication of its transmitted stories is claimed.

## 3. Executed positive reconstruction

Fix a modest but real target: **the original's praising its own comprehensive, knowingly purposive beneficence as praiseworthy**. Do not ask this explanation to select the exact words, occasion or full history.

1. The original's actual production, knowledge and wisdom are inherited affirmative premises. At this target the relevant excellence is not just that an effect exists: it includes knowingly directing production toward fitting good and attaining that good. The source's threefold criterion specifies these respects without reference to an ensuing utterance of praise.
2. Truthful appreciative speech concerning that excellence has an independently describable content: it attributes the actual intentional beneficence to its bearer. That is distinguishable from claiming merely that a benefit happened, from attributing another's achievement to oneself, and from commending a defect as such.
3. The source does not leave the practical relation uninstantiated. At 149–150 it explains praise through those good ends and consequences; at 204 it explicitly attributes the original's self-praise to its comprehensive governance and other named achievements. Within the inherited source-warrant framework, that is evidence of the relevant explanatory attribution, not a conjecture inferred only from temporal coincidence.
4. The intentional agent and the agent of the praise are the same original. At 186 its own self-praise is classed among its immanent acts; actual loved fulfilment is possible through those acts. This prevents replacing the target with a creature's vocal praise or the original's act of enabling that creature.
5. Therefore the source supplies a **positive practical explanation of this own-act content**: knowingly excellent agency is appreciatively expressed as such. This is more informative than “the own act happened because it was chosen” and does not require a future external object to donate a representation or essential attribute.

The broader already accepted self-knowledge and wise-agency warrants support this application; the operation does not re-prove them. Nor does the statement that an own act is loved, taken by itself, explain its particular selection. The decisive addition is the specified content and source-owned reason relation.

### What exactly has been discriminated?

Hold the stated target fixed: praise **for intentional beneficence**.

- If a good effect merely happens through an agent without its being knowingly intended, that effect alone does not justify this praise attribution. The source directly makes that comparison. The missing intention cannot be restored by calling the effect produced or the agent powerful.
- If the agent intends the fitting good but fails to realise it, the source permits praise of good intention. It does not permit silently attributing achieved success. Intention and accomplishment remain distinguishable grounds.
- If the act, fitting intention and achievement are all warranted, appreciative speech attributing them has the positive ground the text states. Merely denying that a unique exact wording follows does not erase that ground.

These are premise-deletion and content-accuracy tests of the actual source argument. They are not new metaphysically possible original-world models. The first two contrasts use distinctions the author explicitly supplies, including a creaturely illustration for praise in a specified respect; they do not posit a genuinely ignorant or unsuccessful divine original.

## 4. Why this is not self-certification, or efficient self-production

A bad reconstruction would be: God praises Himself; therefore the praised act is good; therefore that goodness explains His praise, with no independently warranted connection at either step. That would recycle the occurrence of praise as its own entire support.

The present application keeps its warrants separate. General sourcehood, knowing wise agency and the relevant normative excellence are inherited independently earned commitments. The source passage identifies the particular practical attribution. The act/end/achievement distinction provides an independently describable criterion for what the praise says. The source's reverse argument from deserved praise to wisdom can remain evidentially legitimate, but is not counted here as a second independent proof of the premises just employed.

This is an application within the accepted source framework, comparable in that respect to the ship/wall and achieved-test applications. It does not independently authenticate revelation, establish every historical instance of beneficence, or oblige someone who rejects all the inherited premises to accept the conclusion from this text alone.

Self-praise also does not produce the whole existence or essential excellence of its bearer. Its appreciative intentional relation to that excellence is neither an efficient self-cause nor an external supply of perfection. Conversely, the passage's claimed fulfilment through immanent action is not declared causally idle. What is realised in the activity differs from the source's whole existential actuality, already presupposed by acting.

A further identity restraint is essential: the 186 immanent-kind statement and 204 quoted scriptural examples support the same relevant class of own activity. They do not identify a canonical one-to-one set of intrinsic tokens with inscriptions, creaturely recitations, revealed transmissions, or verse boundaries. No claim that a human utterance is numerically a divine act is made.

## 5. Exact limit: content explanation versus token individuation

The result meets a particular **content/type-level** demand. The appraisal target, practical reason, and source attribution can be stated without the empty phrase “whatever was selected.” It is a positive own-act example, not only a rule excluding punishment with transferred guilt.

It does not meet these stronger demands:

- why this precise act of self-praise occurs at this precise occasion;
- why this formulation rather than every equally truthful fitting expression;
- why silence is impossible at any proposed occasion;
- whether any newly occurring representation is involved, and how it relates to a revealed clause, if such a model is proposed;
- a complete efficient derivation of a newly occurring representation’s content and occurrence, if an explanation invokes such a representation;
- a numerical inventory of distinct intrinsic volitions, or a unique whole history.

In particular, the source's general love of fitting praise plus unlimited power cannot be silently promoted into a fully specified own-act love at an arbitrary time. The wise-reason-asymmetry report's exact antecedent discipline survives. The conditional representation-origin test and general complete-particular-determination question survive at their respective scopes. The exact saved-premise check in PREMISE_SCOPE_CHECK.md finds no mandatory fresh-representation-per-act requirement in the controlling reports.

Calling the current result a **complete** explanation of the act type would also exceed the evidence if completeness means excluding every other activity or specifying all positive causal conditions. The supported result is an actual, contentful practical explanation at a declared respect. Its positive contribution is an articulated own-act example even before an exact temporal token is specified. No inspected report is accused of having denied this possibility.
