AR8R-T143 — Conservative bridges cannot create old-language conclusions
Setting

Let T be a theory in language L, and let B be a bridge theory in an expanded language L+. Say B is model-conservative over T when every model of T has an L+-expansion satisfying T+B.

Let C be an L-sentence.
Theorem

If B is model-conservative over T and T does not entail C, then T+B does not entail C.
Proof

Since T does not entail C, choose M |= T + not C. Model conservativity gives an expansion M+ |= T+B. Expansion does not change the L-reduct, so M+ |= not C. Thus T+B does not entail C. ∎
Cross-track consequence

A bridge registry, provenance annotation, definitional extension, or representation-preserving migration that is conservative over the old track cannot by itself upgrade an old-language metaphysical, source-historical, or owner-authority conclusion.

To change an old-track claim, a bridge must be nonconservative relative to that claim. Nonconservativity is necessary but not sufficient for truth: it may exclude countermodels merely by stipulation. T129's warrant/authority coordinate remains separate.
