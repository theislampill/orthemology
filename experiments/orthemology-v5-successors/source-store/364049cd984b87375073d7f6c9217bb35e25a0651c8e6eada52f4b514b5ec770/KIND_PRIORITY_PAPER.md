# Kind priority and the explanation of existential nonemptiness

Tenth Orthemology research candidate, 3 October 2026

## Result and its importance

A source-sensitive kind argument improves one side of the route to a necessary participant while exposing a more local problem on the other. Asking for an instance of one among the relevant actual kinds requires a much smaller possible production than reproducing the entire actual inventory. It avoids the finite-multiplicity and exhaustive-witness demands attached to the Ninth target.

But the weaker existential target also no longer forces an explanation to account for every actual witness. An activity's inability to explain its own bearer's kind-membership does not, by itself, show that it cannot explain the existential target through a different recipient. The common basis of the activity and the target is not automatically a dependence of the activity on the target. The disputed inference therefore arises already for one productive agent, before the collective infinite-regress question.

The strongest affirmative reply is a focused nonpresupposition judgement about existential nonemptiness. At an explicitly existence-supply question, a bounded relevance judgement is warranted: an activity cannot, solely through its output, supply the antecedent existential availability on which its productive channel depends. This is weaker than a demand for the entire actual origin. Its identification with the precise E promised by the restricted transfer, and its application to the whole joint account, remain unestablished here. The investigation gives a positive reduction of the modal-witness burden, a warranted local relevance judgement and a substantive limitation of a proposed priority derivation, not a new necessary-participant or P1/P2 conclusion.

## 2 What the smaller target genuinely gains

Fix Q from the actual situation. Each member of Q is a classification under examination, and the target p_Q states that there is a contingently existing instance of at least one such classification. An explanatory possibility must keep that same Q fixed. It cannot replace the actual kind family with the family found in the proposed world.

Only one suitable recipient is required. The possible explanation need not preserve the identities, exact counts, whole origins or complete productive histories of every actual contingent bearer. It need not coordinate indefinitely many extra creators, reproduce the whole actual field or show finite witness exhaustion for each type. Those were genuine burdens for a stronger inventory target. They should not be imposed again here as the price of crediting a necessary participant.

This is a real strategic reduction. It is not a claim that the reduced modal premise is already true. A possible outsider must genuinely differ from every kind in the fixed covering family or be necessary; a colourful name for an imagined producer does not establish that difference. If it remains contingent and falls within the same Q, it has not supplied the intended external witness. Nor does actual coverage imply that some metaphysically possible productive type lies outside that coverage. A kind family might be extensionally broad, and its allowed outsiders require an actual modal reason.

The first useful research target is therefore the independent possibility of one outside producer and one suitable effect, rather than the entire actual inventory. Origin-neutrality can help avoid de re reproduction of the actual recipients, but it does not by itself produce a possible donor or its power.

## 3 Kind membership and the existential proposition do different work

The disjunction of the classifications in Q is not itself being declared a natural kind. It is a device for tracking the actual coverage. The source-side claim concerns a particular K and a particular bearer's membership in K, with that membership genuinely antecedent to its relevant activity. The predicate 'contingent being' does not acquire this explanatory status merely because it includes every contingent being.

Let m_s denote the actual fact that a source s instantiates its relevant kind. Let a_s denote its productive exercise. Let m_t denote the corresponding kind-instantiation fact of a distinct recipient t. These symbols distinguish facts; they are not predicates asserting that our metaphysical theory is true.

Now grant the strongest local priority claim: m_s is explanatorily prior to a_s. Also grant that m_s supplies an existential witness for p_Q. These grants give two consequences of one basis. They do not yet establish that p_Q itself is explanatorily prior to a_s.

If a_s produces m_t and m_t supplies another witness for p_Q, there are two routes from m_s toward the existential proposition. One route is direct witnessing; the other goes through the productive exercise and its recipient. Simply displaying those routes gives no local productive cycle and no self-production of s. This does not certify the second route as an adequate E-explanation of p_Q. It establishes that the exclusion cannot be obtained merely by rewriting the common basis as target-priority.

The stronger inventory target had a different inferential resource. To explain all its existential content, an account had to cover every listed bearer at the declared explanatory standard. The existential target needs a witness, not by its logical form an explanation of every witness. Its improved modal tractability therefore comes with reduced explanatory coverage. The claim that an activity does not explain m_s cannot automatically be promoted to the claim that it explains no adequate witness to p_Q.

This is not the discredited move from a bare logical proof to a certified existential explanation. No arbitrary witnessing statement is being certified as E-adequate. The point is an actual inferential distinction within the offered reason: explaining a proposition through one sufficient witness and explaining the other witnesses to it are different tasks. Additional target-sensitive warrant is needed if E rules out the former because one of the latter already supports the activity.

## 4 The affirmative norm at its strongest content

The best reply concerns the explanatory question's direction. The question is why the declared kinds have any contingent instantiation at all. If the account invokes an agent whose own antecedent nature already supplies precisely that instantiation, its exercise can explain another recipient without explaining the original existential availability used by the account. That is a real reason to resist treating additional production as an answer to nonemptiness.

Call the proposed principle witness nonpresupposition:

An account of a declared class's existential nonemptiness does not discharge that demand solely by citing activity whose antecedent productive bearers already instantiate the class.

The principle is not offered as the definition of E or of an original source. Its content can be assessed independently. It contrasts an account of another instance's reception with an account of the target's being instantiated at all. It does not make received agents passive. A received cause may genuinely produce a numerically new bearer with real intrinsic powers. The claimed explanatory shortfall concerns a different respect from that local production.

The norm need not impose E*. An outside producer might explain the instantiation of the target class while leaving its own existence, other products, later acts and many actual histories unexplained. Several outside producers might do so together. The question does not require one independently complete source of the original correction or every productive mode in its history. An infinite subordinate ancestry is compatible with an independently available source of the relevant existential supply.

Two tempting overstatements must be avoided. A successful causal fact entails that its effect exists; that factivity is not prohibited presupposition. Likewise, a sufficient cause can entail its effect without being circular. The candidate norm instead concerns antecedent instantiation of the very target class by the exercising bearer, independently of the successful production under discussion. That is a more specific claim than banning every explanans which entails its explanandum.

This gives the affirmative a genuinely reason-bearing proposal. Its force is not that a rival has failed to describe an empty world, that a totality needs an extra container, or that an infinity cannot activate. It asks whether the offered answer explains the target's existential availability or uses that same availability to explain a further manifestation. A person can rationally appreciate that distinction without an experiment, a universal decision procedure or the agreement of every philosopher.

The argument is also stronger than a bare counterfactual-redundancy objection. Two independently sufficient causes may each explain an effect even when the effect would occur without the other. The present concern is that the proposed recipient's productive channel itself depends existentially on an antecedent witness to the target class. Its dependence on that witness, rather than the bare fact that the target would otherwise remain true, motivates the nonpresupposition judgement. This distinction preserves ordinary causal overdetermination while identifying the particular reason the existential case may differ.

The bounded judgement deserves affirmative credit. For the explicitly existence-supply question, activity whose productive availability requires an antecedent F-witness does not, solely through its output, supply the availability of F-instantiation on which it operates. This is a rationally warranted assessment of explanatory relevance, not merely a coherent stipulation or a request for a still more basic derivation. It does not require p_Q itself to ground the act. Its warrant concerns the stated channel dependence and the specified supply question. Neither the same-E identification nor the joint dependence of an entire history follows automatically.

## 5 Why the active reply is not yet defeated

The active opponent can grant the local source's antecedent reality and all its real productive powers. It can then say that the explanation is of the existential truth through the produced recipient, and that no additional explanation of every witness is required. This is stronger than merely drawing an infinite graph. It identifies a possible structure of the explanatory answer and contests the proposed target demand.

That answer still owes E-adequacy. A proof that a recipient exists is not automatically an explanation of the class's existential availability. A local causal story might answer how this recipient arose while presupposing what the nonempty-class question is asking. The affirmative is entitled to press that objection directly.

But kind-priority alone does not adjudicate the disagreement. No contradiction has been derived from the common-basis structure in §3. Nor has a metaphysically complete internal account been established. The two contentful claims must be kept separate: the antecedent nature of an actual source is prior to its exercise; and the exercise cannot explain the existential target even through another genuine recipient at the transferred E. The first has a familiar local rationale. The second requires the further same-E identification, even though the bounded existence-supply judgement is warranted.

The kind covering premise also retains its own burden. A convenient extensionally complete classification does not establish that its members' powers are grounded in that classification. Conversely, explaining a familiar power through a real nature does not prove that every actual contingent causal candidate is covered by an origin-insensitive classification with the same priority. An argument can defend that generalization, perhaps basically, but finiteness or set membership alone does not supply it.

A justified memberwise exclusion would not automatically establish the required joint exclusion either. One cannot infer that a collection fails merely because no member independently explains the target. The conditional participant argument uses a norm about an account drawn wholly from internally instantiating contributors. That norm can concern the single existential target without requiring every inventory witness to be explained, but its joint scope still needs warrant. In a mixed history, an outside contributor can produce an internal contributor which then produces another recipient. The internal contributor's nature precedes its own act while being supplied within the wider explanation. Such a history illustrates why an admissible account need not contain only necessary contributors.

No claim of a neutral arbitration algorithm follows. An independently targeted basic presentation of witness nonpresupposition would not be refuted by mere rival describability. Here the actual challenge is a distinction in what the weaker source-priority premise says, combined with a substantive alternative account of what the existential question requires. Calling the stronger judgement basic does not erase that distinction, and calling the alternative internal does not certify its adequacy.

## 6 The same-E restriction survives the smaller target

Four dimensions must remain separate: whether an account is adequate or merely contributes; whether it is proximate or ultimate; whether it necessitates its effect or allows indeterminism; and whether it answers a contrastive or noncontrastive question. A nonnecessitating explanation can be adequate, and an adequate proximate account need not trace the full history. Conversely, acceptance of such accounts does not certify every witness-mediated answer to p_Q.

The source's broader standard K-E prevents an easy exclusion based on unique determination or exhaustive detail. An argument cannot promise an ordinary nonnecessitating explanation and reject its actual candidate only because it does not determine every aspect of the outcome or explain all its prerequisites. The law-only null-preservation result in the companion paper therefore does not close this route.

Nor can the argument automatically demand a contrastive answer stronger than the promised E. Witness nonpresupposition may instead be understood as a constraint on the direction and relevance of an existential explanation. That would avoid requiring a probability-one or fully contrastive explanans. The existence of such a legitimate explanatory question, however, does not establish that the restricted transfer applies to that exact question rather than to a weaker witness-mediated explanation of the same proposition's truth.

The causal-participation requirement is also separate. K-CAUSE is additional; the broader discussion admits conceptual explanations of some existential propositions. The question is which explanation kind applies to this substantive target. The mere existential grammar does not settle it. If a nonproductive existential account is proposed, it must be examined for explanatory content rather than dismissed by the rule that absent efficient producers cannot act.

There is an exact conditional check. If the actual witness-based grounding of p_Q already counts as an adequate E-explanation, p_Q already has such an explanation before the modal transfer is invoked. The transfer then supplies no new productive participant. To obtain one, the argument must either exclude that conceptual answer at this target or warrant an extension from it to concrete productive explanation. This is not certification of the witness-based answer. It identifies what would follow if that answer is admitted and prevents an already satisfied weak transfer from being credited with a stronger productive conclusion.

There are thus two valid ways forward. One can show that the E already promised for p_Q has the witness-nonpresupposing scope by a positive analysis of that existential question. Or one can openly formulate an explanation principle for that scope and give it independent warrant. Neither step needs whole-origin E*, but neither is obtained merely from the antecedent nature of each actual agent.

## 7 The exact necessary-participant consequence

<!-- SOURCE_RECONSTRUCTION PRUSS_2009 START -->
Suppose p_Q has an actual causal explanation. If every contributing bearer is contingent, coverage puts each in Q. If witness nonpresupposition excludes that explanation, some actual contributor is noncontingent, hence necessary.
<!-- SOURCE_RECONSTRUCTION PRUSS_2009 END -->

This consequence is important at its own scope. It supplies a particular actual contributor which necessarily exists, not merely the claim that some contributor-role is occupied in every world. It does not require identity of all contributors, complete original coverage of the correction, an intrinsic account of the contributor's necessity, or practical perfection. The existence of a necessary being can be established before knowing whether its necessity is categorically intrinsically explained.

The consequence also does not say that every contributor mentioned in a wider causal account is necessary. A necessary bearer may produce contingent agents which genuinely participate in later effects. The target and the explanatory relevance of those later contributions must be assessed separately. Necessary participation is the minimal result this investigation seeks, and later conditions are not its admission price.

In the current record, however, the covering and modal-witness application, the restricted transfer at exact E, causal participation and the relevant nonpresupposition principle have not all been independently warranted about the actual situation. The conditional consequence must therefore retain its conditional status.

## 8 Current warrant and next test

The positive result is a reduced target with a materially lighter possible-production burden, plus a warranted local relevance judgement for an explicitly existential-supply question. Neither gain requires whole-origin completion. The negative result is the failure of kind-priority alone to yield the full transferred-E exclusion: a common basis is not target-priority, and explaining one witness is not explaining all witnesses. Joint scope remains a separate test.

Those are actual judgements about the inspected argument and its available reasons. They are not a new actual-world necessary-source conclusion. The independent original-possession rationale remains prima facie supported. The active rival has not been certified adequate or metaphysically possible. The point of the new inspection is to locate a better affirmative route and a sharper burden, rather than suppress either side's strongest content.

The next decisive presentation would establish that witness-mediated production by an antecedently instantiating source is genuinely inadequate at the same E used in the transfer, without equating that inadequacy with a stipulative demand for an outside source. Alternatively, an independently adequate E-answer of that form would defeat this particular exclusion. Another statement of kind-priority, or another description of an infinite active series, would do neither.
