# A whole-work appraisal of the received Arabic al-Safadiyya

## Result and evidential scope

Al-Safadiyya is not adequately represented as a proof of a first cause, a denial of natural causation, or a brief defence of miracles. Its received argument moves from a question about reducing prophetic signs to psychic powers into an extensive account of divine and created agency, concrete existence, attributes, knowledge, human fulfilment and revealed practice. Its positive position depends on keeping kinds of relation apart: an efficient maker, a receptive condition, an intrinsic attribute, a conceptual component, an object of love and an evidential sign do not all do the same work.

The entire selected Islamicbook electronic body has been continuously read across five adjacent assignments: 1,947 labelled non-empty lines and 119,021 whitespace words, including the opening frame, intermediate labels and final colophon. The five sequential accounts in the combined notes provide substantial coverage of the full range. This is full coverage of that received electronic sequence, not a word-by-word reading of either available scanned edition or a newly authenticated complete manuscript. The synthesis additionally draws on every range appraisal, reconciliation of the four assignment joins by primary-text rereading, and rereading of the central perfection argument and closing divine/human predicate partition.

The newer Adwa al-Salaf / Dar Ibn Hazm edition supplies materially important corrections. Its modern introduction, manuscript description, editorial method and all 94 numbered correction examples have been read separately. Selected authorial contexts and visible apparatus have been visually controlled. Individual readers' scan receipts enumerate the exact pages; the correction register distinguishes the catalogue's claims, the newer printed body, the older reading reported by its editors and the unchanged electronic witness. Bibliographic identity and coverage must not be collapsed into textual equivalence.

## 1. The opening question and why the reply expands

The opening question asks about a Muslim who says prophetic miracles are psychic powers. The initial answer combines a severe legal-theological judgement with a reconstruction of the view being opposed. The subsequent argument cannot be reduced to that opening judgement. The author identifies exceptional cognition, imaginative representation and psychic efficacy as genuine phenomena; the problem is their proposed sufficiency as the whole account of prophecy.

The first long movement reconstructs the metaphysics believed to drive that reduction. If the world's source is an eternally complete, invariant cause, new voluntary acts and exceptional divine interventions become difficult to accommodate. Prophetic phenomena are then redescribed within a fixed emanative, celestial and psychophysical order. Ibn Taymiyya consequently asks whether this account can explain any new event before asking whether it can explain the specific events called prophetic signs.

The return to prophecy at SF-S001-L0237–0245 is decisive. He affirms special prophetic knowledge, truthful representations and God-given psychic efficacy. He also affirms powers and natures in things: fire heats and water cools. SF-S001-L0238–0240 criticises a view that effects are produced merely at the presence of natural things rather than through them. What he denies is the extrapolation from some such powers to an exhaustive explanation of prophetic distinction. This concession is directly present at Adwa printed 158–159 and its immediate continuation.

The physical and psychical examples should therefore retain their limited function. They establish what the author takes the competing account to explain, what it fails to explain and which distinctions an adequate explanation needs. His reports of particular wonders, magic, jinn or unusual endurance are not independently authenticated by this reading. Nor are all his attributions to philosophers thereby shown historically exact.

## 2. An active Creator and determinate effects

A recurring challenge asks how an eternally complete cause can produce a newly occurring effect. If all conditions of this determinate effect are complete, the effect cannot simply remain absent. If further receptivity, movements or preparations must arise, those further items are themselves events to be explained. This is not merely an objection to using conditions. The author expressly accepts finite sources whose effects await conditions supplied from elsewhere.

The clearest early qualification is SF-S001-L0055–0056, controlled at printed 77–79; it recurs at SF-S002-L0607–0610. A fire needs a recipient; the sun can illuminate a place after an obstruction is removed without a new intrinsic act in the sun. The active intellect, on the opponents' own scheme, is likewise not the independent source of all conditions. Transferring this model to a comprehensive Creator requires another argument because the supposedly external condition is also one of that Creator's effects.

The constructive alternative distinguishes unceasing action in type from a particular product which is beginningless. The agent can always act without this particular sphere, world-member or act being eternal. The text repeatedly separates a succession of effects from a regress in original productive efficacy. It also distinguishes reciprocal correlations, such as fatherhood and sonship, from reciprocal production in which neither source can become effective until the other makes it effective.

These distinctions are used against two opposing positions: an invariant complete cause with co-eternal determinate products, and a previously wholly inactive or incapable deity who begins acting without a relevant determinant. The author often uses conditional dilemmas: even if a beginningless succession is admitted, the eternity of this particular world does not follow; if it is not admitted, the rival account faces a different difficulty. Those argumentative horns must not all be converted into independently established unconditional premises.

The treatment of causal completeness is itself more discriminating than an isolated slogan about simultaneity suggests. SF-S003-L0015–0017 and L0100–0103 qualify the coincidence of cause and effect through immediate succession, and deny that hand/key or hand/ring correlations are examples of complete efficient creation. A modern reconstruction must identify whether it models original capacity, present causal completion, the act, its product or an earlier preparatory condition. This study records these distinctions without declaring every proposed temporal argument formally settled.

## 3. Concrete qualified existence and the necessary bearer

The work repeatedly denies that the widest universal concept is itself a separately existing universal thing. External existents are determinate; their shared meaning is intelligible without one numerically shared external universal being distributed among them. The author does not consequently prohibit abstraction, common predicates or informative general reasoning. A mental universal is itself a determinate mental occurrence, general in its reference.

The critique is important to arguments about God. A bare notion of necessary existence, emptied of every positive determination, cannot automatically serve as the concrete Creator. SF-S002-L0043–0046, confirmed at Adwa printed 245–246, explicitly says that establishing some self-necessary existent does not yet establish the Maker unless that existent is shown distinct from the world. Related identification cautions recur at SF-S002-L0310 and SF-S003-L0348–0353. A proof can establish less than its user subsequently attributes to its conclusion.

Attributes do not automatically introduce an outside maker. SF-S002-L0340–0346 distinguishes a thing's not existing without its own attributes or purported constituents from its having an efficient cause. It therefore resists defining necessary existence in a way that silently rules out all concomitants. In the later discussion of genus and differentia, SF-S003-L0133–0139, conceptual descriptions do not rearrange the concrete individual or produce its attributes. The instantiated properties of a particular human are themselves particular. These passages are controlled at printed 323 and 424–425 respectively.

This account allows shared meanings while excluding equality of divine and created instances. In answering the quoted al-Sijistani position, the author distinguishes common predication, exact likeness and numerical identity. He does not make divine knowledge and created knowledge a single external quality, nor reduce all shared language to homonymy. Quotation boundaries matter: the radical negative-theological challenge at SF-S002-L0242–0244 is the opponent's speech, followed by the author's answer.

## 4. Creaturely dependence and the source of perfection

The work contains strong positive universals about actual creaturely dependence. SF-S003-L0267–0280 says creatures are intrinsically poor or dependent, both when originating and while continuing to exist. Possibility and origination indicate that dependence; they do not efficiently impose dependence upon a separately existing neutral essence. Correspondingly, divine necessity is not self-production. This cluster is visually controlled at printed 450–452.

Source/perfection reasoning is explicit, not an interpreter's invention. In SF-S001-L0132–0135 and the fuller SF-S002-L0330–0337, the author argues that perfections in creatures come from their Creator and that the independent superior source is more entitled to the relevant perfection. The fuller argument also concerns possibility: a perfection possible for dependent, inferior existents should not be impossible for the independent superior bearer. He then argues that the divine possession of it cannot be received from the very product whose perfection depends on him, or from a second ultimate source.

The qualifications are integral. The perfection must be possible and involve no defect. Its manner must be appropriate to the bearer. A created quality does not become an intrinsic divine attribute merely because God creates it in another; SF-S002-L0507 says this expressly. SF-S002-L0337 accepts intrinsic necessitation as an account of necessary attributes while refusing it as an account of created products. The positive comparison at printed 321–322 is immediately followed by the necessary-attribute/efficient-cause distinctions at printed 323.

The final pages provide a particularly clear counterweight to indiscriminate transfer. SF-S004-L0054–0056 rejects an absolute ban on commended shared predicates, permits human knowledge, mercy and wisdom, reserves certain predicates to God, and identifies human need, humility and servanthood as creaturely excellences which cannot qualify God. These pages are part of the book's conclusion, not an exception imported from outside it.

The text thus offers serious material for a comparative argument about actual creatures and their common independent Creator. It does not supply an unrestricted rule under which every relation called an origin or prerequisite efficiently transmits every property or excellence. The separate supplement states the resulting reconstruction obligations without converting this reading into a new formal or Lean result.

## 5. One Creator, cooperation and different kinds of cause

The common Creator is more than an unargued label in this work. SF-S003-L0198–0208 argues from the connection and mutual dependence of the world's parts against their assignment to independent ultimate makers. SF-S003-L0362–0365 uses the unity of a generated human, horse or tree against multiple ultimate producers of the same undivided individual. Ordinary builders or other collaborators have distinguishable contributions. These passages are controlled at printed 436–438 and 473–474.

The subsequent mixing example, SF-S003-L0366–0373, denies that one independent ultimate maker can supply the mixing while another supplies its inseparable resultant state. Yet the same discussion distinguishes vicious productive circularity from concomitance among effects or necessary attributes. The unity and inseparability premises do substantive work. Merely replacing them by a notation for generic dependence would conceal rather than reconstruct the argument.

The distinction between final and efficient causes is equally important. SF-S003-L0319–0321, controlled at printed 463–464, denies that being desired or imitated means efficiently producing the lover's power, desire or action. A represented goal can guide activity even before its external existence. The preceding philosophical language about celestial imitation of the highest good belongs first to a quoted account under examination. Its attribution and the subsequent answer must remain visible.

The work also affirms real human choosing and real created powers while denying their independent sufficiency. Cooperating causes, receptive conditions and impediments remain necessary to its explanations. Divine determination and human agency are presented as compatible through power and determinate will, rather than through an undifferentiated denial of all creaturely efficacy. The discussion distinguishes ability relevant to command from the complete ability present at actual performance; this reading does not independently resolve every resulting issue about moral responsibility.

## 6. Epistemic routes and prophetic attestation

The epistemology is plural. Observation of change, intuitive recognition, valid inference, semantic clarification, trustworthy report, broader transmission, scripture and inward experience all occur. Their being different routes does not make them equally reliable or interchangeable. The author distinguishes possibility from actuality, lack of a proof from proof of absence, a refutation from a positive demonstration, and a dialectical argument binding an opponent from an argument producing knowledge.

Some arguments are explicitly audience-sensitive. A person who already accepts prophetic truth can be challenged with the prophets' descriptions of external speaking angels. A person denying prophethood needs evidence establishing it first. Claims to direct inward insight do not automatically outrank reliable mediated instruction; inspiration is conceded but treated as fallible. Some inherited attributions remain uncertain and receive conditional treatment or suspension.

The staff-transformation argument in SF-S001-L0243–0244 attaches causal discrimination to the event's setting and purpose: the author says the exceptional event in that contest attests a voluntary powerful maker and vindicates the messenger. His other arguments distinguish what an individual soul could intentionally produce from events before its existence, beyond its awareness or after its death. Prayer and personal excellence can contribute to a divine response without being self-sufficient producers of it.

Historical application remains a separate burden. The moon argument appeals to public recitation before potentially hostile audiences; the Abraham/Lot argument appeals to agreement in details without borrowing or collusion. These are identifiable inferential structures with historical antecedents. Reading them does not establish all those antecedents, independently verify every narrated event, or show that an alleged extraordinary event has no competing explanation.

SF-S003-L0414–0419 explicitly denies that every person needs the same sequence of premises. Some people acknowledge the Creator directly; others need concise proof or extended removal of doubts. SF-S002-L0206–0208 treats Qur'anic arguments as capable of guiding reason directly rather than operating only after every proof of messenger-veracity is complete. These passages resist a compulsory single philosophical entry route into all knowledge of God.

## 7. Speech, creation, fulfilment and revealed life

Several major topics cannot be omitted without distorting the work. A long discussion of divine speech distinguishes the speech from the reciter's voice, content from sound-bearing performance, necessary attributes from voluntary acts, and divine speaking from creating speech in another. Its attempted third account resists a binary between one eternally fixed item and a separately created product. It connects to agency while remaining a substantial doctrinal treatment in its own right.

The creation discussions compare reports, temporal expressions, throne, water, pen, decree and the formation of these heavens. They distinguish the history of this created order from a supposed first beginning of every divine act. Later eschatological passages treat cosmic transformation and resurrection as more than illustrations of abstract metaphysics. Their report variants and scriptural interpretations should not be silently combined into one authenticated historical account.

The final large movement concerns human fulfilment. The author grants social order, ethical improvement and practical goods but denies that they exhaust prophecy. Knowing is not identical to loving, willing, delighting or worshipping. Perceiving a hostile object can cause pain; knowledge without corresponding practice does not establish the good for which the knowledge is necessary. Mental universals can aid understanding without replacing knowledge of concrete realities or the determinate divine referent.

The unseen is not simply what can never be perceived. The work treats absence from present perception and future manifestation as distinct. Its discussions of resurrection, divine vision, angels, intercession and devotional practice provide the concrete subject matter with which it contrasts reductive accounts. The ending presents worship, love and reliance as the proper creaturely relation to God, and therefore helps determine what counts as creaturely perfection.

The legal and historical material is also extensive: common prophetic religion and differing laws, abrogation, treaties, war, jizya, religious authority, saintly claims, political conflict and the conduct of intellectual opponents. Severe polemic coexists with concessions to sound mathematics and natural knowledge, genuine ethical benefits, sincere error, limited capacity and justice towards opponents. These qualifications must remain attached to the claims they limit. Punitive legal statements and hostile group descriptions are reported as features of the historical source, not endorsed as current prescriptions or treated as independently established facts about communities.

## 8. What the textual controls change

The newer edition materially changes the evidential basis in places. The received electronic body has a repeated al-Urmawi sentence where printed 78 gives a different argument about successive preparedness; printed 79 omits another electronic sentence. Printed 102 restores missing words within a quoted logical argument. Printed 105 concerns something being within power where the electronic text speaks of elapsed duration. Printed 106 changes the polarity of a statement about conditions. Printed 122 includes a positive-perfection clause missing electronically.

Other corrections affect genuine acquisition versus merely metaphorical agency, the possibility of unusual causes for ordinarily kinded events, intercession as asking God rather than invoking a messenger, interpretation, existence/non-existence, mental/external reality, the inseparability of a sphere and its events, and the difference between sharing and stipulating. Later examples extend to sensation, punishment, definitions, knowledge, laws and devotional language. These are not a set confined to the present metaphysical interests.

The final polemic also meets editorial disagreement: Adwa printed 577, notes 2 and 5, qualifies the portrayal of al-Ghazali by citing humility and interpreting the relevant pride as ascetic detachment. This remains a modern editorial voice, but it should accompany any historical use of that portrayal.

The newer edition is nevertheless not mechanically infallible. Its catalogue has pagination discrepancies; one item about an allegedly improved word is qualified by the body note's preference for the older reading. Another catalogue entry says a word appearing in the new main text should be deleted. The apparatus acknowledges many incorporated manuscript-margin changes without individual signals. A responsible account therefore preserves the witness, the proposed correction, its context and the precise level of verification.

## Overall appraisal

The received work has a coherent wider project despite its length and recurrent returns to earlier disputes. It attempts to coordinate a concrete willing Creator, genuinely dependent and efficacious creatures, informative shared predicates, multiple disciplined ways of knowing, prophetic communication and an affective-practical conception of human fulfilment. Its distinctions are often more precise than the broad labels attached to its opponents or to its own position.

The strongest gain for further philosophical work is a richer set of explicit relations and scope restrictions, alongside real positive dependence and perfection arguments. The principal unresolved burdens are the validity of particular bridge premises, the identification of the intended concrete bearer, the historical warrant of reported signs and attributions, and the resolution of consequential textual uncertainties. Neither the whole electronic traversal nor the bounded scan controls turn those further burdens into completed proofs. They do make it possible to address them from a substantially broader and more faithful textual base.
