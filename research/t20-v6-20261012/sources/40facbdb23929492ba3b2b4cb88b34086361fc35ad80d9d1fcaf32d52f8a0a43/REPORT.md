## Finding

The report's central application is supported at the limited scope it claims. The retained Darʾ passage distinguishes the conditions completing each particular occurrence from an alleged single fixed completion of the collective world. It separately distinguishes accomplished sequential acts from an indefinitely uncompleted condition of the same production. Applied to the representation-origin residual, those distinctions block an unwarranted demand for a first act or one simultaneous total-history production without dispensing with actual differentiation and completed efficacy for the occurrence being explained.

No material correction is required to that conclusion. One nonblocking attribution clarification would make the report more exact: the conditional and dialectical language occurs in Darʾ 114–116 themselves, not only in the preceding 68–80 exchange. The report already avoids treating the argument as an independent metaphysical proof and supplies the positive original-specific account from Majmūʿ 303–305. The clarification therefore does not withdraw its earned result.

The operation is a bounded inferential clarification and application. It supplies neither a new complete selector for an actual representation nor a newly discovered inherited first-act error. Those limits are substantive and must accompany reuse of the result.

## The collective inference and its limit

Darʾ 104–108 reports al-Rāzī's presentation of the eternal-effect argument and proposed voluntary-agent reply. The author's response begins at 109, with explicit criticism of that reply at 111. Within the response, 114 asks whether the completing conditions concern each individual worldly occurrence or the world collectively: “في كل واحد واحد من آحاد العالم؟ أو في جملة العالم؟” The two answers at 115–116 do different work. The first permits later occurrences to depend on prior occurrences. The second denies that the whole world depends on one specified set of things to which the proposed division applies, since different portions depend on different conditions.

This supports the report's objection to changing the explanandum. An adequate account for each particular does not by itself establish a single simultaneously completed productive condition for the entire succession. Common sourcehood is compatible with differently conditioned actual productions. Nothing in the distinction denies the common original, denies comprehensive original provision, or establishes that a whole history cannot have any intelligible explanation.

Nor does it establish a unrestricted local-sufficiency theorem. A particular account still has to warrant the relevant content and conditions it invokes. One cannot replace an explained occurrence by the label “one member of the permitted series” and inherit adequacy from that label. The report expressly retains this obligation in sections 4A, 5, and 6.

The exact application to an intrinsic representation is a synthesis of two sources, rather than a verbatim representation example in Darʾ. Majmūʿ identifies the representation as a particular intrinsic occurrence through willing and power. Darʾ provides a general distinction between completing a particular occurrence and aggregating the successive order into one production. Keeping the representation particular licenses that limited inference control. It does not prove a new representation's actual differentiating history.

## Conditional argument and positive commitment

The closest attribution risk lies in reading all of Darʾ 114–116 as a stand-alone unconditional demonstration of the permissibility of beginningless succession. The passage says “وعلى هذا التقدير” at 114, “جائز عندهم” at 115, and “جائز عندكم” at 116. Likewise 80 and 99–100 invoke the opponent's permission for succession; 141 expressly argues on both alternatives. Those formulations matter.

However, the response is not reducible to a neutral concession. At 113 the author positively attributes the particular-effects succession to both the philosophers and Sunni religious authorities. At 117–120 and 124–138 he develops his own distinctions concerning the origin and completion of efficacy. The report is therefore entitled to identify his explicit distinction and apply it as a limit on the opponent's inference. It is not entitled to credit every imaginable infinite causal arrangement with adequacy.

Majmūʿ 301–305 also supplies a direct positive original-specific account. The first three positions are described and criticized; the fourth at 303 is developed in the author's own explanatory voice. It is followed by his preference on the permissibility of the term resolve, by the judgment at 304 that reason and Qurʾān indicate an additional cognitive determination, and by the unqualified origination and differentiation claims at 305. Thus the report does not need to smuggle a merely adversarial concession into the position attributed to the author.

Suggested precision, if the author elects to revise section 3: “The 114–116 distinction is itself developed under the opponent's conceded succession premise; 113 also attributes the permitted sequential structure to Sunni authorities, while Majmūʿ 303–305 supplies the positive original-specific account used in this application.” This is an optional clarification, not a requested repair of a false conclusion.

## Accomplished succession and incomplete efficacy

Darʾ 49–58 already distinguishes the relevant structures. At 53 the disallowed regress concerns concurrent conditions completing efficacy, expressly not a series of successive things. At 54 a condition of completed efficacy has to be present when the production occurs; earlier presence alone is insufficient. At 58 the author distinguishes the controversial sequence in which one particular precedes another.

Darʾ 125–135 makes the distinction concrete. The allegedly created creative command is made to depend on another command completing its efficacy. At 126 the relevant positive conditions must all exist at the production, with examples such as power, life, and knowledge. At 134 the defect is that no actual completion of being productive is ever obtained. At 135 the contrast is this particular being produced through a command, with another particular produced earlier or later through a command. The latter is a dependence concerning the particular act, not a failure ever to have actual originating activity.

The report correctly preserves both directions of the control. Temporal beginninglessness does not by itself establish a vicious productive circle. Acyclicity does not by itself establish completed efficacy. Merely redrawing an unfinished simultaneous dependence as an acyclic graph cannot cure it. Equally, no inference from a finite readable proof or certificate to finite actual causal ancestry is licensed. That last distinction is the report's metalevel inference control, not a quotation from the medieval text or a new metaphysical model.

The apparent tension between an earlier act being relevant and conditions needing to exist at execution must not be erased. Earlier accomplished activity can condition a later determination; the relevant productive conditions must nevertheless actually obtain when that later occurrence is produced. The report's distinction between predecessor conditions and perpetually incomplete same-act production is adequate to that requirement. It does not claim that an absent past act alone is a complete currently operative cause.

## Representation, cognition, and willing

Majmūʿ 302–303 criticizes the second account even though it places newly arising wills in the subject. Intrinsic location plus new tokens cannot alone explain their specification. This directly supports retaining the representation-origin obligation.

Majmūʿ 303–304 distinguishes earlier willing for a future occasion from intending the particular execution at its time. It also distinguishes prior knowledge of what will be done from knowledge of doing and of its having been done, endorsing an additional determination. Therefore the representation's having arisen after not being at 305 does not entail previous ignorance of the intended event. Nor can all the affirmed determinations be replaced by an undifferentiated prior knowledge premise.

At 304 the intended particular's differentiating features are in knowledge and conception, not an already present external object. At 305 the cognitive-volitional representation arises through the subject's willing and power, with specification grounded in determinants within the subject. The paragraph rejects common capacity as a sufficient differentiator and rejects generic self-specifying will. The report accurately joins those claims without deriving an outside donor.

The representation-dependency report's relation typing also survives. Knowledge's practical bearing on willing and a representation's origination through willing and power do not identify the executive willing with the willing involved in that representation's origination. They do not turn practical differentiation into the same efficient-production relation. The supposed same-token circle requires additional identity and relation premises. Absence of that established circle remains weaker than a complete positive explanation of the content.

The immediate pronoun in Majmūʿ 305's specification clause admits the antecedent caution already recorded in the representation report. Nothing in the present result requires resolving it uniquely: the representation-origination clause is explicit, and the continuous argument independently demands particular differentiation.

## Genus, actuality, and act–effect boundaries

Majmūʿ 303's “لم يزل مريدًا بإرادات متعاقبة” attributes successive willing to the original. It must not be weakened into a claim about bare unused capacity only. Conversely, that statement should not silently be formalized as an actual token occupying every point of an independently imposed dense temporal domain. The report explicitly distinguishes enduring genus, capacity, modal possibility, and token actuality, and attributes the stronger succession doctrine without treating them as identical predicates.

Darʾ 157–160 separates every act, one particular act, and the genus of acts, then explicitly distinguishes an act subsisting in the subject from product after product, or both. Majmūʿ 305 likewise compares the intrinsic representation's origination with that of separate occurrences; comparison is not identity. No divine-act-equals-creature premise is used in the reviewed inference.

Majmūʿ 310–312 permits discrimination among knowings and willings without treating them as physically separated countable components. Its water analogy concerns connected quantity rather than separate coins. The report's role designations respect that warning and do not claim a complete inventory or one-to-one pairing of divine volitions and represented products.
