### Source doctrine in context

The preceding discussion separates hearing sound, understanding meaning, and accepting or responding. The target then presents knowledge as the standard for settling whether things exist, whether claims are correct, and whether actions are beneficial. Its immediate example asks whether the scholar’s ink or martyr’s blood is superior.

The explicit objection is that knowledge appears to judge in its own favor. The reply distinguishes knowledge from an interested human judge: a biased judgment would lose the standing being claimed for knowledge. The received main text also appeals to the attestation of intellects and native constitutions; its apparatus records a variant replacing the latter expression with reflection. This variant was newly noticed during this return in the already-retained electronic apparatus. It is not a new textual discovery, direct scan inspection, or independent manuscript collation. The checked prior reports do not identify any conclusion that depends on choosing fiṭar over reflection; neither reading independently classifies the disputed episode. Nothing here warrants treating the phrase as an uncontroverted technical criterion of fiṭrah.

Crucially, the author does not conclude that the person bearing the title scholar always wins. He proceeds to compare the contestants by their actual religious excellence and permits either to surpass the other, or equality. This surrounding application helps distinguish cognitive authority from an occupational or partisan privilege.

The subsequent discussion says willing presupposes awareness of its object, while cognition does not require producing or willing that object. It gives knowledge a broader range than productive power or determinate will. That is a role distinction within the author’s theory. It is not an empirical demonstration of how a particular Creator-directed seeming was produced.

### Strongest affirmative reconstruction

The defensible insight is that rational appraisal is an exercise of cognition. Treating genuine knowledge as disqualified solely because cognition participates in its appraisal would undermine the proposed appraisal too. The relevant standard concerns what is true and adequately apprehended, rather than which claimant gains a practical advantage. A genuinely cognitive recognition therefore need not first obtain approval from some wholly noncognitive tribunal.

This reconstruction can explain why an unrestricted demand for antecedent faculty certification is misplaced. The source does not itself spell out an infinite-regress theorem here, and the reconstruction is not new reading evidence for one. Nor is it a claim that all human judgments are knowledge.

The distinction also blocks reduction to wish fulfillment: effective willing and warranted cognition have different success conditions. Producing an agreeable response does not, by that fact, constitute apprehending the truth. Conversely, unwanted knowledge can remain knowledge even when its possessor refuses to act on it. The latter point is expressly retained from 229–281, rather than overridden by the target’s encomiastic language about knowledge directing action.

### Strongest surviving local objection

The critic can grant all of the preceding and still ask whether the selected episode actually is knowledge, a defeasibly credible cognitive presentation, or assent shaped by a wish. The distinction between knowledge’s authority and a claimant’s assertion of knowledge leaves that question intelligible.

If knowledge is factive, saying that biased error is not knowledge correctly characterizes its success condition. It does not independently identify which current episode satisfies it. Adding that the episode is “sound” because it agrees with the desired conclusion would simply restate the disputed classification. The claim does not become a counterexample merely because a misleading episode can be imagined either.

The best affirmative reply is therefore role-sensitive and local: preserve the actual content, current use, and supported grounds of this operation. The already-read injunction to hold allies and opponents to the same standards supplies a meaningful corrective discipline that can be applied without first assuming which side is right. But consistency under relabeling is not sufficient for truth, and the text does not turn that discipline into a completed validation of a named recognitional capacity.
