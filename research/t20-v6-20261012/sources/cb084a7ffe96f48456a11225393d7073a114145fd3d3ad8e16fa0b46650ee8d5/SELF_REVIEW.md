## Mathematical challenges addressed

1. **Actual root versus modal necessity.** The antecedent must include contingent-existence dependence. A single brute contingent root refutes the unguarded inference. The actual-ancestry theorem is conditional, and full well-foundedness is not advertised as globally minimal when a root would suffice.
2. **Modal necessity versus underivability in all worlds.** A two-world example rotates the root between two necessarily existing individuals. The positive repair uses the well-founded union of possible dependence edges; this stronger premise is explicit and has its own finite tests.
3. **Necessity in itself.** Neither necessary existence nor uniform absence of incoming edges is silently identified with the source's essential necessity. A parametric theorem is valid only with the corresponding essential-contingency premise.
4. **Totality tautology and conjunctive projection.** The actual contingent member set is frozen across alternatives. Projection of a full total explanation to every selected member is stated as a premise, not inferred from arbitrary explanations of conjunctions. The proof has no hidden well-foundedness assumption.
5. **Self-generation versus coinduction.** Empty-antecedent rules count as explicit sources. Positive cycles cannot generate anything from no seed in least-closure semantics; a larger coinductive fixed point is not thereby shown metaphysically incoherent.
6. **Ancestry versus full support.** AND-contributors and world-dependent rule eligibility are not erased. Root reachability alone does not supply sufficient actualisation.
7. **Missing modal data.** The complete k-skeleton includes negative answers. Treating unqueried sets as absent would invalidate the completion theorem. Lower/upper endpoint downsets are realised by explicit worlds.
8. **Arity versus algorithm cost.** Maximum minimal-cut size and number of minimal cuts are distinct. The query theorem assumes a fixed family and a membership oracle; no efficient general blocker enumeration or real metaphysical oracle is claimed.
9. **Randomisation versus warrant.** The audit game has explicit players, strategies, model alternatives and payoff. The saddle value is a decision-game value, not a probability that theism or a metaphysical claim is true. With a strict insufficient budget, an exception-free positive necessity certificate is unavailable over the full promised family.
10. **Recombination and compactness.** These are additional positive bridge hypotheses. Individual contingency does not establish joint compossibility. Noncompact and nonopen-activation models show where the infinite extraction proof fails.
11. **Conservative signatures.** Theorems 11 and 14 preserve a specified world-local language with local quantifiers. They do not preserve arbitrary de re predicates or a language already containing an intellectual-adequacy axiom. The impersonal-root expansion adds a separate outer sort; it does not enlarge the old quantified domains or replace their causal relations.
12. **Common-bearer weakness.** J2 co-instantiates order and efficacy in one necessary impersonal root, so the control is stronger than merely putting each role on a different node. It still does not show that the model satisfies every strongest noetic/source premise in the repository.
13. **Modal collapse.** Necessary existence of an entity is separated from necessity of its every act or a complete ground-fact description. The noncollapse model is an extensional consistency illustration, not a theory reducing understanding to a truth table.
14. **Proof checker scope.** The three local certificates expand necessity as universal quantification over explicit worlds. They do not verify well-foundedness, topology, source interpretation, or all the manuscript proofs. Eleven invalid proof patterns were rejected. The checker itself remains part of the trusted local code base, not an independently audited kernel.

15. **Mental ownership versus knowledge.** The essential-owner theorem yields a necessarily existing mental bearer under its mental guard. It does not establish knowledge merely from thought ownership, or omniscience from a `Mental` predicate. The stronger knowledge claim now has an explicit additional truth-linked epistemic condition.
