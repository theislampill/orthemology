## What is newly closed

### 1. Finite-family approximate converse

For a finite family of fixed smooth additive scores, one shared Hessian-normal tangent direction at p is equivalent to existence of shared local coherent repairs allowing **some nonnegative eta=o(epsilon)**. The existing general-hull necessity supplies one direction of this iff.

For sufficiency, the proof chooses the actual repair ray `c_n=p+epsilon_n z`. Positive barycentric accessibility gives hull membership; a sequence shift keeps both incoming and repaired forecasts in the open coordinate cube. For every score/truth pair the actual difference of state losses divided by epsilon tends to zero because the first-order terms agree under the common-normal condition. The proof defines

    eta_n = sum over scores k and truth states j of |actual loss difference(n,k,j)|.

That eta is nonnegative, bounds every actual worsening, and eta/epsilon tends to zero by a **finite** sum of limits. Neither an error bound nor a localization oracle is supplied by the caller.

The quantifiers matter. Eta is constructed existentially from the fixed finite family; this is not a guarantee for every prespecified tolerance sequence. The theorem does not set eta=0, prove exact nonlinear common repair from Hessian compatibility, provide an effective rate, or assert an O(epsilon squared) remainder under the weaker stated smoothness assumptions. Necessity can cover an arbitrary score family, but the converse's shared tolerance currently uses Fintype K.

### 2. Literal intrinsic-interior input

`intrinsicInterior_positive_barycentric` genuinely takes Mathlib's literal intrinsicInterior of the actual finite convex hull. It derives weights strictly positive at every listed vector, including duplicated or redundant entries. This extraction theorem does not assume finite dimension of the ambient normed space.

The proof is valid: take the uniform listed-point centroid q; relative interior supplies a point y in the hull just beyond p along q-to-p; write y using nonnegative finite weights; then express p as a strict positive mixture of q and y. The uniform centroid contribution makes every listed coefficient positive. Empty-index impossibility is derived from the supplied interior membership, not silently assumed away. If all points coincide, the construction still works.

The module wraps the exact quadratic iff and the smooth approximate necessity/finite-family iff with that intrinsic-interior input. The quadratic wrapper retains its finite-dimensional and transversality conditions. The smooth wrappers still separately require p to lie in the open **coordinate cube**. Relative interior of a hull does not imply that coordinate condition. Only the forward extraction needed for these wrappers is proved here; a separate positive-weights-to-intrinsicInterior iff is not claimed.

## Independent sharp controls

- A boundary point of the segment with truth values 0 and 1 cannot have a strictly positive weight at both listed truths. The boundary is excluded from intrinsic interior by the checked extraction theorem
- A singleton {0} has 0 in its intrinsic interior, while 0 is not in (0,1). The smooth wrapper's coordinate-interior premise cannot be dropped
- An empty hull has no intrinsic-interior point
- Two identical listed truths admit positive weights; an explicit three-entry list with duplicated zero and one obtains weights (1/4,1/4,1/2) at its midpoint
- An infinite diagonal spike family is eventually zero for each fixed index but has a unit spike in every column. This is a kernel-checked counterexample to exchanging pointwise and uniform vanishing, explaining the finite-family step. It is not presented as a new full infinite smooth-score countermodel
- Replacing relative-interior membership by mere hull membership, or forcing the constructed tolerance to zero, causes the retained source proof to reject the mutation

The exact arithmetic controls verify the centroid-extension/blend identities across singleton and duplicate configurations, and verify the common finite remainder bound. They do not substitute for the topological or limit arguments.
