# Necessary truth sensitivity and operative factive grounds

9 October 2026 UTC. Additive T20 investigation. No integration, closure, protected-state alteration, confidence revision, or retrospective duration certification.

## Result

The strongest inspected account survives the proposed constant-assent challenge when the competent route is fully specified. Known facts actually used in a relevant inference supply a sensitive factive ground even when its conclusion is necessary. A nineteen-line derivation in the authors' named System R verifies that result without invoking empty sets of false-target possible worlds.

The qualification matters. Relevant implication, material implication, and ordinary classical consequence cannot be interchanged. The developed authors expressly supply a bridge from implication to a counterfactual condition; it is not simply missing from their theory. A finite R-sound matrix shows that their particular premise-only bridge does not follow if implication is read materially, or merely as arbitrary classical entailment to an independent theorem. This is a conditional-typing obstruction, not a counterexample to competent-inference closure. A combined material-premise ground defeats that particular attempted matrix counterexample.

For initial mathematical testimony or a nonfactive intellectual seeming, the required sensitivity remains a substantive application premise. The inspected source stipulates it in its example. That gives a coherent sufficient-case description, but not an independent identification of a particular human recognition route. It is not a demonstrated failure of the analysis, and absence of a reductive account is not a first-order defeater.

Generic knowledge requires truth, actual basing, and sensitive reasons. Experiential reasons and known facts can serve as bases. The article allows generic knowledge despite some misleading defeaters. Its closure argument uses a known premise as a factive ground; its methods discussion distinguishes operative routes. Its final section replaces ordinary possible-world subjunctives with relevant conditionals. The necessary-truth example grants sensitive mathematical testimony and then sensitive intellectual experience. Existing conditions and laws enter the stated necessary condition for an implicative subjunctive; the relevant notes provide references, not a selection algorithm. Note 19 rejects automatic inheritance of every premise-belief's reasons. These distinctions govern this assessment.

## A positive relevant inference proof

Write →R for relevant implication throughout this section. Let P and N be arbitrary formulas and let

B = P ∧ (P →R N).

System R proves both

B →R N

and

¬N →R ¬B.

RELEVANT_PROOF.md gives the full nineteen-line derivation. It uses only displayed axiom instances and modus ponens. Conjunction elimination supplies B →R P and B →R (P →R N). Composition, exchange and contraction yield B →R N. Axiom 15 and double-negation introduction yield its contrapositive. The companion checker independently verifies the syntax of every line and rejects a corrupted final consequent. This is a proof for arbitrary substitutions, not a sample of successful truth assignments.

Now add the epistemic facts, expressly and separately:

1. The subject knows P and the relevant conditional P →R N.
2. Those known contents are actually used in the subject's competent inference to N.
3. B, or the corresponding combined known reasons, is an operative factive ground under the account's basing condition.

The theorem then supplies the required sensitivity of that ground. The account can consequently classify the resulting true belief as generic knowledge. This is precisely the place where granting genuine knowledge and genuine basing matters. The proof does not manufacture either from a table.

There is no requirement that N have false alternatives among ordinary metaphysical possibilities. The derivation also contains no premise that a person must articulate, introspect or independently certify this entire theory. It is an analysis of the inference's ground, not a demand for reflective self-certification.

A constant assenter who never uses the known premise facts does not satisfy item 2. It cannot acquire the same ground merely because an observer can write a sound proof beside its output. If a person really does use independently known relevant facts as reasons, describing the person as a constant assenter no longer establishes the intended counterexample. Conversely, a widespread tendency to accept other false propositions need not, on this externalist account, remove every locally grounded instance of generic knowledge.

## The mathematical case and the input that must not be smuggled in

For the source's example, P is 7+4=11 and N is 7+5=12. There is a concrete content-using route: apply equality preservation under adding one, then the arithmetic identities ((7+4)+1)=7+5 and 11+1=12. These particular steps explain how a competent subject may use P in obtaining N. They are stronger input than the statement that N happens to be necessary.

The present propositional proof does not formalize a relevant arithmetic with equality and show that its nonlogical laws establish P →R N. One can instead grant that specific relevant implication or include independently known laws in a genuinely operative relevant derivation. What is disallowed is replacing that work with the classical observation that every premise entails a necessary truth. That would discard the content-use distinction the test is supposed to examine.

The teacher's prior knowledge and the learner's initial mathematical uptake remain substantive inputs. Factive basing cannot bootstrap them by inserting the target N itself as a supposedly known reason. The 2010 non-presupposition qualification reinforces this constraint. Nor does a later correct inference retroactively establish that an earlier experience was already knowledge.

## A precise typing control for the stated closure bridge

Define an algebra with values D = {−2, −1, 1, 2}, designated values {1, 2}, negation −x, conjunction min, disjunction max, and

x →R y = max(−x,y) when x ≤ y;
x →R y = min(−x,y) otherwise.

The script checks all sixteen R axiom schemes on every assignment of their four possible schematic variables, and checks preservation of designation by modus ponens and adjunction. There are 4,096 axiom evaluations. By induction on derivations, this establishes soundness for the inspected axiomatization. It does not establish completeness, nor identify the algebra as the philosophically correct model of counterfactual cognition.

Let N = Q →R Q. Identity is an R theorem. Under every value of Q, N is designated and ¬N is undesignated. No value in this algebra and its negation are both designated. Thus the control does not exploit a contradictory target, even as a purely algebraic example.

At Q=1 and P=2:

- N=1
- P is designated
- The material conditional P ⊃ N, represented by ¬P ∨ N, has value 1
- P →R N has value −2
- ¬N →R ¬P has value −2

Consequently, actuality of P and its material conditional to N do not entail the premise-only relevant sensitivity used in the 2010 note 21 bridge. Reading the informal implication as unrestricted classical entailment does not automatically repair this: the classical counterpart of N is an independent identity theorem, so every classical premise entails it, while the relevant conditional still need not hold.

This is not an allegation that the authors intend material implication. Their language and examples support a stronger implication reading, and the 2017 account explicitly invokes relevant conditionals. The result instead identifies the exact scope of a justified positive claim: relevant factive transmission has been proved; unrestricted classical closure has not been proved by silently replacing its implication with →R. If note 21's bridge is imposed independently, it supplies additional substantive structure rather than following from the material reading and R alone.

### The important compound-ground control

The same valuation is not a countermodel to all knowledge closure. Let Bmat = P ∧ (¬P ∨ N). At P=2, N=1, this compound has value 1, and both Bmat →R N and ¬N →R ¬Bmat are designated. More generally, the checker evaluates all sixteen P,Q assignments with N=Q →R Q: the combined-ground sensitivity passes every one.

The authors already permit combined grounds. Thus a proposed counterexample using only P could omit exactly the additional operative fact that their best reply needs. The independent reviewer also checked the stronger control for every positive target value, with the same result. Success in this one algebra is not a proof of the general combined-ground theorem; failure of the P-only bridge is not a disproof of the complete combined-ground strategy. Both directions of overstatement are blocked by the same control.

## What happened to the experience swap attempt

A preliminary test assigns a necessary identity N and two actual experiential reasons E_C and E_G positive values. Exchanging their intensional values exchanges the sensitivity verdicts while their designation and separately stipulated basing facts stay unchanged. The script retains that result transparently.

It does not show that a fully described competent mathematical route and a constant-assent route are epistemically interchangeable. The reduct contains no bridge connecting the competence description to those intensional values. It therefore leaves out the very additional input under dispute. Against the source's developed case it is a verification of known non-entailment, not a new refutation. The article already rejects the relevant form of explosion from a necessary truth. A fresh count does not create new philosophical evidence against that position.

This failed test usefully directs the investigation away from arbitrary relevance assignments and toward a particular operative ground. The positive proof above is the stronger result: when an independently supported relevant derivation and genuine factive use are available, the account has a concrete solution rather than a mere label.

## Initial recognition and proper function

For an initial nonfactive seeming E, actual occurrence, content N, and actual basing do not themselves prove ¬N →R ¬E. The theory's case description adds the relevant sensitivity. That is a legitimate substantive premise of a sufficient-case analysis; it is not a reductive explanation of why a selected capacity has it. The earlier paper's primitive notions make that limit explicit.

An independent application would need to connect this particular capacity and episode to the relevant relation. Suitable evidence might concern the origin and content of the capacity, its current operative features, competent use of identifiable grounds, or appropriately motivated background conditions. The present work does not determine a universal checklist, demand that a subject possess a complete causal theory, or infer lack of knowledge from our incomplete explanation.

Proper function is a separate comparison standard, not a fourth clause secretly present in the authors' generic-knowledge definition. Their admission of generic knowledge under some poor source appraisals must be tested on its own terms. Calling a process epistemically unattractive under a stronger norm does not by itself refute their narrower classification. No independently specified global proper-function account has been derived here, and none has been shown impossible.

For necessary Creator recognition, the algebra supplies no false-Creator metaphysical alternative and no subject-level observation. The warranted result is neither refutation nor new verification. A contingent rescue prediction must not be substituted for necessary existence. The retained T17 appraisal and the inherited acquisition/exercise and present-use distinctions are untouched.
