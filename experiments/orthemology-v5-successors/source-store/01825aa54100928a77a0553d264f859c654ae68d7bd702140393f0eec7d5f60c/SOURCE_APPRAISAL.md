# Source-centred appraisal: al-Nubuwwat, second unit opening

Dated Nubuwwat-stage assessment, preserved with its actual evidence boundary. The later Safadiyya study and final current appraisal are separate records; this text is not retrospectively revised into their verdict.

## Scope and principal finding

This appraisal follows actual continuous reading of the assigned 24,281-word electronic portion, NB-S002-L0050–0138 (volume 02 windows 001–012), four completed bounded contextual rereads, and eighteen directly viewed critical-edition scan pages. It is not a whole-book or whole-volume verdict. Exact receipt files distinguish first traversal, later rereads and print controls. Adjacent divisions belong to other readers.

This portion offers a substantial theory of evidence and intentional attestation. It explicitly distinguishes an event's causal production from its normative authorisation, a token's source/custody from its communicative purpose, evidence of a person's claim from evidence that the claim is true, and a necessary characteristic from a sufficient identifying criterion. It allows contextual inquiry and suspension when a rival explanation is strong. A reading that reduces it to “an unusual thing happened, therefore God endorsed this prophet” is textually inadequate.

That finding is not yet an independent verification of the divine commission of a particular prophet. The theory's decisive applications depend on substantive premises about divine wisdom/nondeception, the character and truthfulness of particular messengers, the kinds and limits of ordinary/demonic powers, and the occurrence/transmission of particular signs. Those premises are variously argued, treated as necessary natural knowledge, supported by scripture, or stated through reports. The present reading neither erases those positive arguments nor converts their source-internal certainty into an independently established neutral demonstration.

## 1. What knowledge is meant to be

L0059–0063 denies that a bare report, merely capable of truth and falsity, can warrant its own acceptance. One must know the report's truth or the reporter's truthfulness. Clear premises can be sensory or self-evident; proof of less evident matters leads back to them. Guidance identifies an end and a path; evidence shows it is the right path; discrimination answers rival apparent guides and counterarguments. L0061 explicitly says an argument is incomplete without answering its opponent.

This is an ambitious epistemic programme rather than hostility to reason. The author defends rational disputation, distinguishes inquiry from inherited ignorance, accepts that premises needed vary with a hearer's background, and concedes that both syllogistic and analogical arguments may be certain or probable according to their material (L0053; L0098–0100). His attack is on allegedly exclusive and overabstract logical methods, not on all inference.

His “proof must entail its object” rule (L0088, L0108, L0113) is best read here as a criterion for a conclusive sign. It should not be turned into the claim that any fallible evidence is worthless, because the text itself recognises probable reasoning, investigation and strong-versus-weak alternatives. It remains fair to ask how practical contextual confidence earns the stronger label of necessity in particular applications.

The author also knows the difference between hearing a claim and establishing its truth (L0122). This matters: the mere fact that Muhammad or Moses says he is commissioned is not the purported conclusion. The conclusion is the reality of the commission. A further ethical layer constrains what an audience is entitled to affirm: L0125–0126 criticises public accusation beyond evidence and distinguishes confidential advice from public disclosure. That layer sometimes stretches the term “false” beyond simple noncorrespondence; it should be noticed rather than silently harmonized.

## 2. Knowledge, agency and purpose in the divine argument

The creator who develops a human into a knower, speaker and writer is said to be capable of informing that human of commands/news and of teaching recognition of a messenger (L0073–0075). A human messenger's common nature and language are treated as appropriate means of disclosure rather than defects. The source connects epistemic provision to generosity: greater human need calls for greater provision (L0076).

This is a positive a-fortiori argument for the possibility and fittingness of revelation. It is not merely an inference from complexity to an unnamed designer. It portrays an agent with knowledge, intention, goodness, pedagogic purpose and concern for accessible guidance. But ability to inform does not by itself establish actual informing; the move from ability to fitting action relies on the subsequent wisdom, justice, mercy and divine-custom premises. The author supplies those premises rather than overlooking the distinction, although their independent warrant is the remaining question.

Especially important is L0086–0087: God may providentially “send” winds, destructive agents and devils without approving what they do. A commissioned messenger, by contrast, conveys what God commands, loves and approves. Thus generic causal dependence never alone entails divine authorisation. This distinction makes the source's own test more demanding and explains why it needs evidential specificity and intention.

## 3. The source-owned attestation model

The reconstruction below is explanatory prose, not a new formal proof:

1. Establish that the putative sign really occurs and identify the relevant sender independently enough to recognise his conduct.
2. Determine whether the marker comes from that sender and was deliberately given with this envoy.
3. Determine whether the act was performed to inform/certify, rather than for a different purpose.
4. Exclude a strong incompatible explanation or competing claimant that would destroy the marker's discriminating force.
5. Determine what proposition the sender thereby certifies and why the sender is truthful in that certification.
6. Infer commission or the relevant reported truth, allowing distinct evidence from content, character, prior messengers, followers and subsequent events.

Steps 2–4 are explicit in the human-ring/turban/private-secret case, L0103–0107. The strongest exact locator is printed 771, within the complete printed 768–772 argument: «فإن الاستدلال مداره على أنه أرسله بالعلامة، وأنه إنما أرسله بها ليبين صدقه». The author tests loss/theft, a dead sender, unauthorised use of a seal and repair/sealing purposes. He then says: «فإذا عرض مثل هذا الاحتمال وقوي توقفوا». The edited apparatus reports that this suspension clause is repeated in the manuscript witness. The print control therefore reinforces rather than removes its presence.

The correspondence to the divine case is explicit, not merely supplied by this appraisal. L0111, print 779, asks whether the mark is from him or another, whether he sent the envoy with it, and whether he intended information/certification. Print 777–781 contains the full chapter. Sender-purpose is consequently a source-owned category. Speech and action may both attest; no prior agreed code is always needed if circumstances reveal intention. Calling a liar a prophet or an idol a god does not confer the corresponding property (L0105).

A marker may authenticate several genuine envoys because its rule concerns truthful commission as a kind, not unique possession by one individual forever (L0114). Evidence can be earlier/later than the claimant and does not need a simultaneous challenge (L0118–0119). The author further allows testimony from an independently trusted person to suffice without an extraordinary event in some cases (L0111–0112). It is therefore a plural, audience-sensitive evidential account.

## 4. Strongest positive reasons furnished by this portion

### A. It makes the right distinctions before inferring

The source separates extraordinary performance from proof, generic divine causation from approved mission, and material custody from intended certification. It expressly rejects local ignorance as proof that no one else can perform an art. Its ordinary examples include medicine, crafts, poetry, skilled swimming and other regionally exceptional acquired capacities (L0130–0133). This anticipates genuine alternative explanations.

### B. It explains intention without arbitrary conventions

Private possessions and shared secrets make communicative purpose intelligible even without a prior token agreement. The model tracks ordinary practical reasoning, including what is already known about the sender and situation. Its recognition of loss, misuse and alternative purposes prevents a simplistic inference from mere possession.

### C. It permits defeat of a particular proof

If the supposed counterparty produces an indistinguishable sign while denying the claim, that sign no longer discriminates. The author says this repeatedly and specifically states that losing one proof need not falsify its conclusion or defeat other independent proofs (L0122–0124). This is a principled distinction, not necessarily an evasion.

### D. Its moral theology is doing argumentative work

Fitra, rationally intelligible good/bad action, nondeception, wisdom and not obliging the impossible supply the bridge from divine ability to trustworthy attestation (L0068, L0072, L0076–0077). The author does not rest content with an inscrutable will that could equally certify falsehood. If these premises are secured, endorsement by such an agent has powerful evidential force.

### E. It is more than a contest of spectacular powers

Consistency of message, truthfulness in ordinary dealings, purposes, moral fruits, absence of corrupt incentives, and mutual testimony are included (L0069, L0111–0112, L0133–0136). This supplies a richer comparison of hypotheses than a single strange event. His criticism of reducing prophecy to dreamlike imagination also responds to the distinction between an internal experience and an external commissioned encounter.

### F. It includes genuine qualifications and concessions

The author allows early disagreement on Adam's garden, qualifies a report's authenticity, criticises a purportedly weak ablution report, credits opponents' merits and permits their immediate recognition of a true sign despite what he sees as inconsistent doctrines. His generic reading of the Mosaic promise can include Jesus and Muhammad rather than only his preferred particular referent. These details complicate an overly uniform picture of his method.

## 5. Strongest remaining objections and the text's resources for answering them

### A. A successful definition does not identify an actual instance

“A true prophetic sign entails true prophecy” states a valid standard. Identifying which observed event meets it is the hard step. L0109 sometimes moves from named scriptural marvels to necessary recognition; L0120–0121 sorts phenomena by kind, magnitude and mode. Without independently establishing the event and classification, the exclusion of false instances may remain stipulated. The source responds with concrete kinds, character/content considerations and fitri recognition, so the proper criticism is an evidential-burden question, not that it contains only a verbal tautology.

### B. The divine sender differs from the familiar human sender

A family's ring inference rests on known identity, habits, possession and practical purposes. The theological inference requires corresponding access to God's conduct and exclusive capacities. The source appeals to natural knowledge, created signs, wisdom and historical custom, and would also regard an omniscient/omnipotent sender as immune to literal theft or accidental loss. Even so, providentially allowing misleading agents is already admitted. One needs a warranted reason that this event belongs to special certification rather than a permitted trial or another purpose. That burden is explicitly recognised but not independently discharged merely by the human analogy.

### C. “Necessary natural knowledge” is substantive

The claim that all humans necessarily know God never lies or certifies a false prophet is central (L0068), but universal assertion does not settle philosophical disagreement about divine nature. L0072 supplies a rational moral framework and L0073–0077 reasons from beneficence and intelligible guidance. Those are positive arguments, yet they depend on judgements about perfection, proper ends and what an agent must do. The source's own anti-bare-assertion rule applies to this bridge as well.

### D. Sincere testimony can still be mistaken

Character can reduce the likelihood of deliberate deception. It does not by itself distinguish a genuine external revelation from a sincere misinterpretation of an internal experience. The source itself allows a pretender to have been misled rather than deliberately lying (L0124–0125). Its fuller response is the convergence of character, content, exclusive signs and supernatural classification, not sincerity alone. The burden therefore shifts to whether that convergence has been established in a given case.

### E. Follower-signs can generate reciprocal dependence

The author legitimately notes that a follower's wonder could corroborate the prophet being followed. But if one identifies a wonder as genuine solely because its bearer follows the already-approved prophet, then uses it to establish that prophet's authority, the inference is circular. The text says one must distinguish saintly from demonic phenomena and gives broader criteria, while referring fuller demonstrations elsewhere (L0128, L0132–0136). That means a noncircular route is intended; this assigned portion does not supply a fully independently checked historical application for every example.

### F. Universal capability claims and eventual exposure are difficult to verify

The source correctly rejects “no rival here and now” and asks how universal nonopposition could be known (L0109, L0118–0119). Its preferred classification still requires confidence about what no human or jinn can achieve, and about what kinds/quantities separate genuine signs from lesser wonders. Those are not established by the bare failure of a local rival. Similarly, “a liar may flourish briefly but never receive lasting comparable support” (L0077) needs operational historical criteria for lasting support, exposure and a suitable observation period. Otherwise too many outcomes can be accommodated. The source distinguishes ordinary tyrants from divine-commission claimants, so ordinary political success alone is not a direct counterexample.

### G. Historical and textual dependencies remain

The miracles, expert sorcerers' conversions, prophetic predictions, jinn encounters and representative false claimants are received narratives here. Reading them is not a new authentication of occurrence, transmission, or independence. The author gives a useful negative-transmission principle at L0088–0089, but this portion does not run an independent dossier for every conspicuous event it invokes. Nor does a separate print witness automatically supply an independent historical tradition.

### H. Polemical accounts need source checking before generalisation

The author often gives sophisticated distinctions, yet his accounts of philosophers and theologians also make sweeping claims about what they cannot know or what their premises “really” imply. Those reductios must be distinguished from opponents' own express commitments. It would exceed this task to certify them without reading the opposing works.

## 6. What the print control changes

The central attestation passage was not lost in electronic corruption. Printed 768–772 and 777–781 verify its purposive, custody-sensitive, defeater-sensitive shape. Minor variants are documented in SCAN_CONTROL_NOTES.md, including the edited«بصاحبهم» against electronic«على حيهم» and the manuscript's repetition around suspension. The original electronic strings remain unmodified.

The Adam/garden excursus also survives direct print inspection at 705–709. Modern footnote 707 explicitly confronts a contrary passage it attributes to Majmuʿ al-Fatawa and speaks of two positions. Thus neither doctrinal expectation nor another work's attributed position licenses deleting the received Nubuwwat passage. The correct result is a preserved local conflict with bounded attribution, not an invented harmonisation or chronology. This is orthogonal to the central attestation proof but important to reading the source comprehensively.

## 7. Bounded judgement

For knowledge, agency and purpose, this portion supplies an integrated account: intelligible creation/teaching, a knowing willing giver, morally directed guidance, and communicative acts whose special intention can be investigated. For source-owned attestation, it supplies an explicit argument with rival-origin, custody, purpose and suspension conditions. These are meaningful philosophical resources and cannot fairly be dismissed as absent.

The strongest unresolved question is the application gap: whether particular received signs and the divine-nature premises establish this source attribution to a person who has not already granted the prophecy or scripture. The portion contains serious attempts to bridge that gap, including natural moral knowledge, familiar communicative practice, character/content and multiple signs; it also relies heavily on contested necessities and unverified received histories. My reading therefore supports a richer, nontrivial and conditionally powerful source case, while withholding a claim of independent historical or universally compelling metaphysical closure. This judgement is limited to the actual assigned text and controlled pages; it does not forecast what adjacent portions may contribute.
