### Assertion ownership and veracity

The veracity audit separates relevant knowledge, awareness of the exact presented content, deliberate presentation, the classification of knowing counterfeit as a shortcoming, incompatibility of that shortcoming with fitting exercise, actual practical conformance and the connection between owned presentation and exercise.

The distinction between shortcoming classification and incompatibility with fitting exercise is substantive. Calling something a shortcoming does not, by itself, prove that it can never occur in an otherwise fitting act. Token-level truthfulness and a universal source-owned veracity claim also differ: the latter needs the stated coverage of awareness and control.

Under the stated knowledge, awareness and control coverage assumptions, together with factivity of knowledge of falsehood, a no-counterfeit formulation is equivalent to the corresponding T0 formulation. Factivity is needed for this equivalence, not for the positive veracity theorem. This records a genuine equivalence, but renaming the same requirement supplies no additional warrant. Strong informed ownership makes some awareness and control facts projections of the ownership relation; it does not include truth by definition.

The positive model permits a created speaker to make a false assertion using capacities productively supported by the original source. The assertion remains the created speaker's. A common productive source is therefore not automatically the owner of every supported utterance. Source truthfulness, even when warranted, still does not authenticate a particular alleged message.
