# Ameri: argument from reason

In *Proofs of God in the Soul, Reason and Science*, pp.249–274, Ameri argues that naturalism undermines the rational capacities its defence requires. His conditions include meaningful thought, understanding logical laws and their causal relevance to accepting conclusions. He separates epistemic reliance on reason from its proposed ontological dependence on God.

The chapter also considers consciousness, evolutionary explanation and intellectual capacities beyond immediate survival. It argues that adaptive success need not establish truth and rejects mere appeals to future scientific explanation.

Crucially, it anticipates computers: their interpreted success depends on rational designers and users, so they do not independently explain human rationality. A note allows God-created matter to produce consciousness while disputing the adequacy of naturalist matter. The challenge therefore exceeds the claim that a material device cannot execute a correct inference.

This assessment continuously read the entire chapter and notes, with immediate surrounding pages and decisive visual rereads. It identifies separate burdens concerning original content, content-relevant causation and cognitive warrant. The finite implementation test addresses only a weaker local incompatibility; it neither refutes the full argument nor establishes naturalism or theism.
