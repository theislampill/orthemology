## 2. What Ibn Taymiyyah actually allows correction to do

The continuous seven-route fiṭrah argument in [Darʾ electronic division 014](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/dr-taardh-alaql-walnql-014.html), web lines 629–691, was reread from its opening through the end before the next section. The underlying unit is inherited coverage. Its developmental passage is the same locus whose Ameri 4:328 versus electronic 8:460–461 correspondence was previously flagged; no edition correction is asserted here.

The first route begins with a real distinction between true and false judgments and between beneficial and harmful objects of desire. It argues for an internal inclination toward truth and benefit. Its theological application additionally treats Creator-recognition as true and divine love as beneficial. It does not derive those substantive premises from the sheer occurrence of an inclination. The teacher-regress argument then denies that every recognition must depend on a prior human instructor.

The second and third routes are directly relevant to correction. An external instructor can assist the native capacity; development and impediments are explicitly allowed. The text distinguishes three instructional effects: bringing what was not known to attention so that it becomes known, reminding the person of something forgotten, and prompting what was not being willed. These are the author's alternatives, not three stipulated truth labels in a model.

The positive cognitive work is therefore **the recipient's own understanding or recollection of the object and its relevant features**. An utterance does not produce knowledge independently of the recipient's cognitive capacity. The author's comparison with teaching animals or inanimate things establishes that uptake requires a suitable subject-side capacity; it is not an independent demonstration that every human uptake has a truth-directed basis.

This yields two precise restrictions:

1. Being fiṭrah-compatible does not entail being the revival of previously possessed occurrent knowledge. The source explicitly includes learning something previously unknown.
2. Being occasioned by instruction does not entail dependence on the instructor's assertion as the epistemic basis. The source allows internally arising thoughts to perform the same attentional or recollective work. It does not require public premise recital by the learner.

Thus the proposed contrast between “newly learned content” and “warranted basic recognition” is not exhaustive. A person may newly come to see a truth through a competent recognitional capacity when another person directs attention to it. This possibility was already preserved in CP19; it must not be presented as today's discovery.

The seventh route contributes a separate substantive argument concerning the determinate final object of love. It is not a record of a selected person's cognitive history. Repeating it as though it observed the episode required by CP20 would change its evidential role.

## 3. Ibn al-Qayyim's actual correction of a mistaken inference

The [received official *Miftāḥ dār al-saʿāda* witness](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/media/download/86) was consulted through the existing byte-identified file. The complete dispute beginning at 1:243 and its resolution through 1:278 were read continuously in ordered page excerpts, with earlier 229–242 context inspected selectively. The work's surrounding title is the superiority and role of knowledge, not a modern experimental test of belief revision. The edition and body-extraction limitations are recorded in SOURCE_READS.json.

Here there is a concrete mistaken inference to correct: **if a person does not follow the truth, that person cannot genuinely know it**. The author first develops the two opposing positions and their scriptural arguments. His own resolution begins at 263. He distinguishes a complete determinant, whose effect follows, from a contributing disposition whose operation can fail through weakness, a missing condition, or an obstruction. Knowledge has the latter role relative to completed practical guidance unless the additional conditions are supplied.

The content of the correction is not merely “objections can be removed.” It distinguishes **knowing the truth from loving, intending and following it**. At 262, these are different demands on the heart. At 264–270 he gives concrete possible impediments, including competing interests, attachment to status or family, opposition to an adversary, and established habit. His own reported conversations at 267 illustrate refusal motivated by drink or expected inheritance. These are authorial reports, not independently authenticated interviews in this operation, and they concern accepting Islam, not first recognition that a Creator exists.

The second half of the resolution prevents the opposite overstatement. At 271–275 the author asks whether knowledge simply persists unchanged beneath the obstruction. His answer allows obscuring, weakening and actual loss. At 276–278, some denials of knowledge instead concern the absence of its proper practical fruit. Those readings cannot be conflated.

**Application to the proposed recovery test:** a successful intervention that removes a practical obstacle may change conduct while leaving the person's knowledge intact. Another intervention may enable new learning or restore forgotten knowledge. An expression of consent, greater confidence, or changed action does not by itself locate which cognitive transition occurred. Other independently warranted evidence can support a recovery appraisal; the outcome alone is insufficient. Conversely, continued refusal is not, just as such, proof that the cognitive evidence was inadequate. One must identify what the correction addresses.

The positive discriminator in this primary unit is the operative difference between an epistemic obstruction and an obstruction to willing or acting on what is already known. Where the actual impediment is independently identified, that difference is useful. The source supplies examples and arguments for the distinction; it does not supply a universal observer-accessible test that authenticates the earlier Creator-recognition of every corrected person. Its normative and historical diagnoses are not new diagnoses of any contemporary dissenter.

The distinction was already present in the saved Miftāḥ reading and the later evidence-withdrawal report. Fresh rereading verifies its present use; it earns no first-discovery or new case-verification credit.

The narrow warranted outcome is therefore a **source-controlled non-inference from corrective success to recovered knowledge**, alongside a positive description of what correction can genuinely accomplish. It may restore access to a surviving ground, occasion a new competent recognition, supply a warranted testimony, or furnish a competent inference. Those are not inferior outcomes because the earliest acquisition history remains unsettled.

No additional generic source campaign is justified by this local result alone.
