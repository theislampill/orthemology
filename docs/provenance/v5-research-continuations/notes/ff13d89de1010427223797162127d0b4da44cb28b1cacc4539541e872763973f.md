## The strongest affirmative case

The controlled comparison is more than a stipulative preference ordering. It identifies actual harm to a subject, fixes the agent's understanding and available control, removes ignorance/coercion and relevant competing reasons, and then compares fitting practical response with gratuitous injury. Effectiveness does not make the recipient's loss unreal. This supplies a prima facie rational warrant for a practical distinction even though a factual description of injury does not, by logic alone, entail every disputed normative proposition.

The source eligibility argument also has content. Concern's relevant content is intelligent responsiveness to another subject's actual condition, not necessarily bodily appetite, reciprocal dependence or submission to an external superior. Removing those creaturely limitations does not obviously remove that content. The argument thus challenges a rival who treats all concern as necessarily need-based. It appropriately leaves cross-agent normative relevance explicit: the recipient's good must count as a reason for the agent affecting it, with role and competing reasons still relevant.

The original-giver step applies the established source-sensitive structure to a newly specified respect: not bare capacity but genuine reason-responsive practical excellence. This is a real philosophical pressure against assigning every achievement to a wholly received recipient while its original giver is eligible yet wholly inferior in that same respect. It avoids a generic rule that causes must copy all their effects' properties. Its warranted force can exceed bare stipulation without becoming a consequence of efficient production alone.

The crucial remaining gap is comprehensive, essential governance. Producing a virtuous recipient is not a transfer of a conserved quantity of virtue; the priority principle needs its own metaphysical rationale. Even accepting some source benevolence does not exclude a mixed source. Neither occasional virtue, maximal power nor actual flawless exercise by itself establishes a standing nature incapable of every contrary exercise. The text now separates possession, comprehensive scope, intrinsic status and modal preservation precisely.

## Rival strength and eligibility

The malicious rival is treated at useful strength: it may be knowledgeable, powerful, uncoerced and genuinely representation-guided; its positive capacities need not disappear because its ultimate orientation is bad. It can allow genuine derivative knowledge and virtue. Thus a no-positive-actuality target is too weak, and universal deception is not implied merely by maliciousness.

Nonneed alone cannot do the evaluative work. A purpose's involving originated recipients differs from an agent's receiving its existence or intrinsic perfection from an independent completer. The same standard must be applied to renewed satisfaction in beneficent and malicious exercises. This leaves a substantive normative asymmetry to defend, rather than proving the two orientations equally justified or possible.

The v2 eligibility guard matters. Type-level intelligibility of some benevolent source cannot establish this source's ability or essential eligibility. An intrinsically malicious source can contest that de re step; conversely, attaching an essential-maliciousness predicate is no possibility proof. The affirmative sourcehood/perfection argument must engage the proposed nature rather than assume the eligibility dispute away. The final conjunctive principle makes this burden visible rather than hiding it in the word complete.

The controlled harmful-act case is also correctly distinguished from a historical judgment that an actual divine act lacks every justifying reason. The latter needs additional evidence. Identifying the norm's content need not already answer every application or theodicy.
