# Grounded attestation support under retraction

Research design and written result, 7 October 2026. This document precedes implementation. Its mathematical basis is finite positive Horn derivability and provenance, which are established subjects. The proposed programme increment is a source and speech-force sensitive connecting component, with exact retraction conditions, rather than a claim to have invented Horn logic or provenance semirings.

## Prior boundary and selected question

Actual T09 CONTINUATION SYNTHESIS_RESULT.md and CANDIDATE_RELATION_AND_SCOPE.md were read, together with the earlier CriterionTransport specification. They already own all-query information bounds, relational composition, backward coverage, H-MOAT joint realisers, copied-root constraints, current revocation and next-service support. We do not formalise those again or define continuation as a new list of invariants.

The selected question is narrower: when a recipient has several finite grounded paths from source occurrences and applicable rules to a conclusion, exactly which paths survive loss of some currently usable evidence or authority roots? In particular, can one retain warranted knowledge of a source-asserted obligation after losing a separate prospective-command route, without making the old obligation an unqualified current instruction? Can a mutually endorsing cycle supply its own missing authentication?

The intended success criterion is a kernel-checkable equivalence for exact support survival, grounded-derivation soundness and a concrete finite model showing why source truth, speech force, time and prospective authority cannot be substituted. No actual source truth, authority or physical execution is to be proved from the model's metadata.

## Exact definitions

Fix a type B of primitive support roots and a type C of claims. Root identity is the identity of the actual acquisition or supporting duty, not the displayed carrier label. Let base : B -> C assign each root its primitive claim. A positive finitary rule is a pair (P,c), with P a finite set of premise claims and c its conclusion. Let R be the selected rule set.

For a finite available-root set A, define D(A,c) by the least inductive relation:

- Root: if b is in A, infer D(A,base(b)).
- Rule: if (P,c) is in R and D(A,p) for every p in P, infer D(A,c).

Only finite derivations generated by these rules count. A cycle is not itself a derivation. Rules may have empty premise sets, but an empty-premise rule is an explicit axiom with its own soundness burden, not a consequence of a cycle.

A support S for c is a finite root set with D(S,c). It is minimal if D(S,c) and no proper subset T of S has D(T,c). For a fixed finite original root universe U, write M_U(c) for the family of all such minimal supports contained in U. This family can be empty and may contain the empty set when the rules explicitly prove c without roots.

A retraction set Delta removes roots from availability, giving U \\ Delta. Retraction may concern present evidential availability, a grant or another primitive duty. It does not, by the set operation alone, assert that a previously true historical proposition has become false. What any root means remains fixed across the comparison.

## Theorem 1 Monotonicity and conditional soundness

If A is a subset of A' and D(A,c), then D(A',c).

Proof: induction on the finite derivation. A root remains available by inclusion. Every premise derivation in a rule step transports by induction, so the same rule applies. This does not use acyclicity of the rule graph.

Let V interpret claims as propositions. Suppose each available primitive claim V(base(b)) holds, and every selected rule preserves V from all its premises to its conclusion. Then D(A,c) implies V(c).

Proof: induction on the derivation. The root case is the primitive soundness premise. The rule case applies the selected rule's soundness to the induction hypotheses. Source veracity, faithful attribution and legitimate constitutive force belong in the external soundness argument for the rules that use them. The proof does not manufacture them.

## Theorem 2 Exact support survival

For every finite U, every Delta and every c,

D(U \\ Delta,c) if and only if there exists S in M_U(c) with S disjoint from Delta.

Forward proof: because U \\ Delta is finite and derives c, choose among its deriving subsets one of smallest cardinality, S. Any proper subset of S has smaller cardinality and cannot derive c, so S is minimal. It lies in U and avoids Delta.

Backward proof: S disjoint from Delta and contained in U implies S is contained in U \\ Delta. Its derivation of c transports by Theorem 1.

This quantifies over every future retraction profile in the original finite universe. It is stronger than verifying one particular saved proof: a claim can survive through an alternative support even if its original chosen proof is no longer usable. It is not an infinite-service theorem. New rules, changed meanings, newly added roots or negative-premise rules require a changed analysis.

## Corollary 3 Exact defeat cuts and proof alternatives

A retraction Delta defeats c exactly when it intersects every member of M_U(c). Thus minimal defeating retractions are the minimal hitting sets of the minimal-support family. If the family is empty, c was already underivable. If it contains the empty support, no root-only retraction can defeat c.

A flattened union of all support roots is too conservative: requiring that union to survive treats alternative derivations as jointly necessary. A flattened set of individually occurring roots is also insufficient to reconstruct the alternative bundles. Whole derivational supports matter. This connects directly to the earlier source-relative whole-row discipline, but Theorem 2 gives an exact dynamic test rather than another static row warning.

## Corollary 4 Source assertion and prospective command can react differently

Use distinct claims ObligationAt(t0), ImperativeAt(t0), AuthorisedIssuerAt(t0), LiveStanding(now), MayIssueNext(now), ApplicableNow(t0) and ExecutableNow. An authenticated source-owned assertion of ObligationAt(t0), together with the applicable veracity and semantic-fidelity roots, supplies a testimonial derivation of ObligationAt(t0). It does not require an additional AuthorisedIssuerAt(t0) premise merely to repeat proof of that same obligation.

A completed bare constitutive directive at t0 derives ObligationAt(t0) through evidence that legitimate standing, correct force and scope held at t0. A later prospective-grant revocation does not delete those historical facts. Instead a distinct current root LiveStanding(now) supports a prospective conclusion MayIssueNext(now); removing that current root may defeat that conclusion. Evidence invalidation of an old attribution or historical-standing record is a different operation, which can remove an old epistemic route. A surviving alternative testimonial route can still support ObligationAt(t0) without a duplicate jurisdiction proof.

Execution now requires the correctly indexed applicability and implementation premises. Removing ApplicableNow(t0) can block ExecutableNow without retracting the historical obligation conclusion. Erasing t0 or interpreting a historical quotation as a new directive changes the rule/claim interpretation and is not licensed by Theorem 2.

The formal result does not assume that all testimony needs exceptional-source certification. Ordinary defeasible entitlement from T17 remains a separate epistemic route. A chosen primitive ordinary-entitlement root can be represented, but the finite model does not reduce all ordinary warrant to a factive universal calculus.

## Counterexample 5 Cyclic endorsement is not an anchor

Let C={a,b}; there are no available primitive roots and the only rules are a from b and b from a. The set {a,b} is closed under the rules, so a checker accepting any mutually consistent fixed point would accept it. Yet the least grounded derivation contains neither claim. Every alleged finite derivation of a would have a shorter derivation of b, and every derivation of b a shorter derivation of a; there is no leaf. Induction on derivation therefore rules out both.

Adding one genuine root for a grounds both conclusions. The rule graph may contain cycles without making the model useless. The distinction is ungrounded bootstrap versus anchored cyclic reuse, not a blanket ban on feedback or mutual testimony.

## Counterexample 6 Carrier cut minimisation does not commute with root identification

Take carrier labels a1,a2,a3,b1,b2. Six alternative support bundles are {ai,bj}, for i in {1,2,3} and j in {1,2}. At label level, the unique minimum-cardinality defeating set is {b1,b2}, of size two. The alternative cut {a1,a2,a3} has size three and is discarded by minimum-cardinality-first processing.

Now let a1,a2,a3 be three presentations of one acquisition root a, while b1 and b2 have distinct roots b and c. The six bundles become two minimal actual-root supports {a,b} and {a,c}. Their unique smallest defeating root set is {a}, size one. Mapping only the previously selected minimum label cut returns {b,c}, size two and misses the actual weakest support.

Therefore identify actual roots before optimising a root-fault budget, or retain enough of the full cut/support family to perform the quotient correctly. This is a concrete sharp control, not a new general claim that copying improves independence. It extends the prior copied-root ceiling into a failure of a specific plausible order of computation.

The quotient assumes aliases share the source-root failure duty being modelled. If different carrier channels fail independently, those channel duties need their own primitive roots; identifying the source does not erase real channel evidence.

## Finite effective computation

For finite B and C, enumerate root subsets S of U. Compute D(S,-) by ordinary least forward closure, which stabilises after at most |C| additions for a fixed S. Keep those S deriving c for which no proper subset derives c. This exact baseline computes M_U(c) and is enough for exhaustive controls.

A support-propagation algorithm can instead carry antichains: root b contributes {b}; a rule takes unions of one support from each premise; at each claim remove strict supersets of another known support. Iterate from no derived supports. A newly admitted support never needs to be reintroduced after a strict subset permanently dominates it, and there are at most |C| times 2^|U| possible supports. Thus the finite process terminates. Its soundness follows by combining finite derivations; completeness follows by induction on a finite derivation, since replacement by a smaller support preserves availability. Cycles without roots contribute no supports.

The antichain algorithm is proposed for concrete-symbolic testing against the exhaustive baseline. Its generic algorithmic correctness is not to receive Lean credit unless actually formalised. The initial Lean target is the derivation semantics, exact support-survival theorem, soundness and concrete counterexamples.

## Theorem 7 Exact reuse after the actual origin map is learned

Let L be a finite carrier-label universe U, F a finite family of complete support subsets of U, and pi : L -> B any actual-root map supplied at the time of use. The map need not have been known when the family was archived. A cut hits every member of F. Let I(F) contain all inclusion-minimal label cuts. Distinguish this from the generally smaller family containing only minimum-cardinality cuts.

For every pi, the inclusion-minimal cuts of the image support family pi(F) are exactly the inclusion-minimal members of {pi(C) : C in I(F)}.

Proof. The image of a label cut hits every image support: map an element witnessing each original intersection. Conversely, let D be an inclusion-minimal root cut. Its preimage within U is a label cut. By finiteness it contains an inclusion-minimal label cut C. Then pi(C) is a root cut and is contained in D. Minimality of D makes pi(C)=D. Finally an inclusion-minimal image of an archived minimal label cut is a minimal root cut: any smaller root cut contains a minimal root cut, which by the previous argument is another archived image, contradicting image minimality. All empty-family and empty-support cases are covered by the same definitions; existence is invoked only for a set already known to be a cut.

The quantifier order is useful for continuation. Archive the complete I(F) once; for every later supplied fixed origin map pi, image minimisation recovers the exact root cut family. This is not discovery or authentication of pi, and not robustness over an unresolved family of possible maps. Those are separate questions, including the prior T7 unknown-class work. New mappings between actual roots at different times are outside this fixed-map comparison.

The corresponding grounded-inference fact is also exact. Define Base_pi(r,c) to mean that some label l in U has pi(l)=r and supports primitive claim c. Derivability from actual-root availability A under Base_pi equals label derivability from the preimage of A in U under the original base relation. Induct on a grounded derivation in each direction. Consequently every minimal root support is the image of some inclusion-minimal label support. A single root may support several differently labelled facts, without being counted as independent roots several times.

## Sharper four-label counterexample and its lower boundary

The simple support family {{a1},{a2,b},{a3,b}}, with all three ai mapped to a and b to b, has the unique cheapest label cut {a1,b}, of cardinality two. Its image is {a,b}. The discarded inclusion-minimal label cut {a1,a2,a3}, of cardinality three, maps to {a}, the unique cheapest actual-root cut. Thus keeping every cheapest label cut still loses the true root optimum. The issue is not choosing a bad tie-breaker.

The bounded search enumerated all nonempty support antichains without empty support on one through four labels, and every partition as an origin map: 2,589 cases in total, 40 strict failures at four labels and none below four. The K3,2 example remains a second independently checked control.

A direct small-size argument explains the lower boundary for arbitrary support families in this unweighted model. With at most three labels, either the map is injective, all labels share one root, or two labels share one root and a third is separate. Injective maps preserve cardinality minima; a one-root image has cut cost one whenever any cut exists. In the two-plus-one case, if the true optimum uses the singleton root, its singleton label cut is already cardinality-minimal. If it uses the paired root, the two-label preimage is a cut. When a singleton label cut exists its image has the optimal cost one; otherwise that two-label cut is among all cardinality-minimum label cuts and its image again has cost one. Empty-family and empty-support cases do not furnish a strict finite-optimum failure. Therefore four labels are necessary and sufficient for the strict cost error in this particular model. The general small-size minimality proof is written; only the displayed four-label and K3,2 instances have kernel-control claims unless separately stated.

## Semantic scope of loss and cycles

A retraction theorem says whether a derivation remains available in this stipulated calculus. It does not say that losing every modeled support makes a previously true historical conclusion false. A source may have asserted a true duty at t0 even after its original evidence is unavailable. Prospective grant revocation now removes a current authority root; evidence invalidation concerns the current warrant of an old record. The formal occurrence fixture defines these as different operations and preserves historical standing when the live grant is revoked.

Similarly, the least-grounded relation excludes a finite derivation consisting only of an unanchored cycle. It does not say that every true proposition must have a Horn derivation, that every good epistemic route is representable in this model, or that a proposition excluded here cannot be learned through another external route. These are limits of the exact statement, not exceptions added after applying it.
