# Numerical effects, actual production, and proof certificates

8 October 2026 UTC. Successor research under the renewed T20 mandate. Recovered checkpoints are read-only. Recovery and waiting receive no research credit. This is not final integration or a complete metaphysical countermodel.

## Result first

**Numerical effect identity and actual productive attribution can be represented separately without making an owner part of the effect's name. But neither identity nor a complete output-sufficiency proof determines the actual productive ground.**

The new operation makes that distinction operationally exact. It separates a source-neutral effect/birth registry, declared actual operative routes, and logical certificates about output occurrence. Adding a sound but redundant certificate citing B changes no actual production. Adding a genuinely productive route involving B changes the declared production inventory, even when the entire root-presence output table remains identical.

This separation is an **interface contract**, largely enforced by construction. It is not a philosophical resolution. The substantive new application is the coupled comparison: the same endpoint table, same effect/birth identity convention and same minimal occurrence certificates coexist with different operative-route structures. A fine route intervention distinguishes the certificate-only and productive additions. A typed route–source–effect tensor preserves the relevant difference; contracting away the route coordinate loses it.

The strongest current reactive candidate remains the same joined candidate. Its represented intentions, own ends, forecasts, intrinsic resources and actual supports are retained. Neither certificate enrichment nor a one-birth convention gives either participant exhaustive actual attribution. The source's argued bridge from completed original undertaking to unborrowed entire efficacy remains necessary and unproved by this construction.

## 2. Three distinct types

### Effect identity and its observed state

An `EffectID` has an episode and an event key. Neither component names A, B, an owner or a source set. A `BirthID` refers to that effect with an ordinal. The adapter supplies one birth record per event key. This is a disclosed unique-birth convention, not a discovery about metaphysical origination.

The event's state is stored separately. Two records may concern the same effect and birth IDs while differing in state; the strengthened adapter preserves that difference. Thus identity projection is not misreported as equality of complete endpoint states.

The identity convention is sufficient for comparing alternative descriptions at this represented interface. It does not establish that q would remain numerically q in every counterfactual production history. The inherited origin-essentialist qualifications remain in force.

### Actual operative routes

An `OperativeRoute` records a primitive direct route designated as actually active by the preserved interpreter. It refers to a birth, positive original roots and absence guards. These are attribution/execution records. A route record is not automatically an additional created object, a second birth, or an intrinsic act inhering in another subject.

For the direct programmes, route activation is computed from registered productive rules and issued acts. It is not chosen by asking which source the desired conclusion should favour. Nevertheless the decision that a registered rule denotes genuine production, rather than a mere logical consequence, remains part of the causal interpretation. The code does not authenticate that interpretation as metaphysically real.

The inherited engine calls these executed-rule records `Proof`. In that engine **every Rule is productive**. Appending a rule therefore changes the declared causal model. It is not a harmless proof extension merely because the added implication follows from another rule. This audit does not call the inherited engine erroneous for respecting its own declared contract.

### Logical occurrence certificates

A `Certificate` proves an output occurrence under an effect-indexed finite law. Its cited facts need not all be productive causes. For example, if A alone entails e under the law, A together with B also entails e. The extra cited B can be logically redundant.

The combined validation/applicability workflow checks the conclusion's effect and state, admissible literal names, consistency, nonvacuous implication-validity, and programme binding. Specifically, `certificate_valid` checks implication-validity and conclusion typing; `certificate_applicable` additionally checks programme-ID equality, the cited facts at the current profile and the occurrence in the ledger. A certificate for another effect or another programme does not qualify merely because its formula is true somewhere.

These are epistemic/logical checks. Even a sound, applicable certificate is not thereby an actual production route. Programme hashes bind a definition; they do not authenticate an actual-world mechanism.

## 3. The coupled productive-versus-certificate experiment

Begin with the direct programme A→e and actual issued acts A,B. Its actual productive support for e is A.

**Certificate extension:** add the sound certificate A∧B⇒e. It is valid because A already suffices. The effect, birth, state, operative routes and positive attribution stay unchanged. A proof-sensitive union of cited source names changes from A to AB, exposing why that union cannot be used as actual causal provenance.

**Productive extension:** instead register a new productive route AB→e alongside A→e. Both fire in the actual AB profile. Every presence-profile output is still unchanged: e occurs exactly when A acts. But the operative record now includes AB, and the aggregate positive support is AB.

This second model genuinely **declares** an additional contributor; the certificate extension does not. Its actual productivity is not independently demonstrated by endpoint equivalence or by the new record's name. If one denies that such an absorbed route can be genuinely productive, that is precisely a substantive nonabsorption/efficacy objection. The test does not decide it by treating the new route as an extra created trace.

Now disable the original A→e route while keeping the issued acts AB fixed:

- The certificate-only extension no longer produces e. Its earlier certificate must be revalidated and fails under the changed programme.
- The productive extension still produces e through the remaining AB route.

Erasing certificates commutes with actual-state projection; disabling a productive route need not. The route cut is a declared fine intervention, not a certified wise world preserving every original-agent premise. Rule-cut methods and the general observation obstruction are inherited; the new credit is their explicit paired use at this two-layer interface.

## 4. Joint production, overdetermination, and sole positive attribution

The three recovered programmes `coalescence`, `redundant` and `priority_A` all compute A∨B over the four **presence** profiles. At actual AB they have respectively:

1. one joint operative route rooted in AB;
2. two source-only operative routes, one rooted in A and one in B;
3. one actually operative A route, with B's alternative route blocked by A's presence.

They have the same endpoint table, effect states and supplied numerical effect/birth IDs. All three also have the same minimal occurrence certificates: {A} and {B}. Therefore minimising a truth-functional proof does not identify which route is actually operative.

The second case represents ordinary **nonexclusive overdetermination** at this declared route interface: each source has an active source-only sufficient route. The first is an actually joint route, even though either source can produce the endpoint through a different route when alone. The third gives sole positive actual attribution to A, although B remains a possible alternative and its output-sufficiency certificate is true.

In all cases, keep separate:

- some route that would suffice;
- a route actually operative now;
- all actual positive original support for the particular result;
- independence from additional enabling conditions;
- source-level perfected original efficacy.

The first four can be explicitly represented at the chosen interface. The fifth is not established by renaming any of them.

### What “duplicated complete production” could mean

If it means two actually active **source-only sufficient routes**, the redundant model represents it without contradiction. Neither route has to be the exclusive history of the entire result.

If it means that each of two distinct originals is the **sole exhaustive actual source of every productive requisite in the same respect**, that description is incompatible with the recorded presence of a genuine independent contributor. The `exhaustive_positive_sources` classifier reflects that meaning: with support AB it assigns neither individual.

No independent impossibility theorem is claimed from the classifier's singleton rule. Nor is CND inserted as an axiom and then celebrated as a proof of uniqueness. The unsettled step is why a successful completed original undertaking must satisfy that exhaustive criterion, and whether the represented causal inventory really contains all relevant actual requisites.

## 5. Negative conditions expose another scope boundary

The programme A∧¬B→e has A as its only positive productive root in the A-only profile. It also requires B's absence. Activating B prevents the result.

The adapter records the negative guard without converting it into a positive producer or a created object. Thus sole **positive** attribution does not certify unconditioned power or the absence of an independent agent's required noninterference.

The opposite shortcut is also blocked: a negative condition is not automatically an efficient donation by the entity whose absence is required. Source-grounded analysis must distinguish actual positive provision, logical/temporal conditions, and alien enabling. The source's sovereignty argument makes this distinction consequential, but the finite guard record does not itself settle its normative application.

## 6. A concrete typed tensor transport

This is an actual mathematical representation of the new interface, not an analogy.

Choose finite free bases for primitive route records R, original-root indices G={A,B}, and effect IDs E. Encode the declared incidence as

T[r,g,e]=1 when operative route r attributes positive original root g to effect e, and 0 otherwise.

Thus T lies in the declared route–root–effect tensor space. Route records remain a different type from roots and created effects. The entries are incidence data, not quantities of created matter or physical efficacy.

Pad the direct joint and redundant examples to a common two-slot route basis. For the same effect e:

- joint: route rows (1,1) and (0,0);
- redundant: route rows (1,0) and (0,1).

Contracting the route factor against the all-ones covector yields the same source marginal (1,1) in both cases. The endpoint, numerical birth and that marginal therefore fail to recover whether A and B occur in one actual route.

The full tensor does recover the declared co-membership query:

C(e)=sum over r of T[r,A,e] × T[r,B,e].

It returns 1 for the joint route and 0 for the two separate routes. Permuting the route coordinates preserves this query. General basis changes would require transporting all query structure; no coordinate-free physical law is inferred from the chosen indicator basis.

This is a scoped instance of the inherited H fibre criterion and target-erasing contraction warning. Because the two preimages have the same contracted observation and different C, no decoder of that contracted observation alone recovers C, linear or nonlinear. The complete incidence record is sufficient for this query; no minimal-dimensionality or universal tensor-superiority claim follows.

There is also a positive commuting map. Project a full ledger to effect IDs, states, births and operative routes, erasing only certificates. Appending a certificate induces the identity update on that projection. In contrast, the productive-route extension and route cut require information not retained by the endpoint-only observation. This instantiates the inherited continuation/update contract rather than introducing a new information-preservation theorem.

Absence guards, programme definitions and event states remain companion records; this tensor is not a sufficient representation of every causal or sovereignty question. The proved recovery claim here concerns the declared route co-membership query.

The tensor is only as faithful as its declared actual-route basis. It cannot turn unvalidated proof rows into real causal contributions. The source's real effects-in-a-bearer also do not become extra created entities merely because the tensor has several nonzero entries.

## 9. Exact indirect-history obstruction, with a supplied refinement

The direct adapter's rejection is now backed by an explicit witness rather than a general warning. The retained programme contains A→m by two distinct registered rules (`A-m-left`, `A-m-right`) and a downstream m→e rule. Its deterministic least closure stores two upstream proofs and one downstream proof, because both upstream derivations induce the same flattened `Proof(m-e,{A},∅)`.

The inherited `Run.firing_occurrence_history()` makes a **determinate declared choice**: one tuple per retained proof, hence three tuples. Nothing here makes that function ambiguous or changes its answer. Repeated closure iterations are not interpreted as physical firings.

A strictly richer occurrence contract additionally supplies a unique occurrence ID and one earlier provenance witness per created input. Two explicit acyclic histories are:

- H1: upstream occurrences m-left and m-right; one downstream occurrence e-left citing m-left;
- H2: the same three occurrences plus e-right citing m-right.

A small validator checks rule registration, unique occurrence IDs, satisfied present/absent guards, exact required input-event keys and earlier-input ordering. It then projects each supplied occurrence to its inherited output event and flattened proof and takes the proof sets. **Both histories project exactly to the same complete inherited Run**, including events, all proofs and issued intrinsic acts. Yet H1 has three supplied operative occurrences and H2 has four, with one versus two downstream occurrences. Their created event keys remain only m and e; additional operative occurrences are not inferred to be additional births or intrinsic exercises.

These are extra occurrence semantics compatible with the same retained observation, **not two executions of the inherited deterministic scheduler**. Neither history is established as the actual metaphysical history. In particular, the inherited event-level rule does not warrant our choice of upstream provenance witness, a once-per-event convention or a once-per-witness convention. Supplied occurrence IDs denote distinct occurrences under the richer contract, not duplicate representations of one occurrence; repeating an ID is rejected.

This gives a scoped no-recovery result: no function of the full flattened Run can recover supplied occurrence count over both refinements. The old three-tuple convention remains valid within its own contract; it is not a lossless reconstruction of every richer occurrence ontology.

The witness also supplies a conditional continuation obstruction. Delete m-left and its descendants in the **fixed supplied lineage**, without rescheduling. H1 loses e; H2 retains e-right. The equal flattened inputs therefore do not admit a common update that recovers these different projected results. By contrast, disabling `A-m-left` and rerunning the inherited closure retains e through the remaining m proof. Hereditary deletion and closure recomputation are different operations and are not advertised as intervention equivalents.

This is a further concrete application of the inherited H continuation/fibre criterion, not a new theorem or discovered physics. Positive transport is available once a validated explicit history is supplied: the projection really does reproduce the retained Run for both witnesses. Reverse recovery still needs occurrence and lineage data or a justified canonical convention. The adapter therefore continues to reject indirect input from the Run alone rather than silently choose one of the richer histories.
