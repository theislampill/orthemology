# A source can clarify the force of its own present address

9 October 2026 UTC. Focused T20 continuation. This applies an already retained narrative to actual token force; it does not reopen the source's title, authenticate a new historical event, or create a new theorem.

## Result

**The Barīra case supplies a positive route from a live question about an address to an adequately differentiated response: ask whether the speaker is commanding, receive the speaker's clarification that he is interceding, and respond under that clarified relation.** Standing authority does not disappear; the particular move is identified as intercession. The proposal can retain a humane reason without imposing the obligation attributed to a command.

The indispensable evidence is the speaker's actual answer. A recipient's question alone does not establish obligatory force, and refusal alone cannot prove that no command existed. In this narrative, the negative answer and positive identification of intercession resolve the relevant uncertainty. The source then presents the recipient declining the proposed reconciliation rather than being required to treat every request from that speaker as obligatory.

This gives the preceding command-added-goodness result an exact application guard: the newly available response must be a response to **the act actually performed**, not to a stronger command reconstructed from the speaker's rank or the desirability of an outcome.

The relevant narrative is at **printed559–560/PDF632–633**. Ibn al-Qayyim says the Prophet interceded for the lover's reconciliation and that she declined. He introduces Mughīth and Barīra, describes his distress, and gives this exchange:

- Proposal: «لو راجعتيه».
- Her clarification request: «أتأمرني يا رسول الله؟».
- Answer: «لا، إنما أشفع».
- Her response: «لا حاجة لي به».

The first wording is a proposal, approximately “Would you return to him?” It is not independently established as an unmistakable obligatory command which the next sentence then contradicts. The reply's sense is “No; I am only interceding.” These translations are explanatory glosses, not claims to reproduce another published translation.

The body continues onto560 with the contrast between his love and her aversion. The author then distinguishes inward affection from controllable outward fairness. This surrounding material preserves the reality of reasons and feelings without treating one person's strong desire as the other's obligation.

The apparatus matters. At559 note4 the editor retains «راجعتيه», reports it across the collated copies and in Ibn Mājah, calls that linguistic form weak, and contrasts Bukhārī's «راجعته». ## 3. What each element warrants

**The proposal identifies the practical topic.** The issue is this proposed reconciliation, addressed to this recipient. It is not all future requests or every use of prophetic authority.

**The question isolates force.** Barīra asks whether this is commanding. She does not thereby reject the speaker's authority or deny that a real command could have a different claim on her. A sincere request to know what is being asked can be part of appropriate responsiveness.

**The answer supplies new evidence from the relevant source.** The speaker identifies his own present communicative act and expressly denies the queried commanding force. This is a distinctive informational contribution, not a mere repetition of the proposal. Where the recipient has sufficient warrant for the speaker's identity, intended reference, and truthful competence concerning that act, the clarification can warrant the force appraisal.

**The refusal exhibits the practical difference.** It follows the clarification and is consistent with the source's presentation of intercession. It is not itself the proof of non-obligatoriness: people can also refuse commands. Nor does absence of a command prove that no independent reason could ever favor the proposed act. What the sequence removes is the alleged obligation arising from this address as a command.

The positive conclusion is substantive. One need not derive the force of every address from the speaker's general rank, a grammatical form alone, or an independently reconstructed complete normative code. A warranted, intelligible clarification within the actual exchange can settle the issue at its proper scope. This is ordinary source-sensitive epistemic warrant, not a universal protocol requiring every utterance to contain a self-certifying label.

Nor must the recipient recover every ultimate motive or solve an inverse model of the speaker's preferences. The clarification supplies narrower, action-relevant information about this communicative move. Unknown further purposes do not by themselves defeat an otherwise warranted force appraisal.

## 4. Clarification, revocation, and authority

This sequence should not be modeled as a previously established obligation being erased. The evidence never first established the initial proposal as obligatory. The answer clarifies the current exchange; it is not presented as repeal of a law or surrender of the speaker's standing authority. Likewise, updating a recipient's interpretation does not retrospectively delete the proposal or the question.

Actual reasons remain. Compassion for Mughīth explains intercession in the narrative, and the larger discussion positively values lawful affection and reconciliation. Yet a good reason to propose or support an outcome is different from a sufficient right to compel this recipient to choose it. The source can sincerely recommend an outcome while leaving refusal available.

The existing due-response appraisal therefore has differentiated content. Taking an intercession seriously, asking what is intended, and declining it need not amount to defying a command. Conversely, one cannot evade a separately warranted genuine command merely by relabeling it as advice. The actual source act, not the interpreter's convenient label, governs that distinction.

For transport to the broader source-owned-attestation account, identity/authentication, content, force, recipient scope, currentness, and warrant remain separate. A speaker's bare claim “I am authoritative” does not establish its own provenance or jurisdiction. A reliable explanation of an already identified utterance's force does different work. In the prophetic example, no inference to universal inerrancy in every non-delivery act is needed or newly claimed; any general testimony principle must have the scope appropriate to the particular clarification.
