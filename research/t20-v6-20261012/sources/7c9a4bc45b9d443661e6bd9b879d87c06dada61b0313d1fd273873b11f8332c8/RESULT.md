## Result

**An unresolved point prior need not block a warranted conditional evidential conclusion.** Once the relevant conditional likelihoods are justified, their ratio determines the direction of a binary Bayesian update for every nondegenerate prior. A sufficiently bounded prior range can also establish a posterior-threshold conclusion without selecting a point prior. When those bounds are insufficient, the exact remaining dependence can be stated rather than suppressing the evidential question.

This makes a concrete correction to the frozen DAEE M2 text. Its legitimate instruction is to expose an unexamined prior when the interlocutor's conclusion depends on it. Its stronger assertion that the specified evidential arguments can do their work only from a near-zero prior is not a general consequence of Bayesian inference, even if “their work” means reaching a low posterior rather than merely lowering it. The required prior level depends jointly on the likelihood ratio and the claimed threshold.

The finding is deliberately narrower than a general indictment of M2. Its own key move already acknowledges directional lowering, its header scopes the prerequisite to an unexamined dependency, and its release rules permit partial acknowledgment without agreement on a numerical prior. ## 3 Direction and threshold require different premises

Let H be one event in a fixed exhaustive binary partition {H, not-H}; B is the retained background, and E the new evidence. Assume one specified probability model with

    p = P(H | B),                         0 < p < 1,
    a = P(E | H,B) > 0,
    b = P(E | not-H,B) > 0,
    ell = a/b,
    q = P(H | E,B) = phi(p,ell) = p ell / (1-p+p ell).

The denominator is positive. No independence of E and B is assumed. The likelihoods already condition on B, so existing evidence is not silently used twice. These are represented uncertainty alternatives, not an assertion that an atheistic world is metaphysically possible or that an accepted necessity proof fails. Neither H nor its complement is assigned a theological interpretation by the examples.

### Direction

Direct subtraction gives

    q-p = p(1-p)(ell-1) / (1-p+p ell).

Consequently ell<1 gives q<p for every interior p; ell=1 gives q=p; ell>1 gives q>p. This is directional evidence relative to the specified comparison and background. It does not establish how low q is, what one should do, or whether a person's belief loses warrant. An exact point prior is unnecessary for this restricted conclusion.

### A claimed posterior threshold

For 0<tau<1, multiplying positive denominators gives the exact equivalences

    q < tau
    iff ell < tau(1-p) / ((1-tau)p)
    iff p < tau / (tau + ell(1-tau)).

Replacing every strict inequality by a weak one gives the boundary version. The threshold is a declared target of this calculation, not a numerical definition of knowledge or a licence for an action.

For any interior prior p and any positive threshold tau, a sufficiently small positive ell satisfies the first bound. Such a ratio is realizable by a valid binary channel: take b=1/2 and a=ell/2 when ell<=1; take a=1/2 and b=1/(2 ell) when ell>1. Complete each row with the complementary outcome. Thus high antecedent probability is not a universal mathematical immunity to strong evidence. This is a possible-channel construction, not source calibration.

Conversely, for any fixed finite positive ell, phi(p,ell) tends to 1 as p tends to 1 and to 0 as p tends to 0. No such ell forces a nontrivial absolute threshold uniformly over every interior prior. This is where the prior probe can genuinely matter: a directional argument does not by itself establish an unrestricted low-posterior conclusion.

## 4 A positive rule with an unresolved prior

Suppose the likelihood ratio ell is fixed and separately justified, while the justified mathematical prior range is [l,u], with 0<l<=u<1. Differentiation, or direct cross-multiplication, shows

    d phi(p,ell)/dp = ell / (1-p+p ell)^2 > 0.

Hence the exact posterior range is

    [phi(l,ell), phi(u,ell)].

A strict low-threshold conclusion holds throughout the closed range exactly when phi(u,ell)<tau. A strict high-threshold conclusion holds throughout exactly when phi(l,ell)>tau. If the range straddles the threshold, disclose that dependence. One can still establish direction when ell is on a fixed side of one. The positive sufficient conditions are actual bounds and a competent inference from them, not the word “robust” or a fabricated label of soundness.

If likelihood uncertainty is also admitted, phi is increasing in ell. A justified rectangle [l,u] × [r,s] therefore gives exact extrema phi(l,r) and phi(u,s). If background constraints couple p and ell, a bounding rectangle gives conservative bounds only; unattainable corner combinations need not give sharp bounds. This report does not supply prior or likelihood bounds for a theological proposition.

## 5 Executed exact controls

All examples below use synthetic binary events. Their fractions were checked directly from valid channel probabilities, not merely inserted into an unconstrained weight vector.

1. **High prior and a low posterior.** p=99/100, a=1/2000 and b=1/2 give ell=1/1000 and q=99/1099, about 0.09008. The observed event has positive probability 1099/200000. This refutes a general near-zero-prior necessity even for the low threshold 1/10. It says nothing about an actual theological likelihood.
2. **Counterevidence with a high remaining posterior.** p=99/100 and ell=1/2 give q=99/101. The update lowers p while leaving q above 9/10. Direction and an overall defeat claim differ.
3. **A low posterior despite supporting evidence.** p=1/100 and ell=2 give q=2/101. Its being low does not show that E was evidence against H: q increased.
4. **A range suffices.** For every p in [9/10,99/100], ell=1/1000 gives q in [9/1009,99/1099], entirely below 1/10. No point prior is needed for that conditional bound. In contrast, ell=1/2 on the same prior range gives [9/11,99/101], entirely above 4/5 although the evidence is adverse throughout.
5. **A genuine unresolved dependency.** For p in [1/10,9/10] and ell=1/2, q spans [1/19,9/11]. The threshold 1/2 cannot be settled across this range, though downward direction can.

The verifier also checks the strict and weak threshold equivalences over an exact rational grid, neutral evidence, the complement branches, coupled-parameter bounds, support cases, and input custody. These controls corroborate the algebra; they do not prove universal claims by finite enumeration.

## 6 The correction operation and its limits

A source-faithful replacement principle is:

> Probe undeclared prior dependence when it matters to the conclusion being claimed. Separate the direction of evidence from a posterior threshold and from conclusions uniform over justified prior ranges. Keep unresolved background and likelihood assumptions explicit while proceeding with substantive assessment that does not depend on settling them.

Operationally, first identify H, B, E and the claimant's intended conclusion. For a directional claim, engage the likelihood comparison and its grounds. For a low-posterior claim, assess prior and likelihood jointly, using justified ranges when a point value is unavailable. For a broader defeat claim, compare the alleged counterevidence with the actual substantive antecedent grounds; a numerical threshold supplies no substitute for that appraisal. Check the observation, interpretation and dependencies appropriate to the claim. Use the prior probe where the needed conclusion remains prior-sensitive. Release the result that the actual premises establish; carry the other dependency forward. Preserve M2's deductive/pastoral exclusions, single-pass discipline and partial-acknowledgment release. This is proposed wording and research guidance, not an installed route change.

Three boundaries are essential:

- **No blanket prior-free Bayes factor.** Independence of direction from the top-level p assumes fixed conditional likelihoods. Composite hypotheses can require nuisance-parameter distributions or other model commitments to obtain those likelihoods. These may themselves be disputed. Replacing an unexamined p by an unexamined ell is no correction.
- **No automatic double-counting accusation.** M2 line 63 allows hiddenness to be offered as a ground for a low prior. That can designate earlier background B distinct from new evidence E. Double counting occurs only if the same information is reused as if new in the actual model. Track the conditioning history rather than infer duplication from a topic label.
- **No support repair by assertion.** At p=0 or p=1, ordinary conditioning preserves the exclusion whenever the event has positive probability. If antecedent warrant is represented by p=1 in an idealized model, finite ordinary conditioning preserves it; this endpoint does not rescue the blanket near-zero claim about nondegenerate arguments. If an event has zero total probability, its posterior is undefined. With an interior prior, a=0<b gives q=0 and b=0<a gives q=1; a=b=0 is not a conditioning event. Revising a model that excluded the observed event requires new grounds, as the earlier audit report already establishes.

DAEE's sound-reason account sections 1–2 allows competent inference from known premises and distinguishes first-order warrant from reflective self-certification. The present mathematical conditional can accordingly be a legitimate inferential result when its premises are known and actually used. It does not require a complete faculty certification or determine an actual subject's basing. Orthemology's semantic-operator contract independently keeps route adequacy non-factive and distinct from truth linkage. A clearer tactic therefore does not by itself prove human uptake or restoration.
