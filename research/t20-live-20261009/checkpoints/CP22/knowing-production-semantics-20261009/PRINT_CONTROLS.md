# Exact printed controls

These are selected transcriptions from visually inspected retained page images. They are not a diplomatic transcription of every page or a manuscript collation. The source works are premodern; modern apparatus is identified separately.

## Darʾ 10:112 / PDF112

Ibn Taymiyya's reply after al-Ṭūsī:

«فيقال: علمه بذاته لا يمكن أن يكون فعلياً، وإنما يكون فعلياً علمه بخلقه. فإن علمه له تأثير في فعل خلقه، وليس له تأثير في وجود نفسه».

«وبينا أن العلم نوعان: علم العالم بما يريد أن يفعله، فهذا علم فعلي، هو شرط في وجود المعلوم، إذ وجود المعلوم بدونه ممتنع».

The following paragraph explicitly distinguishes this from identifying knowledge alone with creation without power and will. Both predicates, operative role and intended activity, are genuinely in the source. Do not retain the latter in an antecedent while claiming to have inferred it from the former alone.

The problematic Qadariyya clause in electronic L637 lacks a negation in the print scan as well. The closing `علمياً انفعالياً` wording also remains problematic in print. Neither is silently corrected or used as a doctrinal premise. The unambiguous preceding distinction does not depend on either repair.

## Darʾ 10:113 / PDF113

The author's own concession to the choice-denying interlocutor begins:

«وأما من لم يسلم أنه يفعل باختياره، فهذا القول باطل من جهة نفيه لاختياره، لا من جهة أن كونه فاعلاً يوجب العلم بالمفعول».

He goes on to retain the self-knowledge-to-effects inference on that stipulated productive account. The text therefore does not simply define the knowledge premise as already admitting choice.

## Darʾ 10:138 / PDF138

Within al-Ghazālī's quoted discussion, the introduced response begins:

«فإن قيل: بين الأمرين فرق، وهو أن صدور الكل عن ذاته بسبب علمه بالكل، فتمثل النظام الكلي هو سبب فيضان الكل».

This is the primary reported knowing-production rival. It is followed by a claim that the whole would not exist from the source without its knowledge. The passage itself thus prevents limiting all rivals here to extraneously aware natural producers. Detailed historical attribution beyond this reported context has not been independently verified against a separate Ibn Sīnā edition in this operation.

## Darʾ 10:142–143 / PDF142–143

The cognitive-determination argument is Ibn Rushd quoted, continuing across both pages. The self-identifying voice transition to Ibn Taymiyya is the paragraph beginning `قلت: ابن رشد يتعصب للفلاسفة` on143. The prior common attribution must not erase this boundary. The wording concerning an additional attribute, the knowledge of opposites, and willing necessarily through knowledge is present in print.

## Darʾ 10:144–145 / PDF144–145

Printed144 second paragraph:

«فإذا قدر فعل ليس بإرادي، لم يلزم كون الفاعل عالماً».

The next paragraph:

«لأن الفعل الإرادي مستلزم للعلم، وغير الإرادي مستلزم لنفيه».

Printed145 begins:

«وذكروا أن الفعل الذي ليس بإرادي مستلزم لنفي العلم».

And later on145:

«وكل فعل ليس بإرادة فلا علم معه».

All four formulations are plainly visible. The shift from absence of entailment to entailment of absence is therefore in this printed edition; it cannot responsibly be blamed only on electronic extraction. The implication using the stronger exclusion is valid if that exclusion is independently warranted for the intended productive domain. The weaker sentence does not itself furnish that warrant.

The editor's lone144 footnote concerns `لازم` versus `لازما`, not an emendation of this transition. It supplies no manuscript-level resolution of the substantive logical question.

## Darʾ 10:146–147 / PDF146–147

Printed146 expressly locates the discussion after establishing self-knowledge and directs the separate establishment elsewhere.

Printed147 reads:

«أحدهما: أن الشمس ليست علة تامة فاعلة للنور، بل هي شرط في فيضانه».

Electronic dar017L808 omits `ليست`. The scan settles that local omission relative to this edition. The statement that the sun is a dependent condition rather than the complete original producer can now be used as the author's actual response. Its accompanying premodern account of light is not treated as independently verified physics.

## Asfahani721–722 / PDF821–822

The relevant text appears in the extended apparatus note6, continued from721 onto722. The editor introduces it as an addition unique to witness `ط`, whose own pages are294–296. It distinguishes knowledge of an object not acted upon by the knower from knowledge that conditions the knower's activity. The stable central denial is:

«ولكن ليس مجرد العلم موجباً لوجود المعلوم بدون المشيئة والقدرة».

The passage then reports and rejects identifying knowledge, power and will with one another and the subject. Lacunae and proposed corrections are explicitly printed in this supplementary witness. No reconstruction of the damaged end is needed for the distinction.

## Asfahani403–405,408 / PDF503–505,508

403–405 distinguish necessity through an actually qualified bearer with power and will from a bare essence necessitating its effects eternally. Full power and decisive will entail the effect. 408 distinguishes capacity and intrinsic possibility from actual willing under wisdom. This directly corroborates the inherited result that necessity of completed own-reason determination is not external coercion or absence of voluntary activity.

The extended footnote on404–405 quotes al-Rāzī and his alternatives; it is not all Ibn Taymiyya's endorsed position. The authorial main body and apparatus remain separate in the analysis.
