# Wise reasons and the determination of an own act

10 October 2026 UTC. Bounded T20 source-and-inference continuation. Only this separate evidence directory is written. No repository change, integration, closure, new metaphysical model, or reopening of the earned sourcehood, necessity, Knowledge, Will, Wisdom or practical-perfection warrants.

## Result

**The sources support an asymmetric practical explanation with a stronger own-act consequence than mere guidance: apprehended evaluative features can explain fitting love or aversion; God's love for His own act entails its occurrence through His willing and choice, and His aversion excludes its occurrence. But approval of a creaturely action, or of a good considered in one respect, cannot be substituted for that own-act antecedent.**

The content need not be the empty description “whatever was selected.” An executed source application identifies one original-scope exclusion: **imputing one person's wrongdoing as another person's guilt, where the latter did not acquire or participate in it, is excluded as a divine punitive determination.** Its discriminating content is the act–recipient–culpability relation, specified independently of whether punishment occurs. This does not exclude every suffering of a nonculpable person or every consequence of another person's action.

The increment is a premise-level application and a corrected transport, not discovery of these source doctrines. The love/own-act relation was already recorded in the inherited Miftāḥ notes. The new combination shows both how a real reason can restrict an original determination and exactly why moving from subordinate approval to own productive love can fail. It does not select the whole actual history, individuate an intrinsic volition-token, or explain the particular content of every newly originating representation.

## 2 The source supplies an explanatory direction, not just new labels

### Perfection and fitting love

At Miftāḥ 2:969–970, Ibn al-Qayyim replies to an opponent's distinction among perfection, suitability, and praise or punishment. He argues that known perfection is loved and defect disliked, and that divine love of excellent acts is proportionate to their excellence. At 970 he explicitly explains the contrasted love and aversion by the perfection of the former object and defect of the latter. This is his reply, not merely the opponent's taxonomy.

The concluding 971 also reasons from being loved to perfection. That does not cancel the explanatory direction: a warranted attitude can be evidence of its fitting object, even when the object's relevant character explains why the attitude is fitting. **Reciprocal evidential entailment does not establish reciprocal efficient production or erase every order of explanation.** The text supports that distinction through its explicit explanatory wording, not merely through our choosing one direction of a biconditional.

Nevertheless, “perfection” alone can remain an empty placeholder. The surrounding source supplies an independent-content control. At 872–876, the contrasts include voluntary acquisition of property and coercive taking, and just treatment and injustice. The argument rejects “prohibited because prohibited” as self-explanation. At 1114–1119 it says cognition apprehends real features and their consequences; it does not make them true merely by representing them. At 1117–1118 it expressly permits different relevant features in the same act and discusses their relative weight.

These passages do not prove that every evaluative assertion in the book is correct. They establish the author's intended explanatory structure and provide content beyond the fact of selection. The warranted application must still identify which feature matters, to whom, under what conditions, and with what weight. The inherited normative appraisal supplies substantive evaluative warrant; this report does not claim to derive normativity from norm-free premises.

Sources: the [received edited Miftāḥ witness](https://ibnelqayem.com/ar/media/download/86), with retained page-marked extracts in `sources/`; [electronic parallel for the second-volume discussion](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/mftah-dar-alsaadt-003.html). The edited witness controls the wording and apparatus here.

### The relevant love is not always the same intentional relation

At 967–968 the source affirms both obedience loved although not actualized and wrongdoing actualized although disapproved. But this is not the final word on the efficacy of divine love. At 1138–1140 it explicitly distinguishes:

1. God's love for or aversion to **His own act**.
2. God's love for or aversion to **an act performed by a creature**, although created within His provision.

The first is said to entail occurrence or nonoccurrence through God's willing and choice. The second does not. At 1139 the source states the missing link: loving the creature's obedience does not mean loving the own act of providing the particular enabling assistance. If that own act were loved, the corresponding occurrence would follow. The author invokes more-loved ends to explain why the two assessments can differ.

The distinction is stronger than an arbitrary change of labels. Creaturely obedience, divine enabling, the resulting creaturely performance, and the broader intended good are different intentional objects and relations. Their coordination must be warranted; it is not given by using “love” for each.

There is also a respect distinction. Shifāʾ e180 distinguishes assessing the act in itself from assessing what it leads to. These are not exactly the same distinction as own act versus creaturely act. A description may correctly attribute a good to a creature's action while omitting the further consequences relevant to producing it here. Conversely, the own act of enabling an action is not numerically the creature's performance. Both distinctions must be retained.

Therefore the correct result is not “perfect love never suffices for action.” It is: **the sources affirm the efficacy of own-act love in their defended sense, but a subordinate or respect-specific approval does not establish that antecedent.** No claim is made that a hypothetical fully specified own-act love could remain present while its occurrence fails.

## 3 The qualified asymmetric argument

The strongest reconstruction permitted by these passages is:

1. An intended object or proposed action has relevant features and relations describable independently of the claim that it is chosen.
2. The source's knowledge apprehends those features. Their fitting practical significance supports love or aversion at the relevant scope.
3. For the determination at issue, one must warrant the move to love or aversion of **this own act in the relevant circumstances**, rather than stop with a loved subordinate object or a general kind of good.
4. In the source's own-act account, that practical determination is efficacious through the same original's willing and choice.

Step 2 supplies a practical explanation; step 4 preserves productive agency. Neither says an external property first makes God exist, donates an essential attribute, or efficiently manufactures His act. Shifāʾ e207 expressly denies that intrinsic wisdom is a separately supplying other. Majmūʿ 16:304–305 places the relevant intended features in knowledge and conception before the external object exists, while retaining the originating status of the cognitive-volitional representation.

Shifāʾ e189 calls the final cause a cause of the efficient cause. E190 immediately distinguishes the wisdom **for which** an act is done from causes **through which** it is done; e209 describes a known fitting end's role in willing. These passages permit genuine explanatory dependence without making an end a separately subsisting efficient maker of the divine bearer. Nor should guidance be made causally idle: the reason has an operative practical role, although it is not thereby the entire efficient producer.

This structure leaves open how particular cognitive-volitional occurrences are individuated and how their content is originated. It does not erase the representation-dependency result. Its positive contribution is that **an independently warranted practical determination of an own act is not merely a decorative representation of an already selected outcome**. The source gives it a determinate consequence through actual voluntary efficacy. The remaining burden is warranting the antecedent at the claimed scope.

## 4 Executed original-scope exclusion

### The source's actual criterion

Miftāḥ 1123–1135 compares three positions on divine obligation and injustice. The author endorses the third account, not the initial reported positions. At 1128–1131 he specifies the injustice denied of God: another person's wrongdoing is not loaded onto the recipient, and the recipient is not punished for what the recipient did not acquire or participate in. The discussion grounds punitive treatment in the person's own culpability and distinguishes just restraint from mere inability to act.

The criterion is more informative than “God does not do what God dislikes.” It specifies an act, a recipient, and a purported culpability basis. At 1133–1134 the author also rejects bare ownership and capacity as the explanation of why punitive treatment would be just. At 1138–1141 the own-act love/aversion account is then applied in the continuous discussion of self-imposed obligation and prohibition.

The sentence about cancelling good deeds at 1129 has the inherited conditional difficulty. This application does not use that sentence. It rests on the clear transferred-guilt and unacquired-deed clauses, corroborated by 1130–1131. No fresh adjudication of all the surrounding theological or historical claims is made.

### The premise-level test

Consider a proposed description T of divine punitive treatment. Its stipulated content is narrowly fixed:

- A performed wrongdoing d.
- B did not perform, acquire, or culpably participate in d.
- T treats d as B's culpable deed and punishes B **on that basis alone**.

These are analytical descriptions used to test an attribution, not a claim that a complete metaphysically possible world instantiates T. They do not identify any contemporary individual or event.

The inference is:

1. T satisfies the source's independently stated transferred-guilt criterion. The act/recipient/basis can be characterized without saying whether T occurs or whether it is loved.
2. The endorsed Miftāḥ account classifies that own punitive treatment as injustice excluded by divine justice and wisdom, not as an otherwise neutral act made wrong solely by a later prohibition.
3. The same account denies the original's willing and performance of that injustice; its own-act aversion relation at 1138–1140 explains why this is compatible with standing power and voluntary agency.
4. Consequently T cannot be admitted as an eligible wise divine determination under those same premises. In particular, the bare claims “the original has power over B,” “B belongs to the original,” or “T was supposedly chosen” do not supply an adequate reason reversing that classification.

This is an actual content-sensitive exclusion. It is **prior in the offered explanation**, not necessarily temporally prior: the culpability mismatch explains the relevant defect, which supports the own-act aversion and nonoccurrence. It is not the nonoccurrence alone that gives T its content or makes it defective. The criterion therefore exhibits an independently characterizable reason rather than a q-specific representation that merely repeats the selected result.

### Sensitivity and limits

If B did culpably participate, the displayed no-participation premise is no longer true. The transferred-guilt argument then no longer decides that case; the source's other conditions for just punishment would still require checking. If B merely suffers a nonpunitive worldly consequence of A's action, that is a different description and also does not meet T. Altering either element is a change of target, not a refutation of the fixed inference.

No claim is made that innocence eliminates every suffering, that another person's conduct can have no consequences for B, or that the absence of this one defect proves a proposed punishment justified. Likewise, T's exclusion does not positively select one unique alternative, timing, degree of recompense, or complete history. It constrains the content of original action at one definite respect.

The example uses source-owned evaluative and disclosure premises. It neither newly proves the source's veracity nor independently derives all of its normative standards. It is a warranted application within the inherited appraisal, just as the ship/wall analysis uses disclosed circumstances without purporting to authenticate its own framework afresh.

## 5 The remaining selector obstruction

The strongest attempted shortcut says: a particular good is known as perfect; perfect love follows; therefore the particular productive act is now explained. There are two different failures or successes depending on its wording.

**Subordinate-object version.** If the love concerns a creature's obedience or a good in one respect, the move to this own act of producing or enabling it is invalid without further premises. Miftāḥ 1139 supplies the exact counter-typing. This restriction does not refute the own-act efficacy thesis.

**Own-act version.** If the premise is that this fully relevant own act is actually loved, the source permits the occurrence conclusion through willing and choice. But if the only reason for that premise is that the act occurred, the derivation is retrospective certification, not an independent explanation of its particular content. One still needs the relevant practical feature, comparative circumstance, commitment or other differentiator and its actual role. Its being an own act or an intrinsic representation does not manufacture that warrant.

Shifāʾ e180–181 is a useful adversarial control. The source says the own creation is better than omission and uses a broad claim about unrealized good; it immediately rejects an unqualified identification of every consequence of existence with good and every consequence of absence with evil. Locally costly effects can accompany greater goods. Reading the broad sentence as “every independently good possibility must be jointly realized” ignores the preceding respect distinction and the following qualifications. It also would not independently identify one uniquely best total history.

The transferred-guilt application discharges the practical-content obligation for one exclusion. It does not provide the positive content and origin of every other determination. The local ship/wall application discharges a different, means-level obligation. Combining their accomplishments does not make them an ultimate history selector. No full-reason tie, numerical score, or primitive tie-breaking act is introduced.

## 6 What this adds and what it does not

The source ingredients were inherited. Fresh credit is confined to:

1. **The corrected efficacy test:** loved-but-unrealized creaturely action cannot refute efficacy of love for the original's own act. The conclusion “love is insufficient” needs its object and respect specified.
2. **The qualified explanatory composition:** independently characterized evaluative content can ground an own practical determination which is efficacious through willing. Reasons are neither external donors nor necessarily idle spectators; explanatory asymmetry is not identical to efficient production or one-way evidential implication.
3. **The executed content discriminator:** transferred guilt as guilt is an excluded original punitive determination, with explicit premises and failure-of-application conditions. Bare source power or an occurred-choice declaration cannot repair its rationale.

No new first-source reading, proof of unique global selection, revised foundational verdict, new volition inventory, or universal theory of all apparent evil is claimed. An additional generic source campaign would duplicate the present ingredients. A stronger positive particular explanation would require a concrete own-act content and independently warranted grounds at that scope, rather than a new name for the selected q.
