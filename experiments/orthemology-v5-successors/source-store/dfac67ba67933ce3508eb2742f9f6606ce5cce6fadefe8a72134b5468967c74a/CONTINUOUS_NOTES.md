## body 3–20 (continuous-first-pass)

## body 48–62 (continuous-first-pass)

## body 63–77 (continuous-first-pass)

63 expressly separates possibility from evidential/hikma warrant: imagined modes of Satan's re-entry do not establish what happened. 64–66 repeated descent is explained by different associated predicates (punishment and consoling guidance), followed by devotional reflection and poetry about humility. 67–68 is a precise scope correction: already-existing everlasting heaven does not imply identity with Adam's garden; the converse of a sectarian implication was invalidly assumed. 69–76 remaining earthly-side evidence invokes Adam's lifespan, material, earthward purpose and natural cosmology; the claim that above-sphere reality cannot corrupt is historical premise, not accepted science. 77 transitions to the opposing Ibn Taymiyya response, identified by editor as Majmu 4:347–349; note says Nubuwwat supports another view and final chronology is unknown. Thus a single master's reported response must not erase dissent or be called a settled corpus verdict. Transmitted lifespans/hadith variants remain attributed.

## body 78–92 (continuous-first-pass)

78–81 continues quoted Ibn Taymiyya heavenly-garden response; 79 long geographic/editorial digression about Sar(a)/Shara and modern Salibi is modern apparatus, not medieval science. 81–86 opposing reply rejects guilt by association, demands a valid consensus report, distinguishes location from identity, and resists reducing a rival's garden to an ordinary orchard. 86–87 Ibn al-Qayyim closes without adjudication, requesting knowledgeable addition and intellectual humility. 88–89 shifts to guidance covenant and grammatical conditional clauses with illustrative legal enactments. 90–91 is central: distinguishes two-way necessary/sufficient connection from one-way human→animal inference; alternative causes can ground one ruling by TYPE, but specific ruling by sale or inheritance is individuated and tied to that causal source. Philosophical transfer must assess that rationale rather than claim an independently demonstrated universal. 92 distinguishes absence of ruinous fear from absence of any fearful experience. Source strengths include serious counterargument, causal plurality at type level, and source-specific token account; none automatically establishes every-original total provision.

## body 93–107 (continuous-first-pass)

93–100 extends guidance/bliss across present and afterlife, distinguishes inward religious joy from external luxury and interprets prophetic sustained fasting via spiritual nourishment. 96 martial self-sacrifice and violent devotional exemplars are historical rhetoric, not advice or independently verified virtue. 100 sectarian classifications remain author's polemics. 101–107 argues righteous jinn also enter heaven by common promises and shared moral conditions; 106 expressly allows gratuitous blessings, created inhabitants without prior work and increases through others' prayers. ## body 108–122 (continuous-first-pass)

## body 213–227 (continuous-first-pass)

## body 228–242 (continuous-first-pass)

228–230 acceptable action requires sincerity and correctness; undirected effort can cause more harm, and knowledge/working must not displace each other. 231–235 disaggregates knowing good, willing it, power to do it and removal of impediments; renewal of guidance is not mere repetition. 234–235 four levels: natural orientation, explanatory guidance (necessary condition not necessitating result), efficacious divine aid, final destination. 236–239 several independent dimensions of excellence (benefit, need, loss, pleasure, ends, intrinsic worth), with God as highest object; 238 complete cause-knowledge entails effect-knowledge only when the cause is known as complete. 240 religious violence is retained as historical polemic, not justified instruction. 241–242 crucial distinction: productive knowledge precedes chosen effects; receptive knowledge follows independent objects and produces none.
