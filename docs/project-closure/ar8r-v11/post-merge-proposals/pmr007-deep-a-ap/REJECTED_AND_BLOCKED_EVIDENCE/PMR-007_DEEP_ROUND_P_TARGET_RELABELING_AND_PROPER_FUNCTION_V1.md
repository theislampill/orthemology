## 3. TRPF-1 — target-relabeling symmetry

Let `K` be an intended model class closed under two expansions `M+` and `M-` of one neutral base `B` such that:

```text
M+ and M- have exactly the same B;
TL(M+) = true;
TL(M-) = false.
```

Let `G` be any candidate function-status predicate that factors through the neutral base:

\[
G(M)=g(B_M).
\]

Then

\[
G(M^+)=G(M^-).
\]

Therefore no such `G` can be extensionally equivalent, over `K`, to a truth-linked epistemic-proper-function predicate whose value differs between `M+` and `M-`.

### Proof

`M+` and `M-` have identical neutral reducts. Since `G` factors through that reduct, both receive `g(B)`. By construction the truth-linked target predicate differs. Hence `G` neither identifies nor entails the truth-linked predicate over a model class containing the pair. This is a symmetry/factorization application, not a new general theorem.

## 5. Trajectory evidence

Adding the entire neutral learning trace `Tr` to the base does not alter TRPF-1 when the two interpretation expansions preserve that trace. Thus trajectory evidence can discriminate mechanisms only where candidate mechanisms predict different trajectories. It cannot by itself convert task-relative success into objective epistemic proper function.

The Sun et al. OSM study is used only as a source-level example of the first point: multiple models can reach decorrelated final states, while the measured order of decorrelation discriminates the CSCG from the other tested models. It does not test objective fittingness, epistemic proper function, divine design, fiṭrah, or Wisdom.
