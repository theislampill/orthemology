# Objective goodness: source-bound duplicate-support decision

10 October 2026 UTC. Separate T20 research note. No protected edit, integration, implementation change, or closure. This records additional primary support for an earned result, not discovery of a new normative distinction.

## Result

The received Intiṣār li-ahl al-athar / Naqḍ al-manṭiq at printed 271–274 supports basic rational appraisal of the goodness of knowledge, truthfulness and justice. It supplies no newly missing distinction in the inspected Orthemology/DAEE interface. Absence of a universal end-selector here does not withdraw the positive goodness claims actually warranted.

At 271–272 the author distinguishes accepting a proposition because it is known from accepting it because it is popular or conceded; those classifications can overlap. At 272–273 he defends knowledge/justice versus ignorance/injustice as basic rational appraisals and includes truthfulness in the positive explanation. At 273 the goodness of these actions is explained through their attainment of ends desired for themselves. At 274 propositional ḥaqq (what is established) is distinguished from practical ḥaqq (benefit or end-attainment).

Two exact mapping guards:

1. Ṣidq in this list is truthfulness. It must not silently become the entire formal relation of epistemic truth-linkage.
2. The sentence says knowledge, truthfulness and justice attain ends sought for themselves. It does not expressly identify each named good as itself a terminal end. This qualifies the mapping, not the author's affirmative goodness appraisal.

## Strongest rival and disposition

The local goodness explanation has means/end structure. That does not license attributing to the author the stronger thesis that every actual terminal preference fixes genuine goodness: the inherited developed argument expressly admits corrupt ultimate intentions and fitting goods whose practical significance survives unwillingness. Conversely, merely saying an end is desired for itself does not independently establish that end's eligibility. The earned result already preserves basic rational goodness and substantive correction of desire together.

Stop this candidate as duplicated support. No new formal theorem, no actual DAEE defect, and no counterexample or implementation repair follows. The later continuation, if pursued, is a distinct substantive inquiry into the full final-cause argument beginning at Jāmiʿ VI192–193; this note makes no result claim about that continuation.
