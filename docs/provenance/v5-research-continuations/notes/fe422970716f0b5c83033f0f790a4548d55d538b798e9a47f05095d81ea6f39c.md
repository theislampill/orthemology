## Statement

Fix 0<ε≤1/4 and any fixed independent probability seed law ν. At each positive n use the same deterministic report

q_n=1/2+ε (number of true receipts among the first n)/n.

For every fixed α∈(0,1), its literal total-failure count C satisfies

E_α[C] ≤ 32/[ε² α(1−α)] < ∞.

Consequently, for every probability prior μ concentrated on (0,1), this one common report family has C<∞ almost surely under the exact experiment `(μ×ν)×innovationLaw`. No integrability assumption on μ's inverse-variance moment is needed for this almost-sure conclusion.

For the two side priors and every proper mixture μ_θ in the reviewed nonconvexity construction, the empirical policy therefore has finitely many failures almost surely. Yet for every 0<θ<1 its expectation is infinite, and no other admissible measurable policy has finite mixture expectation. This is an existence-level separation between common almost-sure eventual success and common finite expected count in this countably supported parameter-prior interface.

## 1. Source-bound fixed-parameter estimate

`EmpiricalGeometry.empiricalReport` is exactly q_n above. Its output lies in [1/2,1/2+ε]⊆[0,1] for n≥1, so it is coherent. It ignores ν and is measurable. The inherited literal-geometry and Chernoff argument gives

P_α(failure at n) ≤ 2 exp(−n ε² α(1−α)/16).

Writing c=ε²α(1−α)/16>0, the positive-time series is

Σ_(n≥1) 2 exp(−nc)=2/(exp(c)−1)≤2/c,

using exp(c)−1≥c. Tonelli for the nonnegative indicator series gives the stated 32/[ε²α(1−α)] bound. This is exactly the content of `ExponentialSeries.summed_error_bound` together with the actual-process finite-word transport.

A second source route applies `EndpointProcess.empirical_actual_expectation_bound` to μ=δα. The restricted inverse-variance moment of δα is exactly 1/[α(1−α)]. The reviewer's `IndependentNonconvexityProbe.empirical_fixed_parameter_bound` kernel-checks this specialization in the actual experiment, and `empirical_fixed_parameter_finite` checks its strict finiteness. These prove a bound for an actual nonnegative path count, not merely a finite risk array.

## 2. Almost-sure lifting without the invalid moment inference

Finite nonnegative expectation implies P_α(C=∞)=0 for every α∈(0,1). For example, Markov's inequality gives P_α(C≥m)≤E_α[C]/m; the events decrease to {C=∞}, whose probability is therefore zero.

The count is jointly measurable by `EndpointProcess.totalFailures_measurable`. Thus {C=∞} is a measurable event of (α,z,u), and its sections and iterated integrals are legitimate. Under the product experiment, nonnegative Fubini gives

P_μ(C=∞)=∫ P_α(C=∞) μ(dα)=0.

This integrates a zero event probability at every interior parameter. It does not integrate the potentially nonintegrable numerical upper bound 32/[ε²α(1−α)] and then pretend that an infinite upper bound proves finite expectation. Pointwise conditional finiteness and prior-averaged finite expectation are different statements.

The exceptional innovation sets are allowed to depend on α. A single parameter-independent null innovation set is unnecessary: joint measurability and Fubini suffice. The independent seed causes no issue because the empirical report ignores it; it also fits the exact same seeded class as the negative theorem.

Since C is the sum of zero-one failures over the positive integers, C<∞ is equivalent to only finitely many failures, hence eventual absence of failures. This does not bound the expected time of the last failure or provide a uniform deterministic stabilization deadline.

## 3. Application and finite-hidden-family contrast

Both μ^− and μ^+ are concentrated on positive points at most 1/2. Their proper mixtures are likewise interior-supported, so the common empirical witness is almost-surely successful under all of them. The accepted universal mixture theorem applied to this witness simultaneously gives E_(μθ)[C]=∞ at every interior θ.

This does not contradict the finite observed-state hidden-model coBüchi/finite-expectation existence equivalence established for the separate controller family. That result concerns a finite set of fixed Markov models in its specified interface and a specially constructed common controller. It never asserted that every almost-surely successful policy has finite expected count.

Here each side prior contains infinitely many latent Bernoulli parameters approaching zero. Although the menu has two composite priors, it is not a finite set of known fixed Bernoulli transition kernels. The infinitely many small signal scales are precisely what makes the per-component lifetime lower bound sum to infinity. Replacing the latent parameter family by a finite positive set would remove this divergence mechanism, and the common empirical bound would have a finite maximum over that set.

The lesson is limited and exact: an almost-sure/finite-expectation existence equivalence in a finite hidden-model interface must not be transferred to arbitrary composite Bayesian priors merely because only two candidate priors are being mixed.
