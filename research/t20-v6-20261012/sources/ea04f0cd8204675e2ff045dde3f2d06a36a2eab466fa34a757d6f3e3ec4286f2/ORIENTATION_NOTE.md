# P15 orientation note — prospective Orthemological semantics of normative-gap disclosure

P15 begins from a narrow observation in the supplied PDF: tuple coordinates and feasible mixed-policy geometry do not themselves specify how outcomes **ought** to be ranked. The PDF explicitly requires extra structure for scalarisation, ordinary Nash utility, vector improvement, or team objectives, and separately distinguishes descriptive stability from normative desirability.

The prospective Orthemological move is to make that extra structure explicit and inspectable. A useful typed schema is:

- descriptive structure `D`;
- a class `B(D)` of independently motivated admissible bridge structures;
- normative expansions/enrichments `(D,b)` for `b in B(D)`;
- a target normative proposition `phi`;
- a reduct/forgetful operation that recovers the common descriptive base.

The key candidate disclosure condition is **relative underdetermination**: if two admissible enrichments of the same descriptive base disagree on `phi`, then `phi` is not invariant across the admitted normative completions and therefore is not determined by the descriptive base **relative to that bridge class**.

This is only a research orientation. P15 must determine the exact logic, semantics, admissibility conditions and Orthemology bridge. In particular, it must not make disagreement trivial by allowing arbitrary bridge structures, must not hide normative premises inside `D`, and must not call a section or enrichment rule “canonical” without specifying a mathematical criterion such as uniqueness, definability, naturality, or symmetry invariance.

P15 depends on P14's actual completed bridge audit. If P14 rejects or weakens scalarisation/equilibrium bridges, P15 must inherit that negative result rather than forcing the desired is–ought application.
