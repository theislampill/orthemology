## 1. Relations and the two target conclusions

Let O be the domain of candidate original bearers. Its members are not certified as eligible perfect originals just by inclusion. Let A be intrinsic voluntary act-tokens, C external created effects, T times, and F a nonempty subset of C representing the relevant connected created field. Let Act(o,a,t) mean that a is an actual voluntary token inhering in o at t. Let Prod(o,a,c) mean an actual original productive contribution by that act to c, without requiring temporal simultaneity between act and product. C does not include A merely because a token begins. Let Part_F(o) abbreviate ∃a,c,t [Act(o,a,t) ∧ c∈F ∧ Prod(o,a,c)].

Keep distinct:

- E(o): a philosophical eligibility claim, not a synonym for a nonempty predicate or membership in O.
- Pow(o): standing essential power, with its scope specified separately.
- GA(o): ∀t∈T ∃a∈A Act(o,a,t), one deliberately strong discrete no-idle formalization.
- CP(o): ∃a,c,t [Act(o,a,t) ∧ c∈C ∧ Prod(o,a,c)], some actual created output somewhere.
- CPP(o): ∀t∈T ∃a,c [Act(o,a,t) ∧ c∈C ∧ Prod(o,a,c)], a stronger perpetually productive interpretation at act-times. It does not assert simultaneous product existence.
- Part_F(o): some actual original contribution to the target field.
- Full_F(o): original adequacy throughout F, whose interpretation cannot be replaced by Part_F.

Let N(o) denote proposed necessary existence for a distinct external concrete bearer. Neither N(o)→E(o) nor N(o)→GA(o) is installed by notation. GA(g), ∀o [E(o)→GA(o)], and ∀o [N(o)→GA(o)] are three different scope claims.

A claim ∃g [E(g) ∧ Full_F(g)] is not the claim that every original participant is g. It also does not prohibit a further nonparticipant. The field appraisal must not be upgraded silently to either of those claims.

## 2. A no-restart implication is not a universal no-idle norm

A simple no-restart formula is:

NR(o): ∀s∈T [(¬∃a Act(o,a,s)) → ∀t>s ¬∃a Act(o,a,t)].

If o acts at a later time t, NR(o) excludes complete inactivity at an earlier s. A bearer which never acts satisfies NR(o) vacuously. Thus NR alone cannot disqualify an eternally inactive additional bearer. The source's stronger essential-genus assertion, or a perfection argument extending GA to every eligible original, does further work. This is a dependency observation, not an assertion that NR exhausts the Salīm 2:142 argument.

## 3. The precise new independence controls

These are two versions of one sparse relational structure. They isolate activity/output/field bridges. They do not assign a `Perfect=True` flag and claim to have built a divine rival. They do not alter the adopted philosophical conditions; rather, they exhibit what the activity subtheory alone leaves unspecified.

Let T=ℤ. Let O={g,b}. There are distinct tokens a(o,n) for each o∈O and n∈ℤ, with Act(o,a(o,n),n) and no other Act instances. Every token is new at its own index, none is eternal, and every bearer has some actual token at every indexed time. Each bearer consequently satisfies GA and NR. No independent concrete genus object is added. Any act-kind or genus here is a predicate over tokens. Temporal indexing is a logical coordinate, not proof that two disconnected realities share an actual cosmic clock.

Version I: C=F={x(n):n∈ℤ}. Put Prod(g,a(g,n),x(n)) for each n, with no production by b. F can be made productively connected by stipulating ordinary created links x(n)→x(n+1), whose actual original basis remains g. Then g participates in F, b satisfies GA, and b fails CP and Part_F. Therefore GA does not entail CP or Part_F. A transitive-token predicate can be true for g's tokens and false for b's; the rule that every transitive token has a product is preserved.

Version II: add Y={y(n):n∈ℤ} disjoint from F, with Prod(b,a(b,n),y(n)); add created links y(n)→y(n+1) without cross-links to F. Both bearers satisfy CPP, and every represented act now has an external created product. Still Part_F(b) is false. Therefore even perpetual created productivity, and even the stronger every-token-output rule, do not entail participation in this particular field. Actual disconnectedness here is stipulated to isolate the bridge, not empirically established.

These definitions prove their stated properties at every integer index, rather than merely checking a finite prefix. They do not formalize enough metaphysics to demonstrate the consistency of the source's entire account, nor even a full theory of unrestricted modal ability. They have no independent argument for why b exists, why its essence is necessary, why either candidate satisfies unrestricted eligible pure perfection, why its choices have adequate wisdom, or why its activity is actual rather than named. Version I specifically leaves the philosophical legitimacy of permanent external nonproduction unearned. Version II leaves disconnected original realms and their comprehensive created inventory unearned. Both fail the unrestricted external-bearer supremacy norm if g and b are underived, and both fail all-created-effects coverage by every eligible original. No empty receipt graph can remedy these omissions.

## 4. Minimal route from field uniqueness to unrestricted numerical uniqueness

Define the *exclusive participant theorem* as:

X_F: ∀o [E(o) ∧ Part_F(o) → o=g].

Define the *field-engagement premise* as:

B_F: ∀o [E(o) → Part_F(o)].

Then X_F and B_F imply ∀o [E(o) → o=g], by one substitution. Existence E(g) is separately required for exactly one rather than at most one. If X_F is held only defeasibly, adding B_F does not promote it to a demonstrated universal impossibility theorem. The current warrant appraisal explicitly does not supply X_F as a proved theorem.

B_F can be decomposed, more strongly than necessary, into:

1. Every eligible original produces some created effect somewhere: E(o)→CP(o).
2. Every created effect to which an eligible original contributes lies in F (or is connected to F in a way that establishes a genuinely original contribution to the enlarged relevant field).

Clause 2 needs a real inventory/connectedness premise. Defining F to be a connected component does not establish it. A weaker existential localization clause suffices: each eligible original's created output includes at least one member of F. CPP is unnecessary for this minimal theorem; eventual/some output plus localization suffices. In this sense the residual is narrower than requiring every act to create a product.

Another sufficient bridge is all-created coverage: ∀o,c [E(o) ∧ c∈C → originally grounds(o,c)]. With F nonempty and a definition linking such grounding to actual participation, this implies B_F. It still does not by itself imply X_F: several candidates can be asserted to ground the same field. The actual-efficacy/nonabsorption/application problem remains unless an exclusive sufficiency premise is also warranted.

## 5. The genuinely different direct route

Let D(x,o) be precisely the constitutive/original dependence excluded by original independence. If:

- every eligible original is D-independent of every distinct external bearer; and
- every actual distinct external bearer D-depends on every eligible original,

then two eligible originals are impossible immediately. For g≠b, the second premise gives D(b,g) and the first denies it. This universal external-bearer supremacy argument needs neither GA nor CP nor a connected field. It is stronger in domain than all-created coverage. A hypothetical peer cannot be inserted into C in order to obtain the contradiction while its created status is the very disputed conclusion.

This is not a new discovery of the universal-supremacy theorem; the eligibility review already supplied it. Its role here is to contrast the actual scope-changing premises. Essential activity should not receive credit for a conclusion produced by that separate norm.
