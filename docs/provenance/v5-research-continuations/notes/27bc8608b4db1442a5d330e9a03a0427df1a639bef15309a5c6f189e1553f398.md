## Positive philosophical result

The N1–N4 account is not empty because its fidelity relation has inspectable, fallible conditions: this source, these marks, this convention, this attribution and this change. An editor can prefer a different output without making that output a faithful attribution. The argument therefore explains a local correctness relation instead of making an approval score its own justification. Claim-relative independence is particularly useful: direct inspection can be independent of a defective OCR rule while sharing the source image; another copy of that image does not thereby become independent authenticity evidence.

The packet fairly distinguishes this gain from stronger conclusions. A convention may partly constitute content without allowing the present evaluator to make its truth arbitrary. A deflationist can accept accurate representation while denying a further substantial normative property. A thick premise of successful objective correction cannot independently prove its own truth conditions; a thin changed-output trace cannot supply them. The proposed evidence-and-intervention middle is a defeasible warrant, not a faculty-free proof of all faculties.

The revised example expressly uses retained fidelity standards rather than claiming to establish Candidate E's stronger full-basis radicality premise. This prevents a change of explanatory target. The appendix also prevents an inference from source cognition to benevolence or trustworthy testimony: knowingly certifying p while knowing not-p is a defect in an undertaken truth-certification relation, but excluding such an act still requires a separately warranted no-defect premise.

## Common-repair result

For admitted lawful states K and a finite family of commuting idempotent self-maps, each composite output lies in every map's fixed points. The proof moves a repeated map through the composition and uses idempotence. Independently defended fixed-point adequacy then yields satisfaction of all relevant standards. This is a correct elementary construction, not a new general theorem or a proof that its world interpretations are true.

The argument earns explanatory usefulness by specifying when the maps are real corrections. The source-custody example distinguishes preserving original evidence from amending a derived assertion. Independent annotation fields or extensional records are required; a chronological audit log is outside a claim of byte-for-byte commutation. Copying a stale transcription and then fixing that transcription is correctly shown not to repair the dependent claim in every order. The units and invalid-inference examples also preserve their actual targets rather than force numerical or verbal agreement.

Commutation is sufficient, not necessary. Lawful reachability and continuing stability are separate. Pairwise compatible standards need not admit one joint solution. Distinct final metaphysical beliefs can survive a successful shared evidence repair. These are not evasions; they prevent a narrower useful result from being mislabeled as total restoration.

The fail-silent coverage variant is correctly distinguished from the fixed-point theorem: an identity map is not an individually adequate repair. A fixed batch of f+1 genuinely distinct roots nevertheless contains an effective repair under the bounded omission model; no fault identification or success receipt is necessary. The shared containment-gate caveat is important: if every route depends on one corruptible gate, omitting it would manufacture independence. This positive architecture changes the failure semantics; it does not solve destructive faults by renaming them.
