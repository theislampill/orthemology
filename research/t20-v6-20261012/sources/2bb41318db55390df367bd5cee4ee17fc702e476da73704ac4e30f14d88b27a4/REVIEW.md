## Why the recovered argument matters

1. **Own provision versus foreign completion.** The account locates the relevant original adequacy in the same source's power and willing, expressly comparing completeness through its own unassisted activity with completeness through its own attributes or self. Given the inherited original-source account, real dependence on a derivative creature's act is not thereby dependence on another original provider. This is a substantive explanatory classification, not just repetition of “no receipt.” The account does not derive common sourcehood afresh from that classification.
2. **Fitting time versus prior deficiency.** Absence of J before the fitting occasion does not imply absence of a perfection then due. The text argues that an inappropriate occurrence can itself be deficient. Thus the source's comparative statement that J is more perfect than its absence cannot be transported into an unqualified demand for J at every earlier occasion. This is not an exact timestamp proof or a claim that every chronological absence is fitting.
3. **Power and achievement.** Power to realize temporally fitting goods is positively compared with incapacity; events cannot retain their event-mode while being eternally actual. This gives an evaluative/modal answer to the alleged deficiency. It does not replace actual achievement with standing power: the response still occurs, and the servant's actual repentance remains necessary for this specified J.
4. **Fixed target.** Shifāʾ 181–182 rejects requiring the same condition-constituted realization with its requisite removed. The addendum correctly applies this to J over actual repentance, without concluding that no differently specified anticipatory joy is possible or reducing real rejoicing to a semantic label.
