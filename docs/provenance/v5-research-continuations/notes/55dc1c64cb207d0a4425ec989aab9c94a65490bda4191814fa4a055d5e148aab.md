# Which norm weights can a coherence structure identify?

Status: integrated ordinary-mathematical application of the new local repair theorem and a classical diagonal-stabilizer result. No historical theorem-novelty claim.

## Setup and conclusion

Let C=conv(V)⊂R^n, with V finite nonempty, p∈relint(C), and tangent subspace T=span(C−p). Compare positive diagonal quadratic score metrics W=diag(w_i) and Z=diag(z_i).

Call a nonzero u∈T elementary if no nonzero v∈T has support properly contained in support(u). Form an undirected graph on coordinates 1,...,n: join i,j when both occur in one elementary support. Isolated coordinates remain singleton components. These are the support-component blocks; they may equivalently be described through the corresponding representable matroid decomposition, specifically, for a matrix whose row space is T these elementary supports are cocircuits of its column matroid, or circuits of the dual; the connected-component partition agrees under matroid duality.

The following are equivalent:

1. For every transverse direction h, common coherent weak repairs exist at arbitrarily small positive scales along p+εh
2. The W-orthogonal and Z-orthogonal projections onto T coincide
3. diag(z_i/w_i) preserves T
4. z_i/w_i is constant on each support-component block

Consequently, local universal correctability determines relative weights WITHIN each dependency block; independent scaling BETWEEN blocks is invisible to this criterion. For a single categorical simplex, T={x:Σx_i=0}, each two-coordinate difference is elementary, the graph is connected, and this reduces to global proportionality. For independent coherent blocks, multiple proportionality constants survive. Full-dimensional coherence sets and singleton coherence sets have no informative relative-weight coupling at this local affine-interior level.

## Proof of (1)↔(2)

Apply LOCAL_REPAIR_RIGIDITY R1 in each transverse direction. The projections already agree as the identity on T. Thus equality in all transverse directions is exactly equality of linear operators. If T=R^n, condition (1) is vacuous and both projections are the identity, as required.

## Proof of (2)↔(3)

A projection is determined by its range and kernel. The common range is T and ker P_W=W^{-1}T⊥. Therefore

P_W=P_Z iff W^{-1}T⊥=Z^{-1}T⊥
          iff ZW^{-1}T⊥=T⊥.

The diagonal map D=ZW^{-1} is symmetric and invertible. It preserves T⊥ iff it preserves T: for t∈T and n∈T⊥, <Dt,n>=<t,Dn>=0; dimension and invertibility give equality. The converse is identical.

## Proof of (3)↔(4)

First, elementary vectors span T. For any nonzero x∈T, choose a support-minimal nonzero u∈T with supp(u)⊆supp(x). Subtract a scalar multiple of u to eliminate one nonzero coordinate of x, then induct on support size. An elementary vector is unique up to scalar on its support: two independent vectors with that same minimal support could be combined to cancel one coordinate, contradiction.

Suppose D preserves T. For each distinct diagonal value λ of D, its coordinate spectral projector is a polynomial in D (Lagrange interpolation on the finite distinct diagonal values). It therefore preserves T. If an elementary u had nonzero coordinates carrying two distinct diagonal values, applying one spectral projector would yield a nonzero vector in T with smaller support, contradiction. Thus D is constant on every elementary support, and hence on each connected component.

Conversely, suppose its entries are constant on components. Every elementary vector is then an eigenvector of D, since its support lies in one component. Elementary vectors span T, so D preserves T. Invertibility makes the image exactly T. QED.

## Query interface

Because projection differences vanish on T, directions whose images span R^n/T suffice to determine equality of the two projection operators. Thus codim(T) fixed transverse directions suffice for this identification test; this is an upper bound, not a universal minimal-query theorem. In a simplex codimension is one, and one transverse ray exposes all relative diagonal weights. In a one-dimensional tangent block involving n coordinates, fewer than n−1 arbitrary linear first-order observations need not identify n−1 weight ratios. An exact minimal adaptive query claim would require a separate experiment model and proof.

## Dependency and semantic cautions

The relation T is supplied by the declared coherence domain and records AFFINE constraints on truth vectors, not statistical dependence or general semantic relatedness. If the meaningful event space is changed, duplicated, quotiented or translated without retaining its true dependence relations, the blocks and identifiable weights can change. This supplies an explicit M1/M10/M12 formal crosswalk and a conditional M18 application: source-faithful occurrence/event dependencies determine what a norm-repair experiment can identify. An M18 transfer requires an explicit faithful coordinate/valuation map preserving the score class, truth vectors and perturbations. Arbitrary translation, aggregation and coordinate mixing need not preserve diagonality or additivity. Mere equality of language strings does not establish an affine dependence relation. Normative authority and the actual truth model remain independently warranted inputs.

This theorem does not classify boundary-face repair for a full-dimensional polytope. At a boundary, a tangent CONE replaces T, and an arbitrary repair may move into the interior rather than remain in the face. Applying this affine-interior theorem there without a separate face-preservation premise is invalid.
