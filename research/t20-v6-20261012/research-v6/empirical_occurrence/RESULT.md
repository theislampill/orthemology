## Mathematical and foundational cross-contributions

### 1. An endpoint change can exceed the retained statistic

The actual T16 scoring body defines, for correct count c, observed flag count e, roster size m and assigned size s:

- R = 1 when c > m/2, 0 when c < m/2, and missing at equality.
- A_E uses c versus e/2, giving half credit to a nonempty tie; A_E0 gives zero to an empty support.
- B_E0 = 1[c > m/2], and Q_E0 = 1[c > s/2].

Since 0 ≤ c ≤ e ≤ m, R=1 entails A_E=1. The reverse does not follow. More importantly, retaining even (R,m,e) does not generally permit recovery of A_E: with m=5 and e=2, c=0 and c=2 both give R=0 but yield A_E=0 and A_E=1 respectively. That is a concrete same-fibre, different-target obstruction derived from the read implementation. The fresh data contain 130 rounds with R=0 and A_E=1.

This connects empirical rescoring to the mathematical requirement that an observation preserve the target it is meant to determine. The elementary inequality and explicit two-state counterexample, rather than a test count, do the logical work. It does not prove that new scoring would leave participant behavior unchanged, nor does it supersede the analytic final-answer law handled separately.

### 2. Missing-outcome sharpness has a joint-feasibility condition

The T13 missing-outcome body already states that its interval endpoints are sharp when the corresponding completions are jointly feasible. T16's occurrence example makes the general danger concrete: projecting an admissible joint relation to coordinate marginals and multiplying them can manufacture completions. If two missing binary outcomes admit only (0,1) and (1,0), their total is exactly 1, even though each marginal separately ranges from 0 to 1 and the unrestricted product suggests totals 0 through 2.

Consequently, an empirically justified feasible-completion relation C can refine missing-outcome bounds by taking minima and maxima over C. No coupling is asserted for a real dataset without evidence. This is a source-faithful application of the inherited feasibility caveat and relational interface discipline, not a newly discovered general bounding theorem.

### 3. The recovered protocol gives a concrete support-sensitive decision interface

In the prospective protocol, q=L−d and the limit is 80. The same observed gap d∈[17,19] gives q∈[81,83] when L=100, but q∈[71,73] when L=90. Thus the gap alone cannot determine ABOVE/BELOW. The task needs the applicable reference and its support history, not merely an address or content fingerprint. The interval verdict is ABOVE only when all admissible q exceed 80, BELOW only when all are at most 80, and otherwise DEFER.

The protocol preserves surviving members, memory relays and reacquisition as possible supports. Removing direct access is not a stipulation of ignorance. A certificate is constitutive enough that it may improve integration; its measurement role cannot be presumed inert. The endpoint is deadline-bound, and later recovery constitutes a new case rather than retroactive success. These details make the source a much sharper bridge from abstract possession/uptake distinctions to a possible study, while its unspecified planning model and sample size prevent claiming a ready preregistration or completed experiment.

### 4. The five-state argument fixes the background before calling support positive

The exact T13 model uses masses (0.1,0.2,0.2,0.2,0.3), propositions A={0,1,2}, B={0,1,3}, C={0,2,3}, and H={0,4}. Each private cell knows two components, while the admitted conjunction identifies state 0 and entails H. Each private posterior for H is 1/3 versus prior 2/5. Under public background D={0,1,2,3}, prior H becomes 1/7 but the same private posterior and logical entailment remain. The sign of probability increment is therefore background-sensitive, and cannot stand in for an invariant notion of evidence possession or logical support. This is an inherited elementary model now fully read, not a new v6 theorem.

### 5. Foundational source distinctions constrain the empirical bridge

Necessary existence, actual nonreception, essential nonreception and existential sufficiency are different claims. A counterexample that stipulates actual receipt does not refute an argument granted actual nonreception. Retrospective possibilities do not by themselves supply the earlier actual productive participant required by an origin argument. Finite age is not identical to an attained first moment, and a distinct cause need not lie outside every constituent. These distinctions in the T16 source assessment prevent the empirical observations from being promoted to global metaphysical closure, while also preventing an irrelevant stronger requirement from being imposed on a bounded empirical faculty.
