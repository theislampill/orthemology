# Content's causal role and a belief's present basis

8 October 2026. Sibling addendum to the frozen structuring-cause-reply stage. No predecessor is altered, no final integration is made, and the answered same-acquisition circularity objection is not retained.

## Finding

**A genuinely content-sensitive causal contribution need not make that content the premise from which a later belief is inferred.** It can instead direct attention to an independently supporting perception. The resulting belief may be warranted. This is a discriminator against a particular stronger attribution of inferential basing, not a declaration that association, perception, or physical realization is irrational.

The useful question is premise-relative: was Q inferred from these P/R states, or did they instead help the subject encounter another ground for Q? The latter route can itself contain perceptual or other inferential processing. It can also be a rational practical response to the subject's goal. Neither fact makes P/R the epistemic basis of Q.

This operation gives a precise, primary-grounded test of a sufficiency claim. It does **not** refute the strongest complete Dretske account. The inspected primary chapter explicitly allows present cognitive integration and points to a continuation that was not acquired. An access limitation cannot stand in for an argument against that continuation.

## What is granted

Grant the historically established indicator functions, genuine contents of the relevant states, and the legitimacy of their causal explanation at the level defended in Dretske's 1991 reply. Functions precede the current tokens; they are not fixed response weights. Their present effects may depend on other beliefs, learning, context, and motivation. A current failure to indicate does not automatically erase the historically fixed function.

Also grant the strongest sensitive associative rival. The word “associative” alone does not specify either irrationality or absence of inference. A process can track structured relationships, discriminate relevant features, respond to changed contexts, and participate in rational control. The appropriate negative control must lack the particular basing role in question, not merely a formal rule symbol or a verbal self-explanation.

## The bounded contrast

Here is an original diagnostic illustration, whose epistemic commitments are explicit rather than hidden in circuit labels.

The subject has meaningful, antecedently acquired representations of:

- P: the lever is raised;
- R: if the lever is raised, the adjacent lamp is on;
- Q: the adjacent lamp is on.

The content-fixing history of these target representations is held fixed. The operative present pathway and other cognitive states may differ; no complete present physical duplication is claimed.

In an ordinary reference case, the subject accepts P and R and reaches Q by using their supporting relation. Calling that episode an inference is an independently assessed or stipulated reference case, not something a label in a program establishes. For its warranted variant, assume adequate grounds for P/R, competent use of their support relation, and no relevant undefeated defeater. A true Q alone establishes none of those conditions.

Now consider another episode with the same relevant contents and indicator functions. Accepting P as true, rather than merely entertaining the concept of a lever, guides the subject to inspect the adjacent lamp. A learned, context-sensitive association may implement this guidance: merely mentioning a lever or accepting that it is not raised would not prompt the same check. The subject has not yet accepted Q by inference from P/R. The subject then clearly sees that the lamp is on and accepts Q on that perceptual evidence. Crucially:

1. Acceptance of P directs whether and where the subject looks; it does not bias or determine the interpretation of the visual experience.
2. The same illuminated lamp, attended to for another reason, would normally yield the same Q-belief.
3. P/R are not covertly used as additional inferential grounds in this episode.
4. The viewing conditions and visual capacities supply adequate, undefeated perceptual support for Q, without relying on P/R. Background perceptual competences are allowed; their warrant is an explicit case premise, not derived from the bare causal chain.

Content matters causally here. Accepting the information that the lever is raised can rationally guide inspection of the lamp. Yet that role does not make Q an inference **from P/R**. Both cases can end in a true, warranted Q-belief. Both can involve intelligent, meaningful processing. Their basis differs.

These clauses are essential. If P shapes interpretation of the visual experience, if the subject also infers Q from P/R, or if the alleged independent source depends epistemically on P, this simple contrast no longer applies. Overlapping or redundant bases must be assessed as such; one cannot infer absence of a basis merely because another sufficient basis exists.

The negative case is supported by Siegel's explicit attention-versus-basing discussion, independently of accepting her whole theory. It is stronger than a meaningless-symbol or dead-habit example because genuine content-sensitive guidance is conceded. But it remains a conceptual case, not an observation of a human neural route or a completed naturalistic implementation.

## What the primary accounts change

The separate source summaries state their positions and exact scopes. Three consequences matter for the test.

First, a criterion requiring an articulated inference rule or an explicit judgment about support would exclude rival cases too quickly. The inspected literature offers serious accounts on which uptake of a ground occurs in the response itself or through content-sensitive, nonlinguistic control. Whether those accounts ultimately suffice is disputed; they cannot be excluded merely by stipulating explicit reflection as constitutive of every inference.

Second, sensitivity to a represented goal and appropriate action-selection is not by itself the warrant of every belief involved. A useful search strategy can lead to good evidence or to misleading evidence. Its practical success must be distinguished from the conclusion's support, source reliability, and defeat conditions. Buckner himself separates explanatory/intensional adequacy from ecological epistemic appraisal and restricts his main treatment to practical inference. That restriction is not a defect in his project, but it prevents an automatic transfer to theoretical knowledge of necessary truths.

Third, a transition can be a bad inference rather than no inference. Truth of the endpoint, causal production, quality of the supporting grounds, and the presence of inferential basing are distinct questions. Boolean tests that demand justified inputs and outputs always rise and fall together cannot serve as a universal definition. Invalid inference can proceed from good premises, and independent support can leave an output warranted when one candidate premise is defeated. Counterfactual tests must specify the remaining grounds and the degree and kind of epistemic dependence being tested.

Boghossian's 2014 account presses an important person-level guidance question, but its selected scope is conscious, voluntary theoretical reasoning, not all basic cognition. Siegel's actual response challenges universal self-awareness rather than declaring every causal transition inferential. Patronnikov's critique further shows why a strict inference/association dichotomy and a simplified dependence test need separate defence. None of these papers supplies a neutral, settled analysis to insert unargued into a proof against naturalism.

## What would genuinely discriminate current basing

A substantive comparison should preserve the acquired meanings while investigating the **actual role of their present tokens**. Useful evidence would distinguish:

- changes in the conclusion or its confidence in response to the grounds' support, including relevant defeating evidence;
- changes caused only by verbal primes, motor preparation, attention placement, or opportunity to consult an independent source;
- the subject's represented task and available information, rather than the evaluator's preferred task;
- a content-guided response from a merely successful endpoint or a retrospectively supplied explanation.

Such evidence need not always be verbally reportable. Conversely, a sincere retrospective report is not automatically constitutive of the earlier basis. A sophisticated associative system that meets a justified current-guidance criterion should receive the same evidential credit as another implementation; its associative genealogy is not a disqualifier.

No finite response panel by itself proves the entire metaphysics of basing. The content-fixing account, admissibility of interventions, actual psychological route, normative support relation, and relevant reliability/defeat conditions all require warranted interpretation. Stipulating “rational” on one path and “associative” on another would simply encode the answer.

## Exact credit and obstruction

The prior fitrah warranting-basis addendum already distinguished occasion from warranting ground. That distinction is inherited, not rediscovered here. The new increment is its application to a **fully meaningful, content-sensitive present causal route**, supported by directly inspected primary accounts that also reject crude association and reflection dichotomies. This prevents three false shortcuts: content is idle because physical causes suffice; content is causally relevant so this token is inferred from it; and the token was associatively produced so it is unwarranted.
