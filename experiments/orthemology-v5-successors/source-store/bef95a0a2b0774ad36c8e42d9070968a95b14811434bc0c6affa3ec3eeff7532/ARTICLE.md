# Original provision and constitutive possession

Eleventh Orthemology research • 3 October 2026

## Result

The inherited original-supply argument has a strong target-relative reason for including the exercise indispensable to a real receiving mode. That reason does not turn every constitutive relation into productive donation. The exclusion of another donor is also not, by itself, a complete account of how original exercise ownership is individuated. A substantive co-ownership account must therefore be tested at the exact relation used, rather than defeated by inserting it into an uninterpreted ownership predicate.

The inspected comparison does not warrant original co-possession of the correction's complete productive origin. Its role here is to test the accounting bridge, not to supply a metaphysically certified original rival. The separately accepted assembly result remains unchanged: under its inherited full-origin and proper-exercise interpretation, retaining distinct original-member attributions conflicts with promotion of their whole to a complete source. [A1]

## 1. What full-origin inclusion actually requires

The preserved target is the actual reason-responsive correction concerning the displayed bibliographic witness. A whole-origin account seeks the actuality of its relevant productive contributions and receiving modes. This is stronger than an account of why a similar endpoint could exist, and its strength must remain explicit.

Suppose an actual receiving mode consists in the recipient's receiving through b's exercise. That mode requires the relevant exercise of b. The exercise is not an arbitrary object mentioned in a description; it is the productive reality in virtue of which the mode is reception from b. If an alleged complete original source leaves that indispensable efficacy wholly outside its productive account, the account continues to rely on an independently supplied contribution to the very mode it claims to originate.

This is the positive warrant for the prerequisite step. It is not a rule that every truth entailed by an effect-description is efficiently produced by the effect's source. Nor does it identify the receiving mode with the source-intrinsic exercise. The first fixes the covered actuality; the second supplies indispensable efficacy. Both relations must be interpreted correctly. [H1 §§2–4; H2 §6]

This target-relative warrant is retained from the inherited dependent-mode argument. It is not a newly established bridge of this follow-through.

The corresponding limitation is also substantive. A source sufficient for the endpoint alone need not be complete for the actual mode or its entire origin. If the source claim has that narrower target, adding the mode expands its scope. The expansion may be justified by the original whole-origin question, but cannot be hidden as a consequence of endpoint sufficiency.

This reasoning gives a warranted local standard of complete productive independence. It does not establish that any actual bearer fulfils that standard, that every actual productive history has a singular completion, or that a particular constitutive theory expresses the same productive relation.

## 2. Anti-donation and exclusive ownership

There is a direct reason against a particular attribution: if b's supposed unborrowed exercise obtains its required efficacy from another independent source, then it is borrowed in that respect. Calling the receiving agent genuinely active does not remove its source dependence. This remains a valid reason even where the received power is intrinsic to the recipient while possessed.

Now consider a different proposal: another bearer w also has a genuine constitutive ownership claim over the same efficacy, without furnishing it to b. The proposal contests whether the second accounting attribution describes a donor at all. The anti-donation reason does not decide that question simply by referring to another entity.

Two affirmative responses are available:

1. Establish that the proposed w-account really supplies the actuality or efficacy of b's exercise. If that relation holds, b's same-respect unborrowed status fails.
2. Establish that the proper source-intrinsic exercise is individuated by its original subject, so the alleged second original owner is either the same subject or does not own that very proper exercise in the proposed respect.

The first is a claim about actual productive dependence. The second is a claim about the identity and ownership of a proper exercise. They can reinforce one another, but neither should be replaced by the bare observation that w and b are distinct. A direct rational understanding of agency could in principle warrant the second; the absence of an experiment or an exhaustive ontology does not disqualify it. The relevant question is whether that understanding covers the proposed constitutive case.

The individuation response has a positive rationale. A source-intrinsic exercise is the subject's actual agency, not merely an occurrence with an indifferent actor label. Holding one event fixed while changing its purported subject need not preserve that proper exercise. If one event realizes distinct subject-indexed acts, it does not yet exhibit co-ownership of one proper original act. This gives the response substantive content without establishing that every constitutive ownership theory must accept that individuation at the absolute original scope.

The inherited source texts already reserve the exclusion to proper unborrowed exercises and identify shared-exercise ownership as a point where its applicability can be disputed. The present distinction explicates that reserved burden; it does not overturn an unrestricted co-ownership theorem previously proved. [H1 §§3, 6–7; H2 §§4–7]

This supplies a definite comparison, rather than a newly invented co-ownership predicate. Four application questions determine what it could change in the programme.

### The relata must be the alleged source-bearers

A relation among powers is not already a relation among distinct agents each originally supplying the same exercise. Likewise, a constituent plurality is not thereby an additional individual. Before applying the inherited exclusion, identify which relata are actual bearers, which are powers or manifestations, and which actual exercise is properly theirs. An ontological theory can answer those questions differently, but the substitution must be defended.

The requirement does not prohibit a theory in which powers are ontologically primary or ordinary objects are derivative. It prevents a result about the first kind of entity from being reported as the original-source conclusion about the second without the required bridge.

### The possession relation must have the claimed original scope

The concrete construction can make a whole's power explanatory and causally significant while leaving its possession grounded in a cooperative actuality. That possibility is important: genuine power is not confined to an inert aggregate or a numerically simple bearer. It is not yet original possession of the full productive ancestry used in the explanation.

Suppose the proposed whole's relevant power requires earlier or simultaneous component activity. To make that whole P1-complete, the account must include the actuality of that required contribution. Merely renaming the contribution as internal does not supply it. If the whole genuinely provides later component exercises, it can earn that productive credit; the later supply does not retrospectively supply the antecedent availability used to possess the power. This is the inherited fixed-antecedent distinction applied at the accurately identified target, not an assertion that all feedback or top-down causation is impossible. [T1]

If the constitutive relation is not an efficient supply relation, that fact must be respected. It can support a genuine constitutive explanation without automatically satisfying, or automatically violating, the programme's productive-origin claim. The issue is the work done by the relation, not whether one can attach the word dependent to it.

### Shared character does not establish shared original exercise

Sharing a power-kind, sharing one power, and jointly owning one actual exercise are different claims. None by itself establishes that two distinct bearers each originally supply that same exercise without dependence on the other or on a further basis.

Even sharing one occurrence or event does not establish identity of a source-intrinsic exercise under the same subject and productive respect. Different action descriptions or ownership levels cannot silently supply that identity premise. The comparison needs distinct source-bearers, the same token proper exercise, genuinely original possession and full-origin accounting to be independently warranted together.

A co-ownership proposal would change the sourcehood assessment if it supplied that stronger account with actual warrant. Ordinary joint action would not be enough when each participant's accomplishment requires another's independently supplied contribution; that is real cooperation, not the independently complete original role. Conversely, insisting on individual ownership merely by definition would fail to assess the proposed alternative.

The inspected construction does not supply that stronger original-source application. No result here treats it as a complete rival preserving actual P1/P2 while refuting the inherited consequence.

### The identity relation must remain fixed

The programme's source-identity conclusion uses identity of a particular bearer. Counting several nonidentical entities as one does not establish identity. Such a relation may be a substantive ontological proposal; it must not quietly replace identity at the final step.

This is equally important for the affirmative. A warranted singular source need not be known by a personal name, but it must actually be singular in the interpretation under which the source theorem is applied. It cannot be made so merely by a counting convention.

## 4. Warrant assessment

The positive original-supply rationale survives this comparison. When the target includes actual reception through a particular exercise, a purported independently complete origin cannot leave its indispensable efficacy supplied wholly outside its account. This has more force than a blanket demand that all related facts have one cause. Its legitimate scope is fixed by the actual productive role and the declared full-origin target.

The subject-exclusion step remains conditional in the constitution case. The general reason against donated unborrowed power does not independently establish that every genuine co-possession claim is a donation, nor does a statement of the inherited predicate establish exclusive original subjecthood. The case for a source-intrinsic proper exercise must be assessed in its own metaphysical interpretation.

The candidate comparison changes neither side into a warranted complete actual ontology. No new original co-owner, actual P1/P2 conclusion or necessary participant is established. It is not evidence that the original-source belief is irrational. The inherited conditional assembly test still applies whenever its target and ownership premises are genuinely satisfied.

The resulting follow-through therefore locates a substantive choice rather than closing it. An argument may defend ultimate productive provision, or defend a constitutive form of original possession; it must then justify the corresponding exclusion or co-ownership claim. Another nominal whole, another shared-power label, or a merely consistent relation table would not add warrant. A new account earning that precise original productive interpretation would.
