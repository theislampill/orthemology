# Nubuwwat: first-volume continuation, continuous-reading notes

Scope: V01 prepared windows 014–026, NB-S001-L0139 through NB-S002-L0049, latter contiguous half of the supplied volume-1 authorial electronic unit. The reading follows the completed Miftah study; the earlier preparation-only status is historical. These notes credit displayed reading, not mere emission. Electronic witness is not an exact transcription of al-Tuwayyan. Modern apparatus, introduction and all scan pages are not claimed read.

## Segment 1: windows 014–016
Read in order, all END markers present; exact reading receipts are recorded separately.

- Opening is a continuation, not a new self-contained proof. The author accepts an agent who necessitates an outcome through his own will and power while rejecting necessity without will or simultaneous eternal necessitation. Continuing willing is compatible with sustaining an already-created object (L0139).
- Love is made the ground of action and final causation. The received text says `العلة الغائبة`; preserve this rather than silently printing a more expected word. Pleasure is distinguished from cognition: love, apprehension of the beloved, and the delight arising from that apprehension are three distinguishable matters. The heavenly-vision report is scripture/tradition within the author’s argument, not an independently verified observation.
- L0140–0145: motion, volition and an end loved for itself are linked; the move to God as the only intrinsically worthy object of worship has metaphysical and normative premises. The distinction between worship as intended end and seeking assistance as means explains why a final cause precedes in intention but follows in realisation. Fear is instrumentally useful; peace, love and delight are terminal goods. Anxiety is assigned to the worshipper’s fault rather than the intrinsic character of divine remembrance. This asymmetry matters for the treatment of suffering and adverse experiences.
- L0145–0147: extended Ghazali quotation about spiritual travel and knowledge precedes the author’s critical reply (`قلت`). Do not attribute the quoted tripartite ascent or the claim that inward witnessing is stronger than sight directly to Ibn Taymiyya. His reply concedes experiential familiarity but asks whether the experienced content is true and corresponds to reality. He distinguishes knowledge from its object and rejects knowledge as the unqualified final end.
- L0148–0152: polemical genealogies of monism, philosophy and esotericism; the author distinguishes Ghazali from later monists and credits al-Haralli with some true/good material despite severe criticism. His Alexandria anecdote makes accurate explanation of an opponent a condition of persuasive refutation. The absolute universal is treated as mental, not an independently existing extra-mental object. These reports and attributions have not been independently source-verified.
- L0153–0157: knowing and willing are jointly constitutive powers; mere awareness can encompass harmful objects, whereas beneficial willing has an appropriate object. Imitating God is criticised by distinguishing the divine self-directed object of knowledge/love from a creature’s proper object. The passage attributed to al-Razi about the limits of kalam is quoted polemical evidence, not an independently authenticated autobiography here. Prophetic truth and worship are jointly necessary in the author’s account of happiness.
- L0158–0162: the historical excellence of the first generations, enduring communal truth and providential success are claims grounded in tradition and the author’s historical reading. Reconciliation and repentance are real possibilities: an enemy can become an ally. Proper correction requires truth, intended good and mercy. The author distinguishes culpable error from excused and rewarded mistaken ijtihad; this qualifies his harsh group-level polemic. Abrogation is explained as a genuinely authorised practice at one time ceasing to be authoritative later.
- L0163–0165: parity argument against denying wisdom while affirming will, or denying will while affirming agency. The author presses opponents to apply their anthropomorphic objection consistently. The observed coming-to-be of motions and living things is used against self-origination and an infinite regress of dependent agents; the composite’s dependence is then asserted. This is not simply a single design inference. Distinguish his rejection of an infinite hierarchy of efficient agents from a blanket rejection of every temporal succession.
- Non-obvious textual concerns: L0158 begins `واتبع لها` after a concluded passage; L0163 shifts from a Tirmidhi report’s `قال وفي الباب` to `الذي فررت منه` and divine attributes without an obvious bridge. These are apparent received-text discontinuities; no claim yet whether corruption, omission, or authorial/editorial structure. L0151 has `ابن سيا`; preserve raw forms in quotation. Consequential control is reserved for reflective return, without inventing missing wording.

## Segment 2: windows 017–020
Read consecutively after 016, all END markers present. The website-section seam 001→002 is one continuous sentence (`وهو في` / `الباطن`), not a book-volume boundary.

- L0166–0170: purposive action need not mean dependence upon creatures. The author differentiates divine delight, love and approval from externally imposed need, injury or coercion. He is cautious about non-scriptural ambiguous words such as `العشق` and `اللذة`: a possibly defensible meaning does not make the label a good theological formulation. A perfection can be apt when wisdom calls for it rather than previously missing in a deficient way. This is a temporally differentiated perfection account, not an unqualified claim that every new predicate is eternally actual.
- L0170–0176: competing accounts of goodness and wisdom are reconstructed and criticised. The author challenges both moral arbitrariness and an abstract wisdom wholly unrelated to the agent. He objects to disproportionate punishment and inversion of moral rank. Organ-function examples support fitting placement: eyes, tongues, hands and feet have different appropriate uses. These examples are illustrative teleology, not a verified exhaustive biological account. His analogy between the consistency of willing with knowledge and its consistency with wisdom states a substantive principle, rather than proving it merely by defining omnipotence.
- L0172 has a duplicated clause (`أكثر الفقهاء والمتكلمين ...`) and numerous evident transcription roughnesses. They remain in the witness. These do not erase the broad argument but bar diplomatic quotation without control.
- L0177–0180: truth-attestation is the pressure point. If every possible divine act is equally admissible, why cannot an impressive event accompany a liar? The reply attributed to al-Ashari/al-Baqillani tries to preserve God’s power to certify; Ibn Taymiyya objects that this presupposes the very possibility/discriminating force at issue. A second answer invokes necessary knowledge and a royal-message analogy. He says that analogy can work for an agent acting with wisdom, not for an agent acting for nothing. A third calls the miracle the sign of truth; he accepts the conditional relationship while challenging whether opponents’ premises license it. These are importantly conditional concessions, not a rejection of all royal attestation or all immediate knowledge.
- L0181–0187: quoted rivals identify miracle with unusual event plus prophetic claim/challenge and non-opposition. The author argues that contextual declaration cannot turn intrinsically non-discriminating material into a conclusive sign. An ordinary specialized skill can be unusual to an ignorant local audience. Failure to oppose must be distinguished from universal inability. He also notes that necessary knowledge cannot differ between fully identical epistemic situations for no reason.
- L0188–0197: a sign must entail its target and thus be exclusive in the relevant respect. Exclusivity can belong to the class of prophets and their faithful followers, so saints’ marvels can attest their prophet rather than compete with him. The author distinguishes ordinary disease/disaster from the narrated discriminate destruction of prophet-rejecters. Reports of eschatological events are predictive signs upon fulfilment; the events are not already observed in this reading. Diviners’ mix of falsehood and moral corruption is distinguished from prophetic truth and righteousness. Claims that false prophets actually exhibited local unopposed wonders serve as alleged counterexamples to the opponents’ universal rule; their historicity is not independently established here.
- L0192–0196: miracles meeting practical needs (food/water) can be signs without an evidential announcement or challenge. A correctly inspectable relation is evidence even if nobody uses it. Miracle exclusivity is relative to the non-prophetic human/jinn sphere; angelic power is expressly allowed and may mediate it. This prevents misreporting his position as “every sign must be an immediate act using no created intermediary.”
- L0200–0203: flood, destruction narratives, the sea crossing, the Kaaba and the elephant narrative ground claimed qualitative uniqueness. He distinguishes transmission-based and materially trace-based knowledge of past events. Ancient causal/astral alternatives are reported and criticised, not accepted as modern science. The Kaaba’s motivation and endurance claims are his historical interpretation, not neutral verified sociology.
- L0202–0205: all evidential relations are ultimately rationally apprehended, including interpreting a speaker’s purpose through immediate recognition or stable habitual use. The Qur’an’s evidential force is plural: language/order, reports, commands/prohibitions, promises/warnings, significance and power over hearts. The meanings may retain evidential force in translation. He expressly allows earlier scriptures to be signs in their meanings, and suspends a judgement about Hebrew linguistic form to competent speakers. This is broader and more qualified than an exclusively Arabic stylistic argument.
- L0204–S002-L0001: prophetic corroboration is cumulative across persons and generations. Borrowing a prior report is not a fresh sign; previously inaccessible or additional information can be. The liar’s sustaining an entire truthful moral programme is said to conflict with the liar’s ends, so concealed corruption must eventually manifest. This is a substantive psychological universal, not a logical tautology. The section ends rejecting restriction of prophetic evidence to one selected sign and contrasting concrete signs with syllogisms whose conclusion remains general.

## Segment 3: windows 021–023
Read in order, complete END markers observed.

- S002-L0002–0006: close contrast between transporting an existing resource and producing/multiplying it. Stories about jinn flying people to Arafat without the required pilgrimage practices are polemically framed personal reports, not verified contemporary physical findings. He differentiates morally permissible assistance, exploitative/forbidden assistance and unique prophetic authority. Muhammad’s authority is presented as that of the servant-messenger rather than Solomon’s prophet-king. The night journey’s special purpose and heavenly vision, not sheer speed across land, is its decisive distinction. Description of the Jerusalem sanctuary is taken as publicly testable corroboration for a report extending beyond witnesses’ experience; the latter extension needs evaluation, not automatic acceptance.
- S002-L0007–0010: a careful return to the royal-message analogy. He accepts requested conduct as a sign when the relevant alternative motives have been excluded, including mundane counterexamples such as standing because of an animal in one’s clothing. A request for a particular mark and its subsequent occurrence can jointly constitute the sign; the truth-claim itself is not thereby part of the proof. This is a relational/contextual account of the evidence’s kind, not a claim that context never matters. Speaker-intention requires rational apprehension, and deliberate language can be clearer and richer than an unintentional natural sign while requiring more interpretive conditions. He agrees with al-Razi that unaided hearing is insufficient without reason.
- S002-L0009 contains the suspect clause `علم أن الله لم يخلق تلك الأدلة على صدقه`. Do not silently repair the negation or use this one clause alone to establish a positive conclusion. The surrounding argument is clear enough to describe without assuming an emendation.
- S002-L0011: four distinctions: making a claim, the claimed state as conceived, its actual obtaining, and knowledge of its obtaining. Evidence is tied to objective obtaining independently of who asserts or knows it. A conceived object does not entail external existence. Legal examples make the distinction concrete.
- S002-L0012–0016: purported consensuses cannot ground premises of a proof of prophecy if their authority only becomes available after prophecy is established. The author finds the analogy sound when recast in purposive terms, and identifies purported contradictions in restrictions on wonders/saints/sorcery. Crucially, he prefers “God has the power but wisdom and justice exclude its exercise” to “such an event is intrinsically impossible.” He distinguishes destruction of evidence from positive evidence of the opposite: a shared sign loses discriminatory force; it does not thereby prove falsehood.
- S002-L0016–0018: local failure to see truth differs from universal destruction of every means of discrimination. Ordinary truthfulness has public behavioural consequences; repeated communication is alleged to expose deliberate lying over time. The author explicitly distinguishes lasting sincerely transmitted error from deliberate lying. His much stronger universal assertion that conscious lying never remains indistinguishable for long is not established by the few examples or by this reading.
- S002-L0020–0021: twelve comparative differences, extending from character and beneficent teaching through acquisition, capacity, matchability and preceding prophetic traditions. The hypothetical concession “even if prophecy were acquired” still requires a truth-conducive moral route. Distinguish it from his actual denial that prophecy is earned through ordinary learning. Some signs involve no created power (e.g., divine speech); others involve angels who are said not to lie. Reason and fitra, prior transmission, character, content and act are combined.
- S002-L0022–0026: polemical transition into sectarian and political history. General agreement that revelation is true does not settle its particular interpretation. The quoted Ahmad sermon is not newly composed by the author. He notes mixed truth/error in rival schools and the observer-relative visibility of deviations. Ali is ranked nearer the truth in civil conflict while abstaining and reconciliation can still be better than the original fighting. These qualifications cannot be omitted merely to isolate the propositional argument about signs.

## Segment 4: windows 024–026
Read consecutively; this closes only the assigned latter half of the supplied volume-1 electronic authorial unit at S002-L0049.

- S002-L0027–0030: the conflict excursus stresses reconciliation, qualifications on fighting and differences among rebellion, combat and apostasy. It includes violent premodern punishment prescriptions and historical reports of executions; these are source claims, not endorsed present advice. Reports of Ali’s actions, Ibn Abbas’s objections and the companions’ views are not independently authenticated here. The treatment of Khawarij as Muslims despite their violent conduct is an important distinction in the passage’s own taxonomy.
- S002-L0031–0034: refined gradations among groups, not one undifferentiated category. He expressly corrects a misrepresentation of the Karramiyya: their terminological classification of a hypocrite as believer does not mean they place him in paradise. Faith and epistemic/moral obligations vary with capacity, roles, knowledge and historical stage; loss of one part does not entail loss of all. The critique of the kalam origination argument affirms possible unending divine speech/action and targets the particular shared premise prohibiting beginningless successive acts, rather than rejecting creation or divine action.
- S002-L0035–0038: competing theories of divine speech are followed by a sociology/epistemology of consensus. A school’s agreement is not the community’s agreement; an informed dissenter, even one person, defeats a claim of unanimity. No one knows every proof; expertise is distributed across jurists, hadith scholars and others. Numerical majority is not itself the criterion. The claim of the community’s ultimate immunity from collective error remains the author’s revelational premise, not independently proven by these observations.
- S002-L0038 has another abrupt received transition: `لا يحتجون بالإجماع الا علامة وقد يبعث معه نشابه...`. The consensus discussion gives way mid-syntax to envoy-signs. It is flagged for bounded scan control; no missing text is supplied from conjecture.
- S002-L0038–0043: signs may signify honour or punishment, so mere abnormality is not enough. Characteristic envoy-marking can be regular within its reference class while unusual to the general public. If all events are equally immediate divine products and creaturely capacities do no work, defining a miracle as uniquely God’s power no longer discriminates. Inability by a local audience differs from inability everywhere; the universal no-rival rule for false claimants is challenged by alleged historical counterinstances.
- S002-L0044–0047: renewed positive allowance for a request plus appropriately following answer as evidence, explicitly analogized with answered prayer. Challenge, boast and announced demonstration are not universal necessary conditions. Testimony corroborated in a follower can support a prophet and may be stronger than self-demonstration. Source characterises signs as immune to equal or stronger counterevidence; this is an exacting evidential ideal whose real satisfaction still must be shown.
- S002-L0048–0049: closing comparative concession: philosophers’ attention to moral purpose makes them better in one respect, while theologians’ real trust in prophetic reports makes them better in another. Ghazali’s inference from what the prophet does and commands is expressly called a sound path, although the author regards the resulting philosophical construal of prophecy as inadequate. This is not a blanket rejection of all philosophical moral reasoning.
