# The newer edition and its apparatus

The Adwa al-Salaf / Dar Ibn Hazm edition is an important textual control, but it is not an independently witnessed second manuscript. Its editors explicitly say they know one manuscript and use clearer images of the same manuscript used by Muhammad Rashad Salim. The received Islamicbook text is therefore retained unchanged as the continuous-reading base; the newer edition supplies bounded corrections and contextual controls, not an unearned claim of full print equivalence.

## Directly inspected coverage

This apparatus study visually inspected Adwa PDF positions 4–10, 12–30, 32–34 and 40–49 in full, including their printed notes. These contain the publisher's two preliminary pages, the complete al-Sad introduction, the editors' introduction and biography, attribution/title discussion, account of the subject, manuscript description, complete two correction lists and editorial method. Positions 11 and 31 were seen as blank. Positions 35–39, printed 28–32, were inspected as manuscript sample plates and their captions were read; their handwritten text was not fully transcribed or collated. Positions 50–54 were additionally viewed for the title/blank/opening question/blank/start-of-answer boundary. No full scan-body reading is claimed.

Printed numerical pagination in the inspected sections is PDF position minus seven. This is a local observed correspondence, to be checked against each subsequently cited visible printed number, rather than an assumed universal offset.

## Publisher and introducer

The publisher, PDF 4–5 / preliminary alif–ba, presents the book as a defence of revelation against philosophical and theological opponents. It acknowledges Salim's pioneering edition while explaining that clearer manuscript images revealed consequential errors. The highlighted intercession example is a change from reference to God's messenger to asking God. This is the publisher's appraisal and account of the variant, not an independent manuscript finding by this study.

The complete introduction signed by Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman Al Saad ends at PDF 10 / preliminary zay, dated 4 Muharram 1423. It quotes al-Bazzar's report of Ibn Taymiyya's preference for work on religious foundations, then stresses the continuing religious importance of those foundations through Abraham, Qur'anic instruction and the Gabriel report. Its strong theological praise, adverse descriptions of opponents and recommendations for study remain the introducer's voice. The small footnote at PDF 9 / preliminary waw questions a transmitted report's authentication; the introduction is not uniformly an uncritical reproduction of its citations.

## Editors and their framing

The typeset signatures at PDF 15 / printed 8 and PDF 49 / printed 42 directly confirm Abu Abd Allah Sayyid ibn Abbas al-Julaymi and Abu Muadh Ayman ibn Arif al-Dimashqi. Arabic signatures: أبو عبد الله سيد بن عباس الجليمي; أبو معاذ أيمن بن عارف الدمشقي. This direct confirmation supplements the prior catalogue identification and resolves the difficulty of reading their ornate title-page names.

PDF 12–15 / printed 5–8 supplies a confessional introduction explaining why the editors undertook the edition. PDF 16–22 / printed 9–15 gives a compact biography: family/naming traditions, migration, learning, teachers, pupils, writings, death and reception. These are modern statements with citations, not separately verified historical research here. The distinction matters where reported exceptional memory, numbers of writings, funeral attendance or favourable reception are invoked.

## Attribution and title

PDF 23–25 / printed 16–18 argues for attribution through references in Ibn Taymiyya's other works, early biographical sources and stylistic consistency. The editors discuss an erased name in the manuscript opening replaced with Ibn Salah, reject that attribution through the incompatible personal names and internal references, and offer possible hostile or protective motives for the alteration. The motives are expressly possibilities, not established history. Their explanation of 'Safadiyya' associates the question with Safad and the convention of naming responses after their originating locality.

PDF 26–30 / printed 19–23 offers a useful whole-work outline. The editors distinguish the direct answer concerning reduction of prophetic miracles to psychic powers, occupying roughly the first third, from the larger remaining discussions of philosophers and their positions. This is not the same division as the electronic first/second printed-part colophon. They include prophecy and causal powers, jinn and angels, the relation of God and the world, religious and mystical claims, interpretation, universals, beginningless events, practical/theoretical wisdom, the virtues, post-mortem desire and happiness. This broad outline warns against reducing the book to a single metaphysical proof. The footnote on their preferred Nubuwwat edition is an editorial claim and does not independently settle another edition's completeness.

## The manuscript and editorial intervention

PDF 32–34 / printed 25–27 describes one rare manuscript, located in the Selim Aga library in Istanbul, Pertev Pasha section, manuscript 358. The editors give 212 pages and dimensions 26 × 36. They consider it to have belonged to Ibn Taymiyya or at least to pupils, and identify marginal additions as his handwriting. This study has not independently authenticated the handwriting, ownership or dating. They describe clear pointed naskh, approximately twenty lines per page and approximately fifteen words per line, quires, collation marks and additions. They also report disordered continuation locations and say they signal these in the notes. Thus physical manuscript order, reconstructed reading order and modern pagination must not be conflated.

The plates at PDF 35–39 / printed 28–32 show a cover, opening pages and ending pages; seeing them does not establish a full manuscript collation or reading. Their visual existence corroborates the presence of marginal material but not every editorial identification.

PDF 40–47 / printed 33–40 gives the reasons for the edition and two complete illustrative lists: 39 examples classified as materially affecting meaning, followed by 55 lesser omitted/misread-word examples. The editors attribute earlier problems to typography, manuscript-image quality and unreadable passages; they also criticise aspects of the older edition's report verification while acknowledging its contribution. The numbered correction register is separately preserved with mapping status to the unchanged electronic base. Classification as 'lesser' is the editors' judgement: some items still matter for conceptual or historical interpretation when encountered outside their context.

PDF 48–49 / printed 41–42 states the working method: transcribe and collate the manuscript; indicate manuscript numbering in notes; supply Qur'anic references; source and assess reports outside the two Sahih collections; explain rare terms and selected issues; identify people and groups; trace cited passages and the author's cross-references; and correct what they judge certain manuscript errors, noting this in the apparatus. Most importantly, numerous manuscript-margin additions and corrections are incorporated without individual notice, as are modern punctuation and spelling conventions. Consequently the 94 listed examples are neither an exhaustive apparatus nor a complete account of intervention. Nor can a matching selected line certify an entire electronic section.

## Opening control

PDF 52 / printed 45 contains the invocation and question; the note marks the restored Ibn Taymiyya attribution and repeats possible motives for the manuscript-name alteration. PDF 53 is blank. The answer begins at PDF 54 / printed 47. The question's framing and the author's answer remain distinct. The new edition supplies substantial modern notes on named groups on printed 47; these are not part of Ibn Taymiyya's initial answer.

## Limits retained

No denied older-edition object was retried. The two available printed editions are different publications; this does not make them independent manuscript witnesses. The received electronic text is fully studied by contiguous range readers, while printed-body controls remain bounded and explicitly enumerated. No complete reading of either scanned edition, complete manuscript word-by-word reading, independent authentication of all reports, or exhaustive resolution of all variants is asserted.


Bibliographic precision: a final enlarged reinspection of the typeset signature at PDF 49 / printed 42 confirms the nisba الجليمي (with jim). Earlier preparation references to الحليمي / al-Halimi are superseded. The Arabic signature is authoritative; the English transliteration follows the unvocalised name.
