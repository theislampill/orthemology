# Finite-horizon endpoint-contamination crossover

Author ordinary theorem, version 1, 2026-10-01. This refines the accepted same-interface finite-before/infinite-after theorem. It does not claim historical novelty and does not relabel predecessor results as new.

## 1. Actual experiment and simultaneous optimum

Fix the inherited literal loss with 0<epsilon<=1/4. Let 0<a_(i+1)<=a_i/2, a_0<=1/2 and positive weights w_i summing to one. Write mu=sum_i w_i delta_(a_i). For 0<=theta<=1 let mu_theta=theta delta_0+(1-theta)mu. Every experiment uses the identical EndpointProcess sample-space type, a single fixed latent parameter, independent private probability seed and infinite uniform innovations, with Bernoulli receipts generated by thresholding. A report sees exactly the complete length-n word and seed, and is compulsory at every n>=1.

C_N(theta) denotes the infimum of the actual expected number of literal failures at n=1,...,N over all measurable real report families. The inherited literal geometry makes the supported hypotheses, INCLUDING zero, mutually incompatible for an accepted report. Each has a coherent accepting centre report. For every finite word, the unnormalised positive-parameter scores form a nonnegative summable sequence tending to zero and have an attained maximum. Adding the zero score preserves attainment. A smallest maximizing index, with a fixed tie rule for zero, gives ONE deterministic measurable policy simultaneously attaining every stage and every horizon. Thus no exchange of horizonwise policies or unjustified infimum/sum interchange is used.

The all-policy bound is pointwise in the independent seed, so randomisation cannot improve it. Finite word laws and the connection to actual counts are supplied by the inherited actual-process construction, not assumed as a new risk identity. Classical maximization here is an existence theorem, not an effective exact-real implementation.

## 2. Exact increment for arbitrary weights

Put s=1-theta and

    M_n=max_i w_i(1-a_i)^n.

For every N>=1,

    C_N(theta)=s C_N(0)+D_N(theta),
    D_N(theta)=sum_(n=1)^N min(theta,s M_n).             (1)

Only the all-zero word has nonzero likelihood under parameter zero. On that word, let A_n=sum_i w_i(1-a_i)^n. Its old failure mass is A_n-M_n. Its new failure mass is theta+s A_n-max(theta,s M_n). Their difference after scaling the old mass by s is

    theta+s M_n-max(theta,s M_n)=min(theta,s M_n).

Every other exact word contributes precisely its old optimal mass times s. Summing the actual word masses proves (1). No power-weight form or posterior unimodality is used. The formula still applies when the uncontaminated lifetime optimum is infinite, since every finite-horizon quantity is finite.

Consequently

    0<=D_N(theta)<=theta N,
    |C_N(theta)-C_N(0)|<=theta N.                       (2)

The second follows from C_N(0)<=N and C_N(theta)-C_N(0)=D_N-theta C_N(0). It is TOTAL cost continuity, while D_N is the scaled-baseline increment. Total cost need not increase monotonically with theta: theta=1 is the perfectly known endpoint and has zero positive-time cost.

## 3. An exact clipping time

For 0<theta<1, define

    T_theta=max( {0} union
       { log(s w_i/theta)/[-log(1-a_i)] : s w_i>theta } ).

The displayed set is finite because w_i->0. All scores are nonincreasing in n. Thus min(theta,s M_n)=theta for integer n<=T_theta and equals s M_n for integer n>T_theta. At equality both expressions agree. With m=floor(T_theta),

    D_N(theta)=theta min(N,m)+s sum_(n=m+1)^N M_n,

where a sum with its lower bound above N is zero. This is an exact breakpoint, rather than a universal claim that crossover always occurs at 1/theta.

In the p=1 case below, theta T_theta<=s/[exp(1)Z]. No matching positive uniform lower bound exists for the fixed exponential-square support: set t_i=sqrt(a_i a_(i+1)) and theta_i=t_i/(Z+t_i). Then all a_j>t_i satisfy a_j>=a_i, and x->log(x/t_i)/x is decreasing for x>=a_i once a_i/t_i>exp(1). Hence

    theta_i T_(theta_i)
      <=[1/(Z+t_i)] sqrt(r_i) log(1/r_i)/2 ->0.

This demonstrates why sparse gaps matter to finite-horizon visibility.

## 4. Evaluated structural law for p=1 weights

Now assume w_i=a_i/Z, Z=sum_i a_i, and let B=C_infinity(0)<infinity. This finiteness is equivalent to the accepted spacing condition sum r_i log(1/r_i)<infinity. For 0<theta<1 put

    t=theta Z/s,
    L_N(t)=(s/Z) sum_(a_i<=t) (1-a_i)[1-(1-a_i)^N],
    K(N,t)=#{i:1/N<=a_i<=t}.

K is finite. The first structural sandwich is

    L_N(t)<=C_N(theta)
       <=s C_N(0)+L_N(t)+(s/Z)(2+4log2).               (3)

The stronger upper version retains C_N(0); replacing it by B gives a uniform-in-N bound.

### Direct lower bound

On an all-zero word, each small-atom score s(a_i/Z)(1-a_i)^n with a_i<=t is at most theta. If the winning score is the endpoint, all small scores are failures. If a large atom wins, all small scores are again failures. If a small atom ties or wins, the endpoint score is at least that score; the endpoint plus the other small scores still totals at least all small scores. Hence the optimal all-zero failure mass is at least the entire small-atom mass. Summing n=1,...,N gives L_N(t) by the exact geometric series. This proof neither invokes unimodality nor subtracts an infinite baseline quantity.

### Head upper bound

For nonnegative scores,

    min(t,max_i x_i)<=sum_i min(t,x_i).

For a_i<=t, x_i(n)=a_i(1-a_i)^n is unclipped and contributes exactly (1-a_i)[1-(1-a_i)^N]. For a_i>t, the decreasing function f(x)=min(t,a_i exp(-a_i x)) satisfies

    sum_(n>=1) min(t,a_i(1-a_i)^n)
      <=integral_(0,infinity) f(x) dx
      =(t/a_i)[1+log(a_i/t)],

using 1-a_i<=exp(-a_i). Place each head atom in the half-open band [2^k t,2^(k+1)t), k>=0. Half separation permits at most one atom per band (the strict upper endpoint handles equality). Its contribution is at most 2^(-k)[1+(k+1)log2]. Therefore the total head contribution is at most

    sum_(k>=0)2^(-k)[1+(k+1)log2]=2+4log2.

Combine this with (1) to obtain the upper half of (3). This head bound is uniform in theta and the support gaps.

### Head/tail conversion to a support count

Write Q_N(t)=Z L_N(t)/s. Then

    K(N,t)-2<=Q_N(t)<=K(N,t)+2.                       (4)

For the upper bound, every active atom 1/N<=a_i<=t contributes at most one. Atoms a_i<1/N contribute at most N a_i. The half-separated tail has sum below 2/N, so the total extra contribution is below two.

For the lower bound, discard all a_i<1/N. Each active atom's deficit from one is

    1-(1-a_i)[1-(1-a_i)^N]=a_i+(1-a_i)^(N+1)
      <=a_i+exp(-N a_i).

The first sum is at most Z<=1. Reading the active atoms in increasing order, their N a_i values are at least 1,2,4,..., so the second sum is at most sum_(k>=0) exp(-2^k)<1. For example 2^k>=k+1 gives the upper bound 1/(exp(1)-1)<1. Hence the total deficit is below two. The two one-sided estimates are different; no cancellation of finite-truncation errors is assumed.

Equations (3)-(4) imply the explicit uniform bound on TOTAL optimum

    (s/Z)[K(N,t)-2]<=C_N(theta)
      <=s B+(s/Z)[K(N,t)+4+4log2].                    (5)

A negative lower expression is harmless and can be replaced by zero. These constants are coarse but do not depend on theta, N or the component gaps beyond the stated half separation.

## 5. Exponential-square prior: a square-root-log law

For a_i=exp(-(i+1)^2), let

    A(N)=floor(sqrt(log N)),
    H(t)=max(1,ceil(sqrt(max(0,log(1/t))))).

Then the exact integer count is

    K(N,t)=max(0,A(N)-H(t)+1).

This includes equality at a support point and covers t>=a_0 and N=1. By (5), for every FIXED 0<theta<1,

    lim_(N->infinity) C_N(theta)/sqrt(log N)=(1-theta)/Z.

Jointly in theta and N, the integer count describes the crossover with a bounded additive error:

    C_N(theta)=(s/Z)K(N,theta Z/s)+E_N(theta),
    -2s/Z<=E_N(theta)<=sB+s(4+4log2)/Z.

Thus if the difference of square-root-log cutoffs tends to infinity, the support-count leading term is asymptotically exact. This is stronger than a claim only at a fixed contamination size. It does not say the one-step risk is smooth across long support gaps.

An explicit accepted baseline upper bound is available from the formal predecessor constants:

    B<=256 S/Z,
    S=exp(-3)[3/(1-exp(-2))+2exp(-2)/(1-exp(-2))^2].

Numerically this conservative bound is about126.42; the decimal is illustrative and not used in the proof.

## 6. Same-interface noncommuting limits

For every fixed N, (2) gives C_N(theta)->C_N(0) as theta decreases to zero. Nevertheless, for every theta in (0,1), the preceding rate gives C_infinity(theta)=infinity, while C_infinity(0)=B<infinity. Therefore

    lim_(theta down to0) lim_(N->infinity) C_N(theta)=infinity,
    lim_(N->infinity) lim_(theta down to0) C_N(theta)=B.

This is a source-specific quantitative continuity/discontinuity statement for the same passive reporting experiment. It is not a generic minimax interchange theorem for controlled observations and not an almost-sure infinite-error claim.

## 7. Failed shortcuts and limits

With support (1/2,1/4,1/8) and weights (9/20,1/10,9/20), the length-one zero-word scores are (9/40,3/40,63/160). Their total-minus-maximum is3/10, but the adjacent-minimum sum is3/20. Hence arbitrary-weight posterior unimodality and the adjacency identity are false. The exact contamination increment survives because it uses the actual maximum directly. Finite controls include this rejection and nonuniform weights.

On an infinite support approaching zero, raw nearest-support estimation at empirical frequency zero has no minimizing positive support point. A policy based on that unavailable argmin is not well defined. Neither the classical MAP policy nor the explicit cluster policy silently uses it.

The source-count approximation requires p=1 and a finite uncontaminated B. The exact increment does not. No classification of all arbitrary weights, no finite-time certification of the correct prior, no runtime theorem, and no historical-priority claim is made.
