## Blocking findings

### DN-F01 — additive score integrability was allowed to sound like objective normativity

The graph theorem concerns an integer-valued registered increment field. Exactness gives a path-independent additive potential. It does not establish that the increments are correct, truth-linked, fitting, authoritative, or even genuinely normative. Rename the result and make this ceiling part of the theorem statement.

### DN-F02 — transition-registry completeness and stability were not explicit

A potential on the registered graph says nothing about omitted revision routes, changing edge labels, hidden states, or target drift. Add finite, static, complete-for-the-declared-scope, stable-label guards. Dynamic drift remains a countermodel.

### DN-F03 — disconnected components and cross-component comparability require separate treatment

Each connected component admits an independently shifted potential. There is no cross-component comparison unless additional edges or calibration are supplied. State the theorem componentwise and uniqueness per connected component.

### DN-F04 — additive cardinal representation was conflated with weaker ordinal ranking

A finite acyclic strict-improvement relation admits a topological ranking even when no declared additive edge-increment potential exists. Conversely, an exact potential may have zero or negative increments. Separate additive exactness, strict-direction acyclicity, and objective ranking.

### DN-F05 — numerical scale and declared labels remain authority-bearing inputs

Changing the registered increments can change exactness and the potential. The theorem does not discover a canonical norm scale from bare revision topology. Preserve calibration/source as an open burden.

### DN-F06 — the primary “cycle” test reused the same solver

The executable result is useful regression evidence but not an independent algorithmic corroboration. Require a fresh spanning-tree/fundamental-cycle implementation and random generated/perturbed systems.

### DN-F07 — Candidate E and proper-function ancestry were not frozen

Freeze exact hashes and authority roles for the Candidate E formal reconstruction/countermodels and the proper-function baseline. The new theorem is a central application, not a historical Candidate E result.
