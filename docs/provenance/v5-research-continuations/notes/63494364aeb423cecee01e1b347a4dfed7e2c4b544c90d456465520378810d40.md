## Semantic review

1. Recurs means infinitely often, with the atTop filter on natural time. The exact recurrent set contains precisely those actions. Finiteness of the action type supplies both an infinitely recurring action and a common finite time after which all nonrecurrent actions have disappeared. A separate Nonempty A assumption is unnecessary because the supplied total stream already yields an action.

2. A recurrent action belongs to every finite target G that the stream eventually remains inside. This uses the intersection of an infinite occurrence set with an eventual-good set. It does not assert that all actions ever seen are good.

3. The exact recurrent-support event has a pointwise finite set of times outside its support, independently of any model law. Its equality with the countable union of tailEvent X U N is valid: a tail event combines the EXACT recurrent-support event with the deterministic-time restriction. The selected N is not a stopping time, and no common N for all paths or all models is claimed.

4. Recurrence is a countable intersection of countable unions of coordinate equalities. Coordinate measurability and measurable singletons suffice. Exact finite recurrent-support events and deterministic tail events are consequently measurable. The sample space Ω need not be countable.

5. Positive support selection uses the finite partition into exact recurrent sets and a probability law. A positive cell intersects the almost-sure eventual-good event; one path in that intersection identifies a nonempty support contained in G. The core theorem deliberately does not require measurable X: arbitrary-set measure subadditivity and restriction are enough for this assertion about outer measure. The explicitly measurable version separately supplies measurability, so the omitted hypothesis is not covertly assumed.

6. A positive exact cell is the countable union of its deterministic tail cells. Therefore at least one deterministic tail cell is positive. This inference does not turn path-dependent eventual behavior into a uniform almost-sure time bound.

7. The final abstract_self_verifying_support theorem has one common stream map X, a family of probability laws μ θ, modelwise almost-sure eventual target membership, an arbitrary relation Agree θ η U, and an EXPLICIT transfer hypothesis for every specified tailEvent X U N. It uses a θ-positive tail cell, transfers positivity to η, and intersects with η's almost-sure target event to obtain U ⊆ G η. All steps match the exact theorem type.

8. Agree is not defined here as equality of observation laws. Neither the tail transfer nor the common-policy realization is derived in this module. The result is an abstract necessity lemma under that transfer contract. The separate generic-query work and a future original-policy law-equivalence proof cannot be inferred from this seal.

## Sharp independent controls

Eighteen kernel-checked reviewer theorems cover:

- Constant streams have exactly their singleton recurrent support
- On an infinite action alphabet, the stream x(n)=n has no recurrent action, exposing the essential finite-alphabet boundary
- Arbitrarily delayed constant tails all have the same recurrent support but admit no deterministic tail time uniform across the path family
- A tail merely confined to a superset U need not accept every action in U; exact recurrent support is essential to the target-subset conclusion
- A nonempty exact event can have zero probability, and the zero measure has no positive cell
- Two Dirac laws, with the common stream X(ω,n)=ω and targets G θ={θ}, each satisfy modelwise almost-sure success. They have no common nonempty acceptable support. The false-model singleton tail has probability one under its own law and zero under the other. Thus positivity transfer is a substantive premise, not a consequence of the other displayed assumptions

The corresponding independent counterexamples explain why these are genuine contract boundaries.
