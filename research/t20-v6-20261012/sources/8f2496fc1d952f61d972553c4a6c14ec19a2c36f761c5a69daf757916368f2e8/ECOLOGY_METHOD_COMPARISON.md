## Comparison of the five proposals

### 1. Parallel programmes, faithful two-way transport, retained failed mappings

**Already covered:** the integrated research prompt preserves lane ontologies/evidence types, demands materially different rival cores, requires typed interpretation maps and guarded transfers, and retains nonidentity and failed candidates [P, lines 116–172, 245–283]. The flywheel explicitly returns failures and burdens to originating lanes without automatically transferring proof or authority [F, lines 3–37, 43–45].

**Useful additional precision:** independently developing programmes need not first accept one common core. Compare their interfaces after preserving their own problems, sources, constraints and development histories. This is stronger than running different presentations of a shared design. “Bidirectional” should mean separately justified transfers in the directions actually used, not assumed equivalence. Preserve a failed mapping as a scoped obstruction, with the failed premise and separating case, rather than as mere lack of resemblance. The current causal-abstraction report already exemplifies such a retained rejection [L3, lines 17–37].

**Required revision:** forward preservation alone does not transport every negative claim. To carry a source counterexample to premises C and conclusion Q through a map f, one needs target-premise preservation and a guarantee that source ¬Q yields target ¬Q. A sufficient condition for the latter is conclusion reflection: Q_target(f(x)) implies Q_source(x). Coverage or lifting conditions may also be needed for claims about all target cases. Two maps alone prove neither inversion nor equivalence.

### 2. Research state → result → obligations → response → observations → new states

**Already covered:** Decision 0033 separates before/after states, burdens, selected operation, evidence, reread and recursive posture; it distinguishes episode correction from slower standard revision [D33, lines 23–44]. The research and formalization flywheels already return results to revised questions and candidates [F; M, lines 1921–1983]. H §7 separates historical ecology from prospective feasible inquiry, including future value that equal correct outputs need not preserve; §11 distinguishes present from future adequacy [H, lines 410–502, 899–951].

**Useful additional precision:** retain this compact sequence as a research-level reading, not a new runtime or schema. A local theorem or exhausted finite investigation can close while the larger programme changes state. “Latent significance” means a retained result may acquire demonstrable value through later connections; it does not grant significance, novelty or validation in advance. The existing portfolio and anti-microresult rules already resist one small theorem becoming total closure [M, lines 2319–2346, 2538–2591]. The new clarification should preserve those substantive ambitions without requiring one terminal theorem for all inquiry.

### 3. Finite exhaustion and fair effective proof search

**Partly covered:** finite checks, proof search and conditional finite-horizon preservation are present, with explicit nonclaims [P, lines 236–260; T, lines 13–21; H, lines 925–951].

**Genuinely missing as an explicit clause in the inspected method owners:** a qualified search guarantee. A declared finite, exhaustively enumerable investigation with terminating case tests is exhaustible. In a fixed effective calculus, a fair exhaustive enumeration with terminating proof checking eventually reaches any existing finite proof. Soundness is additionally needed to infer semantic truth from acceptance; completeness is needed for any wider claim that every truth sought has such a proof. Neither guarantee supplies a practical cost bound.

Do not strengthen this to universal research termination or philosophical decidability. Failure to find a proof is not a disproof. Finite-countermodel enumeration finds countermodels only where suitable finite witnesses exist and are effectively recognizable; it is not a general semidecision procedure for every non-theorem or semantic counterexample. Timeout, finite test saturation, and lack of a currently available source answer are different outcomes.

### 4. Independent structural convergence and the natural-joint hypothesis

**Already covered:** exact reductions, typed common models, semantically specified shared invariants, guarded transfers and matched countermodels are existing convergence gates. Vocabulary, geometry and performance similarity are insufficient [P, lines 108–129; F, lines 63–66]. Decision 0032 separates source-independence support from warrant; counts and propagation do not create truth [D32, lines 29–44]. Prior-art, cold audit and fresh review are already required, and external review plus replication packages are already programme outputs [P, lines 245–260; M, lines 1731–1789].

**Useful additional precision:** structural convergence across genuinely independently developed programmes can defeasibly support a natural-joint hypothesis when the correspondence preserves the consequential permissions and prohibitions, rather than merely shared successes. Keep development independence, independent checking, external reproduction, and theorem originality distinct. Different writing styles, models or teams are not sufficient evidence of independent origins. Common source, shared training/design assumptions, selected examples and mappings engineered after seeing both outcomes remain alternative explanations. Natural-joint support is a candidate interpretation, not numerical identity, ontology selection or metaphysical proof; the tensor programme already forbids those promotions [T, lines 64–79].

### 5. Recognition/source-guided, joint-first, recursive computational audit

**Already covered:** the source-to-formal-to-rival-to-renewed-source loop is explicit [M, lines 1948–1959]. The flywheel lets source lanes supply typed premises, and the integrated prompt lets proper-function accounts supply normative premises [F, lines 8–11, 20–26; P, lines 198–234]. Theology and philosophy of mind therefore are not merely downstream application recipients. Current source-motivated beginningless controls already constrain an overstrong inductive formalization [L4, lines 82–111].

**Useful additional precision:** make the reciprocal contribution explicit: source and mind/theology inquiries may generate candidate invariants, distinctions, prohibitions and counterexamples that revise the shared mathematical interface. “Recognition-first” or “joint-first” is a useful priority where it means beginning from a discriminating source/problem structure before prescribing a total system. It does not make recognition infallible, source attribution equal world truth, or formal compliance establish the extracted invariant's philosophical warrant. Recursive computational audit remains fallible and open to criticism of its own specification.

## Retain only these deltas

1. Preserve independently developing programme interfaces; justify each actual direction of transport, including reflection/lifting where the claim requires it.
2. State finite-exhaustion and fair-proof-search guarantees with their effective-domain, calculus and witness conditions; forbid universal termination and countermodel-search inflation.
3. Distinguish development independence, checking independence, reproduction and novelty when interpreting convergence; test consequential prohibitions and rival explanations.
4. Explicitly allow theology/mind/source work to supply candidate structural constraints and later significance to emerge, while retaining existing source-world and proof-authority boundaries.

These are candidate methodological clarifications. They warrant no new tranche, reporting machinery, automatic cross-programme synthesis, implementation or premature T20 closure.
