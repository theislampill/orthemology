## General interface

Recipient i has an evidence-compatible nonempty class E_i of possible histories. Actual history belongs to E_i; the model does not derive this soundness condition from a certificate string. A legitimate repair action must preserve the reference, achieve exact target fidelity, retain custody obligations, be currently authorized for that actor and version, and execute through the declared mechanism.

A copied proof establishes its conclusion at recipient j only when its premise interpretation is justified there. One sound sufficient transport condition is that a source-to-recipient relation R covers every recipient-compatible history and preserves the claimed judgment on related histories. Merely preserving judgments on the histories already matched by R does not guarantee a claim when j admits additional compatible possibilities. Backward coverage is not necessary for a fixed conclusion independently warranted in unmatched recipient histories. It is necessary and sufficient for uniform transfer over every predicate pair subject to R-preservation; that exact claim explicitly includes the relation-image predicate as a separating test.

Truth throughout an evidence class is called robust evidence-relative validity here. It is not an analysis of ordinary fallible epistemic warrant. Actual-world truth additionally needs the actual history to belong to the declared class, and the actor's entitlement to use the class is a separate warrant burden.

For the implemented action layer, commands bind source target identity (including its commit), criterion ID, expected draft revision, authorization epoch, actor, destination, operation scope, complete payload, observation time and lease end. Current time, the active grant and revocation state are trusted State inputs checked at the atomic intervention step. The pure model has no commitment-root or support-ID fields in its Command type and implements no root quorum or signed commitment. Those additional mechanism bindings belong to the external dynamic-lane interface described in the support ledger. A guard is declared support; its trust and availability are not derived from copying a signed string.

## Model scope and observations

- Immutable source bytes and a stable interpretation of byte equality.
- Two recipients, two version/authorization epochs, and explicit valid/revoked grant states.
- Draft bytes may have extra trailing newlines or ordinary non-target mutations.
- One command changes only the recipient's derived draft and advances its local version; original source and prior audit history are retained.
- Rejecting a stale or unauthorized command leaves the state unchanged.
- A one-step success theorem assumes the guard and its checked inputs are correct at the atomic step. It does not assume that the recipient knows every hidden field before submitting the command.
- Safe rejection is not restoration. Eventual restoration additionally requires an available authorized attempt during a sufficiently stable window.
- Lossy recoding is assessed by fibers of the representation map, rather than by agreement of normalized outputs.
