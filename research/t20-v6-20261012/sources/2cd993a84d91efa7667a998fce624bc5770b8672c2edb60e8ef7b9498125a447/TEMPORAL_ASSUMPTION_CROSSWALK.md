# Successive addition and the inherited beginningless control

This is our analytical crosswalk, not a new formal proof, new model, physical possibility claim, or whole-book judgment. The selected source locus is Ameri, *Proofs of God*, printed354–358; the actual-infinity objections at350–354 are a different branch.

## The premise at issue

The selected argument treats a beginningless history of successive events as an unattainable completed accumulation. Its force cannot be assessed simply by pointing to a finite consistent simulation. The relevant extra claim concerns which infinite actual histories are metaphysically admissible.

An elementary comparison isolates that claim. For every integer t, let

P(t) = {s in Z : s ≤ t}.

Then P(t+1) = P(t) union {t+1}. Thus every transition adds one new member. No P(t) is a finite initial history. The recurrence alone consequently does not entail generation from an initial finite stage. This observation does not show that the represented past exists, is physically possible, or adequately explains its own actuality. It only prevents silently adding an initial-stage premise to a local succession rule.

Likewise, a beginningless index order does not contain a time called minus infinity from which an agent must depart. Any argument requiring traversal from such a departure needs that interpretation defended. A stronger principle banning a completed beginningless history may still be proposed, but it is stronger than finite-addition reasoning and remains unadjudicated in this screen.
