# Orthemology T20 — the reconciled plain-language field guide

## Start here

T20 is best understood as an investigation of **what a warranted correction has to preserve**. A representation may be good enough to give today’s answer but inadequate for tomorrow’s audit. An equation may have a solution that no genuine probability law represents. A calculus may be sound yet lack a rule needed for a particular construction. A philosophical conclusion may have a surviving affirmative argument even after another route to it fails.

The point of bringing the fronts together is not to say that they are all “about information”. It is to identify the concrete objects, premises and operations that one front can supply to another.

This guide has two parts. The first explains the programme and its cross-contributions. The second is a packet-by-packet atlas of the frozen delivered corpus, with source-owned excerpts and links to complete unchanged documents. ## 1. What the different fronts are trying to accomplish

There are several programme axes in this guide. These are editorial organising categories, not a replacement for Orthemology’s canonical issue numbering.

**Foundational explanation:** what objective orthability, productive dependence, necessity, sourcehood, common source, Knowledge, Will and Wisdom can establish under their defended premises.

**Normative and epistemic warrant:** what fixes the relevant standard; why a source or interpretation should be accepted; what response a communication warrants; and when an argument survives criticism.

**Restoration and investigation:** how to recover a hidden or damaged structure, preserve provenance, choose an informative observation, and account for shared proof or experiment cost.

**Numerical representation:** when a tensor, moment sequence or compressed vector identifies a genuine state, when it can be recovered effectively, and what future operations it supports.

**Corrective dynamics:** how posterior and geometric states evolve; when state validity survives; and how continuous and discrete corrections preserve the declared obligations.

**Formal semantics and type completion:** what the original calculus proves, what additional rules are needed, whether they coexist soundly, and how finite effective certificates relate to a broader semantics.

None of these is merely an appendix to the others. For example, a gradient calculation needs a valid represented state, an objective with the right interpretation, and a current source context. A source argument can benefit from finite proof support and selective audit repair without becoming a probability distribution itself.

## 2. The unifying question: adequate for which next operation?

Consider two records that display the same accepted conclusion. One rests on source A and the other on source B. If the next operation is “print the accepted conclusion”, they may be equivalent. If the next operation is “withdraw source A”, they may not be.

The same pattern recurs elsewhere. Two moment records may match all the measurements retained so far but differ on a later polynomial test. Two posterior models may agree on a conditional answer but disagree about the chance of obtaining that observation. Two proof trees may establish the same statement while using resources with different opportunities for sharing. Two type systems may assign the same intended meaning to a construction while allowing different derivations.

An equivalence must therefore name its contract:

\[
\text{same for the outputs and future operations we have declared}.
\]

The earlier synthesis’s sidecar construction addresses this constructively. Begin with the desired compressed record, then retain the smallest further distinctions required by the declared observations, actions, costs and transitions. In a finite model, partition refinement can identify a coarsest faithful refinement.

The new research in this packet makes one especially important instance explicit: source revision after geometric or stochastic state evolution. It proves when a historical likelihood can be transported to the current representation, what supplement repairs a failure, and when source revision changes the corrective dynamics enough to require a different replay contract.

## 3. The early strands: calibration, hidden structure and experimental cost

Early recovery and calibration work should not be lost underneath the later tensor notation. It asks a practical epistemic question: when a representation fits what we measured, what reason do we have to think it recovered the relevant structure?

A fit can be underdetermined. Different hidden arrangements can produce the same present observations. An additional observation can separate them, but its value depends on what it reveals, its probability, its cost, and whether other evidence or experiments can be shared.

This develops into three later distinctions.

First, **identification is not effectivity**. A mathematical uniqueness argument can establish that one hidden object fits the complete record without giving a finite, computably controlled route to recovering it from approximate data.

Second, **a present fit is not continuation fidelity**. A compressed object may match current outputs and fail after the next transformation, observation, or audit.

Third, **local cheapness is not global cheapness**. A proof or experiment that is individually more expensive can reduce total work when its supporting resources are shared with other required conclusions.

These distinctions explain why the early strands contribute directly to finite moment recovery, separating projections, proof-support catalogues, inquiry value, and the external representation-paper comparison. They are not just preliminary motivation.

## 4. Proof support, provenance and efficient investigation

A claim, a proof of the claim, the sources used by that proof, and the cost of obtaining them are different objects.

For withdrawal, it matters whether at least one adequate proof survives. For shared-resource cost, it matters which resources are reused across different proofs. For cyclic rules, it matters how a finite proof catalogue or fixed-point construction is justified rather than assumed. For collective inquiry, it matters whether apparent independent evidence shares a hidden origin.

The useful global object can therefore be a family of support witnesses, not merely one cheapest proof attached to each conclusion. Discarding a locally expensive proof can discard the globally cheap joint solution when that proof shares material with another obligation.

The newer source-revision interface adds another dependency class. A source can justify not only a belief but also the objective, metric, interpretation rule or permission under which a correction is performed. Revoking that source may require recomputing those dependent parts, while leaving genuinely independent arguments intact.

Theorem F gives an exact finite DAG replay rule. Recompute the descendants of changed primitive inputs and reuse the unaffected nodes, assuming the dependency declarations are complete and the cache is current. Theorem H gives the corresponding proof-support rule: loss of one represented warrant does not imply loss of every warrant.

These are useful complements to RLGWO-style bounded work. They identify a repair frontier and its closure evidence rather than treating activity, an issue filing, or a revised summary as implementation credit.

## 5. The foundational progression must remain dated

The foundational reports do not form one timeless paragraph. Some earlier appraisals express restraint, identify a failed inference, or isolate a premise. Later investigations can develop an affirmative argument, recover a source-supported conclusion, or narrow the force of the earlier objection.

The supplied record includes a progression from provisional restraint to an affirmative target-local sourcehood/exclusivity argument. The appropriate synthesis preserves that progression. It does not freeze the first hesitation as the final verdict, nor rewrite the earlier argument as having proved what a later argument supplied.

The same principle applies to the Wisdom, repentance, responsive-receptivity, self-praise, authority and response work. A small discriminator can expose a confusion while remaining narrower than a subsequently developed source argument.

A complete guide must therefore answer four questions for a foundational result: what conclusion is appraised; what premises it uses; what the appraisal establishes at that scope; and whether a later packet changes that assessment. The atlas supplies original statements, and `Revision_Ledger.md` gathers explicit correction and supersession cues without converting them into an automatic philosophical verdict.

## 6. What the foundational fronts contribute to one another

### Sourcehood, necessity and a common source

Target-local source exclusivity is useful positive progress. It supplies a premise for a wider unification argument. It should not be confused either with no progress or with the unproved substitution of one common source into every local argument.

The new overlap theorem makes the next bridge exact. If each effect has a unique source of the specified adequate kind, adjacent effects have a common adequate source, and the adjacency relation connects the whole intended domain, then the local sources agree globally. The philosophical research must justify those overlap and coverage premises. Simply grouping several effects into one set does not create one individual source.

Necessity and attributes then contribute their own substantive content. A common label in a graph is not automatically a necessary being, an original productive source, or a bearer of the attributes under investigation.

### Wisdom and disclosed reasons

The relevant contrast is not just “good versus bad output”. An operative reason connects an act, circumstances, means and end in the way the argument actually uses. The ship, wall, mediation and self-praise reports concern source-disclosed or argued reasons, not merely a beneficial consequence left to interpretation.

Compressing these distinctions into a scalar utility can erase the very premises of an affirmative Wisdom argument. Conversely, a bare unintended-benefit counterexample does not refute an argument that additionally establishes disclosed intention and the relevant contextual relation.

### Responsive acts and essential perfection

A condition for a particular response is not automatically a condition from which the responder receives an essential faculty or perfection. The repentance and responsive-receptivity sequence requires this distinction to be preserved, together with the source argument that justifies it.

A minimal logical model can refute an invalid entailment between these two kinds of dependence. It does not replace the richer affirmative theological account. The foundational companion document explains how an argument’s surviving premises can be retained through a local correction.

### Authority and the force of a communication

An authoritative source, an authenticated utterance, the source’s adoption of the relevant content, the speech act’s force, and the recipient’s conditions are separate fields. “The source is authoritative” alone does not determine whether a particular response is obligatory, recommended, permitted or conditional.

Once those fields are adequately established, however, a positive conclusion about the fitting response can be warranted. A finite certificate can carry the declared chain while source-facing work establishes why its premises are accepted.

## 7. Tensors and moments: what is being represented?

A moment records an expectation of a product or power of coordinates. In one dimension, the sequence begins with total mass, mean, second moment, and so on. In several dimensions, mixed moments record interactions among coordinates. Organising them as tensors does not remove their interpretation as statements about a represented law.

There are several separate questions:

**Realisability:** is there any probability law with these moments?

**Identification or determinacy:** is there exactly one law in the specified class with the full record?

**Finite recovery:** does a stated finite record identify the object under a support cap or other structural restriction?

**Effective recovery:** is there an algorithm with justified bounds on how much data, precision and computation it needs?

**Continuation:** after a proposed update, do the new coordinates still represent an admissible law, and do they retain the distinctions needed for later operations?

The capped, determinate, exponential-tail, separating-projection and stationary-selection packets investigate different parts of this chain. Their assumptions are not interchangeable. A support cap is not a tail bound; a uniqueness theorem is not a modulus; and a projection must separate the relevant objects before it can support faithful reconstruction.

### A simple finite-support illustration

Within the class of laws having at most \(k\) atoms on the real line, matching moments of orders \(0\) through \(2k-1\) forces equality. The difference of two such laws is supported on at most \(2k\) points. Its vanishing moments form a Vandermonde system on those distinct points, forcing every signed weight to vanish.

This illustrates why an explicit finite-support class can make finite data sufficient. It does not say that the same finite moments identify an arbitrary law without that restriction, or that the recovery is numerically well-conditioned when atoms nearly collide.

The early hidden-structure question has now acquired precise mathematical premises.

## 8. Solvable moment equations are not automatically valid state dynamics

The initial-validity and positive-hierarchy sequence is a central negative/positive pair in T20. It investigates the possibility that formal moment equations evolve coordinates which no probability law represents, even though simple positivity checks or the complete initial record look satisfactory.

The key distinction is between positive coordinates and a positive polynomial functional. For a genuine law,

\[
\mathbb E[p(X)^2]\ge0
\]

for every polynomial \(p\). A single rigorously established negative value defeats realisability. Passing finitely many such tests establishes only the tested finite conditions unless an additional theorem makes them sufficient in the declared class.

The stronger sequence considers determinate initial data, what boundary jets or finite-order tests can miss, and concrete computable detection by a negative-square certificate. Its numerical evidence and its mathematical scope must be kept separate: an outward-interval certificate is stronger than a rounded numerical sample, but it is not thereby a Lean proof.

The positive continuation work is just as important. The coercive square-positivity, polynomial-functional, damping, cumulative-law, diffusion/killing and signed-control packets investigate conditions under which the formal evolution does stay attached to genuine laws. A corrected polynomial-functional packet belongs in the sequence alongside the earlier version; it should not be silently folded into the uncorrected claim.

The new audit-tail transfer theorem adds a direct connection to this positive branch. It tracks how a source reweighting consumes an exponential-envelope budget and how transport changes the remaining exponent. An explicit example preserves a genuine probability law under source withdrawal while losing its exponential-tail certificate. Validity of the law and membership in a particular effective recovery class therefore require separate status fields.

The contribution to the rest of the programme is a **state-validity gate**. An optimisation or inquiry result cannot legitimately use a probability state merely because its coordinate equations were solved. The needed realisability theorem or finite disqualifying witness has to travel with the state at the appropriate scope.

## 9. Geometry: there is actual constructive mathematics here

The later delivered material is not accurately described as merely proposing a geometric interface. It contains concrete posterior-geometry and gradient investigations, joint atomic state-and-weight work, boundary and complete-flow studies, radial/quartic preservation, constructive discretisation, and a full-law gradient/action/tail packet recovered from the Library.

This does not establish a universal Riemannian flow for every possible noetic structure. It establishes that the guide must distinguish **actual developed model classes** from the broader representation and integration questions that remain to be justified for a particular application.

### What a metric does

A metric tells the model how to measure an infinitesimal change. For an objective \(E\), the gradient depends on that metric. A change that is steep under one geometry need not be steep under another.

For retained linear coordinates \(y=Lx\) and a fine-state metric matrix \(G(x)\), the earlier synthesis identifies the reduced co-metric

\[
L G(x)^{-1}L^{\mathsf T}.
\]

If two fine states have the same retained coordinates but different values of this matrix, then the retained coordinates alone cannot carry one faithful gradient rule for every pulled-back objective. A justified leaf or context descriptor may repair the representation.

This connects interaction-preserving tensor records to geometry. Hidden interactions can affect how an apparently identical visible correction should proceed.

### A representative joint state-and-weight model

To see what the variables mean, consider positive weights \(w_i\), summing to one, and positions \(x_i\). For

\[
E(w,x)=\sum_iw_iV(x_i),
\]

a transport/Fisher-type product metric can yield

\[
\dot x_i=-\nabla V(x_i),\qquad
\dot w_i=-\alpha w_i\bigl(V(x_i)-\overline V\bigr),
\quad\overline V=\sum_iw_iV(x_i).
\]

For this representative normalisation,

\[
\frac{dE}{dt}
=-\sum_iw_i\|\nabla V(x_i)\|^2-\alpha\operatorname{Var}_w(V)\le0.
\]

Positions move and their weights change. This is more than changing a belief vector’s labels. The formula is an explanatory model, not an assertion that every T20 packet uses this exact metric coefficient or energy.

A corresponding law-level equation formally combines transport with a centred reweighting term. Its existence, realisability, tail preservation and discretisation still need the hypotheses supplied by the specific theorem. Writing the equation is not a substitute for them.

### Boundary behaviour matters

A metric can put a probability-simplex boundary at finite distance without a particular bounded-potential flow reaching it in finite time. Conversely, a numerical scheme can create a zero weight or leave an admissible domain even when the continuous flow preserves positivity.

The transport-boundary, complete-flow and discretisation work therefore performs a different job from merely identifying the gradient. It asks which trajectories and implementations actually preserve the intended state class.

This also connects to audits. Once a source update sets a state’s weight to zero and the origin record is discarded, removing that source cannot generally reconstruct the lost prior mass. A strict-positivity theorem or a preserved origin record can have an epistemic role as well as a geometric one.

## 10. Moving objectives, observation and correction

Observing evidence and moving a represented state are different operations. So are retaining the actual history, recomputing the state under revised evidence, and making a prospective correction from the present state.

The Gaussian observation/corrective-transition and posterior-gradient classification sequence belongs at this interface. A representation of an observation must preserve the probability and meaning of that observation. A corrective transition must preserve the dynamical and admissibility claims made about it. Identifying a useful relationship between them is not a licence to interchange them without checking the hypotheses.

A moving objective adds another term:

\[
\frac{d}{dt}E_t(x_t)=\partial_tE_t(x_t)+dE_t(x_t)[\dot x_t].
\]

The correction can be descending relative to the current objective while the objective itself changes. Thus a claim of improvement must record which standard and source context it uses.

The new audit theorem makes this concrete. A historical likelihood must be evaluated at its origin, or transported by the appropriate inverse/conditional-origin construction. If the evidence also justified the objective or metric, a retrospective repair can require replay of the corrective field. A one-atom gradient example proves that reweighting alone cannot always do that job.

The positive counterpart is a sensitivity bound: when the revised fields obey common regularity or contraction hypotheses, one can bound the difference between the reconstructions rather than simply declare replay an unbounded obligation.

## 11. Type completion: gap, extension, soundness, effectivity

The type-theoretic sequence needs a clear distinction between the original calculus and an explicitly extended calculus. The source record contains an original-gap/independence strand and a constructive extension strand, followed by joint structural, substitution, induction, computation, recursor-uniqueness, extraction and certificate work.

A proved gap in the original system is not disproved merely by adding a new rule. Equally, it should not be repeated as a permanent obstacle once a sound extension has supplied the relevant capability. Both results can be true and useful.

### Dependent pairs and induction

A dependent pair consists of a witness \(x:A\) together with data of a type \(B(x)\) depending on that witness. Eliminating such a pair requires respecting that dependence; it is not just erasing a tag from an ordinary Cartesian pair.

Induction is also stronger than a list of verified instances. Its dependent form must support the permitted family of conclusions. Substitution under dependent binders must preserve the typing and semantic relationships that the rule uses.

The packets labelled `E`, `HasE`, dependent substitution, mixed substitution, Sigma elimination and induction should be read using their own definitions. Those symbols are not silently replaced here by generic everyday notions of existence or evidence.

### Why joint soundness is a separate result

Two individually defensible extensions need not coexist safely merely because each was considered separately. The joint-completion and structural/mixed sequence therefore contributes more than two isolated positive examples: it addresses the rules together in their declared setting.

Computation rules, induction rules and recursor uniqueness also have different obligations. Existence of a recursor is not automatically its uniqueness, and a semantic interpretation does not by itself provide an effective syntactic checker.

### Semantics versus finite certificates

The FullPER/effective-boundary, raw-gap, source-certificate, serialisation, canonical-syntax, telescope and effective-presentation sequence separates broad semantic validity from an effective proof interface. A finite certificate for a particular derivation can be checkable even when there is no universal procedure deciding every statement in the broad semantics.

This is a constructive design pattern. Preserve the declared source code or syntax, the permitted transformations, the actual witness, and the conditions checked. Do not turn a checker for finite positive witnesses into a claim that it decides every semantic question.

Extraction fidelity then connects the type work to the rest of T20: does the encoded or extracted object preserve the operations and distinctions for which it is being used? This is the same concrete question that governs source-aware compression, though the mathematical objects and proof methods differ.

## 12. Inquiry value depends on more than the answer after an observation

An investigator choosing an action needs more than a table saying what to believe after each possible result. The probabilities of those results matter, as do the permitted actions, their costs, and what is retained for the next decision.

The normaliser in a Bayesian update is one place where this distinction becomes exact. Multiplying a likelihood by a constant can leave the posterior unchanged while changing the predictive weight of that observation in a calibrated model.

The correlated/anticorrelated two-source example in the new research strengthens the point. Two models can agree on each individual source’s expected factor while disagreeing on the expected product. A representation adequate for each isolated report may fail for their combination.

This connects inquiry, shared proof resources, and interaction-preserving geometry. The required state is not necessarily the whole history, but its sufficiency must be established for the joint contract rather than inferred from separate successful checks.

Approximation also has to name its error model. Small absolute prior error can become decisive after rare conditioning. A uniform bound on relative likelihood error supplies a different, positive guarantee: the new sharp theorem bounds posterior total variation by a hyperbolic tangent of the accumulated log-error budget. It does not apply through arbitrary zeros or undeclared policy changes.

## 13. The external weight-subspace comparison

An external neural-weight or low-dimensional representation paper can motivate a useful experiment, but it does not by itself validate a noetic representation.

The discriminating question is whether the compressed representation preserves the outputs and continuations actually required. Retaining a high percentage of present variance is a different criterion. A low-variance direction can become decisive after a later update or audit.

The earlier synthesis’s linear construction closes the required output space under the allowed update operators. This is a concrete way to test continuation fidelity. Its counterexample is a mathematical control, not an empirical finding about the particular networks in an uploaded paper.

The new audit-profile theorem provides a source-sensitive counterpart. A compressed state must retain enough information for the historical likelihood factors and their permitted combinations. Where a nonlinear correction changes the relevant dynamics, the replay contract becomes part of the comparison too.

## 14. The cross-contributions that are strongest

### A. State recovery supplies the object on which geometry operates

Finite recovery and effectivity can produce a usable state representation under explicit structural assumptions. Realisability and continuation determine whether it remains a valid state. Geometry then supplies a correction within a justified class. None of these stages can borrow the conclusion of the next stage merely because their formulas share coordinates.

### B. Historical revision connects provenance to geometric currentness

Provenance is not only the bibliography of a final belief. A source can justify the objective, metric, constraint or interpretation under which the state evolved. The new transport and replay theorems explain exactly when a source audit can be implemented by reweighting and when a different reconstruction is required.

### C. Finite certificates connect negative witnesses to positive repair

A negative polynomial-square expectation can refute a proposed probability law. A finite syntactic proof can certify a derivation. A source-support witness can preserve an argument after an unrelated source is revoked. These certificates have different semantics, but all can carry bounded evidence without requiring a universal reconstruction or decision procedure.

### D. Shared support and joint evidence explain why isolated tests are insufficient

Proof sharing can reverse the ranking of locally cheap proofs. Correlated reports can alter inquiry value even when separate expectations agree. Hidden interactions can alter the effective gradient geometry. These are specific interaction effects, not merely the observation that “everything is connected”.

### E. Affirmative sourcehood can contribute to a common-source argument

Local exclusivity is a real premise. A justified overlap relation and full coverage can turn those local results into a common-source conclusion. The new gluing theorem identifies the missing bridge without pretending that an arbitrary graph establishes its philosophical premises.

### F. Approximation needs separate evidence and dynamics budgets

The sharp relative-likelihood result bounds evidence error. A sensitivity or contraction estimate bounds the error caused by changing a corrective field. For bounded Lipschitz decision observables, the two contributions add. A sufficiently large action-value margin can survive both. A sharp semantic boundary or source-interpretation change requires additional evidence.

## 15. The new constructive contribution in one worked picture

Start with a source-dependent origin law. Preserve the original likelihood meanings and either the origins themselves or a proved sufficient supplement. Evolve the represented state under a declared corrective model, recording the provenance of its objective and geometry.

A later source revision now has three questions to answer.

**Which origin weights change?** The likelihood-transport theorem determines whether the current representation can implement that change exactly. If not, a conditional origin record or a refined audit profile is needed.

**Did the source also justify the correction rule?** If yes, distinguish revising beliefs about the actual historical evolution from recomputing the evolution under the revised source context. Do not silently exchange those objects.

**Which conclusions and computations are actually affected?** Follow complete support and computation dependencies. Preserve untouched warrants and recompute affected descendants. Under suitable bounds, retain a decision whose margin exceeds the combined evidence and dynamics error.

This is a usable synthesis of provenance, Bayesian evidence, moment/state validity, geometric correction, finite certificates and foundational warrant. It is not a completed implementation of every noetic operation; it is an explicit interface, a collection of proofs, and a finite executable test suite with which the existing fronts can be compared.

## 16. What this packet changes about the earlier synthesis

The earlier packet remains preserved as earlier work. Its sidecar, reduced-gradient, hybrid-interface, finite-check and continuation ideas are not discarded. But its earlier horizon cannot be treated as the endpoint of the later corpus.

The larger delivered sequence includes concrete joint state-and-weight and full-law gradient work; stronger realisability and continuation results; joint type-completion and effective-certificate developments; and further source-bound foundational work. The recovered Library packets are registered separately from the uploaded archives so their provenance is not hidden.

The new classification makes the interface sharper: on labelled positive weights and positions, universal positive-audit covariance forces weight-independent position motion and a centred replicator weight field. Under the specified standard joint metric, it forces a separable affine-mixture energy. A discrete Euler control then shows that positivity and energy descent alone do not preserve this audit contract; the packet supplies a rational weight step that does. These are candidate cross-front contributions to compare with the existing classification and discretisation packets, not a claim that the existing implementations have already been shown defective.

The new research is additive. Its most useful result is not another general warning that compression loses information. It specifies which historical likelihood information an evolving representation must retain, provides an exact stochastic obstruction and a constructive supplement, proves a sharp positive approximation guarantee, and identifies the extra replay obligation when evidence changes the correction rule itself.

The foundational companion turns that interface toward affirmative warrant preservation and a conditional common-source bridge. The handoff asks Dot to challenge these particular claims, not to perform another whole-corpus survey merely because this packet exists.
