# Review of the conditional beneficiary right argument

10 October 2026 UTC. Independent reader review of the received scoped original authority report and its bound local evidence. This is a separate assessment, with no author-source edit, protected-repository change, integration decision, or T20 closure.

## Verdict

**The report's narrow application is supported. No substantive correction is required within this review's scope.** Miftāḥ 2:1136–1141 supports a beneficiary's qualification through an actual creaturely response under the original's own undertaking, without making that creature a superior legislator or a supplier of the original's essential perfection. The report also correctly finds no *additional* proof of a particular creature-directed selection title in the governance-versus-lawlessness argument. Neither result withdraws the already accepted practical-allegiance appraisal.

This verdict concerns source fidelity and the bounded inference. It is not a new authentication of the source's theological assertions, a present person's entitlement, or the reported traditions. The conditional-right application earns a concrete application of inherited distinctions, not a new general metaphysics, promise theory, or foundational title theorem.

## The strongest authority argument is preserved

The controlling original-authority report and CP02 appraisal already accept owed practical acknowledgement and allegiance to the actual perfect source. Their operative retained-control principle is also positive: a warranted, adequately communicated, in-scope exercise of rightful selection control can determine an otherwise-open use. CP02 expressly rejects a demand for unrestricted duty creation before any local directed obligation is admitted. The reviewed report respects all three points.

Miftāḥ 2:1077–1089 supplies substantially more than useful advice or detached admiration. The author's argument connects intrinsic excellence, fitting love, worship, obedience, and practical pursuit of what pleases the source. The emphatic obligation language at 1082 and practical attribute applications at 1085–1087 prevent a merely psychological or motivational paraphrase. The capacity qualifications at 1083 and 1089 do not cancel that obligation. They also do not establish the stronger proposition that every more excellent performance is obligatory.

The selected governance argument has a different contrast. At 1067–1069 and 1071–1075 it rejects abandonment without command, restraint, or due worship. At 1074 it affirmatively retains choice with command. Showing that abandonment is defective does not itself decide between two adequate ways of rendering an already owed response. The report's narrower rival therefore identifies a missing discriminator in this particular argument; it does not establish that the rival is true, that an actual domain was surrendered, or that otherwise good alternatives defeat already warranted retained control.

The report does not substitute a weak rival for the accepted source account. Its strongest reconstruction in section 3 gives due weight to practical allegiance and to an actual address creating a distinct occasion for response. Its reservation concerns the move from a wise specification to this form alone being due when another form would otherwise discharge the acknowledged relation. A substantive selection entitlement, an independently established difference in adequate performance, or warranted truthful normative disclosure can settle that local question. Requiring a general jurisdiction theorem would be a different and unwarranted burden; the report explicitly avoids it.

The other source controls fit this conclusion. At 1:17–18, creation and command explain the arena of accountable response within the author's wise-purpose account. At 2:878–881, origination and provision give a substantive reason for worship of the actual source rather than a substitute. Neither is presented in the report as normatively empty, and neither locally compares the disputed otherwise-adequate forms.

## The punishment passage is not an argument against command title

Miftāḥ 2:1132–1135 combines power, lordship, justice, wisdom, and desert. The rejection at 1133–1134 concerns punishment without an appropriate culpability basis; bare capacity or possession is not offered as an adequate justification for that punishment. The same text positively grounds exclusive worship, magnification, and reverence in the servant-source relation.

Consequently the passage would not support an inference from unjust punishment to absence of specification authority. Nor would it establish that the source can never possess normatively significant ownership. The reviewed report expressly refuses both transports. Its modest conclusion is correct: the punitive passage supplies no shortcut around identifying the relevant normative relation in another application.

## The conditional right is genuinely normative and source owned

The relevant argument runs continuously from the accounts at 1121–1124 through the endorsed conclusion at 1142–1143. Five controls establish its force.

1. **The beneficiary condition is explicit.** At 1136 the quoted right of recipients is indexed to their fulfilment of the stated worship condition. The text therefore gives the report a concrete condition and consequent treatment, rather than an unspecified promise. The response's occurrence is relevant to qualification. Mere knowledge about the response is not its performance.
2. **This is not only prediction.** At 1124 the reduction of obligation to truthful reporting is attributed to a criticized position. At 1137 the source distinguishes self-directing or self-restraining commitment from a merely assertoric oath. The report faithfully retains this practical dimension while preserving the separate testimonial route to knowledge of an obligation.
3. **A superior commander is not required.** At 1138 the accepted account explains self-demand without a higher lawgiver. Satisfying the original's stated condition does not make the recipient the author of a law imposed upon it. The report does not infer that every ordinary promise, regardless of authorship or content, produces such a right.
4. **The two love relations remain distinct.** At 1138–1140 love of the original's own act entails its occurrence through willing and choice; approval of a creature's act does not, merely as such, entail that creaturely act's occurrence. The report keeps that distinction. It does not derive actual satisfaction of the beneficiary condition from divine approval of worship.
5. **The source does not acquire its essential praiseworthiness from the beneficiary.** At 1141 the discussion expressly returns from the own-act digression to what is undertaken or prohibited. The account relates the praiseworthiness of fulfilment to what belongs intrinsically to the source, independently of a particular performance. The recipient's qualifying response is therefore not, on this account, payment that purchases or supplies the original's essential excellence.

The inference has the right direction and strength: given the warranted undertaking and warranted condition-fulfilment, the beneficiary can have the specified claim without thereby legislating over or completing the original. It is not an inference from mere creaturely dependence to every possible divine obligation. It does not reverse self-obligation into authority to bind another person's otherwise-open conduct.

Two qualifications matter. The conditional undertaking or entitlement-schema may already obtain before its condition is fulfilled; this passage does not establish a temporally new norm coming into existence at fulfilment. Also, failure to establish the condition does not establish the absence of every other right. The report already states both limitations. Reading its “claim” as the particular qualified beneficiary's entitlement, rather than the first existence of all normative structure, preserves the inference.

## Speaker control and the strongest opposing position

The report's attribution of the developed account to Ibn al-Qayyim is supported. The preferred middle position is announced at 1124; 1135 identifies the preceding exposition as discriminating the correct and incorrect positions; 1141 resumes the author's own argument; and 1142 calls the defended position his own. The repeated reported-speech framing does not make 1136–1141 merely an adversary's view.

One guardrail must remain explicit in later use. The position reported at 1123 also describes obligation through intrinsic essence, wisdom, and sufficiency. The author's criticism that opponents impose a humanly devised law must not replace that stronger self-description when assessing those opponents. The present application does not depend on disproving them or proving that only the author's school can explain internal obligation. It establishes the content and consequence of the endorsed account at this bounded target.

The modern notes attributing 1138 to Majmūʿ 18:150 and referring at 1141 to 18:149 are apparatus evidence, not new readings of those loci. The report correctly grants no independent-witness credit. I likewise did not inspect those sources or perform hadith-chain or manuscript authentication.

## Prior credit and the limit of the addition

The full CP23 achieved-response report already distinguishes the actual accomplishment of a foreknown creaturely response from acquiring knowledge, wisdom, or essential perfection. Its test-completion application remains the owner of that general distinction and its earlier concrete execution. The original-authority report and CP02 retain the due-response, retained-control, nonprohibition-versus-surrender, and testimonial results. The inherited Miftāḥ notes already record self-obligation, own-act efficacy, and intrinsic praiseworthiness at 1133–1147. The wise-reason report already applies the punishment distinction to transferred guilt.

Accordingly, the addition here is narrow: applying the inherited achieved-response distinction to the specific beneficiary condition at 1136, and keeping the governance contrast aligned with the exact selection rival. The reviewed report claims no larger discovery. I found no duplicate-credit inflation against these controlling records; I did not independently rerun a corpus-wide novelty search.

No full causal determination, divine volition-token inventory, temporal norm-creation, or ontological receipt follows from this application. Its success is a normative dependency distinction within the accepted account. The author explicitly maintains those boundaries, so no substantive revision is needed before using it as separate bounded research evidence.

## Separate positive consequence requested during review

**The undertaking also provides a bounded, content-bearing practical discriminator.** This consequence is supported and worth stating expressly; it is implicit rather than independently worked through in the frozen report. It does not require changing the report's verdict.

Fix the warranted undertaking's actual terms and intended scope. In the 1136 illustration, the relevant promised treatment is nonpunishment of the recipient who fulfils the stated worship condition. Establish actual qualification without defining it as receipt of the later treatment. Compare that promised nonpunishment with punishment excluded by those same terms, holding the recipient, occasion, condition, and any qualifications fixed. The source-owned undertaking and the fulfilled condition give a reason for the former over the latter. They do not merely redescribe whichever treatment happens to occur as good.

The own-obligation rationale at 1137–1138 makes this a practical commitment; 1141 explicitly makes fulfilment praiseworthy and contravention incompatible with the source's intrinsic praiseworthiness. The discriminator can therefore identify a respect in which one treatment answers a warranted relation and the other fails it. In this account it is more than a prediction of the source's behavior. It remains conditional on establishing the full operative terms; the abbreviated cited tradition must not be used here to adjudicate the standing of a contemporary individual or resolve its entire theological application.

This is not a new T19 testimonial result. T19 P164–165 supplies an epistemic route for warranting a determinate obligation through a truthful authenticated assertion. The present practical comparison asks why the particular treatment fits the warranted undertaking and qualified beneficiary. These roles can coexist: testimony may establish the terms or obligation, while the resulting commitment-relation supplies the articulated reason. Conversely, this corollary does not independently prove that every purported undertaking binds.

Its ingredients and general form are inherited. CP23 already owns the distinction between actual achievement and knowledge or essential receipt; the earlier Miftāḥ notes own self-obligation; T19 owns testimony-based recognition. The selected report already states the qualified beneficiary claim. Accordingly this is a useful explicit corollary at a particular response-level target, not a fresh normative theorem or a second discovery of those distinctions. It adds clarity and positive explanatory use rather than a separate foundational proof.

The discriminator excludes a treatment that contravenes this undertaking. It need not select a unique implementation among all compatible treatments, explain why this undertaking was initially adopted, create a temporally new norm, or determine every intrinsic act and complete history. The condition's actuality can matter to applicability without contributing the original's essential perfection or becoming an external efficient producer.
