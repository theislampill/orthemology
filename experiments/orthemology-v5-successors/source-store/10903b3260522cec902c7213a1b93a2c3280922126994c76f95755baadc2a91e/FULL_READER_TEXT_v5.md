# Finite certified renewal and effective selection of continuing lineages

5 October 2026. Written mathematics and a stipulated organisational model. Proof level. The six-module renewal core and constructive pruned-witness criterion have recorded independent formal acceptance. The written width-two nonuniformity, converging-plan propositions and additional scope arguments retain their written status. These are mathematical effective services, without a general native code-to-code compiler, physical lineage authentication or actual authority warrant. This is not an empirical demonstration, a numerical-identity criterion or a historically new computability theorem.

## Result and exact contribution

There is a fully decidable, binary-branching model of successor construction with the following combination. Every requested finite renewal horizon has a uniformly computable successful plan. Every transition in such a plan preserves a nonvacuous stateful semantic contract, carries a genuine model-level predecessor–successor history, obeys an independent external permission condition and removes the predecessor token from the successor's operative support. Nevertheless, no computable controller can carry out infinitely many single-successor renewals on one fixed computable always-authorised input history, and hence none guarantees indefinite renewal over all permitted service-input histories. The main claim concerns one irrevocably selected lineage. Effective expanding populations obey a different contract, considered below.

The point is stronger than an observation that one successful handoff can lose the next faculty. One effective procedure produces a successful, locally checkable plan for any finite horizon supplied in advance. What fails is the exchange from this horizon-indexed procedure to one coherent executable policy with no last renewal fixed in advance. An infinite admissible lineage exists mathematically; none is computable. Finite successes retain their full stated credit.

Conversely, for a decidable tree of finite admitted renewal histories, a computable infinite continuation exists exactly when the tree contains a rooted, pruned computably enumerable subtree. Equivalently, it contains a decidable such subtree. Enumeration and a warranted pruning claim supply an effective next-choice procedure. Listing arbitrarily long successful histories, even uniformly and with exact certificates, does not supply this property.

The noncomputable-path mechanism belongs to established computability theory, specifically computable binary trees arising from recursively inseparable computably enumerable sets. No claim concerns the differently stronger tree constructions used to exclude all hyperarithmetic paths. The new programme-specific work is its exact lift through the already owned semantic, lineage, authority and source-token conditions, together with an explicit effective-witness characterisation and its limits. Neither theorem establishes an independently warranted real-world bearer boundary. The model fixes that boundary and asks what further continuation capacity follows.

## 2 Three meanings of indefinitely available finite renewal

Let \(2^{<\omega}\) be the finite binary strings, \(\varepsilon\) the empty string, and \(\sigma b\) the string obtained by appending bit \(b\). A tree \(T\subseteq2^{<\omega}\) contains \(\varepsilon\) and is closed under prefixes. Its nodes are finite admitted renewal-choice histories. Membership in \(T\) is decidable. An edge \(\sigma\to\sigma b\) is admitted exactly when \(\sigma b\in T\). There are at most two children at every node.

A finite plan of horizon \(n\) is a string \(\sigma \in T\) with \(|\sigma |=n\). The plan commits to the bits of \(\sigma\) in order. All its intermediate histories are admitted by prefix closure. A horizon-indexed planner is a total computable function \(H\) with \(H(n)\in T\) and \(|H(n)|=n\). The words \(H(n)\) and \(H(n+1)\) are not assumed to agree on their common positions.

An infinite lineage is a binary sequence \(f\) with every prefix \(f\upharpoonright n\in T\). Its existence is a set-theoretic statement. A computable continuing controller must finish every next choice after finitely much computation and produce such a lineage when repeatedly asked to renew. The controller may retain arbitrary finite memory at each stage, change its own code, replan, or consult other uniformly computable processes counted in its support. It has no uncounted noncomputable advice or oracle.

These are different assertions:

1. For each \(n\) there exists an admitted plan of length \(n\).
2. One computable \(H\) returns an admitted plan for every supplied \(n\).
3. There exists an infinite admitted lineage.
4. One computable controller produces an infinite admitted lineage.

For a decidable binary tree, (1) implies (2): search the finite set of length-\(n\) strings. Classical finite-branching compactness gives (1) implies (3). The following explicit construction proves that even (2) and (3) together need not imply (4).

This is not about a controller being asked in advance to fit infinitely many events into finite time. Every individual renewal may use finite, unbounded computation, and the complete run lasts indefinitely. It is also not a statement that no organisation in the physical world can continue indefinitely.

## 3 A decidable tree with uniform finite plans and no computable path

Fix an effective enumeration \((\varphi_e)_{e\in\mathbb N}\) of partial computable functions from natural numbers to natural numbers. Every total computable binary-valued function occurs somewhere in the enumeration. Fix an effective step semantics, so the statement that \(\varphi_e(e)\) has halted within \(n\) steps with output \(b\) is decidable from \(e\),\(n\),\(b\).

Define disjoint computably enumerable sets

\[
\begin{aligned} A&=\{e:\varphi_e(e)\text{ halts with output }0\},\\ B&=\{e:\varphi_e(e)\text{ halts with output }1\}.\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(A_n=\{e<n:\varphi_e(e)\text{ halts within }n\text{ steps with output }0\}\) and \(B_n=\{e<n:\varphi_e(e)\text{ halts within }n\text{ steps with output }1\}\). These finite approximations are increasing in \(n\) and disjoint. Values other than 0 and 1 impose no condition.

For a length-\(n\) binary string \(\sigma\), define membership by

\[
\sigma\in T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall e<n,\quad\begin{cases} e\in A_n\Longrightarrow\sigma(e)=1,\\e\in B_n\Longrightarrow\sigma(e)=0.\end{cases}
\]

The tree requires a finite sequence to disagree with each relevant diagonal binary computation already discovered at its own verification horizon.

### Lemma 1 Decidability and prefix closure

Membership in \(T\) is decidable. The empty string belongs to \(T\), and every prefix of a member belongs to \(T\).

Proof. For a length-\(n\) candidate, simulate the finitely many computations \(\varphi_e(e)\), \(e<n\), for \(n\) steps and compare the finitely many required bits. At \(n=0\) no requirements occur. If \(\sigma \in T\) has length \(n\) and \(\tau\) is its length-\(m\) prefix, \(m\le n\), every condition visible by stage \(m\) at an index \(e<m\) is also visible by stage \(n\). Therefore \(\tau\) satisfies all its own membership conditions. ∎

### Lemma 2 Uniform finite success

The function \(H\) defined for \(e<n\) by

\[
H(n)(e)=\begin{cases}1&e\in A_n,\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

is total computable and returns a member of \(T\) of length \(n\).

Proof. The bounded simulations decide each coordinate. All \(A_n\) requirements are met. A coordinate in \(B_n\) is not in \(A_n\) because the two sets are disjoint, so it is assigned 0. Those are all the requirements. Lemma 1 supplies every intermediate prefix. ∎

This is a single algorithm valid for all finite horizons. No halting oracle is used. A complete finite route certificate can store \(H(n)\), its prefixes and the finite bounded-simulation checks; all can be recomputed. The running time need not be bounded independently of \(n\), and no such bound is claimed.

### Lemma 3 Infinite mathematical continuation

\(T\) has an infinite path.

Proof. In classical mathematics take \(f(e)=1\) exactly when \(e\in A\), and \(f(e)=0\) otherwise. The sets A and \(B\) are disjoint, so every finite prefix of \(f\) satisfies its membership requirements. Thus \(f\) is an infinite path. This definition is not a computable construction. Alternatively, Lemma 2 and König's lemma give existence. ∎

### Lemma 4 No computable infinite continuation

\(T\) has no computable infinite path.

Proof. Suppose \(f\) is a total computable binary-valued path. Choose \(k\) with \(\varphi_k=f\). Then \(\varphi_k(k)\) halts with output \(f(k)\). Choose \(n\) greater than \(k\) and no smaller than that computation's halting time. If \(f(k)=0\), then \(k\in A_n\) and the path requirement says \(f(k)=1\). If \(f(k)=1\), then \(k\in B_n\) and the requirement says \(f(k)=0\). Either case contradicts \(f\upharpoonright n\in T\). ∎

Equivalently, every path gives a separator \(C=\{e:f(e)=1\}\) satisfying \(A\subseteq C\) and \(C\cap B=\varnothing\); the diagonal argument proves that there is no computable such separator. This is a standard recursively inseparable-set/Kleene-tree mechanism, with its full argument stated rather than assumed from a name.

### Theorem 1 The effective finite to indefinite gap

There is a decidable rooted binary tree with a uniformly computable successful finite-horizon planner and an infinite path, but no computable controller that performs infinitely many admitted renewals.

Proof. Lemmas 1–4 establish the first claims. If a computable controller, including all its uniformly computable support, completed every next renewal, simulate it through its first \(e+1\) completed choices to compute its \(e\)-th output bit. This is a total computable path, contradicting Lemma 4. Code changes and unbounded finite internal computation do not alter that simulation argument. ∎

The theorem rules out every computable online rule on this tree, including rules that repeatedly solve deeper finite-horizon planning problems from their current history. It does not rule out an oracle-assisted choice, uncounted advice or a mathematical noncomputable selector. Those change the supported-controller premise. It makes no positive-probability claim about randomized policies. A guarantee for every random tape would already fail because fixing a computable tape gives an ordinary computable controller.

## 4 Why the finite plans need not agree

A later bounded simulation can discover a requirement on an earlier bit. An \(n\)-step plan may be locally valid but cease to have any extension at a later level. It is never retroactively invalid as a completed length-\(n\) history; what fails is its further extension.

A small stage-pattern illustration is enough. Suppose the first newly relevant diagonal requirement forces bit 0 to be 1 only from stage 3 onward, with no other requirements before stage 3. Then 0 is a valid one-step plan and 00 is a valid two-step plan, but every valid three-step plan starts with 1. The horizon planner may return 0, 00 and 100. These are genuine successes at their respective requested horizons; concatenating them is not one possible history.

The full diagonal construction does more than exploit this one early mismatch. Every computable choice of a complete infinite history eventually encounters a diagonal obstruction. Allowing the controller to choose a more careful initial prefix does not remove the global result.

It would be false to say that every node of \(T\) retains arbitrarily much future renewal. Some nodes are dead ends or have only finitely deep descendants. The root has plans for every finite horizon, and \(H\) gives them uniformly, but preserving that root-level property through a chosen edge is the hard part. This is precisely why horizon competence cannot simply be counted as an invariant of every successful successor.

## 5 Lifting the tree to meaningful organisational conditions

The tree alone is a planning object. To test whether exact state semantics, source lineage, token exclusion or independently governed authority close the gap, put it into the following product model. All physical interpretations remain stipulated model assumptions.

### A nonvacuous service

Use the already inherited four-state delay contract

\[
Q=\{0,1\}^{2},\qquad\Delta((u,v),i)=(v,i),\qquad\Lambda(u,v)=u,
\]

for input \(i\in\{0,1\}\). The two states (0,0) and (0,1) have equal present output but different next output after a common input. Thus the full consequential state matters.

At generation \(n\) use encoding \(E_n(q)=q\) when \(n\) is even and \(E_n(q)=\operatorname{bitwise\ complement}(q)\) when \(n\) is odd. Its inverse is \(E_n\) itself. Exactly one successor is selected at each renewal; the previously chosen history is not later rolled back or replaced by another branch. A live instance stores \((\sigma,E_{|\sigma|}(q))\), the common finite constructor/checker code, the applicable finite authority record, and any specified current request. Its operative readout is \(\Lambda(E_{|\sigma|}^{-1}(\text{stored state}))\). Service execution implements \(E_n\circ \Delta \circ E_n^{-1}\).

A renewal from \(\sigma\) to \(\sigma b\), when admitted, creates a new instance containing

\[
\bigl(\sigma b,E_{|\sigma|+1}(E_{|\sigma|}^{-1}(\text{stored state}))\bigr).
\]

The new instance receives both components of \(q\), not just the visible output. Migration therefore commutes exactly with the abstraction. Execution and readout do too. These equalities hold for every \(q\) and input, not merely for one favourable observed trace. The Companion's finite semantic continuation theorem supplies the general finite-interleaving conclusion; the two-bit encoding equalities also verify it directly.

### Model level lineage and predecessor token exclusion

The successor is produced by the declared renewal transition from its actual predecessor state. Its history tag extends that predecessor's tag by exactly the chosen bit. Lineage here is part of the stipulated causal transition history, not inferred from the presence of a self-reported string. An independently manufactured lookalike has the same possible semantic state without thereby sharing this transition history.

After commitment, the predecessor instance token is removed from the active model configuration. All data used by the successor are the transferred finite state, tag, code and current permission information. No operation reads the retired predecessor token. Each finite valid route therefore witnesses source-token exclusion in this model. The common execution platform, memory supply, interpretation of the tree and legitimate authority remain declared support. This is neither a physical independence test nor exclusion of all original failure roots.

### Independent authority and legitimate termination

An external permission condition \(p\) governs the construction service. The authority policy prospectively permits an active instance to establish one admitted child while \(p=1\); it explicitly scopes this permission to the newly created child's next use under the same condition. The instance cannot set \(p\) or enlarge the grant. The availability test \(\sigma b\in T\) is independent of this authority condition: tree membership is not a claim to normative legitimacy.

Before each request an external input may keep \(p=1\) or revoke permission. If \(p=0\), the only permitted continuation is the specified orderly termination; no successor construction is authorised. No theorem requires resistance to revocation. The impossibility is already present on the admissible environment history with \(p\) always 1. Hence adding accurate revocation handling does not repair it, and the obstruction is not created by a convenient denial of permission.

A permission bit represents a stipulated legitimate condition in this model. Nothing here derives actual authority from a bit, a signature, successful execution or self-endorsement. These limits are inherited and preserved.

### Warrant and finite certificates

A finite route certificate names the contract, encoding rule, route, source and successor states, active permission history, and bounded tree-membership calculations. Its local checks establish the stated model facts. The current controller can compute \(H(n)\), carry out its \(n\) transitions and provide all these checks. Relevant service inputs can be interleaved arbitrarily, with the inherited state/effect-order conditions respected. If permission is revoked, the route terminates rather than falsely claiming \(n\) renewals.

The packet warrants the finite model claim only under the supplied interpretation and assumptions. It does not authenticate a physical history, prove external legitimacy, create resources, or establish that a real organisation fits the model. Those would require independent application evidence. Nor is it a certificate that the final node remains indefinitely viable.

### Theorem 2 Contract preserving finite successes do not force an effective continuing faculty

The product model has a computable procedure which, given any finite horizon \(n\), completes \(n\) renewals on every always-authorised service-input history while preserving the full delay contract, model-level lineage and predecessor-token exclusion at each renewal. It handles external revocation by the stipulated permitted termination. Every finite completed route has decidable checks of the described conditions. There is a mathematical infinite route for every service-input history with permission always present. No computable controller completes infinitely many single-successor renewals on the fixed history with permission always present and service input 0 before every renewal. Consequently no computable controller guarantees indefinite renewal over all permitted service-input histories, even when all the other stated conditions are satisfied exactly.

Proof. Use \(H(n)\) for the renewal choices, and use the fixed service and encoding algorithms for all interleaved service inputs. Each prefix lies in \(T\); every next transition is available and authorised when \(p=1\). The displayed equations preserve \(q\) and therefore the service contract. The transition rule gives lineage and token exclusion. These are independent of which admissible bit was selected. Revocation invokes the terminal branch. Lemma 3 supplies a mathematical infinite route. If a computable controller succeeded indefinitely, give it the computable environment that always keeps permission and supplies, for example, service input 0 before each renewal. Its completed renewal choices would compute a path in \(T\), contrary to Theorem 1. ∎

The negative quantifier matters. An arbitrarily noncomputable external input stream could provide the bits of a path, and a controller that copies this advice could then succeed on that particular history. The theorem does not exclude that different support situation. It excludes a uniform guarantee and already excludes success with the fixed computable zero-input environment; any other uniformly computable environment also composes with the controller into a computable path if renewal were infinite.

No finite construction capacity is demoted by this proof. Given \(n\), the organisation has the stipulated supported capacity for \(n\) renewals and all the associated stateful continuation. A finite horizon is not a failure because an unrequested stronger infinity claim is false. The proof instead rejects promotion of those capacities to an unrestricted effective policy.

## 6 An exact positive repair by effectively accessible pruning

For any tree \(T\), define its indefinitely viable nodes

\[
\begin{aligned}V(T)=\{\sigma\in T:\ &\forall m\ \exists\tau\in T,\ \tau\text{ extends }\sigma\\ &\text{ and }|\tau|=|\sigma|+m\}.\end{aligned}
\]

For a binary tree this means exactly that \(\sigma\) lies on some infinite path. The equivalence uses finite-branching compactness. Every node of \(V(T)\) has at least one child in \(V(T)\). Nevertheless, the existence of such a child need not make it computably selectable.

A rooted pruned subtree \(R\subseteq T\) contains \(\varepsilon\), is prefix closed, and has for every \(\sigma \in R\) at least one immediate child \(\sigma b\in R\). Call \(R\) computably enumerable when one algorithm enumerates all and only its members; repetitions and arbitrary finite delay are allowed. The pruning property is a mathematical condition on that enumeration's range, not a promise inferred from waiting without seeing failure.

### Theorem 3 Exact effective witness characterisation

For any decidable binary tree \(T\) containing \(\varepsilon\), the following conditions are equivalent:

(a) \(T\) has a computable infinite path.

(\(b\)) \(T\) contains a decidable rooted pruned subtree.

(\(c\)) \(T\) contains a computably enumerable rooted pruned subtree.

Moreover, from an enumerator for a subtree satisfying (\(c\)), a path is obtained by one uniform computable selection procedure.

Proof. If \(f\) is a computable path, let \(R=\{f\upharpoonright n:n\in\mathbb N\}\). Given a finite string \(\sigma\), compute the first \(|\sigma|\) bits of \(f\) and compare. Thus \(R\) is decidable, rooted, prefix closed and has exactly one child for each node. This proves \((\mathrm{a})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{b})\). Decidable sets are computably enumerable, so \((\mathrm{b})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{c})\).

For \((\mathrm{c})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{a})\), begin at \(\varepsilon\). At a current node \(\sigma \in R\), restart the enumerator from its beginning and run it until it emits \(\sigma 0\) or \(\sigma 1\) and choose the first such child emitted. Pruning guarantees that this search halts. The output lies in \(R\), so the same argument applies at the next step. Computing finitely many successive outputs terminates, hence their union is a computable infinite path in \(R\subseteq T\). The procedure uses only the enumerator; no test deciding all of \(R\) or \(V(T)\) is needed. ∎

The existence of a successor in an unspecified set would be insufficient. The positive content is an effective way to encounter a suitable successor whose continuation assurance belongs to the same closed range. Enumerable access and pruning work jointly. By contrast, \(T\) itself is decidable but not pruned in the counterexample. Enumerating \(H(n)\) and all its prefixes yields arbitrarily long certified histories and still cannot yield a pruned range; if it did, Theorem 3 would contradict Theorem 1.

A finite invariant with a computable certified next-choice rule is an important sufficient special case. An infinite effective witness is also possible: for the full binary tree, the prefix set \(\{0^n:n\in\mathbb N\}\) is a decidable pruned witness, even though the stored lineage grows. No requirement of finite physical storage, fixed per-step time or independence from common infrastructure follows merely from this witness.

The equivalence is not an algorithm to decide whether an arbitrary \(T\) has a computable path, to discover a valid \(R\), or to verify from an arbitrary enumerator code that its range is sound and pruned. Given an already warranted witness it extracts a policy. The witness's global validity remains a substantive proof obligation. An ordinary effective formal theory may verify useful instances; complete sound effective proof coverage of all possible witnesses is not established here.

## 7 Bounded branching and uniform selection are separate questions

The main construction fixes one irrevocable predecessor–successor lineage. This is not a general ban on branching organisations. If the authority prospectively permits both children and the support supplies the necessary candidate capacity, a computable breadth-first procedure can keep every member of \(T\) at each successive depth. Each generation is finite and nonempty. Each child has an actual admitted parent. This is one prefix-coherent growing population, not a sequence of unrelated horizon plans restarted from the root. It produces population-level renewal indefinitely without computing an infinite selected individual lineage.

Such branching changes the grant and organisational contract. A permission for one selected child cannot be reused as a right to construct both. It also changes which support must be available. The fact that every generation is finite does not supply an unlimited resource budget in the physical world.

A separately authored and reviewed extension establishes the exact fixed-width boundary. If a computable procedure completes nonempty finite generation sets \(P_n\) of length-\(n\) admitted histories, every child extends a member of \(P_{n-1}\), and the complete generation sets have some common finite cardinality bound, their union is a decidable tree with finitely many infinite paths. An isolating finite prefix of one path makes that path computable by searching for dead-branch evidence. This contradicts the diagonal tree construction. Paper 3 contains the full proof and its randomised extension; this paragraph records the interface and does not claim a separate result.

The bound concerns complete generation sets. Each set must be closed before the next generation is accepted, and later reopening of an ancestor or completed generation is outside this protocol. A bound on currently active instances does not, by itself, bound the total generated genealogy when rollback, ancestor checkpoints, restart or backtracking are allowed. The positive full-level construction has unbounded generation-set width. A lower bound on concurrent material instances follows only under an implementation contract requiring all those generation members to be live together. No universal fixed-memory impossibility or quantitative growth-rate lower bound is established.

### Proposition 4 A width bound is not a uniform path constructor

There is a uniform family of decidable binary trees \(S_e\), each nonempty at every depth, of width at most two and containing a computable path, for which no one effective procedure, given \(e\), returns a path in \(S_e\).

Proof. Put \(\varepsilon\) in \(S_e\). At positive length \(n\), its only possible members are \(0^n\) and \(1^n\). Admit \(b^n\) exactly when \(\varphi_e(e)\) has not halted within \(n\) steps with output \(b\). Bounded simulation makes membership decidable, and every prefix of an admitted string is admitted. At most one of the two constant branches is eventually excluded, so every level is nonempty and a constant infinite path exists. Each individual tree therefore has a computable path, though which constant path is appropriate need not be effectively available from \(e\).

Suppose a uniform effective procedure, given \(e\), produced some path of \(S_e\). Compute its first bit and call that bit \(g(e)\). Totality of the promised selector makes \(g\) a total computable bit function. If \(\varphi_e(e)\) halts with bit \(b\), then the \(b\)-branch eventually disappears, so \(g(e)=1-b\). Taking an index \(k\) of \(g\) gives \(g(k)=1-g(k)\), a contradiction. ∎

Thus a proof that a finite-path tree has a computable path is nonuniform unless it also supplies an isolating prefix or another effective selection witness. Theorem 3 is constructive from a warranted enumerator of a pruned subtree; Proposition 4 explains why an upper width bound is not a substitute for that information. This does not impair the earlier finite-graph fixed-point algorithm, whose entire finite graph is explicitly available. The \(S_e\) family retains an unbounded stage coordinate through its simulation guard.

## 8 Converging plans and irrevocable histories

The horizon planner has an additional positive property. Define its full coordinate approximation \(h_n(e)\) as \(H(n)(e)\) when \(e<n\) and as 0 otherwise. For each fixed \(e\), \(h_n(e)\) changes at most once, from 0 to 1, and eventually agrees with \(\chi_A(e)\). If \(e\) never enters A it remains 0. If \(\varphi_e(e)\) halts with output 0, the coordinate becomes 1 once \(n\) exceeds \(e\) and includes the halting computation. Thus the computable horizon plans converge coordinatewise to the particular infinite path \(\chi_A\), despite the absence of a computable path selector.

### Proposition 5 Converging plans need not provide effective commitment

For this \(H\), there is no total computable stabilisation modulus \(m\) satisfying \(H(n)(e)=\chi_A(e)\) for all \(n\ge m(e)\) with \(n>e\). There is also no total computable unbounded sequence of horizons \(n_j\) whose returned words \(H(n_j)\) are prefix-coherent in their listed order.

Proof. Given such a modulus, compute \(N=\operatorname{max}(m(e),e+1)\) and return \(H(N)(e)\). This computes \(\chi_A\), an infinite path of \(T\), contradicting Lemma 4.

For the second claim, given \(e\), search for a \(j\) with \(n_j>e\) and output the \(e\)-th bit of \(H(n_j)\). Computability and unboundedness make the search terminate. Prefix coherence makes the answer independent of which sufficiently long listed word is used. The union of the listed words is an infinite path because every listed word belongs to \(T\), all its prefixes belong to \(T\), and lengths are unbounded. This would again be a computable path, contradicting Lemma 4. ∎

This statement does not assert the existence of a noncomputable prefix-coherent subsequence of these particular horizon plans. The pointwise limit exists; a suitable nested subsequence is a separate condition.

Repeated replanning or synchronising to successively longer snapshots can therefore approach a valid infinite description without supplying an effective time at which each proposed earlier decision is safe to make irrevocable. A revised candidate history is not the same actual causal history with its past changed. In the model a committed successor has already selected one prefix; replacing that prefix by another is a different operation requiring a different state, authority and continuation contract. The theorem does not deny the value of revisable plans or successful finite synchronisation. It prevents their convergence from being treated as an executable single-lineage renewal method.

## 9 The obstruction is effectiveness rather than missing finite evidence

For a decidable binary \(T\) and fixed \(\sigma\), nonmembership in \(V(T)\) is semidecidable: search for a depth \(m\) with no extending node at that depth. The search at each \(m\) is finite. If \(\sigma\) has no infinite extension, finite branching ensures such a depth exists. Thus \(V(T)\) is co-computably enumerable, uniformly from the decider for \(T\).

For the counterexample, \(V(T)\) is not computably enumerable. If it were, it would be a rooted pruned computably enumerable subtree, and Theorem 3 would yield a computable path. There is consequently no sound effective procedure enumerating exactly all indefinitely viable nodes of this \(T\). This is not a claim that no true viability proposition can receive a proof. The root's viability, for example, is established by the written argument above. It is a limit on an effective, complete and usable positive listing at the stated domain.

The exact separation is therefore:

- Every finite route can be checked effectively.
- Every finite horizon can be planned effectively from the root.
- Infinite viability exists at the root.
- Preserving infinite viability along a computably chosen route is unavailable.

Finite-state models have a different boundary. On a finite directed graph with decidable edges, repeated deletion of dead-end vertices stabilises after finitely many deletions. If the root survives, a successor in the surviving set can be chosen effectively forever. The Companion's finite fixed-point account is compatible with this fact. Our counterexample uses an unbounded history space, and a finite quotient cannot erase the history-dependent readiness conditions while preserving the claimed renewal service.

Source lineage supplies a historical relation. The semantic contract supplies the meanings and consequences preserved by a transition. # Randomised renewal in the explicit diagonal lineage

Proof level. This is an independently reviewed written probability argument, without Lean formalisation. It concerns a computable fair-coin transducer selecting one committed lineage in this particular diagonal tree, and permits partial output on unsuccessful tapes. The ingredients are standard. No physical law, human incapacity or general impossibility of organisational continuation follows.

## 1 The fixed target

Fix an effective enumeration (\(\varphi_e\)) of all partial computable natural-number functions and a decidable bounded-step simulation relation. A finite bit string \(\sigma\) of length \(n\) is in \(T\) if, for every \(e<n\), whenever \(\varphi_e(e)\) halts within \(n\) simulation steps with value \(b\) in \(\{0,1\}\), \(\sigma(e)=1-b\). Values outside \(\{0,1\}\) impose no constraint. This definition is decidable and prefix closed.

For every \(n\) one can simulate these \(n\) computations for \(n\) steps and set each constrained bit to its required opposite, choosing 0 at unconstrained positions. This computes an admitted string of every prescribed length. These strings need not extend one another. The set \([T]\) of infinite paths is nonempty: define \(f(e)=1-b\) when \(\varphi_e(e)\) eventually halts with bit \(b\), otherwise 0. This is an existence argument, not an algorithm for evaluating \(f\).

No computable \(f\) belongs to \([T]\). If \(f\) has index \(e\), its value \(f(e)\) is a bit and its own diagonal computation eventually halts with that value. Every path must have opposite value there.

## 2 Coin transducer service

A computable randomised policy is a deterministic effective machine reading successive independent fair random bits. It may take arbitrarily many finite computation steps between outputs. It commits output bits in order; committed bits cannot later be revised. It may stop or diverge on unsuccessful random tapes. Its success event \(S\) consists of tapes on which it eventually commits every output coordinate and the resulting infinite bit sequence lies in \([T]\).

The claim is stronger than failure of every deterministic policy and weaker than a claim about every random process. Noncomputable advice, noncomputable random laws and access to an oracle are not included. Any independently supplied environmental response not covered by the effective transition model requires its own treatment.

**Theorem \(R\).** Every computable coin-transducer has probability zero of success \(S\).

## 3 A finite cylinder concentration lemma

For every measurable \(S\) in fair-coin sequence space with probability \(p>0\), and every \(c<1\), there exists a finite coin prefix \(\rho\) with \(P(S | [\rho ])>c\).

Here is an elementary justification sufficient for \(c=2/3\). Finite unions of cylinders approximate measurable sets in probability. To recall why, let A be the class of sets approximable arbitrarily closely in symmetric-difference probability by finite cylinder unions. It contains the cylinders and is closed under complements. For a countable union of sets in A, first approximate the union by a finite subunion using continuity from below, then approximate its finitely many components with total error below a prescribed tolerance. Thus A is a \(\sigma\)-algebra containing the cylinders and contains the product \(\sigma\)-algebra. Null-set completion does not change approximation in probability.

Choose a finite cylinder union \(C\) with \(P(S\mathbin{\triangle}C)<\epsilon\), where \(0<\epsilon <p/5\). Then \(P(S\cap C)>p-\epsilon\) and \(P(C)<p+\epsilon\), so \(P(S\mid C)>\frac{p-\epsilon}{p+\epsilon}>\frac23\). Refine \(C\) into finitely many disjoint equal-length cylinders. Their conditional success probabilities average to \(P(S|C)\), so at least one exceeds \(2/3\). Every finite fair-coin cylinder has positive probability.

The prefix need not be found effectively. For a contradiction to the existence of a computable function, one may fix and hardcode an existing finite string. No algorithm is being claimed to find it uniformly from the transducer.

\(S\) is measurable: committing a specified finite output string is an effectively open event (a union of finite halting-computation cylinders). Total output and every finite prefix lying in \(T\) can be expressed through countable unions and intersections of these events. No nonmeasurable selection is involved.

## 4 Effective lower probabilities

Fix such a prefix \(\rho\). For a coordinate \(e\) and bit \(b\), let \(E(e,b)\) be the event that the seeded transducer eventually commits bit \(b\) at coordinate \(e\). The two events for \(b=0\) and \(b=1\) are disjoint.

Their conditional probabilities given \(\rho\) have uniform computable increasing rational lower approximations. At stage \(m\), simulate the seeded machine for \(m\) steps on every \(m\)-bit suffix. Count suffixes on which the \(e\)-th bit has already been committed to \(b\), divided by \(2^m\). If a simulation would require unavailable suffix bits, stop it at that request. Completed commitments never change; refinement of suffixes and longer simulation preserve earlier counted mass. Each successful finite computation appears at some stage, so these dyadic approximations increase to \(P(E(e,b)|\rho )\). This is finite exhaustive computation, with no efficiency claim.

Assume now for contradiction \(P(S)>0\), and hardcode \(\rho\) with \(s=P(S|\rho )>2/3\). On \(S\) every \(e\)-th bit is committed, hence

\[
P(E(e,0)\mid\rho)+P(E(e,1)\mid\rho)\ge s>\frac23.
\]

At least one of the two probabilities is strictly greater than \(1/3\). Dovetail their lower approximations until one exceeds \(1/3\), then output that bit, using a fixed tie order if both pass at the same stage. This defines a total computable bit function \(g(e)\). No knowledge of \(s\) is needed by this algorithm; its strict bound is used only to prove termination.

If \(\varphi_e(e)\) eventually halts with bit \(b\), success requires the \(e\)-th output \(1-b\). Therefore the wrong-bit event \(E(e,b)\) is disjoint from \(S\) and has conditional probability at most \(1-s<1/3\). No correct lower approximation of it exceeds \(1/3\). The algorithm must consequently return \(1-b\).

Thus \(g\) is a total computable bit function avoiding every bit-valued diagonal computation. Taking an index \(d\) of \(g\) gives \(g(d)=1-g(d)\), a contradiction. This proves Theorem \(R\), without assuming the transducer produces total output almost surely.

## 5 What this does not say

The absence of computable paths alone is not sufficient for Theorem \(R\). Other computable binary trees can have positive fair-coin measure of paths but no computable member. The proof uses the particular diagonal constraints to turn concentrated success into a computable coordinatewise avoiding function.

It does not say every finite horizon is unreliable: the deterministic bounded simulation in Section 1 succeeds with certainty at each requested finite horizon. Nor does it provide a uniform computable time or horizon at which any given policy must fail. A policy can fail by committing a bad prefix or by ceasing to produce outputs, and the probability-zero result is about indefinite success.

Most importantly, one committed lineage is not the same as an expanding population. An effective breadth-first construction can retain all nodes of \(T\) at each level. Every level is finite and nonempty, so this supplies an indefinitely nonempty population with unbounded permitted replication/storage. It does not effectively select a successful infinite lineage. Any application to organisational renewal must specify which of these services its identity and resource contract requires. The statement does not invalidate branching continuation or finite actual achievements.

An independently substantiated semantic-state, provenance and authority contract can be combined with the diagonal admission rule, but its local correctness does not remove the selection problem. Conversely, the diagonal construction itself supplies no actual warrant that a real organisation faces this artificial constraint. It is an exact counterexample to inferring one effective infinite continuation from a family of effectively certified finite-horizon continuations without additional assumptions.

# A finite width population does not supply the missing renewal

Proof level. This is an independently reviewed written population argument, without Lean formalisation. Its bounded-width negative assumes complete closed generation sets with parent coherence; it does not imply a universal memory or instantaneous-concurrency lower bound. No result concerns finite observed success or an actual physical population without the stipulated effective contract.

## 1 Population service

A deterministic effective service emits a complete finite set \(G_n\) of admitted length-\(n\) strings at each generation \(n\). It eventually finishes every generation. Put \(G_0=\{\varepsilon\}\). Every string in \(G_{n+1}\) extends a string in \(G_n\). Once a generation is declared complete, no later output reopens or enlarges it. Thus \(G=\bigcup_n G_n\) is a prefix-closed binary subtree of \(T\), and its level membership is decidable: run the service until its \(n\)-th output is complete, then inspect that finite set. The emitted sets describe continuing genealogies, not independent plans restarted from generation 0.

Indefinite population success means every \(G_n\) is nonempty. A fixed finite width bound means \(|G_n|\le K\) for all \(n\), for some finite \(K\). \(K\) need not be supplied to the service or computed from its code.

**Theorem P1.** No deterministic effective service has indefinite success at bounded width in the diagonal tree \(T\).

## 2 Finite paths imply a computable path

An infinite binary tree with level \(\text{width}\le K\) has at least one and at most \(K\) infinite paths. Existence follows by the elementary finitely branching argument: starting from a node with descendants at arbitrarily large depths, at least one of its finitely many children again has that property; recursively choose it. For the upper bound, if there were \(K+1\) different paths, some common finite level would distinguish all \(K+1\) prefixes, contrary to the width bound.

Every member of a finite set of distinct infinite bit paths is isolated by a finite prefix \(\sigma\): take a length exceeding all its finitely many first disagreement positions with the other paths. Hence the subtree above \(\sigma\) has exactly one infinite path.

In a decidable binary tree with exactly one infinite path above \(\sigma\), that path is computable when \(\sigma\) is fixed as a finite constant. At a current node on the path, one of the two child subtrees is finite-height. Otherwise both have paths by finite branching, violating uniqueness. Search over depths for a level of one child subtree containing no admitted strings. Membership at a finite level is decidable, so this search is effective and eventually succeeds for the finite-height child. Choose the other child and repeat. At most one child is finite-height because the current node has an infinite continuation. This computes the unique path. No uniform procedure for discovering an isolating \(\sigma\) from an arbitrary finitely-many-paths tree has been claimed.

Applied to \(G\), this gives a computable path in \(T\), contradicting the diagonal construction. This proves P1. More generally, an effective successful generated binary subtree of \(T\) cannot have only finitely many infinite paths, even if its finite-level widths are unbounded.

## 3 Randomised services

A computable coin-transducer may instead emit complete finite sets \(G_n\) using a fair random tape. On its success event it completes every generation, gives a prefix-closed admitted subtree, all levels are nonempty, and the widths along that realised history have a finite bound. The bound may vary with the tape and need not be known. No restrictions are required on failed runs.

**Theorem P2.** This bounded-width population success event has probability zero.

For each fixed finite bit string \(\sigma\), construct a computable coin-transducer \(A_{\sigma}\) which simulates the population service on the same random tape and caches completed levels. It first waits for the level of \(\sigma\) and verifies \(\sigma\) is present. It then outputs \(\sigma\) and applies the two-child finite-height search above, obtaining level-membership answers by waiting for the population service to finish any requested level. If a run is invalid, this machine may diverge or give a bad output; its behaviour there is immaterial.

On each successful bounded-width population run, \(G\) has finitely many nonempty infinite paths. At least one has an isolating finite prefix \(\sigma\). On this tape \(A_{\sigma}\) computes that path, since every requested population level eventually finishes and every discarded child has finite height. Therefore the population success event is contained in the union, over all finite \(\sigma\), of the success events of \(A_{\sigma}\) at producing a path in \(T\). Each event has probability zero by Theorem \(R\). There are countably many finite \(\sigma\), so their union has probability zero. This proves P2. The same argument covers any successful run whose generated subtree has an isolated infinite path, a slightly broader scope than finite width.

No extraction algorithm is claimed to choose the right \(\sigma\) from the random tape uniformly. The fixed countable family suffices for the measure argument. The look-ahead simulations read the original random tape sequentially and cache finite computation; they do not call a noncomputable oracle. They may wait indefinitely on failed runs, which Theorem \(R\) already permits.

## 4 Positive counterpart expanding finite populations

There is a deterministic effective service with a finite, nonempty population at every generation if no uniform width bound is imposed: output exactly all admitted strings \(T\cap\{0,1\}^{n}\) at generation \(n\). Decidable membership makes this a finite exhaustive computation. Prefix closure supplies a parent at the preceding generation; existence of each level makes the population nonempty. Every candidate history obeys local admission, although some eventually terminate.

This service has unbounded width; otherwise P1 would contradict its existence. Its generation-set width is unbounded, and its work per generation may be very large. If every listed member must be concurrently live, unbounded concurrent capacity follows under that extra realisation contract. No monotone growth, general memory lower bound or implementation-independent concurrency requirement is proved. It provides population-level continuation without selecting a computable surviving infinite lineage. This is a legitimate different service, not a failed single-lineage policy.

The separation is therefore among effective success at every independently requested finite horizon, committed lineage continuation, bounded-width population continuation, and unbounded-width population continuation. These distinctions must be fixed by the organisational contract before a mathematical impossibility is applied. Finitely many physical resources at each time is not the same assumption as one uniform finite population bound for all time. Nor does bounded instantaneous activity establish this closed-generation contract: rollback, retained checkpoints, serial reconstruction and reopening past generations can generate a wider historical tree. Those services require their own analysis.

# Every effective finite lookahead still leaves the selection boundary

Proof level. The finite-lookahead extension has recorded independent formal acceptance for a supplied total computable finite demand, strengthened admission, equality of path classes, finite planners and base-tree witnesses, and the operational no-effective-commitment boundary. The implemented finite enumeration order differs from the written lexicographic planner; equality of their finite outputs is not claimed. The inherited randomised and population consequences remain written arguments. Supplementary witnesses concern the base service, not recursively strengthened viability. This programme-specific corollary addresses finite genuine onward capability at every handoff. No historical-priority claim is made.

## Precise contract

Let \(T\) be the decidable prefix-closed binary tree used in the effective renewal construction. Let \(h\) be any total computable function from finite bit strings to natural numbers. Its value \(h(s)\) is the number of further steps whose availability must be demonstrated at history \(s\). The function may depend on the entire history, grow arbitrarily rapidly and return different finite demands for different branches. No fixed upper bound is assumed.

For a finite history \(s\) define \(\operatorname{Look}(s)\) to mean that some \(T\)-admitted extension of \(s\) has length \(|s|+h(s)\). Checking this is a finite exhaustive search over the \(2^{h(s)}\) possible suffixes, after computing \(h(s)\). Thus Look is decidable. When \(h(s)=0\) it says exactly that \(s\) itself is in \(T\).

Define the strengthened admission tree

\[
L_h=\{s\in T:\text{every prefix }t\text{ of }s\text{ satisfies }\operatorname{Look}(t)\}.
\]

Only finite-valued \(h\) supplied by terminating effective code is covered. Totality of that code is an assumption of this theorem; the checker does not decide totality from an arbitrary supplied program. An oracle returning noncomputable demands or an infinitary demand is a different contract. The computation cost can be enormous; no practical efficiency is claimed.

## Theorem

\(L_h\) is decidable and prefix closed, has a uniformly computable admitted plan at every finite horizon, and has exactly the same infinite paths as \(T\). Consequently it has no computable infinite committed lineage. For the diagonal target it also inherits the probability-zero randomised-lineage and closed-generation finite-width population results.

### Proof

For a supplied \(s\), compute \(T\)-membership, enumerate its finitely many prefixes, and run the terminating finite Look test for each. This decides membership. If \(s\) passes, each prefix \(u\) belongs to \(T\) by prefix closure, and each prefix of \(u\) was among the prefixes already checked for \(s\). Hence \(u\) passes. This proves prefix closure.

If \(f\) is a path of \(T\), take any prefix \(s\) of \(f\). For every prefix \(t\) of \(s\), the longer prefix of \(f\) of length \(|t|+h(t)\) is an admitted witness for \(\operatorname{Look}(t)\). Therefore \(s\) belongs to \(L_h\). Every \(T\)-path is an \(L_h\)-path. The reverse inclusion follows from \(L_h\) being a subset of \(T\). This proves equality of path classes.

\(T\) has a mathematical infinite path, so every finite level of \(L_h\) is nonempty. Given \(n\), inspect the finite list of all length-\(n\) bit strings with the \(L_h\) decider and return the first admitted one in lexicographic order. The search terminates because that level is nonempty. Its finite computation witnesses a uniform horizon-indexed planner without assuming a computable infinite path.

Any computable \(L_h\)-path would also be a computable \(T\)-path, contradiction. Likewise, a coin-transducer producing an \(L_h\)-path with positive probability would produce a \(T\)-path with positive probability, contradicting Theorem \(R\). A successful closed-generation bounded-width population in \(L_h\) would be such a population in \(T\), contradicting P1 or P2. No new probabilistic or compactness premise is needed for these inherited conclusions.

## What the finite certificates establish

For a finite admitted plan \(s\), a certificate may include, for every prefix \(t\) of \(s\), one explicit admitted extension of length \(|t|+h(t)\). The same finite search computes these witnesses. The supplied extensions need not be mutually compatible with one another or with the eventual policy's later choices. Their stated existential finite-capacity claims are nevertheless true and decidably checkable.

In particular, \(h\) constantly one requires a genuine admitted next step at each prefix, including the final handoff. Larger \(h\) certifies more future construction. Arbitrarily fast but computable finite lookahead is still covered. This is stronger than keeping the same source code and merely hoping it can repeat.

It remains essential to state which service those witnesses promise. They certify future steps in the base \(T\) service. They do not prove that every intermediate node of each supplementary witness is itself admissible under the strengthened \(L_h\) contract. Requiring supplementary branches to preserve the very same strengthened condition recursively is a greatest-fixed-point/viability requirement, not the finite existential claim just proved. Substituting that stronger meaning for Look would change the theorem's hypothesis.

Nor does this claim failure of every practical planner or every actual organisation. \(T\) is a constructed counterexample and \(h\) is a specified effective local rule. The result shows why any finite lookahead guarantee, even an unboundedly growing one, needs an additional effective closed-region argument before it can justify one irrevocable indefinitely renewing policy.

The separate positive enumerable-pruned-witness criterion supplies exactly such an argument when its applicability is warranted. None of these negative boundaries removes the earned credit of finite completed renewal, finite onward capacity or expanding-population continuation under its own permission/resource contract.

## 1 What is being classified

The existing continuation argument gives an individual decidable binary tree with plans for every finite horizon but no computable infinite path. It also proves that a tree has a computable path exactly when it contains a rooted pruned computably enumerable subtree. Given an already warranted enumerator for that subtree, a uniform algorithm extracts a policy. That theorem expressly leaves witness discovery and certification open.

Here the input is a finite description of a binary admission tree. The decision service asks whether **some ordinary computable committed-lineage policy exists**. A separate verification service receives a proposed policy program or proposed subtree enumerator and asks whether that particular candidate is globally valid. These are distinct from checking a finite route.

The precise results below are:

1. In a syntax-total presentation of rooted primitive-recursive binary trees, having any infinite path is \(\Pi^{0}_1\)-complete.

3. Validating a supplied ordinary program as a total path is \(\Pi^{0}_2\)-complete. Validating a supplied \(c\).\(e\). rooted pruned-subtree enumerator is also \(\Pi^{0}_2\)-complete.
4. Consequently, there is no sound, complete effective finite-certificate scheme for computable-policy existence over this input domain. Useful sound but incomplete certification remains possible.

Completeness means completeness under total computable many-one reductions. All hierarchy levels are arithmetical, with computable matrices. No polynomial-time, finite-resource, or practical-runtime bound is implied.

## 2 A total input presentation including malformed descriptions

Fix a standard primitive-recursive coding of finite binary words by natural numbers, with primitive-recursive length, coordinate, prefix, substring and concatenation operations. Fix a conventional finite syntax for primitive-recursive functions: zero, successor, projections, composition and primitive recursion, with arities checked syntactically.

Given a natural number \(e\):

- If \(e\) is not a well-formed unary primitive-recursive program, set \(T_e=\{\varepsilon\}\).
- Otherwise let \(p_e\) be its total natural-valued function and put \(\varepsilon\) in \(T_e\). For a nonempty binary word \(\sigma\), put \(\sigma\) in \(T_e\) exactly when \(p_e(\tau )\) is nonzero for every nonempty prefix \(\tau\) of \(\sigma\).

Thus every code denotes a rooted prefix-closed binary tree. Malformed input has an explicit negative meaning for both infinite-path questions; no hidden totality promise is used. The root is forced, so the value \(p_e(\varepsilon )\) is ignored. For each fixed valid \(e\), the predicate defining \(T_e\) is primitive recursive. Every rooted primitive-recursive binary tree occurs, by taking its characteristic predicate.

The two-variable evaluator \((e,\sigma ) \mapsto  1[\sigma \in T_e]\) is total computable. It is **not** asserted to be primitive recursive: the universal evaluator for arbitrary primitive-recursive syntax is not a primitive-recursive function. This distinction does not affect arithmetical formulas with computable matrices. For the later \(\forall n\exists s\) formulas, a primitive-recursive matrix can instead use bounded simulation of the evaluator, enlarging \(s\) to include its runtime. For a \(\Pi^{0}_1\) formula \(\forall n L(e,n)\) with total computable \(L\), use the equivalent condition that no computation of \(L(e,n)\) rejects within any stage \(s\); the two universal variables combine into one. Neither conversion raises the stated hierarchy level.

Write \([T]\) for the infinite binary sequences all of whose finite prefixes lie in \(T\). Define

\[
\mathrm{INF}=\{e:[T_e]\ne\varnothing\},
\]

\[
\mathrm{CP}=\{e:[T_e]\text{ contains a total computable binary sequence}\}.
\]

The description is a supplied representation, not an assertion that arbitrary Turing-machine decider code can be automatically recognised as total. Section 8 treats that alternative interface explicitly.

## 3 Mathematical infinite path existence is Pi zero one complete

For a binary tree \(T\), finite-branching compactness gives

\[
[T]\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall n\ \exists\sigma\in T,\quad|\sigma|=n.
\]

For fixed \(e\),\(n\), the right-hand finite-level test is decidable uniformly: inspect the \(2^n\) binary words using the total evaluator. Therefore \(\mathrm{INF}\) is \(\Pi^{0}_1\).

For hardness, fix an enumeration \(\varphi_d\) of partial computable functions with primitive-recursive bounded-step simulation. From \(d\) construct the rooted tree \(B_d\) which contains a nonempty word \(\sigma\) of length \(n\) exactly when \(\varphi_d(d)\) has not halted within \(n\) steps. This membership predicate is primitive recursive, prefix closed, and compiles effectively into the syntax of Section 2. If \(\varphi_d(d)\) never halts, \(B_d\) is the full binary tree. If it halts, \(B_d\) has finite height. Hence

\[
[B_d]\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi_d(d)\text{ does not halt}.
\]

For completeness without merely naming a complete set: if A is any \(\Pi^{0}_1\) set, choose a computable predicate \(R\) with \(a\in A \Longleftrightarrow  \forall n R(a,n)\). A program searching for the first \(n\) with \(\neg R(a,n)\) halts exactly when \(a\notin A\). Its index is computable from a. Applying the bounded-simulation construction to that program gives \(A\le_m\mathrm{INF}\). Thus \(\mathrm{INF}\) is \(\Pi^{0}_1\)-complete. ∎

Absence of any infinite path is therefore positively semidecidable: find an empty level. Existence of an infinite path is not, in general, positively semidecidable. This uses finite branching; it is not the classification for arbitrary infinitely branching trees.

## 4 Computable path existence upper bound

Let \(\varphi_p\) enumerate ordinary partial computable natural-valued functions. Define \(Q(e,p,n,s)\) to say:

- each computation \(\varphi_p(i)\), \(i<n\), halts within \(s\) steps;
- each resulting value is 0 or 1; and
- the resulting length-\(n\) word belongs to \(T_e\).

\(Q\) is decidable uniformly. Then

\[
e\in\mathrm{CP}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists p\ \forall n\ \exists s\ Q(e,p,n,s).
\]

The forward direction uses the finite maximum of the runtimes on inputs below \(n\). Conversely the formula makes \(\varphi_p\) total and binary-valued, and makes every prefix admitted. Therefore \(\mathrm{CP}\) belongs to \(\Sigma^{0}_3\).

This upper bound alone is not the classification. The next section supplies the lower bound for the specific syntax-total binary presentation, including a family with a path at every input.

## 5 A complete Sigma zero three lower bound reduction

### 5.1 Fixed primitive recursive obstruction K

Use the diagonal binary tree already employed by the continuation argument:

\[
\begin{aligned}\sigma\in K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad &\text{with }n=|\sigma|,\text{ for every }i<n,\\ &\varphi_i(i)\text{ halting within }n\text{ steps with bit }b\\ &\text{implies }\sigma(i)=1-b.\end{aligned}
\]

\(K\) is primitive recursive and prefix closed. At each length \(n\) the word \(H(n)\) which uses the required opposite bit at constrained coordinates and 0 elsewhere belongs to \(K\); \(H\) is a total computable finite-horizon planner (indeed primitive recursive in the selected bounded-step coding). An infinite path exists by choosing the opposite of each eventual bit-valued diagonal computation, and 0 at the remaining coordinates.

No computable infinite path \(f\) exists. If \(\varphi_i=f\), then \(\varphi_i(i)\) halts with \(f(i)\); sufficiently long prefixes in \(K\) would require \(f(i)=1-f(i)\), a contradiction. Thus \(K\) has arbitrarily long effectively constructible finite words but no computable infinite path.

### 5.2 A component coding one Pi zero two condition

Let A be an arbitrary \(\Sigma^{0}_3\) set. Fix a primitive-recursive relation \(R\) such that

\[
a\in A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists x\ \forall y\ \exists z\ R(a,x,y,z).
\]

Using a primitive-recursive matrix is legitimate: a decidable matrix can be replaced by a bounded computation certificate and its time folded into the final existential witness. \(R\) is fixed for this reduction; no universal primitive-recursive evaluator is assumed.

For fixed a,\(x\) define the progress function

\[
\ell(a,x,s)=\max\{k\le s:\forall y<k\ \exists z\le s\ R(a,x,y,z)\}.
\]

The set under the maximum contains 0. All quantifiers in this definition are bounded, so \(\ell\) is primitive recursive. It is nondecreasing in \(s\). It is unbounded exactly when \(\forall y\exists z R(a,x,y,z)\). If that condition fails first at \(y_0\), then \(\ell \le y_0\) at every stage, so \(\ell\) increases at only finitely many stages.

Put a cut at coordinate 0. For each positive integer \(s\), put exactly one further cut at coordinate \(s\) if

\[
\ell(a,x,s)>\ell(a,x,s-1).
\]

A jump by several progress values still creates only one cut, at that stage. Cut positions are strictly increasing positive integers after the initial 0.

Define \(U_{a,x}\) on finite binary words as follows. Given \(\tau\) of length \(n\), compute all cuts at positions at most \(n\), listed as

\[
0=b_0<b_1<\cdots<b_k\le n.
\]

Require each completed block \(\tau [b_j:b_{j+1})\) for \(j<k\) to lie in \(K\), and require the current partial block \(\tau [b_k:n)\) to lie in \(K\). If \(n\) is a cut, the last partial block is empty, which \(K\) admits. All cuts needed are found using only stages \(\le n\). Consequently membership is primitive recursive uniformly in the parameters a,\(x\) for fixed \(R\).

This definition is prefix closed. Truncating a word preserves every earlier completed block; its final block is a prefix of either a completed block or the old final partial block, and \(K\) is prefix closed.

**Component lemma.** \(U_{a,x}\) has a computable infinite path iff \(\forall y\exists z R(a,x,y,z)\).

Proof in the positive case. There are infinitely many cuts. To compute the bit at coordinate \(i\), find the last cut \(b\le i\) by the bounded search through \(i\), then search for the next cut \(c>i\). The latter search terminates because cuts are unbounded. Output \(H(c-b)(i-b)\). For every block \([b,c)\), all these coordinate computations use the same endpoints and the same complete word \(H(c-b)\). Thus the resulting total computable sequence fills each block with a member of \(K\). Every finite prefix satisfies the completed-block and partial-block tests, so it is a path of \(U_{a,x}\). No computable bound on the search for \(c\) is claimed or required.

Proof in the negative case. There are finitely many cuts; let \(b\) be the last. If \(f\) were a computable infinite path of \(U_{a,x}\), define \(g(i)=f(b+i)\). For every \(m\), the membership test for \(f\upharpoonright (b+m)\) requires its current partial block \(f[b:b+m)\) to belong to \(K\). Hence \(g\) would be a computable infinite \(K\)-path, impossible. The finite number \(b\) is hardcoded in this contradiction. There is no claim that the last cut can be found uniformly from a,\(x\). ∎

The distinction between an actual late cut and knowledge that no further cut will ever occur is essential. Testing longer finite blocks never certifies that a cut is last.

### 5.3 Taking the existential union without a computable limit spine

The tempting tree with components attached at \(0^x1\) is unsuitable: it would always contain the computable path \(0^\omega\). Instead use \(K\) itself as the skeleton.

For \(\sigma =\sigma(0)\)⋯\(\sigma(x-1)\), define the binary code

\[
c(\sigma)=0\sigma(0)0\sigma(1)\cdots0\sigma(x-1).
\]

At each pair boundary, 0 begins another encoded skeleton bit; 1 is a permanent exit marker. Define \(S_a\) to consist of the following finite words:

1. \(c(\sigma )\), for \(\sigma \in K\);
2. \(c(\sigma)0\), for \(\sigma \in K\);
3. \(c(\sigma)1\tau\), for \(\sigma \in K\) and \(\tau \in U_{a,|\sigma |}\).

These forms have a decidable, unambiguous parse: scan pairs beginning with 0 until a 1 occurs at a pair boundary, or the word ends. A final unpaired 0 is case 2. Once the exit marker is encountered, the rest of the word is ordinary component data, with no further control interpretation.

The tree is rooted and prefix closed. A prefix inside the encoded skeleton is an encoded \(K\)-prefix or its final unpaired 0. A prefix after an exit keeps the same finite skeleton and truncates a \(U\)-word. Each membership check uses only bounded parsing and the fixed primitive-recursive \(K\), \(\ell\) and \(R\) operations; hence the predicate for \(S_a\) is primitive recursive.

For the fixed \(R\), a compiler can substitute the numerical parameter a into this finite membership-program template. This is a total computable map \(a\mapsto e(a)\) of ordinary numbers into valid primitive-recursive syntax. Because \(S_a\) is already rooted and prefix closed, the normalisation in Section 2 leaves it unchanged: \(T_{e(a)}=S_a\). This establishes effectiveness at the supplied-description level, rather than merely naming an abstract family of decidable sets.

Every \(S_a\) has an infinite path: if \(f\in [K]\), its pairwise encoding \(0f(0)0f(1)\cdots\) is a path of \(S_a\). This existence statement is noncomputable and holds whether or not \(a\in A\).

An infinite \(S_a\)-path has exactly two possibilities:

- It never exits. Reading the second bit of every pair gives an infinite \(K\)-path. Therefore a never-exiting \(S_a\)-path cannot be computable.
- It exits after \(x\) completed pairs, with a finite \(\sigma \in K\) of length \(x\). Its remaining tail is an infinite \(U_{a,x}\)-path. If the complete \(S_a\)-path is computable, the tail is computable, so the component lemma implies \(\forall y\exists z R(a,x,y,z)\).

Conversely, if an \(x\) satisfies that condition, choose the computable component path supplied by the component lemma and prepend \(c\)(\(H(x)\))1. This finite prefix is admitted because \(H(x)\in K\). The complete concatenation is a computable \(S_a\)-path.

We have therefore proved

\[
\begin{aligned}a\in A\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_a\text{ has a computable infinite path}\\ &\Longleftrightarrow\quad e(a)\in\mathrm{CP}.\end{aligned}
\]

Since A was arbitrary, \(\mathrm{CP}\) is \(\Sigma^{0}_3\)-hard. Together with Section 4 it is \(\Sigma^{0}_3\)-complete, with hardness witnessed entirely by mathematically infinite rooted primitive-recursive binary trees. ∎

### 6.1 Supplied policy code

Let \(\mathrm{PATH}(e,p)\) mean that \(\varphi_p\) is total, binary-valued, and belongs to [\(T_e\)]. Section 4 gives

\[
\mathrm{PATH}(e,p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall n\ \exists s\ Q(e,p,n,s),

so \(\mathrm{PATH}\) is \(\Pi^{0}_2\).

Construct \(p_b\) whose computation on input \(n\) searches for a witnessing \(s\) and then outputs 0. Let \(e_{\mathrm{full}}\) be a fixed code for the full binary tree. Then \(\mathrm{PATH}(e_{\mathrm{full}},p_b)\) holds exactly when \(b\in B\). ### 6.2 Supplied enumerable pruned region

Fix a standard enumeration \(W_j\) of \(c\).\(e\). sets of codes of finite binary strings. Define \(\mathrm{PRUNED}(e,j)\) by these four conditions:

- \(\varepsilon \in W_j\);
- \(W_j\subseteq T_e\);
- every prefix of a member of \(W_j\) belongs to \(W_j\);
- every \(\sigma \in W_j\) has at least one immediate child \(\sigma 0\) or \(\sigma 1\) in \(W_j\).

Membership in the range \(W_j\) is a \(\Sigma^{0}_1\) condition. Root membership is \(\Sigma^{0}_1\). Soundness \(W_j\subseteq T_e\) is \(\Pi^{0}_1\). Prefix closure and pruning each have the form: whenever a node appears by a given stage, the required finite successor/prefix evidence eventually appears. These are \(\Pi^{0}_2\) formulas, and their conjunction with the other two conditions is \(\Pi^{0}_2\).

For hardness use the same arbitrary \(\Pi^{0}_2\) predicate \(b\in B \Longleftrightarrow  \forall n\exists s R_0(b,n,s)\). Uniformly enumerate \(0^k\) exactly when witnesses have been found for every \(n<k\). The empty word is enumerated immediately. If \(b\in B\), the range is \(\{0^k:k\in\mathbb N\}\), a rooted pruned subtree of the full binary tree. If \(b\)∉\(B\) and \(n_0\) is the least failed requirement, the range is exactly \(\{0^k:k\le n_0\}\); its final member has no child in the range. Soundness and prefix closure hold in both cases. Thus \(\mathrm{PRUNED}(e_{\mathrm{full}},j_b) \Longleftrightarrow  b\in B\), establishing \(\Pi^{0}_2\)-completeness. ∎

The inherited extraction theorem says \(\mathrm{CP}(e) \Longleftrightarrow  \exists j \mathrm{PRUNED}(e,j)\), and provides a uniform ordinary policy from any valid \(j\). The new point is the exact complexity of certifying that the enumerator is valid, not any weakening of that positive theorem. Enumeration without the global pruning warrant still does not justify waiting forever for a next child.

## 7 What a certification service can and cannot promise

### No sound and complete effective finite certificate scheme

Suppose \(C(e,w)\) were a computable acceptance test for finite certificates, sound and complete in the sense

\[
e\in\mathrm{CP}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists w\ C(e,w).
\]

Then \(\mathrm{CP}\) would be computably enumerable, contradicting its \(\Sigma^{0}_3\)-completeness and the strictness of the arithmetical hierarchy. The same conclusion holds for a \(c\).\(e\). acceptance relation: dovetail all candidate certificates and their acceptance computations. A sound effective formal proof system may prove useful instances, but cannot have such complete positive coverage of \(\mathrm{CP}\) on all these descriptions.

Likewise no effective verifier can positively recognise exactly all valid ordinary policy programs or all valid pruned enumerators: those relations are \(\Pi^{0}_2\)-complete rather than \(c\).\(e\). This does not say that no valid instance has a finite mathematical proof, or that checking a given formal proof must be hard. It says the collection of soundly certified instances cannot cover every true instance in this domain.

No total ordinary algorithm decides \(\mathrm{CP}\); neither its positive nor its negative cases admit a complete ordinary semidecision procedure. Even on a supplied always-infinite reduction family, a total correct decision procedure would decide every \(\Sigma^{0}_3\) set.

### Witness extraction remains genuinely constructive

If a valid ordinary path index is supplied with adequate warrant, execute it. If a valid pruned enumerator is supplied with adequate warrant, use the inherited next-child search. Those are ordinary effective services, not the impossible complete certifier.

A service which halts and returns a valid policy exactly on all positive instances would also make \(\mathrm{CP}\) \(c\).\(e\). by its halting domain, and is impossible. A weaker promise-only selector may have arbitrary behaviour off positive instances, so that halting-domain argument does not apply to it. The existing width-two diagonal family independently rules out an ordinary uniform selector even when every input is promised to have a computable path. Do not confuse the two impossibility arguments.

In oracle language, the third Turing jump can decide \(\mathrm{CP}\), while the second jump can test \(\mathrm{PATH}\) and search for a valid ordinary program when one exists. This is a mathematical upper bound on information, not an implementation recommendation or an assertion that those oracles are physically available. The selected successful program, once found, is still an ordinary computable program.

### Legitimate restricted positive services

The inherited finite-graph service remains available: with a completely supplied finite transition graph and fully decidable finite state/authority contract, repeatedly remove vertices without successors. A surviving root yields a finite invariant and a repeatable next-choice rule; a removed root has a finite-depth obstruction. This computes a complete decision and synthesis result for that restricted representation.

A finite graph purported to abstract an arbitrary \(T_e\) is a different input. The simulation/inclusion claim connecting it to the unbounded admission tree must itself be warranted; it is not automatically a finite check merely because the proposed abstract graph is finite. The present classification limits a universal certifier for arbitrary descriptions, not restricted certified models.

## 8 Alternative raw decider interface the presentation changes the level

Suppose instead that input \(d\) is arbitrary Turing-machine code intended to decide membership of finite binary words. Require it to be total and Boolean, to accept \(\varepsilon\), and to define a prefix-closed set. Declare any syntactically malformed, nontotal, non-Boolean, root-rejecting or non-prefix-closed description invalid and negative for both decision questions. This is a mathematical invalid-input policy; it is not an algorithm claiming to detect all invalid descriptions.

Under this convention, nonempty-path existence is \(\Pi^{0}_2\)-complete, not \(\Pi^{0}_1\)-complete. For the upper bound, for every \(n\) require a stage \(s\) by which the machine has given Boolean answers on all words of length at most \(n\), those answers form a rooted prefix-closed finite tree, and some length-\(n\) word is accepted. This \(\forall n\exists s\) condition is equivalent to a valid total binary-tree decider with an infinite path.

For hardness, from \(b\in B \Longleftrightarrow  \forall n\exists s R_0(b,n,s)\) construct a machine which, on a word of length \(m\), waits for witnesses to \(R_0(b,n,s)\) for every \(n\le m\) and then accepts the word. If \(b\in B\), it is the total decider for the full binary tree. If \(b\)∉\(B\), it is nontotal and therefore invalid. This reduces any \(\Pi^{0}_2\) set to the raw nonempty-path problem.

Computable-path existence for this raw interface is still \(\Sigma^{0}_3\)-complete. The validity condition is \(\Pi^{0}_2\), and the existence of a path program is \(\Sigma^{0}_3\), so their conjunction is \(\Sigma^{0}_3\). More explicitly, put the finite validity and tree checks together with the first \(n\) policy-output and admission checks under \(\exists p\forall n\exists s\). For hardness, compile the always-total primitive-recursive tree descriptions from Section 5 into ordinary Turing-machine deciders. Every reduction output is valid.

This comparison is why one must not say simply “the index set for decidable trees” without specifying how totality and malformed codes are handled. The syntax-total presentation in Sections 2–7 makes the \(\Pi^{0}_1\) versus \(\Sigma^{0}_3\) comparison exact without an uncomputable promise validator.

Use the same product construction as the earlier effective-renewal argument. The full two-bit delay state and alternating encoding preserve stateful execution and readout exactly. The Section 5 hardness family is mathematically infinite at every input. ## 1 The exact source language

Let \(\mathbb{N}\) include zero. Define finite expression trees by

\[
e ::= c\mid x\mid e+e\mid e\cdot e
  \mid \operatorname{if}\ e=e\ \operatorname{then}\ e\ \operatorname{else}\ e,
\qquad c\in\mathbb N.
\]

Write \(\llbracket e\rrbracket(n)\) for the ordinary total natural-number interpretation. A conditional compares the actual natural values of its two test expressions and selects its first branch on equality. All four children are recursively in the same grammar. ## 2 Polynomial representations and an effective root bound

A polynomial in \(\mathbb N[X]\) is represented by its finite coefficient map, with zero entries omitted. Equal exponents are aggregated; polynomial equality means coefficient equality. The zero polynomial is the empty map. Integer polynomials are represented in the same way with coefficients in \(\mathbb Z\). Addition, multiplication, composition and coefficient subtraction are effective finite operations. Subtraction used in this construction is an operation on coefficient maps, not a source-language constructor.

For a nonzero integer polynomial

\[
H(X)=a_dX^d+\sum_{i<d}a_iX^i,
\quad a_d\ne0,
\]

define \(B(H)=1\) when \(d=0\), and

\[
B(H)=1+\sum_{i<d}|a_i|
\]

when \(d\ge1\). Set \(B(0)=1\) for bookkeeping only.

**Lemma 1.** If \(H\ne0\) and \(n\ge B(H)\), then \(H(n)\ne0\).

**Proof.** A nonzero constant never vanishes. Otherwise put \(S=\sum_{i<d}|a_i|\). For \(n\ge S+1\), one has \(n\ge1\) and

\[
\left|\sum_{i<d}a_in^i\right|
\le S n^{d-1}<n^d\le |a_d|n^d.
\]

The lower terms cannot cancel the leading term. This covers missing coefficients, zero constant term, \(S=0\), and degree one. ∎

Consequently, if \(P,Q\in\mathbb N[X]\) are distinct, their exact set of natural coincidences

\[
Z(P,Q)=\{n\in\mathbb N:P(n)=Q(n)\}
\]

can be computed by testing the finite range \(0\le n<B(P-Q)\). There is no claim that this bound is minimal or computationally efficient. If \(P=Q\), their coincidence set is all naturals and is handled by a separate branch; it must not be passed to this finite-root construction as though it were finite.

Lemma 1 is the elementary bound already established in Section 10.1 of the Fifteenth polynomial-equality paper. Its use here imports no new number-theoretic premise.

### 2.1 The coefficient sum bound used by the checked implementation

The implementation uses a constant-first finite natural coefficient list \(p=[a_0,\ldots,a_d]\) and exact Horner evaluation

\[
\operatorname{ev}([],n)=0,\qquad
\operatorname{ev}(a::p,n)=a+n\operatorname{ev}(p,n).
\]

Let \(\operatorname{tr}(p)\) remove only the high-degree zero suffix. This retains every coefficient and polynomial value at its correct degree. For two possibly untrimmed lists define

\[
D(p,q)=2+\sum_{a\in p}a+\sum_{b\in q}b.
\]

Here the sums are over list entries, including repetitions if equal coefficients occur at different degrees. They are not sums of distinct coefficient values.

**Lemma 1a.** If \(n\ge D(p,q)\), then

\[
\operatorname{ev}(p,n)=\operatorname{ev}(q,n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{tr}(p)=\operatorname{tr}(q).
\]

**Proof.** Each coefficient in either list is at most that list's sum, so it is strictly below \(n\), and \(n\ge2\). Taking an evaluation modulo \(n\) therefore recovers its constant coefficient. Equal evaluations have equal constant coefficients. Cancel that common coefficient, then cancel the positive factor \(n\), obtaining equality of the remaining Horner evaluations. Repeating this argument down the finite lists recovers every coefficient. If one list ends first, the remaining nonnegative evaluation is zero. Positivity of \(n\) forces each remaining coefficient to be zero, so both trimmed lists are equal. Conversely, removing a high-degree zero suffix preserves every Horner evaluation, and equal trimmed lists have equal evaluations. ∎

This is the ordinary uniqueness argument for positional natural digits. It avoids integer subtraction, division by a leading coefficient, polynomial factorisation and classical root extraction. The checked declarations `polyEval_coefficients_of_small_digits` and `eval_eq_iff_polyEqual_of_bound` prove it for the actual executable lists. `polyEqual_iff_all_eval` derives full polynomial function equality.

The bounds \(B(P-Q)\) and \(D(p,q)\) need not agree. Both are complete at their stated interfaces. The first supports the sparse finite-root construction below; the checked implementation uses the second to obtain a conservative cutoff and evaluates the whole finite prefix below that cutoff. No minimality or efficiency claim is made for either.

## 3 Canonical finite descriptions

A canonical description is a pair \((P,E)\), where:

- \(P\in\mathbb N[X]\) is in the finite coefficient representation above;
- \(E\) is a finite partial map from \(\mathbb N\) to \(\mathbb N\);
- every \(n\in\operatorname{dom}E\) satisfies \(E(n)\ne P(n)\).

The map may be serialised as increasing distinct natural keys and their values. The last condition excludes redundant exceptions. Its interpretation is

\[
\Phi(P,E)(n)=
\begin{cases}
E(n)&n\in\operatorname{dom}E,\\
P(n)&n\notin\operatorname{dom}E.
\end{cases}
\]

The polynomial is called a tail polynomial because the function equals it above every exceptional key. The description does not require retaining a particular cutoff; the exceptional domain is an exact finite set, which can have gaps.

For a finite set \(W\subseteq\mathbb N\), a polynomial \(P\in\mathbb N[X]\), and a computable natural-valued function \(h\) evaluable on \(W\), define

\[
\operatorname{trim}(P,W,h)
=\{n\mapsto h(n): n\in W,\ h(n)\ne P(n)\}.
\]

This is a finite effective map. It guarantees the canonical nonredundancy condition; it establishes a representation of \(h\) only when equality with \(P\) outside \(W\) has separately been proved.

## 4 Syntax directed normalisation

We define \(\mathcal C(e)=(P_e,E_e)\) recursively. In the following clauses, already normalised children are interpreted by \(\Phi\); no claim about their values is inferred from the tail polynomial alone at an exceptional key.

For a constant \(c\), put \(\mathcal C(c)=(c,\varnothing)\). For the input, put \(\mathcal C(x)=(X,\varnothing)\).

For \(e=a+b\), let \(W=\operatorname{dom}E_a\cup\operatorname{dom}E_b\), put \(P_e=P_a+P_b\), and put

\[
E_e=\operatorname{trim}
\bigl(P_e,W,n\mapsto\Phi(P_a,E_a)(n)+\Phi(P_b,E_b)(n)\bigr).
\]

For \(e=a\cdot b\), use the same \(W\), put \(P_e=P_aP_b\), and trim the product of the two complete child functions on \(W\).

For

\[
e=\operatorname{if}\ a=b\ \operatorname{then}\ c\ \operatorname{else}\ d,
\]

let

\[
W_0=\operatorname{dom}E_a\cup\operatorname{dom}E_b
\cup\operatorname{dom}E_c\cup\operatorname{dom}E_d.
\]

There are two cases.

1. If \(P_a=P_b\), put \(P_e=P_c\) and \(W=W_0\).
2. If \(P_a\ne P_b\), put \(P_e=P_d\) and \(W=W_0\cup Z(P_a,P_b)\).

In either case evaluate the conditional using the full child functions on each \(n\in W\), and trim those values against \(P_e\). This defines \(E_e\).

Every step is finite. The root calculation occurs only for unequal polynomials. All tail operations preserve natural coefficients. The method may duplicate syntax or expand large coefficient maps and exceptional sets; no polynomial-time, practical memory, or fixed testing-budget bound is asserted.

**Theorem 2.** For every source expression \(e\), the algorithm terminates and returns a canonical pair satisfying

\[
\forall n\in\mathbb N,
\qquad\llbracket e\rrbracket(n)=\Phi(\mathcal C(e))(n).
\]

**Proof.** Termination follows by structural recursion, finite polynomial operations, finite set operations, and Lemma 1's finite root search. Prove correctness by induction on the expression.

The constant and variable clauses are exact. In the sum and product clauses, outside \(W\) both children equal their polynomials, so the chosen sum or product polynomial gives the source value. At every point of \(W\), the algorithm evaluates both children correctly by induction and records the result unless it is already the chosen polynomial value. Trimming therefore preserves the source value at all points.

For a conditional, first suppose \(P_a=P_b\). Outside \(W_0\), neither test has an exception, so their values agree and the selected branch is \(c\). That branch has no exception there, so the source value equals \(P_c(n)\), the chosen tail. At points of \(W_0\), the full guard and selected branch are evaluated correctly by induction and then trimmed.

Next suppose \(P_a\ne P_b\). Outside \(W_0\cup Z(P_a,P_b)\), both tests equal their respective polynomials and these are unequal. The selected branch is \(d\), whose value there is \(P_d(n)\). On the finite union, the guard and branch again have their exact inductively supplied values. Trimming is therefore correct. Its definition also ensures that every retained map entry is a genuine deviation. ∎

The test may switch branch arbitrarily many times within a finite initial region. The proof does not incorrectly infer that equal tail polynomials make two test expressions equal at their exceptional points.

## 5 Exact expressive image and uniqueness

**Theorem 3.** For a function \(f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(f=\llbracket e\rrbracket\) for a finite expression in the declared grammar.
2. There are \(P\in\mathbb N[X]\) and a finite canonical exception map \(E\) with \(f=\Phi(P,E)\).
3. There are \(P\in\mathbb N[X]\) and \(b\in\mathbb N\) such that \(f(n)=P(n)\) for every \(n\ge b\).

**Proof.** Theorem 2 gives \(1\Rightarrow 2\). For \(2\Rightarrow 3\) choose \(b=0\) if \(E\) is empty and otherwise \(b=1+\max\,\operatorname{dom}E\). For \(3\Rightarrow 2\) retain exactly those \(n<b\) for which \(f(n)\ne P(n)\). This is an existence construction from finitely many natural values, not an algorithm that recognises eventual polynomiality of an arbitrary presented program.

For \(2\Rightarrow 1\), expand the finite polynomial into a source expression using natural constants, repeated multiplication for monomials and finite sums, with zero for the empty sum and one for an empty product. List the distinct exceptional keys as \(k_1,\ldots,k_m\). Surround the polynomial expression by the finite nested conditionals

\[
\operatorname{if}\ x=k_1\ \operatorname{then}\ E(k_1)
\ \operatorname{else}\ \cdots
\ \operatorname{if}\ x=k_m\ \operatorname{then}\ E(k_m)
\ \operatorname{else}\ P(x).
\]

At each listed key the prescribed value is returned; everywhere else the polynomial is returned. This is an effective reification algorithm given the finite canonical data. ∎

**Theorem 4.** A function has at most one canonical description.

**Proof.** Suppose \(\Phi(P,E)=\Phi(Q,D)\). Choose a natural \(A\) strictly above every key in \(E\) and \(D\), using \(A=0\) if both are empty. For every \(n\ge A\), equality of the functions gives \(P(n)=Q(n)\). If \(P-Q\ne0\), choose \(n\ge\max(A,B(P-Q))\); Lemma 1 gives a contradiction. Therefore \(P=Q\).

With the common polynomial fixed, nonredundancy forces both map domains to be exactly \(\{n:f(n)\ne P(n)\}\), and at each key the map value must be \(f(n)\). Hence \(E=D\). ∎

The equality quotient of finite source expressions is consequently in bijection with these canonical pairs. Arbitrary finite prefixes are allowed; an arbitrary infinite set of exceptions is not.

## 6 Decision finite certificates and a complete finite test set

**Corollary 5.** For any two source expressions \(e,f\),

\[
\forall n,\ \llbracket e\rrbracket(n)=\llbracket f\rrbracket(n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal C(e)=\mathcal C(f).
\]

**Proof.** Correctness gives right-to-left, and correctness plus uniqueness gives left-to-right. Both canonical data structures have decidable finite equality. ∎

A positive certificate can consist of a common canonical pair. The verifier validates the finite source trees and the proposed pair, recomputes both canonical forms, and accepts precisely when they both equal that pair. The complete typed Lean checker and certificate producer/verifier now instantiate this mathematical specification using the representation described in Section 10. For a finite evaluation criterion, write \(\mathcal C(e)=(P,E)\), \(\mathcal C(f)=(Q,D)\), and \(W=\operatorname{dom}E\cup\operatorname{dom}D\). Put

\[
A=\begin{cases}0&W=\varnothing,\\1+\max W&W\ne\varnothing,\end{cases}
\qquad
M=\max(A,B(P-Q)),
\qquad
S=W\cup\{M\}.
\]

**Theorem 6.** The expressions agree everywhere if and only if they agree on the computable finite set \(S\). In particular, \(|S|\le |E|+|D|+1\), and agreement on every input \(0,\ldots,M\) is also complete.

**Proof.** Only sufficiency needs proof. Since \(M\ge A\), neither source function has an exception at \(M\). If \(P\ne Q\), Lemma 1 and \(M\ge B(P-Q)\) make their values at \(M\) unequal, contrary to the test. Thus \(P=Q\). Outside \(W\) both expressions equal that polynomial. On \(W\) their equality was explicitly tested. These cover every natural input. The cardinality bound follows from the union bound. Every element of \(S\) is at most \(M\), which gives the prefix version. ∎

The same construction yields a distinguishing input without an unbounded search. If \(P\ne Q\), return \(M\). Otherwise, unequal canonical maps imply that some key in \(W\) has different complete function values; test those finitely many keys and return one. The empty-map case, differences confined to zero, nonzero constant differences, and coefficient cancellation are all included. Normalise polynomial subtraction before determining its degree or bound.

The small cardinality bound is in terms of the already computed exception maps. Those maps or their construction may be very large compared with the source. The result is not an efficient complexity bound in source length.

## 7 Composition and the constant tail case

For any finite expressions \(e,g\), capture-free substitution at the sole input variable satisfies

\[
\llbracket e[g/x]\rrbracket(n)
=\llbracket e\rrbracket(\llbracket g\rrbracket(n)).
\]

The proof is structural induction, including substitution into all four conditional children. Therefore the class is closed under ordinary unary composition. The following direct operation on canonical forms gives a stronger explicit contract and exposes an important constant-tail case.

Let the outer function be \(f=\Phi(P,E)\) and the inner function be \(g=\Phi(Q,D)\).

**Case 1: the inner tail is a constant \(Q=c\).** Outside \(\operatorname{dom}D\), one has \(g(n)=c\), hence \((f\circ g)(n)=f(c)\). The new tail is the constant polynomial with value \(f(c)\), evaluating the full outer canonical pair. The exception map is

\[
\operatorname{trim}\bigl(f(c),\operatorname{dom}D,n\mapsto f(g(n))\bigr).
\]

It would be wrong in general to choose \(P(c)\): if \(c\) is an outer exception, that value ignores an exception reached by infinitely many inner inputs. For example, let \(f(n)=7\) when \(n=2\) and \(f(n)=n\) otherwise, and let \(g(n)=2\) everywhere. Then \(P=X\), but \(f\circ g\) is constantly 7, not constantly 2.

**Case 2: the inner tail \(Q\) is nonconstant.** Since it has natural coefficients, some coefficient of degree at least one is at least one. Thus

\[
\forall n\ge1,\qquad Q(n)\ge n.
\]

Choose \(K=0\) when \(E\) is empty and otherwise \(K=\max\,\operatorname{dom}E\). Put

\[
W=\operatorname{dom}D\cup\{0,1,\ldots,K\}.
\]

Outside \(W\), one has \(n\ge1\), \(g(n)=Q(n)\), and \(Q(n)\ge n>K\). Hence \(Q(n)\) is not an outer exceptional key. The composition there equals \(P(Q(n))\). Its canonical description is therefore

\[
\left(P\circ Q,
\operatorname{trim}(P\circ Q,W,n\mapsto f(g(n)))\right).
\]

The polynomial composition has natural coefficients. On \(W\), exact evaluation and trimming give the correct values; outside it the preceding argument applies. This proves the direct normal-form operation. By Theorem 4 its result agrees with normalising the substituted source expression.

These two cases do not assume that every finite exceptional set has finite preimage under an arbitrary function. Constant tails are precisely why that informal claim would fail. The proof uses either an eventual constant with its full outer value, or the explicit eventual growth of a nonconstant natural-coefficient polynomial.

## 8 Strict increase and exact limitations

Consider

\[
h(n)=\begin{cases}7&n=2,\\n&n\ne2.\end{cases}
\]

Its canonical form is \((X,\{2\mapsto7\})\), and it is expressed by `if x = 2 then 7 else x`.

It is not a function of an old unary zero-test expression. Such an expression agrees with a single natural-coefficient polynomial on every positive input, not merely eventually. Any polynomial agreeing with \(h\) on all sufficiently large inputs must be \(X\), by Lemma 1, but \(X(2)\ne h(2)\). Nor is \(h\) a root equality predicate, whose range is contained in \(\{0,1\}\). Therefore the new language strictly extends the full accepted unary root-test union, not merely its printed syntax.

The following exclusions are consequences of the exact image theorem.

1. **Truncated predecessor is absent.** If \(n\mapsto\max(n-1,0)\) were expressible, its tail polynomial \(P\in\mathbb N[X]\) would agree eventually with \(X-1\). Lemma 1 applied over the integers forces coefficient equality. This is impossible because the constant coefficient of \(X-1\) is negative. Finite exceptions can change values at finitely many inputs; they cannot change the tail coefficient restriction.
2. **Persistent parity is absent.** Every bounded expressible function is eventually constant: a nonconstant natural-coefficient polynomial is at least \(n\) for \(n\ge1\), and a constant tail is eventually constant. A parity function changes value at infinitely many arbitrarily large inputs and is not eventually constant.
3. **An equality guard is eventually fixed.** If the two test tails are equal, the guard is true outside finitely many exceptions; if they differ, Lemma 1 makes it false outside finitely many roots and exceptions. Arbitrary finite nesting does not produce infinitely recurring guard changes in this language.
4. **One independent input is essential to this proof.** For two variables, the coincidence set of \(X\) and \(Y\) is an infinite diagonal. It is not a finite exception set, and their equality test is not represented by the unary construction. The accepted many-variable root-test union is \(\Pi_1\)-complete when arity is part of the input. The present theorem neither claims decidability at every fixed larger arity nor identifies a numerical hardness arity.
5. **Recognition of arbitrary programs is not supplied.** The theorem decides equality from two finite descriptions in the declared grammar. It does not decide whether an arbitrary current-typed endpoint or primitive-recursive program has such a description.

The language can represent any finite pattern of exceptional outputs, even a very long one. Its limitation is the form of the infinite tail, not a fixed bound on the number of branches.

# The time available to witness absence of a bearer

5 October 2026. Proof level. This is a reviewed conditional written argument. Its conclusions require the stated whole-history witness coverage, preservation law, same-bearer temporal premise and, for participation, actual productive contribution. No actual bearer satisfying all these premises is established. The elementary temporal-modal consequences do not establish actual metaphysical possibility or an actual necessary participant in the admitted correction.

## 1 The selected proposition

Fix an actual particular \(g\). Let \(E(g)\) mean that \(g\) exists somewhere in a complete history, not that it is present now, survives forever, or retains one current capacity. Its whole absence is \(\neg E(g)\).

Let \(T\) be a nonempty linearly ordered set of actual times. Let \(A(g)\) be the nonempty set of times when \(g\) is actually present. Mere tenseless existence, on eternalism, is not presence at every time.

Let \(W(t,q)\) say that an actually possessed potentiality at time \(t\) is an adequate witness for the metaphysical possibility \(q\). The possessor can be plural; the potentiality can be joint, extrinsic, or iterated. The following assumptions concern the claim that the manifestation includes the whole-history proposition \(q\). They do not identify every possessed disposition with a usable unmasked power.

**Witness coverage for this absence claim.** If \(\Diamond \neg E(g)\), then there is \(t\) in \(T\) such that \(W(t,\neg E(g))\). This is only the relevant instance of potentialist coverage. A general, independently defended theory could support it. Naming this one instance does not give it independent warrant.

**Strict past preservation.** If \(s<t\) and \(s\) is in \(A(g)\), then not \(W(t,\neg E(g))\). The rationale is that a whole-history absence manifestation would exclude a presence already settled before the witnessing power is possessed.

**Present and past preservation.** The stronger principle replaces \(s<t\) by \(s\le t\). It also excludes witnessing the absence of a bearer already present at the witness time itself. The strict principle does not contain this equality case.

These principles are given directly in terms of actual times and witness content. No ontology of possible worlds is required to formulate the argument.

## 2 Exact witness location

Under coverage and present-and-past preservation,

\[
\Diamond\neg E(g)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\exists t\in T\ \forall s\in A(g),\quad t<s.
\]

Proof. Choose a witnessing \(t\) from coverage. If any actual presence \(s\) satisfied \(s\le t\), preservation would prohibit that witness. By linearity, \(t<s\) for every actual presence \(s\). This proves the claim.

Under strict past preservation instead, the conclusion is weaker:

\[
\Diamond\neg E(g)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\exists t\in T\ \forall s\in A(g),\quad t\le s.
\]

The equality case matters. If \(g\) is present at a first time \(t_0\), strict preservation alone excludes all later witness times but leaves \(t_0\) itself unclassified. It does not follow that the \(t_0\) witness really exists, only that its exclusion has not been supplied by strict past preservation.

## 3 A first time bearer

Assume \(T\) has a least element \(t_0\), \(g\) is present at \(t_0\), coverage holds, and present-and-past preservation holds. Then \(\neg \Diamond \neg E(g)\), and hence \(\Box E(g)\) under modal duality.

Every proposed witness \(t\) satisfies \(t_0\le t\). Actual presence at \(t_0\) therefore excludes it. This is necessity of that actual \(g\), not merely of a role occupied by something. No temporal continuation of \(g\) beyond \(t_0\) is required. Necessary existence somewhere in each complete history is compatible with \(g\)'s finite lifetime within each history.

The proof needs no S5 axiom, reverse accessibility, necessary modal truth bearer, unity of every initial entity, or coverage of every later potentiality. Those stronger questions should not be imposed on this narrower result.

Strict past preservation does not yield the same result merely because \(t_0\) is first. There being no time before \(t_0\) leaves a candidate witness at \(t_0\), not no candidate at all. An additional judgment that a witnessing power cannot alter its own present, or a more targeted exclusion of absence-witnesses at \(t_0\), closes that boundary.

## 4 A beginningless same bearer route

Suppose that for every \(t\) in \(T\), some actual presence \(s\) of \(g\) satisfies \(s<t\). Under coverage and strict past preservation, \(g\) necessarily exists.

Proof. Any witnessing time \(t\) has a strictly earlier actual presence \(s\) of this same \(g\), which excludes the witness. Thus no time witnesses whole absence.

This requires presence arbitrarily far back relative to the actual time order, not an uninterrupted lifetime, infinite age measured by a particular metric, or an actual first instant. The property is coinitiality of this bearer's presence set. It is sufficient, not asserted necessary, for the modal conclusion.

The weaker condition that at every time some suitable bearer exists does not identify such a \(g\). The witnesses may differ. Their plurality could establish a necessary nonempty history under an appropriate version of coverage while leaving every particular's absence open. This is an application of the inherited same-bearer discipline to the temporal criterion, not discovery of the general quantifier distinction.

## 5 Why a finite age is not the needed order property

The order \(T=(0,1]\) is bounded in length but has no first element. A finite elapsed-time bound therefore does not by itself supply \(t_0\). Nor does the existence of something at every time entail a first time of existence. This is a mathematical distinction about the premise, not a cosmological claim that actual time has this order.

The conditional first-time route can thus be scientifically or metaphysically motivated, but an age estimate must not silently be substituted for attainment of the initial boundary. No cosmological model is assessed or adopted in this note.

## 6 Actual contribution is a separate but local input

Now additionally fix the admitted correction \(C\) and suppose \(g\) actually productively contributes to \(C\) in the relevant respect. Applying either sufficient necessity criterion to \(g\) yields one actual necessary productive participant in \(C\).

It does not follow that \(g\) contributes to \(C\) in every alternative, that \(C\) itself necessarily occurs, that \(g\) independently supplies every productive respect of \(C\), or that \(g\) is the sole necessary existent. These are stronger conclusions. The narrower actual-participant result does not need them.

An initial being's potentiality for \(C\), even a potentiality for another subject's potentiality for \(C\), is not automatically its actual productive contribution to this token correction. Conversely an independently warranted actual causal history supplies participation without needing global coverage of every later capacity.

Strict uncausability and intrinsic existential sufficiency are also not conclusions of the temporal proof. A necessarily existing \(g\) can still stand in an explanatory or productive dependence relation unless that possibility is independently excluded. The argument establishes modal existence under its assumptions; it does not derive a recipient-free intrinsic mode.

## 7 The content sensitive warrant question

The positive rationale is intelligible: if real possibilities are anchored in actual powers and every such witness preserves settled actuality, a bearer whose actual presence precedes every eligible witness cannot be wholly absent. Its positive advantage is a concrete, claim-directed reason for exclusion rather than an uncaused-therefore-necessary label.

The decisive application is not whether an opponent can produce a \(g\)-less cosmos. It is whether the modal account warrants witness coverage for whole-history absence, whether the relevant preservation law concerns all metaphysical witness content rather than only changes an agent can perform, and whether the chosen contributor meets the temporal criterion.

Present inability to change an already completed past is narrower than the metaphysical thesis that every alternative must preserve an actual initial segment. The latter may receive basic or explanatory warrant, but its broader content must be presented. A conditional graph or a formal model cannot decide that judgment by itself.

If these premises are independently warranted, defeasibly or basically, the categorical same-bearer conclusion can inherit that warrant. Nothing in the criterion requires exhaustive refutation of every rival, a universal theorem about every being, or completed ultimate sourcehood.

## 8 Relation to earlier programme work

Ninth already tests productive versus absolute modal coverage and exact-origin necessity. Twelfth already permits one necessary actual participant before complete sourcehood and isolates recipient-exhaustiveness. These results are not replaced.

The temporal route is different in its offered mechanism. It does not infer that \(g\)'s absence would require producing \(g\), or that \(g\)'s optionality would be a receptive constituent. It excludes the eligible witness times for the absence proposition itself. Its cost is a temporal actualist coverage thesis. It might therefore provide a genuinely different basic or explanatory route, but it cannot borrow the modal exhaustiveness that the earlier routes still need to warrant.

The main report discusses the source assumptions behind this reconstruction. The criterion does not supply their actual warrant. No external mathematical priority or completed programme advancement is claimed.

# Almost sure private randomisation and effective committed continuation

5 October 2026. Proof level. The exact written synthesis has independent mathematical acceptance. It proves equivalence of almost-sure private-coin continuation, a computable path and a usable pruned witness, supplies a computable successful tape for the original machine, and proves sharpness at every threshold below one. It is written mathematics, without Lean formalisation or a historical-priority claim. It does not remove uncontrolled external choices from a policy.

## The question

The explicit diagonal example excludes even positive-probability success by a computable fair-coin transducer. Absence of a computable path alone does not establish that stronger conclusion for every tree. What happens if the proposed continuation service promises almost-sure success rather than merely a positive chance?

The following equivalence answers that narrower question uniformly at the programme's fixed-tree interface. It combines the effective-output interpretation of randomisation with the positive pruned-witness criterion. It concerns a private random tape and a fixed admitted-history tree, not an arbitrary adversarial or stochastic external environment.

## Definitions

Let \(T\) be a prefix-closed tree of finite bit strings containing the empty string. A computable committed-output coin transducer is an ordinary effective machine that reads successive independent fair bits and emits output bits irrevocably in order. Each finite computation reads only finitely many random bits. It may diverge or stop on unsuccessful tapes. There is no noncomputable advice or uncounted oracle.

For a finite output word \(s\), let \(E_s\) be the event that the machine eventually emits at least the length of \(s\) output bits and that its first such bits are \(s\). \(E_s\) is an effectively open union of finite computation cylinders. Its probability is lower semicomputable, uniformly in \(s\) and the machine description. Equivalently, positive probability of \(E_s\) can be recognised by finding one finite computation cylinder on which \(s\) has been emitted. The machine's later behaviour on that cylinder need not be known.

The success event \(S\) is the set of tapes on which every output coordinate is eventually emitted and every finite output prefix lies in \(T\). Put \(T_n=T\cap\{0,1\}^n\). Then

\[
S=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb N}\left(\bigcup_{s\in T_n}E_s\right).
\]

Each inner union is finite and open. Thus \(S\) is Borel measurable for every fixed \(T\), whether or not \(T\) is effective. An effective presentation of this event is not asserted for an arbitrary noneffective \(T\).

## Theorem

For a fixed rooted prefix-closed binary tree \(T\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(T\) has a computable infinite path.
2. There exists a computable fair-coin transducer with probability one of committing an infinite path in \(T\).
3. \(T\) contains a rooted, prefix-closed, pruned computably enumerable subtree.

A decidable rooted pruned subtree may replace the computably enumerable one, even without decidability of \(T\), because the prefixes of the extracted computable path form such a subtree. For decidable \(T\) these are the same effective-witness conditions as in the existing renewal criterion. The equivalence here does not assert that the conditions can be decided from an arbitrary supplied machine or tree description.

## Proof from almost sure success

Suppose the machine has \(P(S)=1\). Define \(U\) to consist of all finite words \(s\) for which \(P(E_s)>0\), including the empty word.

\(U\) is computably enumerable. Dovetail finite simulations of the given machine over finite coin prefixes and finite time bounds; whenever a finite computation emits a word, enumerate that word and all its prefixes. Stop a simulation if it requests an unavailable coin bit. Every observed emission is stable on the cylinder fixed by the bits actually read, so it has positive probability. Conversely, if \(P(E_s)>0\), some finite output-producing computation occurs, and the dovetail eventually discovers it. The enumeration therefore has exactly the intended range. No probability oracle, convergence modulus or test for zero probability is used.

\(U\) is rooted and prefix closed. The empty output prefix occurs before any computation. If a finite computation emits \(s\), it also emits every shorter prefix of \(s\).

\(U\) is a subtree of \(T\). If \(s\) were outside \(T\), every tape in \(E_s\) would fail \(S\) because its committed prefix could not later be retracted. Thus \(E_s\) would be contained in the null failure event. This contradicts \(P(E_s)>0\).

\(U\) is pruned. Fix \(s\) in \(U\). Almost-sure total output implies that, outside a null set, every tape in \(E_s\) eventually emits a next bit. The two events \(E_{s0}\) and \(E_{s1}\) are disjoint and their union differs from \(E_s\) by at most a null set. Consequently

\[
P(E_s)=P(E_{s0})+P(E_{s1})>0.
\]

At least one child therefore has positive probability and belongs to \(U\). This is pruning of the same enumerable output-support tree, not the existence of an unrelated successful continuation.

Starting at the empty word, restart \(U\)'s effective enumeration for each child search (or retain every previously enumerated word), wait until a child of the current word appears, commit that child and repeat. Pruning proves each finite search terminates. This produces one total computable infinite path in \(U\) and hence in \(T\). The algorithm uses the transducer description, not a decision procedure for \(T\) or a code for a successful infinite random tape.

This gives (2) implies (3) and (1). The construction is uniform in a machine description satisfying the stated almost-sure premise, although verifying that premise is not part of the construction.

For (1) implies (2), a machine can ignore its random tape and emit the computable path. It succeeds on every tape. For (1) implies (3), the finite prefixes of a computable path form a decidable rooted pruned subtree. Finally, (3) implies (1) by the same successive child search. This completes the proof.

## Why this is not fixing one lucky random tape

Almost-sure success does not mean that the all-zero tape or any other specified computable tape makes the original machine succeed. The extraction searches across finite computations and may use a different finite coin witness at each output stage. It constructs a new computable output path; it does not assert that those finite coin witnesses assemble into a successful computable input tape for the original machine.

The new output words remain mutually compatible because each search explicitly extends the already committed output prefix. The claimed object is a path of \(T\). Producing that path does not require preserving the original transducer's internal computation history or choosing a single coin tape that realises it.

## A computable successful tape for the original machine

The preceding output-support construction makes no claim that its chosen path is realised by one input tape. It also implies no impossibility of computing a successful input tape. In fact the same almost-sure premise yields a stronger, separate construction.

For each \(n\) let \(D_n\) be the effectively open event that the original machine eventually emits at least \(n\) bits. Since \(P(S)=1\), each \(D_n\) has probability one. Every finite fair-coin cylinder has positive probability, so each \(D_n\) meets every such cylinder. Openness gives a finite extension of any specified coin prefix whose entire cylinder forces the required finite output.

Construct nested finite coin prefixes \(\rho_n\) effectively. Start with the empty prefix. Given \(\rho_n\), dovetail finite extensions and finite simulation bounds until one extension forces the first \(n+1\) outputs. Require the new extension to have length at least \(n+1\), padding a successful finite witness if necessary. Such an extension exists by the preceding density argument, so each search terminates. Preserve every earlier selected coin bit.

The union \(\rho\) of these nested prefixes is a computable infinite tape: to compute its \(e\)-th bit, run the construction until the current prefix has length greater than \(e\). On \(\rho\), the original machine emits at least \(n\) bits for every \(n\), because the corresponding finite computation has already been forced by one selected prefix. Thus it has total committed output.

Every emitted finite word of any run of this machine belongs to \(T\). Otherwise the finite computation producing a bad word would be stable on a positive-probability cylinder, contradicting almost-sure validity. Therefore the original machine succeeds on the constructed computable tape \(\rho\).

This extraction is uniform in the transducer description subject to the almost-sure-success promise. It does not decide whether the promise holds. The searches need neither a \(T\)-membership test nor a successful-tape oracle. Unlike the first proof, it preserves one coherent input tape and the original machine's own execution, rather than only constructing an admitted output path.

The result is an existence and effective-extraction statement. It does not say that physical fair coin tosses will produce this designated infinite tape, or that an uncontrolled environment can be replaced by it. Feeding the constructed tape instead is a deterministic alternative only when replacing private randomisation is allowed by the service's actual support and authority conditions.

## The exact boundary

Positive-probability success alone is insufficient for this proof. A positive-probability emitted prefix may then belong only to failure runs, so the enumerable support need not lie in \(T\). Even if a prefix lies in \(T\), its output-support children can include prefixes that eventually fail. The almost-sure premise is what simultaneously gives inclusion and pruning without an oracle for eventual success.

The conclusion establishes existence of a deterministic effective continuation in the fixed model. It gives no uniform practical running-time bound, success horizon, bounded-memory controller or physical ability to perform the dovetail. An organisation using the extracted method still needs the stipulated resources, causal realisation and authority.

No claim is made that randomisation can always be removed from a policy facing external choices. A fixed admitted-history tree, whose branching is the controlled output, is a different interface from a game in which an uncontrolled environment selects receipts. The separate finite rational controller theorem has its own actual-law proof and must not be inferred from this corollary.

The diagonal theorem remains stronger on its particular tree: there success probability is zero for every computable coin transducer. The present result says that, throughout the fixed-tree class, almost-sure effective renewal is equivalent to deterministic effective renewal and to the usable pruned-witness condition.

## Sharpness below probability one

For every real threshold \(r<1\) there is a decidable rooted binary tree with no computable infinite path, although copying fair input bits into committed output succeeds with probability greater than \(r\). This is a distinct counterexample family from the diagonal tree, where every computable transducer has zero success probability.

Fix an integer \(k\) at least 2. Enumerate partial computable natural-number functions by indices \(e\). Whenever the \(\text{first }e+k\text{ values}\) of the function with index \(e\) have all halted and are bits, enumerate that finite bit word \(w_e\) as a forbidden prefix. Each index contributes at most one word. The corresponding effectively open union \(O\) of forbidden cylinders satisfies

\[
P(O)\le\sum_{e=0}^{\infty}2^{-(e+k)}=2^{1-k}.
\]

Overlapping or duplicate cylinders only decrease the union's measure. Every total computable bit sequence is in \(O\): choose an index computing it, whose required finite prefix eventually appears in the list.

Choose a computable stage enumeration and let \(O_n\) be the finite list obtained by stage \(n\), increasing with \(n\). Define a length-\(n\) word \(s\) to belong to \(T_k\) exactly when no word in \(O_n\) is a prefix of \(s\). This is decidable. It is prefix closed: a forbidden prefix discovered by an earlier stage would also exclude every later extension. The empty word is admitted because all forbidden words have positive length.

An infinite bit sequence is a path of \(T_k\) exactly when it avoids \(O\). If a forbidden prefix is eventually listed, a sufficiently long later prefix of that sequence fails the admission test. Conversely, avoiding every forbidden prefix makes every finite stage test pass. Hence

\[
P([T_k])\ge 1-2^{1-k},
\]

and \([T_k]\) contains no computable sequence. Choose \(k\) so that \(2^{1-k}<1-r\). The identity coin transducer then succeeds with probability strictly greater than \(r\) in this decidable tree, while no computable deterministic path exists.

All finite levels are nonempty because the path measure is positive. A finite exhaustive search therefore still computes an admitted plan for every requested finite horizon. The high-probability randomised service and the deterministic horizon-indexed service coexist with the absence of one deterministic infinite continuation.

This sharpness result does not license a physical risk conclusion without the declared random law and model warrant. It also does not turn a pseudorandom source with a fixed computable infinite output into independent fair coins: every computable bit sequence is excluded in this constructed tree. The distinction concerns indefinite success in this exact mathematical example, not a claimed finite test distinguishing every practical random generator.

## 5 Finite formula language and local refutation

### 5.1 Formula syntax

A formula consists of Boolean constants, signed atomic literals, binary conjunctions and binary disjunctions. A literal stores a finite atom and an expected Boolean value; its truth is equality of the recomputed atomic result with that expected value. Finite universal quantification is a conjunction fold with terminal true. Finite existential quantification is a disjunction fold with terminal false. Submitted list order and multiplicity are preserved by these folds.

Negation is an executable negation-normal-form transformation: negate constants, flip literal expectations, and exchange conjunction/disjunction recursively. Induction proves that evaluating this transformation gives the Boolean complement of the original evaluation. An implication is the disjunction of the negated antecedent and consequent.

The instantiation used for Ninth is a closed `Atom q n k` datatype. Its constructors contain only exact natural/rational equality or order, finite membership, finite-set/key equality, and the inherited finite support-key order. There is no arbitrary proposition, arbitrary Boolean result, semantic predicate, reachability assertion, controller-success assertion, or positive-checker field. Refutation submissions do not supply formulas or atoms: the negative checker reconstructs the complete formula from the actual input and reconstructed AST.

### 5.2 Refutation syntax and checker

A refutation is one of four finite constructors: leaf, left-child, right-child, or both-children.

- A leaf refutes a false constant or a literal whose recomputed atom differs from the stored expectation.
- A left-child refutes the left conjunct of a conjunction.
- A right-child refutes the right conjunct of a conjunction.
- A both-children refutes both disjuncts of a disjunction.
- Every mismatch between the formula constructor and evidence constructor rejects.

Thus a failed finite universal may identify a failing listed element. A failed finite existential must cover its entire disjunction tree, including the terminal false constant. A submitted leaf cannot stand for a failed whole body check. There is no semantic or aggregate-check atom whose false value the verifier merely trusts.

Structural induction proves soundness:

\[
\operatorname{rejectCheck}\,F\,r=\mathrm{true}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{eval}\,F=\mathrm{false}.
\]

For completeness, `refutationCandidate` recursively chooses a failing conjunction branch using its exact finite evaluation and produces both children for a disjunction. At literals/constants it returns a leaf. Induction on \(F\) proves that if \(F\) evaluates false, the resulting candidate is checked successfully. On a true formula it is merely an unchecked candidate and cannot pass, by soundness. `refute` adds an Option wrapper, returning none on true formulas and the checked candidate on false formulas. This generator is fully executable; it uses finite evaluation rather than a semantic oracle.

The verifier follows only the supplied local rejection structure. Generation can perform as much work as finite formula evaluation requires. Neither compactness nor a faster-than-exhaustive bound is claimed.
