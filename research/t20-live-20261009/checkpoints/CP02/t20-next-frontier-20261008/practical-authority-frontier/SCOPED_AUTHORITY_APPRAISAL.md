# Practical authority: scoped entitlement and specification

2026-10-08. Research appraisal for review; T20 remains active. This does not authenticate a revelation, identify an actual divine directive, or grant implementation authority.

## Finding

A scoped positive conclusion is warranted at the defeasible relational-reason standard already used in T17. The fully wise and good original giver has a claim to appropriate practical acknowledgement. Where a use genuinely falls under his rightful control, an admissible and adequately communicated exercise of that control can specify what the recipient owes. The direction can therefore make a normative difference, beyond supplying information about the independent merits of the available acts. The latter inference unpacks an operative normative title; it is not an independent substantive proof that the Creator possesses that title over the particular act.

This conclusion has two distinct supports: the substantive case for a fitting, directed response to the actual giver, and the ordinary normative significance of rightful control over a use. Neither is an inference from irresistible power. A rival cannot grant that very control entitlement, its in-scope exercise, the subject's capacity, and the direction's admissibility, then defeat it merely by pointing out that another option was previously harmless or permissible.

There remains a serious application question: does the particular human action or resource fall within that retained normative title? This differs from demanding a theory of unrestricted duty creation before admitting any directed obligation. Such a demand would overstate the outstanding burden and understate T17's positive result.

## Discriminating reconstruction

### 1. What the owner case actually establishes

Three cases separate the inference.

1. **No prior permission.** An owner's uninvited visitor uses the resource harmlessly. The use can still be wrongful because consent was never obtained. This is the source's actual illustration.
2. **Permission with retained control.** Several modes are harmless and generally permitted, but the rightful grant retains admissible control of their specification. The capable recipient receives an adequately communicated, permissible exercise selecting one. Here the exercise can bind.
3. **Unconditional grant.** Both modes have been licensed in a way that relinquishes selection control over this use. The original owner's later preference alone does not retrieve that surrendered power. Still owning the resource in other respects is insufficient.

The second case is the strongest proposed specification case. It requires the continuing normative control that distinguishes it from the third. Harmlessness does not settle permission, but the fact that a resource once belonged to someone does not settle the terms of a grant.

In the second case, that act changes the normative situation: a use previously open to authorization can now be authorized in one way and unauthorized in another. Merely discovering which mode produces better physical effects would not explain that difference. The difference rests on the owner's legitimate power to give, condition or withhold permission within this domain.

This is a bounded normative power. It does not establish authority over every act of the user. Nor does it follow that all commands are binding: a rightful owner may advise, invite or request without exercising obligatory control. Whether the addressee actually uses the resource, possesses another title, received an unconditional grant, or is protected by another applicable right matters. An emergency or conflicting right would challenge the stipulated admissibility or scope rather than refute the in-scope principle.

### 2. Why this is not simply assuming the target conclusion

If “owner” meant only efficient producer, the inference would fail. The premise here is normative title over a use, not productive success. Its ordinary significance includes control of permission; denying every such significance while keeping the word would change the operative premise.

For the original source, however, normative title must be appraised rather than inserted by a label. The relevant case combines original and continuing provision, genuine benefaction, perfect practical wisdom and goodness, and appropriate recognition of a personal relation. The reason to take that package seriously is not that a stronger being can compel submission. It is that intentionally treating a rightful giver's provision and standing as normatively irrelevant can itself fail in the response owed to that giver.

At the T17 standard, these are substantive, defeasible normative grounds, not disqualified because they involve basic judgements of fittingness. A demand to derive every norm from norm-free premises would also disqualify the earlier accepted relational reasons. Conversely, recognition of those reasons does not magically expand the title to every conceivable demand.

My positive appraisal of the package has a definite scope. Genuine benefaction makes recognition directed toward this giver, rather than merely an impersonal pursuit of welfare. Original and continuing provision deepens that relation; complete goodness and wisdom exclude the familiar exploitative or mistaken demands that weaken ordinary benefactors' claims. Recognizing a personal relation can appropriately be enacted by responsive conduct, rather than exhausted by a true sentence of praise. These considerations support an actual claim to fitting practical acknowledgement at T17's defeasible standard. They do not, without more, identify which permissible mode that claim entitles its recipient to select.

The title-over-use step therefore has a narrower verdict than the owed-recognition step. Ameri supplies an informal analogy in which ordinary ownership is already normative. He does not independently establish that original production transfers the same selection title to every creaturely act. The powerful Creator package deserves more weight than a bare causation premise, but wise benefaction alone does not distinguish the second and third grant cases. No new proof of that actual domain is claimed here.

The present positive result preserves the inherited owed-response judgement and the valid in-scope specification principle. The source underdescription is corrected, and the missing title can no longer be hidden inside a generic demand for unlimited sovereignty or inside an unexplained ownership label. This is a genuine clarification of where the substantive appraisal belongs, not a fresh derivation of all practical authority.

### 3. The strongest surviving rival

The strongest relevant rival grants the creator's goodness, benefaction and claim to worship, but argues that a particular sphere was granted to the creature's own discretion. On that account, sincere acknowledgement remains owed, while the disputed act falls outside retained control. A gift or delegation can matter normatively even while the giver remains the causal source of every power used.

This is a live title-and-domain objection, not an established account of an actual divine grant. Merely imagining it does not defeat the positive relational judgement. It must show why the particular right was transferred, limited or never included. A rival that instead accepts retained rightful control, a feasible and admissible exercise of it, and adequately communicated binding force has no remaining objection from the availability of another independently good option alone.

A second application rival construes the utterance as non-binding counsel or invitation. That preserves the source's character but denies an obligatory exercise on this occasion. It is settled by communicative evidence, not by revisiting whether any directive could ever create a relation.

## Exact increment and disposition

The increment is a completed reading of a previously catalogue-screened source, correction of its underdescription as merely acknowledgement, and the prior-grant/continuing-control distinction exposed by the actual permission example. Unpacking rightful control is not credited as an independent proof of Creator authority. The stronger package supports the retained judgement of owed practical acknowledgement; this pass does not newly establish the Creator's retained selection title over a specified human act. T17's command-dependent relational reasons and T19's testimony/directive distinction receive no new-discovery credit.

The immediate result does not justify a broad new authority campaign or another generic formal interface. It supplies a reviewable positive scoped principle and identifies what would discriminate a concrete disputed application: the relevant normative title, any grant or limit, the action-domain, and the actual utterance's force. A truthful authenticated duty report remains an independent epistemic route; no general authority theorem is newly imposed as its prerequisite.
