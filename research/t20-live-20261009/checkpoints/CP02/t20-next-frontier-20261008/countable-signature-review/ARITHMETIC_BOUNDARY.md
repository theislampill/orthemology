# Primitive divisors are sufficient, not individually necessary

8 October 2026. This short arithmetic control qualifies the base-4 argument; it is not a separate implementation campaign or a global-priority claim.

Write f_e(a)=1-a^(-e). Zsigmondy's primitive-divisor theorem gives the clean maximal-exponent proof for a=4. It does not follow that losing a primitive prime at one exponent destroys identifiability.

For any integer a>=3, all exponents e>=3 have a primitive prime divisor of a^e-1: the positive-base exceptional case there has a=2. Therefore any finite integer multiplicative relation among the f_e(a) can first be reduced to exponents 1 and 2 by descending maximal-exponent valuation arguments.

If a+1 is not a power of two, exponent 2 has a primitive prime as well; exponent 1 has a prime divisor because a-1>1. All coefficients must then vanish.

If a=2^k-1 with k>=2, the exponent-2 primitive-prime exception requires a separate argument. Here v_2(a-1)=1 and v_2(a+1)=k. Choose a prime p dividing the odd integer a and put h=v_p(a)>0. On the remaining exponent-1 and exponent-2 columns, the valuation matrix is

    v_2:    1       k+1
    v_p:   -h       -2h.

Its determinant is (k-1)h, which is nonzero. Thus these two factors remain multiplicatively independent. For example, at a=3, f_1=2/3 and f_2=8/9 are distinguished by the rows (1,3) and (-1,-2), despite the missing primitive prime for exponent 2.

Consequently every integer base a>=3 gives finite multiplicative independence of its failure family. The clean numerator-prime-order decoder needs an extra {1,2} block at the Mersenne-base exceptions; the base-4 implementation does not claim to implement those generalized decoders.

Base 2 actually fails. Primitive rows eliminate all exponents above 6. The primitive primes for exponents 4 and 5 do not divide 2^6-1, so those coefficients also vanish. On the remaining exponents 1,2,3,6, valuations at 2,3,7 give a one-dimensional integer kernel generated by

    (1,-2,-1,1).

Accordingly f_6(2)f_1(2)=f_2(2)^2 f_3(2)=63/128. This generates every finite integer relation in the base-2 family. The necessity of an independent initial valuation block is a concrete arithmetic issue, not a claim that one private prime per route type is logically necessary for every identifiable design.

The source premise used here is Zsigmondy's original positive-base order theorem and its exceptions, visually checked on printed page 283 of the 1892 paper. The present elimination and two-column arguments are elementary deductions from that premise. No conclusion about admissible physical interventions or additional original beings follows.
