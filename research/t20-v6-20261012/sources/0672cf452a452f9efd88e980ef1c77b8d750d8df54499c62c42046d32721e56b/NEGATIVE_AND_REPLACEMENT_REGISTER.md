## Normalise-before-bracket: explicit witness and provenance boundary

The surviving attempt-0002 handoff reports this negative result but its executable
witness/log did not survive. The following is a newly spelled-out ordinary witness
under that reported statement; it does not counterfeit a recovered log.

Let p=y x, where x is the variable being abstracted and y an older variable.
Let σ(y)=I. Before substitution, p is normal, so normalise-then-bracket gives
`S(K y)I`; applying σ afterwards gives `S(K I)I`. In the other order, pσ=I x
normalises to x, whose abstraction is I. Both results are source normal forms,
and they are distinct. Source confluence therefore excludes raw Conv between
them. This uses the same substitution and the same specified compiler in both
routes, not two unrelated implementations.

For the non-normalising, K-priority compiler, both routes instead give S(K I)I.
The new formal `abstraction_naturality` proves the corresponding literal square
for every capture-avoiding lifted substitution. The hypothesis is essential:
substitution of older variables is not an arbitrary reduction of the body.
