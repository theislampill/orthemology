## What the source positively argues

The Darʾ account is stronger than a generic observation that conflicting rulers can disagree. It claims that every created action draws on capacities, recipient conditions and other contributors beyond that creature’s originating control. Its account of absolute lordship encompasses all relevant aspects of the object, and its source conception includes both originating and sustaining what is produced.

The author explicitly grants the superiority of originating-and-preserving over originating alone in volume 8 P0412–P0421. He does so while distinguishing a craftsman’s intrinsic act from the material’s receptivity and capacity to preserve an arrangement. The distinction does not retract the affirmative comparison. The world-originator is said to originate substances, properties and movements, while a mere mover, commander or beloved has not yet been shown to do so.

Volume 9 016:627–650 advances numerical causal nonduplication as a substantive principle. Actual cooperative work is explained by differentiated contributions: the transported bricks, placement, different ends of a beam, organs and their powers. The author treats a numerically single agent-owned product as indivisible between independently complete producers. A later contradiction from two whole-alone producers is one expression of that principle; it must not be treated as a free-standing derivation of every stronger causal ownership claim.

The source also addresses the harmony and partition proposals. It distinguishes reciprocal efficient derivation from lawful coexistence or concomitance (016:715–720), then argues that intrinsically possessed effective capacity permits acting independently (721–725). It further argues from product-interdependence to producer dependence (739) and rejects partition or turn-taking as incompatible with complete divinity (776–779). These are genuine source reasons, not mere omissions awaiting an obvious objection.

## Where the inferential burden lies

The most precise modern audit questions are therefore:

1. What individuates the numerically same act or product? Does agent-owned individuation already exclude the countermodel, or is there an independent productive account that justifies it?
2. Why must intrinsic capacity include unilateral modal efficacy over the relevant complete outcome? The source argues for this; its appeal must be tested rather than replaced by the weaker premise “the capacity is not conferred by another.”
3. Does lawful noncausal concomitance between agents imply an external conferral of capacity? The author distinguishes concomitance from causal circularity, but then argues it cannot sustain two complete agents. That second step, not a blanket rejection of concomitance, is the relevant burden.
4. Does interdependence of products entail dependence in the producers’ existence, intrinsic acts, or merely their joint outcome? The source’s transfer at 739 deserves full assessment with its intended full-source conception explicit.
5. Does the perfection ranking establish that the already warranted original must possess every productive role? Defining absolute lordship as all-aspects provision is part of the account; identifying a particular minimally established original with that role is an additional argument. The source contains broader arguments toward that identification and must not be reduced to the definition alone.
6. Is a finite modern role inventory demonstrably exhaustive? Neither the medieval phrase “all aspects” nor a syntactic list supplies that extension automatically.

These are bounded questions, not a conclusion that the source has no argument. Equally, a successful formal consequence of an adopted premise is not yet independent warrant for adopting it.

## Intrinsic conditions are not external donations

Across the two volumes, having one’s own act or produced object as a condition differs from receiving completion from an independently existing source. The creator’s acts can occur successively, and a later act’s occurrence can depend on the earlier act’s completion. Such sequencing is not itself an independently supplying other. This positive point is essential to assessing proposals that incorrectly infer defect from every relation or every condition.

The converse must also be retained: causing a required condition, acting as a goal, or commanding a bearer does not alone amount to originating that bearer and its complete constitution. Volume 8 P0396 and the surrounding response use precisely this distinction. A typed dependence analysis should preserve both directions.

## Epistemic distinctions that constrain the reconstruction

The full volume 8 reading repeatedly separates a sound route from a uniquely necessary route, a known proposition from awareness of knowing it, and either of those from accurate knowledge of the route by which it was learned. Its conclusion at P1867–P1871 warns that a later argument may confirm earlier knowledge and may even be falsely credited with producing it. This prevents a present formal dependency graph from automatically being a universal account of all warranted human recognition.

The fitra discussion is strongly affirmative, but not a claim of articulated neonatal omniscience. P1591–P1611 distinguishes an innate directional power from the total present conditions of every individual’s cognition. Some arguments allow teaching or other assistance while preserving the disposition; others argue that an endless necessary chain of external human teachers cannot explain first recognition. Auxiliary triggers, receptive powers, impediments and direct apprehension must not be flattened into a single “independent knowledge” predicate.

Volume 9 016:871–878 supplies a further guardrail for the attestation track. Conceiving what one intends, knowing that it will occur, and knowing that it has occurred are different. Knowledge is one condition of production alongside power and will, and divine self-knowledge does not produce God. Productive intention alone therefore does not establish universal knowledge, truthfulness or that a particular occurrence is an intentional attestation. At 881–884 the author also explicitly argues that knowledge conditioned on one’s own act need not be borrowed from an independent other. Both the limitation and the affirmative intrinsic-source claim matter.
