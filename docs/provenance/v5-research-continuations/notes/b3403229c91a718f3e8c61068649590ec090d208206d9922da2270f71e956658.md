# Relative cluster smoothing preserves the p=1 sparse classification

Author theorem, ordinary mathematics. Version 1, 2026-10-01. Exact source and qualification identities will be supplied by the frozen packet. No new historical-priority claim is asserted.

## 1. Contract and result

Fix 0<e<=1/4, 0<=d<=e/4, a_i>0, a_0<=1/2 and a_(i+1)<=a_i/2. Put

    r_i=a_(i+1)/a_i, L_i=log(1/r_i),
    Z=sum_i a_i, S=sum_i r_i L_i,
    I_i=[(1-d)a_i,(1+d)a_i].

For each i let nu_i be ANY Borel probability supported on I_i, and let mu=sum_i (a_i/Z)nu_i. The support promise is nu_i(I_i)=1; no density, common within-cluster shape, computability, or posterior unimodality is assumed. Geometric domination gives 0<Z<=2a_0<=1. The intervals are disjoint and lie strictly inside (0,1).

The actual probability law is (mu product eta) product U^N on ((a,z),u), where eta is any fixed probability seed law independent of the real parameter a and all uniform innovations, and receipt j is 1{u_j<=a}. The inherited EndpointProcess theorems construct its finite word probabilities and identify measurable literal-count expectations. A policy q_n(y_0,...,y_(n-1),z) uses only the full observed word, time and seed. It reports at every n>=1. Failure means D0(a,e,q)>0 or D1(a,e,q)>0, where

    D0=q^2-1/4-a e(1+e),
    D1=(1-q)^2-1/4+a e(1-e).

Theorem. For every such eta and every measurable report family pi,

    E_mu count(pi) >= exp(-1) S/(9Z).

A single deterministic measurable coherent report family P, explicitly constructed below, satisfies

    E_mu count(P) <= 30000 S/Z.

Both bounds are extended-nonnegative inequalities. Consequently a finite expected-count policy exists iff S<infinity. This statement is uniform over all nu_i and d in the stated interval. It does not infer a finite mean from almost-sure convergence.

## 2. Literal geometry and exact cluster decision problem

The centre report q_i=1/2+e a_i is coherent. Direct algebra gives

    D0(a,e,q_i)=-e^2 a_i(1-a_i)+(a_i-a)e(1+e),
    D1(a,e,q_i)=-e^2 a_i(1-a_i)+(a-a_i)e(1-e).

For a in I_i, e<=1/4, a_i<=1/2 and d<=e/4 imply

    D0(a,e,q_i)<=-(3/16)e^2 a_i<0,
    D1(a,e,q_i)<=-(1/4)e^2 a_i<0.

Thus the centre accepts the WHOLE cluster, including both boundary points.

Since d<=1/16, any x in I_i and y in I_j with i<j have

    x/y >= [(1-d)/(1+d)] a_i/a_j >=30/17>5/3.

The inherited division-free acceptance-width inequality says a real report accepting two positive parameters x>=y must have x<=(5/3)y. Thus no report accepts points in two distinct clusters. This applies to arbitrary real reports, not merely the centre reports.

For a fixed word w, write m_i(w)=(a_i/Z) integral P_a(w) nu_i(da). These are nonnegative and summable, with total at most one. Their positive maximum is attained because m_i->0. At least one is positive for every finite word since every component is strictly interior. Every report misses all but at most one cluster, hence its failure mass is at least sum_i m_i-max_i m_i. A centre report for a maximizing cluster attains equality. Selecting the smallest maximizing index for each word gives one measurable deterministic policy simultaneously optimal at every stage and horizon. There are only countably many finite binary words. It need not be effective, and the quantitative upper witness below does not require computing these integrals or maxima.

## 3. Universal lower bound without unimodality

For ANY nonnegative summable sequence m_i and maximizing index j,

    sum_i min(m_i,m_(i+1)) <= sum_i m_i-m_j.

For i<j charge the minimum to m_i; for i>=j charge it to m_(i+1). Each nonmodal mass is charged at most once. No monotonicity or unimodality is used. Equality may fail, and is not claimed.

On the all-zero length-n word, m_i(n)=(a_i/Z) integral (1-a)^n nu_i(da). For a in I_i,

    a<=17 a_i/16<=17/32, 1-a>=15/32>=1/3,
    log(1-a)>=-a/(1-a)>=-3 a_i.

For n<=L_i/(3a_i), therefore,

    m_i(n)>=a_i exp(-3a_i n)/Z>=a_(i+1)/Z,
    m_(i+1)(n)>=a_(i+1) exp(-r_i L_i)/Z
                        >=exp(-1)a_(i+1)/Z,

using r log(1/r)<=1. At every n>=0, each of these zero-word masses at n+1 is at least one third its value at n. Therefore every n from 0 through floor(L_i/(3a_i)) supplies a genuine POSITIVE report index n+1 with overlap at least exp(-1)a_(i+1)/(3Z). There are at least L_i/(3a_i) such indices, so

    sum_(n>=1) min(m_i(n),m_(i+1)(n))
       >=exp(-1) r_i L_i/(9Z).

At each n, the all-word Bayes error dominates the all-zero error, which dominates the adjacent minima. Integrate the pointwise bound over any independent seed and use nonnegative Tonelli in n and i. The result is the universal actual-expectation lower bound. Infinite S is not passed through a nonsummable real tsum: all final counts and their sums are extended nonnegative.

## 4. A distribution-free explicit cluster policy

Let K=320/3 and define a boundary in each gap by

    b_i=a_i min(3/4,max(3 r_i/2,1/(K L_i))).

As 0<r_i<=1/2, this gives

    (3/2)a_(i+1)<=b_i<=(3/4)a_i.

Consequently b_(i+1)<=b_i/2, b_i>0 and b_i->0. For a length-n word (n>=1), let H_n be its empirical frequency and x_n=max(H_n,1/n). Define J_n as the least i for which x_n>b_i. It exists since x_n>0 and b_i->0. Output P_n=q_(J_n).

Equivalently, J_n=0 if x_n>b_0, and J_n=i>=1 if b_i<x_n<=b_(i-1). Equalities are assigned as written. This uses neither a hidden parameter nor a correctness label, and no knowledge of nu_i. The positive 1/n floor is an intentional part of the policy: raw nearest-frequency classification sends every zero prefix toward the nonexistent support endpoint and is not this construction.

For a in I_i, the output is accepted whenever J_n=i. An error is thus contained in

    x_n>b_(i-1)   (i>=1),  or  x_n<=b_i.

The same policy is used for all true parameters, all stages and all component distributions.

## 5. The elementary one-sided probability bounds

For Bernoulli(a) Y and |t|<=1, the proved predecessor centred MGF estimate is

    E exp(t(Y-a)) <= exp(t^2 a(1-a)).

Independence raises this to the nth power. Exponential Markov with t=1/10 yields the following two bounds (also at equality):

    a<=(4/5)b  => Pr(H_n>=b)<=exp(-n b/100),
    b<=(4/5)a  => Pr(H_n<=b)<=exp(-n a/100).

For the first, b-a>=b/5 and a(1-a)<=a<=b; the exponent is at most -n b/50+n b/100. For the second, a-b>=a/5 and a(1-a)<=a; use t=-1/10. No asymptotic normal approximation or stopping-time concentration is involved. All indices are deterministic physical report indices.

For a in I_i, a<=17a_i/16<=(17/24)b_(i-1)<(4/5)b_(i-1), when i>=1. Also b_i<=3a_i/4<=(4/5)a since a>=15a_i/16.

Upper-boundary errors: There are at most 1/b_(i-1) positive indices with n<1/b_(i-1). At every other n, x_n>b_(i-1) implies H_n>b_(i-1). Summing the exponential tail and using 1/(exp(h)-1)<=1/h gives

    sum_(n>=1) Pr_a(x_n>b_(i-1)) <=101/b_(i-1).

Lower-boundary errors: x_n<=b_i is impossible unless n>=1/b_i. Hence

    sum_(n>=1) Pr_a(x_n<=b_i)
      <= exp(-a/(100b_i))/(1-exp(-a/100))
      <= (101/a) exp(-a/(100b_i)).

The last inequality uses 1/(1-exp(-h))<=1+1/h and a<=1. For a in I_i this is at most

    [1616/(15a_i)] exp[-3a_i/(320b_i)].

There is no upper-boundary term for i=0.

## 6. Evaluation of the boundary series

Write c=3/320, so cK=1. The definition of b_i gives

    a_(i+1)/b_i <= r_i max(4/3,K L_i)
      <=[K+4/(3log2)] r_i L_i.

For the other term,

    a_i/b_i=max(4/3,min(2/(3r_i),K L_i)),
    exp(-c a_i/b_i)
      <= exp[-1/(160r_i)]+exp(-cK L_i)
      <= (160/exp(1)+1)r_i.

The last inequality follows from x exp(-x)<=1/exp(1). Thus the mixture-weighted expected count is at most C S/Z, with

    C=101[K+4/(3log2)]
       +[1616/(15log2)] [1+160/exp(1)].

In particular C<30000, using log2>=1/2 and exp(1)>=2. Explicitly these crude replacements give 427432/15<30000. The smaller symbolic C is about 20271.55, but that decimal is not a proof input. Integrating the uniform parameterwise bound within each nu_i and applying Tonelli proves the stated upper bound.

## 7. Absolutely continuous example and robustness boundary

Take the SAME centres as the fifth-tranche accepted example, a_i=exp(-(i+1)^2). Then r_i=exp(-(2i+3)), L_i=2i+3 and S is finite. For every 0<d<=e/4, choose nu_i uniformly on I_i. The resulting measure is absolutely continuous, with density

    f(a)=1/(2dZ) on the disjoint union of I_i, and 0 elsewhere.

Each component contributes at least 1/[(1+d)Z] to integral 1/a mu(da). Therefore its first inverse moment and inverse-variance moment are infinite, while the explicit cluster policy has finite actual expected literal failures. Exact point masses were not essential; the protected gaps and literal acceptance widths support the classification.

This density is NOT bounded below on a full endpoint neighbourhood. The large empty multiplicative gaps make the previously accepted lower-growth hypothesis fail: there are arbitrarily small x for which F(2x)=F(x)>0. Thus no earlier necessity theorem is contradicted.

Adding theta delta_0 for any 0<theta<1 invokes the accepted endpoint-atom necessity in the same EndpointProcess interface and forces infinite expected count for EVERY report policy, even if redesigned. Positive relative smoothing is therefore safe in this explicit class, while even a tiny endpoint atom remains unsafe. These are different perturbation classes with stated quantitative interfaces.

## 8. Scope

This result is p=1 with weights exactly a_i/Z and component support promises. Comparable cluster weights follow by measure comparison if their component laws are held fixed; arbitrary irregular weights are not classified here. The theorem is distribution-free within clusters but does not decide or empirically certify the support promise. It does not authorize replacing a known gap by a finite-data guess. The bounds are intentionally coarse; no runtime, finite-memory, prescribed report latency, or exact-real computational claim is made. Ordinary proofs, kernel-checked subclaims, and exact finite controls remain separately labelled in the packet.
