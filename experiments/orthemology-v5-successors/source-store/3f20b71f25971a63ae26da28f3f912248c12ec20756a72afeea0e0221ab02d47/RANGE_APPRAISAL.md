# Range appraisal: al-Ṣafadiyya, range 4

## Bounded result

The full assigned electronic range, SF-S002-L0612–SF-S003-L0367, has been read continuously: 17 windows, 496 labelled lines, 23,751 whitespace words. Its central argument is an extended reconstruction and attempted reversal of a proof for an eternal world. It repeatedly argues that perpetual divine agency does not entail any eternally coexistent particular effect. It also supplies a substantial account of concrete creaturely dependence, two local arguments for a common creator, a distinction between mental universals and concrete qualified individuals, and explicit warnings about what a proof of a necessary existent does not yet establish.

This is a report of the author's argument in the supplied witness, not a new proof of the conclusions, a historical verification of his reports, or a formal result. The initial and final arguments cross assignment boundaries. Textually questionable passages need control before exact quotation or inferential use. Later bounded scan controls, if completed, will be recorded separately rather than retroactively described as full scan reading.

## 1. The continuous causal argument

The beginning assumes an earlier discussion and immediately tests the claim that complete efficacy might depend on something outside the alleged first cause. The author distinguishes priority-circularity, a regress in producers, and an infinite temporal series of successive acts. Their shared English description as “regress” can obscure their very different roles. An infinite series of mutually deferred sources of original power is rejected; an unending sequence of determinate actions by an already capable agent is treated as a different question. An earlier parent conditioning a later child is his own explanatory example, not an objection that he overlooks (S002-L0728–L0733).

The decisive distinction is between an agent's intrinsic capacity/activity as a type and its complete efficacy for a determinate effect. The former may have no beginning without any product having existed eternally alongside God. The author therefore opposes both the particular eternal cosmos and a divine transition from total incapacity to capacity. His formula requires particular actions arising successively, not an eternal particular action indistinguishable from an essential attribute (S002-L0623–L0628; S003-L0096–L0115).

“Complete cause and effect coincide” is itself qualified. The author ultimately accepts immediate succession, not the strict temporal simultaneity of creator and created product; he rejects hand/key or hand/ring correlation as an example of complete efficient production. These qualifications in S003-L0015–L0017 and L0100–L0103 prevent exporting an earlier slogan into a simplified modern causal axiom without further interpretation. The text also distinguishes productive action from the product and surveys rival identifications or separations of the two (L0025–L0032).

A recurring move is to argue that a permanently unchanging, perfectly complete cause cannot explain new differentiated events. Appealing to celestial movements, successive conceptions, or volitions simply moves the explanatory burden unless the new items themselves have a sufficient source. The author turns the philosophers' own accepted rejection of preference without a determinant against an invariant emanation scheme. He also criticises theologians who invoke sufficient determination against human libertarian agency but waive it when discussing God's first action (S002-L0647–L0677).

The discourse is deliberately modal and disjunctive in places. The author often argues that, whether successive effects without a first are possible or impossible, a particular eternal sphere is not established. Such passages should not be converted into an unconditional endorsement of every antecedent assumed in one horn. He reports alternative body/accident ontologies and past/future regress positions as well as articulating his own solution.

## 2. Actual dependence, self-sufficiency, and the scope of source claims

The strongest positive dependence statement is S003-L0267–L0280. Every other-than-God is intrinsically poor or dependent. Origination and possibility are signs or entailments of that poverty, not properties which efficiently make an otherwise neutral thing dependent. Before its existence there is no external creaturely essence alongside the creator; after its existence it remains dependent for continued reality and existence. In parallel, God being necessary or sufficient “through himself” is not a self-production relation. The text blocks both a created thing's becoming independent after inception and God's being made necessary by his own essence as though cause and effect were externally separate.

The passage has genuine universal wording and should not be diluted into a merely existential or occasional dependence claim. S003-L0195 adds that all that belongs to the product is from the producer, while L0342 expressly includes the possible thing's self, attributes, and states in its dependence. These are important positive evidence for comprehensive divine creative dependence.

Their limits are equally substantive. The author distinguishes the creator's necessary attributes from products; distinguishes an act from an effect; accepts conditions of particular production; and denies that every concomitant is an efficient cause. Every creature's intrinsic poverty does not by itself identify the metaphysical status, unit of individuation, timing, or causal mode of every proposed requisite. The text rejects interchangeably substituting efficacy in general, efficacy towards a particular, and efficacy towards everything (S003-L0057–L0060). “SourceMode”, “original”, and “requisite” are not the author's defined vocabulary in this range. A formal universal over those categories would therefore require explicit bridge definitions and arguments, not simply translation of the word “all”.

Important safeguards are S002-L0686–L0688 on impossibilities and conditional ability; S003-L0005 on created materials; L0035 and L0207 on mutually dependent effects or created agents from one cause; and L0155 on the non-convertibility of “created” and “originated”. These are not incidental exceptions to be erased by a proposed universal. They are part of the author's explanation of what kinds of claim can legitimately be made.

## 3. A common creator and comparative hypotheses

There is actual common-Creator reasoning in this range. At S003-L0198–L0208, independently complete producers cannot each independently produce the same single act; mutually conditional original powers are not independent. The author adds that the world's parts are connected and need one another in a manner incompatible with partition between independent creators (L0205). At L0359–L0361 the same pressure is applied to a plurality of celestial governors. At L0362–L0365, unity of a particular human, horse, or tree is used against multiple independent or co-operating ultimate producers of that undivided individual. Ordinary collaborators remain intelligible because their acts are distinguishable.

These passages can inform a comparative claim that actual connected creatures have a common creator rather than separate independent gods. The author's intention is stronger than merely suggesting that explanation as convenient: he argues that rivals are impossible under the stated independence and unity conditions. An appraisal should report that strength accurately while still separating the argument's intended force from our verification of its premises.

The claim is nevertheless distinct from an unrestricted every-original/every-requisite SourceMode assertion. First, the common-Creator argument concerns actual connected creation and independent complete production. Second, it acknowledges created collaboration, mutual conditionality of powered creatures, and distinguishable actions. Third, extending from one created unity or one connected world to every candidate entity and every kind of prerequisite requires determining what belongs in those domains. Fourth, an efficient creator, a final cause, a condition, a material, and an inseparable attribute are not interchangeable. Any proposed formal reconstruction must preserve these distinctions and must show how its universal quantifiers follow, rather than treat comparison with rival creators as already proving them.

## 4. Concrete bearers and mental universals

S003-L0133–L0139 rejects reading conceptual genus/differentia composition as literal composition of an extra-mental human. Different conceptual descriptions make different predicates internal or external to the description; they do not rearrange the concrete individual. The human himself bears the attributes; his instantiated attributes are particular. Universality belongs to the common concept insofar as it is in the mind. The differentiation of real, mental, spoken, and written existence is explicit.

This analysis recurs in L0262 and L0277–L0280 when possibility is predicated of a mentally conceived not-yet-existent object. It blocks an externally existing neutral essence which first receives existence or non-existence. Conversely it does not deny that concepts can truthfully describe reality or that different individuals share a conceptually recognised nature; it denies the illicit extra-mental reification or decomposition described in the argument.

For the specific necessary-bearer question, L0348–L0353 is especially valuable. A route from existence may establish a necessary existent without identifying it with the first mover, establishing its difference from the sphere, or proving separation from the world. The author complains that attributes stripped away under a composition argument leave an inadequate imagined object. Thus “a necessary existent has been established” is not automatically “this concrete explanatory bearer with all these predicates has been established”. The range supplies a warning about identification gaps, not a complete freestanding account of every valid necessary-being proof.

## 5. Sources, perfection, ends, and real agency

The author treats divine power and perfection as intrinsic rather than gifts from an effect or another source (S003-L0194–L0197). A produced thing cannot confer the original capacity through which it is produced. This is a direct dependence asymmetry. Yet statements about perfection in L0312–L0318 belong to the quoted philosophical account of celestial imitation of the highest good. They are not simply the author's own transfer principle.

His response is philosophically important: a beloved object can be a final cause without efficiently producing the lover's faculty, desire, or action (L0319–L0321). Food, drink, beauty, and scent are examples. A represented goal can guide action before its external existence. The author therefore refuses to infer productive origin from being loved, imitated, or regarded as perfect. This distinction is contrary evidence against any model in which all explanatory dependence is collapsed into one uniform relation.

Real human agency is also a substantial topic, not a digression to discard. The text distinguishes capacity relevant to commands from complete capacity at performance; preserves acting by power and decisive will; and argues that created choices require determinants rather than originating unexplained. It treats divine creative determination and human choosing as compatible under a qualified sense of necessitation, while rejecting both a will-less bare cause and uncaused preference (S002-L0642–L0688). Moral responsibility is not fully theorised in this range, and the claimed natural recognition of differential assistance should be reported as argument, not empirical demonstration.

## 6. Epistemic routes, signs, and attestations

Several kinds of evidence are used rather than a single universal modal route: observed new rain/plants/humans/animals (S002-L0637); natural recognition of need and help (L0681–L0685); ordinary meaning of creation and making (S003-L0112–L0115, L0145–L0155); logical distinctions about causes and conditions; Qurʾānic testimony; and reports of early scholarly opposition. S003-L0144 explicitly describes revelation as perfecting natural recognition. L0062–L0063 and L0189 use scriptural eschatology to loosen identification of all time with present astronomical motion.

The range distinguishes refuting a theory with counterexamples from explaining where its argument fails (S002-L0698–L0700). It also distinguishes an opponent-binding argument from a knowledge-producing one (S003-L0042–L0044). These are controls on argumentative method, not merely embellishments.

The extensive historical and transmitted-report material at S003-L0167–L0187 must retain its separate voice and evidential status. It includes political conflict, denunciations of schools, isnāds, a reported execution, and a qualified genealogy of influence. Neither the chains' authenticity nor the historical claims are independently established by reading them. Received Qurʾānic references also need checking where the witness is visibly irregular; no such reference has been silently corrected here.

## 7. Balanced local assessment and limits

The range is unusually useful for keeping three propositions apart: ongoing intrinsic creaturely dependence; identity of the creator of actual connected effects; and a formal universal concerning every possible original and requisite under one source relation. It strongly asserts the first, actively argues the second, and does not itself define or prove the third as posed in that modern vocabulary. This conclusion is a scope distinction, not a predetermined positive or negative verdict on any broader project.

Any stronger reconstruction needs the adjacent ranges, accurate control of consequential wording, an explicit account of the act/effect timing distinction, and definitions respecting efficient/final/conditional relations. A local argument's validity, a quotation's fidelity to its historical source, and a witness's textual soundness are separate questions. Neither manuscript evidence nor print-wide reading nor a new Lean result is supplied by this appraisal.


## Post-reading control update

The separate bounded controls confirm the principal mental-universal, intrinsic-poverty, final/efficient-cause, and common-Creator passages. They also verify a consequential divergence at S003-L0346: printed469 reads that the sphere cannot exist without its occurrences, whereas the electronic text says with all its occurrences. Exact dependence arguments should use an explicitly identified witness at this point. Several apparently awkward electronic phrases are retained by the scanned edition and remain unresolved.
