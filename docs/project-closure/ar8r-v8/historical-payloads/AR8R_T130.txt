# AR8R-T130 — Image-relative reflection soundness and guard independence

## Typed setting

Let `M_T` be a class of target models and `M_F` a class of formal models. Let

```text
I : M_T -> M_F
A_T, C_T : M_T -> {0,1}
A_F, C_F : M_F -> {0,1}.

I is an interpretation map. A_T and C_T are the target antecedent and conclusion; A_F and C_F are their proposed formal counterparts.
Theorem T130.1 — image-relative reflection

Assume:

    antecedent preservation: for every m in M_T, A_T(m) -> A_F(I(m));

    formal theorem on the image: for every m in M_T, A_F(I(m)) -> C_F(I(m));

    conclusion reflection: for every m in M_T, C_F(I(m)) -> C_T(m).

Then:

for every m in M_T, A_T(m) -> C_T(m).

Proof

Fix m with A_T(m). Assumption 1 gives A_F(I(m)); assumption 2 gives C_F(I(m)); assumption 3 gives C_T(m). No assertion about formal models outside im(I) is required. ∎
Theorem T130.2 — guard independence

Neither antecedent preservation nor conclusion reflection follows from the other two assumptions.
Antecedent-preservation countermodel

Take singleton target and formal classes {m} and {f} with I(m)=f. Set

A_T(m)=1, C_T(m)=0,
A_F(f)=0, C_F(f)=0.

The formal theorem and conclusion reflection are true vacuously, while A_T(m)->C_T(m) is false.
Conclusion-reflection countermodel

Set

A_T(m)=1, C_T(m)=0,
A_F(f)=1, C_F(f)=1.

Antecedent preservation and the formal theorem hold, while conclusion reflection and the target implication fail.
Track-T consequence

A formal nonaugmentation, indistinguishability, or fixed-point theorem can be genuinely load-bearing in a world-directed argument. Its target force is conditional on two separately attackable interpretation claims:

target antecedent -> formal antecedent on the image;
formal conclusion on the image -> target conclusion.

This does not presuppose strict separation. It also blocks direct transfer.
Scope and novelty

This is elementary compositional reasoning about an interpretation map. General theorem novelty is rejected. The project-specific contribution is the exact decomposition of the formal-to-metaphysical joint and its two independent failure modes.
