# Agent causal production and complete particular determination

9 October 2026 UTC. Additive source-bound assessment for the particular-agency comparison. No protected edits, integration, closure, formal countermodel, source redistribution, or claim of demonstrated human agent causation.

## Result

The chapter supplies a substantive rival theory of production, supported by conceptual and abductive arguments. It does not independently establish an actual instance of that production. Its principal challenge is to the necessity of complete contrastive determination, not a successful satisfaction of that requirement. Consequently, dismissing it as merely naming the selected outcome understates its argument; presenting it as an actual metaphysical counterexample overstates the evidence.

## Concise source synopsis

O’Connor assumes causal powers, motivating substance causation through agency and control (189–193). Emergent powers can be irreducible yet physically generated and sustained (193–195). Reasons structure propensities; agents produce intentions; their causing is not separately produced (196–199). Goal-content strengthens reasons explanation, which need not be contrastive (199–201).

Against Broad’s timing objection, relevant dated states remain explanatory; Clarke’s event/substance asymmetry is disputed (202–203). Against uniformity and probability-raising objections, permissible power-types and probabilistic influence need not satisfy Clarke’s restrictions (203–207).

Hiddleston’s redundancy objection receives three replies: disabling agency need not preserve behaviour; precise timing and purposive content might distinguish accounts; phenomenology and responsibility beliefs support agent causation. The proposed discriminators are tentative (207–210). Pereboom’s objection is answered by distinguishing probabilistic constraint from production and single-case settling (211–213). Notes identify further control objections not answered here (211 n.21).

Actual human instantiation is expressly not defended (193); the conclusion offers defeasible intelligibility while allowing possible metaphysical impossibility (214).

## Independent comparison with the complete determiner requirement

The following judgments are this report’s analysis. The Arabic-side constraint is inherited from `wise-particular-determination-return-20261009/REPORT.md`; no new Arabic source reading is claimed here.

### What earns positive explanatory credit

A bare selector leaves its inscription doing all the work: an outcome is actual because the model labels it selected. This rival instead commits to a bearer, a distinctive causal capacity, a causal relation to an intention-event, motivational conditions, and an intentional content. It provides a reason to posit those elements that is independent of the present theological comparison. Whether that reason succeeds is another question.

Such an ontology has consequences: agency cannot simply be deleted while every causal fact is held fixed, and the identification of an event as genuinely produced differs from its mere placement in an outcome list. The proposed relation is meant to ground production, not merely record which member of a possibility set happened. These commitments deserve assessment at their strongest scope.

Nevertheless, a substantive explanatory posit is not self-authenticating. Citing the agent’s primitive efficacy establishes actual production only conditional on the disputed ontology and its instantiation. Here “independently motivated” can mean a general powers framework plus agency-related explanatory considerations. It cannot mean experimentally established, neutrally demonstrated, or supported by an independently verified instance in this chapter. The early causal-powers assumption and the contested standard of freedom also prevent treating the argument as premise-free.

### Actual production is distinct from antecedent necessitation

Three demands should remain separate: identify a producer of an actual event; identify why a particular producer acts with particular intentional content; and give conditions excluding every alternative occurrence. A theory can claim the first two without offering the third. That is an intelligible location for a philosophical disagreement even if the third demand is ultimately correct.

The present rival does not preserve complete particular determination by adding the accomplished act to its own antecedent. That manoeuvre would yield only an entailment from occurrence to occurrence. Its more serious position is that explaining production need not entail an antecedently or independently distinguishing sufficient basis for the entire contrast. This is a rival standard of explanatory adequacy.

The clash is therefore not about whether all causes must be external, whether knowledge alone moves an agent, or whether every permissible choice has a unique normative optimum. None of those propositions follows from the Arabic assessment. The clash arises when all relevant circumstances apart from the actual settling leave alternatives genuinely open, and no further adequate differentiating basis is supplied or required. Calling the resulting act intrinsic does not make that disagreement disappear.

### A causal relation is not automatically a second produced event

Regress arguments must individuate their explananda before multiplying them. Producing an intention need not consist of producing a second event called the production of that intention, which in turn requires a third production. Otherwise the demand would threaten ordinary causation as well as agency. The anti-reification argument at pp. 196–198 is significant at this level.

But declining the extra event does not settle why the original causal relation obtains with this effect and timing. One can ask for its adequate determining ground without claiming that a relation is a separately manufactured object. Conversely, an opponent cannot answer the rival simply by asserting that every production requires a second production. The viable disagreement concerns sufficient grounding of the particular exercise, not a grammatical iteration of the verb “cause.”

The argument also should not be promoted into the general impossibility of an agent’s being created. Conditions of a bearer’s existence and possession of its power are not identical to its exercise as an immediate cause. Nor has this report accepted O’Connor’s substance terminology as automatically interchangeable with the programme’s bearer semantics.

### Noncontrastive adequacy is argued rather than overlooked

An explanation of an occurrence and an explanation of why that occurrence prevailed over another can succeed or fail separately. The causal provenance of an actual event may be informative even when it does not predict or necessitate it. Rejecting that information as literally no explanation requires an argument about the appropriate explanatory standard.

Conversely, informative provenance does not show that the stronger contrastive question is misconceived. In the current comparison, the point is precisely whether a final unexplained difference between admissible outcomes is acceptable. The chapter supplies a principled way to answer yes, rather than accidentally omitting the question. It does not prove that answer to a reader who retains the complete-determiner principle.

Some local contrasts may still be explained: identifying the end represented in an intention can distinguish its purposive description from another description. That does not by itself explain why this entire intentional act occurred instead of another available act. Likewise, propensity-changing conditions can explain a temporal tendency without uniquely fixing the actual instant. We must not erase either the partial explanatory gain or its limit.

### Which objections survive the replies

The categorical impossibility arguments carry a demanding burden. For example, a requirement that every cause be an event, or that every causal power contribute in exactly one ontological manner, cannot be used against substance causation without independent support. Identifying this unsupported restriction is a real defensive success. It removes a proposed short argument for impossibility; it does not establish the rival’s actuality.

The same discipline applies to redundancy. Matching observed frequencies does not logically identify two causal structures, and failure to isolate a power empirically does not alone establish its nonexistence. A critic must address the offered phenomenological and explanatory case rather than repeat that there is none. But phenomenology can support a question about agency without uniquely deciding its metaphysics; a commitment to responsibility also does not settle which account of control is correct. Thus the strongest remaining evidential objection is underdetermination after the reply, not the fiction of an unanswered objection.

The proposed timing and content discriminators would need a clearer model and evidential support before serving as actual tests. There is also a specification issue: definite probabilities for specific event-types at pp. 197–198 must be reconciled with the exploratory suggestions at pp. 208–209 about precise timing and fuller goal-content. A coarse interval/type interpretation might reconcile them. The chapter does not supply enough precision to count this as either an established contradiction or an established discriminator.

For the luck objection, statistical constraint does not itself entail that something other than the agent produces the token choice. That blocks a quick inference from probabilistic regularity to idle agency. The residual question is whether the primitive token-level control is a real power with the claimed significance. Merely redescribing it as a random event begs the disputed identity; merely calling it settling does not establish its existence. The independent evidential burden remains on the substantive posit.

### Creaturely emergence supplies no original sourcehood result

Irreducibility and unreceivedness answer different questions. A capacity might fail to reduce to lower-level properties while depending for its acquisition and preservation on causes. Consequently, a theory designed to make creaturely agency fit a wider causal order cannot be transported directly to an unreceived original.

Additional argument would be needed for necessary existence, unreceived possession of power, comprehensive sovereignty, perfectly wise action, or origination of other bearers. Nothing in the comparison licenses those conclusions merely because an agent is the immediate cause of one intention. Similarly, a rebuttal to the demand for a separately produced causal relation is not a proof that an original’s complete determining basis must come from elsewhere.

The result neither gives original agency an arbitrary-choice exemption nor establishes its impossibility. It provides a richer opponent to a specific sufficiency premise while leaving the original-scope qualification unanswered.

## Bounded disposition

Retain the rival as a substantive challenge to complete contrastive determination. Do not certify an actual counterexample, a metaphysical possibility theorem, or a divine-agency instance. Preserve the distinction between its positive causal account and the evidential question whether such a power exists. The next relevant issue would be the independent grounds for accepting or rejecting the stronger differentiating-basis requirement; no broader literature campaign or new formal construction has been undertaken here.
