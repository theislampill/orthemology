## 1. What the source actually contains

*Graded Representations, Witnessed Revision, and Inquiry Landscapes* is dated 8 September 2026. Its introduction explicitly identifies three tasks: construct a tensor representation of an episode, give witnessed degree-lowering operations, and distinguish the inquiry landscape from the historical ecology. It is not merely a suggestion to consider tensors someday. [S1, §§1–3]

The mathematical construction maps a selected episode e into a typed, graded tensor algebra. The factors have semantic roles, and their order is not silently discarded. Finite support is a property of the declared representation, not a claim that the represented world or the entire programme is finite. [S1, §§2–4]

There are concrete calculations. Section 5 shows how proper marginals can erase a higher-order parity distinction and expresses marginalisation as tensor contraction. Section 6 supplies degree-shift operations and a counterexample to equating contraction with warranted discharge. Section 8 represents payoffs as covariant tensors and evaluates mixed strategies by contraction. Sections 10–11 develop conditional energy, stability, compression and continuation results. These are mathematically substantive uses of the declared structure, even where the ingredients are standard rather than novel. [S1, §§5–11]

The continuation supplement asks a stronger question than “did we save the old record?” It asks whether retained information suffices for the future inquiries that remain authorised. Its finite-reference-model results include exact worst-case information-loss measures, indistinguishable alternatives and predictive-memory bounds. This is directly relevant to research handoffs: a stored citation is not automatically usable knowledge of what the citation establishes. [S2]

Accordingly, the earlier dismissal of tensor methods as only a possible future addition was incorrect. Describing the minimal record signature accurately did not justify treating that signature as the whole mathematical programme.

## 2. What an assumed manifold legitimately allows

The owner's point is a modelling move: choose a Riemannian space in which selected represented states and their transformations can be studied. It is not an assertion that an unencoded tuple is intrinsically a smooth manifold.

That move can be made parametrically. Conditional mathematics can begin with a suitable smooth state space Q, metric g, realisation map and objective. It need not wait for a uniquely identified universal metric of cognition. A Riemannian gradient is then a genuine mathematical object, characterised by g(grad_g J, v) = dJ(v), not a metaphor. [S7, §§3.7–3.8, 8.9]

The remaining application question is what the model represents and preserves. That is a separate obligation, not a reason to forbid the conditional calculation. The correct discipline is to retain both an assumed geometry and an explicit model-to-case contract.

The September dynamics section already adopts this pattern: it presents a possible hybrid realisation and names the declarations required to instantiate it. Its qualification about G2 concerns literal gradient-flow claims and particular runtime implementations. It does not prohibit developing a specified geometric model. [S1, §10.2; S3]

## 3. How the philosophical foundations fit

The owner's foundationalist and proper-functionalist commitments orient this research lane towards genuine warrant, truth-directed cognition and appropriate correction, rather than simply maximising a preferred output. Their role should not disappear from an account of the modelling programme.

However, they should be recorded at their actual scope. The minimal record core, a philosophical account of warrant, a particular representation and a runtime implementation are not identical commitments. The retained proper-function programme explicitly distinguishes proper function from reliability, current success, fitness and authorisation. The noetic application treats fiṭrah as normative orientation rather than one coordinate or one measured scalar. [S3; S6]

Thus, the appropriate synthesis is: these philosophical commitments guide what a useful model must be accountable to; a declared geometry provides a mathematical means of investigating part of that structure. A metric is not the ground of warrant merely because the metric is convenient, and a lower objective value is not the definition of truth.

This corrects both earlier errors: under-describing the mathematics and over-describing the philosophical commitments as though they automatically supplied every geometric choice.

## 4. The interfaces Dot should retain

A useful layered representation is:

    Audited episode and research state
        → declared tensor representation
        → selected geometric realisation
        → mathematical operation or trajectory
        → decoded claim, action or continuation
        → renewed audit.

This diagram is a proposed synthesis, not a claim that one completed implementation already realises every arrow.

The tensor representation and the geometric realisation are different objects. Semantic factor spaces need not be tangent spaces; payoff tensors need not be metric tensors. A model may place typed feature tensors in bundles over Q while its Riemannian metric acts on tangent vectors. The connecting maps must be declared instead of inferred from the shared word “tensor”.

The full state may retain exact identity, source, authority and history outside its smooth coordinates. Two records that have equal numerical summaries but different source authorisation must not acquire the same verdict merely because the geometry cannot see the difference. The existing fibre criterion gives the right test: where an assessment is to be recovered from the representation alone, equal represented states must not hide unequal required assessments. [S1, §§4, 11]

Also keep an ordinary coordinate change separate from a genuine change of metric or normative analysis. A well-formed geometric calculation transforms consistently under a change of description. Changing the metric may change the algorithm's preferred direction. Neither operation, by itself, changes which bearer a claim concerns or what evidence authorises it.

Finally, do not reuse the same symbol for different objects. In the September document, L_t is the prospective inquiry landscape. A scalar objective for the proposed geometric model should have its own name, such as J. The project-level GDOPL gloss also should not be silently identified with a physical action integral or an already implemented differentiable loss.

## 5. What would be a productive next step

The immediate step is recovery and reconciliation, not a new unbounded campaign. Dot should trace a few live obligations to the inherited mathematics and say what each proposed geometric bridge would establish.

Good discriminators are representation fidelity, coordinate invariance, metric sensitivity, feasibility-preserving correction, and continuation after contraction or compression. These have a direct connection to the existing programme. For example, a contraction that makes a representation smaller but destroys a future source-ownership distinction should fail its preservation contract. A coordinate transformation that preserves the model should preserve its corresponding calculations. A metric chosen solely to reward the preferred conclusion should be exposed as a changed modelling assumption, not counted as new evidence.

A conditional gradient-flow model can prove objective decrease from the gradient identity. It needs further premises for feasibility, global continuation, convergence or interpretation as warranted correction. The right response is to formulate those additional claims, not to downgrade the model to metaphor or to pretend objective decrease already proves them. The existing energy and continuation work is a starting point for precisely this decomposition. [S1, §10]

Geometry can therefore supply real mathematical leverage. That leverage lies in preservation, interaction, change and bounds—not in renaming a philosophical claim as a coordinate.
