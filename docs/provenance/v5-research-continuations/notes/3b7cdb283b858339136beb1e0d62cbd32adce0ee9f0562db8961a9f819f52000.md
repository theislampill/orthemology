# The exact deterministic reporting-clock trade-off

Status: fresh ordinary corollary awaiting independent review of this note and FINITE_HORIZON_BETA_RATES.md. This supplement does not change the one-receipt-per-report contract of the main result.

Suppose instead that evaluated report j>=1 observes exactly n_j receipts, where (n_j) is a fixed deterministic nondecreasing sequence of positive integers. Receipts remain passive iid conditional on one Beta(a,b) parameter, the policy does not know that parameter, and its seed remains independent. Only reports at this declared schedule are counted. Repeated n_j values are permitted; repeated reports are charged separately.

The per-step universal lower and empirical upper bounds in the finite-horizon note apply with n=n_j. Therefore a common policy has finite expected total evaluated-report failures if and only if

  sum_j [n_j^(-a)+n_j^(-b)] < infinity.

Equivalently, with m=min(a,b), the exact condition is sum_j n_j^(-m)<infinity. The retained empirical learner witnesses sufficiency; the lower bound excludes every common randomised policy when the sum diverges. Randomisation over reports cannot evade the posterior bound.

Concrete schedules:

- n_j comparable to j^gamma: a finite budget exists exactly when gamma*m>1.
- n_j comparable to j^gamma [log(j+1)]^p: it exists when gamma*m>1, or when gamma*m=1 and p*m>1; it fails when gamma*m<1, or at equality with p*m<=1. Assume the sequence is eventually nondecreasing and positive; finitely many initial terms do not affect the criterion.
- A geometrically growing receipt count gives a finite expected evaluated-report budget for every proper Beta prior.
- Under a uniform prior, linear receipt counts cannot give a finite budget. A schedule comparable to j[log(j+1)]^p works precisely when p>1.

These are direct series tests with both directions justified by the all-policy rate theorem. There is no claim that changing the reporting schedule creates a finite physical per-receipt mistake budget. If an application must issue and evaluate a report at every receipt, those intervening reports still count and the original impossibility remains. Likewise the theorem does not cover adaptive stopping times: conditioning on a data-dependent acquisition time changes the relevant posterior-history events and requires a separate proof.

This identifies the resource bought by the apparent improvement: more acquisitions for each evaluated report, with the physical evaluation clock explicitly changed. It can guide an interface that already permits deferred evaluation, but it supplies no authority to omit costs that the original interface requires.
