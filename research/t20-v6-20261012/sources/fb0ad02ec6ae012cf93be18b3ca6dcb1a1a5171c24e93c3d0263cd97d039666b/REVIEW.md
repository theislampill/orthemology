## A. Joint disclosure: actual source and inferential force

### A1. Voices, sequence and the two repairs

The complete retained 42-line Arabic unit was read from the Abū Yaʿlā heading through the final discussion of basic recognition, before the Ibn al-Zāghūnī heading. Abū Yaʿlā's initial inference and al-Shīrāzī's intervening argument remain reported positions. Ibn Taymiyyah's replies qualify them.

The author grants that knowing a messenger as messenger includes a sender. But that relational entailment alone does not settle how the messenger is known. If the sign's authentication already requires the very source existence and veracity being established, the proposed acquisition route circles. His first repair invokes basic Creator recognition; it does not require an antecedent technical demonstration. His second says the sign itself can indicate its producer and the purpose of confirming the messenger, even without prior acknowledgment of the producer. The two routes cannot be collapsed into the first merely because the source preserves fiṭrah in his conclusion.

This supports the report's rejection of an automatic testimony-first circularity charge. It does not provide an exemption from every evidential premise. In particular, an assertion that a relation exists is not automatically adequate evidence of that relation.

### A2. Copyist and royal examples do distinct work

The copyist scene includes a disputed unseen copyist, blank paper, a poem freshly prepared in the scene, a request to copy, and return of the matching inscription. The claimant's speech tells observers what response to inspect. Accepting that instruction as a specification need not involve trusting the claimant's claim about a hidden producer. Perception, comparison, memory and competent causal understanding can supply ordinary positive warrant.

The directly supported conclusion is a producer competent to perform the displayed copying. The scene does not itself establish a commission, unrestricted truthfulness, omniscience, uniqueness, or comprehension of every meaning copied. Nor does it contain modern experimental controls that the reviewer may silently add. A real observation or substitution defect could matter; bare possibilities do not automatically defeat the ordinary example.

The royal scene instead places the act in an openly specified confirming context. That can make the source's intentional response intelligible. Its force does not follow from movement alone, the claimant's request alone, or calling the movement a miracle. The theological proposal combines producer-indication with purpose-indication in the same occurrence; it need not borrow all warrant from antecedent Creator assent. But the ordinary examples do not by themselves establish that any selected historical episode satisfies both contributions.

The source also claims extraordinary transformation discloses a powerful, knowing and wise producer, with greater force than ordinary generation. The report accurately records that positive claim while declining to reconstruct its entire created-capacity exclusion. The failure of some performers is not silently treated as proof of every possible creature's inability. This is a bounded explanatory comparison, not a new complete proof of divine attributes.

### A3. Discovery, rational acceptance and narrative authentication

The intervening reply expressly permits an argument to be scriptural in how it is taught and rational in how its correctness is known. Thus a reader can learn the copyist reasoning from scripture-directed discussion without resting its rational force on prior revelatory authentication. The report correctly distinguishes that from using a narrative accepted solely through the authority under investigation as independent historical proof of that authority.

The Moses narrative is source-owned illustration here, not a newly authenticated observation. A present reader and an observer inside the described event occupy different evidential positions. Independently warranted transmission could mediate knowledge, but neither the author report nor this review supplies a new transmission reconstruction. These limits do not erase inherited canonical or source-standing appraisals.

A newly supported conclusion also does not certify the earlier route's acquisition history or restore every part of its warrant. And one occurrence supporting producer and commission is not automatically two independent acquisitions. These controls are consistent with the full withdrawal, correction, local-resolution and particular-recognition predecessors reviewed here.

The attestation-capacity predecessor already grants al-Juwaynī's stronger present-appointment response with its source, force and scope premises. ## B. Message-established accountability: practical explanation and scope

### B1. Wrongness, prior desert and the further condition

The complete held Miftāḥ 2:872–878 selection, including its apparatus, was read. The opening examples and 874–876 oppose treating the offered normative reason as identical with the command or prohibition. At 877 the author describes the prior actions as bad and deserving of adverse treatment, then distinguishes actual punishment after sending messengers and establishing the case through the message. At 878 he rejects both converse errors: absence of punishment does not eliminate objective wrongness, and rational wrongness alone does not license pre-message punishment in this account.

The report preserves the difficult prior-desert wording rather than redefining it away. “Condition governing punitive treatment” is consequently well chosen. Its target is not the creation of all culpability by communication. It also does not infer that fulfillment of the message condition is sufficient for punishment or makes punishment mandatory.

Modern apparatus is not additional medieval argument. The held selection's apparent 17:23 locator for the zina prohibition is already separately noted as erroneous; the central 28:47 reasoning does not depend on it. Original source bytes remain unchanged.

### B2. Intention is not inferred from consequence alone

The complete held Shifāʾ electronic190–191 was read, including the fifth principle, purposive examples, consequence objection and first general reply. The reviewer also read electronic192 as adjacent context, completing the sentence and paragraph begun at the end of191. The later extended series of individual replies is not claimed as completely read.

The fifth principle distinguishes the wisdom for which an act is done from causes through which it is done. Its cited 4:165 explicitly links messenger-sending with the no-excuse end. The objection proposes a mere eventual-consequence interpretation; the author's first answer directly rejects that treatment of the divine-action language. The remaining local continuation does not reverse the answer.

This grounds attributing the stated purpose within the source's own account. It is not an inference that every foreseen creaturely uptake is endorsed, nor a neutral proof from omniscience alone that every outcome is desired under every description. Shifāʾ and Miftāḥ are same-author contextual evidence, not independent historical witnesses. The report uses them at that limited role.

All Arabic verses28:44–50 in the retained connected-service returns were also read. Mercy, warning and possible remembrance occur before the no-message plea; resistance follows the coming of truth. Provision therefore need not be reduced to securing punishment, and provision does not logically require universal willing acceptance. The response diagnoses in these verses are not exported into verdicts on unspecified later recipients.

### B3. Own act, actual provision and the tested replacements

The explanation's object is God's described communicative provision, not merely approval of a creature's obedience. The wrongness of prior action and the normative condition can be specified independently of whether the communication occurs. Actual provision addresses the identified absence of messenger/book. Thus the report supplies a practical reason at one respect rather than relabeling an occurrence as wise solely because it occurred.

Three replacements fail for identifiable reasons:

- Prior knowledge and a true norm do not themselves perform the required communicative provision.
- Appointment, a text located somewhere, or audible syllables do not by definition establish the relevant explanatory relation.
- Merely declaring that no excuse remains does not substitute for the ground that would make that declaration true. An adequately warranted truthful report of an already fulfilled condition is different: the prior fulfillment then does the work.

The second restriction correctly depends on inherited commissioned-content analysis, not on pretending Miftāḥ 877 gives a complete per-person understanding test. That predecessor distinguishes appointment, successful explanatory discharge, availability, actual uptake, retained access and later custody loss. It also allows culpable neglect to matter and refuses a blanket perpetual-access theorem. None of those distinctions is removed here.

The source's conditional structure does not turn individual assent into a universal accomplishment guaranteed by sending. It also does not decide every case of ignorance, inability or refusal. There is no licensed inference to named-person guilt, group guilt, or a human permission to punish.

### B4. What the own-act result accomplishes

The complete wise-reason predecessor leaves a practical-content requirement for this own act. The new argument meets that requirement at a limited respect: why warning has a real role beyond knowing a true norm. It does not specify every circumstance, exact wording, messenger, time or intrinsic volition-token. Nor does it prove that this end uniquely selects the entire history.

The nearby conditional-right and continuing-title reports already distinguish an actual fulfilled condition from knowing it, and source-owned undertakings from foreign legislation. The responsive-demonstration and confirmatory-question reports already show communicative achievements beyond initial information acquisition. The new warning application earns credit for its different prior-wrongness/message-condition relation and executed substitution test, not for rediscovering those general distinctions.

Knowledge, Will, Wisdom and source/disclosure warrant remain inherited foundations of the theological application. They are not re-proved here and are not demoted to unsupported guesses.
