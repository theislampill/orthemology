## The continuous primary argument

The justified principal locus is **11:283–302**, ending before the new *fasl* at 303. It starts where the preceding catalogue of reported wonders turns to their differential role and their interpretation. Reading only the quoted list at 302 would omit the explanation for the restriction.

1. **Need is not rank (283).** Extraordinary help may address someone's need or weak faith; someone better qualified may lack the same occurrence because it is unnecessary. The author separately distinguishes wonders for the guidance and needs of others. Thus presence, frequency and possession are not a simple ranking measure.
2. **Accuracy or assistance is not unrestricted trustworthiness (283–286).** The author reports divinatory and deceptive phenomena, including mixtures of true information and added falsehood. His narrated examples are offered as factual within his account; this reading does not independently authenticate them.
3. **Causal and normative setting matters (287–294).** The explicit contrast concerns faith/piety versus forbidden means, wrongful purposes, impersonation and departure from accepted revelation. The rational consideration at 287 is that what is prohibited cannot, as such, be the cause of an honor granted for it. This uses substantive prior normative commitments, not an inference from impressive effects alone.
4. **Both blanket denial and blanket certification are rejected (294–295).** He rejects denying all nonprophetic wonders and treating every possessor of a wonder as a divine ally. He proposes differentiating conduct and the nature of competing powers. The rejected positions are reported alternatives; the third position is his own.
5. **The governing value is right conduct (295–300).** The argument contrasts modes of religious practice, then classifies extraordinary capacities alongside ordinary knowledge, power and wealth. Their use can be good, wrongful or permissible. The same possession does not remove accountability; it may become harmful to the possessor. His particular legal and historical claims are not being independently adjudicated here.
6. **Gift and deprivation can both be tests (300–301).** Apparent favors and apparent losses are not transparent measures of divine esteem. The text supplies purposes other than approval, including testing and gradual entrapment, and discusses people mistaking misleading displays for credentials.
7. **The quotation's actual antecedent (302).** Immediately before Ameri's excerpt, the primary requires faith and piety as the cause of saintly karamat. The following diagnostic compares the conditions under which the phenomena appear or diminish and the person's conduct. The passage then appeals to Quranic passages and an attributed Ibn Abbas interpretation. These are distinguished from the author's own connective argument.

These are linked source claims, not seven independent proofs. Full electronic reading, decisive scan checks and pagination controls are separated in `EXACT_READING.md`. [Primary locus](https://quranislam.github.io/fatwa/ibn-taymiyyah/fatwa173.html).

## Discrimination at the actual T20 boundary

### A real restriction, with a conditional application

If a recipient has good independent evidence that an occurrence depends on an illicit practice, supports wrongdoing, or fits an independently established deceptive source profile, those facts can count against interpreting it as an authenticating divine honor. If the background standards themselves are already warranted, this need not be circular relative to a particular later claimant. The passage is therefore more informative than an unrestricted appeal to prodigies.

But this operation has supplied **textual warrant for attributing that criterion to the author**, not independent warrant that a particular event has the relevant cause, that a narrator's report is accurate, or that every competing interpretation is excluded. A criterion concerning saintly phenomena within an already accepted revelatory framework cannot simply be reused to establish that very revelation from scratch. This dependency point is an application check, not a finding that every use of scripture or background knowledge is circular.

### What it does not settle about courtesy, gifts and tests

The retained experiment deliberately separates a material gift goal from adopted indication; the source text does not collapse that distinction. Conversely, discovering a benevolent or courtesy purpose does not show there was no concurrent answer or public acknowledgment. Nothing in this locus establishes that a gift purpose excludes a concurrent informing purpose; the inherited means–end review already explains that distinction. Removing a merely terminal-goal label was already the review's correction.

The Majmu passage does not show that the constructed gift-only planner is an admissible actual alternative under a recipient's full evidence. Nor does it show that this alternative is excluded. It supplies no observed disclaimer, verified causal dependence, source-character record, available-plan evidence, or historically authenticated communicative setting for that planner. Thus it adds no new executed or independently grounded intention discriminator to the existing recovery result.

### Knowledge is not exhaustive identification

A source-faithful assessment still allows ordinary contextual recognition of an answer, even if remote alternatives or incompletely known ultimate motives remain. There is no need to recover every utility weight, eliminate every imaginable psychology, or run the stipulated laboratory protocol before admitting defeasible knowledge. Equally, one cannot infer actual source ownership merely by naming a capacity divine or calling an episode a sign. Both boundaries were already preserved in the later records.
