# Premise defenses — Candidate E

## E-P1 — Objective correction is not mere transition

**Premise.** `Corr_r(t,s)` attributes more than chronological succession or preference change: it says that `t` is better, more accurate, more adequate, or less defective in respect `r`.

**Defense.** Denying this collapses the target into a descriptive relation. A self-modifying optimizer can switch rewards, and a community can replace conventions, without either event being an objective correction. The premise does not establish a particular ontology of normativity; it fixes the explanandum.

**Countermodel pressure.** Error theory can deny that any such `Corr` facts exist. The argument is conditional on their existence.

**Status.** `DEFENDED_AS_TARGET_FIXING`

## E-P2 — Full-basis radicality

**Premise.** Some corrections, if objective, can be correct partly because the current token's complete alleged authority basis is inadequate in the relevant respect.

**Defense.** This is stronger than revising first-order rules. The self-amending-constitution objection shows why the full basis must be specified: retained higher-order amendment rules can authorize dramatic revision. Candidate E applies only where the alleged token-autonomous source is itself in question.

**Countermodel pressure.** A constitutivist may deny that the full type-level basis is ever corrected; only defective tokens are. That places the view in the trans-state/type-level horn rather than refuting the partition.

**Status.** `CONDITIONAL_EXISTENCE_PREMISE; INSTANCE_REQUIRED`

## E-P3 — Authority adequacy

**Premise.** A basis that is insufficient to settle a normative question cannot be the complete sufficient authority that settles that same question.

**Defense.** This is a same-respect and same-question constraint, not a global asymmetry axiom. It prevents equivocation between (a) a defeated standard causally producing its successor and (b) that standard normatively authorizing the successor. A bad rule can accidentally cause a good outcome; causation is not normative authority.

**Countermodel pressure.** A dialetheist or nonmonotonic authority theory may allow defeated standards to retain partial authority. The theorem permits partial authority and denies only *complete sufficient* authority.

**Status.** `DEFENDED_WITH_SCOPE_RESTRICTION`

## E-P4 — Token/type distinction

**Premise.** A standard may transcend a current token while remaining constitutive of a type or practice.

**Defense.** Belief may be assessed by truth even when a believer endorses false epistemic rules; an organism may malfunction relative to a selected-effect account despite its current dispositions. These models show why token-transcendence does not entail nonnatural or divine normativity.

**Status.** `DEFENDED; LIMITS_CONCLUSION`

## E-P5 — Regress needs a dependency diagnosis

**Premise.** An infinite correction hierarchy is not vicious merely because it is infinite.

**Defense.** The relevant question is whether authority for the trigger is deferred along a dependency chain that never supplies it, or whether the sequence is globally constituted or generated from an independently authoritative seed. This follows the general distinction between benign and vicious regresses by dependency direction.

**Status.** `DEFENDED; BLOCKS_OVERCLAIM`

## E-P6 — Contract-relative success is weaker

**Premise.** Success under a selected contract is not yet objective correction of the contract or standard itself.

**Defense.** Any destructive or arbitrary objective function can define relative success. To infer objective fittingness from success, one needs an independent premise that the goal, contract, or function has noninstrumental authority.

**Status.** `DEFENDED`

## E-P7 — No automatic bridge to Wisdom

**Premise.** State-transcendent normative authority does not by itself entail a purposive intellectual ground.

**Defense.** Type-level constitutivism, natural normativity, brute normative realism, coherentism, and impersonal normative structure are live models of the conclusion without Wisdom.

**Status.** `DEFENDED_AS_COUNTERMODEL_BOUNDARY`
