## 1. Mathematical and predicate check

The finite-observation theorem is sound: if every p(w)>0, every loss(w)≥0 and Σ_w p(w)loss(w)=0, each nonnegative summand is zero and therefore each loss(w)=0. Any other finite weighted score is then zero. The formal theorem needs no probability normalization of p and no positivity of the comparison weights; that slightly stronger algebraic scope is legitimate.

The concrete obstruction imports the actual retained `AnnularLiteral.failureIndicator`; it does not redefine a lookalike predicate. Both predecessor files are byte-identical to the candidate-2 nonconvexity source and the accepted prior source. Independent rational evaluation at ε=1/4, q=9/16 gives:

- α=1/4: D0=−3/256 and D1=−3/256, hence literal failure is zero
- α=1/2: D0=−23/256 and D1=9/256, hence literal failure is one

A common nonnegative bit table (l0,l1) with expectation zero under Bernoulli(1/4) must have l0=l1=0. Its Bernoulli(1/2) expectation cannot then equal one. `literal_no_common_next_bit_score` proves exactly this obstruction, including the indicator's ENNReal-to-real conversion at values 0 and 1.

The signed countercontrol and the endpoint-support countercontrol are both valid. In fact the signed table (−1,3) has exact expectations 0 and 1 at the two displayed parameters. Shifting it to be nonnegative adds a positive per-stage baseline, so it does not preserve the zero/one literal loss or the finite-total-loss premise. The obstruction depends on nonnegativity and full support, not on the upper bound of one.

Every finite Boolean word has strictly positive probability under any interior iid Bernoulli parameter. Applying the general theorem to such words therefore gives the stated fixed-finite-word ordinary consequence. This precise Bernoulli word instantiation is not a new formal theorem in the author packet. Infinite-stream observations and parameter-revealing feedback are outside the claim.

## 2. Primary-source comparison

The 2001 attribution is accurate. The author's publication page identifies ICML 2001, pp. 210–217. The linked PDF is dated 10 April 2001. Section 3.1 defines a common observed-symbol loss table in [0,1], and Theorem 2 on PDF page 4 compares the mixture action rule with the informed rule. Its discussion explicitly gives finite-total-loss transfer when the informed total loss is finite. The binary-action scope of that particular theorem should remain visible. Sources: [author publication page](https://hutter1.net/ai/ploss.htm), [paper, Section 3.1 and Theorem 2](https://hutter1.net/ai/ploss.pdf).

The later paper's cited Sections 2.4 and 3.4 are also correct: the former concerns posterior convergence, and the latter defines bounded loss indexed by the observed next symbol and the chosen action, with general finite observation alphabets and action spaces. The companion's limited comparison is supported. [Hutter, Optimality of Universal Bayesian Sequence Prediction for General Loss and Alphabet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0311014).

The review inference is narrow: the literal latent-parameter indicator cannot be imported directly as that same common nonnegative finite-observation loss. The theorem does not refute proper scoring, probability estimation, posterior convergence, signed estimation, approximate representations, different feedback or parameter-aware scoring. Neither does it assert that Hutter's results require a finite model class; his class can be countable. The mismatch here is the loss/information interface, not merely cardinality.

This distinction is especially useful for the two-prior nonconvexity construction. Those two priors induce perfectly valid sequence laws, and a two-element Bayesian mixture dominates either law. A generic observed-loss finite-cost transfer would require a common observed loss representing the criterion; the explicit obstruction shows why that identification is unavailable. This explanation does not claim to exclude every possible indirect reduction.
