## 1. The actual example

The source uses

    a_i=exp(-(i+1)^2),  w_i=a_i/Z,  Z=sum_j a_j,  i>=0.

It proves positivity, the upper bound a_i<=1/2, and half separation. The ratio
and logarithmic ratio are proved exactly:

    a_(i+1)/a_i=exp(-(2i+3)),
    log(a_i/a_(i+1))=2i+3.

Consequently the p=1 spacing term is
`exp(-(2i+3))(2i+3)`. Its infinite series is proved summable by expressing it
as a fixed multiple of a linear-times-geometric series plus a geometric
series. No finite truncation, numerical convergence test, or desired lifetime
bound is assumed.

The actual-process iff then supplies a measurable common policy with finite
expected literal count for every permitted epsilon and independent
probability-space seed. This is an actual seeded infinite-process expectation,
not an unnormalized score or a newly defined surrogate objective.

The support starts with exp(-1), not an endpoint. Independent probe examples
also check a_0=exp(-1), a_1=exp(-4), and a_1/a_0=exp(-3), guarding the intended
negative-square formula and index convention.

## 2. Infinite moments under the same prior law

For each i the source proves

    w_i/a_i=1/Z>0.

The latent-law integral is decomposed using the already established mixture
integration theorem. The receipt part integrates a constant with total mass
one. The first inverse moment therefore becomes an extended nonnegative sum
of the same strictly positive constant over all natural indices, and is
proved equal to infinity.

The inverse-variance moment is bounded below by the first inverse moment,
because `0<a_i<1` and `a_i(1-a_i)<=a_i`. Monotonicity of the nonnegative
integral proves its divergence. Neither argument applies a real-valued
nonsummable `tsum`, whose default convention could have erased divergence;
the decisive infinite sums/integrals are in extended nonnegative reals.

The endpoint event is empty pointwise: every supported parameter is positive
and at most one half. Its measure is therefore zero. This is an interior
discrete prior with no endpoint atoms, not a claim that the prior is
nonatomic everywhere.

Lean's real reciprocal uses a total division operation, including at zero.
That convention cannot explain this counterexample: the reciprocal
denominators are strictly positive at every sample point. The review probe
explicitly proves the sampled parameter lies strictly inside (0,1).

## 5. Limits retained

This formal example does not establish a general classification of arbitrary
priors, comparable-weight perturbations, endpoint-atom necessity, or
almost-sure/pointwise convergence. It does not formalize a computable-real
input interface or an efficient policy implementation.

In particular, its exponential-quadratic support differs from the exact-
rational executable reference's geometric and doubly exponential dyadic
families. The mathematical finite-budget example and that executable must
not be presented as the identical implemented prior. The broader ordinary
effective-input theorem is separately scoped.

The counterexample establishes that the interior inverse moment is not
necessary for an optimal finite expected literal-failure budget outside the
earlier comparable-density class. It does not contradict the earlier theorem
within that density class, whose endpoint-neighborhood assumptions exclude
this sparse discrete support. No historical novelty claim is made.
