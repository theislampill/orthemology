## 1. Why the finite tree covers all real codebooks

Suppose a fixed codebook has risk `r<B`, with the target `B` specified by its
universe size. Set the LP variable `t=r`. The risk is a maximum over finitely
many corruption sets, so this substitution is well defined even for arbitrary
real row entries. F1 gives `t>=2/u`. For every four-set `C`, at least one row
satisfies `p_m(C)<=t`. There is no approximation by a positive rational margin,
no grid, and no numerical strict-feasibility assumption.

The atom cap is justified without circularity. If any row has an atom at least
`B`, select that root and discard the satisfied row. Normalize the other two
rows on the remaining `u-1` roots, substituting an arbitrary auxiliary row if
a residual has zero mass. The reviewed two-message value is
`V_fixed(u-1,3,2)=B` for each of these even universes. Its three-root witness,
together with the selected root, has mass at least `B` in every original row.
This contradicts `r<B`. Thus each atom is strictly below `B`; relaxing the cap
to `p_m(i)<=B` only enlarges the counterexample domain to be excluded.

The symmetry restrictions are valid globally. Pick a row containing a largest
atom among all three rows, relabel it as row 1, then apply one common permutation
of root labels to sort that row. Risk is invariant under both operations. Its
first atom is at least every atom in the other rows. The proof does not sort
the other rows independently, and no additional unproved leaf-specific
symmetry reduction occurs.

Every internal node branches on an as-yet-unassigned corruption scenario and
has all three message choices. A putative counterexample satisfies at least
one child inequality. Each child adds precisely that assignment to the parent
domain. Hence a counterexample can follow a root-to-leaf path. Earlier
constraints are never dropped, and a scenario is never reassigned.

## 2. Independent reconstruction and topology checks

`independent_certificate_verifier.py` does not read the generator's matrix or
its descriptive base labels. It reconstructs the inequalities from their
mathematical meanings using symbolic row/root coordinates:

- atom caps `B-p_m(i)>=0`
- nonincreasing first row
- first-row initial atom at least every atom in rows 2 and 3
- known bound `t-2/u>=0`
- each edge-derived assigned scenario's slack `t-p_m(C)>=0`

Row normalization is represented separately as three equalities. Nonnegative
row coordinates and nonnegative `t` are the variable-domain assumptions.

Paths are reconstructed from traversed edges, beginning at the empty root;
stored node paths are checked against those derived paths. Each internal node
has all three distinct children with correct message assignments. Each branch
scenario is valid and not previously assigned. Reused nodes are accepted only
when their complete domains coincide; differing-domain reuse is rejected.
Increasing path length excludes cycles, and all payload nodes must be reached.
Leaves must contain exact certificates and cannot contain branch data.

The checker independently un-ranks lexicographic four-subsets using binomial
counts. All 790 scenario indices across `u=6,8,10,12` were checked against the
standard lexicographic enumeration as a separate control. An individual path
need not mention every possible corruption set: its exact bound may close a
larger domain sooner. Full three-child coverage at every actual branch is the
relevant completeness condition.

## 3. Exact leaf proof, in a different algebraic form

The author uses `Ax<=d`, `Ex=1`, `x>=0` and dual multipliers `y<=0`, with
`A^T y+E^T z<=c` and `d^T y+sum z>=B`. These are valid lower-bound weak-duality
signs: multiplying the primal inequalities by nonpositive multipliers reverses
their direction. Coordinate domination then uses `x>=0`.

The independent replay instead sets nonnegative slack weights `lambda=-y`.
It expands a polynomial identity

`t-B = sum lambda_i*(d_i-A_i*x) + sum z_j*((E*x)_j-1)
       + sum s_k*x_k + (L-B)`,

where `s=c+A^T lambda-E^T z` and `L=sum z-d^T lambda`. It verifies every slack
weight is nonnegative, every coordinate remainder `s_k` is nonnegative, and
the constant gap `L-B` is nonnegative. It checks the recorded leaf bound equals
the value actually derived. Multiplying by a common positive denominator
turns the polynomial's coefficient and constant identities into exact integer
checks. On a feasible node domain, all non-equality summands are nonnegative,
so the identity proves `t>=B`.

This is ordinary exact weak duality. It assumes neither optimizer optimality
nor floating infeasibility, and it does not trust a reported numerical bound.
The solver-generated choices of branches and rational candidates can be
arbitrary: only their reconstructed coverage and exact certificates matter.

## 6. Ordinary proof regimes and complete assembly

The block construction `1,q,q+epsilon` has the claimed value. If the singleton
is corrupt, only three corrupt roots remain for the two large blocks, one of
which has at most one. Conversely the allocation `1,1,2` attains `1/q` because
`2/(q+epsilon)>=1/q`; all counts fit for the stated `u>=5`.

At `u=5`, at most three roots can have mass above `1/2` in any of the three
rows. Omit another root. At `u=6`, a row's pairs of mass strictly above `1/2`
are pairwise intersecting, hence lie in a star or a triangle. Three such
families cannot cover `K6`: three stars leave a triangle outside their centers;
two stars plus a triangle cover at most 12 edges; the remaining combinations
cover at most 11 or 9. This establishes the elementary endpoint independently
of its redundant certificate.

The retained odd-family argument was inspected. Its heavy-atom branch uses F2
on the even residual universe. Otherwise a minimum-sum covering-polytope vertex
has at most three fractional coordinates and total mass below three. With two
unit coordinates, remaining fractional mass is at most `1/s`. Positive row
deficits are below `1/s^2`; each deficit-covering set has at least `s-1` roots.
Three such sets in `2s-1` positions cannot be pairwise disjoint for `s>=3`.
Two added roots suffice. All strict inequalities, padding, and zero-residual
cases are accounted for.

For the even tail `u=2q+2`, `q>=6`, the heavy-atom branch uses the two-message
value on `2q+1` residual roots. In the no-heavy-atom branch, the difficult vertex
has two unit coordinates and fractional mass `X<=2/q<1`. Each positive deficit
`delta_m` satisfies `0<delta_m<t*X<t`, so
`beta_m=delta_m/(t-delta_m)` is well-defined and positive. Deficit-covering sets
are nonempty. If they were pairwise disjoint, their total cardinality is at
most `2q`; total row mass gives `sum beta_m>(q-3)/q`. Feasibility on the
fractional coordinates gives `X(H_m)>beta_m*(1-X)`, so
`X>(q-3)/(2q-3)`. This contradicts `X<=2/q` because
`q*(q-3)-2*(2q-3)=(q-1)*(q-6)>=0`.

The endpoint `q=6` is sound precisely because the preceding inequality is
strict. Pairwise overlap supplies a root covering two deficits and one further
root for the third. There are no missing even universes: `u=6` is elementary,
`u=8,10,12` are certified, and all even `u>=14` lie in the tail. The odd
endpoint is `u=5`, followed by the odd family for every odd `u>=7`.
