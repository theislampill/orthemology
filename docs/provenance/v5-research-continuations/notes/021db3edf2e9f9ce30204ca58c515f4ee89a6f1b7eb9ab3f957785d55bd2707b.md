# Exact sourcehood with actual Knowledge and a standing Knowledge boundary

## Checked result

The new `ModalKnowledge.lean` cold-compiles after three byte-identical imports: `SourceIdentity.lean`, `ProductiveCompleteness.lean`, and the fifth-tranche `KnowledgeScope.lean`. It preserves the exact retained productive and modal source relation schemas while adding actual cognition to the original source. The worldwise existential cognitive instance of original-giver priority holds, but worldwise standing source cognition fails.

The counterinterpretation is stronger than the fifth one in a particular respect. The fifth source did not know in any represented world. Here the source knows at the actual world, and a recipient's actual cognitive perfection is accompanied by source cognition wherever the recipient exists. The interpretation therefore passes the actual Knowledge endpoint of the original-giver argument and still isolates the additional essential-standing bridge.

This is a logical scope result. It does not establish that such a source is metaphysically possible, refute the full Taymiyyan perfection doctrine, or exhaustively formalize objective orthability.

## The model

The imported frame has worlds a, b and c and entities u, r, ka, kb and z. The same u exists at every world and receives no productive supply. The recipient r exists and knows p at a and b; its thought tokens ka and kb are separately owned. At c, u actually produces z. The proper unborrowed mode cz is an actual productive witness there. Hence source activity is never absent.

The new cognitive interpretation gives u Knowledge of p at a and b and no cognition at c. Recipient Knowledge at a and b is preserved as a granted semantic input. Factivity and subject existence are checked. Knowledge is not inferred from a table's behaviour, and u does not own r's thought token.

`ActualGiverPriority` means exactly: at each represented world, if u is a complete source of a cognitively actual recipient, u has some actual Knowledge. The conclusion is existential. It does not transfer every content known by the recipient to the source and is not a definition of omniscience.

At c the cognitive recipient antecedent fails, though the source is productively active. The resulting counterinterpretation establishes the **modal recipient gap**: applying an actual-giving rule at every world need not establish a standing attribute independently of effects.

All worlds are accessible in the added possible-giving predicate. There is a possible cognitive recipient from every world. That strengthens the premise only to possible cognitive giving, not present cognitive activity. Calling that predicate a complete metaphysical or causal analysis of ability would overstate it.

The formal law `HasKnowledge w u ↔ Active w` is the same across worlds. `Active` is a world-varying intrinsic state in the interpretation. The model does not show why such a state is metaphysically admissible. Its purpose is to prevent existential independence from being mistaken for a premise fixing every intrinsic state.

## Positive implications also checked

Three generic theorems expose the routes which do work:

1. Actual cognitive giving plus the cognitive priority rule yields actual source Knowledge
2. Cognitive giving at every world plus that rule yields source Knowledge at every world
3. Necessary source existence, presence of its nature whenever it exists, and complete intrinsic determination of cognition by that nature yield source Knowledge at every world

The third theorem does not prove its intrinsic-determination premise. The second does not prove that every possible world contains a dependent knower. The first does not obtain priority from productive completeness alone.

The formal `StandingKnowledge` predicate means worldwise possession of some cognition. Essentiality in the explanatory sense, being due to the source's nature, is stronger than this modal profile. The generic intrinsic-determination theorem marks that extra interpretive content explicitly; no essence theory is manufactured by defining a universal quantifier.
