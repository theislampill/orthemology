## Verdict

The upper recovery guarantee, finite rational-arithmetic certificate, calibration-bias corollary and setting/coordinate counts are correct under the stated known-K response model. The separately developed lower bound matches the exponent of K for the prescribed fixed-p mask instrument. No unresolved mathematical blocker was found in the frozen files.

This is a statistical identification theorem within a declared causal model. It is not a practical sample recommendation, a universal experimental lower bound, a proof that K is valid, or a validation of the physical or source-level intervention.

## Analytic recovery checked

For r>=1 and a known total-route bound K>=1, set p=1/(2K). Every relevant route succeeds with probability at most p, so q>=1-Kp=1/2. This probability floor follows from a union bound and does not itself establish independence. The product formula and logarithmic subset transform still need the stated response law.

Taking epsilon=p^r/(16·2^r), every empirical coordinate within epsilon of its ideal mean lies in [1/4,1]. The log error is at most 4epsilon. Boolean inversion has at most 2^r signed terms, while the denominator magnitude |log(1-p^|P|)| is at least p^r. The exact empirical inverse is therefore within 1/4 of the true integer count. Rounding these aggregate counts first makes the subsequent guard and effect-hit inversions exact on the good event. There is no missing continuous-error amplification through those later inverses.

The same global epsilon works at smaller issued profiles: fewer mask terms and denominators at least as large only improve the bound. The local-profile reindexing in certify_guarded_effect_histogram preserves the actual root masks and is consistent with this proof.

## Certified arithmetic checked

The rational artanh series uses t=(z-1)/(z+1), and its remaining tail after n terms has the stated one-sided sign and absolute bound 2|t|^(2n+1)/[(2n+1)(1-t²)]. For empirical z in [1/4,1], |t|<=3/5; coefficient-log arguments are in [1/2,1). The code's common target B<=p^(2r)/(64·2^r) is thus reachable after finitely many term doublings.

Let s=p^r. Each numerator interval differs from the exact empirical numerator by at most 2^r B. That numerator has absolute value below 2^(r+1). The negative denominator interval stays at magnitude at least s/2. Endpoint division error is consequently at most 5·2^r B/s²<=5/64. Combining this with the statistical 1/4 error puts the complete count interval within 21/64 of the true integer, strictly inside its rounding cell.

The decoder checks the whole interval rather than a floating midpoint. Exact fractions and integers are enforced at the certificate boundary. A pre-freeze float-acceptance defect was identified, reproduced in a failing regression, corrected, and rechecked. The frozen suite rejects floating interval arguments, estimates, confidence inputs and noninteger coordinate counts. An inadequate externally imposed arithmetic cap returns an uncertified result; the success theorem presumes the proved required budget is available.

A numerical certificate concerns the empirical log inversion. It does not certify that the data were generated by the model or that the good event occurred. The latter distinction is retained in the source and returned metadata.

## Statistical and calibration costs checked

The Bernoulli concentration argument in THEOREMS.md correctly derives the log-MGF bound and two-sided Hoeffding estimate. Union bounding L coordinates gives n>=ceil(log(2L/delta)/(2tau²)) trials per setting. No within-trial independence across effect queries is needed.

Under exact calibration tau=epsilon, so 1/(2epsilon²)=128·(16K²)^r. For the full guarded problem, the number of issued-profile/mask settings is G=sum_(S≠empty)(2^|S|-1)=3^r-2^r. Each paired endpoint observation supplies all 2^m-1 effect-query indicators; L=G(2^m-1), while total trials are Gn. The manuscript correctly separates settings, coordinates and total trials.

With independent calibrated gate laws and at most Kr relevant incidences, coupling bounds the bias in any endpoint event by Kr eta. Allowing eta<=epsilon/(2Kr), including leakage at nominal zeros, and using tau=epsilon/2 yields the claimed fourfold sampling coefficient. Accurate marginal gate probabilities alone do not control unknown correlations; the provided counterexample correctly separates them. A full-joint-law TV bound would be an additional externally warranted premise, not a statistic inferred by this decoder.

Polynomial dependence holds in K at fixed r and observation menu. Root/effect dimensions, confidence, constants and physical rate precision have not been optimized. The calibration changes with K, so no contradiction with the earlier unbounded-count fixed-calibration obstruction arises.

## Matching restricted-family bound

RESTRICTED_MASK_LOWER_BOUND.md gives the independent hard-pair argument. For K>=2, use K-1 singleton routes and compare them with the same inventory plus one full-support route. Only the full mask is informative. The full-mask absence probabilities satisfy q1>=1/2, q0<=4/5, and a Bernoulli KL bound of 10p^(2r). The adaptive fixed-budget chain rule, Pinsker inequality and testing identity give

    N >= (2K)^(2r)/45.

When K>=r+1, the common baseline can include every root. The proof is valid for adaptive allocation among the masks at the prescribed p. It does not cover arbitrary nonzero rate choices, extra measurements, narrower classes or a claim about expected stopping times. Thus the K exponent is matched only within this instrument family.
