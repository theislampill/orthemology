# Exact author controls

Run `python exact_controls.py` from this directory. Only Python's standard library is required. Results are saved in results/exact_controls.json and stdout in results/exact_controls.log.

Twelve exact rational control families pass:

1. 135 raw-mask product extractions with lower-order background supports and known counts.
2. 135 full interior/zero-grid endpoint equalities for star, partial-anchor and even-cycle gauges, with strictly ordered endpoint-preserving interpolation knots.
3. Triangle product inversion with no unary anchors.
4. Partial root identification for AB and ABC.
5. The unsigned graph criterion on all 1,098 edge selections through five candidate vertices, restricting columns to participating roots and verifying one anchor per bipartite component.
6. The false probability-mass-row counterexample and full-rank four-triple hypergraph.
7. Equal finite sampled data but different unsampled values, even in a full-rank triangle.
8. Fifteen positive unary-count recoveries by 75 certified half-integer sign decisions using rational root enclosures. The observed maximum 16-bit refinement is fixture-specific, not a uniform bound.
9. The zero unary count leaves these half-cut intervals unresolved; this is not an impossibility claim for a richer presence test.
10. The boundary reduced-inventory identity and triple reconstruction with original ABC count three versus aggregate BC count seven.
11. Pair reconstruction with a positive neighboring unary count, plus rejection of a raw boundary contrast saturated by a unary route.
12. Six all-command point-oracle polygonal approximation controls, for x,x^2,x^7 at two requested tolerances. Every stopped grid has a certified whole-cell enclosure, and the rational polygonal perturbation has positive slopes and the proved error margin.

The unary author controls start from exact rational population contrasts and ratios, then use certified rational intervals for the required roots. The independent review additionally exercises raw probability Cauchy intervals. Neither test is a claim of a physical population oracle or finite empirical observations.

The polygonal controls use new dyadic points when refining a representation. They do not suggest that a fixed finite panel identifies the whole calibration function. General proofs, rather than finite grids or observed refinement costs, establish the stated scope.
