# Continuous notes: al-Ṣafadiyya, assigned range 1

## Reading basis and attribution

These notes cover only the received electronic Arabic at SF-S001-L0001–SF-S001-L0238, 238 electronic lines and 23,915 manifest whitespace words. I displayed and read windows 001–017 in order, including every END marker. Electronic lines are not printed lines or pages. No modern scan or external source was consulted; no diplomatic agreement with a print edition is established. Arabic spellings and apparent transcription defects remain evidence about this witness, not silently corrected quotations.

The received title at L0001 and the honorific question-frame at L0003 are distinct from the answer beginning at L0004. Statements about opponents below are the answering author's representations unless identified as embedded quotations. Scriptural citations, reported hadith, quoted philosophers/theologians and authorial argument must not be flattened into one voice. There are no independently authenticated modern headings in this range: the WINDOW labels and SF locators are study apparatus; internal numbered arguments belong to the received text. ## Window 001 — SF-S001-L0001–L0015 (1,416 words)

L0001–L0003 supply the title, invocation and question whether prophetic miracles are psychic powers. L0004 begins a severe doctrinal and juridical answer: the claim is declared false and unbelief, followed by instruction, establishment of proof, repentance and the stated consequence of persistent public avowal. This is a report of the historical author's polemical/legal position, not an instruction or endorsement. L0005–L0008 situate the target among philosophers, Qarmatians, Ismāʿīlīs and esoteric readers, connect Ibn Sīnā's family with the movement, and describe graded allegorical treatment of ritual and eschatology. These group descriptions are the author's claims, not independently established social history. The account also extends to certain Sufi and kalām writers and the alleged continuing or acquired availability of prophecy.

L0009–L0010 reconstruct three claimed prophetic qualifications: rapid intellectual insight (including unassisted discovery of a middle term), strong imaginative/internal-sensory representation of what is known, and psychic power acting on the world's matter. The second is said to turn angels and speech into internal images and sounds, and theological/eschatological reports into exemplary imaginings. The third is compared with the evil eye. The underlying target is said to deny the Creator's discretionary power to change the world and to limit or deny detailed divine knowledge.

L0011 introduces a precise bearer/universal distinction: every external existent is particular; universals are universal in minds rather than external individuals; the Creator himself is a particular necessary existent. A knowledge confined to universals would therefore fail to know himself, emanated intellects and souls, or the spheres. This is an argument against the attributed epistemology, not a claim that no shared concept or genuine resemblance is possible; L0153–L0155 later qualify that point.

L0011–L0013 turn to a necessarily productive eternal complete cause, emanative intellects and souls, and the observed occurrence of changing phenomena. The intellectual hierarchy and active intellect are opponent positions being described. The author contrasts them with creation by will and power. A complete eternal cause would have its effect with it; the opponent's own reason for eternity appears to leave temporal events unexplained. L0013–L0015 distinguish indefinite succession of events (three reported positions, including permission in both directions) from a regress of efficient producers or the completion of efficient production. Precedence-circles are rejected, while simultaneous correlation is accepted, exemplified by fatherhood/sonship and inseparable divine attributes. This distinction is essential: the passage is not a blanket rejection of every infinite series or every mutual implication.

## Window 002 — SF-S001-L0016–L0025 (1,520 words)

L0016 separates the assertion that the entire genus of events requires a prior event from each determinate event requiring an earlier event. The former, construed as putting the genus before itself, is circular; the latter is the disputed temporal succession. Eternal willing speech is offered as an orthodox model. L0016–L0020 deny that revealed books or early Muslim authorities teach an eternally idle deity who only later begins all activity. At the same time, everything other than God, including spheres, angels, intellects and souls, is said to be created, preceded by its own non-existence, with nothing equally eternal alongside him.

L0017–L0020 quote or discuss the ʿImrān b. Ḥuṣayn report and two Muslim reports. Three Bukhārī wordings, 'nothing before him', 'with him' and 'other than him', are distinguished; the author argues for one utterance and paraphrase in the other transmissions. His contextual reading takes the Yemeni question to concern the beginning of this six-day world, with throne, water and prior determination already mentioned. This is an intra-textual hadith argument, not a collation of those collections performed here.

L0020–L0025 repeatedly test the eternal complete cause against present events. If all completion was eternal, no effect could be delayed; if some requisite or completion appears later, its occurrence needs explanation. A second producer merely reproduces the difficulty unless the eternal-completeness thesis is relinquished. The suggested escape through successive preparations, sphere motion, new desires or conceptions does not help on the stated premises: those too are events. L0024–L0025 begin the further inference that an eternal item inseparable from events cannot be produced by an eternally complete cause while its inseparable events remain unproduced. This remains under argument into window 003.

## Window 003 — SF-S001-L0026–L0040 (1,432 words)

L0026 completes the previous reductio and introduces regress among efficient sources. L0027–L0030 criticise conflation of the finitude of producers with the finitude of successive effects, naming al-Ghazālī, al-Rāzī, al-Āmidī and al-Urmawī in descriptions of their argumentative conduct. These historical and bibliographical claims are not checked against their works here.

L0030–L0034 confront a composition objection: if every member is contingent, must the aggregate be contingent, especially when each event is originated but the succession allegedly has no beginning? The answer does not accept an unrestricted transfer of predicates from member to whole. Aggregation can generate a new property, as with human parts, a house, a city, ten units, length or duration; it cannot itself turn things lacking self-sufficient existence into a self-sufficient source. More dependent producers increase rather than remove the need for something existent in itself. The conclusion at L0034 is a producer existent in itself outside all contingents/events. In contrast, duration of a succession need not be the duration of any individual. The contrast is part of the proof, not a dispensable analogy.

L0035–L0038 reject replacing a regress of producers with a regress of causal completions. The distinction would help if an independent source supplied the conditions; it fails under the hypothesis that the one stipulated eternal complete cause alone accounts for all events. L0039–L0040 set up an exact argumentative exchange: the author introduces al-Rāzī's al-Arbaʿīn, then quotes the eternalist dilemma about whether all requirements for producing are eternally present. The quotation continues in window 004.

## Window 004 — SF-S001-L0041–L0056 (1,498 words)

L0041–L0045 continue the attributed al-Rāzī quotation: the dilemma is followed by a catalogue of kalām replies (will, knowledge, hidden wisdom in timing, incompatibility of origination with eternity, changed possibility, or unmotivated selection) and al-Rāzī's objection that an eternal sufficient productive chain would exclude observable change. He reports an escape by receptivity, celestial motions and configurations, then asks what produces a determinate new accident.

L0046–L0048 are the attributed al-Urmawī objection and proposed 'brilliant' answer. He distinguishes a complete cause from an efficient cause with newly occurring conditions and proposes an eternal intellect or soul generating successive conceptions until one conditions the corporeal world's emanation. L0049 marks the author's return with 'قلت'. He identifies the answer with al-Rāzī's al-Maṭālib al-ʿāliya and challenges both its use of beginningless succession after kalām had excluded it and its psychology: philosophical souls require bodies, while separate intellects supposedly have no changing states.

L0050–L0054 separate the admissibility of endless succession, which the author says does not contradict Islam's foundations, from accepting eternal created intellects or souls. The latter is rejected. Successive acts in the necessary existent are presented as more compatible with prophetic religion; numerous school attributions and claims of intuitive impossibility of non-indicable possible entities are reported rather than independently endorsed by these notes.

L0055 is an especially important scope limiter. A fixed finite source may produce differently as an independently supplied recipient becomes ready: fire, sunlight and the active-intellect account are examples. Such a reply does not work for the Creator of everything other than himself, because he also produces the receptivities and conditions. The argument is conditional on that common comprehensive authorship. It must not be recast as a universal claim that every finite original/source independently produces every condition of every effect. L0056 concludes that successive divine production fits the permitted sequence while defeating an eternal determinate cosmos.

## Window 005 — SF-S001-L0057–L0067 (1,534 words)

L0057–L0059 expand the independence question. An external necessary co-producer would either duplicate complete production or entail interdependent power; an external possible producer would itself be a product whose production needs explanation. The author argues that observed motions cannot arise from a changeless eternal complete cause and that successive acts in the first agent remove the alleged inference to a beginningless determinate world.

L0060–L0063 revisit al-Urmawī's distinction between agent and total cause. What completes a particular event's cause must occur with that event, on the disputed framework. An earlier event cannot simply be the currently missing completion. The walking example at L0062 is central: the second stretch follows the first, but its present complete cause includes newly actual complete power and decisive will. The author reports Abū al-Barakāt as affirming successive divine volitions and criticising an allegedly reverent abstraction that would abolish efficacious agency.

L0064–L0067 explicitly compare causal models rather than presuming all thinkers share one. Most are said to require a sufficient determining cause, with the preferred version being the willing powerful agent whose will and ability entail its intended event. Philosophical self-necessitation by bare essence is distinguished from this. Other thinkers allow a choosing agent to select without an additional determinant, or distinguish necessary from voluntary causation. The al-Rāzī objection is said to work across these specifications: an originated producer series cannot account for itself. The simultaneity of agent and actual production is stressed; a temporal lineage of originated producers would not eliminate the need for an unoriginated producer either. This must be read with the permission of temporal effects, not as an unnoticed retraction of it.

## Window 006 — SF-S001-L0068–L0077 (1,455 words)

L0068–L0069 state the result narrowly: events must issue from an unoriginated agent, and he cannot have been their eternally complete cause. This objection alone does not disprove a beginningless succession of events. The author explicitly distinguishes forcing an opponent's concession from resolving the underlying puzzle. L0069–L0072 reconstruct the argument that an utterly inactive being cannot begin acting without a new determinant, while condemning unmotivated selection. The difficulty depends on hypothesising total inactivity, not merely a new particular act.

L0073–L0074 distinguish three senses of 'effecting', 'acting' or 'originating': creating everything other than God; producing a determinate item; and being an agent of something in general. Eternal total production is refuted by temporal observation; production of this item is new with the item; uninterrupted agency in general does not entail an eternal determinate effect. This disambiguation is one of the range's most portable conceptual results, but should be kept indexed to its distinct scopes.

L0075–L0077 apply the distinction to successive intentions/conceptions and to the dispute about regress. The author says the philosophical argument either illicitly invokes a ban on a kind of succession its proponents allow, or establishes only the impossibility of beginning agency absolutely without a cause. 'He cannot act until he acts' is a vicious self-precondition; 'he does this only after another act' need not be. A beginningless succession can therefore coexist with each individual effect's origination. L0077 begins a comparative criticism: the attributed eternalist model leaves events without a producer, a worse consequence than its kalām rival's produced events without a new cause.

## Window 007 — SF-S001-L0078–L0092 (1,422 words)

L0078–L0084 conclude that the philosophers' best argument establishes at most uninterrupted agency, not an eternal sphere, matter, intellect, soul or any named product. The author tests a bare essence or a composite of eternal co-causes and says either would imply all effects' eternity if fully sufficient. Dependence on another for essential activity would undercut self-sufficiency. At L0082, however, dependence of a later act on the same agent's prior act is expressly exempted from dependence on an external other. The alternative creation-after-inactivity model is also compared favourably to the target model on the question of partnership, without thereby becoming the author's own preferred position.

L0085–L0087 quote al-Rāzī's concluding assessment of the perfection/deficiency and necessity/possibility premises. Al-Rāzī's reported ranking of necessity/possibility as pre-eminent is then reversed by the author: perfection/deficiency is more noble. The quoted 'great battle' is between inferring a lasting effect from its source and inferring a new production from the nature of an effect.

L0087–L0092 again disambiguate continuous productive efficacy: efficacy concerning a particular object, concerning everything, or in general. The author's preferred reconciliation preserves permanent perfection and action while allowing all individual acts/products to occur successively. If some causation can be new, its newness does not itself require an eternal determinate product elsewhere; if all effects were eternally entailed, there would be no observable change. The claim that being a product entails prior non-existence is stated strongly, while discussion continues to distinguish this thesis from the weaker failure of an opponent's argument.

## Window 008 — SF-S001-L0093–L0101 (1,445 words)

L0093–L0094 apply the same type/individual distinction to kalām: the prior non-existence of each act/product does not establish prior non-existence of action as a kind. The author extends this to speech, lifelong divine perfections and the claim that ability would otherwise begin after inability. 'Azal' is described as absence of a first beginning, not one particular time; likewise everlasting futurity is absence of a last end. These are the author's metaphysical and lexical explanations.

L0095–L0100 give an extended quotation attributed to a work of al-Rāzī on talismans, astral invocation and magic. Its own disclaimer of contravening religion is reported. The quoted account defines talismanic practice by mixing celestial active and terrestrial receptive powers, argues through complete causation and beginningless successions, and illustrates a continuing source with a descending clod and a person walking to a friend. It then derives everlasting circular motion, a sphere and differentiated stars as nearby causes of differing sublunary events, ending in a report about worship of stars, temples and images. These are adversarially presented causal/cosmological theses, not the answering author's proposed practices. The received sentence at L0097 is visibly awkward and must not bear exact formal inference before print control.

The 'قلت' in L0100 resumes the author's voice. L0100–L0101 reply that a cause complete for every effect eternally would abolish observed newness. A cause of event after event is not eternally complete for each of those events. He turns the quotation's demand that the complete cause be present with its effect against its placement of successive conditions exclusively in the past. This crosses into window 009.

## Window 009 — SF-S001-L0102–L0113 (1,586 words)

L0102–L0104 insist that all present requirements for producing an event must be present with it. Merely past conditions do not make the unchanged eternal agent a presently complete cause. Under comprehensive divine authorship, the cause of his newly complete efficacy cannot be an independent gift from something else: the prior acts conditioning later products are his acts. Difference and temporal succession cannot arise from a wholly simple complete unchanging cause with no attributes or acts, the author argues.

L0105–L0110 develop a proof aimed beyond defeating the opponent's proof: whether an alleged eternal creature is event-free or inseparable from events, it cannot be the product of an eternally complete necessitating source. The text considers whether an agent must produce concurrently, must produce later, or could do either, and appeals to sufficient determination to remove the third escape for an eternal product. A succession cannot itself be eternally co-present merely because its kind lacks a first member: it exists successively. If a bearer requires events, producing the bearer without them is incoherent, while producing them under eternal complete causation is also said to be impossible.

L0111–L0113 anticipate the limiting objection that the spheres might have a complete eternal cause while their motions have another mode. Since these spheres are stipulated to require motion, the cause cannot yield them without yielding requisite motion. No particular member of an endless sequence is selected as itself eternal. This is a carefully conditional inseparability argument, not an unrestricted transfer from 'source of X' to 'source of every fact related to X'. The author names it a proof of origination of all other-than-God and begins applying it to rival accounts of will; the latter discussion continues next window.

## Window 010 — SF-S001-L0114–L0127 (1,412 words)

L0114–L0117 complete the comparison of will and effect: a will eternally entailing an eternal object inherits the target problem; a will whose product must be later yields origination. The author represents most theorists as holding that when productive conditions are complete the event is necessary-through-another, and when absent it is impossible-through-another. He then offers dilemma-based rebuttals: whether a new event without a new cause is possible or impossible, and whether endless succession is possible or impossible, the claimed necessity of an eternal determinate world fails. The preferred account preserves both the newness of every creature and unceasing divine perfections/acts.

L0118–L0123 revisit present causal completion in numbered proofs. L0119 raises the objection that the absence of the previous event could be the contemporaneous requisite. L0120 answers that bare absence is not causally productive unless it entails something positive; walking instead includes renewed power and will. The passage allows a difference between pure non-being and removal of an incompatibility as implicated in positive complete capacity. This is a qualification to any simplistic reading that all negative conditions are categorically unreal or explanatory nothing. L0122 contains an apparently problematic 'before' relation that needs exact print control.

L0124–L0127 turn from later Avicennian arguments to Aristotle as represented by the author. A desired object explaining celestial striving would be a final cause, not yet an efficient producer of the motion. Dependence on a final cause would also conflict with the sphere's being self-sufficiently necessary. Ibn Sīnā is said to combine the philosophers' route with the kalām distinction between originated and unoriginated, using body/composition/dependence to deny self-necessity. The move opens the extended critique of composition and attribute-denial.

## Window 011 — SF-S001-L0128–L0137 (1,438 words)

L0128 distinguishes composition by an assembler, aggregation after separation and separability from necessary perfect attributes. 'Other', 'part' and 'need' are said to be ambiguous: an inseparable attribute can be non-identical to the bearer without being an external independent producer. L0129–L0130 describe a polemical slide from denial of attributes to denial of names and then denial of both contraries, framed by a hadith about truthfulness and lying. The criticism is both logical and moral; the reported school genealogy is not independently verified.

L0130–L0131 address the reply that life/death or knowledge/ignorance are possession/privation rather than straightforward contradictories, so a subject unable to bear either might evade them. The first answer ranks inability to receive perfection below a deprived but capable subject, using an inanimate thing and a blind animal. L0132–L0133 provide the source/perfection maxim: every creaturely perfection gives the Creator a greater claim, because the created perfection is from its creative agent; the deficient does not originate the more perfect, and sheer absence is not perfection. This needs to be read with the defect-free and creature-specific restrictions at L0134 and L0158.

L0133 supports positive content in divine negations with Qur'anic sleep, fatigue, knowledge and vision citations; the vision verse is read as denying encompassing sight rather than sight itself. L0134–L0135 argue that a defect-free perfection possible for an existent cannot be merely contingent in the necessary existent, because acquisition from another would require a more perfect giver or a circular exchange. L0135–L0137 use this to argue unity of creative lordship and test two putative universal creators: complete duplicate creators, independently capable partners and mutually enabling partners. Ordinary collaboration is not thereby denied; the target is each partner's allegedly unconditioned complete creative power.

## Window 012 — SF-S001-L0138–L0159 (1,514 words)

L0138–L0141 finish the interdependence argument and reiterate that a self-prior efficient source or a circle of efficient empowerment is vicious, whereas simultaneous relation among effects is allowed and succession of effects remains disputed. L0142–L0144 give further replies to the possession/privation objection: scriptural usage calls idols and land dead; God can create life in every individual thing; and simply negating life/knowledge/power carries the deficit even if an opponent avoids the corresponding privative names.

L0145–L0152 criticise stopping at silence about both contradictory predicates and distinguish several sceptical positions: suspended judgement, denial of realities/knowledge, realities following beliefs, and real things held inaccessible to knowledge. These are compared with attributed esoteric and unity-of-being positions, including a verse about affirming all religious conceptions. The polemical classification is wider epistemic material; it cannot be reduced to the subsequent ontology of universals.

L0153–L0155 insist upon genuine resemblance between external individuals and a shared universal meaning in the mind, without a numerically common universal thing inhabiting them. 'Participation' in humanity means resemblance in a meaning, not shared possession of Zayd's numerically particular humanity. This is neither reified universals nor sheer verbal nominalism. Creator and creature cannot share one externally existing particular attribute; even two creatures cannot. Yet agreement in some names/meanings is unavoidable and does not establish parity.

L0156–L0159 contrast an attributed all-or-nothing likeness theory with likeness in one respect and difference in another. Defects must be excluded; creature-specific features include deficiency; perfect divine attributes have no equal. Scriptural affirmation is detailed and negation denies equivalence. L0159 begins the further argument that affirming a living/knowing/powerful bearer while denying life/knowledge/power removes what the agent noun requires. The sentence continues in window 013.

## Window 013 — SF-S001-L0160–L0180 (1,477 words)

L0160 completes the agent-noun/source-noun argument. L0161–L0166 list five allegedly forbidden compositions attributed to the philosophers: existence and essence; general and specific; bearer and attributes; bodily atoms; and matter/form. L0167 announces that the classification is not genuine composition, that no proof excludes its relevant meanings even if so called, and that evidence positively supports the meanings. L0168–L0170 disambiguate composition, part, dependence and otherness by contrasting manufacture or aggregation with necessary features and with inseparability.

L0171–L0172 tell an Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal disputation narrative, reporting how a question 'is the Qur'an God or other than God?' is answered with the parallel question about divine knowledge. Oaths by divine attributes are then used to show why unqualified 'other than God' can mislead. These are the text's reports and arguments, not independently checked historical testimony. L0173–L0174 reject externally bare essences and free-standing attributes, explain how 'additional to essence' can mean additional to a thinker’s incomplete conception, and offer a linguistic explanation of dhāt as originally relational.

L0175–L0180 analyse the dependence inference. Requiring an external productive cause differs from not existing without oneself or one's constitutive/inseparable features. Self-production is impossible, but identity-conditional self-presence is trivial, and absence of an indispensable feature can imply the subject's absence without making that feature its maker. This is direct contrary evidence to collapsing every prerequisite relation into efficient-source dependence. The argument does not say that a necessary being manufactures its attributes, or that each necessary condition must be an effect of the entity requiring it.

## Window 014 — SF-S001-L0181–L0197 (1,439 words)

L0181–L0188 criticise an abstract existence stipulated as unlimited, using the opponents' own logical distinctions. The received exposition separates natural, logical and rational universals and discusses the difference between an absolute considered without a condition and an absolute under the condition of unrestrictedness. The author says the latter is mental, and that what is mentally universal is external only as a determinate particular. Even granting separate Platonic universals or Aristotelian immanent ones for argument's sake, a self-subsistent necessary existent distinct from the general concept is still needed. These are not grants that either universal ontology is true.

L0189–L0191 extend the criticism to Ibn Sabʿīn and al-Qūnawī as represented here: a 'no-condition' universal would include contingent existence and would not itself identify the Creator. The argument requires a particular reality with proper attributes and determination, not an abstract unqualified existence. L0192–L0193 contrast detailed prophetic affirmation with detailed philosophical negation and revisit ambiguity in composition.

L0193–L0196 contain substantial natural-philosophical material. Atomistic and matter/form accounts of bodies are rejected. Three divisibility positions are distinguished; the author prefers a finite limit without indivisible atoms. A body can become too small to persist with its current characteristic and transform or join another, while still allowing distinguishable aspects. Very small water droplets are his illustration. This is a historically situated physical account, not modern scientific verification. L0197 uses uncertainty or error in analysing visible bodies to caution against confident composition arguments about unseen realities, then opens the question of existence as a predicate of a specific reality.

## Window 015 — SF-S001-L0198–L0215 (1,523 words)

L0198–L0201 distinguish linguistic 'finding' from the technical affirmation that something really exists. External existence is not an added entity beside the external reality. A mentally conceived triangle can differ from the actual external triangle; that mental/external difference must not be converted into externally fixed nonexistent essences bearing a later existence. Both 'existence is essence' and 'existence differs from essence' can express a true claim under different locations of reference. The attributed Avicennian division is criticised for both extra external essences and turning the necessary existent into abstract existence.

L0202–L0203 provide a second important distinction: receptive/substrate causality is not productive/efficient causality. An attribute's requiring a bearer is not evidence that the necessary bearer was made by an external producer. The author's preferred ontology is separated from a dialectical defence of a less correct opponent against a worse inference. Do not quote the conditional defence as his unconditional endorsement that external existence is an added attribute.

L0204–L0212 examine general/specific 'composition'. Common signification can be graded or univocal, but a shared concept and differentiating predicates do not supply externally independent parts. Human and equine examples test whether animality and rationality are accidents, subsistent substances, or mental distinctions. One human is not several substances corresponding to each predicate, and predicates dependent on a substance cannot simply be its independently composing stuff. A mental construction is admitted as mental; its projection into reality is challenged.

L0213–L0215 relate this to the Aristotelian categories and five named kinds of substance. Intellects, souls, matter and form as philosophically construed are charged with being mental posits; bodies and accidents are acknowledged as externally real. The author returns to bearer/attribute composition: there is no pre-existing bare subject to which life, knowledge and power are subsequently attached. The two negative stages of the announced critique are summarised before the positive stage in window 016.

## Window 016 — SF-S001-L0216–L0236 (1,537 words)

L0216–L0219 argue positively for distinct meanings in a single necessary existent. The philosophers themselves use multiple descriptions such as intellect/intellection/intelligible and lover/love/beloved. The author rejects identifying all properties with each other or with the self-subsisting bearer. A polemical comparison with Christian Trinitarian discourse follows, including an attributed argument by a Christian philosopher whose received name needs scan verification. These comparisons must remain attributed, not adopted as a neutral account of Christian doctrine.

L0220 explicitly reconnects the long discussion to the opening question: psychic reduction of miracles rests on the eternal-world/complete-cause doctrine, assisted by denying attributes under the label of composition. Once that denial fails, it cannot independently forbid voluntary acts in God. L0221–L0225 map two kalām premises concerning subjects of events and beginningless succession, then name groups said to dispute either or both. Divine perfection is held continuous while every other particular is originated. The range's many historical school attributions should be separately controlled if used historically.

L0226–L0227 restate contemporaneous causal sufficiency for a complete cause and for a powerful voluntary agent: all requisites, including present power and will, must accompany production. L0228–L0236 present seven cumulative rebuttals of the eternal-world argument. They alternate internal critique and the author's own model: either endless succession is impossible, or it can precede these spheres; either effects may be delayed or the target cause cannot explain events; an indeterminate simple existence cannot distinguish world-specific possibilities; admitting events in an eternal subject permits successive divine action; opponents themselves accept the succession and uncompleted causality their proof forbids; their alleged events without a producer are worse than a rival's events without a new cause; and arguments defeating total inactivity do not establish an eternal particular. The distinction between defeating the inference and positively demonstrating the alternative remains necessary throughout.

## Window 017 — SF-S001-L0237–L0238 (267 words)

L0237–L0238 finally return directly to prophetic signs. The author explains that these metaphysical preliminaries underlie denial of heaven's rupture and reduction of prophecy to powers of souls. The opponents may honour prophets, defend their laws and recognise some of their true excellences, yet are charged with excluding the rest. The concession is explicit: prophetic special cognition, truthful waking or dream representations and effects produced by God-given powers of souls are not denied. Their alleged exhaustiveness is denied.

L0238 then states that the early authorities, jurists and majority recognise powers and natures in things through which effects occur, giving fire's heating and water's cooling as examples. A kalām view that God creates effects 'at' these things rather than 'through' them is reported as the contrasting position. This is crucial limiting evidence against reading the opening as rejection of all secondary causal powers. The window ends mid-sentence, 'ويقولون ليس في العين'. No continuation has been read in this assignment. Reconciliation with block02 must establish the next examples, qualifications and development before extending the conclusion.

## Receipt and conventions

I read every supplied window, 001–017, in order, displaying the full Arabic text. Every output was complete and every `END WINDOW` marker was visible. The assignment is SF-S001-L0239–SF-S002-L0135: 298 electronic lines and 23,811 whitespace-delimited words according to the supplied manifests. These are electronic locators, not verified print-page or manuscript references. The transition from S001 to S002 is a received electronic segmentation; I do not claim a diplomatic volume or page equivalence.

The notes below preserve sequential argument as well as material not directly relevant to the proposed philosophical fronts. “The author argues” refers to Ibn Taymiyya's voice in this supplied text. Reports about philosophers, earlier authorities, particular incidents and consensus remain his reports unless expressly marked as quotations. Qur'anic wording, hadith, alleged quotations of opponents and verse are distinguished from his own assertions. - **L0079–L0091: sufficiency of this prophetic dispensation.** The author allows, for argument, different arrangements for earlier peoples but insists that Muḥammad's message suffices for his community. Earlier law counts as binding through his authorisation. Reports from the People of the Book not known true or false should be neither affirmed nor denied, with general belief in genuine revelations retained. This evidential suspension is narrower and more careful than wholesale acceptance or rejection of inherited reports. Preservation of needed revelation is argued to include the Qur'an and Sunna.
- **L0092–L0095: other wisdom traditions.** Claims to need Israelite lore, Persian, Roman, Indian or Greek wisdom, or untested taste and opinion, are criticised within the argument about the completeness and normative authority of prophetic guidance. Earlier affirmations of sense-grounded reasoning remain part of the same range. It would overreach to recast these lines alone as an unqualified ban on every external empirical or practical learning.
- **L0096–L0099: legal-polemical consequences.** The author reports severe legal consequences for insulting prophets or elevating saints to their status, while distinguishing an ordinary saint from a prophet. These are the treatise's historical juridical assertions, recorded for completeness; this study neither endorses them nor provides present-day legal advice.
- **L0100–L0103: the Creator's distinction from the world.** Pharaoh is said knowingly to deny what the mystical opponents fail to distinguish clearly. Their wavering among identity, inherence and contradictory formulations is attributed to refusing a Maker distinct from the world. The author criticises the distinction between a thing's established entity and its existence as deployed in Ibn ʿArabī's account. The argument leads into a taxonomy of positions, not into a new proof already completed here.

## Window 001: SF-S002-L0136–SF-S002-L0158

The range begins with “وهذه الطريقة”, an unresolved backward reference. L0136–0138 distinguish two criticisms of a kalām method: its unprecedented, dangerous, lengthy character, and the stronger charge that its premises are false and defeat the intended result. The author associates it with denying attributes, divine vision and divine elevation, and the created-Qur'an thesis. The preceding range is necessary to establish precisely which formulation “this method” resumes.

L0139–0141 report philosophers exploiting their opponents' denial of beginningless occurrences: an eternally inactive maker allegedly begins without any new reason. Scriptural creation in six days is discussed in their reported reply, with pre-existing material and the smoke verse. The ensuing polemic contrasts allegorical reinterpretation with the theory that prophets communicate useful imaginative representations rather than literal truth. These are attributed opponent positions, not authorial endorsements of deception.

L0142–0145 criticise transferring the status of each individual occurrence to an entire succession. A particular occurrence cannot be eternal; neither can all occurrences be collectively eternal at once. This does not, the author argues, establish the impossibility of occurrences successively without a first. Aggregates sometimes retain their members' predicates, while lines, bodies, houses and numbers acquire different aggregate predicates. L0146–0148 diagnose defenders' false concessions and inadequate knowledge as enabling opponents, but do not vindicate the opponents' ultimate doctrine.

L0149–0153 turn against a featureless simple source and “one produces only one”. A merely abstract existence cannot act externally; ordinary worldly effects are said to involve two causes, and even a supposed unitary worldly cause has attributes and action. The emergence of multiplicity remains unexplained by interposing a first effect with several respects. L0154–0158 attack an eternally complete cause whose effects must accompany it: temporal occurrences would either vanish or lack a producer. Successive action supplies a different model, but it is not eternal necessitation of a determinate act or product. Type, token, succession and simultaneous totality must remain separate.

## Window 002: SF-S002-L0159–SF-S002-L0183

L0159–0164 present alternative arguments regardless of whether beginningless occurrences are possible. If impossible, the world must originate; if possible, this particular six-day world may be preceded by other occurrences. The complete producer of a new event is complete only with that event. Regress in becoming an effective producer must terminate in one whose efficacy does not depend on another; this does not prohibit his successively producing different events. “Eternity” is explicitly not a bounded time, but beginningless past duration (L0164).

L0165–0175 return to polemics about Ibn ʿArabī, Ibn Sabʿīn and Ibn Hūd, claiming spiritual pretensions above prophetic ranks and connections between universal existence, philosophical theology and attribute-negation. These biographical allegations and classifications are evidence of the author's polemical construction, not independently established biography. L0168 even acknowledges their extensive praise of the Prophet and offers several explanations rather than a single certain motive. L0171–0175 describe strategic alliances and the author's encounters.

The epistemic turn at L0176 is integral: knowledge requires sound premises and answers to objections, expressed as an operative ground with the obstacle removed. L0177–0180 criticise some self-identified defenders of tradition for treating fabricated or unusably weak reports like accepted sound reports. Collection of material is distinguished from reliance: some texts establish a case, some support it, and some are included because corruption is not known. Understanding follows authentication.

L0181–0183 attack equating unknowable interpretation with wholly unintelligible revealed meaning. The author explicitly says he knows no Muslim school affirming genuinely meaningless divine speech, resists attributing it to the Karrāmiyya, and then separates the falsity of such a thesis from the adequacy of al-Rāzī's purported refutation. This is important contrary evidence to treating every polemical attribution as unqualified: here the author demands attributional evidence and consistency in criticism.

## Window 003: SF-S002-L0184–SF-S002-L0210

L0184–0193 distinguish three uses of taʾwīl: the ultimate reality to which an utterance refers, explanation of intended meaning, and the later technical sense of diverting an expression from its more apparent to its less apparent sense. Knowing what names mean need not entail knowing the reality's manner, illustrated through paradisal substances. The author reconciles apparently conflicting early positions on who knows taʾwīl by disambiguation. Denying human knowledge of a divine manner is differentiated from denying there is any manner or reality at all. L0187 also concerns inaccessible reasons for acts; it should not be made an unrestricted denial of every possible knowledge of wisdom.

L0193–0197 distinguish false interpretation, interpretation whose truth is unknown, translation, and withholding material inappropriate to an audience. Sayings attributed to ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd concern audience capacity. A cited report about hidden knowledge is expressly labelled unestablished (L0196), although later writers used it. The author rejects deploying such sayings to justify esoteric negation.

L0198–0205 extend criticism to proponents of tradition who neglect sound rational arguments or rely on weak transmitted evidence, and to rationalists who exclude scriptural evidence. Clear rational truths and sound reports from the infallible source are asserted not to contradict; actual disputants can misidentify either. Prayer, guidance and intellectual work figure together in resolving perplexity.

L0206–0208 reject the view that Qur'anic proof consists only in authoritative testimony after a messenger's veracity is known. The Qur'an also directs hearers to rationally recognisable arguments: evidence can be simultaneously scriptural and rational. This range thus contains substantial epistemic-route material, not only metaphysical sourcing.

L0209–0210 begin the exchange with a proponent of absolute “encompassing” existence. The author's objection uses their own logical principle that unrestricted universals are not externally instantiated as unrestricted universals. The attempted exception for existence is rejected. An actual human must be existent, determinate and one or several. The subsequent window distinguishes the several senses of “absolute”.

## Window 006: SF-S002-L0254–SF-S002-L0284

L0254 identifies common names, including so-called graded names, as belonging to broad agreement in predication. L0255–0256 quote the opponent's own devotional introduction. Its affirmations of generosity, mercy, majesty and power are used at L0257–0266 against total removal of attributes: scriptural usage also assigns corresponding names to creatures, so either the opponent contradicts himself or his non-identity answer is available to others. L0285–0288 complete the co-creator argument. Reciprocal production of each maker's original capacity would be a vicious prior causal circle. Cooperation among finite agents differs: their powers already exist, combine, or are supplied by a third. The author recognises permissible concurrent relation or cooperation while rejecting a circle furnishing original causal capacity. This is narrower than denying every dependence between agents in every respect.

L0289–0297 stress inequality between divine encompassing knowledge and limited creaturely knowledge, with scriptural corroboration. L0298–0304 contrast partial resemblance with a reported all-or-nothing account of resemblance accepted by several named theologians. The author rejects that account and criticises selectively permitting some attributes while excluding others merely by “body” terminology. Some transmitted forms, especially “كون النفي مشابها” at L0299, call for scan control.

L0305 affirms the necessary/contingent and creator/created distinction. It also limits historical attribution: a wholly originated reality without any originator is not known to him as a sect's position, but can be mentally posited for refutation. L0306–0311 classify negative and absolute-existence positions. In particular, Ibn Sīnā's route is said to establish merely a necessary existent, without establishing its distinctness from the world by the proposed route. The author distinguishes that route from Aristotle's alleged motion-and-desire route.

## Window 008: SF-S002-L0318–SF-S002-L0341

L0318 is an important scope statement. The claim concerns the world as a whole allegedly caused by an eternally complete cause; it distinguishes other hypothetical arrangements involving successive causation and a persisting other item. L0319–0322 maintain voluntary and successive activity without an eternally determinate act or product. Intransitive and transitive acts are distinguished: a persistent type of intransitive act would not by itself entail a created product, whereas a transitive act entails one. This is not an unqualified inference from all divine activity to a creature.

L0323–0329 recast the proof through necessity, independence, self-subsistence, completeness and creatorhood. The contingency of observed occurrences is evident from their coming to be; the contingency of something known only as existent would need another argument (L0323). Poverty is treated as a defect and absolute independence as a perfection. These routes are alternatives serving a shared conclusion, not separate beings identified by unrelated universal concepts.

L0330–0337 give the strongest source/perfection material in this range. The proposed perfection must be possible for existence and free of defect; created life, knowledge, power, hearing, sight and speech supply examples. It would be unfitting for the inferior to possess such perfection while the superior could not. Every perfection in created possibles comes from the Creator (L0332), so the giver of perfection is more entitled to it. Its actuality cannot be borrowed from effects or another independent necessary being. A complete intrinsic ground entails the attribute permanently.

L0337 immediately restricts the use of intrinsic necessitation: it correctly establishes necessary attributes, not eternally necessitated creatures. L0338–0341 challenge arguments from “composition”. Requiring one's own constitutive attributes is not the same as having an efficient maker. Even a supposed whole whose existence requires its parts is not thereby demonstrated to need an external producer. This distinction is central contrary evidence against unrestricted every-requisite SourceMode claims.

## Window 009: SF-S002-L0342–SF-S002-L0379

L0342–0347 extend the terminological warning: “necessary through itself” can mean no efficient cause, or be inflated into having no attribute, concomitant or receptive subject. The proof needs the first, not all the others. Attributes depend on their bearer in one respect without being products of a separate efficient cause. Calling the essence a “cause” of its attributes can be valid if it means necessitation; it must not be confused with the alleged efficient cause of the world.

L0348–0362 revisit temporal regress arguments. The author reports objections to beginningless inactivity followed by ungrounded activity, then argues that past/future distinctions are relative and fail to support the proposed impossibility. The application or matching argument involving events since the Flood and since the Hijra is criticised; beginningless or endless series can still have comparative increase. These passages are historical arguments about successive occurrences, not modern set-theoretic proofs. The dirham illustration distinguishes “I have never given one without an earlier one” from postponing every prospective gift until another prospective gift is supplied. Four orderings are separated at L0362.

L0363–0366 criticise basing transcendence on undefined body-negation, treating both affirmation and negation of that word as innovations. The discussion of Jewish claims uses Qur'anic criticism as a model; the statement concerning the Torah's attributes is the author's exegetical-polemical claim and has not been checked against another textual tradition.

L0367–0379 present a staged inter-school exchange. Each level of negators can press the next level's objection against its own retained predicates. Conversely, predicates such as living or knowing require the relevant attribute, while collapsing knower, knowledge and other qualities confuses distinct realities. Selectively rejecting anger and approval by creature-specific physiology is challenged through an analogous objection to will. The author is not importing the creature's blood, need or appetite into God.

L0380–0382 answer an epistemic objection: absence of a particular proof does not establish absence of its object. Rational routes may be available for the disputed attributes, including effects, ordering, specification and reward/punishment, as well as defect-free perfection. Grief and weeping are excluded as deficient, illustrating a substantive filter rather than indiscriminate transfer from creatures.

L0383–0390 connect necessary existence with non-equality. Common terminology does not make divine and created instances equal; an unrestricted mental common meaning is distinguished from each concrete instance. L0389 excludes creature-specific characteristics even when they count as perfections for creatures. L0391–0395 discuss the epistemic sequence for speech and messenger-attestation. L0396–0406 explain how the body/occurrence argument generated disputes about divine speech. A newly possible act requires a relevant change; otherwise the two conditions are identical and their different outcome lacks a differentiator. The theological maxim about what God wills occurs at L0413–0414 within this analysis. It does not identify all prerequisites as efficient sources.

## Window 011: SF-S002-L0418–SF-S002-L0441

This window is a sustained development rather than a new topic. L0418–0423 distinguish eternal independent agency from an eternally complete determinate act. Even if occurrences have no first, each comes successively; no time contains all originated things together. God is described as always acting without depending on something distinct from him, but no single act or product accompanies him eternally. “Ancient” and “eternal” are therefore explicitly equivocal between a particular enduring item and an unceasing succession of non-eternal particulars.

L0424–0433 consider an alleged everlasting determinate product bound to temporal occurrences. If it is grounded in a complete eternal determinate act, its necessary concomitants cannot be delayed; yet the occurrences are not eternal. Replacing “all at once” with “one after another” changes the asserted act into successive activity, conceding the author's distinction. The argument depends on the scope and meaning of complete efficacy, necessary concomitance and the world-member's non-separation from events. It is not rendered a formally verified argument by the text's repeated descriptions of contradiction.

L0434–0439 recast the same incompatibility through one-way and two-way concomitance. The author says one-way implication from a worldly part to an accompanying event suffices, and then offers an alternative bilateral formulation. Several received phrases are grammatically awkward or apparently damaged, notably L0432 and L0436; they need scan control before quoting as precise formal premises. The decisive distinction remains “فعل الحوادث في الأزل” versus unceasingly producing events, L0434.

## Window 012: SF-S002-L0442–SF-S002-L0473

L0442–0452 derive successive agency from perfection. One who can produce new acts and effects is more perfect than a source incapable of origination. A permanently inseparable item without origination is assimilated to necessary attributes rather than voluntary products. The philosophers' appeal to everlasting generosity is redirected: repeated voluntary beneficence is the relevant perfection, not one coeternal world. L0452 even ranks a finite beginning of many continuing acts above the opponent's one everlasting unchanging product; this is a comparative rebuttal within a larger preferred account, not adoption of initial inactivity.

L0453–0458 revisit the historical genealogy of created divine speech from the body/occurrence proof. The author distinguishes what many proponents intended, defending revelation, from what he says their premises entail. An allegation about the first propagator is introduced as something “some scholars say” (L0458), not direct evidence established here.

L0459–0465 report one response to “uncreated”: an eternal single meaning, independent of voluntary choice, expressed as Torah, Gospel or Qur'an. The author reports the majority's objection that different contents and speech acts cannot be one reality; translation does not turn the Torah into the Qur'an. Attempts to defend multiple attributes by consensus while collapsing speech meanings are described as unstable.

L0466–0473 report another response: eternal determinate letters and possibly sounds, concurrent rather than successive. Scriptural temporal constructions, including “when”, “then” and prospective speech, are used against it. Sound is compared with motion, whose beginning and end cannot occur simultaneously. ## Window 013: SF-S002-L0474–SF-S002-L0507

L0474–0475 reject identifying the presently heard human sound with an eternal divine sound. L0476–0481 report a third type: God speaks voluntarily through speech subsisting in him, yet did not always speak, because these thinkers disallow beginningless occurrences. The author marks one attribution with “أظن”, a reminder not to erase his uncertainty. His count of four and then five groups depends on subdivisions in the preceding account, and should not be flattened into a definitive later taxonomy.

L0482–0489 turn to philosophers who locate divine speech in prophetic souls while admitting eternal celestial effects. Their supposed objections from bodily substrate, occurrence or composition are challenged by their own cosmology. “Composition” must be disambiguated: external assembly, prior dispersion and separability do not follow from a plurality of necessary attributes. Depending on one's attributes is again separated from needing an external producer.

L0490–0491 state the author's positive portrayal of the predecessors: continuous entitlement to perfection, voluntary speech and action, and inability of creatures to exhaust divine praise. L0492–0502 read the five negations in the Throne Verse as implying positive perfections: unique divinity and lordship, life and sustaining activity, power and self-sufficiency, teaching and guidance, and effortless preservation. Intercession is permitted by God rather than an independently imposed influence; human knowledge is taught by him. Denial of encompassing vision is distinguished from denial of vision. These are exegetical claims within the received work, not separate verified exegesis here.

L0503–0504 compare speech with muteness and voluntary speech with fixed unchosen utterance. L0505–0507 explain “uncreated” and “from him” as rejecting speech created in another subject. L0507 gives an essential limitation: the Lord is not described by a quality merely because he creates it in something else. Its examples are colours, tastes and motions. This prevents turning source-priority into undifferentiated property inheritance.

END WINDOW 013, L0507, inspected.

## Window 014: SF-S002-L0508–SF-S002-L0536

L0508 distinguishes attributive annexation from ownership or servanthood: knowledge and speech differ from a house, she-camel, spirit or servant. Owned things can also be specially associated with God without becoming divine qualities. L0509–0512 interpret predecessors' formulas in light of their polemical occasion, then introduce the creative command “be”. If every such command were itself a created prerequisite generated by another command, nothing would begin to be made.

L0513–0520 insist that this is not rejection of every infinity. Regress in sources of original efficacy is distinguished from successive acts and products. Endless dependent non-existent makers supply no independent producer. L0517 extends the argument to original knowledge and power: one must not make every knower or capable agent acquire its entire original capacity from another similarly dependent source. But L0520 explicitly permits this act to follow another act once an independently effective agent is admitted.

L0521 states the boundary unusually clearly: “لا يتوقف كونه خالقا على غيره أصلا” but creating this thing “قد يكون مشروطا بخلقه لغيره”. Particular action can require prior creation while original creatorhood depends on no other. L0522–0527 apply this distinction to the “be” regress. L0528–0530 distinguish an act or attribute subsisting in God from a separately created product. “Creator of everything” is not made to include the Creator himself or what enters into the designation of the Creator, his attributes and acts.

L0531–0534 contrast an eternally determinate “be” with successive voluntarily uttered commands, and apply the type/token distinction to letters. L0534 is particularly liable to misquotation: a type may be called uncreated or ancient, but a letter token subsisting in a particular person at a particular time is not thereby uncreated. L0535–0536 reject both the eternal-world/attribute-negation scheme and the opposing origination proof that removes divine activity, including alleged future cessation.

END WINDOW 014, L0536, inspected.

## Window 015: SF-S002-L0537–SF-S002-L0573

L0537–0548 challenge a model of final annihilation and recreation, and deny that scripture states the six-day heavens and earth were made from no antecedent thing. Numerous creation passages concern clay, water, fire and light, followed by bodily nourishment and respiration. The scope is creation from already created materials; this is not endorsement of matter independent of God. The claim “Creator of everything” remains in force. A received prophetic report is attributed to Abū Mūsā at L0546 and warrants external textual control rather than silent correction.

L0549–0552 discuss the earth's provisions, the smoky heaven, six days and the throne upon water. L0553–0560 compare variants in the ʿImrān b. Ḥuṣayn narration: nothing before him, nothing other than him, and nothing with him. The author argues one occasion probably had one original formulation and others were transmitted by meaning, then prefers the wording corroborated by another prayer. His method combines narrational occasion, variant comparison and agreement across reports and scripture. No hadith-critical verdict is independently issued here.

L0561–0573 discuss throne-versus-pen priority. The throne's presence at the prior decree is used to prefer its earlier creation. A supposed first-created Intellect report is expressly called fabricated, and even a hypothetical acceptance is said not to establish the philosophers' conclusion. Identifying pen with intellect and tablet with soul is criticised on ordinary Arabic, reported content, chronology and causal-role grounds. L0569–0573 limit the pen's recorded content to events of this world through resurrection, rather than all possible or actual creations. This temporal and contextual limitation is relevant to any universalised sourcing reading.

## Window 016: SF-S002-L0574–SF-S002-L0599

L0574–0576 finish the scope restriction: the reported decree does not cover all creations after resurrection, so the pen's priority need not be absolute priority over every created thing. The author adduces alleged concord among prophetic reports, predecessor reports and the beginning of the Torah concerning water before the six-day world. This remains a report of his comparison, with no independent Torah collation.

L0577–0584 move from cosmology to historical semantics. Created things are originated, but whether everything originated must be called created is contested. To identify a speaker's meaning, one must use that speaker's language and habitual usage, rather than later technical definitions. Qur'anic “old” often means relatively earlier, not strictly beginningless; “new” revelation can be new relative to earlier revelation. L0584 contains a difficult repeated wording about created/originated remembrance and should be scan-controlled before making it bear a fine doctrinal distinction.

L0585–0596 diagnose a false binary: either eternally determinate or a separately created product. That binary misreads “uncreated Qur'an” and makes evidence for voluntary temporal speech seem inconsistent with evidence for a divine attribute. The third category is something subsisting in God and occurring by his power and will, intrinsic by its bearer and active by its relation to choice. Life, detached products and voluntary subsisting speech illustrate the three categories. L0592 restates why originating all created speech cannot make all speech God's own speech, anchoring attribution in its bearer.

L0597–0599 return to the cosmological consequence of the binary: indefinite divine inactivity followed by ungrounded activity, sometimes followed by future cessation. Philosophical opponents exploit the weakness to argue for these particular everlasting heavens. The final window will accept the force of one objection without accepting that conclusion.

## Window 017: SF-S002-L0600–SF-S002-L0611

L0600 reports Ibn Sīnā's argument that an inactive essence cannot become active with no relevant alteration. The author grants the force against the specified inactive scenario but denies that it proves an eternal world, any eternal particular act, or any eternal product. He turns the same reasoning against an invariant source alleged to yield novel events.

L0601–0606 follow the attempted relocation of novelty to the world's preparations. The new preparation itself needs an account; the first effective source must have the appropriate newly complete efficacy. The author says this can be through its own successive activity, rather than another independently making it active. “Nothing but the agent and its products” and “nothing of its products except from it” (L0603) are claims concerning the actual comprehensive creator in this dispute. They are not an unrestricted thesis about every conceivable source and requisite.

L0607–0610 give a particularly important causal qualification. The philosophers' active intellect can be conditionally effective because, on their account, other sources supply the recipients' preparations. It is not a complete, independently sufficient cause. That model cannot simply be transferred to the first universal source, whose alleged outside conditions are themselves its products. The author substitutes the sun as a recognisable illustration: removing a roof or cloud can bring illumination without a new intrinsic act in the sun. Thus not every effect demands the same local intrinsic novelty in every contributing cause; the argument turns on the comprehensive source's scope.

L0611 begins a second objection: either the first source is an eternally complete cause, yielding all its effects eternally and thus not explaining temporal events, or its necessitation waits on another. Only the first branch is developed here. The assigned range stops inside this continuing argument. Reconciliation with SF-S002-L0612 onwards is necessary before stating the complete dilemma or the next conclusion.

# Continuous reading notes: al-Ṣafadiyya, assigned range 4

## Scope and reading discipline

These notes concern only the received electronic Arabic at SF-S002-L0612–SF-S003-L0367: 496 labelled lines and 23,751 whitespace-delimited words in 17 supplied windows. I read every window in sequence, displaying its full text and its END marker. The counts and hashes in the separate receipt are corroborating checks, not substitutes for reading. No scans, print pages, external sources, or whole-work reading are claimed. Electronic section/line identifiers are the sole exact locators; their diplomatic equivalence to any printed edition has not been established. The text contains apparent transcription and reference defects, retained rather than silently corrected.

The dominant voice is Ibn Taymiyya's polemical argument, with many objections and concessions introduced by “if it is said”. His reports about schools and historical actors are his claims, not independently verified history. Longer passages from Ibn Sīnā are expressly quoted in windows 13–15; the author's “قلت” resumes his commentary. Scriptural quotations and transmitted reports have separate status. Received chapter-like numbers and sūra/verse references may be editorial additions; this electronic witness does not establish their provenance.

## Window 001: SF-S002-L0612–L0642 (1,442 words)

- L0612–L0619 continues an objection begun before the assignment. A first cause whose efficacy awaits another is not already a complete cause. If the other is its own effect, explanatory priority circles; if independent, a regress or reciprocal dependence among alleged necessary beings follows. The author then turns the philosophers' own complete-cause/effect concomitance principle against their account of newly occurring events. Treating celestial motions, representations, volitions, or preceding events as conditions postpones rather than supplies their cause. Whatever completes efficacy must be present when the effect occurs; an earlier event absent at that point cannot simply count as the presently complete cause. These are arguments about completeness and dependency, not a denial that conditions exist.
- L0620–L0623 puts a serious alternative: the second act can be conditional on the first act's cessation, while the agent's own self acts successively. An endless simultaneous stack of newly required completions is rejected. The author accepts the relevance of this response to a first, self-necessary agent acting one thing after another, not an eternally complete cause of one fixed eternal effect.
- L0624–L0628 diagnoses confusion between a type of efficacy and a particular efficacy. Perpetual agency need not entail an eternal celestial sphere, act-token, or effect-token. Conversely, temporal origination of every creature need not entail prior total inactivity or incapacity in God. L0627 explicitly includes conditions and removal of impediments in particular effective action. L0628 connects this to voluntary matters subsisting in God and scripturally named love, displeasure, satisfaction, joy, and related predicates.
- L0629–L0637 presses the mismatch between an eternally invariant cause and diverse successive movements. Criticism cuts both ways: theologians allegedly construe creation as a shift from incapacity to capacity; philosophers allegedly infer an immutable eternal cosmos and deny its eschatological transformation. Observed rain, plants, humans, and other animals supply signs of an agent originating events (L0637).
- L0638–L0642 opens the human-power dispute. The author distinguishes enabling capacity presupposed by commands, shared by obedient and disobedient persons, from the complete capacity that actually entails performance. The cross-window continuation is essential.

## Window 002: SF-S002-L0643–L0680 (1,419 words)

- L0643–L0646 completes the survey of human capacity: al-Ashʿarī and followers are reported to restrict it to performance, but their jurisprudence still calls the non-performer capable of obedience. The author makes scriptural usage govern a potentially verbal dispute. He affirms that the complete complex necessitating a person's action coincides with its occurrence; this is not equivalent to saying that all enabling capacities begin only then.
- L0647–L0653 contrasts theories of divine acts. Kullābī approaches appeal to old power and will, or an allegedly privative new relation; Karrāmī approaches allow voluntary matters but posit an unexplained first arising. Muʿtazilī accounts permit preference without a determinant. The author's charge against al-Rāzī-like dialectic is inconsistency: a sufficient determinant is demanded against the Qadarites but denied against the philosophers when defending free choice.
- L0654–L0655 distinguishes two senses of “necessitating through itself”: a bare self stripped of attributes and acts, versus a self qualified by power, will, and action. The latter is compatible with choice. Complete power plus decisive will entails an act. This is a particularly important qualification: causal sufficiency is not being identified with non-voluntary mechanism.
- L0656 offers an apologetic distinction between a created chooser dependent on another and the eternal independent chooser. L0657–L0665 rejects using that distinction to exempt divine action from the general connection between complete determinant and result. L0658 explicitly calls these universal principles not susceptible to exception, whether the determinant is ancient or originated. A new relation either itself requires production, or, if sheer non-being, does not positively explain occurrence.
- L0666–L0677 analyses willing one of apparent equivalents. A particular will is not one indifferent will related equally to both alternatives. The author rejects unexplained origination of wills and attributes located nowhere or outside their subject; reported Baghdādī and Baṣran Muʿtazilī positions must not be mistaken for his own.
- L0678–L0680 returns to contemporaneous power/will and begins the practical objection that believers and unbelievers receive exactly equal divine assistance. This carries into window 3 and broadens the argument beyond cosmology.

## Window 003: SF-S002-L0681–L0711 (1,464 words)

- L0681–L0685 appeals to ordinary recognition of dependence on guidance and assistance: equal assistance to the Companions and Abū Jahl/Abū Lahab, or to the praying and non-praying, is presented as contrary to natural recognition. This is the author's theological argument about grace and agency, not an independent psychological experiment. The need for complete capacity and decisive will is reiterated.
- L0686–L0688 sharply limits omnipotence's domain: intrinsic contradictions are not possible things, while an event not willed or foreknown not to happen may nevertheless be within power conditionally. Ability to do either opposite in alternation does not entail simultaneous realisation of both. These distinctions block careless inferences from “can” to actual occurrence, or from “does not occur” to inability.
- L0689–L0693 gives a self-sufficiency argument for unity: two independently complete powers cannot independently produce the same single act. The author affiliates his solution with the early authorities and with philosophers such as Abū al-Barakāt who allow voluntary matters in God. “Necessitating through itself” is again accepted only for the self qualified by choice, producing the particular effect when produced. Qurʾān 36:82 is invoked as scriptural expression of this willing-and-doing account.
- L0694–L0700 distinguishes a sound counterexample from a full explanation of an argument's failure. Daily occurrences refute a theory entailing no occurrences, but an explanatory dissolution must diagnose its premises or equivocations. al-Urmawī's criticism and al-Hindī's transmission are discussed through the author's reports.
- L0701–L0711 examines a proposed answer attributed to al-Rāzī/al-Urmawī and connected to Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd: an eternal intellect or soul undergoes successive conceptions until one permits bodily creation. Replies appeal to the soul's relation to body, to equal applicability of anti-infinite-event reasoning to souls, and to the incompatibility between a fixed eternal necessary emanation and newly choosing to produce bodies. The author argues conditionally that if the theologians' proof works, it does not stop at bodies. He has not here endorsed every premise of that proof.

## Window 004: SF-S002-L0712–L0735 (1,423 words)

- L0712–L0723 surveys ways of contesting supposedly separate immaterial possibilities: body/accident exhaustion, the requirement of spatial indication or inherence, and a proof that bodily origination would entail origination of all other-than-God even without antecedently proving such exhaustion. The first positions are reported alternatives. The continued objection to an eternal soul/intellect mediating a first bodily world includes unsupported temporal selection, abstract entities lacking all spatial relations, and the absence of a new causal source for new representations.
- L0724–L0728 returns from rebuttal to resolution. Whether an endless succession of effects is possible or impossible, the philosophers do not thereby establish the antiquity of a particular sphere. Regress in producers is distinguished from unending successive products. L0728 explicitly reports three positions concerning backward and forward endless succession rather than treating all temporal infinitude as settled by one slogan.
- L0729–L0733 separates completion of a determinate act from the source of agency as such. An earlier segment can condition a later motion; an earlier created parent can condition a later child. It is different to say that absolutely any act must await an already accomplished act which would first make agency possible. The latter requires agency before agency and is rejected. The argument that the divine “Be” is not created is presented using this distinction: if no creation is possible without a prior created command, the very source of creating never arrives. The received L0729 repeats a clause and needs scan control.
- L0734–L0735 reconstructs the philosophers' proof as relying on no preference without a determinant and no regress in the origin of efficacy. Its proper reconstruction is crucial: substituting “no endless effects” misstates it. The continuation into window 5 retains the question of whether the agent was ever altogether without efficacy.

## Window 005: SF-S002-L0736–SF-S003-L0019 (1,403 words)

- L0736–L0740 and S003-L0001–L0004 show why an agent cannot first make itself completely efficacious from a condition wholly without efficacy: the proposal presupposes what it purports to generate. Viewed by successive putative events it is regress; viewed as the genus of agency depending on itself beforehand it is priority-circularity. Agency itself can be intrinsic while the production of one particular thing depends on another. The section boundary splits this single argument; it is not a substantive new beginning.
- L0005–L0010 applies this to cosmic origination. The author cites creation in six days, the throne on water, and the heaven as smoke; the identification of smoke with vapour is explicitly attributed to exegetes. Pre-existing created material and material before that defeat an inference from perpetual agency to the eternity of the present cosmos. This passage cannot be used for an unqualified claim that every production in the text excludes materials or earlier conditions.
- L0011–L0014 distinguishes God's self-sufficient successive words/acts from a possible made thing requiring its maker in every respect. The first act may precede the second without an outside supplier of the divine capacity. An allegedly eternal created bearer inseparable from successive occurrences faces the complete-cause problem. L0014 is textually unstable in the received wording and must not be silently regularised to support a proof.
- L0015–L0017 makes a second, stronger claim: the act/agent must precede the product, and “concomitance” is acceptable as immediate succession, not strict simultaneity. Hand and ring/sleeve motions are said to be correlated products of one agent rather than one movement's being the complete productive cause of the other. This changes the interpretation of earlier “coincidence” language and must be carried through the range.
- L0018–L0019 opens a further objection: an eternal act producing a sphere, together with successive acts producing its inseparable occurrences, makes the supposedly eternal act conditional on those successive acts. The completion of that objection is in window 6.

## Window 006: SF-S003-L0020–L0057 (1,418 words)

- L0020–L0024 distinguishes necessary concomitance from causal or conditional dependence. Events can follow from and depend upon the eternal, without the eternal depending on their individual instances or on their successively instantiated type. Treating an eternal act as conditioned by later acts would make what belongs to the divine self depend on its effects. This is a third argument in the local sequence, not an assertion that every logical correlate produces every other correlate.
- L0025–L0032 disputes identity of act and product. The author reports theological motivations for that identification and three alternatives: an eternal act with delayed product; individually originated acts without regress; individually originated acts with a possibly beginningless type. These are differentiated positions, not interchangeable statements of his own doctrine. Philosophers allowing an endless succession cannot simply borrow the anti-regress motivation for act/product identity.
- L0033–L0040 adds an important contrast. Mutual conditioning can occur between two effects of one cause, such as fatherhood and sonship, or between uncaused eternal self and attributes. It does not follow that an eternally necessary agent may depend on successively originating products. Calling the sphere indispensable to God makes the possible product a condition of its allegedly self-sufficient producer. This is presented as a fourth independent argument.
- L0041–L0053 distinguishes an argument binding an opponent from one that establishes the proponent's conclusion. If regress means successive effects, the philosophers themselves allow it; if it means the source of efficacy, it establishes continuing agency rather than a particular eternal world. The author allows the possibility of beginningless intrinsic actions and/or successive transitive products while maintaining each creature's origination after non-being.
- L0054–L0057 extends the diagnosis to arguments from agent, matter, form, end, and divine generosity. Generosity and essential agency establish at most a type, not an eternal particular act or material. L0057 identifies three scopes of “efficacy”: efficacy as such, efficacy towards a particular, and efficacy towards everything. They cannot be substituted for one another; the last reading would make observed new events impossible.

## Window 007: SF-S003-L0058–L0082 (1,411 words)

- L0058–L0065 completes the scope distinction. “Eternity” is not a determinate time. Always being efficacious does not mean eternally producing this particular or producing all things at once. Temporal priority, time as measure of motion, and a possible material before each new thing can establish succession without establishing the eternity of this sun, moon, heaven, or earth. The afterlife's different motions and periods serve as an explicit counterexample to identifying all time with the present astronomical system; Qurʾān 19:62 is cited.
- L0066–L0074 broadens the polemic to theological accounts of divine inactivity, the destruction/recreation of the cosmos, and speech. The author rejects both eternally fixed particular utterances and an initial total absence of the ability to speak. Moses' call is treated as occurring when he reached the place, not merely as his new hearing of an eternally identical call. The positive formula is unceasing ability and activity of speaking when God wills. Reports about Jahm, Abū al-Hudhayl, Kullābīs, Ashʿarīs, and Sālimīs remain the author's classifications.
- L0075–L0079 returns to the central inference: perpetual possibility, time, and activity yield continuity of a type; an eternally complete invariant cause would not yield successive events. Even before proving voluntary agency, successively originating events require an agent that acts successively. The voluntary account is then stronger.
- L0080–L0082 rejects dividing the divine self into a supposedly bare part eternally producing a bearer and later voluntary occurrences producing its changes. Such a self is never actually stripped of voluntary matters; a counterfactual abstraction does not establish an existent separated productive component. The completion of this tightly connected argument is in window 8.

## Window 008: SF-S003-L0083–L0115 (1,428 words)

- L0083–L0095 develops inseparability of an effected bearer and its successive requisites. A voluntary agent's activity cannot be partitioned into an eternal bare-self production and a different will-associated production of its necessary concomitants. Producing something requires producing it with those concomitants. If one alleged production exists only successively, the inseparable production cannot be eternally fixed. The author distinguishes this from self/attribute and self/activity relations: attributes are not products of the self, and the self's necessity is not made dependent on its effects.
- L0096–L0105 articulates the act/attribute distinction. An attribute may be invariant in particular (life) or continuing in type (speech, will), whereas a productive act is intelligible as something occurring. A fixed eternal particular operation indistinguishable from an essential attribute is not thereby an act. The hand/key example again distinguishes concomitance from efficient production, and temporal priority of the producer is asserted independently of voluntary agency.
- L0106–L0110 tests the proposal that an eternally essential particular might itself be an object of power and will. If the necessary power and will are also produced, the account either circles or regresses in the source of production. If the product-like eternal concomitant is elective but the otherwise analogous eternal powers are not, the author challenges the distinction. These are modal arguments about the alleged eternal product, not a stand-alone theorem concerning all properties or all conditions.
- L0111–L0115 invokes Qurʾān 67:10 and then ordinary understanding of “made”, “created”, and “originated”. Knowing something as made carries its having come to be after not being. The author treats this as naturally evident, not as a modern lexical survey or an independently demonstrated truth. This linguistic/epistemic claim continues in window 9.

## Window 009: SF-S003-L0116–L0144 (1,423 words)

- L0116–L0129 attacks the conjunction “eternal yet made” and the attribution of possibility to an entity unable ever to fail to exist. The author contrasts Ibn Sīnā and followers with his account of Aristotle and earlier Peripatetics; Ibn Rushd is named as a critic. Pure continuing non-being needs no efficient cause; absence of a cause can entail absence of an effect without being an efficient producer of that absence (L0124–L0126). The received syntax in L0126 is suspect and needs control.
- L0130–L0135 moves from necessary divine attributes to the status of essence. An extra-mental essence allegedly additional to the particular existent is rejected. Definitions dividing constituent essential attributes from inseparable external attributes depend on what was included in a conception and its expression. Correspondence, inclusion, and implication of expressions clarify this rather than proving exterior assemblages out of universals. “Rational animal” and “laughing animal” change what is internal to the description without changing the concrete human.
- L0136–L0139 supplies the clearest universal/particular passage in this range: one human is himself bodily, growing, sensing, voluntarily moving, and rational. He is not assembled from separately existing substantial universals or from a bundle of prior accidents. Attributes of an individual are particular; sharing is in a universal that is universal only mentally. The fourfold distinction between real, mental, spoken, and written existence prevents confusing conceptual composition with external construction.
- L0140–L0143 locates al-Ghazālī in the reported reception of these distinctions, then revisits essence/existence through the conceived triangle. The received L0142 contains an apparent negation problem; the surrounding contrast is clear, but the exact wording remains uncontrolled. Even granting an essence/existence distinction arguendo, a genuinely eternally necessary existent cannot be potentially nonexistent in the asserted manner.
- L0144 introduces revelation as confirming and perfecting natural recognition rather than changing it, then begins a Qurʾānic quotation completed in window 10.

## Window 010: SF-S003-L0145–L0178 (1,403 words)

- L0145–L0155 collects scriptural language of creating, building, raising, and making the heavens and their contents. The claim is that ordinary understanding entails origination, not mere atemporal dependence upon an eternal cause. A careful asymmetry appears at L0155: every created thing is originated; whether every originated matter is created is disputed. This matters directly for God's acts and speech and limits indiscriminate conversion of the two predicates.
- L0156–L0161 contrasts an Aristotelian first end with an Avicennian productive cause, and reiterates two routes to cosmic origination: the individual world's evident dependence on a maker, and its inseparability from successive occurrences. L0159 locates evidence in determinate things rather than an undifferentiated universal. Polemical descriptions of theologians obscuring revelation are not neutral historiography.
- L0162–L0170 repeats the rejection of inferring a particular eternal cosmos from continuous agency, then includes a substantial historical excursus on Maḥmūd, Seljuq rule, the Qādirī creed, interschool conflicts, public denunciations, al-Bāqillānī, al-Qushayrī, and al-Bayhaqī (especially L0167). This material is orthogonal to the immediate causal proof but central to the author's genealogy of theological error. It has not been checked against independent historical evidence.
- L0171–L0178 links Jahm's alleged symmetry between past and future finitude with annihilation of paradise and hell. Transmitted statements then begin: reports via Ḥarb/al-Khallāl, Khārija b. Muṣʿab, Ibrāhīm b. Ṭahmān, and a story involving Muqātil. Qurʾānic statements about enduring provision and seeing God are invoked inside these reports. These are quoted attestations, including severe judgments about groups, not the present reader's endorsements or independently authenticated isnāds. The last anecdote crosses into the next window.

## Window 011: SF-S003-L0179–L0205 (1,423 words)

- L0179–L0186 continues a chain-heavy dossier of hostile statements about Jahmiyya, including Ibn al-Mubārak, Ibn al-Mājishūn, Ibrāhīm b. Ṭahmān, Sulaymān al-Taymī, Ḥammād b. Zayd, Yaḥyā al-Qaṭṭān, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, Yazīd b. Hārūn, and the reported killing of al-Jaʿd by Khālid al-Qasrī. Their evidential role is the author's attestation of early opposition. Reading these reports does not authenticate their chains or authorise their evaluations or violence.
- L0187 gives an intellectual genealogy from Ḥarrān's philosophers to al-Jaʿd and Jahm; the author expressly qualifies part of it with “as it is said”. L0188–L0189 admits philosophers differ, credits early leading figures with cosmic origination and attributes, and again separates generic time from a particular celestial motion. Afterlife periods are used to reinforce the distinction.
- L0190–L0197 argues that perpetual capacity includes perpetual possibility of its exercise. Newly acquiring an intrinsic divine perfection would require a supplier; an effect cannot confer the original power by which it is produced. L0195 says all that belongs to the product derives from the producer, but its immediate use is to rule out the product's conferring its producer's original capacity. It is not permission to ignore the different senses of causation elsewhere.
- L0198–L0205 develops unity: two independent complete producers cannot jointly produce one identical act; disjoint creation would be expressed as each deity taking its creation away; conflicting wills yield frustration; necessarily harmonious wills, if dependent on mutual permission, fail to be independent. L0205 adds actual worldly connectedness and mutual need against partition between independent creators. This is an explicit common-Creator argument, not merely a generic existence proof. Its completion is in window 12.

## Window 012: SF-S003-L0206–L0237 (1,463 words)

- L0206–L0208 completes the unity argument and expressly distinguishes reciprocal priority-dependence from possible concurrent dependence. Two created agents deriving power from a third can have mutually conditional powers; two alleged original, self-necessary creators cannot borrow one another's original capacity. This limiting case must accompany the preceding claim against independent complete producers.
- L0209–L0212 revisits eternity of possibility and possibility of eternal activity. The author rejects a beginningless possibility of one intrinsically initial type understood as necessarily beginning at some first boundary. L0211 nevertheless insists that each particular created thing's own possibility of origination has a beginning. The scope of the subject, type versus token, must not be lost in extracting a modal formula.
- L0213–L0226 returns to the invariant-cause dilemma and to the difference between fixed ancient particulars and an ancient type with new particulars. The author claims the philosophers' strongest principle can support every creature's origination rather than their conclusion. Both parties' reported errors are distinguished, though he regards the philosophers' error as worse.
- L0227–L0235 discusses the limitation claimed by later theologians that their proof reaches bodies but not intellects/souls. Earlier theologians' body/accident ontology is defended against that particular complaint; L0235 concedes that “body” and “accident” disputes include both terminological and rational components. This prevents treating all these labels as settled univocal vocabulary.
- L0236–L0237 supplies a more complex intellectual history of Ibn Sīnā: he is praised comparatively for improving earlier philosophical theology by drawing on theologians, while borrowing their errors or exploiting their distinct errors. Three criticised positions include needing a cause only at inception, preference without determinant, and sufficient preference for existence short of necessity. The final received phrase here may itself need textual control.

## Window 013: SF-S003-L0238–L0264 (1,419 words)

- L0238–L0248 continues that historical evaluation. Ancient metaphysics is described through being and its concomitants, substance and accident, the ten categories, and the foundations of mathematical/natural sciences. A mnemonic verse is quoted at L0240. The author distinguishes the first mover as desired end from a creative cause, and says the proved force of “necessary existence” is existing through oneself and independence from a producer, not the absence of attributes. His comparative criticism includes explicit acknowledgement that Ibn Sīnā's treatment contains truth as well as error.
- L0249–L0250 introduces a quotation from Ibn Sīnā's al-Najāt. It divides newly occurring things by whether they persist, rejects certain accounts of successive instants, and distinguishes a cause of coming-to-be from a cause of persistence. Examples are water held in shape by a mould and a statue produced by an artisan but maintained by the dryness of its material. These are Ibn Sīnā's examples within a quotation, not direct independent assertions by the author.
- L0251–L0253 comments favourably on the core premise: some originated things persist, and persistent creatures still require a cause. A reported interpretation of al-Naẓẓām is treated as a possible clarification rather than a blanket attribution. The author explicitly calls continued dependence correct and introduces Ibn Sīnā's following words as correct.
- L0254–L0257 quotes the distinction between intrinsic possibility and necessity conditional on actual existence, with parallel conditional necessity of non-being. Zayd's existing is distinguished from his necessarily existing while he exists. The argument opposes turning a creature into something self-necessary once produced.
- L0258–L0264 resumes commentary, permits a genuine shared concept of possibility across states, then specifies two objections: reifying a conceived possible nature outside the mind before existence; and making an eternally necessary entity capable of non-being under an impossible supposition. Thus his endorsement of persistence-dependence does not endorse every part of Avicennian ontology. The quotation is revisited in window 14.

## Window 014: SF-S003-L0265–L0294 (1,422 words)

- L0265–L0266 resumes Ibn Sīnā's claim that possible existence, rather than its being after non-being, is what depends on a cause. At L0267–L0268 the author's “قلت” counters the search for a property causing dependence: the creature is dependent in itself; possibility and origination indicate or entail that dependence, rather than making an otherwise independent essence needy. “In itself” is customary speech, not an uncreated essence alongside God. No creaturely reality originates or persists without divine production.
- L0269–L0276 mirrors this clarification for God: existing, being necessary, or being sufficient “through himself” does not mean God efficiently makes himself necessary. There are not two external entities, an essence and an added existence, related as producer/product. A detailed lexical discussion distinguishes verbal nouns, divine bringing-to-existence, finding/seeing something, and the concrete existing reality. Creaturely need before existence means that it cannot become real without the creator; afterwards it means neither continued reality nor continued existence is independent.
- L0277–L0281 explains that statements about the not-yet-existent concern a mental conception. The genuine difference between mental meanings and external realities must not be converted into the external existence of mental meanings. The author reopens the disagreement about possibility without origination without pretending all traditions agree with Ibn Sīnā.
- L0282–L0287 quotes the persistence argument and an internal objection: if the preserving cause is eternal, why is its effect new; if its being a cause is new, what causes and preserves that new causality? Ibn Sīnā's answer appeals to continuous motion, especially circular local motion, allowing changing causal states.
- L0288–L0294 rejects that escape under the opponent's own premises. An endless simultaneous set of originated causal completions supplies no first complete source. Appealing to motion merely relocates the question of what newly causes motion. Several passages of the quoted technical text have apparent defects and should receive scan control before exact inferential use. The response continues immediately in window 15.

## Window 015: SF-S003-L0295–L0323 (1,417 words)

- L0295–L0298 quotes Ibn Sīnā on stable general intention and changing particular intentions/representations. Bare intellect does not directly execute changing particular motion; a soul with successive volitions is introduced, with an appeal to Aristotle's theoretical/practical distinction. The “universal” will here belongs to the quoted explanatory model and is not an endorsement of a separately real universal producer.
- L0299–L0303 turns the quotation against an invariant first cause: what causes the successive particular representations and volitions? If only an unchanged intellect and unchanged will stand above them, both origination and persistence remain unexplained. A celestial cause allegedly explaining changes in human willing does not thereby explain its own changing activity.
- L0304–L0310 quotes and critiques a further account of enduring subjects with changing states. The first reply asks for the source of those new states; the second asks how a passing cause accounts for persistent effects when the proposed complete-cause rule requires concomitance. The author presents “neither originator of events nor preserver of enduring things” as a consequence of the opponents' theory, not as their explicit self-description and certainly not as his own metaphysics.
- L0311–L0318 quotes the first mover as beloved good and the heavens' movement as imitation of maximal abiding perfection, preserved in type where not possible in numerical identity. This section concerns finality, desire, goodness, imitation, and celestial purpose, not only abstract modal dependence.
- L0319–L0323 gives a crucial counterweight to indiscriminate source language. An object of love or desire can move one as food moves an eater or drink a drinker, without efficiently making the desiring power, appetite, or act. A represented end can be prior in conception before it exists outside. A desire to imitate the first principle therefore does not establish a creative producer of the lover's changing volitions. The author distinguishes final from efficient causes and argues that the philosophers have not supplied the latter. He again attributes the being/necessity/possibility reconstruction to Ibn Sīnā rather than ancient Aristotle.

## Window 016: SF-S003-L0324–L0350 (1,435 words)

- L0324–L0329 considers two possibilities for a celestial sphere: dependent on a creator or self-necessary. If the former, its maker can produce its motion; voluntary motions might be performed by angels worshipping God rather than imitating unattainable perfection. These are possibilities offered against the rival inference, not verified astronomy. If the sphere needs a mover to subsist, calling it self-sufficient contradicts that need. Further ad hominem consequences concern divine voluntary motion and bodily/spatial language; they must not be extracted as unqualified positive definitions of God.
- L0330–L0337 revisits contemporaneous complete explanation of each new movement and the impossibility of making them eternal effects of an unvarying simple cause. Different motions and the diversity of stars intensify the question of how differentiated products issue from the alleged simple source. Inserting a mediator does not remove diversity at whichever stage diversity arises.
- L0338–L0347 distinguishes the independently necessary self's succession from a possible sphere's succession. L0342 explicitly says the possible effect depends in self, attributes, and states on another; the creator of all these cannot be modelled as an attribute-less, act-less simplicity. L0343 accepts “from one only one proceeds” within this stipulated context while rejecting the imagined absolutely simple first and first product. This is a contextual conditional argument, not a universal endorsement of the opponents' emanation system. The printed-looking defects and compressed syntax in L0340–L0347 require scan control for a formal reconstruction.
- L0348–L0350 questions what each epistemic route really establishes. Motion as desire establishes an end, not a creator. An existence argument establishes a necessary existent, but does not by itself identify it with the first celestial mover, distinguish it from the sphere, or establish separation from the world. A further attribute-negating inference is not accepted as a repair. This is unusually explicit evidence that an existential result does not automatically establish bearer identity or every proposed theological predicate. The continuation is in window 17.

## Window 017: SF-S003-L0351–L0367 (938 words)

- L0351–L0353 finishes the two-route critique. The alleged attribute-less, act-less simple item cannot perform the required explanatory work; combining inadequate routes does not automatically close their gaps. The received L0351 contains intrusive characters and L0353 is semantically delicate; the denial of extra-mental reality concerns the opponent's described necessary existent, not a denial of the existence of a self-necessary creator.
- L0354–L0358 ranks the philosophers' celestial account against Qadarite, naturalistic, and Magian accounts. These are the author's polemical comparisons. His stated charge is that the celestial theory supplies neither a maker of the sphere's power nor a producer of its new volitions, whereas some opponents at least admit a creator for the underlying animal or natural power. Each new act needs its determinant; merely being an object of imitation does not originate motion. The author's inference to the creator is explicit, although the alleged rival positions have not been independently checked.
- L0359–L0361 revisits coordinated celestial agents. If their necessary agreement derives from each other, original power circles; if from above, they are governed creatures. If disagreement is possible, their mutual frustration reveals limitation. This parallels the earlier distinction between dependent created agency and independent deities.
- L0362–L0365 adds unity of a created individual: a human, horse, or tree is one. Two independent complete creators of that single unity contradict independence; ordinary collaborators such as builders, tailors, weavers, writers, farmers, and carriers perform distinguishable acts. The passage advances a single-creator argument for an undivided creature, while acknowledging ordinary cooperation rather than denying it. Any common-Creator comparison should preserve this distinction.
- L0366–L0367 begins a new objection to the active intellect's emanation of forms once things are prepared. Is the emanated item an accident like light, or a substance like animals, plants, minerals, and human spirit? Substance issuing from another is ordinarily understood through separation, like flowing water; being a cause of another thing is then conceded as a possibility. The argument ends mid-course at this assignment boundary. No conclusion beyond these lines is supplied here.

## Window 001: SF-S003-L0368–0393

- **Opening join, separately read at L0367:** The previous line asks whether what emanates is substance or accident. Light or radiation supplies an analogy for accidents, whereas animals, plants, minerals and the human spirit are substances. A self-subsisting emanated substance is known where something separates from its source, although something may instead be a cause of another occurrence. This joins the assigned opening at L0368; the division is not a fresh argument.
- **L0368–0373:** The author attacks separating the producer of movement/mixing from the producer of the resulting mixture or substantial form. His example is water and earth becoming mud: producing the antecedent without its inseparable consequent is impossible. Two independent agents would permit the supposed inseparables to separate; two necessarily interdependent agents cannot each be independently necessary. A distinct maker is then required. He distinguishes impermissible prior/efficient circularity from concomitant correlation, illustrated by fatherhood and sonship. The text also exempts necessarily concomitant divine attributes from the structure of caused effects. This is a tightly specified inseparability argument, not a proof that every condition is an independent efficient source, or that all dependencies are vicious circularity.
- **L0374–0377:** The author introduces Ibn Sina's account of God's knowledge and quotes the Najat on the unity in concept of knowledge, power, will and life. The quotation proceeds across the line breaks. The author first grants an intelligible route from being originator to knowledge of what is originated, linking it to Qur'an 67:14, while denying the philosophical school's account of its genealogy. Attribution of that genealogy is his assertion.
- **L0378–0390:** A sequence of objections distinguishes knower, knowing and known; the particular existing order from its maker; and knowledge from the productive capacity and volition required for action. Merely conceiving a practical object does not bring it about. Knowledge tracks whichever order is known, so a further ground of its selection is needed. L0387 asks whether the order's goodness is independently necessary or received from its maker. L0388 expressly separates knowledge, will and power. L0389–0390 uses natural bodily powers as a comparison, while rejecting both knowledge-without-power and power-without-knowledge as adequate divine accounts.
- **L0391–0393:** The criticism turns to theological opponents who argue from “God was, and nothing was with Him”. The author disputes an added phrase, “and He is now as He was”, and rejects its use to deny attributes, divine elevation, or Creator–creation difference. The reported monist slogans are opponents' sayings. No authentication of the hadith variants or reported sayings was independently performed.

## Window 002: SF-S003-L0394–0413

- **L0394–0398:** The author compares received variants of a report attributed to al-Bukhari: nothing before/other than/with God. He treats one utterance as underlying the variants and privileges “before”, reading the throne-on-water context as an account of the beginning of this six-day cosmos rather than everything antecedent to it. Eschatological texts describe transformation rather than the total disappearance of every creature. A long Qur'anic sequence on cosmic change is followed by cases of revivification, including Qur'anic stories of human resurrection, birds, the food and drink, and the sleepers. L0398 distinguishes evidence of occurrence from evidence of possibility, the latter including first creation, vegetation and creation of heaven and earth. These are explicit evidential categories, not interchangeable proofs.
- **L0399–0401:** The author opposes reducing prophetic disclosure and extraordinary deeds to psychic powers. The disputed account lacks living speaking angels, divine speech outside the prophet's own soul, and a willing creator able to transform the upper cosmos. Strong denunciations belong to this author's evaluation of that account.
- **L0402–0408:** The critique of “one” targets a definition excluding attributes and acts. A necessary attribute need not be another independently necessary deity: the author compares a human attribute not being another human, or a prophet's attribute another prophet. He distinguishes one self-subsisting necessary God from necessary concomitant attributes. The unity taught by prophets encompasses perfection, freedom from deficiency and incomparability, together with exclusive worship, reliance, fear and hope. L0405's received “القول العملي” stands beside L0406's practical-volitional heading and needs control.
- **L0409–0412:** “Division” and “composition” are disambiguated. Actual assembly from previously separate things is denied of God. Atomist, matter–form and essence–existence compositions are also disputed in creatures, making their application to God still less acceptable on this argument. Merely possessing life, knowledge and power is not thereby composition in this concrete sense. A stipulated terminology must not eliminate real perfections in favour of a mentally conceived absolute existence.
- **L0413:** The author questions the strength of the eternalists' proof of an originator and the way later reports treat Ibn Sina as preserving a maker. This is the transition to the next window's assessment of multiple proofs, rather than a completed demonstration here.

## Window 003: SF-S003-L0414–0439

- **L0414–0419:** An important methodological concession: knowledge of a maker can be innate/immediate, inferential, or different in different people. Some premises, including refutations of regress and circularity, are required only to remove particular doubts. They are incidental to some routes, not necessary components of every route. False arguments are rejected; a sound but lengthy argument may be useful for a particular hearer; an unnecessary assertion that everyone needs it is criticised. A sound concise route has no such fault. This is not a blanket rejection of reasoning or a monopoly for one proof.
- **L0420–0423:** The author moves from cosmology to practical philosophy. He accepts the genuine reform of character, households and cities, but denies that those exhaust the aims of prophecy. L0420's received “لا يقولون” is in tension with the immediately following charge and requires control. The opponents are represented as reducing prayer to psychic action on matter, reward to the soul's acquired disposition, and post-mortem pleasure to knowledge. Their ideal of the soul as an intelligible world is then criticised: abstract classifications of existence do not themselves give knowledge of the concrete God, angels, prophets, heavens and earth.
- **L0424–0430:** Human perfection requires both the knowing and practical-affective faculties. Recognition without love, acceptance and worship may accompany misery; Iblis and Pharaoh are scriptural examples in the argument. Worship combines utmost love and humility. The author distinguishes several defective alternatives, including faith reduced to cognition, mercenary worship, and worship pursued only for disclosure or efficacy. He concedes that the religious worshippers he criticises can be better than the philosophical reductionists. Divine love, rejoicing, pleasure and analogous scriptural expressions are affirmed in scriptural language, rather than by treating all attributes as one.
- **L0431–0438:** Pleasure is distinguished from its cognitive condition: appetite, tasting and pleasure are different; knowledge or perception can lead to pleasure without being identical with it. Love is an additional requisite. Reports about seeing God in the next life and the prayer for the pleasure of that vision support the author's account. Their reported authenticity is not newly verified here.
- **L0439:** Even hypothetically granting that cognition alone perfected the soul would not make cognition of abstract universals sufficient. Ibn Sina's reported restriction of immaterial cognition to universals excludes concrete extra-mental particulars. The stated primary end of genuine knowledge is knowledge of realities existing outside the mind.

## Window 004: SF-S003-L0440–0468

- **L0440–0443:** The critique continues: knowing a merely mental absolute cannot substitute for knowing God. The author invokes transmission and experience of outward benefits and punishments to challenge the thesis that worship only changes internal character. He affirms a freely acting God who answers prayer and produces effects beyond known natural and psychic powers. These are his evidential claims, not newly established empirical conclusions.
- **L0444–0453:** A substantial historical-theological argument rejects a supposed esoteric division between philosophical truth and prophetic representations useful for civic order. The author opposes exempting the “knowing” elite from commandments and prohibitions. He appeals to the public and private conformity of Muhammad and his closest companions and locates the denial of attributes later. He reports disputes over whether Jahmiyya belong within the seventy-two sects. Jewish and Christian acceptance of prescribed worship, bodily resurrection and external reward/punishment is treated as a relative advantage over the targeted philosophical view, without approving every part of those religions.
- **L0454–0465:** A sustained scriptural anthology gives exclusive worship as the common mission of the prophets. “General Islam” denotes submission to God across prophetic dispensations, illustrated by Noah, Abraham, Moses, Israelite prophets, Bilqis and the disciples. The author compares the four groups in Qur'an 2:62 with six in 22:17, using the difference between promised salvation and final judgement to classify communities. These exegetical classifications should not be mistaken for an independently established comparative-religion survey.
- **L0466–0468:** The criterion of accountability is expressly qualified: punishment follows opposition to an encountered messenger. The author reasons from Qur'an 17:15 and other texts, then refuses blanket decisions that every child of non-Muslims is in heaven or every such child in hell. The cited birth-upon-fitra report and “God knows what they would have done” carry the discussion into the next window. The restriction matters against flattening the preceding polemics into an unconditional judgement about every individual.

## Window 005: SF-S003-L0469–0492

- **L0469–0475:** The author continues with reports of an otherworldly test for those not reached by the message, including people unable to understand or hear it and people living between dispensations. He ties this to the non-punishment principle. After a scriptural contrast between guidance and misery, he distinguishes philosophers who knowingly reject a message after its proof reaches them from believers who agree with philosophers in truths consistent with revelation or in religiously neutral natural and mathematical matters. The latter agreement is expressly possible. Mixed adherence receives a comparative judgement that changes with the aspect considered; it is not one undifferentiated ranking.
- **L0476–0479:** Happiness requires beneficial knowledge and good action in conformity with the prophetic way. A report from Ubayy and an interpretation attributed to al-Fudayl demand both sincerity and correctness. Ascetic regulation of desire, anger, wealth and ambition is acknowledged as part of religious practice but insufficient to define its whole purpose.
- **L0480–0488:** The four virtues and the regulation of appetitive and irascible powers are presented, then criticised for generality and incompleteness. Knowing simply that bodily humours must be balanced is compared to an ethical formula that says “be moderate” without explaining acts, occasions and quantities. Even achieved moderation does not by itself prove happiness, and the right kind and extent of knowledge remain unsettled. The author explicitly narrows one severe judgement to Aristotle and his followers and says earlier masters were closer to truth. The received “القوة العلمية” and “الحكة” at L0480 need textual control.
- **L0489–0492:** The fifth objection distinguishes bodily appetites from the rational soul's abiding love and will directed to its worshipped object. The opponents' post-mortem intellect is represented as motionless, without fresh desire, pursuit or love. An emanative hierarchy of intellects and spheres follows. Identifying the active intellect or a prophet's inner images with Gabriel is reported as an attempted reconciliation, not accepted by the author. The exact celestial numbering needs control before reconstruction.

## Window 006: SF-S003-L0493–0511

- **L0493–0495:** The account is extended to Ibn Arabi's reported ranking of sainthood and prophecy: a saint allegedly receives directly from the source from which an angel takes, whereas prophetic reception is routed through imagination. The author then registers two views of celestial souls, as bodily accidents or self-subsisting substances, associating Ibn Sina with the second. This preserves disagreement inside the criticised school.
- **L0496–0499:** A long, expressly introduced quotation from Abu al-Barakat describes Aristotle's followers: a single first production from a first one, intellect as immaterial actuality, soul as connected with bodily change, and the active intellect as teacher and proximate principle of human souls. The quotation itself distinguishes Arabic uses of intellect from its translated Greek philosophical use. These lines are not unmarked first-person endorsements by Ibn Taymiyya.
- **L0500–0503:** “I say” restores the author's voice. He holds that Arabic intellect can include knowledge and action upon knowledge, and can designate an inborn capacity. He contrasts this with accounts that deny natures, powers, causes and wisdom, and therefore reduce intellect to necessary cognitions. His criticism names al-Ashari and later theologians but should not be adopted as a complete description of each. Crucially, “at these things, not through them” is the opponent's occasionalist formula, which he rejects. His positive position permits created powers and genuine explanatory means.
- **L0504–0507:** The author again separates philosophical parties. Abu al-Barakat is a dissenter from the alleged identity of intellect, knower and known. A claim that nobody dissented is restricted to its reporter's knowledge or to familiar Aristotelian writing; the author gives an analogy with prominent legal schools obscuring other eminent jurists. Aristotle's physics is credited more highly than his theology. This is a sustained qualification of claims about philosophical consensus.
- **L0508–0511:** The critique returns to the human soul. Permanent, identical cognitive pleasure without growth or love is rejected on several grounds. The sixth objection distinguishes knowledge as a condition from both a sufficient producer of happiness and happiness itself. The food/taste/pleasure example is repeated to make both distinctions clear.

## Window 007: SF-S003-L0512–0537

- **L0512–0515:** Hearing, sight, knowledge and touch can occasion pleasure but are not themselves pleasure. Perception of harmful objects may hurt. Compatibility, glossed through love, is necessary; the favourable conjunction of love with perception yields pleasure. The author explicitly uses “condition” and “cause” in a qualified way, describing a conjunction rather than making bare cognition sufficient. He compares the contrary identification to collapsing knowledge, will, power and their subject.
- **L0516–0526:** Fitra includes acknowledgement and love of God. Qur'anic and hadith material is followed by reports about al-Jad's denial of divine love and speech, including the violent execution story, then criticisms of theological deniers and philosophical identifiers of attribute and subject. Al-Ghazali and al-Farabi are said to reinterpret the beatific vision as cognition. The author judges the mixed philosophical-theological positions nearer to truth than some alternatives while still insufficient. The execution narrative and the historical priority claims remain reports in the source, not verified history or prescriptions in these notes.
- **L0527–0531:** Reports of the vision and the prayer for it reappear. Divine and human love is made foundational for questions of purpose, willing and moral evaluation. L0531 carefully distinguishes a necessary condition from a converse: if God intends good for someone He grants religious understanding, but not every recipient of understanding thereby has the good outcome, because practice is also required. This is particularly important evidence against converting a requisite into a sufficient source.
- **L0532–0537:** The discussion broadens into competing accounts of survival and resurrection. It attributes three opinions concerning the learned and ignorant souls to al-Farabi's corpus, then opens a fourfold taxonomy: bodily and spiritual resurrection together; bodily resurrection alone; and spiritual resurrection alone, with the fourth in the next window. It explains how some theologians describe the soul as accident or bodily part and account for grave punishment through recreated bodily life. The author favours surviving souls with activity and later reunion with bodies.

## Window 008: SF-S003-L0538–0564

- **L0538–0544:** The fourth position denies both resurrections. The seventh objection then argues that post-mortem pleasure itself requires love and pursuit, and that human perfection calls for knowledge, capacity and will rather than one faculty alone. Love of knowing universals is expressly allowed because their comprehensiveness leads to knowledge of particulars (L0542). Knowledge may also be pleasurable by removing doubts or helping secure a good or avert harm. Thus abstract reasoning is assigned a subordinate genuine use, not dismissed in every use.
- **L0545–0547:** The eighth objection works with the opponents' broad taxonomy of motion: quantitative, qualitative, local and positional change. Learning after ignorance, loving after hatred and relief after pain are qualitative changes. Post-mortem activity therefore need not mean mere locomotion. The author argues against a static soul using this expanded vocabulary rather than silently shifting between meanings of “motion”.
- **L0548–0555:** The ninth objection can proceed even without deciding whether pleasure is identical to, or caused by, perception: on either view it requires a fitting object. Compatibility/repugnance, love/aversion and cognition jointly explain the outcome. Reports about seeing God, prayer, remembrance and the greatness of the Throne verse and Surat al-Ikhlas support the devotional conclusion. The received verse reference at L0553 is 225, needing control. The author also divides Qur'anic discourse into commands, reports about creatures and reports about the Creator to explain the one-third saying.
- **L0556–0564:** The tenth objection turns to the highest philosophical science, its division of existence and ten categories, including a mnemonic poem. It distinguishes partition of a whole into actual parts from division of a universal into species. Ordinary legal divisions and a grammatical debate over “speech divides into noun, verb and particle” supply non-theological examples. The window ends within that grammatical dispute, which continues next.

## Window 009: SF-S003-L0565–0589

- **L0565–0570:** The grammatical objection confuses conceptual division with division into constituent parts. Houses, bodies and speech illustrate actual composition; universal classification is an abstraction from particulars. The key positive contrast at L0568 is between “existence” or “thing” as the widest common meaning and the referent of “Necessary Existent”, “Lord of the worlds” and “Creator of the world”. The latter referent exists in itself and is what the soul knows and worships. A shared term's extension does not make an extra-mental universal bearer.
- **L0571–0579:** Natural, logical and intellectual universals are distinguished. Universality as such is mental. The natural universal can properly be called existent outside the mind insofar as the nature is instantiated in determinate individuals; it is not externally universal. The fourfold existence of an object in reality, mind, speech and writing clarifies correspondence. Qur'an 96:1–5 is read as naming creation and instruction across those modes. A written sign requires the mediation of language: a script does not independently supply its linguistic meaning. None of this means that all universal knowledge is fictitious or that common predication is forbidden.
- **L0580–0584:** The author recounts successive reifications: Pythagorean numbers, Platonic forms, then Aristotelian forms or essences said to accompany sensible individuals. He credits the later group's criticism of its predecessors but charges it with retaining another reification. Their division of necessary attributes into internal constituents and external entailments supplies the next target. Historical descriptions are the author's reports and require external checking if independently used.
- **L0585–0589:** A division between necessary and accidental attributes is accepted. What is disputed is the tripartite metaphysical division of necessary attributes and the external composition of a thing out of genus and difference. Essence may legitimately differ from existence when the terms distinguish mental conception from extra-mental reality. It is not legitimate, in his account, to posit two extra-mental things, essence and existence, or two corresponding mental components merely by changing words. The dispute is resolved by semantic and ontological disambiguation, not by an undifferentiated “essence equals existence” slogan.

## Window 010: SF-S003-L0590–0624

- **L0590–0596:** The author identifies projection from mental to extra-mental being as the recurring error behind supposedly separate intellects and celestial souls. He allows that a human soul is separate from its own body after death, without conceding the rest of the alleged immaterial system. The distinction of soul and intellect is explained as drawn from that human case. The author disputes identifying the philosophers' intelligible world with prophetic unseen realities. L0594's received “عرض قائم بنفسه” requires control because the immediate discussion makes these mental forms dependent upon souls.
- **L0597–0608:** Unseen and witnessed are relational to the present observer, not necessarily two absolute orders of imperceptible and perceptible being. Angels, jinn, paradise, hell and God may be perceived in the relevant circumstances; God is affirmed to be seen in the next life. A long scriptural sequence illustrates present absence becoming future presence, including death, angels, judgement and punishment. These theological claims do not amount to a general claim that every current mind can perceive every reality now.
- **L0609–0614:** The author rejects the identity of the emanative first/active intellect with a scriptural angel, because the former is made creator of whole domains and sometimes eternal, while scriptural angels are created servants. Reports and Qur'anic passages give their creatureliness, obedience, fear and dependence. Subordinate agency is not thereby made an independent creatorhood.
- **L0615–0624:** Intercession is the next application. The author reports a philosophical account of influx transmitted like light through a mirror and contrasts it with petition to a willing God. He also rejects courtly analogies in which an outside intercessor alters a ruler's decision through influence. The scriptural sequence denies independent ownership, partnership and support, and permits intercession only by divine permission and approval. The topic continues into window 011.

## Window 011: SF-S003-L0625–0649

- **L0625–0634:** Further scriptural and transmitted material introduces a threefold classification of intercession. The author rejects both unlicensed quasi-political intervention and the denial of intercession for grave sinners. The approved alternative involves permission, bounded scope and the prophet's worship; a report depicts successively delimited groups. Sincerity and exclusive worship, rather than petitioning other beings, are linked to the best benefit. L0633 describes how ordinary intercessors affect another agent and why this model is inappropriate to divine independence; the received word “العقل” there needs control before close argument.
- **L0635–0637:** An extended demonological account explains apparently effective invocations of the dead, idols, ecstatic performances and travel through air or water. These are claims and reported experiences in the author's cosmology, not independently observed events. He distinguishes wrongful practice, divine allies and occasional obedience of spirits to a saint, while reserving unrestricted subjection of jinn to Solomon. This is substantive material in the passage rather than an expendable illustration.
- **L0638–0644:** Returning to philosophical abstraction, the author grants real intellectual strengths in medicine, mathematics and much natural science. He criticises claims that “the philosophers agree” based only on Ibn Sina or Peripatetic literature. The eleventh objection addresses supposedly unchangeable cognition grounding immortal happiness. Conditional powers matter: fire does not invariably burn in actuality because conditions can fail and impediments intervene, and such impediments are not exhausted by a fixed list. This restricts an unconditional universal about actual burning; it does not deny fire's power or all useful natural generalisation. The particular enduring divine referent, rather than the universal term “existence”, is proposed as the appropriate object.
- **L0645–0649:** Definitions distinguish the defined object with coextensive inclusion/exclusion; they need not reveal an extra-mental essence composed of genus and difference. A mentally composed concept is allowed, and the relations of complete signification, inclusion and entailment are analysed within it. The final line distinguishes an inferential middle term from an intervening cause or condition in the thing itself. A difference in how an observer comes to know a property must not be converted into an extra-mental structure of dependence.

## Window 012: SF-S003-L0650–0682

- **L0650–0653:** The author attributes errors partly to philosophical origins and partly to translation and later transmission, while crediting Muslim-context philosophers such as Ibn Sina with genuine improvements. Genus–difference composition is challenged under both readings: if substances compose the human there are too many substances; if accidents compose it, dependent attributes would have to precede and constitute their subject. These are objections to one account of constitution, not a denial that subjects possess many properties.
- **L0654–0658:** He rejects a timeless efficient producer with a coeval product. The moving hand and ring are said to have one mover and concomitant motions, rather than the hand's motion being the efficient maker of the ring's motion. The Avicennian eternally possible-in-itself, necessary-through-another being is assigned to a small group rather than every philosopher. The literal priority formulation requires control before giving it a wider technical meaning; no general theorem about all dependency relations should be inferred from this local efficient-production argument.
- **L0659–0666:** The highest enduring object returns. Even the opponents' eternal spheres and intellects would be determinate particulars, so the combination “God knows Himself and what proceeds from Him” with “He knows only universals” is charged with inconsistency. Al-Tusi's reported answer identifying knowledge with the objects known is separately criticised. Other philosophers, including Abu al-Barakat, are credited with rejecting both attribute-denial and the ban on occurring acts or cognitions. The author again differentiates pre-Aristotelian cosmologies and debates about matter.
- **L0667–0682:** The range returns to the common prophetic religion, with a long anthology concerning Noah, Abraham, Moses, Pharaoh's magicians, Joseph, Bilqis, Israelite prophets and the disciples. General Islam, belief and good action structure the argument. The comparison between four and six communities recurs. L0669's junction of the first earthly messenger report with Qur'an 39:12, and L0681's grammatical discussion of the Sabians, have received-text anomalies requiring control. These are not silently repaired in the notes.

## Window 013: SF-S003-L0683–0710

- **L0683–0690:** Qur'anic and hadith reports develop the unity of prophetic religion. The author reads humanity's original unity as unity on the truth, citing an Ibn Abbas report of ten generations between Adam and Noah, and rejects an alternative interpretation. Subsequent division is associated with rivalry after disclosure, not presented as evidence of multiple equally valid ultimate religions.
- **L0691–0698:** A single religion is compatible with changing revealed laws, illustrated by the change of prayer direction and by successive prophetic dispensations. Adherence to earlier law before supersession differs from refusal of a later messenger once the call arrives. The author discusses pilgrimage and cites a warning report from al-Tirmidhi. Scripture is used to connect religious schism with rivalry and to command fidelity to the revealed law. No new authentication of the particular pilgrimage report is supplied.
- **L0699–0707:** The author's “middle” position is elaborated in polemical contrasts: divine perfections without creaturely deficiencies or creature-deification; recognition of all prophets rather than their rejection or excessive elevation. L0704 contains an explicit historical-juristic claim about execution for insulting a prophet and disagreement over repentance. It is recorded as part of the text, not supplied as a modern legal recommendation or independently checked law. The concluding verses of al-Baqara and reported prayer recitations join this doctrinal discussion to devotional practice.
- **L0708–0710:** The author contrasts positions on abrogation, insisting that God may replace a law but creatures may not do so on their own authority. Rules concerning permitted goods, prohibited impurities and purity practices illustrate his account of a middle way. These broad representations of Jewish and Christian practice are polemical generalisations in the source and cannot be presented as neutral exhaustive ethnography.

## Window 014: SF-S003-L0711–0737

- **L0711–0723:** The “middle” pattern is applied within Islam to companions, threats of punishment, predestination and attributes. The author combines affirmation of divine decree with wisdom and affirmation of attributes without assimilating their manner to creatures. Submission and worship must follow the current divine command; abrogated law no longer counts as the commanded practice. Surat al-Kafirun is interpreted as reciprocal dissociation from religious practice, and paired with al-Ikhlas as practical-volitional and informative-cognitive unity respectively.
- **L0724–0726:** A useful semantic excursus distinguishes a litigant's hujja, which may be false, from sound demonstration. Qur'anic expressions about arguments and a report warning that eloquent litigants may receive judgements contrary to the right are used. This prohibits treating the bare word “argument” as a success or truth marker in extraction.
- **L0727–0734:** A lengthy discussion of fighting, truces and abrogation follows. The author narrates stages from permission to command; at one stage peaceful noncombatants and treaty partners are excepted. He describes Tabuk, the jizya verse, the proclamation at pilgrimage, Abu Bakr's leadership and Ali's assigned role. Fixed-term treaties must run to their term, whereas an indefinite treaty is described as terminable. This is a historical-juristic argument with distinctions, not a single isolated “sword verse” maxim. The received sentence about binding contracts, partnership and agency at L0734 needs textual control before reuse.
- **L0735–0737:** The four-month respite in Bara'a is distinguished from the four named sacred months. Fighting until payment of jizya is distinguished from fighting until conversion; the author also distinguishes verbal forms in Qur'an 48:16. The relevant claims should remain within their historical exegetical frame. The window leaves the scope of jizya for the next one.

## Window 015: SF-S003-L0738–SF-S004-L0017

- **S003-L0738–0742:** Three juristic opinions on the classes liable to jizya are recorded, along with disagreements over treaty duration. Surat al-Kafirun is said not to legislate fighting either positively or negatively, so its religious dissociation is not abrogated by the sword verse. The author separates postponement of fighting from approval of other religions. The claim about “approval” is his theological stance; the surrounding limitations on fighting must not be erased. The electronic section changes after L0743 without a new opening or authorial reset.
- **S003-L0743–S004-L0005:** The author explains philosophy as love of wisdom, disputes a story about a founder named “Sophista”, and rejects attributing comprehensive denial of all truth or knowledge to an actual known school on that basis. Particular sophistical errors can occur without global scepticism. The illustration from theories of juristic judgement ends with a concession: the named thinkers may be virtuous seekers of truth despite a gravely mistaken position. This is important evidence against treating polemical condemnation of a thesis as proof of its proponent's conscious dishonesty.
- **S004-L0006–0010:** Wisdom combines knowledge and practice, supported by attributions to Malik and Ibn Qutayba. Prophetic knowledge of divine matters is ranked far above Greek wisdom. Nevertheless the author distinguishes people faithfully following an unsuperseded prophetic law, those rejecting a received messenger and those unreached in intervals between missions. He reiterates the asserted harmony of uncorrupted reasoning with revelation and points to his larger work on the issue.
- **S004-L0011–0017:** Theologians are criticised for meeting philosophical error with other rational and scriptural errors falsely credited to Islam. A military analogy leads to the positive norm that justice is obligatory for everyone towards everyone in every circumstance (L0013), supported by scriptural injunctions including justice towards hated opponents. This is a substantive ethical constraint on disputation and conflict. The window closes by returning to cosmological creation and eschatological transformation, continuing in the next window.

## Window 016: SF-S004-L0018–0045

- **L0018–0022:** Cosmic transformation is distinguished from the claim that every part of the universe is annihilated and then recreated after total absence. The author criticises Jahm's alleged finite bounds on divine action and the extinction of paradise and hell. L0020 combines two claims: God creates everything and every other particular is originated, preceded by nonexistence; this does not imply that God was inactive with respect to willing speech and action. The distributive claim about creatures must not silently become a claim about one moment at which no created thing at all exists.
- **L0023–0028:** The author rejects both an attribute-less intrinsically necessitating principle and an agent selecting one of equals without any differentiating ground. Complete will together with power necessitates its intended outcome, but that necessity is grounded in the will, and absence of willing yields non-occurrence. He expressly criticises denial of natural powers, created causal connections, wisdom and purposes, and returns to intellect as an inborn capacity. This recognises real secondary powers and conditional determination; it is neither arbitrary causeless choice nor a single bare attribute sufficient for everything.
- **L0029–0035:** After a methodological recap and an explicit chapter marker, a final topic contrasts love and worship with philosophical “assimilation to god as far as possible”. A marked quotation from Abu al-Barakat's al-Mutabar explains relational uses of “god” for an exemplar/teacher and for an unseen agent with authority. It discusses “god of gods” as the highest instance. This is a quotation about philosophical terminology, not the author's own classification of teachers as deities.
- **L0036–0045:** “I say” resumes the author's interpretation. He understands the quoted usage as imitation and obedience rather than loving worship of the Creator. The celestial mover argument is said to establish an exemplar for imitation, not an efficient Creator or a genuinely worshipped beloved. Translation of “god” into Arabic can hide the difference. Imitating a model can arise from rivalry or envy, so imitation by itself does not entail love, humility or reliance. The argument does not deny that a disciple may actually love a teacher; it denies the entailment.

## Window 017: SF-S004-L0046–0063

- **L0046–0049:** An internal-consistency challenge asks how philosophers can reject scriptural language of likeness yet prescribe making oneself like God. If a qualified likeness is possible, God can bring it about more readily than a human; if all likeness is impossible, their own ideal fails. The Torah wording here is a reported quotation within that argument, not independently verified scriptural transcription. The author then rejects making humans, prophets or angels objects of divine worship and cites Qur'an 3:79–80.
- **L0050–0056:** The author criticises a reported exhortation to “take on God's character”, and al-Ghazali's use of names such as the Compeller, Proud and God for a programme of assimilation. He also reports al-Mazari's and others' excessively sweeping contrary denial that any divine name can be reflected in a human character. His own resolution is threefold: some qualities, including knowledge, mercy and wisdom, commend a human; some, including divinity, domination and pride, are blameworthy in the human; still other human excellences, including servanthood, need, dependence, humility and petition, cannot characterise God. Ibn Barrajān's preference for “worshipful service” over “assimilation of character” is reported. This is decisive limiting evidence for any undifferentiated transfer-of-perfections schema.
- **L0057–0062:** Ibn Sina's stages of knowers, al-Razi's praise and philosophical Sufi annihilation are criticised. The approved fana consists in worshipping, loving, fearing, hoping in and obeying God to the exclusion of rival objects. Reports about dying with knowledge or confession of divine unity support the conclusion. The final authorial synthesis combines divine perfection, absence of deficiency, incomparability and exclusive love, worship and magnification. Differences among knowers and believers are measured by realisation of this unity; it closes “God knows best”.
- **L0063 and marker:** The received colophon is: “تمت القاعدة من كلام شيخ الإسلام مفتي الأنام تقي الدين أبي العباس أحمد بن تيمية الحنبلي مؤلف كتاب الفرقان بين أولياء الرحمن وأولياء الشيطان”. It is an attribution/closure voice, not securely part of the author's preceding argument. It calls the completed item a qaida and identifies the attributed author by another work. It does not by itself authenticate this edition, the project title, print completeness or the end of every form of al-Safadiyya. END WINDOW 017 follows at SF-S004-L0063.
