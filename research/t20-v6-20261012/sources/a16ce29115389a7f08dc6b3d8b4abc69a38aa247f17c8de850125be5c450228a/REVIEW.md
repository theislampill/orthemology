## Strongest objections tested

### A. Circular self-certification

The bad inference would be: praise occurred, therefore its content is true and the bearer perfect; then those same unearned perfections explain why praise occurred. Nothing about an agent's bare self-ascription independently authenticates its truth, wisdom or normativity.

The report does not use that inference. The epistemic warrants are the accepted source framework, knowing wise agency and normative excellence; the independently describable content criterion is act/intention/achievement. The practical ground is the relevant knowing excellence and its praiseworthy end, not the fact that a reader has accepted a source. The source's praise-to-wisdom argument is acknowledged as an evidential direction rather than counted again as independent support. Independence here is from the bare occurrence of the self-praise; it is not a demand for a second foundation wholly independent of every previously earned premise.

This remains an application within an already warranted framework. It does not freshly authenticate revelation or certify every historical instance of claimed benefit. That is a proper scope boundary, not a circularity defect.

### B. Mere fittingness is not automatically an agent's operative reason

A true and fitting description alone need not explain why its speaker performed an act. That is the strongest source-faithful objection to promoting this result into an action explanation. Here the needed bridge is not inferred merely from truth or temporal coincidence: 149–150's explanatory `وهذا لما` and 204's `ولهذا حمد نفسه` supply the author's own attribution, while 186 assigns own self-praise a role in realising what is loved.

Together these warrant positive practical basing at the declared content/type respect. They do not disclose every efficient condition, show that any silence is impossible, or choose among all truthful formulations. The report's refusal to claim completeness is therefore necessary; treating the absence of a full token mechanism as eliminating this real rationale would be an equally unwarranted downgrade.

### C. Accidental benefit and good intention must not be collapsed into accomplished beneficence

At 203, a person intending good but unable to realise it can deserve praise in that respect over a powerful producer without that intention. Thus effective output alone is insufficient, but the source does not say failed good intention is wholly unpraiseworthy. The report's three premise-deletion tests preserve these distinctions. They test descriptions and warranted praise-content, not metaphysically possible ignorant or unsuccessful divine worlds.

“Achieved beneficence” is the relevant warranted content where achievement is asserted. It does not follow from a verse's presence that every praised result had first been temporally completed before a newly individuated intrinsic praise event. Nor does “beneficence” erase the wider 204 vocabulary of governance, justice and dominion. The report's target is one specified respect within that broader account.

### D. A new cognitive representation is not owed per self-praise token

The initial report's compressed residual wording could imply that every own act requires an additional newly originating representation. The controlling records do not impose that premise. The representation report §4B explicitly tests an explanation that introduces such an `r`; the succession report §4A says “If r is invoked.” Both reject canonical one-to-one token inventories. The retained Majmūʿ 16:303–305 does affirm successive willing, intentional-temporal cognitive differentiation and origination of the representation under discussion. Those positive source claims must not be erased; neither do they identify a newly acquired cognitive representation with every self-praise clause or indicate prior ignorance of that clause's content.
