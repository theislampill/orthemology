# Exact common repair is not determined by the full smooth jet

Ordinary mathematical research result. The complete smooth construction below is not yet a kernel theorem. It strengthens the finite-order critical example: even every derivative of the supplied potentials at the coherent baseline can fail to determine exact local repair. The flat-function mechanism is classical; historical priority for this scoring application has not been established.

## Precise comparison

There are globally smooth scalar potentials psi0 and psi1 with strictly positive second derivatives, and an ordinary quadratic anchor, with these properties:

- psi0 and psi1 have identical derivatives of every order at s0=1/2, including their values
- On the complement truth hull {(0,1),(1,0)}, define Fi(x,y)=psii(x)+psii(1−y). The two potentials therefore have identical full jets at p=(s0,1−s0)
- The pair (quadratic,F0) has a common coherent weak repair for every forecast sufficiently near p
- The pair (quadratic,F1) has no common coherent weak repair for b_epsilon=(s0+epsilon,1−s0), for every sufficiently small positive epsilon
- Nevertheless the positive minimax worsening required for the second pair is smaller than every fixed positive power of epsilon

This is a statement about exact weak all-truth-state repair under forecast-second Bregman losses. It does not say that a full function representation fails to determine repair, or that functions with the same full jet have the same local germ.

## 1 The positive smooth curvature patch

Choose a smooth cutoff chi taking values in[0,1], equal to1 on a neighbourhood I of1/2 and supported inside(1/4,3/4). Define

    r0(s)=1+chi(s)(s/(1−s)−1)

on the support neighbourhood and extend it by1 where chi vanishes. The apparent singularity at1 is outside the support and is removed by that constant extension. The result is globally smooth and strictly positive: on the support it is a convex combination of1 and the positive odds s/(1−s).

Let

    h(s)=0                              for s≤s0
    h(s)=exp(−1/(s−s0)²)                 for s>s0
    r1(s)=r0(s) exp(h(s)).

The one-sided h is smooth and all its derivatives at s0 vanish. It is strictly increasing to the right. Both r0 and r1 are everywhere strictly positive and smooth. Choose psi0 and psi1 as twice-integrated curvatures with the same value and first derivative at s0. They then have identical full jets there. Equivalently, integrate both from0 with the same constants: the curvatures coincide on the whole left half-line, so the normalisations agree at s0.

This gives potentials defined and smooth beyond both truth endpoints. No endpoint-singular generator is being smuggled into a theorem requiring a neighbourhood of the compact truth hull.

## 2 The baseline pair admits the exact RMS correction

Use transformed forecast coordinates t1=x,t2=1−y. Coherence means t1=t2=q. On I, r0(t)=t/(1−t). Write

    A(t)=D_psi0(0,t),     B(t)=D_psi0(1,t).

The forecast derivatives are

    A'(t)=t r0(t),        B'(t)=−(1−t)r0(t)=−t.

Consequently B(t)=constant−t²/2 on I. Put q=sqrt((t1²+t2²)/2). For t1,t2∈I, the RMS lies between them, so q∈I. The F0 truth1 comparison is equality.

For truth0, the function u↦A(sqrt(u)) has derivative

    r0(sqrt(u))/2 = sqrt(u)/[2(1−sqrt(u))],

which is increasing. Jensen's inequality yields2A(q)≤A(t1)+A(t2). Thus F0 weakly improves at both truths.

For the quadratic anchor the truth0 comparison is equality by the RMS definition. At truth1 the difference between coherent and baseline losses is proportional to mean(t1,t2)−q≤0. Thus the same q is a common weak repair. This reasoning applies to every sufficiently nearby forecast, not only to the selected ray.

## 3 The flat perturbation destroys exact repair on a fixed ray

Take t1=s0 and t2=s0+epsilon inside I. Let u0=t1²,u1=t2² and m=(u0+u1)/2. Quadratic truth0 dominance requires

    q²≤m.                                                    (A)

For F1, write B1(t)=D_psi1(1,t). On I,

    B1'(t)=−t exp(h(t)),
    B1(t)=constant−K(t²)/2,
    K'(u)=exp(h(sqrt(u))).

K' is strictly increasing on(u0,u1), so K is strictly convex on[u0,u1]. The F1 truth1 repair condition implies

    K(q²)≥[K(u0)+K(u1)]/2>K(m).                              (B)

A candidate q≤s0 already fails the F1 truth1 requirement: this loss is strictly decreasing on[0,1), while the average baseline includes the strictly larger forecast t2. Every remaining candidate allowed by A lies in(s0,sqrt(m)]⊂I, where the displayed K identity applies. Since K is increasing, A contradicts B. Therefore no coherent q works.

The argument uses exact strict convexity, not a floating-point distinction between the almost identical curvatures.

## 4 The obstruction is positive but beyond every algebraic order

The old RMS q=sqrt(m) still satisfies both quadratic comparisons. For F1 truth0, the derivative of u↦D_psi1(0,sqrt(u)) is r1(sqrt(u))/2, increasing on[s0,s0+epsilon]. Hence Jensen still gives truth0 improvement. Its only failing comparison is F1 truth1, with excess

    J(epsilon)=[K(u0)+K(u1)]/2−K(m)>0.

Set L=u1−u0=epsilon+epsilon². Since K' ranges from1 to exp(h(s0+epsilon)), elementary integration bounds the Jensen gap by

    0<J(epsilon)≤L[exp(h(s0+epsilon))−1]/2.

For small epsilon, exp(h)−1≤2h. Therefore

    J(epsilon)≤(epsilon+epsilon²) exp(−1/epsilon²),

which is o(epsilon^N) for every fixed natural N. All losses are continuous and the coherent interval is compact, so the actual minimax excess R1(epsilon) is attained. Nonexistence of an exact weak repair makes it strictly positive, while the RMS candidate gives

    0<R1(epsilon)≤J(epsilon)=o(epsilon^N)  for every N.

For the baseline pair R0(epsilon)=0: the quadratic truth0 and F0 truth1 constraints force the same RMS, where both tie. Thus every algebraic-order asymptotic test can return zero while one pair permits exact repair and the other does not. This does not assert differentiability of the minimax value at every order; the all-order asymptotic bound is sufficient.

Any fixed positive polynomial-scale worsening tolerance eventually covers this flat obstruction. Exact and approximately non-worsening correction therefore diverge at a genuinely finer scale than the already studied first- and second-order tests.

## 5 Which jets agree

The scalar generators, full forecast potentials, Hessians and all higher forecast-potential derivatives agree at p. Unnormalised Bregman state-loss values at p can differ by the truth-only constants psi1(v)−psi0(v). Those constants cancel in every repair comparison. If full state-loss jets including zeroth order are wanted, use the centred losses

    D_psii(v,b)−D_psii(v,p).

These have identical full forecast jets at p and exactly the same repair decisions as their respective uncentred losses. The distinction prevents a false claim about far-away truth-potential values.

## 6 Scope of the information obstruction

A procedure receiving only the full jet at p must give the same answer for the two inputs, so it cannot correctly decide their opposite exact-repair questions. A procedure that can inspect the supplied function away from p is not covered by this claim; the explicit formulas above allow an exact proof of the difference.

The result concerns the smooth class. In a genuinely analytic neighbourhood an entire infinite jet determines the local germ, so this particular all-order ambiguity disappears. It does not follow that finitely many derivatives always suffice. Replacing h locally by(s−s0)^(2m), with m arbitrarily large and normalising the integrations at s0, gives same prescribed finite jets but the same strict Jensen obstruction on the right. Both potentials can be analytic near p and globally smoothly patched. No additional claim of global analyticity across the full truth hull is needed or made for that variation.

For a polynomial class with a supplied degree bound, sufficiently many coefficients do determine the polynomial; an exact symbolic or semialgebraic procedure has more information than an unbounded-class finite-jet procedure.

## Programme connection

This locates the limit of trying to complete exact common-repair classification by adding higher and higher local derivative tests. Positive primal and negative dual second-order certificates remain sound. At criticality, a completeness theorem needs additional structure, such as an exact interval comparison, a restricted analytic representation, or direct function-level information. The supplied representation, tolerance scale and score domain are substantive parts of the research claim.

The phenomenon does not supply a metaphysical source conclusion. It illustrates the same disciplined distinction between a representation and the complete object represented: losing function-level information cannot be repaired merely by treating an arbitrarily rich derivative record as the object itself.
