# Counterexample attempts and scope probes

All records below are retained, including unsuccessful attacks on the valid bounded statement. The finite arithmetic controls supplement the analytic argument; they are not evidence of a physical experiment.

1. k=1 / smallest total budget L=r+1. The AM-GM sum is a single term; the second factor is (1-u)^2. Both inequalities remain valid. The second-factor maximizer lies at a boundary where the actual pair has identical laws, but a loose relaxation may have a nonzero boundary supremum. No counterexample.

2. Mixed zero/one rate vectors. A zero makes the added full route fail; a one makes at least one baseline singleton succeed surely. The full-profile endpoint laws agree in both cases, including vectors containing both extremes. No counterexample.

3. Proper issued profiles with arbitrary rates elsewhere. The extra route is inactive even if excluded roots have nominal rate one; issuance and attenuation are separate. No counterexample. Treating an unissued root as secretly active would change the model rather than the inequality.

4. Unequal rates, including rates close to zero and one. Rational controls check every factor of the bound, rather than only the final C comparison. Asymmetry is harmless: root 1 controls one denominator, root 2 the other, and all remaining roots supply their squared signal factors. No counterexample found; the analytic bound covers all interior values.

5. Adaptive, randomized action selection over profiles and rates. Exact branch enumeration checks a history-dependent policy with randomized actions and proper-profile branches. The full-transcript KL enclosure agrees with the sum of conditional KL enclosures and stays below N C. No counterexample. The proof itself covers continuum-valued rates, not merely these branch examples.

6. Unbalanced baseline, leaving some roots without singleton competitors. This attack succeeds against an attempted shortcut, not the proposed pair. If only root 1 has k singleton routes and every other root is set to rate one, the experiment reduces to distinguishing counts k and k+1. At r=3,k=20,a_1=1/21, exact log intervals certify a one-trial KL greater than the proposed multi-root C(3,20). Therefore an arbitrary-rate multi-root bound cannot simply reuse the old single-root-heavy mask baseline. The balanced baseline is essential to this proof.

7. Route-addressable gating. Outside the declared menu, turn off every singleton route while enabling the added full-support route. Then q0=1 and q1=0, and one endpoint perfectly distinguishes the hard pair. This confirms why rates must be shared by all incidences at each root. The theorem does not cover this enriched actuator.

8. Confidence approaching one half. For delta=(j-1)/(2j), j=10,100,1000, exact log enclosures show kl(1-delta,delta)/log(1/delta)<10/j^2. An unqualified conversion to a uniform log(1/delta) lower bound fails. Corrected scope: delta<=delta0<1/2 or a stated small-error regime.

9. Per-root versus total count. For all exact budget controls, H1 has rk+1<=L, rather than rL+1. Using k=L would violate the intended total bound. No problem with the actual floor construction.

10. More effects, all-mask upper, or growing dimension. All routes in the pair may emit the same known nonempty bundle, so extra endpoint effect coordinates add no information about this pair. However the inherited general upper contains Q=(3^r-2^r)(2^m-1) and cannot be described as dimension-free. The matching assertion fixes the declared observation class.

11. Finite expected stopping without a deterministic cap. A fixed-N KL calculation alone is insufficient for that new theorem. No such extension was attempted or approved here.

12. Correlated route responses or inaccurate calibration. The product expressions can fail despite correct marginal incidence rates. These are outside this lower theorem's stipulated laws and remain independent modeling obligations; the inherited robust upper has its own separately warranted error budget.

13. Perfectly shared random gates. Independently enumerating the four equally likely root-gate assignments at r=2,k=2,a=(1/2,1/2) gives q0=q1=1/4 when each random root gate is reused for every route incidence. The stipulated independent-route model instead gives 1/16 and 3/64. Thus common numerical rates must not be read as identical gate random variables. This is a response-kernel change, not a counterexample to the proved theorem.

14. A rate-uniform variance floor. Exact rational rate sequences near zero and near one produce q1(1-q1) tending to zero. A constant denominator lower bound would be invalid. The actual proof's two factor-dependent denominator bounds survive both limits, as required.
