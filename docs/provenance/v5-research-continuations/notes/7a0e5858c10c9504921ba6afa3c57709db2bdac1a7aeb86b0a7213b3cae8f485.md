# Uniform safety limits finite issuance at a degenerate norm endpoint

Ordinary theorem for the same Bernoulli audit model. It concerns a fixed positive disturbance and the closed weight family[0,1]. It is separate from the earlier shrinking-disturbance finite-query obstruction on strictly positive weights.

## Statement

Fix0<epsilon≤1/4. A shared measurable causal procedure observes fresh Bernoulli(alpha) bits and may use parameter-independent private randomness, independent of the Bernoulli stream. Its stopping and reporting events are measurable. It may wait or abstain. At its first issued report it outputs a real q, interpreted as the coherent pair(q,1−q) when0≤q≤1.

Let N0 be the number of observations before first issuance on the all-zero source(alpha=0), with infinity for nonissuance. Then

    liminf_(alpha down to0, alpha>0)
      P_alpha(first issued report is incorrect) ≥ P_0(N0<infinity).

'Incorrect' means it fails coherence or one of the two literal expected-score repair inequalities. Nonissuance itself is not counted as an incorrect report.

In particular, if first issuance is almost surely finite at alpha=0, its failure probability tends to1 as alpha approaches0 through positive values. A uniform bound delta<1 on the probability of any incorrect issued report therefore cannot coexist with almost-sure finite first issuance at that endpoint. More precisely, such a bound requires P_0(N0<infinity)≤delta.

The analogous conclusion holds at alpha=1. Its unique endpoint repair is q1=1/2+epsilon, while successful reports for alpha<1 lie in the punctured interval[q1−2epsilon(1−alpha),q1). The same proof uses an all-one prefix with probability alpha^K.

## 1 The moving successful-repair cell

For alpha>0, the successful coherent reports satisfy

    q²≤1/4+alpha epsilon(1+epsilon),
    (1−q)²≤1/4−alpha epsilon(1−epsilon).

The second inequality and coherence force q>1/2. The first, together with q+1/2≥1, gives

    q−1/2≤alpha epsilon(1+epsilon).

Hence

    G_alpha ⊆ (1/2, 1/2+C alpha],   C=epsilon(1+epsilon).

These punctured intervals decrease to the empty set as alpha decreases to0. In contrast, the endpoint cell is G_0={1/2}. The loss functions are continuous; the issue is the exact boundary of the non-worsening requirement, not a discontinuous definition of the score.

For any fixed finite measure Q on reports, Q(G_alpha) therefore tends to0. In particular, an atom at the endpoint's correct report does not help at positive alpha:1/2 is excluded from every G_alpha with alpha>0.

## 2 Causal coupling without an expected stopping-time assumption

Use one common private seed r with law rho. On the all-zero input let q0(r) be the first issued report whenever that report occurs; give it an arbitrary value otherwise. Put E_K={r:N0(r)≤K}.

For each n≤K, if the n acquired observation bits are zero and N0(r)=n, causality makes the procedure issue q0(r). The event refers only to that realised acquisition prefix. The seed is independent of the Bernoulli stream. Summing over the disjoint seed events gives

    P_alpha(first report incorrect)
      ≥ sum_(n≤K) (1−alpha)^n rho({N0=n} ∩ {q0 not in G_alpha})
      ≥ (1−alpha)^K rho(E_K ∩ {q0 not in G_alpha}).

One may equivalently couple using the first K coordinates of a latent infinite stream, but coordinates after issuance are not thereby observed. No unused future tail is acquired or inspected.

For each fixed K, the finite-measure moving-cell argument makes the right side tend to rho(E_K) as alpha decreases to0. Thus the liminf is at least rho(E_K) for every K. Monotone convergence of E_K to{N0<infinity} proves the statement.

The raw infinite Bernoulli(alpha) and Bernoulli(0) source laws are mutually singular for every alpha>0. No continuity of those whole-stream laws is asserted. Only each fixed finite zero-prefix probability approaches one.

No finite expected sample count, uniform stopping bound, or premise about a last probe is used. The first-issuance locality condition is the standard causal stopping contract. A procedure with computation delays can be treated the same way when finite issuance entails finitely many acquired observations and the report is determined by that finite information.

## 3 Consequences for the implemented certificate

The exact confidence-sequence procedure can abstain forever at alpha=0. This is not merely a defect caused by conservative numerical rounding: under a uniform all-issued error bound delta, its endpoint issuance probability cannot exceed delta. Its strict zero-issuance behaviour is compatible with that necessary bound.

This does not prevent finite issuance almost surely for every interior alpha. The first positive observation already separates an interior source from the zero endpoint with eventual probability one, while never arriving at the endpoint. Nor does it prevent uniform finite-confidence procedures on a supplied compact interior interval[a,1−a], where the oracle repair margin has a positive lower bound.

It also does not prevent a shared procedure from making finitely many wrong reports almost surely and then being permanently correct. An always-issued plug-in estimate may make early mistakes and has a different guarantee. The theorem concerns a uniform probability bound on even the first error, not merely eventual correctness.

The corresponding distinction for action policies is important: a safety certificate can be sound whenever it is issued without being a liveness certificate; an eventual-repair theorem can permit early errors without being a uniform all-time confidence guarantee.

## 4 Scope guards

- The closed parameter family imposes endpoint liveness. The finite-prefix argument also applies to an open family if finite issuance on the counterfactual all-zero stream is separately required; open-family safety alone need not require that issuance
- Epsilon is fixed and positive; shrinking epsilon gives a different identification problem
- The private seed and procedure are shared across parameters; an alpha-coded seed would invalidate the coupling
- Only actual fresh observed bits are counted; selective logging or changing source laws needs another model
- The compared success cells concern expected audit charge at each truth, not every realised selector charge
- No historical novelty claim is made for the underlying finite-experiment continuity argument
