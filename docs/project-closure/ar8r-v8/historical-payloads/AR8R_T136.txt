AR8R-T136 — Finite policy stabilization does not establish future completeness
Theorem

Let X be a fixed finite state set and let P_0 subseteq P_1 subseteq ... be an increasing sequence of registered policy families. Their indistinguishability kernels form a descending chain and can strictly refine at most |X|-1 times.

Even after the chain stabilizes, the current quotient is safe for a later policy p* exactly when the behavior of p* factors through the stabilized quotient. Stabilization alone does not prove that condition.
Proof

The first claim is T128. For the second, apply the factorization criterion to the stabilized quotient map and p*'s behavior. A counterexample is any pair merged by the quotient but separated by p*. ∎
Consequence for closure

A finite period with no new registered policy distinction does not authorize the universal claim that no future policy can expose a hidden burden. Closure must be relative to the declared policy family and state domain, or it needs an independent completeness theorem for future admissible policies.
