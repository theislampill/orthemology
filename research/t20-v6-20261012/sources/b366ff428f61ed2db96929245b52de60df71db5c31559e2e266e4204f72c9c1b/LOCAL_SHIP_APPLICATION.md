## Result

**A particular local reason is supplied, not merely postulated: creating a defect in this ship is intended to frustrate the disclosed seizure threat while preserving the owners' use of their means of livelihood.** The factual threat, the preservation objective, the means–end relation, and the agent's stated intention are independently distinguishable. The explanation therefore passes the non-choice-encoding test at the declared target: why this kind of intervention rather than leaving this ship sound in that situation.

The disclosure does not derive an exact damage geometry, the uniquely best intervention among every possible alternative, the ultimate selection of the whole undertaking, or the individuation of a divine intrinsic volition. Its bounded explanatory success does not require those stronger results. This is a concrete positive application supplementing the narrow restriction in REPORT.md.

## 2. The disclosed argument and the indispensable interpretive premise

The narrative first identifies a servant given mercy and knowledge (65), then reports the ship intervention and Moses' objection (71). The later speech announces an explanation (78) and relates the ship's poor working owners, the intention to introduce a defect, and the king's seizure policy (79). The concluding statement denies that these actions were taken on the speaker's own initiative (82).

The unqualified surface wording “every ship” in 79 must not be used as if the text explicitly says “every sound ship.” Ibn Kathīr supplies that restriction in his explanation: the king seizes sound/good ships; introducing a defect deters him; the owners continue to benefit from theirs. Without this restriction, damage would not be shown to prevent seizure, and the instrumental derivation would fail. This is an explicit exegetical premise, not a silently inserted fact.

The episode does not narrate a subsequent inspection by the king or independently observed continued use. The anti-seizure and continued-benefit claims here are the disclosed intended means rationale, with its causal interpretation adopted from Ibn Kathīr; they are not a new measured achieved-outcome report. Crediting the canonical disclosure and adopting this commentary's sound-ship restriction are distinct evidential steps.

The commentary's appended report about the king's name is unnecessary. Its returned apparatus marks one chain weak. Neither that name nor any ancillary genealogical assertion is adopted or used. The central means explanation is the commentary's own explanation of the verse.

## 3. Executed local derivation

Fix the disclosed situation and this objective: preserve the working owners' use of their ship against the specified seizure threat. Compare two described courses only:

- U: leave the ship sound through the relevant encounter.
- D: introduce the defect of the kind described in the account, while the owners retain a usable asset.

The premises are:

1. The owners' continued use is an intelligible benefit with content independent of the intervention chosen. The source identifies their work and need; the commentary expressly states continued benefit as the point.
2. Under the disclosed threat, soundness exposes this ship to the king's seizure.
3. The relevant defect deters that seizure while leaving the owners with the benefit of their ship. This is the commentary's case-specific causal interpretation, not a general law of damaged ships.
4. Preventing seizure preserves a good that seizure would remove, despite the local cost of the defect.
5. The speaker expressly relates his intention to create the defect to these facts. The relevant end is not inferred solely from an advantageous outcome.

From 2–3, D and U differ in their means–end relation to the stated preservation objective. From 1 and 4, this difference is a practical reason favoring D over U **in that respect**. Premise 5 supplies the actual basing claim within the accepted disclosure: the intervention is described as undertaken for this reason, rather than merely coinciding with it.

The result is stronger than “there might be some reason.” It also goes beyond a passive record that damage occurred. It identifies what makes this otherwise puzzling intervention intelligible and why leaving the ship sound is not equally effective relative to the disclosed objective and threat.

It is weaker than a proof of all-things-considered unique optimality. Nothing in the premises establishes that D was the sole available means, that every imaginable alternative was worse, or that the exact degree and timing of damage have been completely causally derived. Within the inherited authenticated-disclosure premise, the narrative warrants the local wise intervention; the displayed practical derivation makes one genuine part of that warrant intelligible without claiming an exhaustive decision calculation.

## 4. Choice-encoding and sensitivity checks

This application supplies a **local means-level instance of the non-choice-encoding standard**, not the missing intrinsic ultimate-content H in REPORT.md.

**The local differentiator is the relation between the king's seizure policy, the ship's condition, and the owners' continued use.**

That local differentiator is not “the representation whose content is D,” nor “whatever wisdom justified the chosen act.” Ownership, livelihood, and the selection criterion of the threatening agent can be stated without naming the intervention. The causal link makes their relevance testable at the argument level.

Remove the premise that the defect deters seizure, and the displayed preservation reason no longer favors D over U. Remove the threat, and the same defect no longer has this anti-seizure rationale. These are premise-deletion checks of the inference, not assertions that the alternative histories are genuine metaphysical possibilities, that the entire divine decree remains fixed, or that every other reason for an intervention would disappear.

That sensitivity is why the account is explanatory. If every conceivable condition equally licensed D under the very same stated reason, the alleged differentiator would add no particular discrimination. Here the reason has a stated success condition and a clear failure condition.

The fact that the explanation is reported after the act does not make it circular. Epistemic access after an event differs from defining the reason by the event. Its content and means–end relation are independently intelligible; its being the actual reason rests on the retained disclosure premise. Without that premise, the text would supply an intelligible attributed explanation rather than independently verified access to the actor's practical state.

Verse 82's denial of acting on his own initiative places the episode within disclosed divine direction; it does not make the servant's intrinsic act numerically God's, erase received agency, or provide a one-to-one inventory of divine volition-tokens.

Accordingly the positive original-scope conclusion is modest but real: **an originally directed undertaking has a disclosed particular means rationale**. The source does not leave every particular purpose undisclosed. This supports the applicability of wise, reason-sensitive direction while preserving the act/product and original/derivative distinctions already defended.

The seized-or-preserved ship is an extramental created object at the relevant episode. Its condition is a genuine circumstance for the derivative intervention. It is not thereby an independently original donor of God's essential knowledge, power, or wisdom. Nor should the local threat be promoted into an ultimate explanation of why the whole created order and this threat were selected. The two targets differ.

This case does not fill REPORT.md's missing ultimate-content premise, and it need not do so to count as a successful local explanation. Demanding the entire original history before crediting this disclosed discrimination would repeat the very scope error the existing CP23 achieved-end result already guards against.

## 6. The wall: a directly attributed original end

The wall case gives a stronger link to the declared original-scope target. Verse 77 reports a wall about to collapse, the servant's repair, and Moses' suggestion of payment after the town refused hospitality. Verse 82 explicitly attributes to the Lord the intention that the two young beneficiaries reach maturity and recover their treasure, describing it as mercy. It also distinguishes that divine end from the servant's deed and denies the deed's autonomous origination in his own command.

Ibn Kathīr's own explanation links the repair to the wall's belonging to the two orphans and their treasure beneath it. His commentary on the final denial says the servant had been directed and informed. The commentary separately reports disputed descriptions of the treasure and varying claims about ancestry and the servant's status. None of those ancillary claims enters this argument. The exact identification of the treasure's material or contents is unnecessary.

The local target is: **why restore this wall as part of preserving these beneficiaries' eventual recovery, despite the town's refusal of hospitality?** The derivation is:

1. The disclosed end is these beneficiaries' future recovery at maturity. It is not defined as “whatever the wall repair achieves.”
2. Their treasure is beneath a wall presently tending to collapse. The relation between their asset and that structure is independently described.
3. Repair restores the protective structure rather than leaving its impending collapse unaddressed. Interpreting that repair as preserving the site for later recovery is the practical means inference supported by the stated end and Ibn Kathīr's explanatory link. The passage does not supply a universal engineering law or an explicit certainty that thieves would otherwise seize the treasure.
4. The end belongs to these beneficiaries; it is not compensation for the townspeople's hospitality. Consequently the town's refusal does not cancel this beneficiary-directed reason. This does not establish that charging any fee would have been impossible or forbidden.
5. The narrative explicitly attributes the future-directed end to the Lord, while locating the repair in the servant's action. Under the inherited disclosure warrant, the intentional link is actual within the account, not an explanatory motive we invent after noticing a benefit.

This yields a particular, future-directed means rationale with original attribution of the end. It is stronger at that scope than transferring Khidr's first-person “I intended” directly to a divine act. It does not establish a divine volition-token corresponding one-to-one to each repair, or derive an exact age, day, or total history. The repair is not identical with the later treasure recovery; present means and future achievement are distinguishable without treating the original as ignorant of the outcome.

The threatened wall's condition and the beneficiaries' immaturity supply a concrete local practical relation: preserve this asset through the intervening period for their own later access. Removing the treasure/beneficiary relation removes this particular rationale for the repair; it does not prove that no other reason for repairing a wall could exist. Again this is premise sensitivity, not a licensed change to a complete divine decree.

## 7. Strongest rival and noncircular evidential use

A rival may say that the Creator could prevent the king's seizure without damaging the ship, or convey the treasure later without this wall. Neither disclosed means account establishes those alternative arrangements to be impossible. They therefore do not answer a claim of unique best possible original history, and neither report makes that claim.

A stronger local objection would identify a genuinely available, equally effective, less costly intervention under the same relevant background. That could challenge an all-things-considered justification of D, rather than merely the global choice of context. The displayed argument has not excluded such an alternative; its conclusion is the stated D-over-U reason in one respect, together with the disclosure-based actual-basing claim.

The rival has not thereby refuted the narrower explanatory relation. Given the described threat, asset, beneficiaries and disclosed objective, the selected means has an intelligible purpose. Showing that an end might be obtained through other arrangements does not show that the actual means was unrelated to that end. The actual-basing premise is supplied by the narrated explanation and the inherited disclosure warrant, not by a finite model exhibiting a possible benefit.

Nor is the account an independent proof of the source's veracity or general Wisdom. Those warrants are among the background on which the disclosure is credited. The positive gain is an application: once that evidence is admitted, particular reasons are disclosed and intelligible at the local target. Treating the same example as a fresh independent proof of the premises used to credit its testimony would be circular. Conversely, excluding all such applications merely because they do not prove their own testimony framework would erase ordinary warranted use of a source.

## 8. Earned change

The new addition is a worked local means application across the two complementary cases with an identified interpretive premise, actual-basing evidence under the inherited disclosure warrant, and two premise-deletion controls. It gives a concrete instance of the local H requirement rather than leaving that requirement as an indefinitely unspecified placeholder.

The inherited general thesis that local explanations are possible is not new. The new application differs from CP23's accomplished-test case: there the attained end removes a claimed need for a later ostensible transfer; here a disclosed threat changes which condition of an existing asset serves its owners' continued use, and a protected treasure supplies an explicitly Lord-directed future end for repairing a different structure. Neither is a unique complete-history derivation.

The conclusions are complementary: representations cannot automatically explain their own ultimate selection merely by containing a particular; nevertheless a disclosed, independently described practical relation can genuinely explain a particular directed means. No general permission for present-day interference with others' property or for other harms is inferred. No protected judgement is reopened.
