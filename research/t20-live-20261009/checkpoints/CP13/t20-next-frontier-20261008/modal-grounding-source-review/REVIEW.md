Printed 152 applies this to all individualistic manifold facts and expressly retains manifold regions. Notes 71–73 defer fuller argument and identify a change in grounding's logical form. ### 2. Necessitation is conditional and does not distribute grounds

Printed 119 explicitly denies the converse from modal necessitation to grounding. Printed 152 invokes a plural modification of Necessitation to establish agreement on individualistic facts wherever the qualitative state obtains. The source's consequence is stronger than mere agreement on an unlabelled isomorphism class, but still conditional on that state's obtaining.

From the proposed joint ground, each member's truth follows at Q-worlds. The additional transition to a singleton grounding assertion is exactly the right-distribution rule the proposal rejects. The report does not infer necessary Q, existence through one's own essence, or productive nonreceipt from that conditional truth.

Note 10 supplies only the original single-output transitivity schema and treats transitivity/irreflexivity as disputable assumptions. The mapping correctly observes that this schema does not itself supply the missing singleton intermediate premise. Its whole-plurality composition schema is explicitly labelled a reconstruction, not attributed as a fully specified author-endorsed calculus. No formal nonderivability or consistency result for every possible plural calculus is earned or claimed.

### 3. Counterpart semantics is not hidden global-bearer semantics

Printed 127–130 distinguishes qualitative individuating roles, counterpart theories and thin/thick substantivalism. Dasgupta grants a counterpart response for the discussion while noting uncertainty about the precise version used by Brighouse. These are not all endorsements of his own metaphysics.

Note 11 explicitly substitutes a sentential Necessitation schema when cross-world obtaining of fact-objects is not well defined, citing the counterpart setting. The reviewed texts preserve that warning. Their fact-domain notation is explanatory shorthand; it is not a supplied construction of global rigid identities, world-to-world counterpart maps, or an AG modal semantics. The global `B` interpretation in the inherited Lean code still requires separate authentication.
