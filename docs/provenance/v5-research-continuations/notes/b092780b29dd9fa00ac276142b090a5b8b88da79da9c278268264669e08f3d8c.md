# Markov acceptance with revealing support changes

## Result and qualification

The candidate recursive criterion is correct for the finite experiment below. Its winning regions characterise both almost-sure eventual acceptance and existence of a common policy with finite expected total bad actions. The main new proof obligation is finite expected navigation time when a candidate predicts a revealing exit that the actual model cannot take. A completed-event counting argument resolves that obligation without assuming iid state trajectories or conditioning a Hoeffding bound on a data-selected sample count.

The proof resets statistical counters on entry into an operating component and allocates half of an attempt's false-rejection allowance to each stage. This is a concrete policy choice; it does not change the candidate's finite criterion.

This is a complete ordinary mathematical argument submitted for independent review. It is not a new Lean theorem or a historical-priority claim. The accepted common-support theorem and its audit remain separate and unchanged.

## 1. Experiment and exact recursion

Let Θ, S and A be finite nonempty sets. A fixed unknown θ∈Θ determines known rational transition rows Pθ(s,a,y), each a probability distribution on S. The controller observes states and its own actions. It does not observe θ or the literal acceptance bit. Gθ⊆S×A is the set of accepted pairs in model θ.

The controller tracks the nonempty live support B⊆Θ. After selecting a at state s and observing y, it updates

    C(B,s,a,y) = {η∈B : Pη(s,a,y)>0}.

The actual model remains live almost surely, so the empty update is impossible. Hard menus Γ(B,s)⊆A are known and may depend arbitrarily on the observed live support and state. Empty menus are permitted and cannot support an infinite lawful run. Policies can use the entire observed history and newly supplied independent private randomisation, sufficient to make the uniform choices below. They have no additional hidden information correlated with θ. The theorem does not assert realisation through every preassigned private-seed distribution, including a Dirac seed with no other randomness. A deterministic scheduling refinement would require a separate construction.

Write Badθ=Σt≥0 1[(St,At)∉Gθ]. The aim is one common policy for every θ initially in B. Almost-sure eventual acceptance means Badθ<∞ almost surely. No observed stopping certificate of permanent correctness is required.

Compute regions W_C for nonempty supports C in increasing cardinality. For a current support B, suppose every proper nonempty C⊊B has already been processed. For W⊆S define A_B,W(s), for s∈W, by all a∈Γ(B,s) satisfying:

1. if C(B,s,a,y) is nonempty and proper, then y∈W_C(B,s,a,y);
2. if C(B,s,a,y)=B, then y∈W.

These conditions range over every possible y, not only outcomes possible under a favoured candidate. Thus every revealing successor is safe for its surviving family, and every nonrevealing successor stays in the current region.

The internal support of (s,a) is

    I_B(s,a) = {y : C(B,s,a,y)=B}.

Every internal edge is positive under every model in B. The internal support may be empty. Define qθ(s,a) as the θ-probability of a nonempty proper support update. It is the sum of Pθ(s,a,y) over such y.

A candidate component D=(C,U) has a nonempty state set C⊆W, nonempty U(s)⊆A_B,W(s), internal closure I_B(s,a)⊆C, and a strongly connected graph of its internal edges. For a target of model θ additionally require:

- qθ(s,a)=0 on every used pair;
- every used pair is in Gθ;
- every σ∈B whose **full** transition rows equal θ's on every used pair also accepts every used pair.

The zero-exit condition ensures that these internal rows are normalized θ-transition rows, so a target is a genuine θ-end component. Other models may have revealing exits from it. Equality of conditional rows after renormalising on “no reveal” is not the definition and would give a different, incorrect test (§7).

Let Tθ(W) be the union of these component states. Let Eθ(W) contain states with some action in A_B,W(s) having qθ(s,a)>0. The graph operator R_B(W) consists of the states in W from which every θ∈B has an internal-edge path to Tθ(W)∪Eθ(W). Zero-length paths are allowed. Descend from W0=S by Wj+1=R_B(Wj); the stable region is W_B.

For fixed lower-support regions, the action sets, targets and graph-reachability regions are monotone in W. The operator contracts W and hence stabilises after at most |S| strict decreases. This is a greatest fixed point inside a bottom-up recursion on |B|, rather than a single reachability pass.

## 2. The theorem

For every nonempty B⊆Θ and state s, these are equivalent:

1. s∈W_B;
2. one common lawful policy from (B,s) has Badθ<∞ almost surely for every θ∈B;
3. one common lawful policy from (B,s) has Eθ[Badθ]<∞ for every θ∈B.

The constructed policy has a uniform finite bound over all actual models, winning initial states and nonempty initial supports, for the one supplied finite system. It may use unbounded memory. The statement does not say that every almost-surely winning policy has finite expectation.

We prove the equivalences simultaneously by induction on |B|. Proper-support policies and budgets used at the current level are supplied by the induction hypothesis. Empty support is never a recursive premise or a realised continuation.

## 3. Necessity

Let V_B be the states admitting a common almost-surely winning policy when the live support is B. Assume inductively that V_C=W_C for every proper nonempty C⊊B.

### 3.1 Positive histories and safe continuations

At a positive observed history, the live support is exactly the set of initially possible models assigning positive probability to that history. For a common behavioural policy, its action-probability factors are model-independent; the transition factors determine which models survive. A common independent private seed gives the same behavioural representation: after fixing the observed action/state history, its model-dependent transition likelihood factors out of conditional seed weighting.

The residual policy after such a history wins under every surviving model. A positive conditional failure under any survivor, multiplied by its positive prefix mass, would contradict its original probability-one guarantee. Consequently, at histories whose support is B, each action used with positive probability has all same-B successors in V_B and all proper-support successors y in V_C=W_C. It therefore belongs to A_B,V_B.

### 3.2 A revealing exit or a zero-exit recurrent component

Fix actual θ∈B. If a proper support exit has positive probability, there is a finite positive history ending at a state with an allowed action whose θ-exit probability is positive. The preceding same-B transitions give a graph path to Eθ(V_B).

Otherwise, or on a positive-probability event with no exit, examine the pairs used infinitely often while remaining at B. With probability one on that event they form an internally closed strongly connected component. In particular an infinitely repeated pair cannot have qθ>0: each repeated use would have a fixed positive chance of leaving B, so avoiding exit forever has probability zero. The same repeated-trial argument makes every positive internal successor recur, and after finitely occurring pairs disappear, paths on the run give strong connectivity.

Eventual acceptance makes every recurrent pair θ-good. There are finitely many recurrent pair sets, so every positive no-exit event contains a positive-probability event with one fixed recurrent component D.

### 3.3 Full-row matching transfers the tail

Suppose σ∈B agrees with θ on all full rows of D. Choose a finite positive prefix after which only pairs of D are used and every such pair recurs; the countable union over possible prefixes supplies one with positive mass. Its live support remains B, so σ also assigns positive probability to it.

Kill the residual process at the first selected pair outside D or first proper support update. The θ and σ laws before killing have identical transition rows and the same action kernels. They agree on finite cylinders and therefore on the measurable event of staying in D and visiting every used pair infinitely often. Equality of rows also gives qσ=0 on D. A σ-bad recurrent pair would therefore cause failure on a positive σ-probability event, a contradiction.

Thus D is a qualifying target component. It is reached by a same-B graph path. For each θ either a positive exit event or a positive no-exit recurrent event supplies a path to Eθ(V_B) or Tθ(V_B). Therefore V_B⊆R_B(V_B), and monotonicity gives V_B⊆W_B.

This argument does not condition the transition process on avoiding exit and then call that conditioned process Markov or iid. The repeated-trial and killed-law arguments are made in the original process.

## 4. A navigation lemma that handles absent candidate exits

Fix B, its nonempty fixed point W=W_B, a candidate φ∈B and actual θ∈B. The baseline navigation policy μ selects uniformly among A_B,W(s), until it reaches Tφ(W) or makes a proper support exit. It is well-defined at every state of W: a path to a target or positive-exit state supplies an action, and a target itself has a component action.

Let N=|S|, a=|A|, M=|S||A|, and let p>0 be the smallest positive probability in any supplied transition row. Use the global conservative constants

    q=(p/a)^N,       L=N/q.

Let H be the set of allowed navigation pairs whose full rows differ between θ and φ; put d=|H|≤M. The controller need not know H. It tests all sampled rows against the candidate. For each fixed actual θ, H is a fixed set of observed pairs, so the event times defined from H below are legitimate stopping times in that model's observed-process filtration. They are analysis devices, not oracle inputs to the controller.

### 4.1 A bounded-block event

From any current state, the fixed-point condition gives a simple internal path to Tφ(W) or Eφ(W), of length at most |W|−1. Under θ, each edge of that path is positive because it leaves B unchanged.

If it ends in Tφ, following the path terminates navigation. If it ends at a φ-positive exit pair e, either e∈H, or its full row is equal under θ and φ. In the former case, selecting e completes a sample of a mismatching row, regardless of its realised successor. In the latter case, some revealing successor has positive θ-probability as well. Taking that successor terminates navigation safely.

Consequently, within at most N physical actions there is conditional probability at least q of reaching the target, taking a proper support exit, or completing an H-row sample. If a statistical test rejects earlier, that also terminates the attempt. The conclusion holds conditional on every active history. Repeating it over blocks bounds the conditional mean waiting time to the next such event by L.

This is the exact repair for a candidate's nonexistent actual exit: sampling its exit-predicting row supplies statistical evidence even when the revealing successor never appears.

### 4.2 Fresh row tapes and a rational threshold

Let Δ be the least positive absolute coordinate difference between any two supplied model rows at the same state-action pair. If there is none, set Δ=1; then H is always empty. Put

    c=Δ²/2,       r=1/(1+c),       0<r<1.

For each fresh statistical stage allocate independent next-state sample tapes with the actual row laws, consuming a tape entry when its pair is selected. This realises the Markov experiment. The whole observed state trajectory is not iid, but each row's unused tape remains fresh conditional on the past.

Fix ε∈(0,1). Choose n0≥1 by exact rational arithmetic so that

    2 M N r^n0/(1−r) ≤ ε/2.                             (1)

After every completed sample k≥n0 of a pair, reject the candidate if any empirical coordinate differs from its candidate row by at least Δ/2. Since exp(−c)≤r, Hoeffding's inequality gives a two-sided deviation bound 2r^k at each deterministic sample count k. Union over pairs, coordinates and k≥n0 proves false-rejection probability at most ε/2 whenever all tested rows match. In particular this holds throughout a true-candidate navigation stage, conditional on its starting history.

### 4.3 Completed-event filtration and the selected-count issue

Call a completed navigation event either (i) a completed H-row sample, or (ii) termination by target entry, support exit or rejection. A transition can satisfy both; a terminal event ends the sequence. Let J be the number of completed events up to and including the terminal one. If navigation starts inside its target, take J=0 and duration zero. Otherwise put τ_0=0 and extend event times after termination by the terminal time. Each event time is a stopping time for the observed process. Whether J exceeds j is measurable at the j-th completed-event time, so J is a stopping time for that completed-event filtration.

On {J>j}, the first j events were nonterminal H-row samples and no test has rejected. For j≥dn0 and d>0, one mismatching row has been sampled k≥ceil(j/d) times. Fix for each such row one coordinate whose true/candidate gap is at least Δ. Survival at that count forces a true empirical deviation of at least Δ/2.

The row and its count are data-selected. We therefore take an unconditional union over every row e∈H and **every deterministic** k≥ceil(j/d), on the allocated tapes. We do not apply a fixed-k Hoeffding statement after conditioning on the selected count. This gives

    P(J>j | stage-start history)
      ≤ 2 d r^ceil(j/d)/(1−r),       j≥dn0.              (2)

Summing (2), with the trivial bound before dn0, yields

    E[J] ≤ d n0 + 2 d² r^n0/(1−r)²   when d>0.

When d=0, the next completed event is terminal and E[J]≤1. Thus a uniform bound for every actual/candidate pair is

    Jnav = 1 + M n0 + 2 M² r^n0/(1−r)².               (3)

Let τ_j be the successive completed-event times. Conditional on survival after event j−1, §4.1 bounds the mean wait to event j, including a terminal event, by L. Since {J≥j} is measurable at time τ_(j−1), conditional expectation and monotone convergence give

    E[τ_J] = Σj≥1 E[1{J≥j}(τ_j−τ_(j−1))] ≤ L E[J] ≤ L Jnav. (4)

One can define the differences only on survival, with zero contribution thereafter. The block bound makes each required next event finite almost surely; (2) makes the terminal event index finite almost surely. This also justifies the stopped-sum representation before asserting finite expectation.

Equation (4) bounds the entire navigation duration, not only the bad portion. It needs neither a BSCC-entry conditioning argument nor independence of event counts and inter-event waiting times.

## 5. Operating stages and complete recursive policy

Cycle through the candidates in B. For each candidate φ:

1. Start fresh navigation counters and use μ until rejection, a support exit, or entry into Tφ(W)
2. On rejection, start the next candidate from the current state
3. On a support exit to (C,y), immediately use the already constructed policy for that winning lower-support state
4. On target entry, select a qualifying component containing the state, reset counters once, and operate uniformly among its U(s). Use the same threshold test; rejection starts the next candidate, and a revealing exit invokes the lower-support policy

Proper exits take precedence over rejection if both are detected in the same transition; either ordering would leave the allowed action lawful, but this choice makes the recursive handoff explicit. A target already containing the initial navigation state requires no navigation action.

### 5.1 Three operating cases

Fix actual θ and a selected φ-component D.

**Matching full rows.** Self-verification makes every operated pair θ-good. Since qφ=0, row equality gives qθ=0, so no support exit occurs. The fresh operating test falsely rejects with probability at most ε/2; any time before that rejection costs zero bad actions.

**A θ-positive support exit exists in U.** Internal paths are positive under θ and remain in D until a revealing exit. Uniform operation has conditional probability at least q of taking such an exit within N steps. Thus the expected time to exit is at most L, and tests can terminate the attempt earlier. Its bad cost is at most that duration.

**No θ-positive exit, but some full row differs.** Now D is a finite irreducible true-model chain under uniform U. For analysis, continue this uniform component policy while ignoring every test, thereby defining an uninterrupted chain and its complete row tapes. Choose one differing coordinate and let K be the first sample count at which that coordinate alone would reject. For k≥n0

    P(K>k) ≤ 2r^k,       E[K] ≤ n0+2r^n0/(1−r) =: Jop.

From every component history the mean time to the next completed sample of its row is at most L. The event {K≥k} is determined after the previous sample. The same conditional stopped-sum argument gives mean rejection time at most L Jop. Tests of other rows can reject sooner.

These cases exhaust all possibilities. Hence every potentially harmful operating stage has conditional expected bad cost at most L Jop, since Jop≥1. Matching stages can last forever harmlessly.

### 5.2 Candidate cycles and lower-support budgets

For the true candidate φ=θ, navigation rejects falsely with probability at most ε/2. Conditional on target entry, the fresh operating stage rejects falsely with probability at most ε/2. Without such a rejection, the attempt either hands off to a winning lower support or remains forever in its actually good component. Thus continuing through a true-candidate attempt without a successful handoff or harmless permanent operation has conditional probability at most ε.

Every completed candidate cycle contains a true-candidate attempt. Iterated conditioning therefore bounds the expected number of attempts begun at support B by |B|/(1−ε). From (4) and the operating cases, their local expected bad cost is at most

    C_B = |B| L (Jnav+Jop)/(1−ε).                      (5)

Only one proper-support handoff ends the current level. Its next-state continuation starts after the already counted transition, so there is no double counting. If H_C is a uniform finite budget for the already constructed policy on W_C, set

    H_B = C_B + max{H_C : ∅≠C⊊B and W_C≠∅},           (6)

with the maximum of the empty set equal to zero. This closes the induction. All constants in (1), (3), (5) and (6) can be rational. Using the global constants above also gives the simpler uniform bound

    H_B ≤ |B|(|B|+1) L (Jnav+Jop) / [2(1−ε)].         (7)

Finite expectation implies almost-sure finiteness of the nonnegative bad count. Together with necessity, this completes the theorem.

## 6. Exact crosswalks and computation

### 6.1 Common positive supports

If all initially live models have common positive supports at every lawful pair, no nonempty proper update occurs. Then Eθ is empty, qθ=0, and the allowed actions are exactly the old W-safe menu. The operator and full-row self-verification are the accepted common-support Markov definitions. The recursion is unused. This is an exact special case, not just a similarity of controller descriptions.

### 6.2 Controlled iid RecursiveWinning

Suppose Pθ(s,a,y)=Pθ(a,y), Gθ(s,a) depends only on a, and Γ(B,s)=Γ(B). Let the observed state be the last receipt, with any supplied initial state. For each support B the new region is either all of S or empty.

Inductively, every proper W_C is S exactly when the inherited controlled-iid recursive predicate holds for C. Therefore A_B,S is precisely the inherited `recursiveAllowed` action set: every possible nonempty proper receipt successor must already win.

For each θ, a positive-progress action makes Eθ(S)=S. If there is no such action, all allowed actions have qθ=0. A Markov target component then yields a nonempty self-verifying action set by taking the union of its used action labels; state independence makes full-row matching and acceptance exactly the inherited action conditions.

Conversely, given a nonempty inherited self-verifying action set I with no θ-progress, let C be the union of θ-receipt supports of its actions and use every a∈I at every state of C. Each such row stays in C; the union graph reaches every state of C from every state in one step. It is a qualifying component. Every initial receipt-state has an allowed edge into C. Thus R_B(S)=S when every model has either progress or such a self-verifying action set. If a model has neither, its target and exit sets are empty and R_B(S)=∅.

This is exactly the witness/stage equivalence expressed by the retained `RecursiveWinningCertificate.lean` and `recursiveWinning_iff_stage` in `RecursiveKernelEquivalence.lean`. Those prior Lean results are credited as existing results. They do not by themselves kernel-check the stateful theorem proved here.

### 6.3 MEC targets and algorithmic ceiling

For each B,W,θ start with allowed pairs that are θ-good and have qθ=0. Their θ-transition supports consist entirely of same-B edges. The accepted repeated-MEC-deletion proof therefore applies: compute MECs, find models in B matching every used full row, delete pairs those models reject, and recompute. Every valid subcomponent survives, and the stable outputs are exactly the maximal qualifying targets.

Eθ is exact positivity of a finite rational sum. Internal reachability is a finite graph search. There are at most |S| strict region decreases per B and at most M pair deletions in a target computation. Across at most 2^|Θ|−1 nonempty supports, the procedure takes 2^|Θ| times a polynomial in the explicit state/action/model/rational data. No polynomial-in-|Θ| claim is made. With a fully listed support-indexed menu table, this is polynomial per table entry up to the stated ordinary graph operations; a succinct menu representation requires its own complexity analysis.

This complexity statement concerns finite graph-region decision only. It does not include the potentially long integer search for n0, the size of its rational expressions, or the physical statistical runtime. The proof also supplies a statistical policy construction, but does not show that its expected cost bound or controller memory is small.

## 7. Boundary controls

The controls retain the common-support informative and indistinguishable examples and add the following support-changing cases.

**Pure revealing identification.** An initial action sends θ0 to u0 and θ1 to u1; the terminal rows are identical across models. Each revealed singleton has an accepted continuation. The recursive initial state wins even though whole-family self-verification of each terminal component fails. This is precisely the failure of the old unrefined criterion outside common supports.

**Losing child.** Change one revealed terminal so its surviving singleton has no accepted continuation. The initial revealing action is forbidden by the child-region condition. Model elimination is useful only when its observed successor is itself winning.

**A candidate exit absent under the actual model.** At state 0, both available actions under φ return to 0 with probability 1−η and reveal φ by reaching state 1 with probability η>0. Under θ they always return to 0. State 1 is good for φ; at state 0 only action b is good for θ. Then φ can win by revelation and θ by the self-verifying b-component. During a false φ-navigation attempt under θ there is no exit or φ-target entry. However every sampled row differs; once either action has n0 samples, the test rejects. With two actions this occurs within 2n0−1 physical steps, pathwise. Continuing untested navigation would use bad action a infinitely often.

The example works for every rational η>0. At η=0 the rows coincide, revelation disappears and the opposite requirements can make the initial state lose. Exact zero versus positive mass is therefore material; no numerical tolerance may silently replace it.

**Full rows versus conditional no-exit rows.** With one action, θ0 remains at state 0 and accepts the action there. θ1 stays at 0 with probability 1/2 and reveals itself by moving to 1 with probability 1/2; it accepts only at 1. Both terminal rows self-loop. Repeating the action wins, with zero bad count under θ0 and mean bad count 2 under θ1. The component at 0 is self-verifying for θ0 because the full rows differ. Their conditional no-exit rows are identical; treating those as the matching criterion would wrongly delete this necessary target.

**State- and support-dependent menus.** A probe lawful before revelation may be replaced by different accepted actions in each revealed singleton state. Correct handoff uses Γ(C,y), not the earlier Γ(B,s). Conversely every outgoing proper-support state is checked against its own W_C, rather than against an unindexed union of all known winning states.

**Irreversible traps and opposite identical targets.** The accepted common-support counterexamples remain losing under this extension. The new revealing progress option does not authorise guessing, unsafe child entries, or a one-pass viability approximation.

## 8. Evidence and ancestry

The inherited controlled-iid recursion already handles strict support decreases, admissible child continuations and finite lifetime bad cost. The accepted Markov theorem already handles same-support recurrent components, full-row self-verification and a viability greatest fixed point. This note proves their stateful support-changing combination and isolates its new quantitative navigation obligation.

Chadès and colleagues' 2012 hidden-model MDP already has a finite static hidden model, visible states, model-indexed transition and reward functions, and belief updates from observed transitions. Thus hidden model-dependent utility is established modelling ancestry. Their Proposition 1 concerns finite-horizon value-threshold planning; it does not by itself decide the present infinite-horizon universal finite-bad-count question. [Primary paper, printed page 269](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8171/8029)

Finite-model MEMDP support refinement, belief-restricted winning regions and common end components have established ancestry. Suilen, van der Vegt and Junges (2024), especially their J-local and belief-restricted constructions, are directly relevant to the recursive structure. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07006) The inspected common-objective formulation does not automatically supply the hidden model-dependent acceptance theorem.

Chatterjee, Doyen, Raskin and Sankur (2025) likewise combine revealing transitions and learning in common components. [Primary paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.150/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.150.html) Their common observable objective and qualitative quantifiers must be preserved when making a comparison. The static hidden-parameter model also has POMDP-lite ancestry; discounted Bayesian optimisation is a different guarantee. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04875)

Duan and colleagues study asymptotically perfect detection of a fixed finite-family MDP from the observed state/action history, with informative recurrent pairs and recursive policy synthesis. That is close active-learning ancestry. Acceptance can succeed without identifying the model; conversely a policy that identifies it through infinitely many rejected diagnostic actions need not achieve finite bad count. Thus detection is not automatically the requested theorem. [Primary preprint, introduction and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.12338) The separate source review addresses its journal version and exact detection criterion; no mathematical-correction claim is inferred from the existence of a later corrigendum.

The concentration estimate is classical Hoeffding on independent row tapes. The completed-event union and stopped-sum conversion above are written out to supply the required physical-cost argument. The dossier's separate regenerative reduction further demonstrates classical bounded-regret ancestry for common-support sufficiency. None of these facts alone establishes historical originality or an exact published duplicate of this recursive iff. The source search here is bounded, and no priority certificate is offered.
