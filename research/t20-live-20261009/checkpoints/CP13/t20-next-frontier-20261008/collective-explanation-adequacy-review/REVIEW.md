## 1 The strongest positive argument is represented fairly

The opening of section 9 on p. 18 explicitly distinguishes removing problems with singular explanation from showing that the proposed plural explanation really explains. It identifies the apparent scale remainder: why these kilogram facts rather than a uniformly doubled assignment? It mentions relevance-plus-necessitation as a possible abstract response, but does not establish a general sufficiency theorem. The report correctly treats this as the problem that the later positive argument addresses.

On pp. 19–20, the Dravid example supplies that positive argument. A community introduces numerical predicates governed by a ratio inference. Their coordinated applications allow recovery of the world's mass-ratio structure. The explanatory claim is about the facts expressed by the complete system, not merely the existence of a useful notation. Dasgupta proposes that underlying mass relationships collectively explain those facts because ratio representation is the discourse's role. Page 20 expressly denies logical implication from linguistic use and instead presents the structuralist explanation as the best available explanation. The report's description of positive but defeasible warrant is therefore warranted.

The report also correctly separates that argument from a proof of its ontological antecedent. Pages 2–3, 7, 15, and 24 explicitly limit the paper's defended conclusions to conditionals. Pages 6 and 15 offer brief motivations and refer fuller defense elsewhere. This source consequently adds support for an explanatory form conditional on qualitativism or comparativism, rather than a new independent demonstration that either ontology is actual.

The move to individuals is genuinely compressed: p. 22 invokes the existential quantifier's introduction and elimination rules and directs the reader to the 2009 development. The report neither ignores this positive analogy nor awards it the evidential detail of the mass case. Its strongest remaining objection, whether representational role warrants the explanatory priority of the represented facts, is a legitimate appraisal of an abductive bridge. It would be unfair to say the author merely confused semantics with ground; the report does not say that.

**Nonblocking precision guard.** The fullest affirmative reconstruction should retain pp. 21–22 as well as the fictional Dravid example. Dasgupta uses two IPK thought experiments, one involving a nonexistent apparent standard and another involving a standard half as massive as believed, to argue that his account predicts our linguistic reactions. That is his evidential bridge from the fictional community to our discourse. The frozen report captures the argument's form without detailing these cases. A later summary should not reduce the bridge to a bare stipulation about a fictional language.

## 2 Aptness and ordinary individual questions stay separate

The report's aptness proposal is explicitly its reconstruction of the affirmative argument, not an author-stated autonomy criterion. This is essential. Page 10 allows versions of structuralism in which a proper subset of individualistic facts is collectively grounded and other individualistic facts are grounded singly in members of that subset. Page 8 note 22 distinguishes direct from derivative grounding; p. 9 and note 23 recognize familiar explanations through particular constituents or origins while denying that those are the relevant purely qualitative foundations.

The report accordingly does not infer that all questions about a person's existence are meaningless or that all singular explanations are impossible. Nor does it infer numerical identity from qualitative indiscernibility. Pages 7 and 25–26 preserve the two-sphere case, and note 47 explicitly distinguishes Dasgupta's own view from making existentially general facts fundamental. The displayed existential formula establishes at least two suitable distinct objects; its syntax alone does not say there are exactly two. The report's caveats are sound.

Pages 14–15 and 22 also explicitly allow ordinary compositional truth conditions. Thus separate sentences do not force separate fundamental grounds. Conversely, the availability of collective grounds is not itself a demonstration that the actual underlying basis is autonomous or that every question about its obtaining is inapt.

## 3 Accounting is not silently promoted to grounding

Page 11 defines accounting by a plurally grounded intermediate body of facts and logical consequence. It immediately explains why this is not a purely explanatory notion under the assumed relevance constraint. The report and audit accurately preserve that distinction.

This cuts both ways as the report says: logical consequence cannot simply supply a singleton ground, but the absence of that singleton ground does not by itself refute collective adequacy. A critic must assess the offered collective explanation rather than impose distributivity as an unexplained requirement.

## 4 Both substitution patterns are faithful to page 5

With G denoting primitive ground and D its stipulated derivative closure, the audit correctly reconstructs:

1. G(X,Y) and G(Y + U,Z) license D(X + U,Z).
2. G(X,Y + U) and G(Y,Z) license D(X,U + Z).

Page 5 explicitly declines to assume any transitivity-like principle of primitive ground before defining derivative ground through the displayed closure. The audit states that the analogous operations iterate with derivative claims; it does not identify G with D. The first pattern preserves unused input U. The second preserves unused output U. Neither is an unrestricted weakening rule or an entailment-substitution rule.

For the ordinary existential test, the additional premise G({e},{E}) and the plural premise G(Q,{e} + J) yield D(Q,J + {E}) by the second pattern. The unchanged remainder J cannot simply be erased. A separate G(I,{E}) premise could instead yield D(Q,{E}) by the first pattern. This is a correct local rule analysis. It is not a theorem that no possible further grounds could ever support an existential conclusion, and the audit does not present it as one.

The p. 11 conjunction case is also correctly typed: conjunction grounding is a separate explanatory premise, and substitution produces derivative grounding of the conjunction. It does not identify a plural list with its conjunction or make the conjunction ground its conjuncts.

## 5 Overdetermination does not establish nonduplication

Note 4 on p. 3 and note 12 on p. 5 expressly permit a disjunction to have more than one full ground when both disjuncts obtain. The p. 11 objection instead concerns the conjunction-only reconstruction's disconnected explanatory bases: its qualitative grounds and its otherwise groundless conjuncts. The source objects to that explanatory arrangement, not to every case of multiple full grounds.

The audit correctly prevents transport of this particular objection into a general causal nonduplication law, a common-source theorem, or exclusion of a coexisting productive explanation. Those stronger results have not been furnished by this source.

## 6 The modal reply is genuinely conditional

Sections 9–10 cannot be assessed just by displaying two rescaled number arrays. Note 38 supplies attributed measurement-theoretic background: representations under appropriate constraints and uniqueness up to common positive rescaling. That is not an independent grounding theorem. Page 18 note 37 also distinguishes a metasemantic unit-fixing question from the question of what grounds kilogram facts.

On pp. 23–24, the mass-counterpart proposal treats relational role as a basis for how a world represents mass changes. It is not an interworld same-mass relation and is sensitive to conversational context. The report correctly blocks promotion of this proposal to a global identity map or to the inherited bearer semantics.

**Nonblocking precision guard.** Page 24 explicitly concedes the objections in the fundamental sense of possibility and supplies its answer in the looser, reduced sense. Thus the response is not an unrestricted modal vindication. The frozen report's context-sensitive description and note-45 qualification are compatible with this concession, but future short summaries should retain it.

Note 45 leaves unsettled which fundamental or reduced necessity, and which contexts if reduced, validate the earlier necessitation assumption. Its discussion of Finean essential necessity is a suggestion, not a demonstrated autonomous essence. No ordinary uniform S5 semantics or necessary original bearer follows from this passage.

## 7 Whole-field Completion remains an application conditional

The retained `OriginalBearerBridge.lean` was read without modification or execution. Its declarations distinguish an actual unreceived resource from an actual whole-original bearer, and its Completion premise supplies a relevant actual unreceived resource for a received target. Realisation supplies an actual intrinsic bearer; SupportTransport permits the contrapositive nonreceipt inference for that bearer. None of these predicates is defined by plural grounding or accounting.

The target type is unrestricted in the formal interface. Representing the whole received field by one target therefore does not require deriving a qualitative singleton explanation of every individual. This agrees with the frozen report. The application still needs warrant that the selected whole-field target is received and that Completion, Realisation, and SupportTransport hold under the proposed metaphysical interpretation. Formal permission to select that target provides none of this substantive warrant.

The interface's conclusion is existential, not a uniqueness result. Measurement uniqueness up to scale does not establish one productive bearer. Further modal and reception premises are separately visible in the retained interface. This review supplies neither new premise warrant nor fresh proof-test credit.

The source assessment's residual question is consequently legitimate: what warrants the actuality and explanatory status of the obtaining qualitative basis, and what connects it to the productive resource and bearer premises? This need not assume an empty world is metaphysically possible. It is also not a proof that structuralism cannot answer that question or cannot coexist with a productive source. The examined paper's explicit conditional scope leaves those options undecided.
