# The Jaʿfar storm story: provenance and the recognition chronology

9 October 2026 UTC. Additive T20 source criticism. No protected artifact or repository modification, programme integration, historical active-time certification, or closure.

## Result that changes the use of the example

The story is textually attested in early Arabic compilations, but the checked witnesses do not authenticate the event or identify the sailor. More importantly, they do not distinguish a previously known God becoming salient from a first recognition of God in the crisis. Their explicit sequence is a remembered rescue-directed state, elicited in a later conversation, followed by Jaʿfar's identification of its object as God.

This makes the third possibility in the predecessor report a particularly appropriate **source-control alternative**, with two qualifications. The dialogue does not expressly say that the sailor was uncertain, and the divine identification's being spoken later does not establish that it was first acquired then. The source supports neither an unqualified verdict of “prior recognition recovered” nor “new recognition acquired,” and it does not prove “mere uncertain hope.” Its acquisition chronology is underdetermined.

A second correction is important: the anecdote itself does not narrate an actual spoken prayer or describe the man as an atheist. It elicits an inward orientation toward someone able to save him. Calling it an observed atheist's rescue appeal imports details from its surrounding theological use. The strong philosophical case can still be considered conditionally, but this anecdote cannot independently verify that the strong case actually occurred.

## 1. Exact Ameri footnote and complete immediate unit

Retained PDF: Sami Ameri, براهين وجود الله في النفس والعقل والعلم, first 1445/2024, SHA-256 1ed74efd7d1648f71fd0853f7974d7284e141240db3347379f6e8f88198403b6. [Source PDF](https://almobadarah.com/uploads/books/5f6ae0298582cce21002cbe4.pdf#page=175).

The complete immediate section is pp.173–176, from “المبحث الرابع / كانط والخير الأقصى المطلوب” to the end of the Linker quotation, before the new section on consensus at177. I read its connected extracted text and visually checked173–176, including the footnotes.

There is **no footnote attached to the Jaʿfar anecdote**. Page175's single footnote is attached to the opening paragraph about the Otago study: “Death anxiety increases atheists’ unconscious belief in God, April 2, 2012,” followed by its university news URL. It must not be transferred to the anecdote. The anecdote begins with the qualified report formula “ومما روي”. Its immediate literary source is not named.

The paragraph immediately before it is Ameri's authorial interpretation of crisis: following Qurʾān31:32, he describes the atheist or polytheist turning directly to the one supreme being. The following dialogue is a separately reported example. Its questions concern sea travel, fear of drowning, lost hope in the vessel/sailors, and the remaining rescuer-directed inward state. The last identification is Jaʿfar's. The next authorial paragraph resumes the human difficulty of confronting a godless world. These voices must remain separate.

The section also expressly discusses the objection that needing something does not imply its existence. This does not by itself settle the distinct recognition argument, but prevents treating the surrounding authorial unit as oblivious to that distinction.

## 2. Closest wording: al-Tawḥīdī and al-Zamakhsharī

### A. Abū Ḥayyān al-Tawḥīdī, al-Baṣāʾir wa-l-dhakhāʾir

**Verified print locator:** Wadād al-Qāḍī edition, Dār Ṣādir, first1408/1988, part6, pp.36–37, item91. The [part6 scan](https://archive.org/download/ALBSAER/BSAER06.pdf#page=36) has matching physical and printed page numbers here. Title and imprint were visually checked. The entire item, its Qurʾānic continuation, the preceding Abū Ḥāmid reports (items89–90), and the following item92 were read in the scan. The complete same unit was first read in [electronic division005](https://www.islamicbook.ws/adab/albsaer-waldkhaer-005.html), lines797–801; the relevant sixth-part heading is at616. A separate reflowed PDF gives it on physical page472, but that is not the print locator.

The story begins “قال أبو حامد: سأل رجل جعفر بن محمد”. It asks for evidence for God without invoking the world, accident, and **body** (الجسم). It asks whether the person's expectation from the vessel and sailors ceased, whether his inward state turned toward someone who could save him, and ends “فإن ذلك هو الله تعالى”. Two Qurʾānic clauses follow (17:67 and16:53).

The text supplies Abū Ḥāmid as al-Tawḥīdī's immediate reporting voice. It supplies **no intermediate transmission chain from Abū Ḥāmid to the man or Jaʿfar**. The neighbouring item90 explicitly says al-Tawḥīdī heard Abū Ḥāmid discussing a different matter; that does not turn item91 into an eyewitness statement about Jaʿfar. The editor's item91 note points to Rabīʿ1:663, a parallel quotation, not a hadith authentication.

A biographical lead identifies al-Tawḥīdī's Qāḍī Abū Ḥāmid as the earlier Shāfiʿī jurist associated with Marw al-Rūdh, rather than al-Ghazālī; see the [Ṭabaqāt notice](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/12920/86). This identification is useful chronology control but does not fill the missing chain. A search lead to al-Baṣāʾir1:60–61 did not map to that passage in this edition; it was not silently treated as confirmation.

### B. al-Zamakhsharī, Rabīʿ al-abrār

**Verified print locator:** ʿAbd al-Amīr Muhannā edition, Muʾassasat al-Aʿlamī, Beirut, first1412/1992, part2, p.48, item6, in chapter19. [Scan](https://maknoon.org/ai/bk/rana2/rana2.pdf#page=48). The title/imprint, chapter opening at47, and full item6 including its notes at48 were visually read. The [electronic witness](https://www.islamicbook.ws/adab/rbia-alabrar-002.html), lines1456–1465, was also read.

The chapter groups pointed answers and exchanges in disputation; it is not a collection of subject-level cognitive observations. The story has the world/accident/**substance** (الجوهر) wording closest to Ameri and the same sequence and identification. The explicit Abū Ḥāmid reporting introduction is absent. No chain is supplied. The editorial footnote identifies Jaʿfar and the two verses, rather than authenticating the story. Ameri omits the two final scriptural clauses and adjusts some wording; that is compatible with this textual family but does not prove that he copied this edition or this work directly.

The print scan controls an acquisition hazard: the search engine's snippet for this same PDF offered a different pair of Qurʾānic clauses. The actual p.48 scan and direct electronic text agree on17:67 and16:53. The snippet was not used as textual evidence.

## 3. Early related form: al-Ṣadūq, al-Tawḥīd

**Locator:** chapter31, “باب معنى بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم”, report5, inherited printed pagination230–232. [Downloaded electronic PDF](https://download.almohsinlibrary.com/Aqaed/Aqaed578.pdf#page=174). Physical PDF pages173–175 contain the entire chapter31, which was read with its notes and the boundary before chapter32. The story proper is printed231, physical174. The PDF is a modern reflowed electronic edition naming editor Hāshim al-Ḥusaynī al-Ṭihrānī, **not a print-page photographic scan**.

The report presents Muḥammad b. al-Qāsim al-Jurjānī transmitting from Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf b. Muḥammad b. Ziyād and Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī b. Muḥammad b. Sayyār, with wording about their parents, to al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī b. Muḥammad. In this reported exposition, the shipwreck story is then attributed to al-Ṣādiq. The editor's footnote disputes how the parents clause attaches and refers the reader to al-Dharīʿa. This is explicit transmission/interpretive information, not a verified chain assessment by this investigation.

The interlocutor asks to be shown what God is because disputants have confused him. Jaʿfar asks about a wreck where neither ship nor swimming could help. The key reported question is: “فهل تعلق قلبك هنالك أن شيئاً من الأشياء قادر على أن يخلصك من ورطتك؟” The answer is yes, followed by Jaʿfar's identification of that object as God, capable of rescue when other rescue is absent.

Read in full, the report has a decidedly theological frame: God is the one to whom creatures turn in need when other hopes end; the dialogue illustrates that interpretation; it proceeds into teaching about invoking God's name. A following embedded exposition, reported through ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn from his family, describes a person returning to associationism after relief. That latter generalization is not the sailor's separately recorded autobiography. The narrator's theological interpretation should neither be discarded nor relabelled independent access to the sailor's acquisition history.

The al-Ṣadūq and al-Tawḥīdī works locate the motif much earlier than the later Rabīʿ repetition. Al-Ṣadūq is a fourth-century compiler, while al-Tawḥīdī reports the story from his earlier Abū Ḥāmid source. **This pass has not established priority between the composition dates of these two works, a date for the original story, or genealogical independence of the two forms.** They are the earliest relevant compilation-level attestations actually retrieved here, not a demonstrated earliest surviving manuscript or an original record from Jaʿfar's lifetime.

## 4. The precise cognitive discriminator

Separate three times:

1. Before the emergency: no witness gives the man's earlier recognition, teaching, warranted belief, doubt history, or lack of such a history.
2. During the emergency: a later yes-answer reports an inward orientation toward someone represented as able to rescue after ordinary means were exhausted. The object is described under a rescue role. In al-Ṣadūq the attachment question contains a proposition about something's ability to rescue, so it should not be flattened into objectless emotion. But an embedded proposition under heart-attachment does not by itself specify settled assent, certainty, warranted acquaintance, or uniquely divine content.
3. At the conversation: Jaʿfar identifies that object as God. This can function as reminding, clarification, retrospective interpretation, or instruction, depending on what was already cognized. The story does not itself select the acquisition/basing history.

Accordingly:

- **Recovered recognition:** consistent with the intended religious interpretation and the man's preexisting conceptual question, but no explicit prior knowledge episode is supplied.
- **New recognition during crisis:** also consistent with a generous recognition reading, but the source does not describe the moment of first acquisition or its truth-directed basis.
- **Uncertain practical orientation later identified:** a live control consistent with the narrated sequence; it is not established that the man was uncertain, that his orientation was merely practical, or that the identification was cognitively novel to him.

A later utterance is not proof of a later first thought. Conversely, an author's or interlocutor's identification cannot simply be projected back as an independently observed feature of the earlier state. This is the earned discriminator: **the anecdote's reported state and the interpretive identification are textually distinguishable, but the epistemic relation between them is unreported**.

No present articulation of an argument is needed for knowledge in general, and this source criticism imposes no universal requirement for such articulation or for exhaustive faculty certification. It does not rebut a genuinely granted immediate recognition. It limits the evidential use of this particular literary example.

## Stopping point and safe downstream use

Use: “An old report attributed to Jaʿfar portrays a teacher identifying a remembered rescue-directed state as directed toward God. Whether this recalls earlier recognition, describes new recognition, or interprets an uncertain orientation is not settled by the report.”

Do not use: “The sources document an atheist who independently recognized the Creator during a storm,” or “The sources prove that he only acquired the God-identification from the teacher.” Both overstate what was retrieved.
