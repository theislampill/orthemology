# Real contributions in one bearer: source individuation and preservation

Targeted T20 continuation, 7 October 2026 UTC. This report tests the question in the efficacy appraisal §8 and provision report §§6.4–6.5. It does not restart the project, certify closure, establish a complete rival metaphysics, or enlarge an earlier verification envelope.

## Result first

The strongest source reading is **real, numerically differentiated contributions**, not merely two names for agency. Ibn Taymiyyah says each carrier's effect *existing in the carried thing* differs from the other's and treats each as producing part of the joint product. That affirmative commitment must be preserved. Yet the passages examined do not independently supply the stronger ontology needed by the trace reduction: a source-neutrally individuated created token for every original participant, its fixed manifestation under suppression of another participant, and its exclusive entire attribution to that participant.

Three further discriminations are now explicit:

1. **Reality is better warranted than separable individuation.** Shifa e145 rejects a supposed power whose effect is sheer nonbeing. Miftah 1115–1120 argues that practical properties and effects are real rather than invented by thought. Neither entails a distinct externally identifiable token per productive root.
2. **Real joint causation does not generally require per-input output tokens.** Dar subsection015:135–136 describes one generated effect from two causes. Miftah 501–502 and 1272/1279 likewise describe a composite causal basis. This is derivative multimodal causation, not an endorsement of coequal original creators; it establishes that an original-agent-specific bridge is needed.
3. **Continual dependence cannot be turned into fresh sustaining accidents on these authors' authority.** Jami sixth E209 reports that account as a school theory. Sawaiq explicitly includes the thesis that an accident cannot persist for two times among the rational theses it rejects. The sources affirm preservation without thereby affirming one fresh created state per time, much less per sustaining root.

Thus this targeted extension strengthens the reality requirement, exposes a misleading shortcut for preservation, and sharpens the missing argument. It does not furnish a new deductive exclusion of the strongest direct-coalescence rival. This is a bounded negative result about the specified bridge, not a negative verdict on all original-efficacy arguments or corpus exhaustion.

## 1. What “two effects in one bearer” actually says

### 1.1 Jami sixth: a positive effect-parts ontology

I read the continuous electronic E094–104 sequence, then directly checked the retained scans at printed95 and98, which correspond to E099 andE102. Printed95 distinguishes:

- each actor's own act, inhering in that actor;
- the carried bearer;
- the effect of each act existing in the carried bearer;
- the joint product, which is from their aggregate.

The central wording is “فأثر فعل أحدهما الموجود في المحمول ليس هو أثر فعل الآخر، بل هو غيره.” This is stronger than “we can attribute the same event to A and B.” Its immediate continuation says that, if the partner really influences the product, “فقد فعل بعض المفعول.” The author argues from real influence to partial actual production, and from partial actual production to the absence of sole entire production. He separately allows that one partner could have carried the burden alone, while denying that its operative activity would then be unchanged.

The charitable reconstruction is therefore substantive: genuine cooperation involves two real contributions to a product, and each contribution is its contributor's work rather than the other's. The source is not merely defining a function that assigns every effect one owner.

The outstanding point is **how these contributions are independently individuated**. The passage identifies them through their actors and through part/whole language. It gives no further source-neutral state description by which the two external effect-parts can be identified independently of those actor-relative descriptions. Nor does it separately establish that the same intrinsic act has the same external manifestation in solo and joint contexts. The unchanged-activity claim is itself defended through the partial-versus-entire classification and familiar cases of changed effort and enhanced control.

At printed98 the text says cooperative products are not distinguished from one another in the relevant way, while actual things in the world are distinguishable. The awkward sentence “لا يتميز مفعول أحدهما عن مفعول الآخر” is present in the scan; it is not silently corrected. Read with95, it does not withdraw real contribution plurality. It cautions against turning each contribution into a separately distinguishable entire bearer. Distinct effect-parts, distinct external objects and separate entire production are different claims.

### 1.2 The preceding legal argument shows an individuation commitment

E094–098 distinguishes one ruling in type from one ruling numerically. Its overlapping-ground examples are not treated as one numerically identical ruling with two complete grounds: the author posits multiple rulings whose manifestations can overlap in a restricted locus. Removal of one ground leaves the other ruling in force. This is an argument for the individuation, not just an unexplained pronouncement.

Miftah main89–92 makes the indexing still clearer. Main91's examples of a ruling one *in particular* include ownership **by sale or inheritance**, and it distinguishes the ruling of this particular ground from a ruling retained through another ground. Those qualifications matter. The evidential inference is strong if the individuated item is an entitlement with its constitutive legal ground. It does not establish that every source-neutral physical state, or one bearer's unchanged continued existence, must be individuated by its productive source in the same fashion.

A possible defender can argue that causal origin is constitutive of every concrete produced actuality, as legal origin is constitutive of the particular entitlement. But that is precisely the additional metaphysical claim to defend. Counting the origin-indexed items and then citing the count as evidence of extra source-neutral created actuality would bypass the question.

These are historical legal analyses. Their penal examples are not contemporary advice or endorsed present-day rulings.

## 2. Shifa tests both effect-reality and distinct attributions directly

### 2.1 The best new positive premise: a real power is not idle attribution

In the Sunni response at e145, Ibn al-Qayyim examines the view that divine power produces a movement's existence while human power produces only its description as obedience or disobedience. If the added description is an existent effect, human power already influences something existent; if it is sheer nonbeing, the alleged power has no positive effect. His statement “أثر القدرة لا يكون عدما صرفا” supplies a clear reality requirement.

E131–134 supplies its semantic setting. The servant is truly active while also made active, and the creator's making is not the act inhering in the servant. E132 quotes al-Jawhari's lexical description of influence as leaving an effect in a thing. E134 differentiates making-speak from speaking, causing-motion from moving, and active production from the recipient's activity/reception. The lexical quotation is useful evidence of intended influence, but is not a separately proved ontology of additive traces.

The warranted premise is: an alleged efficacious power must have a real positive effect, rather than sheer nonbeing or a wholly idle attribution. The unwarranted strengthening is: every distinct efficacious power must have a distinct positive output token wholly its own, identifiable without reference to its provenance and unchanged across different co-causal contexts.

### 2.2 Distinct causal attributions are not a question the source ignored

E145–147 directly reports Abu al-Husayn's response that the same product may be related to two powers without one attribution being identical to the other. E146 even formulates the proposed difference in terms of its existing through this power and motive versus through the other's. This is a much closer historical comparator to a producer-relative account than a generic carrying example.

Ibn al-Qayyim rejects it: “وهذا لا يجدي عنه شيئا.” He then retains several claims together: universal divine power, real creaturely agency, impossibility of a single effect between two independently complete producers, and the need for an originator of each occurrence. His positive settlement is **asymmetric causal dependence**: the servant's capacity is itself an effect of divine capacity, the servant's capacity is part of the cause, and creator–created attribution differs from cause–effect attribution.

This is not acceptance of the modern rival. But the immediate rejoinder does not derive source-neutral token multiplication from the bare difference between two attributions. It appeals to the stronger whole doctrine and distinguishes causal levels. Applying its result to two proposed unreceived original powers requires independently warranting the exclusion of that profile; the source's actual one-creator/derived-agent settlement cannot itself serve as that warrant without further argument.

E049–052 is a useful control: the author accepts cooperation including a load either participant could have carried alone, while denying that each actually did the entire same production independently. Neither mere standing solo capacity nor a multiplicity of causal predicates decides exhaustive actual authorship.

### 2.3 An indivisibility argument has a different source voice

E121–127 also directly reads the al-Juwayni/Nizamiyya passage. At e123 the claim that one act has no parts belongs to the quoted argument, not an independently announced Ibn al-Qayyim atomism. The quotation reaches an asymmetric created-power account; Ibn al-Qayyim calls it a good mediation at e125 and specifically endorses the efficacy of enabled human power at e126, while rejecting its equation of will and love.

This prevents an easy misuse in either direction. One cannot cite the quoted “one act has no parts” as a settled rejection of every effect-parts ontology, or infer that the author endorsed every premise of the extended quotation. It does show that indivisible act, multiple causal grounds and mere labels were live distinctions in the immediate controversy.

## 3. Ordinary composite causes do not supply the missing original-source bridge

Dar subsection015:121–144 moves continuously from knowledge and rational means to created causation. At135–136 its preferred middle account says generated effects arise through the servant's act and other causes. The text calls the resulting occurrence “الأثر المتولد عن هذين السببين.” Miftah500–502 likewise says the generated result comes from acts and other factors, the human acts being parts of its cause. Moral credit is proportionate to the acts; it is not an assertion that credit measures separate physically contained tokens.

Dar subsection016:621–651 supplies the strongest opposing pressure. After quoting Ibn Rushd, Ibn Taymiyyah's own reply at627–629 rejects double entire production and pictures cooperation as doing different work: transporting versus placing bricks, or carrying different sides of a log. At635–643 it then treats light, nourishment and downstream motion as arising through multiple cooperating requisites. These examples offer real, often independently discriminable contributions. They do not establish that all primitive original cooperation must have that ordinary spatial or task-partition structure.

Miftah1270–1273 and1278–1281 similarly includes sun, earth, air, relation and receptive subject in a compound cause. Miftah908–911 distinguishes a persistent causal disposition from its actual effect in a recipient: a sharp sword can remain sharp where its cutting is prevented. Jami E211–216 explicitly distinguishes the agent's act alone from that act together with the patient's reception. “I taught him but he did not learn” preserves the first without the completed second.

The result is not a refutation of locality for a fully specified complete original productive exercise. It is a restriction on the evidence for that locality: **unchanged intrinsic act or power is not generally enough to fix its external manifestation.** If “complete original exercise” is strengthened to mean an independently sufficient, actually exhaustive source of its whole manifested effect, the required bridge may follow, but the source-mode norm is then doing the work.

The medical, cosmological and ethnic claims in these historical examples are not independently certified scientific premises. The causal distinctions can be attributed accurately without endorsing, for example, the treatment of emergency diet or climatic stereotypes. Miftah1115–1121 positively defends real properties and plural effects in a single act against a purely mental account. It strengthens realism and plurality, not per-root disjointness or fixed manifestation.

A bounded ancillary scan return to Jami printed410–412 confirms bodily labor and asset-use as differentiated contributions to shared yield. This is a transmitted excerpt with visible “then he said” transitions. Contractual entitlement to part of yield should not be mistaken for identification of the precise created effect-token produced by each partner.

## 4. Preservation is the decisive stress test

### 4.1 What the maker-preserver passage establishes

Dar subsection013:231–267 was reread continuously. Ibn Rushd's initial account is quoted; Ibn Taymiyyah explicitly accepts the superiority of the agent on whom its product continually depends at257–258 and264, and affirms creatures' dependence in both coming-to-be and remaining at266. He rejects a merely final-causal exemplar and the substitution of motion alone for production of the world's substances and qualities.

At261 he distinguishes the craftsman's own activity from the receptive bodies' ability to receive and **retain** their composition. That retention is due to their created powers, not continuing human craftsmanship. This blocks conflating incidental preservation of one arrangement with original existential conservation. It also shows why retention of an already present structure is not automatically a new distinct output of each currently named contributor.

Shifa303–307 provides another positive control. E305 distinguishes originating the recipient, preparing it, and supplying ongoing help: without the supplying agent's help the received effect will not occur. The setting is developing human receptivity, knowledge and will, not an independently stated theory of a fresh sustaining token for each persisting bearer at each instant. E306 distinguishes a sufficient tendency from one requiring another factor.

### 4.2 A tempting fresh-token argument is positively unsafe

Jami E207–216 discusses destruction and nonbeing. AtE209, repeated production of subsistence or of accidents that cannot last two times is **reported** as the view of certain theologians. It is not silently the author's own explanation of preservation. The subsequent division distinguishes absence of a cause, a positive impediment, and complete versus partial causes.

Sawaiq source001:1455–1461 places “العرض لا يبقى زمانين” among the allegedly rational theses the author regards as contrary to reason. The earlier occurrence at001:276–290 is dialectical “if you say,” not an unconditional adoption. The fresh-accident route cannot be imported by combining these two voices. Miftah1387–1389 also rejects reducing present origination to rearrangement of a fixed stock of indivisible atoms, positively affirming the coming-to-be of concrete entities. Neither objection supplies a replacement discrete token theory.

Rejecting universal accident nonpersistence does not prove that preservation never involves new effects or states. It establishes a narrower but material result: the general inference from continual existential dependence to mandatory fresh sustaining accidents is unavailable here. Even a separate argument for one continued-existence actuality at each stage would leave the **per-root** multiplication unproved.

## 5. Test against the strongest current rival

The tested rival has primitive unreceived cognition, each agent's own full-Q activity and own end, and direct joint realization of the same source-neutral Q. Its own success does not require the peer. It does not attribute sole entire actual production of the joint occurrence to both agents.

The new source findings produce these exact results:

- A mere label declaring B productive remains inadequate. Shifa's reality argument genuinely presses the rival to explain real efficacy.
- If the rival's primitive joint causal relation is accepted as a real explanatory fact, the reality argument alone does not generate a second created token. A causal relation's reality and its realization as a new separately individuated created state are distinct issues.
- Jami's effect-parts assertion directly conflicts with the rival's denial of individually entire external contributions. That is a substantive disagreement with the source, not an unnoticed textual gap. But its independent warrant against primitive direct co-production remains the point at issue.
- CND rules out two exhaustive original providers of the same contribution once such contributions and their exhaustive individual provision have been justified. It does not supply those contribution witnesses or their attribution.
- Locality requires more than fixed cognition, intrinsic act, command and own end. Ordinary source examples themselves require recipient and co-cause conditions. Whether a perfect original's actual productive manifestation must instead be individually exhaustive returns to the original-efficacy argument.
- Counting an original agent's own act does not by itself change the **created** history. Its status as an intrinsic act of the proposed original bearer is not the same as a created manifestation in the preserved bearer.
- Faithful accounting must retain every real created occurrence, including real cancellation or side effects. But accounting cannot establish that a direct co-preservation episode contains additional occurrences merely because it has additional causal attributions.

None of this establishes the metaphysical possibility of two necessary perfect original bearers. The direct joint law, full perfection profile and source-worthy agency interpretation remain obligations for the rival. Conversely, the inspected source comparisons do not discharge the affirmative trace route by treating those outstanding rival obligations as a proof of its impossibility.

## 6. Precise remaining argument and bounded stopping point

The useful next argument is now narrower than “each real act has an effect”:

**Why must every genuinely operative original participant have an external created manifestation whose numerical identity and entire sourcehood are fixed by that participant's own productive state, independently of the presence of another original participant, even when the product's continued existence and the participant's own end are unchanged?**

There are two potentially successful routes:

1. Defend a real effect-parts individuation principle independent of simply indexing parts by their causes, and show why its manifestation is local in primitive original production and preservation. Ordinary spatially partitioned labor and source-indexed legal entitlements do not alone establish the generalization.
2. Defend the perfection claim that primitive joint actual efficacy necessarily receives constitutive completion from the peer, even where cognition, intrinsic activity, source-neutral success and own end do not. That could exclude the rival without a trace ontology, but it is the distinct efficacy-perfection obligation, not an event-count argument.
