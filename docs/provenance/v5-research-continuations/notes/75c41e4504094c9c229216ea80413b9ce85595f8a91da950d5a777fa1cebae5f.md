# Quantitative interface and limits

This note explains how to use the theorem without broadening its permission or proof scope.

## Protected model perturbations

Inputs to certify are the centres a_i, their half separation and upper half-bound, the weights a_i/Z, the literal tolerance epsilon, and component support inside [(1-delta)a_i,(1+delta)a_i] with delta<=epsilon/4. Within those intervals, the theorem is uniform over every Borel distribution. The controller does not need the within-cluster density or shape.

The actual explicit policy's general budget bound is useful independently of the canonical logarithmic formula. For any boundary sequence satisfying

    (3/2)a_(i+1)<=b_i<=(3/4)a_i,

its positive-floor selector has expected count at most

    (101/Z) sum_i a_(i+1)/b_i
      +(1616/(15Z)) sum_i exp[-3a_i/(320b_i)].

The protected-gap inequalities make b_i decrease geometrically and keep the one-sided concentration margins uniform. The chosen clipped-log boundary evaluates BOTH series in terms of S=sum r_i log(1/r_i). A geometric midpoint gives a valid policy, but its crude budget estimate involves sum sqrt(r_i), which can diverge even when S is finite. Thus choosing a visually central boundary and calling it optimal would lose the sharp classification.

## Output precision

The centre report has literal margins at least(3/16)epsilon^2 a_i and(1/4)epsilon^2 a_i across its entire cluster. This quantifies how much output approximation can be tolerated; the margin shrinks toward the endpoint. A fixed absolute rounding tolerance is not justified uniformly over all i. No exact-real implementation or prescribed-time controller is claimed merely because a mathematical selector exists.

## Contamination visibility

A prior perturbation of theta toward the endpoint changes total optimal expected failure count at a fixed horizon N by at most theta N. The exact clipped-max increment and the support-count formula give substantially more structure for the specified class. The small finite-horizon change does not certify finite lifetime cost. For each positive partial endpoint mixture, the same model has infinite expected lifetime cost.

At any fixed finite prefix, every strictly interior Bernoulli parameter gives positive probability to the observed word. The theorem therefore does not furnish a zero-false finite-data certification of an exact endpoint exclusion or of a countably infinite family of protected gaps. Those are model promises to justify using domain knowledge, controlled construction or an explicitly probabilistic uncertainty statement.

## Countermodels to broadening the claim

- Continuous density is compatible with the sparse improvement if it occupies separated islands. Absolute continuity alone does not restore inverse-moment necessity
- A density lower bound throughout a punctured endpoint neighbourhood fills those gaps and belongs to the previously established inverse-moment boundary instead
- An atom at zero has zero own positive-time loss, but mixing it with the finite-budget sparse or smoothed prior can make every common policy have infinite mean count. Finite-budget components are not closed under arbitrary mixtures
- Changing weights arbitrarily can destroy the posterior unimodality identity. The exact finite-horizon endpoint increment remains valid, while the evaluated p=1 support-count law and smoothing classification have their stated weight hypotheses
- A finite expected count does not bound the harm of any particular error or yield a catastrophic-error safety guarantee
