# The second-order common-repair programme after Hessian compatibility

Research return, 2026-10-01. Ordinary finite-dimensional variational theorem. This is a second-order specialization of standard minimax sensitivity and finite linear programming; it is not a claim to have invented those methods. It explains the nonproduct rescue in GLUING_AND_COUPLING.md.

## 1. Assumptions and finite programme

Let V⊂R^n be finite nonempty, C=conv(V), p∈ri(C), and T=span(C−p). Let F₁,…,F_m be a nonempty finite family of C³ potentials on an open convex neighborhood Ω containing C. Assume their Hessians are positive definite on Ω. This convenient regularity scope makes every forecast-second Bregman loss continuous on C; an endpoint-singular extension is not asserted here. The potentials need not be coordinate additive.

Fix h∉T and suppose there is one z∈T such that

    H_k(h−z) ⟂ T  for every k,    H_k=∇²F_k(p).

Thus the first-order common-repair obstruction vanishes. Define

    E_k = (H_k[h,h]−H_k[z,z])/2
        = H_k[h−z,h−z]/2 >0,

    B_k(v) = E_k + {D³F_k(p)[p−v,h,h]−D³F_k(p)[p−v,z,z]}/2,

    A_kv(w) = B_k(v)−H_k[p−v,w],    w∈T.

The **second-order margin** is the finite linear programme

    τ* = max_(w∈T) min_(k,v) A_kv(w).

Equivalently maximize τ subject to A_kv(w)≥τ for all k,v. It has a finite attained maximum. For additive F_k, its cubic-tensor term is simply

    (1/2)Σ_i f_ki'''(p_i)(p_i−v_i)(h_i²−z_i²).

The actual finite truth vectors enter as the rows p−v. Replacing C by the product of its projected blocks changes these rows and can change τ*, even when every affine tangent, Hessian and component label remains unchanged.

## 2. Exact variational asymptotic and its consequences

Let

    R(ε)=min_(c∈C) max_(k,v)
           [D_Fk(v,c)−D_Fk(v,p+εh)].

Then

    R(ε)=−τ* ε²+o(ε²),   ε↓0.                        (S)

Consequently:

- τ*>0 gives one common strict coherent repair for every sufficiently small positive ε
- τ*<0 rules out common weak repairs for every sufficiently small positive ε, and even rules out every allowed worsening η=o(ε²)
- τ*=0 is genuinely undecided by second-order information alone; both exact weak success and exact failure occur

The last distinction is essential. The critical rank-one global theorem settles its zero-margin cases using an exact mean comparison valid across an interval. It does not license treating an arbitrary feasible zero-margin second-order programme as an exact repair certificate.

## 3. Proof, including localization of real optimizers

### 3.1 The programme attains its optimum

Because p is relatively interior, C contains a tangent ball p+B_T(0,r). Positive definiteness of one anchor H₀ gives κ>0 such that

    max_(v∈V) H₀[p−v,w] ≥ κ‖w‖,  w∈T.

To see this, evaluate at p−r w/‖w‖ and use the smallest eigenvalue of H₀ on T. A linear function has the same maximum on V and its convex hull. Therefore

    min_(k,v) A_kv(w) ≤ max_v B₀(v)−κ‖w‖.

The objective is continuous and tends to −∞ at infinity on T, so it attains a maximum. If p=Σ_v a_v v with a_v≥0 and Σa_v=1, then

    Σ_v a_v A_kv(w)=E_k.

Hence τ*≤min_k E_k, so its value is finite.

### 3.2 First localization of the actual minimizers

Compactness of C and continuity give an actual optimizer c_ε. The candidate p+εz is coherent for all sufficiently small ε and has excess losses O(ε²), since all their first-order terms vanish. Thus R(ε)≤Kε².

Averaging the anchor-score comparisons with barycentric weights for p yields

    D_F0(p,c_ε)≤D_F0(p,p+εh)+R(ε)=O(ε²).

Nonnegativity of D_F0(p,c_ε) also gives R(ε)≥−D_F0(p,p+εh), so the upper bound above is accompanied by R(ε)=O(ε²).

Strict convexity gives D_F0(p,c)>0 for c≠p. Compactness first forces c_ε→p, and the positive quadratic lower bound near p gives c_ε−p=O(ε). No localization assumption is built into the programme.

### 3.3 Second localization

Put u_ε=c_ε−p and x_ε=u_ε−εz∈T. Taylor expansion of each anchor forecast loss, uniformly over the finite truth set, gives

    D_F0(v,c_ε)−D_F0(v,p+εh)
      =H₀[p−v,u_ε−εh]+O(ε²)
      =H₀[p−v,x_ε]+O(ε²).

The last identity uses H₀(h−z)⊥T. Every left side is at most R(ε)=O(ε²). The tangent-ball coercivity above therefore forces

    c_ε−p−εz=O(ε²).

So the actual normalized second corrections w_ε=(c_ε−p−εz)/ε² lie in one fixed bounded subset of T. This is derived from the loss inequalities, not postulated.

### 3.4 Uniform second-order expansion

The derivative and second derivative of forecast-second loss are

    D_b D_F(v,p)[u]=H[p−v,u],
    D_b² D_F(v,p)[u,u]=H[u,u]+D³F(p)[p−v,u,u].

For every bounded family of w,

    D_Fk(v,p+εz+ε²w)−D_Fk(v,p+εh)
       =−ε² A_kv(w)+o(ε²),

uniformly in k,v and in those w. Applying this to w_ε gives

    R(ε)/ε² ≥ −τ*+o(1).

Conversely take an optimizer w* of the finite programme. The point p+εz+ε²w* belongs to C at small scales by relative interior, and evaluation there gives the reverse bound. This proves S.

The same localization argument applied directly to any sequence of exact common repairs yields bounded second corrections and a subsequential limit w satisfying A_kv(w)≥0. Thus weak feasibility of this LP is a genuine necessary condition even without mentioning minimizers.

## 4. Dual obstruction certificate

The usual finite LP dual, or finite minimax separation, gives

    τ*=min Σ_(k,v) λ_kv B_k(v),

over λ_kv≥0 with Σλ_kv=1 and

    Σ_(k,v) λ_kv H_k[p−v,w]=0  for every w∈T.

The dual feasible set is nonempty: put all mass on one score and use barycentric weights for p. For any feasible λ, multiplying and summing the primal inequalities proves τ≤ΣλB directly. In particular a strictly negative dual objective is an independently checkable nonrepair certificate. Positive primal margins are independently checkable repair certificates. Strong duality identifies the optimal coefficient, but checking either kind of certificate does not require trusting a numerical solver.

For a rational truth matrix, rational Hessians and rational cubic derivative data, this is a rational LP. Exact finite certificates can therefore be checked using rational arithmetic. Smooth arbitrary-real input data still require their own analytic or certified numeric representation; the LP does not make them effectively decidable.

## 5. Crosswalk to the prior first-order programme

The earlier FIRST_ORDER_REPAIR_REGRET.md identifies an order-ε price when candidate metric projections disagree. Its κ=0 case is automatically favorable for quadratic potentials because D³F=0. Then w=0 gives every A_kv=E_k>0.

For nonlinear potentials, κ=0 only removes the first-order term. The cubic derivatives shift the truthwise constraints and can make τ* positive, zero, or negative. This is exactly the missing gap between Hessian compatibility and exact common repair.

On an isolated rank-one hull, the LP collapses to scalar interval overlap and the differences between centers factor into positive variance times log-curvature-ratio derivatives. On a nonproduct hull, other tangent corrections change the row constraints and may rescue overlap. The AND-hull construction is an explicit positive primal certificate of this phenomenon.

The sign distinctions are invariant under independently rescaling a finite set of candidate scores by positive constants, since simultaneous weak or strict feasibility is unchanged. The numerical value τ* is not invariant under those rescalings; a quantitative use must retain declared loss units.

## 6. Zero-margin deletion control

CRITICAL_SECOND_ORDER_CONTROL.md gives an everywhere-positive polynomial curvature r(t)=1+4(t−1/2)+12(t−1/2)². At s=1/2 its log derivative exactly equals the isolated binary bound 4, so the second-order LP is weakly feasible with τ*=0. Yet a rational polynomial envelope proves no exact common weak repair on a positive interval of ε. The failure begins at order ε³.

Conversely take ψ(t)=exp(4(t−1/2))/16, with curvature r(t)=exp(4(t−1/2)). This potential is C∞ on all of R and has everywhere-positive curvature, so it meets the present open-neighborhood regularity assumptions. At s=1/2 it has r=1 and r'=4, giving the same four second-order LP rows and optimum0 as the polynomial obstruction. Yet its log-curvature slope is identically4, and4≤1/[s(1−s)] everywhere on(0,1). The sharp rank-one theorem therefore supplies exact common weak repair throughout the attained box. This positive comparator was identified in independent review.

The odds-ratio example in ANCHOR_FAILURE.md is another exact zero-margin success, but its endpoint-singular curvature is outside the open-neighborhood assumptions used for the general minimax asymptotic here. It should not by itself be used as the within-class comparator. The exponential and polynomial pair above establish that the τ*=0 caveat is necessary even inside the stated regular class.
