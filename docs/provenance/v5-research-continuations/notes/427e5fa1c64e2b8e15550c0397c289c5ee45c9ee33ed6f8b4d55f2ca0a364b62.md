# Kernel closure of the continuum expected-error upper bound

This successor strengthens the preceding ordinary cost note without replacing its frozen source.

`EmpiricalFourthMoment.lean` proves the exact first, second and fourth centred moments directly from the finite Bernoulli prefix masses. Its fourth-moment recurrence is derived by expanding the actual next-bit alternatives, not by accepting a caller-supplied moment bound. It also proves the coarse fourth-moment estimate4n² for the centred sum.

`EmpiricalExpectedCost.lean` combines that identity with the previously proved literal repair radius r=alpha(1−alpha)epsilon/4. At opportunity n≥1 the actual source probability of an incorrect empirical report is at most4/(n²r⁴). A telescoping majorant yields the kernel-checked expected total bound8/r⁴. This is a finite, explicitly nonuniform and deliberately loose bound for every fixed interior alpha. The sharper ordinary constant from the companion note remains a separate result.

Together with `EmpiricalRepairCost.lean`, the same always-emitting procedure now has both a finite upper expected-error bound and a lower bound diverging as alpha approaches either endpoint. Each statement is under the actual infinite iid law and counts every acquired-word opportunity. The lower bound is for this particular policy, not a minimax lower bound over all policies. The independent audit is pending at this author freeze.

`check_empirical_moments.py` supplies2,480 exact rational finite checks, including50 first-report-error controls and480 endpoint zero-error controls. These tests corroborate the formulas and do not prove the arbitrary-real theorem. The actual-law endpoint kernel closure is separate.

No performance-optimality claim is made for the fourth-moment bound. A sharper concentration estimate could lower the constant without changing the distinction between pointwise finite cost and parameter-uniform finite cost. Even the probability of an incorrect report need not decrease at each successive sample size because the empirical grid changes with n.
