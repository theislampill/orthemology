# Why the prior counterexample does not contradict universal prediction bounds

The new nonconvexity result concerns a failure score determined by the unknown Bernoulli parameter and the report, rather than a common next-symbol loss table.

Hutter's General Loss Bounds for Universal Sequence Prediction (ICML 2001; author technical report dated 10 April 2001), Section 3.1 and Theorem 2, evaluates a prediction or action against the observed next symbol through one bounded loss table. Its finite-total-loss transfer compares the Bayesian mixture predictor with the informed predictor in that experiment. The later Optimality of Universal Bayesian Sequence Prediction for General Loss and Alphabet develops the same distinction between observation prediction and action-dependent bounded loss. These results do not identify every latent-parameter tolerance failure with such an observed-symbol loss. Sources: https://hutter1.net/ai/ploss.pdf (PDF pp 3–4, Section 3.1/Theorem 2); https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0311014 (Sections 2.4 and 3.4).

## Exact obstruction in the retained loss

Fix epsilon=1/4 and report q=9/16. The retained AnnularLiteral.Accepted predicate accepts parameter 1/4 and rejects parameter 1/2. Both are interior Bernoulli parameters. If a common nonnegative next-bit score had expected value zero at parameter 1/4, both possible bit scores would have to be zero, since both outcomes have positive probability. Its expected score at parameter 1/2 would then also be zero, contradicting the desired literal failure value one.

The Lean theorem literal_no_common_next_bit_score imports the actual retained failureIndicator and checks this concrete obstruction. The general finite-observation theorem zero_score_under_full_support states the same positivity argument for any finite observation set. Thus allowing a fixed finite word rather than one bit does not by itself restore an exact nonnegative score representation: every word has positive probability under an interior Bernoulli law. The word-probability positivity instantiation is an ordinary consequence here, not an additional claimed formal binding.

This is a statement about exact equality of these losses in the stated information structure. It does not rule out proper scoring rules for estimating a probability, asymptotically consistent reports, signed estimators, approximate scores, an oracle revealing the parameter, or different feedback. It also does not challenge Bayesian coherence. The inference blocked is the direct import of a finite-loss prediction guarantee into a different, parameter-dependent compulsory-failure criterion.

## Research relevance

A single policy may eventually make no further errors almost surely while its prior-averaged total error has infinite expectation. The accepted finite-family controller result and the countably supported prior example can therefore coexist: their quantifiers, information structures and uniform cost requirements differ. The nonconvexity packet and its independent review supply the actual prior theorem; this companion isolates why a familiar prediction guarantee is not an automatic counterargument.
