# Ibn Taymiyya’s cooperation, coalescence, and common-Creator argument

## 1. Result of this reassessment

The strong argument has now been reconstructed from the actual supplied Oneness object, directly checked against the Arabic Asbahani passage, and checked against all three newly specified Minhaj intervals. Ibn Taymiyya explicitly considers agreeing makers, necessarily agreeing makers, powers remaining intact in mutual presence, voluntary cooperation, optional coalescence, a third cause, and a proposed common good or foreknowledge explaining agreement. It is inaccurate to say that he overlooked harmony or simply preferred one creator for explanatory economy.

His intended conclusion is that the actual interdependent creation cannot be partitioned among two independent ultimate makers. His reasoning also claims to exclude any two equally original lords, whether their agreement is necessary, their opposition is necessary, or either is possible. Those claims are stronger than a preference for a unified explanation.

No full-premise countermodel has been produced in this reading assessment. The independent model audit likewise reports that the existing controls do not preserve the entire strengthened profile. That is a correction to any earlier suggestion that the mere availability of a cooperative graph defeats the argument. It does not, by itself, prove every metaphysical bridge. The philosophical reassessment must separately state its judgment of the exact bridge it accepts or withholds; this source reconstruction does not supply that verdict.

The most precise remaining assessment question is this: does a requisite supplied by a different original producer necessarily become a received component of the first producer’s intrinsic productive perfection, rather than a condition of a differentiated joint outcome? The source argues affirmatively, especially in Minhaj 2:180–183. It must be assessed at that strength. A generic distinction between power and effect cannot simply be announced as an answer.

## 3. Terms, causal roles, and quantifier discipline

The argument concerns a lord/original maker, not any agent with some unbestowed disposition. Distinguish:

1. Standing intrinsic power: what an agent can contribute from itself.
2. Complete power for a specified effect: an adequate productive capacity with the relevant completion conditions.
3. Determining will: the will whose intended effect occurs with complete power.
4. Intrinsic action: an act belonging to a particular agent as its bearer.
5. External product: the created effect, whose different contributors may perform different acts.
6. Actual whole-alone production: one agent actually produces the entire numerical effect without a coproductor.
7. Product-condition dependence: a result needs another component, material, recipient or cooperating act.
8. Source-power dependence: a producer receives its own power or the perfection that makes its productive exercise adequate.

The source recognizes several of these distinctions. It is therefore unfaithful both to reduce its lord to an intrinsically powered partial contributor and to attribute to it an indiscriminate ban on every condition or every relation.

P4 is about the same numerical effect, not two effects of the same kind. P5’s equality concerns corresponding target powers, not merely both agents having at least one power. P6 concerns complete power and complete will for exactly the specified effect. For the fixed-background cooperation test, the actual determining will and productive exercise must remain unchanged, not merely a standing inventory of capacities. It is not the proposition that all possible acts are actually willed. Oneness34/Asbahani147–148 expressly retains unwilled possibilities within power.

The actual-world unity inference is about created things and their causally connected field. It is not, without further argument, a modern universal over every item classified as a SourceMode requisite. Conversely, failure to obtain that modern universal does not answer the actual common-Creator argument.

## 4. Strongest source-faithful reconstruction

### Stage A: original productive capacity cannot be borrowed into existence

P1. Every actual efficient agent must have power; action without power is impossible.

P2a. An ultimate creator’s original power and will must belong to it intrinsically, not be supplied by its effects, a peer or a higher creator.

P3. An efficient-priority circle is impossible, including a circle in a partial cause or in what completes a cause. An infinite regress of original efficient makers likewise fails to supply original efficacy. This is distinguished from succession of effects and noncausal concomitance.

Suppose A and B have no power whatsoever when considered separately. Their coming together cannot make either powerful unless prior power in one or an external producer supplies the new capacity. If A first makes B powerful and B first makes A powerful, the relevant efficient prerequisites circle. A third giver would make the supposed ultimate pair derivative. Hence ultimate original power cannot arise that way. (Oneness17–22; Asbahani134–139; Minhaj3:306–310.)

This stage does not deny ordinary combined strength. The source expressly recognizes that each ordinary collaborator already has its own power before cooperation, and their joint strength can be greater. That example does not establish that either is an original lord.

### Stage B: perfection strengthens original power into independent efficacy

M1. Realized efficacy is a perfection: an efficacious producer is more complete in that respect than a nonefficacious one.

P2b. A necessary-by-itself being does not receive its productive perfection from an external source or depend on another’s leave to retain it.

Minhaj2:180 makes the bridge explicit. If an alleged original’s operation requires motions, connections or materials from another, its efficacy is externally conditioned. Since this efficacy is its perfection, it depends on another for that perfection and is not necessary-by-itself. The surrounding argument distinguishes intrinsic necessary attributes from external companions and reasons that a necessary bearer cannot receive a necessary part of its completeness from something else. At2:181 the conclusion is applied to the creator of a human and the makers of parental material, air and water.

This is substantive metaphysics, not merely the dictionary definition of “lord.” The independent warrant of M1 and of the transfer from effect-completion to source-perfection is precisely what must be appraised. A countermodel preserving only P2a while denying P2b has not preserved the whole intended premise package. That observation describes the full source position, not a valid reason to reject a countermodel to the derivation of P2b. When that derivation is under test, P2b is downstream: denying it alone does not disqualify the countermodel. The test is preservation of every independently retained upstream premise, as specified in section7 below.

### Stage C: equal complete makers cannot each produce the entire same effect

P4. If A actually produces numerical e wholly alone, no distinct B actually produces any part of that same entire production, still less the whole of it alone. Two claims of sole entire production contradict each other.

P5. Equal original makers have the same relevant target capacities; their original powers cannot disappear merely through their coexistence.

B3. The source infers that two equally unrestricted powers over the same target cannot remain jointly available. One can do the effect alone only if the other does not do it; making its efficacy await the other’s nonaction or permission is a restriction incompatible with original power.

Oneness23–25 and Asbahani140–141 explicitly reason across the distinction between actual entire production and concurrent capacity. Minhaj1:439 says the two simultaneously independent capacities would entail the effect twice in the same state.1:440 treats dependence on the other’s noninterference as being made able by the other. These are answers to an alternative-capacities proposal, not omissions of it.

P4 alone is secure only about actual sole production. The transition to B3 also uses the source’s strong meaning of unconditioned capacity and determining agency. It is not enough to show two ordinary unexercised abilities, and it is not enough to invoke P4 without explaining that stronger capacity reading.

The opposition branch is supplementary: opposite determined effects cannot both occur; if neither or only one occurs, the assumed equality and unrestricted efficacy fail. The source distinguishes an intrinsically contradictory target from an otherwise possible target blocked by a peer. Its reasoning therefore does not simply confuse inability to realize a contradiction with a defect of power.

### Stage D: neither necessary nor optional cooperation rescues the pair

For necessary agreement, the source asks whether either can will/act without the other. If each will and act is conditioned by the other, it takes each to be part of what completes the other as willing agent. That is B2: mutually conditioned original determination is an efficient-priority circle unless a third complete cause produces the concurrence. The source contrasts this with fatherhood and sonship, whose concomitance has an independent producer. (Minhaj3:318–323; Oneness40 note32.)

For contingent agreement rather than disagreement, a determining preference is required. Neither equivalent original is prior to the other; their joint preference cannot explain the very agreement without reintroducing that agreement as an explanatory condition. (Minhaj3:319.)

A proposed common good or advance knowledge of the common outcome is considered at3:322. Created benefits and occurrences are downstream of the makers’ wills; knowledge of such an occurrence answers to the occurrence whose productive explanation is itself in question. It cannot simply be inserted as an external efficient motivator producing those original wills. This is an explanatory-direction claim, not the assertion that divine knowledge temporally learns from a previously independent event.

The examples of cooks, rulers, builders, advisers and partners at3:326–329 add an induction/analogy about organized cooperation. Sometimes one participant follows another, sometimes both answer to a third standard or director, and roles may alternate. These examples do not mean that an abstract plan is automatically an efficient creator; the argument still needs the relation of dependence appropriate to the original pair.

### Stage E: coalescence specifically connects effects to producers

Assume A and B nevertheless have distinct products. If the products necessarily coalesce because neither producer can achieve its effect without the other, the source returns to dependent assistance.

P7. If their products need not coalesce but actually do, the contingent mixing requires an efficient producer. Under the stipulated ultimate pair, no independent third producer exists. The earlier exclusion of derivative coproduction is used when the text turns to one of the pair producing the mixing.

B1. If A mixes B’s product with its own, A influences what B’s will and power determine. If B consents, this is assistance; if B does not, restriction. The author claims both imply influence upon B’s determining will/power and therefore defeat its original independence. (Oneness30–32; Asbahani144–146.)

P6. Complete power and determining will imply the exact intended effect. An alteration contrary to that determination would thus require altered determination or incomplete power.

The contrary-will branch has a relatively direct structure if exactly the same target, time and respect are held fixed. The consent branch requires more: that the coalescent completion supplied by another is a received perfection or element of adequate determining agency. P6 alone does not say that every later state or every interaction of a product was itself exhaustively specified by the first maker’s original act. The strong source claims this closure through P2b/M1 and its conception of lordship. This is the exact place to test a purported consenting countermodel, rather than merely say that consent prevents conflict.

### Stage F: actual interconnectedness excludes the separate-domain remainder

C1. The preceding exclusions leave each original maker confined to its own independent, noninteracting production domain.

O1. Actual creation is interconnected: generation from prior living/material sources; dependence on air, water, soil and other creatures; sunlight affecting earth; vapour/dust/clouds affecting the light and atmosphere. Thus the actual world does not exhibit such independent domains. (Oneness32–33; Asbahani146–147; Minhaj2:180–181;3:317.)

This uses genuine causal/material relations, not merely a shared name “world.” The claim that all actual creation forms the relevant connected field is stronger than saying each node has some neighbour. Multiple internally connected components would satisfy the latter weak claim. The source intends the stronger connected whole, illustrated by cross-domain dependencies. An invented disconnected cosmos does not answer the empirical argument about this connected creation, and the editorial note26 should not be substituted for an authorial proof about every conceivable world.

The unequal-lords branch further argues that a genuinely superior power can dominate the inferior, whose efficacy then requires assistance or forbearance. Such an inferior is not an original lord. Its generalization from unequal abilities to globally comparable domination is another substantive premise, not a consequence of mere inequality between sets of powers. (Oneness37–40; Asbahani150–152; Minhaj3:323–325.)

## 5. Is the trilemma exhaustive?

The superficial enumeration is: two entire producers; cooperation requiring another; or independent distinct products. As a classification of ordinary causal situations, this is not exhaustive without specifying numerical effects, partial contributions, reception, temporal stages and the strength of independence. The source itself allows differentiated contributions in ordinary cooperation.

For the intended original-lord profile, the elimination is stronger: actual duplicate whole production violates P4; needed contribution violates P2b/M1; optional mixing is assigned to assistance/restriction by B1; necessary concord is assigned to reciprocal productive completion by B2; an external maker of the pair’s powers removes their ultimacy. On those premises the remaining independent domains are excluded by actual interconnection.

Thus the trilemma is exhaustive only relative to the argued metaphysical closure of those causal roles. It is not legitimate to weaken that closure and announce a counterexample to the original argument. Equally, calling the list exhaustive cannot establish the closure premises. The right challenge isolates one of those bridge claims and preserves all independently retained earlier premises.

The new value is not a new generic dependence slogan. Efficacy itself is called a perfection, and dependence for it is incompatible with original necessity. The human/material/air/water example directly targets distributed creators whose products meet in one dependent creature. Earlier broad statements of P2 should be split into intrinsic possession of power and independence of productive completion.

Classification: restatement plus strengthened multi-branch argument; direct answers to necessary agreement, concurrent wills, common benefit, foreknowledge and ordinary cooperation models.

## 6a. Majmu 20:174–183: two additional explicit bridges

This supplementary passage was directly read from the companion reader’s captured primary-text display, L906–926, after the initial reconstruction. The IDs below match MODEL_AUDIT.json. The [primary passage](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/2032/) is another authorial argument, not an English translator’s addition.

### B6_EXCLUSIVE_COMPLETION, printed176–177/L912–913

The author does not stop with “complete will plus complete power entails the outcome.” He says the effect must exist through that agent alone. If a second agent also has complete determining power and will for that very numerical effect, the first is both its sole producer and not its sole producer. He calls this intrinsic opposition, as distinct from opposed intentions.

Printed175/L908 anticipates the example of someone lifting a log or cooking with another and later doing the same alone. Its answer is that will, motion, instruments or another feature of the productive condition must change. The comparison is explicitly under unchanged agent-state and actual productive determination. A model that retains general ability while varying effort, the act, will, instruments, target specification or which producer actually acts is not a countermodel to this fixed comparison.

B6 nevertheless deserves a separate ID because exclusive numerical causal authorship is stronger than outcome necessitation. Calling an agent’s power “complete” can include independence from another’s contribution, but if complete power only means counterfactual sufficiency, the no-overdetermination conclusion still needs its own causal argument. The author argues for it; he does not merely omit the issue. No current model has been verified with all those strong conditions while falsifying B6.

Classification: strengthened premise and answer to a changed-effort/merely-standing-power countermodel; locates a substantive outcome-entailment to sole-production inference.

### B5_TRANSFER, printed178–181/L916–921

The author asks whether each currently cooperative producer can acquire power sufficient to act alone. If an effect is possible for two, he argues, it is possible for one: either the obstacle belongs to the effect, whose possibility does not change with the number of producers, or it belongs to their powers. The powers present in two bearers can, he argues, exist in one united bearer without diminishing, indeed with greater strength. He then infers that each existing partner is receptive to whole-alone power; each can therefore be more perfect than it currently is. Since neither can complete itself unaided, neither is necessary and self-sufficient.

This is not a bare inference from “the result has two inputs” to “the sources were created.” It explicitly supplies the productive-perfection bridge and a modal capacity-unification argument. It also creates a precise transfer question: possibility of one bearer with the combined powers is not, simply as a matter of quantifier logic, possibility of that power for each already identified partner. The source moves between those propositions at179–180. To evaluate it one needs a defended account of bearer identity, admissible modal changes, and why the relevant perfection is eligible for each original. Labeling the united entity a mereological sum does not itself answer the source; calling it one bearer does not prove the identity-relative transfer either.

At180–181 the source expressly concludes that dependence in an external product entails dependence in the source’s intrinsic act and quality. That strengthens and helps explain M1/P2b and B1. It makes a thin distinction between external effect-conditions and internal power an insufficient response unless that exact transfer is addressed.

Classification: explicit strengthened modal/metaphysical premise; answers the “each original is essentially limited to its own partial power” response; exposes an identity-relative modal-transfer obligation rather than establishing a countermodel.

## 7. Exact status of the controls and the next philosophical judgment

The prior finite Ability graph, a CND switch, an external noncausal ground, and an entity outside the model’s evaluated field do not establish a source-faithful countermodel. They fail, or leave undefined, one or more of original full productive independence, complete will, corresponding target powers, numerical-effect individuation, reciprocal completion and field coverage. MODEL_AUDIT.md contains the independent control-by-control assessment.

A valid countermodel must specify which conclusion is false and keep every earlier accepted premise fixed in meaning. It cannot preserve only unbestowed partial power and claim to preserve the source’s complete original productive independence. It cannot replace “complete determination of e” with “contribution to e if B supplies the rest.” It cannot replace a genuinely shared numerical outcome with two labelled effects, or treat a common abstract reason as a third efficient maker without argument.

Conversely, if the disputed inference is B1, B2, B3 or M1’s transfer, a model is not disqualified merely for falsifying that exact inference. It is disqualified if it achieves that by also falsifying independently retained premises. The distinction matters: otherwise the requested countermodel test becomes circular.

My bounded assessment is that no full-premise countermodel has been established, and the actual source’s conditional exclusion is much stronger than the earlier thin models engaged. There remains a precise substantive warrant issue about productive completion and its intrinsic bearer, especially the consent/coalescence branch, B6’s exclusive-completion inference and B5’s identity-relative capacity transfer. That issue is not a demonstrated refutation. The philosophical reassessment must say whether it accepts that source-specific metaphysical principle, withholds it, or has an actual preserving countermodel. A merely imaginable cooperative story cannot carry the verdict.

This source reconstruction does not replace the separately authored 14:34 philosophical reassessment by editorial decree. It supports a direct reassessment of actual common creation and divine agency separately from the stronger unrestricted SourceMode schema.
