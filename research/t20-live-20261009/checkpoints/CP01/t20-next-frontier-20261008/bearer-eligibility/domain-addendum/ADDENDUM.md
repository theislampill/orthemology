# Addendum: universal actual dependence is stronger than ordinary omnipotence

8 October 2026 UTC. This addresses the eligibility review’s domain concern and preserves `CURRENT_FINDING.md` unchanged. It qualifies the synthesis, not the primary text. No new source acquisition, whole-reading credit, or global closure is claimed.

## The source has a potentially distinct uniqueness route

Akmaliyya6:125, canonical L216–223, compares one being whom everything needs, while it needs nothing, with one whom only some things need. The author also compares independent production of the whole with assisted production of a part. These formulations should not all be reduced to a claim about whether a particular coactive effect has an entire original provider.

If the first comparison warrants the norm that **every independently original necessary bearer must be the original ground on which every other actual external bearer depends**, it yields uniqueness directly. Let A and B be distinct proposed necessary originals. B is an actual external bearer relative to A, so the norm makes B originally dependent on A. That contradicts B’s proposed underived self-sufficiency. A symmetric argument is available but is not needed for this contradiction.

This conditional inference does not use nonabsorption of the common created effect. It therefore supplies a potentially distinct source route, provided its stronger universal-supremacy norm is independently warranted. The preceding report’s focus on the local actual-efficacy question must not make this broader source claim disappear.

## Four domains and claims

1. **Ordinary power over coherent possible objects:** A can perform every relevant intrinsically possible task. This is not a demand to do contradictions.
2. **Original provision of all actual created effects:** every realised created effect has its original basis in A. This is not by itself a statement that every other actual bearer is created.
3. **Universal actual supremacy:** every actual bearer external to A depends originally on A, including a purported peer if one is admitted into that domain. This rules out a second underived original.
4. **Plenitude:** every possible external good or creature is actually produced. Neither2 nor3 entails4, and the source’s wise particular-act discussion rejects using perfection to require every possible act or product at once.

“External bearer” matters. A’s own inhering attributes cannot simply be inserted as separately created things; the source explicitly distinguishes their necessary belonging to A from efficient production. “Dependence” must likewise mean the relevant original/ontological dependence, not mere relational co-occurrence, inclusion in a description, or the tautology that co-producing with B requires B.

## Why creating a necessary peer is the wrong test

Akmaliyya6:86, canonical L073, expressly treats creating something that remains necessary through itself as contradictory. Consequently an opponent cannot be refuted by demanding that A create the putatively underived B while preserving B’s underived status. Failure to perform that description is not an ordinary limitation of power.

Nor should the universal-supremacy argument be defended by silently replacing the domain of all actual **created** effects with all actual **external** bearers. Whether the proposed peer belongs in the former domain is part of the conclusion sought.

The source’s stronger argument instead contests B’s classification: if universal actual supremacy belongs to original perfection, B cannot be an underived peer in the first place. This is comparative disqualification, not a demand for contradictory production.

## What warrants the stronger norm?

The source offers a positive comparative judgement: complete original governance, provision, and freedom from need are superior to a partial field of original efficacy. This can support a substantive account of absolute lordship rather than a desire for exclusive credit. It deserves consideration as rational comparative warrant, even though it is not an analytic consequence of modal necessity.

Two limits remain precise:

- **Direction of dependence:** A’s not needing an external source does not, just by logical reversal, entail that every external being needs A. The latter is a stronger claim about the extent of A’s actual original explanatory role.
- **Pure-perfection application:** the universal-supremacy property must be shown to be an eligible pure perfection of an underived original, not merely assumed because it excludes peers. Its stronger scope is not established solely by a standing constructor for every created target or by the impossibility of external supplementation of A’s intrinsic attributes.

The earlier donor-quantifier audit remains correctly limited to realised created perfections. It does not prove universal supremacy. The a-fortiori/perfection argument can supply an independent warrant, but its application must be assessed at this stronger relational scope. An essential-nonreceptivity label alone is still not a successful answer once that pure excellence has been justified; the new source reading already showed why.

## Corrected synthesis

Two affirmative routes should remain visible:

- The local route: genuine peer participation allegedly limits or completes A’s actual efficacy, contrary to unborrowed original perfection.
- The universal route: an original bearer must occupy universal actual supremacy, which already excludes an underived external peer.

The local route requires the nonabsorption/actual-agency application. The universal route requires substantive warrant for the stronger pure supremacy norm and its domain. They are related through the source’s account of perfection but are not identical logical arguments.

Neither route requires plenitude, and neither may demand creation of a being while it remains uncreated. This clarification preserves the strengthened comparative appraisal while preventing ordinary omnipotence or an all-created-effects conclusion from being promoted to universal actual supremacy without argument.
