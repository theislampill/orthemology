# Final-quarter appraisal

Dated Nubuwwat-stage assessment, preserved with its actual evidence boundary. The later Safadiyya study and final current appraisal are separate records; this text is not retrospectively revised into their verdict.

## Scope and judgement

This appraisal follows the full continuous reading of volume 02 windows 013–025, NB-S002-L0139–NB-S003-L0078,26,385 words, followed by bounded visual returns to printed 946–957,1064–1071 and 1085–1087. It is the last contiguous division, not a separate whole-book claim.

The final quarter strongly reinforces a purposeful and morally intelligible model of knowledge and divine attestation. It also supplies unusually clear safeguards against conflating generic creation with communication, a token with its intended use, capacity with exercise, or a truthful sender's commission with unbounded credibility assigned to every intermediary. I judge those conceptual distinctions sound and significant. The source's full positive application remains substantially conditional on its account of wisdom, the independent identity of the sender, and the occurrence and classification of particular signs. This is neither a refutation nor an independently completed historical authentication of those applications.

## Positive case at its strongest

S003L14–16 argues that ordered arrangements understood as apt means to ends signify knowledge, specific willing and wisdom together. If the eye's location, a vessel's opening or the form of a house evidences knowing through its suitability for a use, denying every purpose while keeping precisely that evidence is unstable. Perfect knowledge is then connected with willing the fitting rather than arbitrarily inferior course. In the source's account, a wise creator does not systematically deprive creatures of every means of distinguishing his true envoys from impostors while demanding their obedience. This is a substantive reason for truthful guidance; it is more informative than attaching a benevolent label to unknown agency.

The argument's normative premise has to be evaluated, not merely dismissed because it is normative. A demand accompanied by intentionally indistinguishable contrary credentials would indeed defeat the demand's publicly intelligible justification. The human-envoy analogy therefore has real philosophical force once the sender and relevant circumstances are known. The source allows immediate skilled recognition as well as articulated inference, and it treats qualities of informants and context as important rather than fixing a magic number of witnesses (L54–55). Repeated signs and corroborating persons need not be opponents: evidence of one true commission can be instantiated through several faithful agents.

Printed 946–957 clarifies the inference. An originated thing can indicate an originator independently of knowledge about communicative intention. An entrusted ring, speech or gesture instead indicates a message through intentional designation. A similar physical phenomenon can occur without being that intentionally designated true sign. Printed 952–953 rejects an argument that withholding an act destroys the power to perform it. Printed 1064 rejects using universal divine causation as a discriminator between miracles and other happenings. Printed 1065 permits mediated action. Printed 1085 allows an answered prayer even for a distressed unbeliever. Taken together, these distinctions establish that generic source dependence plus coincidence with a claim is not the source's own account of warranted endorsement.

## Remaining burdens

1. **Purpose from order.** The source exposes an inconsistency in borrowing an openly purposive description while denying all ends. It does not follow that every nonintentional account of functional organisation is incoherent. Whether the actual order sufficiently favours intentional intelligent origination is an explanatory comparison, not settled by the semantic content of an already purposive description. Specific historical physiology in L14–15 is not independently vindicated here.
2. **Knowledge, goodness and will.** The text treats perfect knowledge and perfect preference as excluding inferior action. In imperfect beings, knowledge of the better does not ensure choosing it. The stronger divine conclusion rests on perfection and intelligible willing, not knowledge alone. Neither suffering nor obscure purpose can be addressed just by asserting that there must be a good end; the relevant grounds for that confidence matter.
3. **History-wide regularity.** Divine customary support is argued by analogy with familiar stable practices and the histories of prophets (L30–36). Those are meaningful inductive materials if established, but cross-tradition independence, representative sampling and the meaning of 'lasting support' must be investigated. Ordinary regularities do not transfer their empirical strength to a disputed moral-historical universal automatically.
4. **Definition and instance.** A genuine conclusive proof cannot prove falsehood. That principle does not establish which striking occurrence is such a proof. The source supplies multiple discriminators, so its position is not mere stipulation; nevertheless, their observable application must avoid defining 'true prophetic kind' through the very conclusion to be established.
5. **Specific truth versus general authority.** Attesting a commission does not entail that every later statement by a follower or every attributed report is true. The opening of the book and the private-token passage elsewhere supply explicit scope restrictions; the final quarter's broad 'signs support all true reporters' concerns one specified proposition. It should not be expanded into blanket immunity for all those persons.
6. **Alternative origins.** Claims about human, jinn and angelic capacities are doing discriminating work. They remain a substantive ontology and asserted history, not an independently exhausted list of rival explanations. The author's criticism of bare local ignorance applies to an interpreter's own ignorance too.

## Broad source portrait

The last quarter includes extensive philology, moral psychology, political modes of prophetic leadership, created cooperation, source criticism, report epistemology, ancient natural explanations, and the history of rival schools. Its discussions of mistaken attribution, counterfeit appearances, context-dependent language and the reputational cost of denying what another person has witnessed are notable beyond the miracle dispute. Its severe judgements about groups, reported violent practices and claimed supernatural incidents are historical source material, not endorsed practices or independently verified facts.

The received text's final subject returns to the epistemology of sense, reason and report and the critique of reifying mental universals. This is a philosophically structured culmination rather than a catalogue that simply stops after wonders. The edited-print/electronic discontinuity at 1067–1068 is preserved; the broad doctrinal continuity cannot justify inventing its missing grammatical bridge.

## Bearing on one actual common creator

The positive model is a unified explanatory picture of knowing, willing, creating and guiding. That unity offers an actual common-creator hypothesis more content than numerical simplicity alone. The final quarter nonetheless mostly reasons inside the commitment to one creator and argues how signs function there. Allowing secondary causes is compatible with that view but does not by itself exclude a principled plural original basis. No automatic change of the earlier independent actual-common-source verdict follows. The book adds genuine reasons and constraints; it does not turn a disputed universal source-mode principle into a settled theorem through its attestation vocabulary.
