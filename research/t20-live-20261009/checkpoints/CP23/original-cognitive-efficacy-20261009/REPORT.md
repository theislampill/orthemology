# Original cognitive efficacy: living will, productive willing, and the remaining bridge

9 October 2026 UTC. Bounded source and premise audit. No protected edits, integration, closure, new model, numerical recalibration, metaphysical-possibility certificate, or historical research-duration claim.

## Result

**The source has a route against a wholly will-less cognitive original that is independent of Darʾ 10:144's disputed local transition: living cognition entails willing, although cognition and willing are not identical.** Majmūʿ 18:310–312 states this within a discussion of divine attributes. The newly acquired print witness confirms both the implication and the denial of identity. This does not establish that the original's entire particular production is practically determined simply because its bearer possesses will.

**The general life–will principle is inherited, not newly discovered.** Shifāʾ e306 already explicitly calls awareness and willing inseparable from the rational soul; Jāmiʿ VI's first treatise already rejects complete elimination of a living person's will. The increment here is the freshly checked divine-attribute application, its print control, and the separation of its bearer-level conclusion from the act-level conclusion now at issue.

A connected return to Ṣafadiyya's actual intellectual-production reply adds a second exact control: **its quoted Najāt target already calls the first an intellectual willer.** The reply's objections to identifying knower, cognition, power and will cannot be counted indiscriminately as new arguments against a producer that denies all practical willing. Its strongest remaining act-level exclusion is substantive: knowing an order without willing it does not produce it. The surrounding anti-identity and correspondence arguments do not independently establish that exclusion for every original cognitive productive constitution.

The affirmative practical-perfection appraisal remains strong. The surviving dispute is neither a bodily-awareness counterexample nor a request for a norm-free deduction. It is whether the proposed original's operative cognitive differentiation amounts to apprehended-end-directed determination, and, if it does not, whether such an omission is compatible with complete original practical excellence.

## 2. A newly controlled locus: Majmūʿ 18:310–312

### Continuous context and speaker

This short authorial discussion explains the prayer that the Qurʾan become the heart's spring and the breast's light. It connects life and illumination with human perfection, then discusses attempts to reduce the divine attributes to being living and knowing. Al-Ghazālī and philosophers are described by the author; their characterizations are not independently authenticated here.

The author's own response expressly says life entails the attributes under consideration, and that necessary connection is useful in inference and existence but does not identify their meanings. In particular, being a knower does not mean being a willer. Thus the source can maintain real entailment without treating two different descriptions as synonymous. It likewise need not turn each attribute into a separately subsisting agent or an extra executive event.

This is a genuine answer to the suggestion that conceptual distinction by itself establishes separability. **Different content does not prove separate possible existence.** Equally, the text's claim of inseparability is not made true simply by noting that both are attributes. The disputed general life–will connection is a substantive source commitment.

## 3. Executed test: from a willing bearer to its productive activity

Keep one actual original, its complete intrinsic cognitive productive constitution, and its actual production fixed throughout the comparison. Grant intrinsic cognition with a genuine causal role; do not replace it by awareness of a bodily occurrence, an external spectator, or cognition appended to an independent mechanism.

There are two distinct denials:

1. **No willing anywhere in the bearer.** On the source's life–will principle, an actually cognizing living original cannot satisfy this description. The rival has to contest that principle's application or truth. Merely saying that willing is absent by essence is no reply to its independently defended perfection eligibility.
2. **Will exists, but this productive activity does not proceed through practical determination.** A conclusion that the bearer is a willer does not settle this. Its willing might concern something other than the productive activity under examination. The next premise must relate will to this original's productive operation.

This is a scope audit, not a certified possible mixed producer. It yields no argument that such a producer exists, is eligible, or is equally credible. It prevents a genuine bearer-level source answer from being overstated as the complete act-level answer.

Neither exhaustive knowledge nor complete original provenance automatically supplies that missing relation. Exhaustive knowledge excludes ignorance of the production. Complete original provenance excludes a foreign original producer or borrowed completion. Both are substantial restrictions. But their content does not yet say whether the original's cognitively discriminated efficacy operates as a practical exercise for an apprehended end.

Nor may the act-level bridge be supplied through an unannounced control principle:

- Knowing the outcome is distinct from having power to prevent it.
- Having that power and not preventing it is distinct from positively producing it through willing.
- Permissive noninterference is distinct from practical approval of the outcome.
- Creative willing is distinct from loving or approving every derivative feature for itself.
- An originally established productive order and each derivative occurrence within it need not correspond to separately individuated micro-volitions.

Unthwarted willing means that what is actually willed is not defeated. It does not, solely by that implication, establish that every occurrence is positively willed, still less that each occurrence is intrinsically approved. The stronger claim that nothing occurs without creative willing requires its own warrant and appropriate original/derivative scope. These distinctions are inherited safeguards, applied here rather than rediscovered.

## 4. Ṣafadiyya addresses the cognitive producer, but not by one undifferentiated proof

Read continuously: retained S003 L357–390, with the main Najāt quotation/reply at L374–390. [Primary electronic subsection](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/alsfdit-003.html).

### A. The quoted thesis includes intellectual will

L375 introduces a Najāt chapter on the first's knowledge, power, willing and life being one in concept. L375–377 describes apprehension of the whole good order as its productive principle and explicitly calls this a distinctive intellectual willing. Ibn Taymiyya begins his reply at L378.

Consequently, the literal reported target is not simply “an intellect which denies every kind of will.” Part of the controversy concerns whether an expressly admitted intellectual volition can have the same reality/concept as cognition and power. The primary Avicennian thesis must be checked in its own source separately; this report establishes only what Ṣafadiyya quotes and argues against.

### B. Four different objections and their actual force

1. **Knower, knowledge and known:** L379–382 denies that the subsisting producer, its knowing, and the produced order are numerically identical. This is relevant against the reported reduction. A rival that already grants a real cognizing bearer and real productive power is not refuted merely by repeating that the known order is not its knower.
2. **Cognition alone and productive adequacy:** L383–384 says practical cognition is a condition rather than, by itself, the sufficient producer. Conceiving something does not universally make it exist. This challenges an unsupported assertion of intellectual sufficiency. It does not alone exclude a uniquely original cognitive power with an independently warranted productive constitution, and it is not directed against only bodily awareness.
3. **Correspondence and selection:** L385–387 asks why this particular good order rather than another is actualized. Cognition as truthful correspondence would concern whichever order was actual. The source rightly demands an account of the productive discrimination; correspondence alone does not provide one. But object-correspondence cannot be treated as incompatible with all causal efficacy, since Darʾ112 and Bayān explicitly admit operative cognition which also corresponds to its object. Nor does counterfactual covariance, by itself, prove a reverse efficient dependence. The force is the demand for an independently warranted specifying role, not the bare fact of truthfulness.
4. **The direct volitional exclusion:** L388 expressly asserts that a bearer which knows the good order but does not will it cannot perform it; one which wills without power also cannot. This is the relevant substantive premise. L389–390 reinforces the need for productive power and rejects both a divine power-without-knowledge account and knowledge-without-power account. Those neighbouring conclusions do not alone prove that every adequate original cognitive power must be volitional.

The source therefore does not overlook the actual intellectual producer. It presents a differentiated challenge to that producer's identity claims, sufficiency, and practical explanation. But the inference to will cannot be made independent merely by citing the whole cluster as though each objection separately entailed L388.

### C. Bayān corroborates the evidential burden, not a shortcut

The already studied Bayān L76–77 distinguishes productive cognition from merely object-conforming cognition, then appeals to the experienced distinction between apprehension, desire, means, and willing the means. Its human practical examples support the reality and difference of those roles. It also says a claim about cognition's unobserved sufficiency would require argument by generalization or analogy. The bounded rereading preserves that challenge.

There is no exemption for the rival: stipulating an intrinsic cognitive configuration “which produces just this” is not an independently established explanation. But a diagnosis that the rival still owes its positive metaphysics is different from a demonstration that original cognitive efficacy entails practical willing. The latter requires the substantive account of practical discrimination, or the separately defended purity and eligibility of wise self-governance.

## 5. Affirmative appraisal and exact stopping point

The general life–will connection provides positive source evidence against a wholly will-less knower. Its divine application is now directly controlled. The practical-perfection comparison supplies a further positive reason: bringing about a fitting end because its fittingness is apprehended and practically owned is an intelligible excellence of the producer, beyond the quality of the output. Intrinsic knowledge, willing and power need not be alien contributions, and necessity through wise willing need not be involuntariness. The earlier strong affirmative appraisal is retained.

A rival whose intrinsic intellectual orientation actually directs production toward the apprehended fitting end may already grant the relevant non-needy willing. Denial of a second executive intermediary or of alternatives under identical complete determination does not establish a nonvoluntary rival. If it instead denies all practical determining force, it must defend the adequacy and eligibility of that limitation; equal useful output is not enough.

No new universal act-level exclusion was established by these additional loci. The honest obstruction is now precise: **the newly controlled life route establishes a willing bearer; the relevant Ṣafadiyya clause asserts the productive relation whose warrant is under examination; its anti-identity and correspondence arguments do not independently finish that relation.** Repeating those passages or producing a formal assignment with cognition true and will false would not improve the result.

The justified next comparison is the original intellectual-production account itself: does its alleged cognitive efficacy include intrinsic practical affirmation of a fitting end, or does it supply an independently supported mode of production without that relation? That primary-source task is being handled separately. The present report supplies the Taymiyyan tests and their scope, rather than prejudging its outcome. It does not authorize a general attributes campaign or claim T20 closure.

The newly supplied companion report05 and source ledger A11 were read. Their temporal-perfection result was already substantively present in the inherited Shifāʾ work. A targeted return to the retained chapter22 opening and e207–208 supports three affirmative claims:

1. A later occurrence's being good then does not show that its absence earlier was a missing required excellence. An act's time and conditions belong in the appraisal. The source explicitly says that what is not then a perfection is not then a deficiency through absence.
2. The agent's intrinsic wisdom is not a foreign supplier. Necessary qualification by one's own wisdom is different from being perfected by another independent producer. The local universal-creator formulation presupposes its stated comprehensive scope; it is not counted here as an independent proof of uniqueness.
3. Obtaining an intrinsically loved end in the fitting manner and time is positively appraised as excellent effective agency, compared with having no loved end or being unable to accomplish it. This is substantive practical-perfection support, not merely the stipulation of four distinct labels.

The argument leaves a precise burden on an objection: it must show either that the later act really remedies a perfection already required earlier, or that its occurrence depends on an independently foreign source of the original's relevant excellence. Merely calling all purposeful exercise “acquisition” does not establish either. Conversely, asserting fittingness does not independently justify a particular history; the source's general account is not a claim that we know every actual reason.
