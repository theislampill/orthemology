# Shifa al-‘Alil: middle electronic tranche appraisal

Historical appraisal recorded during the Shifa study on 7 October 2026, before the later Miftah, Nubuwwat and Safadiyya studies. Its stated judgement and reading limits are preserved; subsequent final appraisals are separate.

## Main positive result: real but non-independent created efficacy

The received text repeatedly and unequivocally rejects both an independently creating creature and an inert creature merely coinciding with divine production.

- E103–104 distinguishes God's creating/willing, Satan as external cause, and the subject's own love, aversion and turning away as internal causes.
- E122–125 quotes Juwayni's *Nizamiyya* approvingly as closer to truth than the Ashari/Baqillani accounts. The quotation insists real created power effects the action. E126 immediately criticises its conflation of cosmic will with divine love. Approval is selective; the quotation must not be presented as wholly unqualified authorial prose.
- E131 gives the author's direct formulation: acts truly belong to servants as acts and to God as effected objects; divine creating act and created product are distinct. The servant is active and acted upon in different respects, being made an agent by the creator.
- E143–146 supplies the clearest causal architecture: creature capacity, motives and decisive will form only part of a complete cause. The creator originates the power, recipient, instruments, environment, absence of obstacles and outcome. E146 differentiates divine creative production from creaturely proximate causation/direct performance.
- E176 applies ordered attribution to death through angels, strengthening through angels, revelation through the holy spirit, destructive cries, and Noah's loading the ark. The same occurrence can be attributed to divine creation and to a real subordinate cause under distinct relations.
- E188–189 explicitly affirms bodily powers and natures. Natural burning/heating/drowning are real effects even when not technical voluntary acts of a knowing agent. Denying them does not protect monotheism.

The text's incompatibility target is two coequal, independently sufficient effectors of one effect, not one independent source producing a subordinate efficacious agent. E150 says mutual-hindrance reasoning excludes a second lord whose power belongs intrinsically to itself rather than being received from the other; it does not exclude created causation.

## Capacity, will, knowledge, and actual effect

E104 distinguishes antecedent enabling capacity (organs, powers, available causes/conditions and access) from act-concurrent necessitating capacity. The former grounds obligation but does not itself entail performance; the latter entails occurrence. E110–112 reads prayers for help as asking for actualizing aid, not just the already supplied common organ-capacity.

E137 and E163 defend conditional ability: the agent could refrain if it willed, yet settled will with complete conditions makes action necessary. This is not a claim that the agent can actualize either contrary under an identical total causal situation. It is also not the modern libertarian claim of ultimately self-originating will. Distinguishing voluntary necessitation from literal compulsion is central. The analogy to divine willing attacks the inference from necessity to compulsion; it does not erase the difference between uncreated divine will and created human will.

E143's vision and striking examples distinguish intrinsic contribution from external completion. Seeing needs an object fit to be seen, healthy senses, and removed obstacles. Striking needs receptive target, space, no overpowering counterforce, and bodily integrity. Neither a will nor a muscular power alone is a complete sufficient cause. E189 then distinguishes three cases: God can abolish a cause's causal power, can install an impediment while that power remains, or can let the cause produce its effect. Power, causal relation, and actual manifestation are therefore not synonyms.

E164 sharply separates knowledge from productive will: knowledge discloses its object; it does not itself necessitate by efficient production. E149 says will is not identical with reflective awareness of willing. General awareness can suffice for action without exhaustive knowledge of bodily details.

E178 is a major control against atomistic reading: every particular will, breath, flowing droplet or uttered letter need not receive a separately individuated special divine volition. The general will that establishes the creature as naturally active/willing suffices, with special guidance through additional aid and removed obstacles. The paragraph nonetheless expressly retains universal divine decree, power and creation. It supports sustained created causal organisation; it does not license deism or independent nature. The text does not settle a complete metaphysics of individuation of divine volitions.

## Original sourcehood, essential perfection, and sufficient warrant

E109 denies independent created ownership, partnership, ministerial assistance, a substitute during absence, relief from fatigue, and unauthorized intercession. E118–119 develops creature poverty in preservation, worshipful beloved end, protection and forgiveness. E129–130 is particularly precise: God is intrinsically self-sufficient; his all-respects perfection belongs necessarily to his essence and is not acquired from creatures. Creature acts do not supply a divine deficiency. Wisdom and use of means are consequently not need for an outside helper.

E155–157 distinguishes divine active perfection from created outcome, and offers several temporal accounts. It rejects a regress of derivative independent efficient sources while distinguishing that from endless successive divine activity or possible beginningless species of created effects. Every individual creature can have a beginning although there is no first member of a type. These reported and partly approved alternatives must not be flattened into denial of every infinity or affirmation of a coeternal individual world.

E175–176 positively argues that newly occurring contingent will needs a complete differentiator and cannot be ultimately explained by finite creature self-originating wills or indefinitely transferred creature explanation. It combines this original grounding with immediate evidence of creature efficacy. This is pertinent to necessary/original-source warrant, but the tranche is not a clean standalone proof from the concept of necessary being alone to every divine predicate. Essential perfection, universal causality, scriptural witness and moral/introspective premises do additional work. The provisional acceptance of necessary-being sufficient warrant should therefore be retained only at its already specified scope, not silently enlarged.

E200–201 supplies an indirect methodological control, not a direct adjudication of the broader common-source/coequal-original hypothesis. E200 explicitly says bare power/will cannot discriminate truthful revelation from a liar's apparent success; wisdom and truthfulness are required. E201 says all possible opposites are known and within power, so those shared features alone cannot explain why a particular judgement is best. Likewise, among creatures, having a common maker does not erase differences in causal power, receptivity, impediments, moral description or intended end. These are the source findings. Their transfer to the research hypothesis is indirect: the insufficiency of common-maker provenance by itself neither refutes nor establishes the classification of concordant, underived, role-differentiated and nonexclusively sufficient originals. That separate question asks whether such originals violate an independently warranted complete-divinity norm. E129–130's intrinsic perfection/self-sufficiency and E145–150's qualifications on independent/coequal efficacy are relevant materials, but the exclusion of two independently sufficient effectors cannot simply be extended to a stipulated nonexclusive model without further argument. This tranche therefore does not settle that broader hypothesis merely through E200–201 or through common-maker insufficiency, and no change to the earlier provisional warrant assessment follows from that methodological analogy alone.

## Wisdom and moral/epistemic controls

The author's strategy is neither sheer sovereignty nor imposed creature-like utility. E125–128 rejects inferring divine love from universal cosmic willing, and rejects the idea that complete self-sufficiency makes all acts equally good or purposeless. E180 distinguishes an act considered intrinsically from its place in a wider order or its consequences: sin can be disliked in itself and decreed for another wise end.

Theological psychology is extensive: responsive versus hard or unstable hearts; light of fitra augmented by revelation; gratitude, repentance and prayer as real means; habit and chosen antecedents shaping later incapacity; love and fear guiding action. E116–118's long repentance/hadith dossier matters because dependence is also dependence on pardon and on a perfected beloved end, not only efficient production. E138 and E147–149 carefully separate causal agency, culpability, deliberate intention, physical manipulation, coercion, sleeping, intoxication, insanity and forgetting. Some unaccountable subjects still act causally; causality is not identical with responsibility.

E170–173 distinguishes morally inert omission from intentional restraint and hypocrisy, and partial propositional knowledge from fully operative knowledge with conditions present and obstacles absent. The latter ethical conception can risk circularity if defined solely by successful obedience. The text does acknowledge knowing rejection and supplied proofs, so it should not be reduced to 'all wrongdoing is innocent factual ignorance'.

E179–184's privation account does not deny positive pains or destructive causes. It distinguishes missing required goods, blocked reception, misuse of genuine capacities, and external harmful processes whose wider order is good. Claims about overwhelming goodness and the wisdom of every local harm remain theological assertions, not empirical measurements here. E184–185 raises the strongest objections, including infant/animal suffering, Iblis, religious deception, ruin and hell. E185–201 begins general principles rather than completing all particular answers. E163 openly limits a local argumentative burden and appeals to unknown wisdom; no comprehensive theodicy should be claimed on this tranche alone.

Hermeneutic controls recur: E110 declines an attractive infinitival reading of 37:96 in favour of contextual relative pronoun with inferential causal support; E159–167 carefully distinguishes fortune/misfortune from deeds and direct from derivative attribution; E192–193 gives four senses of naskh and three of ihkam; E195 disallows inferring identity in every respect from analogy; E201 distinguishes correct implication from exact intended exegesis. Polemical descriptions of rival groups remain reports within this source, not independently established historical verdicts.

## Corruptions and limitations that must travel with citations

The witness contains duplicated headings, repeated snippets within Qur'anic quotations, malformed punctuation and words, and apparently substantive errors. No silent repairs or source substitutions were made. Important examples include:

- E120: parallel chapter headings with الكتب and الكسب.
- E122: malformed قالك and حاظ! in a quotation introduction.
- E124: القدرة التي اخترعها العبد conflicts with surrounding claims of divinely created capacity.
- E129: السنة بلفظ الجبر conflicts with the j-b-l wording of the cited temperament narration.
- E138: doubled عدم عدم الطلاق.
- E143: خلق الدرك in the perception discussion.
- E146: إضافة السبب إلى مسببه, السبب أو المسبب, and قدرة الرب التي هي سبب have direction/conjunction/referent difficulties; intact adjacent sentences independently support the substantive interpretation.
- E157: وكلا الفريقين يقول أن ... مخلوق appears inconsistent with nearby account of uncreated divine act and may lack a negation.
- E172: a knowledge/ignorance compatibility sentence is difficult as transmitted.
- E178: لم يكن بذلك ضالا له is problematic in the surrounding defense of abandonment's justice.
- E189: الخبر لا يشبع appears in the ordinary feeding example.
- E200: quoted Shu'ara refrain العزيز الحكيم conflicts with ensuing power/mercy exposition.

These are flags, not a critical edition or exhaustive error inventory. The notes sometimes paraphrase the likely argument from redundant intact context while marking suspect wording. Exact quotation must reproduce this witness or await authorised collation with an independently identified text. Hadith grades and chains were read as the author reports them; no independent isnad adjudication, manuscript collation, print-pagination certification, or external opponent-text checking occurred. Ancient physiological/cosmological examples are explanatory history, not present scientific certification. Legal examples are source analysis, not contemporary legal advice.
