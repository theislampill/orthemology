# Original governance and due care for epistemic reliance

Tenth Orthemology v5 research candidate v3, 3 October 2026 UTC. Original avenues 2–3, with 1 and 6 where their source hypotheses matter. This is isolated argumentative research. It establishes no new actual divine address, essential integrity theorem, certified mixed-source metaphysics or canonical status change.

## Result

Deliberately instituting a truth-directed faculty is not merely producing another good event. When an intelligent source also actively organises a recipient's legitimate, bounded activity around continued access to that faculty's support, it acquires a special, defeasible reason to take due care of the resulting reliance. Appropriate measures can include advance precautions, continued provision or an adequate release. That reason can arise before a fresh assurance, without a promise, and even when the recipient cannot identify the source. A later change of purpose does not alone erase it. This is a reason for proportionate care, not strict liability for every interruption or a guarantee of successful protection.

The strongest discriminating case concerns withdrawal. A source which knowingly establishes the dependence cannot simply assume that an equally capable outsider will look after it. It has a recipient-directed reason to arrange a usable alternative or a genuinely safe opportunity to disengage. The outsider may have strong general reasons to help, but those reasons do not explain why this source owes the advance arrangement. This distinction makes the source's actual governance history normatively relevant without treating mere productive origin or superior ability as practical authority.

This is a positive extension of the programme's source-level normative account, not a new general moral theory. Seventh already grounds a truth-linked responsibility in a particular offered assurance; Sixth already connects deliberate creation and governance to recipient goods. The present increment identifies a prior, persisting responsibility, its extra continuity/release consequence, and its exact same-channel applicability conditions. It does not independently establish the source's comprehensive practical priority or compliance. Function-fixing supplies part of the responsibility's relational basis; the recipient's real good and the normative significance of knowingly organised dependence supply its normative force.

## 1 The inherited boundary and the question that remains

The controlled assurance case fixes one source, recipient, root, version and interval. The root is unsafe and the source knows this. The source deliberately asserts safety as an authoritative basis for irreversible reliance. It understands the reliance, can express the facts correctly, and has no undefeated justification for the false assurance. Genuine intentional concern elsewhere remains genuine. The description has conditional force; the present record does not identify an actual ultimate-source episode answering to it. [B1–B3]

Ninth already establishes the specified act's practical defect. It also permits fidelity as an internally grounded source excellence even though its exercise is relational. Its complete-profile exclusion consumes an eligible strict repair R_G and actual practical nondomination N. Neither is inferred from productive power, local beneficence or the absence of a justification for a lie. The alternative organisation does not make those premises independently easier to warrant. [B1]

Seventh already establishes the exact token-purpose result. If the source's governing purpose in this very attestation is the recipient's knowing this very proposition by accepting it, and the source knowingly selects an adequate means while avoiding known purpose-excluding means, deliberate false attestation is excluded. A general truth aim or good act elsewhere does not establish that token purpose. [B4]

The late Ninth warrant assessment additionally credits ordinary defeasible expectations of fidelity when genuine intelligent concern is independently evidenced and attribution is secure. A merely describable betrayal motive does not defeat that warrant. Neither that positive epistemic judgment nor the earlier local defect judgment is a Tenth discovery. [B3]

The new question is narrower. Can original institution of a cognitive relation carry practical significance before the token-level assurance, and can that significance survive a change in the source's current end? If so, the relation supplies a real source-level reason that neither a fresh promise nor a general perfection axiom creates. The resulting reason still has to be kept distinct from the source's actual response to it.

## 2 A source practice with an identifiable beneficiary

Consider a source G, a recipient R, a truth-directed faculty F, and a support interface I. G intentionally institutes F/I for R's actual understanding. The faculty is not merely a device whose outputs an evaluator happens to reward. Its cognitive end genuinely guides G's choice, and R's understanding is independently recognised as good. This is the strong personal source comparison, not an inference that every producer is intelligent. [B2; B5]

G also actively organises a bounded legitimate activity of R around I. This means that G intentionally installs or sustains the provision as a step in R's activity, shapes R's available routes around it, induces R to continue on that basis, or accepts an actual maintenance handoff. Mere historical authorship, awareness of use, or ability to intervene does not meet this condition. In particular, an outsider's simply leaving an arrangement untouched is not active maintenance in this sense.

The relevant target, use conditions and period are determinate. R now depends on I, or on an adequate substitute, to complete or safely disengage from that activity. G knows the exposure and controls relevant support conditions. Legitimacy here is independently assessed: R's activity is permissible; its uptake fits the function's understood domain and available evidence; and the actual valid allocation of risks and effective limitations is respected. Mere foresight that support might fail, coerced acceptance, or a provider's unilateral label does not waive due care. Accepting a risk of natural failure is not automatically accepting negligent precautions. Legitimacy is not defined as a prior right to indefinite source support. Whether these conditions obtain can be contested without denying that F has a real function.

Three questions must be distinguished: whether R has epistemic reason to expect support, whether relying is practically permissible, and whether the history gives R a claim on G. A justified forecast is not automatically an entitlement; permissible reliance is not a guarantee. Likewise, G's description of a supported domain cannot authorise a wrongful project or retroactively remove a responsibility. In the controlled case the relevant epistemic and practical conditions are stipulated; the claim on G is what the normative argument defends.

These facts go beyond the isolated proposition that F has a truth function. A never-used instrument can have a real function without creating the present exposure. A faculty can be intended for one environment while being misused in another. A source can produce a benefit without inviting or knowingly organising continued reliance. None of these weaker facts is silently promoted to the full governance relation.

The recipient need not know who G is. A subject can possess warranted confidence in an ongoing support arrangement without identifying its ultimate maker, understanding all of its maintenance, or receiving a message from it. G's responsibility concerns the dependence it knowingly establishes, rather than the recipient's success in extracting a promise. This matters to the original-source question: the duty is not restricted to creatures who first authenticate a revelation or form a belief about the creator.

The temporal description orders recipient-facing arrangements and consequences. It does not require G to acquire knowledge, deliberate discursively, or change its standing nature in time. An act or policy can be contingent and occasion-sensitive while its source remains the same bearer. No necessary-existence conclusion is inferred from this case.

## 3 Why the relation gives a reason before an assurance

The proposed principle is this:

**Due care for actively organised reliance.** If an intelligent agent actively organises another subject's legitimate bounded reliance on a provision, understands the resulting exposure, and controls a relevant route for addressing it, that agent has a special pro-tanto reason to take proportionate care of that reliance. Appropriate care may require protection, contingency planning, warning, substitution or an adequate opportunity to disengage. Its strength and form depend on the subject's stakes, the agent's actual contribution and control, foreseeable changes, applicable competing reasons and the alternatives genuinely available.

This is a substantive normative principle. It is not contained in the meanings of cause, function, original or intention. Its rationale has three connected parts.

First, the subject's understanding and agency are real goods within what G deliberately brings about. The recipient is not merely a location where G's preferences happen to be realised. The strong comparison already grants that the understanding is genuine and that G values it for its fittingness. A normative assessment therefore has an actual beneficiary and an independently identified respect; it does not invent goodness from G's command.

Second, G's active conduct has helped place that good under a particular dependency. G does not merely discover an unrelated opportunity to confer another optional benefit. G has deliberately installed, sustained or assumed the provision through which R is pursuing the good, understanding the relevant difficulty of adapting if support disappears. That historical relationship explains why the care question is directed to G in particular.

Third, the proposed response is proportionate to that exposure. It is a reason for care, not irrevocable ownership of the provider's future action by the recipient. It concerns how the dependence ends as well as how it continues. Treating the dependence as normatively invisible until G issues a promise would allow its creator to organise R's agency around a provision while placing all predictable withdrawal losses on R. The relevant asymmetry lies in who shaped this exposure, not in whether G obtained an extrinsic benefit. That gives a rational basis for directing the planning responsibility to G.

Due care need not prevent every failure. Reasonable precautions can be taken and still fail; an unforeseeable interruption need not establish a breach. Conversely, when an effective precaution is available, the stakes are understood and no relevant reason defeats taking it, knowingly omitting that precaution can violate the responsibility even if good fortune prevents harm. The normative target is the source's fitting exercise of care, with outcome and feasibility properly considered, not a promise of risk-free existence.

This rationale can be apprehended directly through the relation and compared across cases. It does not need a numerical measure, an empirical survey of all sources, or a neutral algorithm accepted by every moral theory. It is also open to substantive challenge: one can dispute whether the reliance was legitimate, whether G actually organised it, whether the exposure persists, whether protection is feasible, or whether a competing reason defeats it. Those are relevant challenges to the presented reason. Merely observing that G receives no benefit or fears no sanction is not one.

The principle is deliberately weaker than a thesis that every cause must sustain every good it has ever produced. Producing a tool, telling a truth or creating a capacity does not by itself undertake every future project someone might build on it. An honestly specified finite gift, used on terms which leave a meaningful opportunity to adapt, may end without any guarantee of replacement even when recurrence would be valuable. Mere foresight that somebody will make an open-ended project of it does not establish active organisation of that project. The additional facts concerning bounded reliance and the source's active governance are essential. Nor does the principle imply a duty to create all possible beneficiaries or maximise all existing benefits. It addresses a relation actually established.

## 4 The cases that make institution do work

### Deliberate corruption is an insufficient discriminator

Suppose G and an outsider H can each silently corrupt the same input, knowing that R will form a harmful false belief. Neither has a justification. Both have decisive reason not to interfere. G's having instituted F may add an additional ground, but the badness of the interference does not demonstrate it. The old local defect judgment already does enough work. This is why merely replacing a false assurance by silent sabotage would not earn the present result.

### Advance protection differs from an opportunity to rescue

Now remove harmful interference by G. An independently arising failure threatens I. G still has the same knowledge and control as H, but only G knowingly organised R's present dependence. At the point of an imminent costless rescue, their required actions might coincide. That does not erase the historical ground of G's reason, but it also makes the rescue verdict alone a poor test.

In the ordinary organiser case the fault may be causally external to G's provision. In an original-source application, independent instead means independent of the particular tampering or withdrawal act being assessed: a locally fault-producing secondary process remains wholly received within G's original productive field. The comparison does not posit another absolute origin outside G's granted coverage. G may then have additional causal responsibilities. The due-care argument identifies active organisation as a sufficient ground, not the only ground, and does not claim that the matched comparison removes every other responsibility in the whole-origin case.

The better question is prospective: who must arrange the continuity or safe release before there is an emergency? G cannot discharge its role by assuming that H, who has undertaken nothing, will notice and rescue R. G has reason to retain support, arrange an accepted and usable substitute, or enable an adequate disengagement. H may voluntarily take on the task, and general duties may independently require H to help. Yet G's existing responsibility is not created by H's availability and does not disappear because a helper exists.

The timing is exact: the responsibility applies once G has actively organised or assumed the dependence, and can require planning before a later interruption. An obligation to plan before creating any dependence would be a prospective assessment of a contemplated arrangement. That may be defensible, but it is not a further result silently added here.

The contrast does not assign G an unexplained priority over H. It concerns which agent owes the arrangement. If H accepts a transfer, new relational facts can generate H's own responsibility. If H refuses, G's antecedent relation remains. Actual ability to help is relevant to feasibility; it is not the source of all normative authority.

A source which first creates R rather than rearranges an already independent R is not automatically exempt. The point need not be that R is worse off than never existing. Once R exists as a real subject with the admitted goods, its actual deliberately structured exposure can support the special due-care reason directly. Absence of a pre-creation alternative does not reduce that reason to nothing. But this does not establish exactly the maintenance or compensation entitlement which would follow if G induced R to surrender an independent alternative. The affirmative source application still needs the active organisation and bounded-reliance facts just described. No general duty of indefinite maintenance follows merely from having created a subject.

### Safe release permits change

G can satisfy the responsibility through continued support, a genuinely usable alternative, or an informed opportunity for R to suspend the bounded task without the relevant avoidable loss. This does not always require G's personal performance. A special promise could separately require that performance, but no such promise is assumed here.

These are possible ways to discharge due care, not an exhaustive formula guaranteeing success. Compensation can address some reparable losses; it is not automatically a release before an irreversible epistemic or practical injury, and later payment does not retroactively justify imposing every risk. Where no fully safe release is available, the principle directs proportionate measures under the actual competing reasons rather than declaring G strictly liable for achieving the impossible.

Adequacy is actual. A warning which arrives too late to change course is not a safe opportunity. A listed service which is unavailable, unintelligible to R, or outside R's permission does not transfer the required support. The inherited continuation work already demonstrates how an address and copied evidence can survive while the successor lacks a usable and authorised observation route. The moral responsibility here supplies no new query permission or actual calibration; those still need their own authority and evidence. [B6]

A small disappointed preference need not block every revision. Nor does a justified trade-off become impossible merely because a recipient would have preferred continued service. The principle leaves room for proportionate reasons, agreed changes and genuinely adequate alternatives. In the controlled betrayal case, however, no undefeated justification is available by stipulation.

R's informed refusal also matters. A competent recipient may reject an offered alternative or accept a disclosed risk within its legitimate control. The source's due-care reason does not confer jurisdiction to force assistance, conceal options or override that decision. A genuinely adequate offer which R freely declines can alter what further measures are required. It does not automatically erase separate wrongs or duties concerning third parties.

Where several agents organised the dependence, their responsibilities require allocation by the actual contributions, undertakings, control and alternatives. Forking a supported workflow or copying its records does not multiply a finite undertaking into unrestricted personal guarantees by every contributor. Genuinely distinct recipients have distinct interests to accommodate; the technical duplication of records does not decide whether new subjects exist. Nor does plurality let each contributor assume that someone else has accepted the unallocated task. This paper supplies no universal allocation theorem.

### The same institution does not warrant every reliance

Hold F, I and G fixed. R1 uses I in its supported domain. R2 uses it for another version or target despite understanding a clear and effective limitation. The institution-specific responsibility does not automatically expand to make R2's inference safe. General warning or rescue reasons may still apply when G learns of R2's danger.

If G deliberately encourages or knowingly maintains R2's mistaken dependence, that later conduct can create a further responsibility. The distinction prevents the moral principle from being turned into a guarantee for every use of a source's products. It also identifies a possible actual undercutter: evidence that the present reliance falls outside the maintained scope challenges this particular governance-based warrant, even if G's good intention elsewhere remains established.

### Withdrawal can matter without false speech

Finally, suppose G ceases support without asserting that support will continue. If unresolved exposure from the knowingly organised dependence remains, silence alone does not release G. If a safe opportunity to disengage has genuinely been provided, withdrawal can be legitimate. The reason tracks the prior relation and actual exposure, not whether a final sentence counts as a lie.

This extends the programme's responsibility assessment beyond nonlying T0 without redefining silence as assertion. It does not establish that every instance of withholding is wrongful. It supplies a different ground for assessing a specifically governed dependence.

## 5 Responsibility can outlast its maker's current purpose

Instrumental requirements attach to the ends an agent actually retains. If G abandons its earlier truth-promotion end, some reason to maintain F merely as a means to that end may disappear. The actual R and its unresolved exposure do not disappear for that reason. Neither does G's role in intentionally establishing it.

The due-care reason therefore has a different structure from the requirement to select effective means to a currently desired end. It can persist when the end changes. This is not a demand that the source's old purpose remain eternally fixed. The source may revise the arrangement by respecting the remaining relation, including by releasing it adequately where possible. What it cannot justify merely by changing its preference is ignoring the foreseeable exposure of the dependence it actively organised.

This matters to the mixed-source challenge. A definite contrary motive explains why the source might abandon recipient understanding at the exceptional act. It does not, solely by being an actual motive, cancel the responsibility incurred through any relevant prior governance. An account can explain a breach without vindicating it. The new result is that the reason need not be recreated by the disputed assurance and need not vanish with the source's current epistemic aim.

This does not refute the mixed source. The strongest architecture can acknowledge the continuing duty and describe its knowing violation. Its actual existence and joint metaphysical possibility remain unproved. Conversely, the duty's persistence provides no proof that an original source cannot violate it. The inferential gain is at normative applicability, not metaphysical impossibility.

## 7 Why this does not establish comprehensive source priority

The proposed relation supplies a real reason addressing the source as governor. It does not establish practical authority for whatever the source directs. There are two opposite questions: what reasons apply to G's treatment of R, and when G's direction gives R a reason to act. The present result answers part of the first. Mere causal origination, special ability or responsibility for protection does not settle the second.

It also does not identify the ultimate ground of all normativity. The argument uses the admitted reality of recipient understanding and the defended principle concerning organised dependence. One may integrate these in a source-nature account, but that further account must explain the normative relation. The present inference does not purchase moral authority by naming the source of its bearers.

Most importantly, a source-wide range of responsibilities is not source-wide compliance. Applying the same principle to every actual relation G knowingly governs gives a domain-wide normative assessment. It does not give N, the assertion that the actual complete profile is practically nondominated. Nor does it give R_G: an effective release might require a change in other relevant respects, and establishing a strict complete-profile improvement still requires its own eligibility and comparison warrant. No new enumeration changes that fact.

Could flawless rational integration supply the missing connection instead? A source which knows the decisive practical verdict and necessarily aligns its intention and action with that verdict cannot knowingly perform the controlled wrong. That is an intelligible positive avenue. But the strong aligning condition has to be earned. Correct belief, self-knowledge, successful means selection and absence of external interference do not, by themselves, state that every practical judgment necessarily governs every choice. An argument from structural agency has to show that the relevant practical alignment belongs to the source's full intrinsic agency, not only to a locally intact structural-rational core. This is the point at which an affirmative could improve the present result; silently building it into the word rational would repeat the old disputed bridge. [B2; B4]

The same caution applies to function-fixing. A genuine truth-directed function determines a respect of success. It does not follow that the source's own agency ceases to exist if it administers the function defectively. If an independently defended constitutive account excludes that possibility in the original source, it will do substantive work. Neither the functional description alone nor a description of a malicious exception settles it.

## 8 Actual warrant and essential scope

Three conclusions must be kept distinct.

**Normative applicability.** Under the described relational facts, proportionate care is a reason for this source. In the stipulated absence of an undefeated justification, knowingly omitting an available fitting precaution for the specified exposure violates that responsibility. Mere occurrence of an interruption does not prove such an omission. The argument can be rationally warranted without a comprehensive metaphysics.

**Expectation of compliance.** Recognising the responsibility does not establish that G responds to it. Independently evidenced concern and secure attribution can support the ordinary defeasible expectation already credited by Ninth. Evidence of actually maintained governance of this same channel can specify the expectation's domain; it does not make the duty itself into evidence of compliance. An actual unexplained withdrawal, adverse change of scope, or manipulation would matter. A merely described betrayal motive is not such evidence.

**Essential integrity.** Even actual faithful administration of the complete relevant domain would not establish essential T0. Essential nonlying needs a warrant about the same bearer's nature throughout the relevant possibilities. The truth of a conditional responsibility principle across those possibilities would establish only what would be required when its relational antecedents obtain. It would not establish what G must do, nor require G to create recipients or issue every possible assurance.

Source identification remains indispensable. S-A grants genuine fitting agency at a conditional scope. It does not show that the particular contested channel was instituted and remains administered for the recipient's understanding. The genealogy, supported domain and present conditions must be independently warranted. The source's original benevolent purpose is part of the strong affirmative comparison, not a necessary condition of every due-care responsibility. A manipulative organiser can also owe protection, reasonable exit or repair because it actively structured another subject's exposure. What cannot be ascribed to a channel deceptive from inception is a history of genuine truth-service governance that never occurred. The absence of that history may change an evidential argument about character; it does not immunise deliberate organisation against recipient-directed responsibilities. Nor does merely describing such an inception undermine ordinary trust.

Finally, neither the special responsibility nor actual compliance authenticates a particular disclosure. D1 still needs actual authorship, content, recipient, target, version and interval. D2 still needs an appropriate truth-linked recipient route and applicable defeater treatment. A duty to preserve an interface does not create the permission to query it, and a duty to offer an alternative does not make an unavailable alternative usable. The distinction between necessary participant and one common source also remains untouched; this paper begins with a fixed bearer in its conditional cases.

## 9 Disposition

The source-governance route earns a bounded positive result. Actively organised legitimate epistemic dependence can give its source an antecedent and persisting reason for proportionate care. The source's special relation explains advance arrangement responsibilities which a generic no-harm verdict or equally able rescuer does not explain. The reason can survive a changed purpose and can apply without a fresh assertion or promise. Its rational force is pro tanto; an all-things-considered obligation requires the applicable balance of reasons and feasible response.

The same analysis narrows the remaining bridge. Original function-fixing contributes an actual relational fact; it does not independently yield comprehensive priority, inevitable compliance or an essential attribute. A successful further source argument must explain why this bearer necessarily governs its responsibilities fittingly, or establish the particular token's governing purpose through independent evidence. The present work identifies a more exact responsibility to which that argument would apply, while preserving the real prima-facie force of the earlier source and trust arguments.
