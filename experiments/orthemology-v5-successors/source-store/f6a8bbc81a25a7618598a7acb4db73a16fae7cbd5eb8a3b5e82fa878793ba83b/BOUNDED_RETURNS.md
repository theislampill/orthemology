# Bounded returns after the first contiguous quarter

The initial 13 windows were completed before these returns. Page numbers are both observed print labels and PDF positions. Whole displayed pages, including visible notes, were read; only the claims below are drawn from the apparatus. No full-edition apparatus traversal is implied.

## Printed 237–243: wisdom, signhood and actual purpose
The context first distinguishes obligatory acknowledgement/worship from any one obligatory method of speculative acquisition. The following capacity dilemma and consensus objection are internal critiques of rival principles. Printed 239–240 gives the decisive positive concession: a genuine total truth-sign cannot coexist with the falsehood it rules out, but an outward phenomenon of the same general kind remains within divine power. Its misleading use is excluded through wisdom. Printed 240 explicitly makes the relevant relation the Lord's intention to attest the truthful person. Printed 241–242 connects arranged means and ends with knowing; removing all ends threatens the account of order on which the opponent's knowledge argument relies. Printed 243 concludes the reductio that mere restraint of competitors could otherwise turn ordinary conduct into a supposed miracle. The long modern notes quote other works; they are not additional lines of this authorial text. At 240 [بدليل] is an editorially selected reading and [الصادق] comes from the reported manuscript margin. The main argument is not based upon an unmarked conjecture.

Reflective result: 'necessarily truth-indicating' is not a claim that any bare unusual event or divine product necessarily certifies its apparent claimant. Wisdom and actual intended signhood do substantive work. The present passage alone does not identify that purpose in a historical episode.

## Printed 322–324: material-origination corruption
The electronic L115 contains a consequential omission. Printed 322 says: «والمشهود المعلوم للناس إنما هو إحداثه لما يحدثه من غيره، لا إحداثا من غير مادة». The electronic form drops the contrast's middle and can read as asserting observed origination without material. This is not harmless spelling variation. The raw electronic witness stays unchanged; any interpretation of this sentence must use the scan with explicit notice. Printed 323 retains another awkward formulation about bodies/material rather than solving every difficulty. The physical illustration on 324 is an author's premodern explanatory example, not an independently verified scientific account.

Reflective result: the source rejects the inference from divine origination to the requirement of an initially material-free event. The type/temporal-succession context must be kept distinct from a regress of dependent simultaneous creators.

## Printed 357–360: whose 'first opinion'?
Printed 357–358 discusses rival reductions of divine love after defending genuine responsive love. The author then returns to the earlier lexical issue of al-Wadud. Printed 359 note 2 expressly warns that 'the first opinion' here is al-Wadud interpreted as the one loving his servants, not the immediately preceding first denial of divine love. Printed 360 continues the reports and marks [قولين] as a marginal/interlinear manuscript addition. This control resolves an attribution risk rather than furnishing a new standalone divine-perfection proof.
