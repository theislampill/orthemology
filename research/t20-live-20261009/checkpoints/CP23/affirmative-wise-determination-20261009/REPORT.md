# Particular wise execution through a foreknown achieved good

9 October 2026 UTC. Additive affirmative T20 investigation. The existing sourcehood, intrinsic-necessity, knowledge, agency and pure-perfection warrants remain in place. No protected change, integration, closure, new universal theorem, metaphysical possibility certificate or retrospective duration claim.

## Result

**The source gives a concrete way for a particular wise course of action to depend on the actual completion of a good without the original acquiring knowledge or wisdom from that completion.** An actual response can be the intended good even when that response is already known. Once the response occurs, an eliciting means has accomplished its particular purpose. The same wisdom can therefore explain initiating the means and then ending it.

The worked case is Ibn Taymiyya's explanation of the almsgiving test at *Majmūʿ* 8:436. The purpose is to elicit the person's actual response; after his approving response, the angel tells him to retain his property, explaining that this was a test. The source distinguishes that achieved response from the ostensible transfer of property. Its own immediately preceding discussion affirms God's knowledge of creatures' actions before those actions occur. Consequently this case cannot faithfully be reconstructed as an experiment through which an ignorant original learns the answer.

The exact positive increment is **a source-grounded, staged practical inference**: a foreknown good's actual accomplishment defeats the claim that literal collection is still needed to achieve that same good; the eliciting interaction has served its purpose. The source supplies an intelligible reason for the concrete later directive, rather than merely attaching a name to an unexplained choice. It does not give a uniquely necessitating explanation of the initial test, every later detail, or the entire original history. Nor does removal of one instrumental rationale alone prove that every subsequent collection, solicitation or test would be forbidden.

This is a useful constructive instance of the inherited account, not discovery of a new doctrine. The currentness check defeats a broader novelty claim for endogenous practical context, intrinsic succession, non-unique optimization, and the distinction between general willing and separately indexed micro-volitions. Those results were already earned. The new work is the cross-context argument and its particular execution test below.

## 1 The source in its complete local setting

The retained continuous *Majmūʿ* 8:428–436 unit was reread in full. The author separates recognition of real good and bad from denial of divine determination. At 428–430, he explicitly affirms prior divine knowledge of creatures' acts and distinguishes that thesis from further controversies about creation and determination. At 430–431, he affirms real created causes, not merely coincident signs. At 431–434, he rejects both fixed act-class labels insensitive to circumstance and a will whose commands have no reason-sensitive good or bad content.

At 434–436, he distinguishes three ways moral/practical value can arise: an act's already relevant qualities; a qualification produced by an authoritative directive; and a testing purpose accomplished through the response to a directive without executing its ostensible object. The third is the present case. The author first uses Abraham's test, then the almsgiving narrative. The latter says that God sent the questioner, the person identified in the narrative as blind answered, and the angel then said:

> أمسك عليك مالك فإنما ابتليتم

Keep your property; you were being tested. The passage continues with a differentiated assessment of the respondents. This excerpt reports the answer and the favorable assessment, not the answer's full wording; “approving response” summarizes that context. No reading of the full cited hadith is claimed. The concluding authorial explanation locates the relevant wisdom in the directive rather than the ostensible commanded performance.

The source does **not** say that literal transfer was first the intended end and later ceased to be so. Literal transfer was not this test's intended endpoint. What changes is whether the eliciting interaction has accomplished its purpose. The distinction is essential to the inference.

## 2 The positive inference

Fix this one test episode, its intended actual response, and its announced purpose. Do not generalize from one completed response to all possible future responses.

1. **An independently stated end:** the elicited actual response is the purpose of the testing interaction. This is stated before the inference to retaining the property. The end is not defined retrospectively as “whatever the later directive realizes.”
2. **A genuine means:** solicitation provides the occasion for a voluntary answer. Mere prior knowledge that an answer will occur is not itself the person's act of answering. The source explicitly affirms created causal means in the surrounding unit.
3. **An independently identifiable completion:** the person's actual approving response is a distinct event. Whether it has occurred can be described without defining it as compliance with the later instruction to retain the property.
4. **Instrumental consequence:** once that answer has actually occurred, obtaining that same answer no longer requires a subsequent transfer of property. Insisting that transfer must occur because otherwise the test's purpose remains unfulfilled confuses the ostensible object with the actual end.
5. **Concrete wise execution:** the angel's instruction to retain the property is expressly explained by the testing purpose. The preserved property and completed test are an intelligible beneficent outcome. Actual collection is not required to make this achieved response real.

Steps 1–4 yield a definite exclusion: **the completed test cannot justify subsequent collection on the ground that collection is still needed to obtain this already accomplished response.** Step 5 supplies the source's actual later directive and its stated positive rationale. The argument explains an action-relevant difference between the stages; it does more than repeat that the later action was willed.

The conclusion deliberately has the right strength. A means may have more than one purpose. Completion of one end does not erase every independent rationale for an otherwise similar act. In this case the source expressly presents testing, rather than receipt of property, as the point of the interaction and explicitly directs retention. From the mere fact of completion alone, we do not derive a universal prohibition of later collection, a moral rule for ordinary donations, or a uniquely optimal possible ending of every test.

### Why this is not a selected choice hidden in the premise

Three items are separable: the good sought, the person's actual response, and the subsequent directive. The response could be stated as an accomplished approving response without mentioning the later directive. The aim of eliciting such a response likewise has content independent of the later wording. The relation between them explains why the test can be complete without transfer.

By contrast, defining the initial plan as “ask, receive precisely this answer, and then say precisely these words” would merely encode the conclusion. No such premise is used. The exact wording, timing and entire causal determination of the angel's instruction are not deduced from a generic end. What is derived is the practical reason for no longer treating literal transfer as necessary to this completed undertaking, with the source's actual directive as the controlled application.

## 3 Knowledge does not replace the good it knows

The complete local context rules out an otherwise tempting interpretation. The testing purpose is not to repair an ignorance in God. The source has just expressly affirmed prior divine knowledge of creatures' acts. The distinct object is the actual exercise of the creature's response in the created course of events.

Knowing that a response occurs at its proper time is different from that response's occurring before its proper time. Likewise, the truth of knowledge about an event does not make the event's productive means idle. If a factive knowledge claim includes the event's occurrence, one cannot hold that exact claim true while deleting its object and call the result a coherent counterexample. The present argument is a distinction between explanatory roles and temporal objects, not such a counterexample.

This supplies a positive answer to a specific worry about purposeful original activity: **foreknowledge does not make a wise test practically pointless, because the good can be the actual response rather than information about it.** The end can be foreknown, valued as an achieved good, and accomplished through the source's own adequate creative ordering. Nothing here requires the source first to lack knowledge or to receive wisdom from the answer.

This also clarifies the Knowledge–Will relation. Knowledge identifies the end, the actual response and the relevant means. Will concerns the end's actual realization through the undertaking. Wisdom relates the means and its appropriate continuation to that end. Complete knowledge alone is not being offered as a fresh sufficient producer; nor does the practical account require an additional separately subsisting executive entity. The inherited distinction between operative cognition and merely object-conforming cognition is retained.

The narrated messenger and the original must not be conflated. The text gives an angel's successive directives, a person's response, and a divine testing purpose. It does not identify the angel's temporal information state with God's, enumerate divine intrinsic volition-tokens, or establish a new essential attribute arising after the response.

## 4 Why the created response is not a donor of essential perfection

The achieved response is genuinely relevant. Removing its actual occurrence would remove the achievement whose completion makes the later instruction appropriate in this test. It is therefore insufficient to call the created event an idle decoration.

But its role is not to supply the original's essential knowledge, power or wisdom. It is the realized object of a purposeful course of action and a condition of applying the description “this test's good has been achieved.” The surrounding source treats both the messenger and the responding creature as within divine creative provision, while retaining real creaturely acts. No independently original peer is introduced to complete divine agency.

The distinction is supported by the inherited *Shifāʾ* e207–208 argument: an end may be excellent at its fitting time without its earlier absence being a missing required perfection, and intrinsic wisdom is not a separate outside donor. The same source at e211 distinguishes continually accomplished ends from an endlessly postponed end; e213 refuses to erase constitutive means merely by invoking unlimited power. These general positions are inherited, not newly proved here.

The argument therefore does not reduce the good to a wholly internal description. The person's real response matters. It also does not infer foreign existential or essential completion from that relational relevance. The positively defended account contains both: a real achieved good and unborrowed original excellence.

The intrinsic-necessity report's bearer-presupposition argument remains intact. An activity cannot supply the whole original bearer whose actuality that activity already requires. Here the target is a later stage of a course of action by an already actual original; an earlier accomplished event helps explain what that stage does. Applying the own-bearer prohibition to every earlier-to-later practical relation would change its target. The independently read source-noncirculation report expressly preserves prior acts as genuine conditions of later ones.

## 5 What is established about particular determination

There are three levels of result.

**First, a particular practical discrimination is supplied.** Before the response, solicitation can be part of eliciting the test's intended actual good. Once the response is accomplished, actual transfer is not still needed to obtain that same response. The directive to retain the property has an articulated end-sensitive rationale. The source does not remain at “God is generally wise, and something happened.”

**Second, the distinction does not require a unique best world or an outside source of wisdom.** It depends on a concrete purpose and its actual stage of realization. It makes no claim that all fully articulated original reasons tie, or that this was the uniquely best possible test. A normative reason can discriminate a later execution without being a global ranking of every possible original history.

**Third, full causal determination of a new intrinsic original act is not exhibited.** The case explains an originally directed created sequence. Its causes include the messenger and the responding person within the source's creative scope. The passage does not tell us whether the sequence corresponds to one comprehensive volition, successive intrinsic acts, or another permissible individuation of willing. The general-will passage at *Shifāʾ* e178 prevents demanding a distinct divine micro-volition for every created movement; it does not settle that individuation question.

Consequently the present operation advances the practical side of particular wise determination and gives a substantive affirmative instance of end-sensitive execution. It does not solve why the original adopts this particular complete undertaking or the entire history containing it. Those are not answered by declaring the prior context self-produced. The complete-determiner requirement and the earned affirmative warrant for intrinsic wise agency remain; no arbitrary-choice exemption is introduced.
