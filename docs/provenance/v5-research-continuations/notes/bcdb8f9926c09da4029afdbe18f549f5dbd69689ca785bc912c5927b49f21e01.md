# End to end sparse prior theorem for the infinite receipt process

This separate source-only supplement closes the actual probability-process expectation bridge. Every earlier author packet and acceptance boundary is preserved unchanged.

## Kernel checked end to end theorem

The terminal declaration is

    SparsePriorProcess.exists_finite_actual_expectation_iff

Fix p>0, 0<epsilon<=1/4, and any sequence of real support points satisfying

    0<a_i<=1/2,    a_(i+1)<=a_i/2,    i>=0.

The source constructs the probability law that draws one latent index I with weights a_i^p/Z_p, holds that index fixed, draws an infinite iid Bernoulli(a_I) receipt stream, and independently draws one seed from an arbitrary probability space. A policy may use that seed and the entire ordered first-n receipt word to issue an arbitrary real coherent report after every n>=1 acquisitions.

For that actual law, not only an explicit surrogate risk definition, the theorem proves

    A measurable common policy with finite expected literal failure count exists
    iff sum_i [a_(i+1)^p/a_i] log(a_i/a_(i+1)) is summable.

The companion theorem every_policy_infinite_of_not_summable quantifies over every such policy and proves its actual expected count is infinity when the spacing series diverges. actual_expectation_lower supplies the universal normalized Bayes lower bound, and exists_actual_expectation_eq supplies one common policy attaining that cost.

The mathematical policy existence is not a computable-implementation claim for arbitrary real inputs. The source never grants access to the sampled latent index or unobserved receipt tail. There is no feedback of literal correctness, stopping, abstention, or extra acquisition clock.

## Exact scoring semantics

The report coordinate q represents the coherent binary report. Acceptance is exactly the conjunction

    q^2 - 1/4 - a epsilon(1+epsilon) <= 0,
    (1-q)^2 - 1/4 + a epsilon(1-epsilon) <= 0.

literalFailure is0 on that accepted set and1 otherwise. Equality is accepted. totalFailures is the extended nonnegative sum of those indicators at n=1,2,... . The theorem therefore concerns expected literal failure count, not accumulated excess-loss magnitude or a claim about infinitely many failures on typical runs.

## How the stochastic bridge is proved

The complete file contains the frozen analytic source and the frozen normalized Bayes-decision bridge, followed by the new SparsePriorProcess namespace.

- sourceCoinMeasure is a proved probability measure on Bool
- receiptLaw is Mathlib's actual Measure.infinitePi of that coin law
- prefixSet records the one positions among receipt indices0,...,n-1
- receiptLaw_prefix_mass derives the exact full-word probability from a cylinder of the infinite product measure and evaluates its finite product
- latentLaw draws one index once through a weighted sum of dirac-index/product-receipt measures; its total mass is proved1
- experimentLaw is eta.prod latentLaw, making the seed independent of both the latent index and the entire receipt-generating experiment
- stageFailure_measurable and totalFailures_measurable follow from coordinate-measurable policies, finite prefix partitions, countable latent indices, and nonnegative countable sums
- lintegral_prefix_function integrates an arbitrary finite-prefix function using the proved word marginals
- latentLaw_lintegral derives the fixed-index mixture decomposition from the actual measure
- stage_expectation_eq_prefix_risk proves the per-stage equality with the normalized risk sum
- conditional_total_expectation_eq uses nonnegative Tonelli over n>=1
- actual_expectation_eq_randomizedCost uses product-measure Fubini over the independent seed

No probability marginal, expectation equality, Bayes-optimality condition, lifetime bound, or desired summability criterion is inserted as an assumption of the terminal theorem. Its assumptions are only the support/weight/loss contract and a probability-space seed.
