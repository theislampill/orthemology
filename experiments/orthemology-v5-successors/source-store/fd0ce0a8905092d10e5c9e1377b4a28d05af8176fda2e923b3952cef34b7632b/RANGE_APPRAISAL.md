# Range appraisal: al-Safadiyya, SF-S002-L0136–SF-S002-L0611

## Scope and status

This appraisal rests on continuous inspection of all 17 assigned electronic windows: 476 lines, 23,749 words. It does not claim to cover al-Safadiyya as a whole, all five study ranges, or a print or manuscript witness. The received text has conspicuous possible corruption, intrusive digits and an editorial part boundary. The argument is interpreted at its recoverable level; exact disputed clauses are sent to textual control rather than normalised silently. No new Lean result or predetermined doctrinal verdict is offered.

## Connected argument

The sequence is organised around two rival errors and a proposed third account. One error makes an eternally inactive creator start acting without a relevant reason. The other makes the first source an eternally complete, invariant cause whose determinate effects accompany it. Ibn Taymiyya argues that the first account undercuts the reasoning used to establish a maker, while the second cannot explain the evident occurrence of new events. His alternative is an independently effective, willing agent whose activity can be unceasing in type without any determinate act or product being beginningless. This organising contrast appears at L0139–0164, recurs at L0315–0329 and L0399–0441, governs divine-speech disputes at L0453–0536, and returns at L0597–0611.

A second thread concerns the identity of that agent. Merely demonstrating “necessary existence” is not enough if it is then interpreted as unrestricted common existence or as a concept stripped of every positive determination. L0209–0239 distinguish mental universality, common predicability and concrete particularity. The author does not ban general concepts; he denies their external realisation as one numerically shared, unrestricted thing. At L0216–0217 a universal is itself a determinate mental item, general in reference. At L0223 the necessary existent must have a distinctive positive reality. L0310 explicitly faults an Avicennian route for failing, on its proposed terms, to establish an existent distinct from the world.

The rebuttal of al-Sijistānī links these two threads to attributes. The opponent's quoted objection argues that using predicates shared with creatures exposes the creator to their incapacities (L0242–0244). The response separates numerical identity, exact likeness, general predication and resemblance in a respect. Divine and created knowledge are not a single external attribute, nor exact equals distinguished only by age. Yet “knowledge” need not be meaningless or merely equivocal. General meanings and legitimate consequences are preserved while the creature-specific limitations are withheld (L0254–0282; L0383–0390).

The source/perfection argument then connects creaturely perfections to a particular comprehensive maker. L0330–0337 argues that a perfection which is possible and carries no defect cannot be impossible for the superior independent creator when it is possible for his inferior dependants. All such perfections in created possibles are from him. Their ultimate giver is more entitled to perfection, and the divine possession of it cannot itself be borrowed from his products. Intrinsic completeness therefore entails necessary attributes. This is not merely an inference from a disembodied universal property, and not simply a slogan that every cause contains everything found in its effects.

## Actual common Creator and unrestricted SourceMode claims

The strongest relevant textual statements should be kept together:

- L0332: “فكل كمال في المحدثات الممكنات المخلوقات فمنه”. The quantified domain is perfections in originated, possible, created things, within an argument about the already established independent creator.
- L0333–0336 connect those perfections with lack of dependence on products or a second necessary source.
- L0498 connects all creaturely knowledge with divine teaching and guidance, in the author's reading of the Throne Verse.
- L0603: “فما ثم إلا الفاعل ومفعولاته ولا شيء من مفعولاته إلا منه”. This concerns the comprehensive first agent in the causal dispute, rather than arbitrary original instances or arbitrary conditions.

Together, these support a substantial actual common-Creator comparative hypothesis: created instances of defect-free perfection ultimately depend on one independent, perfect source, and that source is more entitled to the relevant perfection in a manner proper to it. They do not, without additional argument, state or prove a universally quantified principle over every conceivable original, every requisite, every relation of priority, or every mode of being a source.

Several explicit boundaries resist that inflation.

First, “perfection” is qualified. L0330 requires possibility and absence of defect. L0382 excludes grief and weeping as deficient. L0389–0390 says a creature-specific perfection can be a defect relative to God, and that perfections are appropriate to the kind of bearer. Sharing the word does not make the concrete instances equal.

Second, efficient production is not inherent qualification. L0507 denies that God is described by a quality just because he creates it in another. L0508 distinguishes attribute annexation from ownership and special association. L0592 argues that making all created speech cannot make every speech God's own speech. Thus “from him” does not by itself license token identity, intrinsic inherence, or literal reproduction of the effect's manner in its maker.

Third, requisite dependence is not invariably efficient causation. L0340–0346 state that an existent's requiring its own attributes or supposed components does not establish a separate efficient maker. “Necessary in itself” is not automatically “having no attributes or concomitants”. Calling the essence a cause of necessary attributes can mean intrinsic entailment; this differs from making the world. L0337 explicitly accepts intrinsic necessitation for necessary attributes while refusing it as an account of creatures. A formal vocabulary that combines these into one SourceMode would lose a distinction the text insists on preserving.

Fourth, condition-governed particular action is permitted. L0521 says original creatorhood depends on no other, while creating this thing may be conditional on creating another. L0607–0610 acknowledge finite sources whose effects wait on externally provided receptivity. The sun can illuminate a newly uncovered place without intrinsic change in the sun. The argument against transferring this model to the common creator rests on his comprehensiveness: the allegedly independent condition is also his effect. It is not a denial of every conditioning relation or a universal demand for the same sort of change in each contributor.

## Necessary bearer versus mental universal

This range makes a strong distinction between a real bearer and a shared concept, but it needs careful formulation. The author allows that “human” or “existent” applies to external particulars and that common meanings have consequences. He denies that a common universal is externally one and numerically present in many as universal. Therefore the target is not conceptual abstraction or valid general inference as such. Nor does rejection of a bare universal establish every further claim about the one actual creator without the dependency and uniqueness arguments that accompany it.

L0285–0288 reject two original makers whose powers arise entirely through reciprocal empowerment, while permitting finite cooperation supported by pre-existing capacities or a third. L0305 distinguishes independent/necessary from dependent/created reality and acknowledges that a source-less origination of all existence is a hypothetical objection rather than a known school's profession. L0312–0314 then offer alternative formulations through independence, sustaining existence and creatorhood. These routes strengthen the particular-bearer reading but remain arguments in this received theological work, not scan-verified universal formal derivations.

## Agency, temporal conditions and the completeness of efficacy

The author repeatedly grants a principle of complete efficacy: a complete cause or full set of effective conditions cannot be present while its relevant effect is absent, and the effect cannot be present without the completeness required for it (L0161–0164; L0407–0417). The dispute concerns when completeness obtains and what is included within its scope. An eternally complete determinate cause of the whole world would, on the author's reconstruction, place all its effects eternally alongside it. A successive agent's determinate efficacy is complete for each event when that event occurs.

Original efficacy must not regress through nothing but dependent producers (L0513–0519). Yet an agent that is already independently effective can perform a sequence in which the later act presupposes the earlier (L0520–0522). The difference between original capacity, completion for this act, prior created preparation and co-operation is therefore indispensable. L0151's statement that worldly effects involve two causes and L0608–0610's concession about the active intellect and sun are important limiting evidence, not embarrassing outliers to be omitted.

The central temporal result asserted by the author is that no determinate created world-member is eternal, even if the type of divine action or occurrences is beginningless. L0322 distinguishes intransitive and transitive acts; L0420–0439 repeatedly distinguish type, token, simultaneous totality and unceasing succession. The exact arguments concerning a world-member's necessary accompaniment by events need adjacent-range context and scan control for damaged wording before serving as formal premises. The appraisal records the proposed proof rather than declaring every formulation sound.

## Epistemic routes, signs and attestation

The range contains more than ontological source reasoning. L0176 requires sound premises and removal of objections. L0178–0180 distinguish dependable transmitted evidence from merely supporting or unclassified reports. L0182–0183 refuse an unsupported attribution of meaningless divine speech and evaluate a refutation separately from the alleged thesis. L0184–0193 reconcile uses of taʾwīl through speaker-relative meaning and unknown manner. L0578–0584 later generalise the demand to interpret historical speakers in their own linguistic usage.

L0200 criticises both reliance on poor reports instead of better rational evidence and exclusion of divine and prophetic testimony. L0206–0208 insists that Qur'anic arguments can guide reason directly, rather than operating only after messenger-veracity has been established. L0381 warns that lacking one particular argument does not establish a negative conclusion. These passages support plural, interconnected epistemic routes without treating all alleged routes as equally valid.

L0392–0395 discusses miracle as the equivalent of a sender's attestation of a messenger, and whether establishing divine speech or divine freedom from defect must precede reliance on revelation. This is directly relevant to signs and attestation, but the assigned range does not provide a complete standalone theory of miracles, testimony or prophetic verification. Wider claims on that front require the other ranges.

## Wider material that should not disappear

A substantial stretch addresses divine speech, including the uncreated Qur'an, the difference between speech and the reciter's voice, the diversity of speech contents, temporal grammar, necessary attributes and voluntary subsisting acts (L0453–0536; L0585–0596). The author's third category resists a binary of an eternal fixed item versus a separately created product. This is connected to the agency argument, but should also be recorded as a major doctrinal discussion in its own right.

The creation-material, throne, water, pen and decree discussion at L0537–0576 is similarly not ornamental. It supplies an exegetical alternative to both absolute initial inactivity and the eternity of these particular heavens. The author compares variants, narrative occasions, linguistic usage and alleged scriptural concord. His rejection of Intellect/pen allegory and restriction of the pen's decree to events through resurrection place limits on sweeping readings of “first”. These historical claims and hadith evaluations need their own verification if used independently.

Polemical anecdotes and classifications run throughout: Ibn Sabʿīn, Ibn ʿArabī, philosophers, Qarmaṭians, Muʿtazilites, Ashʿarites and named transmitters. The work sometimes asserts severe consequences and sometimes explicitly distinguishes intent, uncertain attribution and hypothetical adversarial positions. A responsible summary must preserve these levels of confidence and distinguish reported opponents from the author's own conclusions.

## Interpretative and textual limits

1. The inbound demonstrative at L0136 lacks its preceding formulation within this range.
2. The editorial part division occurs inside L0239; L0240–0241 are labels, and L0242 resumes the argument. It is not an established print pagination division.
3. L0611 ends after the first branch of a fresh dilemma. The second branch and immediate conclusion lie outside this reading.
4. Apparent defects at L0220, L0234–0236, L0299, L0432–0436, L0546 and L0584 require scan control. Unusual wording must not be silently repaired.
5. Named-school and textual attributions here remain authorial reports, except that the quoted al-Sijistānī passage can be identified as an explicit quotation within the received text. No external edition of that work was checked.
6. A recovered argument can motivate a formal reconstruction, but does not itself discharge a universal formal claim. Common creator, intrinsic attribute, efficient source, finite contributor and necessary condition must not be equated by notation before the text's distinctions are respected.
