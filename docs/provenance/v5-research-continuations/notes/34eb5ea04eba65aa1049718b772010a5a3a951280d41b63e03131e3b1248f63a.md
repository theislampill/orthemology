## 2. Contract and endpoint audit

**Lines 11–38.** The game is an infimum over fixed probability rows of a
worst-case corruption set's minimum row mass. A randomized sender cannot
improve the minimum. Nonnegative masses allow a corruption set of size less
than `b` to be padded to exactly `b`, so all subsequent complement arguments
have their claimed fixed cardinality.

For one message, averaging over the `b`-subsets yields the lower bound `b/u`,
and a uniform row attains it. The finite formula agrees because each term is
at most `b/u` and its `q=u` term equals that value. For `M>=b+1`, the sender can
select an intact root among `b+1` distinct singleton actions. Such roots exist
because `b+1<=u`; unused labels may duplicate rows even when `M>u`. This also
handles `b=0` and `u=1,b=0`. The denominator scan is not evaluated outside its
stated range, so no empty maximum or negative `N` is introduced.

The nontrivial proof correctly restricts to `2<=M<=b`. Every partition has a
positive worst-case value because the adversary can place one corrupt root
in every block and pad to `b`. Conversely its minimum fraction is bounded by
the size-weighted average `b/u<1`. Thus its finite minimum `B` is strictly
between zero and one. The rational fraction used later comes from this finite
partition optimum; no rationality of the arbitrary input rows is assumed.

## 3. Partition thresholds, attainment, and denominator

**Lines 42–60.** The two floor/ceiling conditions have distinct, correct risk
directions. To force loss strictly greater than `t<1`, every block needs
`floor(t*s_j)+1` corrupt roots. Each requirement fits its block; if their total
is at most `b`, padding is possible. Negating this condition gives
`R(s)<=t` exactly when the floor sum is at least `N=b-M+1`. To force loss at
least `t`, the corresponding exact cost is the sum of the ceilings.

For a fixed partition, `R(s)` is the maximum over finitely many integer
allocations of a minimum of finitely many block fractions. Hence it equals
one of those fractions. A minimum over the finitely many positive block-size
partitions is attained as well. For reduced `B=a/q`, some attaining block size
is divisible by `q`; since the other `M-1` blocks are nonempty, that size is at
most `u-M+1`. This proves the scan's denominator bound. It would be invalid to
bound an arbitrarily unreduced presentation's denominator in the same way.

The weighted-average argument for `B<=b/u` works for each allocation of exactly
`b` corrupt roots and hence survives both finite optimizations. This inequality
is subsequently needed for the expanded hypergraph's size condition.

## 4. Residue-transfer lemma

**Lines 64–100.** This is the delicate elementary step. It checks in full.
Write `f=sum floor(a*s_j/q)`, `d=f-N`, `z` for the number of zero residues, and
`delta=a*u-q*N`. Feasibility at `B` gives `d>=0`; it also gives `delta>=0`.

A sufficiently small positive left perturbation decreases exactly the `z`
integer block thresholds by one. Therefore a globally optimal partition must
have `d<z`. This strict inequality is essential: `d>=z` would already supply
a lower threshold without making a transfer. In particular, there is a
zero-residue block available as donor.

Since `a,q` are coprime and `0<a<q`, there is a unique nonzero residue class
`k` modulo `q` with `a*k=-1 mod q`; choose `1<=k<q`. A zero-residue donor's
positive size is divisible by `q`, so removing `k` roots leaves at least one.
Adding those roots to the recipient preserves the total population and the
number of nonempty blocks. These positivity facts are not optional.

If `d>0`, the inequality `z>d` supplies two zero-residue blocks. Transferring
between them changes their residues to `1,q-1`, decreases the total floor
sum by one, and decreases `z` by two. The new excess remains nonnegative.
If it is no longer less than the new zero count, the left perturbation closes
the contradiction immediately. Otherwise repeat. This phase terminates
because `d` decreases at each transfer.

Under the contrary assumption `delta>=M`, after reaching `d=0` the total
residue sum is at least `M`. While at least one residue is zero, some other
residue must be at least two. The same transfer sends residues `0,r` to
`1,r-1`; their sum and the total floor sum are unchanged, and the zero count
decreases by one. Eventually no zero residue remains, and a small left
perturbation preserves all floors while their sum is still `N`, contradicting
global optimality. For `q=2`, the hypothesized second phase cannot have such a
residue sum; the first phase must already yield the contradiction. There is
no missing special case.

An explicit safe perturbation after any terminal partition is
`eta=1/(2*q*max_j s_j)`. It is smaller than `B` and too small to cross any
nonzero residue's preceding integer. This corroborates the written
“sufficiently small” step without a limiting argument.

The lemma's conclusion `0<=delta<=M-1` is therefore established for every
optimal reduced partition fraction.

## 5. Both directions of the explicit formula

**Lines 104–122.** For a positive reduced `t=a/q<1` with
`a*u-q*N<=M-1`, consider any partition. If its excess `f-N` were at least its
zero count `z`, then

`a*u-q*N = q*(f-N)+sum residues >= q*z+(M-z) >= M`,

a contradiction. Thus `f-N<z`, which yields `sum ceilings<=b` and therefore
`R(s)>=t`. The argument holds for every partition, proving the lower bound on
the **best** partition, with no swapped quantifier.

For a scan candidate, the numerator is nonnegative and strictly smaller than
its denominator: `N<=u-M` gives
`q*N+M-1 <= q*u-M*(q-1)-1 < q*u`. Zero candidates can be ignored. Reducing a
positive candidate divides the quantity `a*u-q*N` by the same positive
integer, preserving its upper bound by `M-1`. Consequently every candidate is
at most `B`.

Conversely, the optimum's reduced denominator is within the scan, and the
residue lemma gives its numerator at most the numerator chosen by that
denominator's floor. Hence `B` is at most the scan maximum. Together these
give equality. For `N=0`, every numerator is zero because `M-1=b<u`; that
endpoint is handled separately and never invokes the positive-fraction lemma.

## 6. External theorem: identity and precise applicability

**Lines 126–146.** Independently opened source:
[Azarpendar and Jafari, arXiv:2002.01748v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01748v3),
Theorem 1.1 on printed page 2 (PDF page 2). It applies for every integer
`r>=2`, with `n>=r*k` and a partition of the expanded ground set into parts
of size at most `r`. Vertices are transversal `k`-subsets; edges are `r`
pairwise-disjoint such subsets. Its chromatic number is exactly the ceiling
quoted in the frozen argument.

The published identity is independently supported by
[publisher metadata](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S009589562030099X):
Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B 146 (2021), 372–381,
DOI `10.1016/j.jctb.2020.09.014`. The version stamp is 7 October 2020 and the
document's heading is dated 9 October 2020, as the binding note accurately
records. Publisher-indexed metadata was available; a subsequent direct page
open returned HTTP 403. No claim of inspecting a different journal PDF is made.

The all-integer-uniformity qualification is material, not cosmetic. The proof
cannot replace this theorem with an older prime-only or incompletely justified
multiplicity result. A reduced optimal denominator of six already occurs at
`(u,b,M)=(7,2,2)`.

As secondary corroboration only, [Noga Alon's authored note](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/prwise1.pdf),
*Partitioning all k-subsets into r-wise intersecting families*, explicitly
attributes its all-`r` conclusion to Azarpendar–Jafari Theorem 1.1 and
Corollary 1.1, citing this v3. This note is not substituted for the primary
theorem and is not an added dependency of the frozen proof.

## 7. Copy-block reduction and the all-real contradiction

**Lines 150–187.** Suppose a fixed codebook's risk is below `B=a/q`. Every
`b`-subset then has at least one row with mass strictly below `a/q`. Coloring
its `(u-b)`-element complement by one such row uses at most `M` colors. The
choice is finite; neither a computable real representation nor a positive
uniform numerical slack is needed.

Give each original root exactly `q-a` distinct copies, grouped in a partition
block. Set expanded `n'=(q-a)*u`, `r=q`, and `k=u-b`. All hypotheses check:

- `q>=2` because `0<B<1` is reduced
- `q-a` is a positive integer at most `q`
- `k>=1` because `u>b`
- `n'>=q*k` follows exactly from `a/q<=b/u`
- a transversal expanded vertex projects to exactly `k` distinct original
  roots, so its complement still has exactly `b` roots

The substituted chromatic numerator is
`q*(b+1)-a*u = q*M-delta`. With `delta<=M-1`, its quotient by `q-1` exceeds
`M`, so the chromatic number is at least `M+1`. Thus the inherited coloring
has a monochromatic edge of `q` disjoint expanded vertices.

After projection, an original root appears in at most `q-a` of those sets,
because its copies cannot be reused across the disjoint edge. It therefore
appears in at least `a` of their corruption complements. For the common row,
nonnegativity and normalization force the sum of these `q` complement masses
to be at least `a`. Each one is strictly below `a/q`, forcing the sum to be
strictly below `a`. This contradiction proves the universal lower bound.

Projected sets can repeat: disjointness is on copy identities, not original
labels. The argument never requires projected sets to be distinct. This
distinction is checked explicitly in the independent controls. Conversely,
dropping transversality would change corruption-set cardinality, and using
`q` copies instead of `q-a` would destroy the multiplicity lower bound.

The finite sum argument is valid for arbitrary real row entries. Combined
with an attained partition upper bound, it proves the infimum exactly; no
attainment assumption about arbitrary nonuniform codebooks is required.
