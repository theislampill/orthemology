### Quantitative result not developed in the reader

The exact defect is the best sup-norm approximation of a target by the retained span, half the largest equal-summary law separation, and the minimax arbitrary-decoder error. The minimum **continuous real coordinate** count is `dim(C_h)-1`; exact extra coordinates cost `dim(V+C_h)-dim(V)`. These appear in crosswalk prose, but the particularly useful Theorem 3 horizon spectrum is not developed there or in the main reader chapters:

For all ordered updates `x_i <- x_i x_j` with i≠j on `{-1,+1}^n` for n≥2, one trivial observation per action, all unary readouts plus mass, and continuous encoding on the full simplex of probability laws, `C_h` after at most h elementary updates is precisely the linear span of Walsh characters of degree at most `min(n,h+1)`. Thus the exact continuous-memory requirement is `sum_{k=1}^{min(n,h+1)} binom(n,k)`.

This is a stronger design discriminator than the static parity example. Every update is pairwise, yet arbitrary-law future unary answering eventually needs `2^n-1` continuous coordinates. It is not a bit, sampling, ontology, or physical-memory bound. Existing moment/chronological-jet descendants provide different explicit dynamics; none should be presented as having replaced this finite horizon law.

### Inquiry is not merely proof price

H-formalisation §§7 and H-Continuation §5 distinguish historical ecology, objectively feasible inquiries, estimated/certifiable inquiries, and the linear span used to evaluate plans. Two closures may establish the same correct theorem while leaving different future options. Smaller option sets need not have lower value. Hard authorization is not a reward penalty. These are substantively summarized in the crosswalk but not operationally instantiated by PT-01/02's proof-cost frontier or ET-17's fixed-model stochastic objective. A faithful combined model must supply the menu, permissions, access, horizon and value functional independently. No new empirical claim follows.

## 3. T2/T3 repair: the target and Bregman argument order matter

### Common repair is not Bayes normalization

The actual smooth-score source (`3d1c58…`, S1/S2) treats **forecast-second**, all-truth-state Bregman nonworsening. Under separability, positive coordinate Hessians, at least three outcomes, and common local repair at every interior simplex point, the two generators differ by one positive scale and affine terms. The two-outcome complementary example is a genuine exception: quadratic and binary-entropy scores admit the same arithmetic repair despite nonproportional generators.

GEO-03 instead classifies **first-argument** Bregman projection onto the simplex for every positive-cone input, admitting perspective extensions `F(sp)=s f(p)+a(s)`. This is valuable new work but not an equivalent replacement for the older common-repair theorem. Its broader nonseparable admissible class does not refute the additive forecast-second rigidity statement. A future integrated reader should put the two interfaces side by side with their argument order, truth hull and quantifiers.

### Full smooth jets fail for a different exact target

The pinned T3 source `FULL_JET_REPAIR_BOUNDARY.md` (`7b8487…`) gives two globally smooth positive-curvature generators with the same complete jet at the coherent baseline. One, paired with a quadratic anchor, has common local weak repair; a flat one-sided perturbation destroys exact repair on a fixed ray. The positive minimax excess is `o(epsilon^N)` for every fixed natural N. Exact repair and polynomial-tolerance repair therefore diverge.

MOM-10's later Fisher-endpoint pair likewise separates full jets from germs, but its object is a calibrated statistical/moment geometry. It neither supplies nor removes the T3 scoring-feasibility result. The safe common lesson is representation-relative: all derivatives at a point need not determine a smooth germ or an exact downstream decision. The counterexample does not apply unchanged to a genuinely analytic germ, or to a polynomial with supplied degree bound.

## 4. Unknown-standard repair: finite stopping does not reconcile continuum restoration

The pinned T2 statistical repair source (`002eab…`) derives an actual decision interface: successful common binary quadratic repair at disturbance epsilon identifies its supplied weight within epsilon/2. With at most N fresh Bernoulli(weight) probes, the local-minimax repair failure is at least `[1-sqrt(1-(1-4epsilon²)^N)]/2`, and hence at least `exp(-(16/3)N epsilon²)/4`. Copied records cannot increase the fresh-information budget. A separately supplied target-preserving acquisition process permits an eventual-correct positive construction using summable error probabilities.

The continuum source (`4878fd…`) takes shrinking disturbances, target-bad probes and parameter-independent nonprobe feedback. A shared policy has positive probability of eventual always-correct repair for at most countably many standards. The proof restricts to the event of finitely many probes, establishes common domination there via deterministic cutoffs, and reads the standard as the limit of scaled repair outputs. It does **not** condition on a random last probe as a stopping time. A blind zero-query guess attains exactly the guess law's singleton success masses. Later scoped source statements cover actual causal evaluators and the tolerance boundary: `eta_n/epsilon_n -> 0` preserves the obstruction, whereas `eta_n=epsilon_n` permits a no-information repair.

This is not PT-21's finite fixed-kernel exact-stopping problem. Nor does the finite T2 self-verifying-support positive result imply it for an uncountable family with shrinking accuracy demands. These older sources identify precisely which acquisition, tolerance or domain change would make a continuation legitimate. The gap is consequential because “restoration,” “almost sure,” “finite identification,” and “finite expected errors” otherwise sound interchangeable.
