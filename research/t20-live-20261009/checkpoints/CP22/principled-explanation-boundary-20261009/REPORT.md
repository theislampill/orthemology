# Does autonomy justify stopping at the brute original's own existence?

## Result and genuine increment

**The strongest already-read Dasgupta argument does not supply a principled inexplicability exemption for the proposed original's own genuinely contingent existence.** Its positive stopping rationale concerns autonomous explanatory bases. Applied faithfully, an existence-grounding essence explains the existence claim instead of exempting it. A qualitative version may explain the same existential subject matter without an essentially existing particular. That remains a substantive alternative to an exhaustive productive-bearer inference; it is not a vindication of the original's unexplained obtaining.

The most exact source control is printed 393: Dasgupta separately motivates the claim that autonomy excludes contingency by regarding genuine contingency as a reason why the grounding question is legitimate. Printed 395 then explicitly distinguishes a definitional reading of the associated necessity from a substantive modal thesis requiring argument. Thus the brute defender can dispute that thesis without making a semantic mistake. But the defender cannot cite Dasgupta's own positive argument as already establishing its contrary application.

## 1. Fix the question before applying a stopping rule

Let p be the assertion that the very original B under discussion actually exists. Retain the strongest inherited proposal: B has genuine powers that explain the received effects, requires no external existential provision, never produces itself, and may be beginningless. Its defender nevertheless proposes genuine possible absence and no adequate intrinsic or external account of its obtaining. This is an unverified rival proposal, not a metaphysical possibility certificate. [Direct anti-brute return, “The strongest rival and fair adjudication.”]

Three assertions must remain separate:

- p: B exists.
- k: a core claim about what B is, potentially including conditions on its existence or powers.
- d: B produces particular downstream effects.

Explaining d does not explain p. Establishing a principled stopping point for k does not, without a further argument, establish one for p. But explaining p through k would answer the original existential question; that route must not be dismissed merely because its explanans belongs to a different ontological category.

For PCE II, keep both the question and explanatory relation explicit. An account of why p lacks a grounding explanation is not automatically an account of why p lacks every relevant true explanation. Conversely, no argument here requires that every adequate explanation be efficient production. Dasgupta expressly distinguishes ground from causal explanation and uses fact-talk as a convenience for a sentential operator, not as a commitment to separate fact-objects. [2016, §§1–3, printed 381–387; O'Connor, printed 37.]

## 2. What the autonomy argument actually supports

The positive argument is stronger than permission to stop wherever explanation becomes inconvenient. Arithmetic illustrates that causal questioning can be inappropriate to a subject; nominal definition illustrates that a demand for proof can misidentify the role of a statement. Real definitions are proposed as the worldly counterpart: they specify what a thing is rather than distribute essential properties over an independently specified domain. That gives an independently intelligible candidate difference in explanatory role. It is not merely a report that a theorist feels satisfied. [2016, §§3–4, printed 384–389.]

The qualifications are part of the argument. Autonomy's intelligibility is assumed after illumination rather than exhaustively defended. Core essentialist autonomy is a working hypothesis; its converse is another working hypothesis, not a definition of all possible autonomy. Other candidate autonomous categories are explicitly left open. The domain-specification account must therefore be assessed as a substantive metaphysical proposal, not dismissed by assuming every essential claim is already a quality inhering in a prior bearer. Equally, its analogy does not make an actual existence-entailing essence available just by stipulating one.

Printed 387 is decisive for the proposed transfer. The autonomous claim concerns the essential status of its content; the content itself may be grounded by that claim. The essential-grounding schema is presented as a possible general principle, not something established merely by factivity. Consequently:

1. A core essence concerning B's powers does not by itself settle whether B exists.
2. A successfully established essence-grounding account of B's existence gives an explanation of p. It does not leave p brute and then excuse it.
3. Merely writing that p is autonomous provides none of the independent role-based support given for core essences.

Section 13 reinforces the distinction. Classificatory conditions need not entail an instance in free logic. The proposed existence-entailing chair essence is conditional, and note 55 expressly says the example falls short of the existence-grounding essence needed for the stronger rationalist result. We neither delete an actual existence-entailing clause nor pretend the conditional supplies its own antecedent. [Printed 402–403; note 55, printed 416.]

## 3. The direct contingency test, with its modal boundary

The conditional inference under examination is short:

- If autonomy excludes genuine contingency in the relevant modal sense, and p is genuinely contingent in that same sense, p is not autonomous.
- Nothing about that inference identifies a fact with B, turns ground into receipt, or infers a bearer from a proposition.

At printed 393 Dasgupta gives a positive motivation for the first premise: a fact's obtaining while admitting genuine nonobtaining makes the question why it obtains legitimate. This is independent of the alternative subargument through the two working assumptions concerning autonomy and essence. It directly addresses the attempted application to p.

It is nevertheless a **substantive explanatory-aptitude judgment**, not a contradiction in the brute proposal's syntax. The defender may deny that genuine possible absence always licenses the relevant demand, or may accept the demand's legitimacy while denying that it must have an answer. The former disputes the aptitude premise; the latter disputes a sufficient-reason demand. Neither acquires positive warrant simply by denying it. Conversely, calling either denial unintelligible by definition would conceal the philosophical disagreement.

The modal qualification is essential. Sections 7–8 distinguish possibility relative to an item's own nature from metaphysical possibility, world-relative necessity from independently understood necessity, and ordinary necessity from amodality. A chair can be open to nonexistence relative to its own essence while its existence follows from another essence. That is not the brute original's proposed absence of both intrinsic and external existential explanation.

At printed 395 the source acknowledges that, under an essence-based definition of metaphysical necessity and the working identifications, the necessity claim can collapse into a restatement of the rationalist account. That definitional result cannot independently defeat a rival using a separately understood modal claim. Where necessity is separately understood, the source itself treats the autonomy-to-necessity step as nonlogical and requiring defense. The subsequent domain-priority argument motivates the nonvariation of core essences but does not certify an arbitrary bearer-world interpretation. This comparison retains genuine contingency as the rival's claim, rather than silently replacing it with conceptual consistency, relative openness, or a hand-drawn possible-world table.

## 4. Which PCE II branch could the qualitative route satisfy?

PCE II permits a true explanation of a contingent target or a nonvacuous true explanation of why it lacks one. The availability of a justified limitation is broader than Dasgupta's particular autonomy category. Rejecting autonomy of p therefore does **not** prove that every conceivable meta-explanation of its inexplicability fails. An explanation-apt question might lack an answer for independently established reasons. The present negative result concerns this supplied autonomy argument, not every possible limit argument.

The faithful cross-comparison has four cases:

**Ordinary core essence.** A justified autonomous k gives a candidate stopping point for why k has no further ground. If k does not explain p, the original existential question remains. PCE II's second branch for p has not been supplied by an answer about k.

**Existence-grounding essence.** If k adequately grounds p, the existential explanation is provided. Where the accompanying necessitation and noncontingency premises apply, the resulting p also loses the genuine contingency at issue. Either way this is not unexplained contingent p licensed by an autonomy exception. Nothing here proves that such an actual essence exists.

**Autonomous qualitative explanation.** Note 44 challenges the additional premise that grounds of existence must include existence facts of particulars. Its proposal explains existential subject matter through qualitative essential grounds while avoiding an essentially existing particular. A successful version would therefore be an explanatory alternative, not a meta-explanation of why the existential target has no explanation. Rejecting it merely because the base is not already an individual would import the disputed bridge. The published sketch does not establish an actual autonomous obtaining base or its productive completeness. [2016, §9 and note 44.]

**Direct autonomy of p.** The unqualified autonomy-based PSR is formally neutral about which facts are autonomous, but that neutrality supplies no positive reason to classify this p as autonomous. The strongest positive account actually read points in the opposite direction for genuine contingent obtaining. A defender could develop a different substantive autonomy theory or a different impossibility-of-explanation argument. No such warranted application appears in the held argument.

The plural qualification prevents an overstatement in the third case. The already-read 2011 proposal grounds an entire individualistic plurality without separate grounds for its individual members; 2016 note 9 explicitly leaves room for that form. Hence a successful collective account cannot automatically be projected into a singleton first-order explanation of p. Nor can the missing singleton ground automatically be called autonomous or brute. How collective adequacy answers the singleton demand remains the inherited explanatory-form question. It is not a supplied meta-explanation of why contingent B's own obtaining is inexplicable. [Modal-grounding report, §§3–5 and mapping test.]

## 5. What changes in the philosophical comparison?

The anti-arbitrary-stopping warrant survives. Dasgupta offers a motivated category boundary rather than a favored terminus's name. This answers the overbroad complaint that every explanatory terminus is simply brute. But transporting that reason to the brute original's own contingent obtaining is not supported by the cited boundary and conflicts with the source's substantive aptitude judgment when meanings are held fixed.

This is not a new proof of ContingencyNeed. The direct anti-brute report's positive claim that complete existential independence is intrinsic sufficiency remains a distinct foundational judgment. Dasgupta's contingent-aptitude judgment concerns grounding questions; it does not itself turn nonnecessity into whole-existence reception. The unchanged typed theorem still needs its concrete-resource, realization, transport, and modal assumptions under their declared interpretation. A sentential explanatory assertion has not been converted into an intrinsic resource or a bearer.

The results must therefore be differentiated:

- No source-backed autonomy exemption for the strongest contingent brute original is supplied.
- No demonstration that all principled PCE II exceptions are impossible is supplied.
- The qualitative explanatory alternative retains its inherited positive motivation and unresolved application burden. Its failure to vindicate brute obtaining does not vindicate exhaustive productive-bearer ontology.
- A critic's refusal of the substantive autonomy/contingency or intrinsic-sufficiency principles is a genuine dispute, not merely mistranslation. A critic who substitutes k for p, ground for productive receipt, or relative openness for genuine contingent obtaining changes the question instead.

One narrow cross-link is useful. Printed 386 expressly declines to assume that even a collection of essential contents uniquely specifies its subject. The separate Spinoza formal-transfer screen shows that A22 assumes injectivity of complete attribute profiles. Thus the words real definition or essence do not already supply A22's individuation criterion. This is neither a refutation of A22 nor an equation of essential contents with all attributes. No identity theorem or further uniqueness result follows. [Local source lines 337–350; Spinoza screen, “A22 and the exact identity question.”]

## 6. Remaining discriminator and stopping condition

The bounded source-critical operation is complete. No new finite model is useful: assigning an autonomy predicate to p would stipulate the disputed classification, and assigning worlds would not warrant its possibility. The exact source comparison already decides whether the **held** autonomy argument supplies the requested exemption: it does not.

A genuinely new continuation would need one of two distinguishable results:

1. For the brute-original route, an independently defended reason why this same contingent existential question lacks every relevant adequate explanation, preserving B's claimed contingency and downstream powers. The argument must identify its explanatory sense and confront the positive aptitude and intrinsic-sufficiency judgments. A label of fundamentality, temporal beginninglessness, or unreceivedness is insufficient.
2. For the qualitative explanatory route, a supported actual autonomous essential basis and a defended grounding application to an existential target, permitting a plural target where warranted. That would assess explanation rather than license bruteness. The inherited full productive-ontology bridge remains separate.

No specific unread passage in the held material is presently identified as supplying either result. Repeating its already completed reading or broadening into plural formalism alone would not close this particular question. These are residual obligations, not a new campaign, closure claim, or demand that a critic first reconstruct the entire cosmos.
