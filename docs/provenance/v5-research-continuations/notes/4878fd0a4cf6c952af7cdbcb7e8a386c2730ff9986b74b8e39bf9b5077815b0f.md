# Continuum standards: the finite-information restoration barrier

Status: new integrated ordinary-proof candidate, pending independent review. It joins the local repair-precision theorem to controlled-information tail equivalence. The countability-of-atoms mechanism is classical; the present scientific object is its exact consequence for this norm-repair task.

## The declared interaction model

A hidden supplied norm alpha∈(0,1) is fixed. At round t an exogenous deterministic 0<epsilon_t<=1/4 is supplied, with epsilon_t→0. The disturbed input is b_t=(1/2+epsilon_t,1/2). The shared controller chooses either:

- Probe: obtain one fresh Bernoulli(alpha) observation; this action is target-bad
- Repair(q), q∈[0,1]: issue coherent c=(q,1−q); its subsequent observation kernel is independent of alpha, conditional on the observed history and chosen action

Path space here contains the observed actions and actually received observations (and may include independent private randomness). It does NOT include an unused infinite parameter-dependent oracle tail; such raw Bernoulli-product laws can be mutually singular even when the controller asks no questions. Private randomization is independent of alpha. Histories, action spaces and kernels are standard Borel and measurable. Every permitted finite history has mutually absolutely continuous law across parameters; this follows from positive Bernoulli likelihoods, common controller kernels and parameter-independent repair feedback.

A repair is target-good under alpha exactly if it weakly improves the weighted quadratic loss at BOTH truth vertices. Success E_alpha means that after some finite random round every action is a target-good repair. This is an action/opportunity target; it does not assert that an externally disturbed physical plant is never momentarily incoherent.

## T1. Exact statement

For every shared controller, the set

    {alpha∈(0,1) : P_alpha(E_alpha)>0}

is at most countable.

Consequences:

1. Every non-atomic prior on alpha assigns overall success probability zero, provided the joint parameter/path family is measurable
2. The worst-case success probability over the full interval is zero, for every controller, and therefore also after taking the supremum over controllers
3. The theorem is stronger than failure of an almost-sure guarantee: almost every norm in the ordinary Lebesgue sense has ZERO probability of eventual all-state-correct repair
4. It is an obstruction to this combination of performance, information and path requirements, not to ordinary approximate learning, finite-confidence repair or correctness under a known norm

## Proof

Let F be the event that only finitely many Probe actions occur. It is parameter-independent as a subset of path space. Put F_N={no Probe after round N}, so F=union_N F_N.

Fix reference alpha_*=1/2. On F_N, after the first N rounds the two environments have identical killed continuation kernels: any attempted Probe is sent to a common cemetery, and Repair feedback is parameter-independent. Their length-N prefix measures are mutually absolutely continuous, with strictly positive likelihood ratio determined by the finitely many observed probe bits. Hence P_alpha restricted to F_N and P_alpha_* restricted to F_N are mutually absolutely continuous. Taking the countable union over N gives the same equivalence restricted to F. This does not condition at a random last probe and uses no false stopping-time premise.

For a Repair(q_t) action define z_t=(q_t−1/2)/epsilon_t; assign any fixed value to z_t on Probe rounds. On E_alpha there are only finitely many probes. G1 of STATISTICAL_REPAIR_BRIDGE gives |z_t−alpha|<=epsilon_t/2 at all sufficiently large rounds. Thus z_t→alpha.

Let Z be the real limit of z_t when it exists, and a distinguished default outside (0,1) otherwise. A sequence of measurable real functions has a measurable convergence set and measurable limit on that set. Therefore

    E_alpha ⊆ F∩{Z=alpha}.

If P_alpha(E_alpha)>0, then P_alpha(F∩{Z=alpha})>0. Absolute continuity on F yields

    P_alpha_*(F∩{Z=alpha})>0.

The finite subprobability law of Z under P_alpha_* restricted to F has at most countably many positive-mass singletons. (For each k≥1, only finitely many disjoint singletons can have mass at least 1/k; every positive mass exceeds 1/k for some k.) Therefore only countably many alpha can have positive success. QED.

The non-atomic-prior consequence follows by integrating the measurable success probabilities over a countable set. The minimax-zero consequence follows because an interval is uncountable.

## T2. Sharpness of the positive-success set

For any specified nonempty finite or countable set S⊂(0,1), assign each s∈S a strictly positive probability and draw one S-valued candidate once. Issue q_t=1/2+s epsilon_t forever, without probing. If the draw equals the true alpha=s, every repair strictly improves both truth states. If alpha differs from the drawn s, G1 implies that success cannot hold as epsilon_t→0. Thus the positive-success parameter set is EXACTLY S.

This is an ideal measurable-controller construction. Effective implementation additionally requires an effective sampling representation of the countable distribution and sufficient computability/precision for the selected constants. A finite rational S has a direct executable version. The empty set is realised by the policy that always probes. No claim of implementing arbitrary noncomputable parameters is made.

## T3. A sharp finite-class contrast

For two distinct known candidate norms, finite Bernoulli experiments separate them to arbitrary confidence. After a sufficiently long batch, commit to the more likely candidate and use its exact repair law forever. This gives success at least 1−delta simultaneously for every delta>0, but no single policy attains success one under both while only finitely probing. Therefore finite-class limit-sure value is one while almost-sure restoration is impossible; for the full continuum, the supremum worst-case eventual success is ZERO.

These are probabilities of an INFINITE eventual-correctness event. They must not be confused with the finite-horizon repair-error bounds, which can be made small with increasing sample budgets.

## T4. Premise deletions that remove the obstruction

- A known supplied alpha permits direct correct repair
- Target-preserving informative feedback permits the growing-sample construction in STATISTICAL_REPAIR_BRIDGE P1
- A fixed nonvanishing epsilon or finite number of repair opportunities no longer forces exact norm identification by the output limit
- A sufficiently relaxed performance tolerance can stop the readout from converging to alpha. In particular, the binary bound with tolerance eta_t is epsilon_t/2+2eta_t/epsilon_t; eta_t/epsilon_t→0 retains this theorem, but arbitrary O(epsilon_t) tolerance need not
- An alpha-revealing singular observation violates finite-prefix mutual absolute continuity and can make finite exact identification possible
- A common target action valid under all norms changes the task, as in the dynamic lane's self-verifying supports
- Parameter-coded private randomness or a hidden norm oracle violates the shared-controller information contract

## T5. Stronger common-reference version

Full support between every pair of actual environments is not necessary for T1. It is enough that all informative observation kernels are dominated by one common measurable reference kernel, while nonprobe feedback is parameter-independent. This permits zeros, history-dependent probes and real-valued query actions.

A concrete sufficient case is one COMMON countable observation alphabet Y with a fixed reference distribution r(y)>0 for every y. Build an artificial reference environment that returns r at every probe and otherwise uses the original shared controller and parameter-independent repair feedback. Every actual finite-prefix law is one-way absolutely continuous with respect to this reference. Per-step likelihood ratios may be zero; only zero-set transfer in that direction is needed. On F_N the killed no-probe continuations coincide. Thus P_alpha restricted to F is dominated by the reference restricted to F, and the same countability-of-atoms proof applies.

The reference need not be a true possible normative model; it is a mathematical dominating law. An alphabet that is separately countable for each alpha but has uncountable union is not one common countable alphabet. Unrecorded timing or side channels invalidate the observation specification if they reveal additional parameter information.

The result also applies to many continuous noisy channels possessing a common dominating reference kernel. An exact real-valued observation of alpha has mutually singular point laws and no common sigma-finite dominating law across all alpha; it is a genuine deletion of this premise. Singular FINITE-alphabet observations may permit exact identification among finitely many standards, but still cannot give positive eventual success on uncountably many standards after finitely many probes.

A kernel-checked measure-theoretic core is being developed in `lean/CountableIdentification.lean`: a dominated family plus one measurable exact readout has positive-success parameters only countably often, including event-restricted and common-component mixture forms. The full adaptive trace factorisation and the geometry-to-limit interpretation remain separate obligations unless explicitly mechanised later.

## T7. Robustness to sublinear loss tolerance, and its sharp scale boundary

`ApproximateRepairIdentification.lean` defines BinaryApproxGood by allowing eta_n
additive worsening in EACH of the two actual score comparisons. If epsilon_n>0,
epsilon_n<=1/4, epsilon_n tends to zero, eta_n>=0, and eta_n/epsilon_n tends to zero,
the actual inequalities imply

  |(q_n-1/2)/epsilon_n-alpha| <= epsilon_n/2 + 2 eta_n/epsilon_n -> 0.

The same causal finite-query countability obstruction therefore holds. The final
`countable_positive_approximate_causal_restoration` derives it for arbitrary
seed/oracle joint laws whose initial marginals share a dominating probability
measure, not necessarily an identical marginal. Actual input laws need not be
probabilities for this measure-theoretic countability statement; probability
interpretations use probability instances in the application.

The first-order scale cannot simply be discarded. The kernel theorem
`no_information_linear_tolerance` proves that q_n=1/2 works for EVERY
alpha in [0,1] whenever eta_n=epsilon_n>=0. Such a controller never asks a question.
Thus vanishing absolute tolerances alone do not preserve the no-go; the relative
scale matters. This result does not claim that every tolerance proportional to
epsilon is sufficient, nor give the exact minimax constant for an arbitrary norm
family. It supplies a sharp order-of-magnitude separation.

## T8. Sharpness through the literal zero-query controller

`BlindGuessSharpness.lean` proves `blind_causal_success_mass`. Its state is a
real-valued initial guess, it never probes, its transition leaves the guess
unchanged, and at time n it outputs 1/2 + guess * epsilon_n. For every true
alpha in [0,1], eventual literal BinaryGood holds if and only if guess=alpha.
Consequently its success probability is exactly the initial law's atomic mass
rho({alpha}), even when the unused oracle is arbitrarily correlated with the
initial guess. This is an actual evaluator example, not merely abstract
measurable disjoint events.

Ordinary consequence: choosing a probability with a positive atom at every
member of any prescribed nonempty countable S subset [0,1] gives positive
success exactly on S. Countability is therefore the exact possible size of the
positive-success exceptional set, rather than a claim that no parameter can
succeed. The standard existence construction of an atomic law on any countable
S is not separately formalised in this module. The exact atom-mass identity is.
