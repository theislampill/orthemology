# A source-sensitive test after granting genuine inference

## Target and attribution

The attention case cannot test the strongest inferential issue because it denies that Q was inferred from the nominated P/R. Here genuine competent deduction is granted. The question is whether its epistemic status transfers, not whether a merely associative transition deserves the word inference.

This is an elementary conditional reconstruction, not a new theorem or a simulation that produces beliefs. [The primary exchange](https://fitelson.org/epistemology/text_ch2.pdf) is described in PUBLIC_SOURCE_SUMMARIES.md. The equivalence/distribution argument is Hawthorne's, not an original discovery here. Dretske explicitly accepts its distribution cost; it is not an unnoticed contradiction in his position.

## Exact model and proof

Let P be the conjunction Q and A, so P logically entails Q independently of any chosen world set. Let S(e,X) abbreviate: in the closest admissible worlds where X is false, the evidential condition e is absent. The following finite, centered ordering supplies a model of that conditional, not a model of all information-theoretic formulations:

- Actual w0, distance 0: Q true, A true, hence P true; e present
- Alternative w1, distance 1: Q true, A false, hence P false; e absent
- Alternative w2, distance 2: Q false, A false, hence P false; e present

The interpretation of e remains fixed: the complete operative nonfactive basis to which the condition will be applied in this episode. That same basis can occur veridically at w0 and misleadingly at w2; there is no additional operative conclusive ground for Q hidden outside e. It is not relabelled after observing the answer. P and Q also retain their meanings. The displayed assignments respect the logical identity P = Q and A; the entailment is not manufactured by omitting counterexamples.

The nearest not-P world is w1, where e is absent, so S(e,P) holds. The nearest not-Q world is w2, where e is present, so S(e,Q) fails. Thus sensitivity to a proposition need not pass to its consequence, even with one fixed ordering and unchanged evidence type. No change of conversational context is needed for this mathematical fact.

Now add, explicitly and separately:

1. At w0 the subject knows P.
2. The subject competently deduces Q from P, thereby accepting Q, while retaining knowledge of P.
3. For the inferential episode, e remains the complete operative evidential basis to which the proposed conclusive-reasons requirement applies, and that basis persists at w2. No additional factive or independently conclusive operative ground for Q is available in this case.
4. For this episode, knowing X requires S(e,X).

By the model and premise 4, the subject does not know Q. This conflicts with unrestricted knowledge transfer in premise-2 cases. Neither competence, actual basing, knowledge of P, nor the applicability of premise 4 is derived from the truth table. In particular, premise 1 is additional to S(e,P): a necessary condition cannot generate knowledge merely by being satisfied.

The contradiction is between the enlarged package and unrestricted transfer. It is not a contradiction in a view that deliberately rejects that transfer. A denial of knowledge at the conclusion also does not by itself classify the deduction as irrational or the conclusion as false.

## The discriminating control: does the inferential ground change?

Premise 3 is substantive. The persistence of an earlier experience in a causal explanation does not establish that it remains the conclusion's epistemic basis. The subject may instead employ what they know, the fact P, as their operative reason. This is precisely where the inherited occasion/basis warning matters.

Under that alternative, use the fact P as e_Q. Since P entails Q at every admissible world, P is absent in every not-Q world. Therefore S(P,Q) holds, including at w2. The preceding failure cannot be reproduced with this factive ground. This still does not prove knowledge of Q: passing a necessary test is not sufficient. Nor is a factive reason the same thing as a potentially false belief that P.

This control blocks a would-be argument that inferential basing has already been handled just because the original sensory evidence continues to matter causally. It also prevents an easy refutation of the critic by announcing that all relevant reasons must be nonfactive. A choice between these accounts of the actual inferential ground needs an argument. Merely renaming the reason is not a completed solution.

## Why the model does not decide the epistemology

The ordering and alternatives have to be justified for a real case. Add w3 at distance 1.5 with Q and A false and e absent, while retaining w1 as the nearest not-P possibility, and the same actual episode can pass both sensitivity tests. That is a conditional dependence of the criterion on its modal interpretation, not an argument that an externalist subject must introspect or independently certify the ordering. Real environmental differences can matter to knowledge without being known by the subject.

The test leaves three distinguishable positions open:

- Retain the nonfactive-ground sensitivity requirement and accept restricted knowledge transfer.
- Defend factive operative grounds where applicable; this defeats this specific sensitivity counterexample, without alone proving unrestricted transfer.
- Defend another account of knowledge, relevant alternatives or inferential warrant, with its own obligations.

The strongest challenge is consequently about the right actual ground and warranted transmission conditions. It is not the already answered acquisition-circle objection, a demand that abstract truth act as another simultaneous physical trigger, or the claim that content has never been given any present role.

## Proper-function and programme limits

All cognitive content and actual competence in the test are granted. A full account of truth-directed proper functioning would still have to justify its epistemic condition and its application to the particular episode. The formal result does not supply that account, and it does not prove such an account impossible. It provides no direct transport to Creator recognition, original-source nonduplication, or the earlier skyline mathematics.

The earned increment is a more discriminating set of bridge premises: not simply that information exists, but which actual ground the inference uses and which modal or normative constraint is appropriate to that ground. No programme conclusion is forced.
