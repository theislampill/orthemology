## Posterior and seed quantifiers

After an all-zero prefix of length n≥1, the posterior is Beta(a,b+n). Its complementary exponent b+n-1 is nonnegative for every b>0. The substitution x=R y in its CDF gives F(z/R)≥R^(-a)F(z). This argument works for all positive real shapes, not only the integer shapes used in many finite checks. The normalizing constant is finite and positive, and substitution is legitimate at an integrable singularity at zero. No posterior asymptotic approximation is used.

A report may depend arbitrarily on the common seed and acquired prefix. Conditioning on that fixed prefix leaves the source-independent seed independent of alpha: the joint likelihood factors into the seed law and the parameter's prefix likelihood. Therefore each fixed seed report is subject to the same posterior lower bound, and averaging cannot improve it. If the seed encoded alpha, or if the policy acquired extra uncounted receipts, this step would fail; those experiments are excluded explicitly.

## Infinite cost and uniform-prior clock

The all-zero and all-one prefix events are disjoint for n≥1. Their probabilities are the Beta mixed moments, not fair-coin probabilities. Tonelli permits summing their nonnegative contributions. The resulting lower bound involves E[(1-alpha)/alpha] and E[alpha/(1-alpha)]. The Beta density makes these integrals diverge precisely at a≤1 or b≤1. No endpoint-correctness assumption is needed, and the prior has no endpoint atoms.

For a,b>1 the two moments are b/(a-1) and a/(b-1), giving the stated finite lower bound. For the uniform prior and epsilon=1/4, each extreme-prefix probability is 1/(n+1), and the coefficient is 3/5+5/9=52/45. Summing n=1,...,N yields (52/45)(H_(N+1)-1). The empty prefix at n=0 is excluded from this double-event sum. Counting it twice would give a one-step probability lower bound greater than one.
