# A source-coupled audit instance with exact anytime confidence intervals

Research construction, 2026-10-01. This is a **constructed, contract-relative instance**, not a report of an existing deployed audit service or empirical norm authentication. The confidence method is classical. Its purpose is to make the same selector supply the score and the evidence, then to expose the boundaries of that binding.

## 1. One source supplies the observation law and operational score

Fix a binary proposition with possible truth vectors v=(1,0) or v=(0,1). A reporting API accepts two numbers b=(b₁,b₂) for the proposition and its complement. A contract, fixed before the evidence is observed, charges the squared error of a randomly selected coordinate. Let J=1 select the first coordinate and J=0 the second, with conditional law Bernoulli(α). Here α is fixed but not disclosed. The contract supplies fresh selector draws independent of the reports under comparison and, conditional on either possible truth, has the same selector probability α.

The actual single-draw charge is

    C(b,v,J) = J(b₁−v₁)² + (1−J)(b₂−v₂)².

Its conditional expectation is exactly

    E[C(b,v,J) | v] = Sα(b,v)
                   = α(b₁−v₁)² + (1−α)(b₂−v₂)².

Thus α is not separately wired into a sensor that happens to read an independently stipulated norm. One physical/procedural selector both chooses the charged coordinate and creates the observable coordinate-selection receipt. The expectation identity is the source-to-score map.

A concrete implementation would have to authenticate this identity. The abstract contract only stipulates it. A normative reason to accept this contract, legal validity, and any wider epistemic or moral authority are separate questions.

### Law, score, licence and evidence bindings

- **Law:** conditional on the full past before the next selector draw, the draw has probability α. A fixed Bernoulli product law is enough. The law is conditional on each relevant truth, not merely a marginal mixture over truths
- **Score:** the charge formula and selector law above determine the expected score. Neither weight nor scoring revision is fitted after observing the repair
- **Licence:** users may read their completed selector receipts and may replace the still-open prospective report with any coherent pair (q,1−q) before its lock time. Completed charges cannot be changed. This access/commitment rule is fixed independently of the statistical repair test
- **Evidence:** every counted receipt is bound to one actual draw, the same selector revision and score contract, the relevant sampling population, and a recording order before the repair decision. No duplicated or value-selected receipts are counted as fresh draws
- **Decision:** the inference procedure only selects among licensed prospective coherent reports. Its confidence certificate supplies a risk guarantee; it does not manufacture permission

Viewing past logs is passive information acquisition in this instance. It does not automatically instantiate the separate action-sensitive restoration theorem or its physical bad-action budget. A service that charges for, censors, or changes its selector when logs are requested needs its own acquisition contract.

## 2. The correct all-state reading integrates out the selector

The root's robust-repair theorem compares Sα(c,v) and Sα(b,v) for both v. This is all-truth-state dominance **in expected audit charge**. It does not imply improvement for every realized pair (v,J).

An exact control is b=(3/4,1/2), α=1/2, c=(5/8,3/8), v=(0,1). Expected charge decreases by 1/64. But if the second coordinate is audited, charge increases from 1/4 to 25/64, an increase of 9/64. The selector has been integrated out of the stated score. Adding J to the truth-state space changes the mathematical contract.

This distinction is essential for genuine application binding: a verified expectation identity does not authenticate a pathwise guarantee with a different state space.

## 3. Exact rational procedure

For n≥1 observed draws, let s be the count of J=1. Choose rational δ∈(0,1). Spend

    δₙ = δ/[n(n+1)],     aₙ = δₙ/2.

Define inclusive binomial-tail polynomials

    Gₙ,s(p) = Σ(k=s..n) binom(n,k) pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ,
    Hₙ,s(p) = Σ(k=0..s) binom(n,k) pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ.

For s>0, let ℓ* be the unique root Gₙ,s(ℓ*)=aₙ; for s=0 set ℓ*=0. For s<n, let u* be the unique root Hₙ,s(u*)=aₙ; for s=n set u*=1.

The implemented interval [ℓ,u] is an **outward dyadic enclosure** of [ℓ*,u*]. At the lower root, rational bisection retains a bracket satisfying G(ℓ)≤aₙ≤G(ℓ_inside). At the upper root it retains H(u)≤aₙ≤H(u_inside). Every operation uses Python integers/Fraction, including all tail sums. No floating approximation decides validity.

Default bisection depth is bit_length(n)+8, so each endpoint is displaced outward by at most 2^−depth<1/(256n). Returned inside endpoints allow the final bracket inequalities to be rechecked independently. Fixed precision can be requested for implementation tests, but shrinking precision is part of the consistency claim.

## 4. Coverage proof for every real α∈[0,1]

The proof is not a numerical test over parameter values.

### Tail monotonicity and roots

For 1≤s≤n, differentiation gives

    Gₙ,s'(p) = n binom(n−1,s−1) pˢ⁻¹(1−p)ⁿ⁻ˢ > 0,

for 0<p<1. Also G(0)=0 and G(1)=1. This gives its unique root. The lower-tail polynomial is strictly decreasing for s<n, by complementation. At any p,

    Gₙ,s(p)+Hₙ,s(p) = 1 + Pₚ(Sₙ=s) ≥ 1.

At the lower root G=aₙ<1/2, therefore H>aₙ. Since H decreases, its upper root lies strictly above the lower root. Endpoint cases also produce a nonempty interval. Bisection's invariant proves the implemented enclosure contains the exact interval, regardless of whether a root is rational.

### Discrete upper-tail superuniformity

Fix an arbitrary real α and let S have its Binomial(n,α) distribution. For any a∈(0,1), the set of integer counts s with Gₙ,s(α)<a is either empty or an upper tail {m,…,n}, because G decreases in the count index. Its probability is exactly Gₙ,m(α)<a. Thus

    Pα(Gₙ,S(α)<a) ≤ a.

The corresponding lower-tail statement follows in the same way. Endpoint α=0 or1 causes no problem: only its degenerate count occurs, and both relevant inclusive tail probabilities are one.

If α<ℓ*(S), monotonicity implies Gₙ,S(α)<aₙ. If α>u*(S), then Hₙ,S(α)<aₙ. A union bound yields

    Pα(α∉[ℓ*,u*]) ≤ 2aₙ = δₙ.

Outward rounding can only reduce noncoverage. Finally, countable subadditivity and the telescoping identity Σₙ≥1 1/[n(n+1)]=1 give

    Pα(∀n≥1: α∈[ℓₙ,uₙ]) ≥ 1−δ.

This holds for **each arbitrary real α** under its source law. It is not a claim that one realized confidence sequence contains every possible parameter simultaneously.

The same proof applies when the full-past conditional probability of each counted bit is α: induction gives the binomial count law. It does not apply after selecting which bits to count based on their realized values, to repeated copies of old bits, or to a changing conditional parameter without replacing the estimand and inference method.

## 5. Sound composition with the robust repair certificate

For bε=(1/2+ε,1/2), 0<ε≤1/4, and interval [ℓ,u], put m=(ℓ+u)/2, d=(u−ℓ)/2. The root's exact formula gives

    q = 1/2 + mε − dε²,
    R = dε − m(1−m)ε² − 2mdε³ + d²ε⁴.

For c=(q,1−q), every α∈[ℓ,u] and both truth states satisfy

    Sα(c,v)−Sα(bε,v) ≤ R.

Hence accepting only a certificate with R≤η gives excess expected score at most η on the simultaneous-coverage event. For strict repair accept R<0. The interval endpoints, q, R and η can all be rational and checked without floating-point decisions.

On the single event ∀n α∈[ℓₙ,uₙ], the guarantee holds at every data-dependent finite index and for every choice of ε and η in the allowed domains. This is a pathwise implication from a simultaneous statement. It requires no separate optional-stopping argument. It does not turn the numerical confidence level into posterior probability conditional on the realized interval.

All claims concern the fixed source/score contract. Reusing an old interval after the selector or scoring revision changes has no such guarantee.

## 6. Safety is distinct from termination

The intervals shrink to the true parameter almost surely. One way to see the width bound is the standard binomial Hoeffding inequality: with rₙ=sqrt(log(1/aₙ)/(2n)), the exact roots lie within rₙ of s/n, clipped to [0,1]. Outward rounding adds less than 1/(256n). Thus interval width tends to zero deterministically, and the strong law gives centre convergence to α.

For a fixed 0<ε≤1/4 and α∈(0,1), R tends to −α(1−α)ε²<0. Therefore the first strict-certification time is finite almost surely. This termination statement is an ordinary deduction using standard concentration and the strong law; the implementation tests are not its proof. Its safety probability remains at least 1−δ, not one.

At α=0, every honest interval has the form [0,u] with u>0. Weak all-standard repair is impossible: truth (0,1) at α=0 requires q≤1/2, while truth (1,0) at α=u>0 requires q>1/2. At α=1, the endpoint truth requires q≥1/2+ε, whereas any included ℓ<1 requires q<1/2+ε under the other truth. Therefore no finite nondegenerate interval certifies weak repair there, even though the endpoint-only oracle problem permits a tie.

A shrinking perturbation schedule also changes termination. Exact valid intervals [1/2−1/(4n),1/2+1/(4n)] converge to α=1/2, but εₙ=1/(4n²) makes R>0 at every n≥2. Consistency alone is insufficient when the required precision tightens faster than the evidence.

### 6.1 A conservative finite expected acquisition bound

Suppose the fixed true parameter satisfies α∈[κ,1−κ] for some 0<κ≤1/2. This κ is used for analysis; the certificate need not know it. Let

    rₙ = sqrt(log(2n(n+1)/δ)/(2n)),    hₙ = 1/(256n).

The exact interval construction gives dₙ≤rₙ+hₙ and |mₙ−Sₙ/n|≤rₙ+hₙ. Choose N≥2 with r_N+h_N≤κε/8. Both terms decrease for n≥2, so the condition holds thereafter. If n≥N and |Sₙ/n−α|≤κ/4, then |mₙ−α|<κ/2 and consequently

    mₙ(1−mₙ) ≥ (κ/2)(1−κ/2) ≥ κ/4,
    dₙ ≤ κε/8 < mₙ(1−mₙ)ε.

Because interval bounds give dₙ≤mₙ, the remaining cubic/quartic terms combine as dₙε³(dₙε−2mₙ)≤0. The strict leading gap therefore gives Rₙ<0. If τ is the first strict certificate time, τ>n implies failure of that sample-mean deviation condition, regardless of whether the uniform coverage event held. Hoeffding gives

    Pα(τ>n) ≤ 2 exp(−nκ²/8),   n≥N,
    Eα[τ] ≤ N + 2 exp(−Nκ²/8)/(1−exp(−κ²/8)) < ∞.

The formula follows from Eτ=Σₙ≥0 P(τ>n), bounding the first N summands by one. It is deliberately conservative. Its dependence on κ is material: no uniform endpoint-free claim survives as α tends to0 or1. It counts actual fresh selector draws, not replayed entries, and does not establish that reading n receipts has no other operational cost.

This expected-time bound is an ordinary mathematical consequence of the specified Bernoulli source law and the interval-width bound. It is not covered by the generated Lean certificate cases below, and finite termination does not upgrade1−δ safety to almost-sure safety.

## 7. Adversarial controls and what they establish

The replay script checks the following with exact rational arithmetic.

1. Direct binomial summation agrees with the tail recurrence across the count/parameter cases recorded in the result
2. Exact finite-history probability flow verifies spending coverage through 64 observations at seven parameters. This is an implementation check beside the all-real proof above
3. Reusing fixed-time 95% Clopper–Pearson intervals at every observation through time128 exceeds the nominal5% failure probability at α=1/2. Spending is genuinely doing work
4. A symmetric count record n=4096,s=2048 yields a strict rational repair certificate. This is a possible constructed record, not external observed data
5. Under true α=4/5, replaying an initial mixed two-bit block fabricates the symmetric record. The event that the initial pair is mixed has probability8/25. The forged certificate then worsens one actual expected truth-state score
6. Value-selecting ones with probability1/4 and zeros with probability1 changes the visible parameter from4/5 to1/2, while the underlying audit score remains weighted by4/5
7. Changing selector probability **only for the already coherent corrected report** is benign for its immediate expected charge: both coordinate errors coincide. The harmful change is using old evidence for a new baseline selector or otherwise changing the compared contract. No blanket claim that all endogenous selectors fail is made
8. Expected versus realized charge, endpoint nontermination, and a shrinking-ε nontermination schedule are checked explicitly

Receipt IDs and hashes can detect an ordinary duplicate but cannot themselves prove a fresh underlying draw. A malicious service can invent fresh IDs for replayed values. The evidence-to-source correspondence remains an empirical/authentication obligation, not a consequence of the rational checker.

## 8. Primary ancestry and novelty limits

Clopper and Pearson (1934), *Biometrika*26(4),404–413, pp.406–407, constructs central binomial intervals by inclusive-tail inversion and explains conservative coverage. Our exact interval is that classical construction with allocated error probabilities and outward rational enclosure. Primary scan: https://www.barestatistics.nl/uploads/1/1/7/9/11797954/clopper__pearson_1934.pdf ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404 . The scanned formulas and confidence interpretation were inspected; no priority credit is attached to exact binomial intervals.

Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon (2021), *Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences*, Eq.(1), §1.1 and Lemma3, gives the simultaneous-coverage and arbitrary-random-time framework, and reviews mixture and error-spending ancestry. Our simple every-time spending bound is deliberately conservative; it does not reproduce their sharp boundaries. Primary preprint, current checked version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08240 . Confidence sequences predate the present work by decades.

Hoeffding(1963), *Probability Inequalities for Sums of Bounded Random Variables*, Theorem1, printedp.15, inequality(2.3), supplies the bounded-Bernoulli tail bound used for width and termination. The original scan was visually inspected: https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf . Its lower-tail version follows by complementing the Bernoulli variables.

The contribution of this worked instance is the explicit binding of selector, expected score, observable receipt, permitted prospective correction and exact robust certificate, with failure controls. It establishes internal soundness of a constructed protocol. It does not establish a new general inference theorem, a deployed application, or ultimate normative authority.
