## The shape of the commentary

The short creed supplies a sequence from creator and attributes to prophecy and the afterlife. The commentary accepts broad conclusions but repeatedly opens their meanings and proofs. Its long discussions of action, succession and speech therefore investigate what an active, qualified creator is, rather than simply interrupting a unity argument.

The later prophecy discussion examines how a guide becomes known through message, conduct, signs, experience and testimony. The extended treatment of al-Ghazali tests both the strengths and limits of an experiential route. Graded faith and mixed intellectual contributions explain how the commentary can criticise a doctrine while distinguishing a person’s knowledge, intentions and circumstances. The ending returns to the opening distinction between general assent and authenticated detail.

Read the movement through predication (A02–A03), existence and unity (A06–A10), activity and wisdom (A11–A24), speech and meaningful attributes (A25–A28), and prophetic knowledge and its reception (A29–A38). These are connected questions, not interchangeable proofs.

## A01 The creed and its commentator

main pp. 5–8; PDF 105–108. Source voice: Premodern transmitting prefacer; al-Asbahani; then Ibn Taymiyya.

The compact creed supplies claims about creator, attributes, prophecy and the afterlife. The commentator initially accepts the broad affirmations while reopening their demonstrations. Acceptance of a conclusion must not be mistaken for acceptance of the base text’s proof.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A05](#a05-when-an-outline-becomes-an-argument), [A27](#a27-speech-bearer-and-voluntary-exercise).

Qualification: The prefacer’s praise is not the commentator describing himself; the source voices change within this passage.

## A02 True predication and fitting names

main pp. 8–11; 426–431; PDF 108–111; 526–531. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with scriptural citations.

A true attribution does not automatically supply an unrestricted divine name. The later discussion of harmful outcomes returns to this distinction: naming must preserve the relevant wisdom, justice and context, rather than detach one predicate from its qualifying relations.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [A23](#a23-meaningful-negation-and-authorised-response), [A28](#a28-meaningful-names-and-distinct-perfection-routes).

Qualification: This later passage refines the earlier naming rule; it does not erase real attribution of will or speech.

## A03 Speaking and causing another to speak

main pp. 11–25; PDF 111–125. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, distinguishing positions he reports.

Producing a speaker or utterance differs from being the subject whose speech it is. Likewise, a quality’s originating cause is not simply its bearer. The early argument distinguishes an agent’s act from its separated product.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A19](#a19-transmission-through-another-voice), [A27](#a27-speech-bearer-and-voluntary-exercise).

Qualification: The attribution distinction is not itself a proof that two complete productive causes are impossible.

## A04 Reason testimony and differentiated attributes

main pp. 25–43; PDF 125–143. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya; opponents’ positions identified within his replies.

Failure to supply one rational proof does not establish a predicate’s absence. Reliable testimony has evidential work to do, while beneficence discloses more than bare power. Occurrence, willing and approval remain distinct. Understanding an opponent’s reasons also bears on fair judgement.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A05](#a05-when-an-outline-becomes-an-argument), [A23](#a23-meaningful-negation-and-authorised-response), [D24](#d24-false-attribution-and-mistaken-conviction), [A32](#a32-qualified-guidance-and-different-paths-to-knowledge), [A33](#a33-graded-faith-and-judgements-in-different-respects).

Qualification: Testimonial authority presupposes warranted reliability; school labels do not settle every member’s position.

## A05 When an outline becomes an argument

main pp. 43–47; 133; 138–139; 638–639; PDF 143–147; 233; 238–239; 738–739. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

An indication of a proof is not a completed proof when its premises and objections remain unsettled. The later qualification allows genuinely evident, agreed premises to remain unstated. Completeness therefore concerns justified inferential work, not maximal verbal length.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A06](#a06-necessary-existence-and-persistent-dependence), [A26](#a26-inferential-form-does-not-replace-premises), [D30](#d30-historical-penalties-and-the-limits-of-shared-names), [A30](#a30-cumulative-evidence-and-learning-from-a-guide), [A32](#a32-qualified-guidance-and-different-paths-to-knowledge).

Qualification: The evident premise at main pp. 138–139 is collaborators’ antecedent capacity. Its status cannot simply be transferred to every subsequent causal-nonduplication premise; knowledge and its articulable presentation also need distinction.

## A06 Necessary existence and persistent dependence

main pp. 47–62; PDF 147–162. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, examining the base creed and alternative proofs.

Observed origination establishes concrete need more directly than an abstract detour through possibility. Created things depend on a source both in beginning and in continuing. The aggregate argument denies that a member needing another can be the complete source of all dependent things including itself.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A07](#a07-correlation-is-not-efficient-causation), [A10](#a10-complete-production-and-a-connected-world), [A15](#a15-rival-cosmologies-and-open-alternatives).

Qualification: The conclusion needs care: necessary existence alone does not yet identify the world’s creator or exclude every rival plurality.

## A07 Correlation is not efficient causation

main pp. 62–107; especially 93–95 and 103–104; PDF 162–207; especially 193–195 and 203–204. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with base-text and al-Razi arguments distinguished.

The commentary rejects deriving external need merely from inseparable attributes, conceptual distinctions or mutual entailment. A shared universal is not a single external component divided among instances. Its alternative route to uniqueness expressly requires a further premise about two agents’ participation.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A10](#a10-complete-production-and-a-connected-world), [A26](#a26-inferential-form-does-not-replace-premises).

Qualification: Rejecting this composition proof neither rejects unity nor independently proves that multiple necessary beings are possible; see the volume’s textual caution.

## A08 Knowledge worship and ultimate ends

main pp. 107–116; PDF 207–216. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

The author distinguishes attributes, universal lordship and exclusive worship. Knowing a very general concept is not yet knowing the concrete highest object. Human intentions lead into a separate discussion of ends sought for their own sake and of the fitting ultimate object of devotion.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A09](#a09-conflict-between-alleged-equal-creators), [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [D22](#d22-creator-acknowledgement-and-exclusive-worship).

Qualification: The move from explanatory source to rightful end needs its own normative warrant; a shared label does not supply it.

## A09 Conflict between alleged equal creators

main pp. 116–133; especially 121–123; PDF 216–233; especially 221–223. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with attributed religious polemic.

Incompatible outcomes cannot both occur. The further reply to inevitable agreement distinguishes an intrinsically contradictory object from a possible act blocked by another agent. The surrounding discussion separately asks why acknowledged creation should direct worship.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A10](#a10-complete-production-and-a-connected-world), [A08](#a08-knowledge-worship-and-ultimate-ends).

Qualification: Whether primitive necessary concord counts as another-imposed obstruction remains a comparison question. Medieval accounts of other religions remain attributed representations.

## A10 Complete production and a connected world

main pp. 134–154; PDF 234–254. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

The author presents a positive demonstration against two complete independent producers of one effect, treating independence as production alone. He then examines cooperation, domination and separate domains. Ordinary cooperation uses capacities already present; ecological and cosmic interdependence challenge isolated creation-domains.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A07](#a07-correlation-is-not-efficient-causation), [A12](#a12-real-causes-with-conditions-and-impediments), [A23](#a23-meaningful-negation-and-authorised-response).

Qualification: The whole-alone contradiction does not by itself exclude nonexclusive double sufficiency. That further exclusion requires the broader causal-nonduplication principle and a separate assessment of its warrant.

## A11 Perpetual activity and particular products

main pp. 154–184; 295–303; PDF 254–284; 395–403. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya; opposing arguments marked as such.

An ongoing kind of action need not make any particular creature eternal. The commentary asks how renewed effects arise if every productive condition is wholly unchanged, while separating a regress of dependent agents from a succession of acts.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A16](#a16-real-succession-without-simultaneous-parts), [A17](#a17-perfection-at-the-appropriate-level), [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits).

Qualification: Do not translate these distinctions directly into a modern modal system or treat the critique as endorsement of eternal heavens.

## A12 Real causes with conditions and impediments

main pp. 166–184; especially 170–179; PDF 266–284; especially 270–279. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with scriptural examples.

Creaturely powers genuinely contribute but need accompanying conditions and may meet impediments. The conditions of acting must be present with the act. This does not establish that every former producer must remain present while its external product persists. Instrumental and purposive relations are both retained.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A21](#a21-power-will-and-created-agency), [D02](#d02-remedy-use-receptivity-and-obstruction), [D06](#d06-decree-includes-means).

Qualification: A currently acting agent, a historical producer, the act and its surviving product have different roles. Absence of an impediment is not itself a positive efficient producer.

## A13 Voluntary response and temporal sequence

main pp. 201–278; especially 256–263; PDF 301–378; especially 356–363. Source voice: Quoted early authorities, then Ibn Taymiyya’s direct argument.

Transmitted narratives and conditional wording support acts at fitting times. Obedience and disobedience are described as causes of approval and displeasure. This responsive relation must be preserved alongside the rejection of another supplier of essential power.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [A23](#a23-meaningful-negation-and-authorised-response).

Qualification: The sequence contains distinct quoted positions; the later reconciliation is still a comparative question, not a newly settled metaphysical verdict.

## A14 Narratives within the attribute discussion

main pp. 263–278; PDF 363–378. Source voice: Reports selected and interpreted by Ibn Taymiyya.

Repentance, hospitality, character, remembrance, fear of death and forgiveness belong to the argument’s devotional setting. The collection uses exchanges between speaker and respondent to make succession intelligible, without reducing its ethical purposes to abstract metaphysics.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [A13](#a13-voluntary-response-and-temporal-sequence), [D08](#d08-warning-narratives-and-interpersonal-rights).

Qualification: Reports remain attributed testimony; this index does not independently authenticate them or turn their imagery into physical observation.

## A15 Rival cosmologies and open alternatives

main pp. 278–294; 303–334; PDF 378–394; 403–434. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with nested reports from al-Razi and other writers.

The five-eternals account assigns different explanatory roles to maker, soul, matter, time and space. The critique asks whether this relocates the origination problem. Later discussion rejects treating the known rival physical theories as exhaustive and distinguishes defeating one view from proving its competitor.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [A06](#a06-necessary-existence-and-persistent-dependence), [A26](#a26-inferential-form-does-not-replace-premises).

Qualification: These are mediated medieval accounts, not independently verified histories of ancient philosophy or accepted modern physics.

## A16 Real succession without simultaneous parts

main pp. 334–357; PDF 434–457. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, replying to an account transmitted through al-Razi.

A motion, utterance or transaction can genuinely occur successively even though its parts never coexist. Its universal concept, a selected phase and the whole temporal process are different objects of thought. An incomplete journey is still travel that has occurred.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A11](#a11-perpetual-activity-and-particular-products), [A19](#a19-transmission-through-another-voice).

Qualification: The examples clarify an ontology of succession; they do not themselves establish identity or continuity criteria for every modern system.

## A17 Perfection at the appropriate level

main pp. 358–361; 507–508; 532–536; PDF 458–461; 607–608; 632–636. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

Continually renewed goods differ from one eternal particular product. The later argument separates exclusion of defect, intrinsic fittingness and the giver’s priority to a received perfection. Intrinsic perfection is not made dependent on finding a creaturely example; the giver argument does use one.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A20](#a20-concrete-signs-and-qualified-inference), [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [A28](#a28-meaningful-names-and-distinct-perfection-routes).

Qualification: Apply the conditions to each candidate perfection; do not transfer a creature’s benefit unqualified to the creator.

## A18 Reasoning and fair historical judgement

main pp. 361–393; especially 365 and 383–388; PDF 461–493; especially 465 and 483–488. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with quoted doctrinal and historical testimony.

Sound reasoning is permitted even without a recorded predecessor. The objection is to making a defective or exclusive route mandatory. Historical criticism also distinguishes disputed teachings from personal slander and recognises mixed contributions within rival schools.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [A04](#a04-reason-testimony-and-differentiated-attributes), [A05](#a05-when-an-outline-becomes-an-argument).

Qualification: The author’s accounts of controversies and coercion are historical claims, not verified causal histories or judgements about present communities.

## A19 Transmission through another voice

main pp. 389–391; PDF 489–491. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

A message can remain its originator’s speech when another person conveys it through a different voice and bodily act. This supplies a positive account alongside the earlier rejection of an attribute physically migrating between subjects.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A03](#a03-speaking-and-causing-another-to-speak), [A16](#a16-real-succession-without-simultaneous-parts), [A27](#a27-speech-bearer-and-voluntary-exercise).

Qualification: Preserved authorship is neither numerical identity of acoustic events nor proof that a particular carrier transmitted faithfully.

## A20 Concrete signs and qualified inference

main pp. 393–397; PDF 493–497. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

General inferential forms supply universal descriptions, while concrete signs are presented as directing knowledge toward their particular source. The knowledge argument combines intention, ordered production and perfection-based reasoning. These are distinguishable supports rather than one interchangeable proof.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A06](#a06-necessary-existence-and-persistent-dependence), [A17](#a17-perfection-at-the-appropriate-level), [A26](#a26-inferential-form-does-not-replace-premises).

Qualification: A concrete sign’s use within the author’s ontology does not independently discharge every contested uniqueness premise.

## A21 Power will and created agency

main pp. 397–408; supplementary text at 721–722; PDF 497–508; 821–822. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, separating his position from reported alternatives.

Living agency and natural forces have real effects. Complete power together with decisive will entails the intended act. The later supplementary passage distinguishes knowing an independent object from knowing an intended act: the latter conditions production without replacing power and will.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A12](#a12-real-causes-with-conditions-and-impediments), [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [D23](#d23-mediation-without-divine-deficiency).

Qualification: Knowledge as correspondence, a necessary condition and a complete productive cause must remain distinct. An intrinsically possible alternative need not actually occur under the relevant will and wisdom.

## A22 Wisdom timely goods and multiple benefits

main pp. 409–431; PDF 509–531. Source voice: Al-Razi’s objections, followed by Ibn Taymiyya’s replies.

A good attained at its fitting time need not have been a missing earlier perfection. A chosen means may benefit several parties, so directly providing one outcome can omit other goods. The author also distinguishes an intrinsically loved end from endlessly deferred instrumental ends.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A02](#a02-true-predication-and-fitting-names), [A13](#a13-voluntary-response-and-temporal-sequence), [A17](#a17-perfection-at-the-appropriate-level), [D27](#d27-uninvited-thoughts-and-fitting-reflection), [A35](#a35-wisdom-and-the-authentication-of-messengers).

Qualification: Unknown particular wisdom is not thereby absent, but this gives no automatic warrant to defend every human claim of hidden benefit.

## A23 Meaningful negation and authorised response

main pp. 432–443; especially 435; PDF 532–543; especially 535. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with scriptural and transmitted examples.

Praiseworthy negation points to a positive perfection rather than mere absence. Intercession is considered through permission and the creation of both means and outcomes. This gives the responsive-act passages a later context without eliminating genuine response.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A13](#a13-voluntary-response-and-temporal-sequence), [A10](#a10-complete-production-and-a-connected-world), [D23](#d23-mediation-without-divine-deficiency).

Qualification: The reconciliation distinguishes authorised means from an independently needed supplier; whether every disputed relation fits that division requires further testing.

## A24 Moral qualities and divine wisdom

main pp. 444–450; PDF 544–550. Source voice: Reported Muʿtazili argument and al-Razi objection; Ibn Taymiyya’s reply.

Acts can differ in truth, justice, fittingness and harm. Reducing them all to intrinsic indifference threatens to make praise of good commands empty. The author resists an inherence taxonomy that would erase ordinary distinctions between better and worse acts.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [D21](#d21-degrees-of-wrongdoing-and-their-purposes), [D25](#d25-distinct-rights-and-qualified-resemblance).

Qualification: Nested arguments must remain separate; the implications for necessarily good willing and capacity are not resolved merely by assigning a school label.

## A25 Life and specification

main pp. 450–452; PDF 550–552. Source voice: Base creed followed by Ibn Taymiyya.

Knowledge and power require life. The world’s particular determinations invite explanation; attributing differences to receptive natures postpones the question if those natures also require specification. The account rejects an externally indeterminate source.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A06](#a06-necessary-existence-and-persistent-dependence), [A21](#a21-power-will-and-created-agency).

Qualification: Compare the earlier refusal to require an external specifier of the necessary being at main p. 364; the relevant senses of specification need care.

## A26 Inferential form does not replace premises

main pp. 453–467; PDF 553–567. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, examining logical forms and emanation arguments.

Syllogistic and analogical presentations depend on justified content, not certainty supplied by form alone. A proposed universal cannot be established by inadequate examples or by using its intended divine conclusion as its sole instance. Shared concepts need not be external constituents.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A05](#a05-when-an-outline-becomes-an-argument), [A07](#a07-correlation-is-not-efficient-causation), [D05](#d05-success-does-not-isolate-its-cause), [D25](#d25-distinct-rights-and-qualified-resemblance).

Qualification: Natural examples remain historically situated. Criticism of an opponent’s generalisation is not the author conceding a second ultimate source.

## A27 Speech bearer and voluntary exercise

main pp. 468–508; PDF 568–608. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya; quoted schools and early authorities distinguished.

A reliable report can establish an actuality whose impossibility was not known beforehand. Later discussion distinguishes lexical names, a quality’s bearer, an act and its product. The completed reply affirms both speech inhering in the speaker and its voluntary exercise, rather than reducing attribution to either alone.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A03](#a03-speaking-and-causing-another-to-speak), [A19](#a19-transmission-through-another-voice), [D05](#d05-success-does-not-isolate-its-cause), [A28](#a28-meaningful-names-and-distinct-perfection-routes), [A38](#a38-authority-beyond-the-initial-claim-of-office).

Qualification: Mere conceivability does not authenticate a report. The speech argument distinguishes an enduring capacity, a kind of activity and a particular utterance; none should be substituted for another.

## A28 Meaningful names and distinct perfection routes

main pp. 508–537; PDF 608–637. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, distinguishing quoted alternatives from his replies.

Hearing and sight are not reduced to generic knowledge, and divine names are not empty labels. The inquiry distinguishes semantic contradiction from merely negative wording. It also separates several positive routes to perfection instead of treating every appeal to perfection as one argument.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A02](#a02-true-predication-and-fitting-names), [A17](#a17-perfection-at-the-appropriate-level), [A27](#a27-speech-bearer-and-voluntary-exercise).

Qualification: The discussion of capacity and incapacity is a theological argument; it is not an assessment of the worth of people with impairments.

## A29 Prophecy and more than extraordinary events

main pp. 537–548; PDF 637–648. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

Miracles are affirmed among several evidential routes. A claimant’s message, actions and character also matter. Deliberate fabrication differs from sincerely mistaken experience, so evidence addressing one possible failure cannot automatically settle the other.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A30](#a30-cumulative-evidence-and-learning-from-a-guide), [A34](#a34-testimony-character-and-independent-agreement), [A35](#a35-wisdom-and-the-authentication-of-messengers).

Qualification: An unusual occurrence is not sufficient merely because it is unusual. The author’s interpretation of unseen experiences remains his theological account.

## A30 Cumulative evidence and learning from a guide

main pp. 549–572; PDF 649–672. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, interpreting reported inquiries and historical signs.

The Heraclius inquiry combines several marks rather than making any single one sufficient. The author allows evidence to accumulate into knowledge, while interpreting trials as formative and discriminating. Recognising a guide through independently known truths can warrant receiving further details without already possessing the guide’s whole knowledge.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A05](#a05-when-an-outline-becomes-an-argument), [A29](#a29-prophecy-and-more-than-extraordinary-events), [A32](#a32-qualified-guidance-and-different-paths-to-knowledge), [A34](#a34-testimony-character-and-independent-agreement).

Qualification: The account is not a numerical guarantee about a fixed number of reports. Historical deliverance and adversity receive the author’s theological interpretation, not an independently verified causal law.

## A31 Experience description and fallible disclosure

main pp. 573–620; PDF 673–720. Source voice: Al-Ghazali’s quoted autobiography, followed by Ibn Taymiyya’s qualified response.

Al-Ghazali’s quoted narrative distinguishes describing a state from undergoing it and presents several sources of certainty. Ibn Taymiyya accepts substantial experiential recognition, while denying that recognising expertise means possessing its whole science or that a saint’s disclosure replaces detailed prophetic testimony.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [A29](#a29-prophecy-and-more-than-extraordinary-events), [A32](#a32-qualified-guidance-and-different-paths-to-knowledge), [A34](#a34-testimony-character-and-independent-agreement).

Qualification: The quoted autobiography and the commentator’s response retain separate voices. The main p. 606 variant remains in the textual caution; disputed historical and scientific examples are not independently established here.

## A32 Qualified guidance and different paths to knowledge

main pp. 620–657; PDF 720–757. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya, with nested critics and historical reports.

Prophetic address and relevant qualification are held together. A route helpful to one learner can restrict another; discovering one route does not eliminate others. Actual grounds of knowledge may exceed the argument a person can articulate. Unwanted thoughts are distinguished from accepted propositions.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A04](#a04-reason-testimony-and-differentiated-attributes), [A05](#a05-when-an-outline-becomes-an-argument), [A31](#a31-experience-description-and-fallible-disclosure), [A33](#a33-graded-faith-and-judgements-in-different-respects), [D27](#d27-uninvited-thoughts-and-fitting-reflection).

Qualification: An inarticulate conviction is not thereby knowledge. The author’s selective defence and criticism of al-Ghazali must not be flattened into complete endorsement or rejection.

## A33 Graded faith and judgements in different respects

main pp. 657–676; PDF 757–776. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

A person can have grounds for praise and blame in different respects. Obligations vary with received revelation and present capacity. Missing effect excludes a complete cause, but not every contributory cause; pride, envy and other impediments can interrupt the fuller practical consequences of assent.

Qualification: A doctrine’s classification does not alone decide each adherent’s standing. Excusing sincere error does not mean rewarding the error itself.

## A34 Testimony character and independent agreement

main pp. 676–695; PDF 776–795. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

Character can become known through sustained dealings without an endless chain of external validators. A criticism needs reasons; honesty still differs from immunity to mistake. Detailed agreement between independent accounts can add evidence, alongside message, conduct and context.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A19](#a19-transmission-through-another-voice), [A29](#a29-prophecy-and-more-than-extraordinary-events), [A30](#a30-cumulative-evidence-and-learning-from-a-guide), [A32](#a32-qualified-guidance-and-different-paths-to-knowledge).

Qualification: Habitual reliability is not logical impossibility of an exceptional lie. General transmitted agreement does not authenticate every detailed report.

## A35 Wisdom and the authentication of messengers

main pp. 695–711; PDF 795–811. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya’s positive account and separately reported rival routes.

The author grounds one authentication route in known wisdom, mercy and beneficial governance. He then reports alternatives that treat a contextual miraculous act as ratification. The discussion also returns to moral value arising through both an act’s qualities and a wise command.

Status: Relevant. Links: [A22](#a22-wisdom-timely-goods-and-multiple-benefits), [A24](#a24-moral-qualities-and-divine-wisdom), [A29](#a29-prophecy-and-more-than-extraordinary-events), [A38](#a38-authority-beyond-the-initial-claim-of-office).

Qualification: The wisdom premise must be assessed rather than inferred merely from success. The reported alternatives are not all endorsed or exhaustively refuted in this passage.

## A36 General and detailed recognition of inimitability

main pp. 712–715; PDF 812–815. Source voice: Base creed followed by Ibn Taymiyya.

The Quran is presented as one among several prophetic signs. A general challenge-and-inability argument differs from detailed recognition of eloquence, reports and other features. Identifying one feature does not entitle a reader to make it the whole account.

Qualification: The failure-to-produce inference depends on the asserted motivation and opportunity. Its historical examples remain attributed reports.

## A37 Afterlife teaching and shared foundations

main pp. 716–725; PDF 816–825. Source voice: Base creed followed by Ibn Taymiyya.

The ending returns to belief in God, messengers and the last day, alongside truth, justice and worship. A rigid split between rational and transmitted knowledge is questioned. General acceptance differs from knowledge of authenticated particulars; bodily resurrection differs from survival of a soul alone.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [A01](#a01-the-creed-and-its-commentator), [A04](#a04-reason-testimony-and-differentiated-attributes), [A38](#a38-authority-beyond-the-initial-claim-of-office).

Qualification: The text distinguishes positions among opponents. It does not independently authenticate every particular afterlife narration by affirming the general foundation.

## A38 Authority beyond the initial claim of office

main pp. 725–728; PDF 825–828. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya.

Authenticating a messenger bears on the reports he conveys from the sender, not only the original claim of appointment. The conclusion therefore returns to the opening problem: broadly true creed statements can leave proof, meaning and detailed knowledge unfinished.

Qualification: The relevant scope is reporting from the sender; it does not make every ordinary practical opinion an authenticated revelation.

## The shape of the response

The response begins with remedy, prayer, conditions and self-deception, then develops consequences, habits, rights and repair. Its long penal discussion leads back to the question of an overwhelming attachment. The later analysis distinguishes competing loves, ordinary affection, active restraint and lasting goods.

The affirmative literary case for love is deliberately given to an interlocutor before the author replies. The reply differentiates objects and consequences, affirms lawful marital affection, and separates involuntary feeling from chosen conduct. The closing criticism of a report used by the interlocutor makes source attribution part of the argument’s resolution. Earlier severe formulations must be reread with these later distinctions, without erasing the source’s historical commitments.

## D01 An unnamed affliction and a request for help

main p. 3; PDF 76. Source voice: Anonymous questioner and transmitting introduction.

The opening asks how to escape an affliction that has persisted despite attempted remedies and threatens this-worldly and ultimate welfare. The response’s subsequent breadth should be read against this practical starting point.

Qualification: The opening does not name the affliction. At main p. 414 the author only tentatively proposes overwhelming attachment as its intended subject.

## D02 Remedy use receptivity and obstruction

main pp. 4–8; PDF 77–81. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with transmitted reports and personal testimony.

Remedy, agent, receptivity and obstruction receive distinct causal roles. The wounded traveller’s reported mistreatment makes unqualified advice itself a possible injury. Personal healing testimony differs from a general demonstration, and failure leaves several explanatory possibilities open.

Qualification: This historical therapeutic framework is not clinical evidence and does not authorise moral diagnosis of a person who remains unwell.

## D03 Mitigation persistence and abandonment

main pp. 9–15; PDF 82–88. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with devotional reports.

Prayer is presented as preventing, lifting or mitigating affliction, including resistance that falls short of elimination. Premature abandonment can obstruct a hoped-for result. The discussion also distinguishes a defective request from ineffective performance or another impediment.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D05](#d05-success-does-not-isolate-its-cause), [D09](#d09-worldly-outcomes-attention-and-hope), [D18](#d18-remembering-the-self-and-three-kinds-of-care).

Qualification: Persistence is not a warrant to repeat every unsuccessful method indefinitely; graded benefit must not be confused with guaranteed cure.

## D04 Exemplary supplication in its own setting

main pp. 16–25 opening; PDF 89–98 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim; transmitted prayers, stories and narrator comments.

Attention, timing, humility, repentance and appeal through divine names give the examples their devotional setting. The collection contains several speaking roles, including comments following reports, rather than one continuous prophetic voice.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [D05](#d05-success-does-not-isolate-its-cause), [D08](#d08-warning-narratives-and-interpersonal-rights).

Qualification: Retain reported narratives as narratives; the index does not independently establish particular formulas, numerical effects or transmission reliability.

## D05 Success does not isolate its cause

main pp. 25–26 opening; PDF 98–99 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim.

Copying a successful prayer’s wording can omit the need, intention, prior conduct or fitting occasion that accompanied it. An answer at a particular place does not by itself identify that place as the cause. This directly qualifies the preceding examples.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D02](#d02-remedy-use-receptivity-and-obstruction), [D04](#d04-exemplary-supplication-in-its-own-setting), [D06](#d06-decree-includes-means), [A27](#a27-speech-bearer-and-voluntary-exercise).

Qualification: The passage concerns accompanying conditions; it does not isolate which condition is sufficient in a particular successful case.

## D06 Decree includes means

main pp. 26–36 opening; PDF 99–109 opening. Source voice: Reported objections followed by Ibn al-Qayyim’s answer.

Decreed outcomes are considered together with their means. Prayer, food, cultivation and protective action have genuine roles within that order. The author rejects both abandoning action as futile and reducing means to causally idle signs.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D02](#d02-remedy-use-receptivity-and-obstruction), [D23](#d23-mediation-without-divine-deficiency), [A12](#a12-real-causes-with-conditions-and-impediments).

Qualification: The passage affirms dependent efficacy; it does not by itself settle every question about alternative complete histories or independent productive sources.

## D07 Presumption and graded repair

main pp. 36–50; 290; PDF 109–123; 363. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, separating rationalizations from replies.

Reassuring texts can be misused to excuse continued wrongdoing. Good expectation should shape conduct. Remedial acts can cooperate and differ in strength; the later grading of expiation qualifies any mechanical rule that one outward act always has the same effect.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D09](#d09-worldly-outcomes-attention-and-hope), [D15](#d15-restoration-can-return-above-or-below), [D21](#d21-degrees-of-wrongdoing-and-their-purposes).

Qualification: Cooperating expiatory means can strengthen a result without making either idle. This dependent contribution is a different hypothesis from two complete, independently sufficient original producers.

## D08 Warning narratives and interpersonal rights

main pp. 51–76; 87–98 opening; PDF 124–149; 160–171 opening. Source voice: Scriptural, prophetic, Companion and ascetic reports selected by Ibn al-Qayyim.

Warnings include sincerity, reputation, entrusted property, injury to others and animal cruelty, alongside afterlife scenes. Rescue and forgiveness remain within the sequence. Reported refusals to certify others also raise a distinct question about what a bounded answer implies.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [D25](#d25-distinct-rights-and-qualified-resemblance), [D28](#d28-speech-and-silence-each-have-risks).

Qualification: Afterlife imagery and reported devotional fear are not present-day instructions for violence or self-harm; quotation inside a report is not the author’s eyewitness testimony.

## D09 Worldly outcomes attention and hope

main pp. 77–98 opening; PDF 150–171 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with scriptural and reported examples.

Prosperity does not invariably mean honour, nor hardship humiliation. Knowledge varies in strength and present attention; habit and desire can impede its fuller consequences without erasing assent. Hope involves effort according to capacity and remains dependent on help and removal of impediments.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D03](#d03-mitigation-persistence-and-abandonment), [D10](#d10-sin-and-narrated-social-consequences), [D14](#d14-burdens-blessing-and-lived-time).

Qualification: Carry the prosperity-and-affliction qualification into later stronger harm language rather than diagnosing others from outcomes.

## D10 Sin and narrated social consequences

main pp. 98–132 opening; PDF 171–205 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim’s framing with scriptural history and many reported voices.

The work develops individual and communal harm through historical and admonitory narratives: complicity, governance, reputation, repeated minor breaches and delayed consequences. Its concern extends beyond isolated private conduct.

Status: Presently orthogonal. Links: [D09](#d09-worldly-outcomes-attention-and-hope), [D12](#d12-environmental-blessing-and-causal-language), [D19](#d19-historical-penalties-and-different-kinds-of-repair).

Qualification: These are theological causal interpretations, not verified diagnoses of disasters, political decline or particular victims; righteous people may share an affliction in the author’s account.

## D11 Habit disposition and diminished agency

main pp. 132–157 opening; 212–220 opening; PDF 205–230 opening; 285–293 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with poems and cautionary reports.

Repetition forms dispositions, may weaken intention to change, and can continue after enjoyment fades. The later crisis examples make sustained formation important before capacity is strained. Lost opportunities and meaningful time join the discussion of harm.

## D12 Environmental blessing and causal language

main pp. 157–163 opening; PDF 230–236 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, interpreting scripture and earlier reports.

The discussion extends to water, air, crops and habitation. It asks whether corruption names wrongdoing, its consequences or both, and places material abundance within a theological history of blessing.

## D13 Moral protectiveness genuine excuses and rank

main pp. 163–177; PDF 236–250. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim; reported interpretations clearly distinguished.

Moral protectiveness must be joined to genuine excuse, justice and mercy. Fierceness and laxity both need qualification. The subsequent discussion of diminished faith explicitly distinguishes a lost rank from automatic departure from the broader Muslim community.

## D14 Burdens blessing and lived time

main pp. 178–204; PDF 251–277. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim.

Incapacity and unwillingness, future worry and past grief, lawful debt and wrongful domination are distinguished. Benefit is assessed through use and blessing as well as quantity. Spiritual joy still allows pain; universal created dependence does not make every act approved.

## D15 Restoration can return above or below

main pp. 205–212 opening; PDF 278–285 opening. Source voice: Ibn Taymiyya’s reported judgement followed by Ibn al-Qayyim’s explanation.

Repentance may restore a former level, leave the person below it, or lead higher through changed humility and understanding. The response depends on the quality of return, not a simple arithmetic cancellation of a fall.

Qualification: A fault’s later constructive effect is a retrospective possibility, not a recommendation to commit it or a guaranteed gain.

## D16 Discerning well and acting well

main pp. 220–224; PDF 293–297. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim.

Insight and effective resolve are separate capacities. Someone can recognise the better course yet fail to act, or act vigorously under a mistaken appraisal. The work’s account of excellence requires both, with patience, certainty and mutual counsel.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D11](#d11-habit-disposition-and-diminished-agency), [D17](#d17-the-heart-and-its-available-supports), [D27](#d27-uninvited-thoughts-and-fitting-reflection).

Qualification: This is a normative classification, not a validated psychological score or a judgement of a named person.

## D17 The heart and its available supports

main pp. 225–243 opening; PDF 298–316 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, including extended personified adversarial speech.

Reason, knowledge, faith, attention, speech and bodily action receive differentiated roles. Harmful framing and social reinforcement can interfere with understanding as well as conduct. The dramatic account portrays resistance as requiring sustained support at several points.

Qualification: Instructions voiced by the adversary are material to resist, not the author’s advice. The military imagery is pedagogical, not a practical campaign plan.

## D18 Remembering the self and three kinds of care

main pp. 172–174; 243–258 opening; PDF 245–247; 316–331 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim.

Self-forgetting includes neglect of true interests, faults and conditions needing repair. Later medical analogy coordinates nourishment, removal of harm and prevention of recurrence. Care therefore includes positive cultivation as well as abstention.

Status: Relevant. Links: [D02](#d02-remedy-use-receptivity-and-obstruction), [D03](#d03-mitigation-persistence-and-abandonment), [D17](#d17-the-heart-and-its-available-supports).

Qualification: This expands the earlier gloss of self-forgetting; it is not autobiographical amnesia or a modern instruction to purge the body.

## D19 Historical penalties and different kinds of repair

main pp. 258–267 opening; PDF 331–340 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with reported juristic differences.

The work turns from inward consequences to historical legal deterrents. Its distinctions include punishment, expiation and reparation, including repair without culpable wrongdoing. Harm, restraint and competing interests figure in its rationales.

## D20 Protection guidance and the limits of metaphor

main pp. 268–286; PDF 341–359. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with scriptural interpretations and reports.

Protection from doing wrong differs from protection from its consequences. Guidance includes understanding, capacity, will, action and persistence. A character analogy later expands into a literal transformation claim, so the earlier image cannot safely be classified as exclusively metaphorical.

## D21 Degrees of wrongdoing and their purposes

main pp. 287–296 opening; PDF 360–369 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, distinguishing opposing criteria from his resolution.

Omission and commission, inward and outward conduct, and divine and creaturely claims organise the taxonomy. Differing degrees are ultimately related to the purposes of worship and justice; their shared description as disobedience does not erase differences in harm.

## D22 Creator acknowledgement and exclusive worship

main pp. 296–318 opening; PDF 369–391 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with reported objections and confessional classifications.

Affirming a creator is distinguished from directing devotion rightly. The extended reply to intermediary worship appeals to intrinsic sufficiency, perfection and the dependence of creatures. Intention and conformity both enter appraisal of worship.

## D23 Mediation without divine deficiency

The author rejects treating divine access like a deficient ruler’s need for informants, helpers or pressure. Yet instituted dependent means remain. He also rejects both acts outside divine power and total compulsion that denies the servant any genuine capacity or influence.

## D26 Four entrances into conduct

A thought’s arrival is distinguished from deliberately entertaining it. Useful reflection includes benefits, harms, present duties and difficult priorities. ## D32 Ultimate love and ordinary affection

main pp. 424–446; PDF 497–519. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with reports and poetry.

Exclusive ultimate allegiance does not exclude love for God’s sake, authorised intercession or ordinary affection. The taxonomy explicitly sets natural love of spouse, child, food and rest outside its earlier theological division; censure concerns their obstructive role, not their mere presence.

## D33 Active restraint and immediate or lasting goods

main pp. 447–463; PDF 520–536. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim.

Not acting because no motive arises differs from positively restraining an action. Immediate pleasure or pain also differs from the value of its eventual result. Fitting lesser loves and lawful worldly goods remain within the account, while inward benefit is not identified with outward prosperity.

## D34 Love cosmic motion and good order

main pp. 464–482; PDF 537–555. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with a separately attributed interpretation from his teacher.

Love is extended into a premodern account of motion and appointed angelic mediation. The unity discussion explicitly distinguishes good order from mere existence. The teacher’s interpretation of seeking a way to God concerns approach and obedience, not conquest; it is distinct from the preceding rivalry argument.

## D35 Competing pressures and the rights of others

main p. 475; pp. 482–507; PDF 548; 555–580. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim, with scriptural interpretation, poetry and cautionary stories.

Joseph’s trial joins attraction, pressure and coercion with the need for help. Later discussion warns against damaged reputations, manipulated hope and corrupted loyalties. Repentance does not erase another person’s claim. The earlier main p. 475 distinguishes agents’ direct deeds from consequences generated through them, while allowing moral credit through those consequences.

## D37 The reply distinguishes object conduct and pleasure

main pp. 532–552 opening; PDF 605–625 opening. Source voice: Ibn al-Qayyim replying to the interlocutor.

What occurs differs from what is permissible; love receives neither global praise nor global blame. Evaluation depends on its object and consequences. Pleasure can support lasting good, obstruct it, or remain merely permissible without the greater benefit. The third category prevents lack of lasting benefit becoming prohibition.
