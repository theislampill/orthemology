# A fair standard of philosophical appraisal

The inquiry asks what follows from a Necessary Being and the stronger description of a complete original Creator. Once a premise is granted for that question, the analysis must retain it. Replacing it repeatedly with a bare correction episode changes the subject. Conversely, a necessary participant, an original producer of one contribution and a complete original Creator are different descriptions. The latter's scope cannot be recovered merely by repeating the word necessary.

The appropriate standard gives a substantive rational principle the opportunity to warrant belief. A principle need not first be derived from a vocabulary neutral between every metaphysical school. The sources themselves distinguish actual knowledge, the argument a person can articulate, and the route needed to answer a particular opponent. Asfahani printed pp. 184–186 / PDF pp. 191–193 rejects a single compulsory route or initial doubt. El-Tobgui's thesis, printed pp. 281–282 / PDF pp. 305–306, similarly preserves ordinary empirical and rational knowledge without prior testimonial certification.

This is not immunity for alleged intuition. Asfahani printed pp. 78–82 / PDF pp. 85–89 asks whether a purported universal has intelligible grounding in relevant particulars and supporting judgements. A felt certainty can be mistaken. Appraisal should explain what is recognised, why it bears on the conclusion, and what a relevant objection challenges. Simply calling a judgement basic neither establishes nor defeats it.

A contrary diagram has a similarly limited role. If it satisfies explicitly stated formal premises while lacking the proposed conclusion, it may reveal a deductive gap. It does not by itself establish a metaphysically possible reality, equal credibility, or an actual defeater of stronger background reasons. The affirmative account must not inflate a philosophical rationale into a logical theorem; the critic must not inflate formal underdetermination into total absence of warrant.

The report therefore distinguishes three assessments. Historical attribution concerns what the supplied author or translator says. Philosophical appraisal concerns whether its reasons support the conclusion. Actual application concerns whether the relevant bearer, source relation, event or communicative token has been identified. Agreement at one level does not settle the others.

This standard permits determinate positive judgements alongside remaining disagreement. Ordinary testimonial belief may be warranted without a uniquely correct theory of testimony. A local wrong may be recognised without a complete metaethic. A conditional attribute conclusion may be established even while an upstream existence argument remains disputed. Each result receives the credit its own premises and evidence support.

# Contingency and intrinsic necessity

Asfahani's existential argument has a stronger content than the suggestion that an explanation would be desirable. Printed pp. 22–27 / PDF pp. 29–34 distinguish actual origination, existential contingency, dependence and sufficiency. Actual animals, plants and minerals provide concrete cases: they exist, so they are not impossible; they begin, so they are not intrinsically necessary. The author regards an originated thing's need for an originator as clearer than a longer modal detour.

The general principle is not an induction from those temporal beginnings. Printed pp. 25–26 / PDF pp. 32–33 treat a whole reality's openness to existence and nonexistence as a lack of existential sufficiency through itself. If actual existence is not supplied through another, the reality must instead possess sufficiency through itself. This concerns a mode of existence. It does not posit a nonexistent subject manufacturing itself, nor a detachable abstract essence acting before its instantiation.

The affirmative rationale asks us to distinguish an actual bearer from an inventory of powers conditional on that bearer already existing. Describing what something would do if instantiated does not supply the actuality presupposed by the description. If its whole reality remains genuinely existentially open, the account judges that something remains to be determined. Complete intrinsic sufficiency is the opposed positive mode, in which that residual existential need is absent.

I accept this limited contingency-to-need principle as a defensible rational starting point. Its strength is that it concerns the whole reality, applies uniformly rather than exempting a favoured deity, and distinguishes existential adequacy from an exercise of an already possessed power. It does not say that every fact has an efficient cause, that every necessary source must have an external producer, or that every particular act is necessary.

Under this actual bearer’s established or granted complete whole-existence nonderivation, the resulting inference is affirmative. The accepted principle excludes genuine existential contingency; actuality excludes impossibility; the absence of received existential supply makes the necessity intrinsic. The conclusion is therefore necessity from nonderivation together with a rationally warranted metaphysical principle. It is not necessity from nonderivation by modal logic alone.

The completion argument at printed pp. 26–27 / PDF pp. 33–34 adds that an allegedly complete cause cannot leave required productive provision outside its account. A collection of received existences does not automatically become sufficient merely by being collected. This is a substantive claim about original provision. It should not be misdescribed as a rejection of every infinite temporal sequence: printed p. 70 / PDF p. 77 and pp. 107–109 / PDF pp. 114–116 distinguish agents from successive acts.

# The existential disagreement and what actually changed

The independent challenge grants substantial merits to the stronger argument. It targets an actual whole, takes existential standing seriously, avoids a deity-specific exemption and rejects circular self-origination. Its dissent is narrower: absence of intrinsic necessitation is not yet, in its judgement, sufficient reason to conclude that actual whole existence needs a determinant. The claim that a nature does not necessitate existence and the claim that its existence is actually received remain conceptually different.

That objection succeeds against two weaker arguments. An impossibility of self-production does not refute every brute-actuality proposal, since such a proposal need not claim that nothing produces something. Nor may the analyst import a temporal transition, a pre-existing bearer of potentiality or an observed productive environment into a beginningless original reality merely because its absence can be considered. The positive appraisal does not rely on either move.

The remaining affirmative position is direct acceptance of contingency as existential need, clarified by the contrast between conditional powers and whole actuality. I judge that connection sufficiently rationally supported for defeasible belief. The critical reader finds the connection intelligible and weighty but not sufficiently established by the reasons articulated. There is no further neutral derivation here that makes those verdicts unanimous.

The disagreement is neither a proof that the principle is false nor a demonstration of a possible contingent ultimate. It also does not require the critic to produce a complete alternative cosmology before objecting. The precise issue is the sufficiency of the positive rational connection. Reporting only favourable passages would conceal the disagreement; reporting only a logical gap would suppress the affirmative basis of the judgement.

The historical framing needs equal precision. Sixth section 2 already contained and evaluated the stronger source claim, including positive intrinsic sufficiency and noninferential recognition of need and independence. Sixth section 7 used a narrower explanatory-appropriateness reconstruction together with an additional explanatory-exhaustiveness commitment whose warrant it separately questioned. The present work re-evaluates the stronger material in fuller context. It does not discover a wholly missing premise or correct an earlier programme that knew only the weaker formulation.

Several application questions remain separate. Actual original completion, the same bearer's identity throughout the inference, exhaustive scope and the route from a particular correction are not supplied by this judgement alone. But when the Necessary Creator is expressly granted, those upstream questions do not license withholding its conditional consequences. The attribute inquiry below proceeds with that grant intact.

# Actual supply and original existential completion

Actual received activity warrants actual productive suppliers with real efficacy. This can be known without recovering an exhaustive causal history. Asfahani printed pp. 22–27 / PDF pp. 29–34 and Oneness printed pp. 17–22 / PDF pp. 24–29 give that judgement positive content. A receiver is not powerless merely because its possession of power is received.

The stronger original-completion principle addresses the complete existential provision of the actual received field, including the actuality through which its productive resources act. An exhaustive account cannot discharge that target solely by presupposing the same received actuality throughout. Passing to another receiver supplies a real local explanation without changing its received manner of possession. Original actuality provides a positively different existential basis, not merely a shorter causal chain.

I judge this principle sufficiently supported for defeasible rational belief. It preserves the whole-existence target, distinguishes intermediate contribution from original provision, and gives a non-presupposing mode of adequacy real explanatory work. Infinitely many subordinate effects or relations remain compatible with original completion. The judgement is not a theorem from local causality or a demand that every fact have a necessitating explanation. Sixth already contained the stronger rationale; its present appraisal is more favourable, not historically new.

The initial completion review withheld this verdict because a supplied dependent remains dependent, and an active infinitary proposal gives real local suppliers throughout. A subsequent challenge exposed the objection’s insufficient force at whole-field scope. Continuing receivedness concerns beneficiaries after supply; the affirmative never requires them to become original. It does not show that their exhaustive existential account can rely solely on received actuality throughout. The original reviewer accepted that correction. The negative assessment is preserved as superseded, rather than portrayed as continuing dissent or silent initial agreement.

The explanatory type remains a separate question. A fixed plurality’s joint existence can be contingent if one of those very beings could be absent; all need not be absent together, and no new aggregate is required. But contingency plus generic sufficient reason for facts does not alone identify a concrete efficient cause. If reception includes all existential derivation, a genuinely autonomous noncausal ground counts as original in that broad respect. Identifying such an ultimate resource with an efficient Creator needs a further bridge. Restricting receipt to efficient production cannot silently yield complete ontological nonderivation.

Under the additional productive-resource and bearer ontology, real original efficacy requires actual concrete reality possessing or constituting it. Yet joint completion does not itself identify one individually complete common bearer. A plurality, a real holistic source and one universally complete individual remain distinct. These distinctions establish neither a rival’s metaphysical possibility nor equal credibility.

The minimal actual-witness question must now be distinguished from common coverage. Under the adopted whole-existence and qualified-bearer commitments, it is already warranted; it does not await a name or unique identification. Originality of one local power alone remains insufficient. The next section states the resource-to-bearer step and resulting Necessary Being conclusion. Where the complete Necessary Creator is granted, its downstream consequences also remain available immediately.

# The actual witness and the Necessary Being conclusion

The accepted completion argument supplies original existential actuality relevant to the actual receiving target. Under the adopted concrete productive/bearer account, this warrants some actual particular whole-existence original bearer. An existential witness need not be named, uniquely identified or individually complete for every contribution. A plural original basis therefore leaves common coverage unsettled without keeping every actual original witness hypothetical.

Two steps need separate reasons. First, a real productive resource has an actual bearer. Second, some such bearer is original as to its whole existential actuality. The first does not alone entail the second: a received organism has genuine intrinsic powers and can initiate a new exercise. The required additional principle concerns the concrete instantiation of the original resource, not merely who performs a local act. The bearer commitment applies to that original resource itself, not only to a downstream effect.

If a bearer’s being actual at all is existentially received, the concrete instantiation of its intrinsic power is not thereby an originally existing resource independent of that receipt. Its efficacy may be real, essential and locally sufficient, while its existential possession remains received. Asfahani printed p. 34 and pp. 80–82 / PDF p. 41 and pp. 87–89 supplies the qualified-bearer rationale; Oneness printed pp. 19 and 34–35 / PDF pp. 26 and 41–42 concerns actual possessed efficacy. Sixth already articulated the received-active-power distinction.

This transport concerns genuine ontological support, not mere modal co-occurrence. It does not infer an efficient-production edge from bearer to attribute. The power need not be a separate object manufactured by its bearer. The point is that the bearer’s received actuality cannot be a required condition of the same intrinsic resource’s instantiation and then be omitted when declaring that instantiation wholly original. Under the accepted exhaustive whole-existence target, that principle has sufficient rational warrant.

If some members of a plural basis possess original whole-existence actuality, one such actual member supplies the witness. If a genuine original holistic bearer supplies derivative members, that whole supplies it without making every member original. A proposal assigning originality only to a relational or noncausal explanation, while every concrete bearer is derivative, changes the adopted realisation commitments; it is not ordinary plurality preserving them. This does not refute every alternative ontology or turn a noncausal ground into a material body.

Fix the actual original bearer so secured. The accepted contingency-as-need principle says that genuine openness to complete nonexistence entails existential need. If this same bearer were contingent, its whole existence would be received, contrary to the established originality in that respect. Actuality excludes impossibility. The conclusion is therefore some actual Necessary Being, with necessity attached to the same witness throughout.

This is a real philosophical entailment under the adopted rationally warranted principles, rather than an unconditional theorem from local causal axioms or generic fact-PSR. By itself it does not prove essential nonreceipt, necessary particular acts or a necessary whole history. The next section earns the first of those further conclusions through an additional constitutive judgement. One individually complete common Creator remains a stronger result; that separate question must not erase the actual Necessary Being conclusion already earned.

# Essential independence of that same bearer

Necessary existence and actual nonreceipt are different claims. A reality might be described as necessarily supplied; actual originality does not alone exclude a different existential explanation elsewhere. The stronger source account supplies an additional reason: whole-existence reception or self-sufficiency concerns the constitution of the particular reality itself. I accept that constitutive judgement as sufficiently warranted for defeasible belief. With it, the same actual Necessary Being is essentially independent in its whole existential actuality.

Asfahani printed pp. 25–27 / PDF pp. 32–34 gives positive existential sufficiency through the reality itself, rather than absence of an observed supplier. Printed pp. 30–34 / PDF pp. 37–41 allows real intrinsic qualifications while rejecting an externally assembled, attribute-free substrate. El-Tobgui, printed pp. 266–268 and 299–301 / PDF pp. 290–292 and 323–325, explains the particular reality and its mode of existence together. These passages support the interpretation; the exact inference across alternatives is an analytical reconstruction, not a quotation of a modern modal formula from the source.

The positive rationale concerns what makes the bearer available for any exercise or alteration. A recipient can change suppliers without ceasing to be a recipient. But replacing whole-existence receipt with sufficiency through the particular reality itself changes the source of its existential adequacy. There is no already actual, existentially neutral bearer to which either manner of having actuality is simply added. That explanatory role gives a reason to treat this difference as constitutive, rather than as another contingent manifestation.

The strongest objection asks why the very same individual could not exist through itself here and through another elsewhere. Actual inseparability alone does not answer it. Neither does the word intrinsic: a currently intrinsic configuration might be contingent. Asfahani printed pp. 83–84 / PDF pp. 90–91 itself distinguishes standing qualification from conditioned manifestations. The affirmative therefore needs the further judgement that the source of whole existential adequacy belongs to this reality's constitution. I judge its explanatory rationale sufficient; I do not claim every identity theory has thereby been refuted.

The transport is general and applies in the required direction. If this same bearer were wholly existentially received elsewhere, the constitutive account would make it a received reality wherever it exists, including actually. That contradicts its established actual originality. The result is essential nonreceipt together with the already warranted necessity of that same bearer. Merely asserting that it cannot receive elsewhere would repeat the conclusion without giving this rationale.

This does not make every actual predicate essential or identify all dependence with efficient production. It permits externally related activities and renewed acts, and does not establish numerical uniqueness, complete actual creatorhood or one necessary history. An alleged redundant supplier either contributes genuine existential dependence, which conflicts with autonomy, or falls outside the relevant receipt relation. Sixth already owned the essential-reception distinction and conditional inference. The increment is the favourable appraisal of its constitutive premise applied to the now-earned actual witness.

# Real attributes and unrestricted standing power

Actual efficacy directly warrants real power. Arabic Asfahani printed pp. 399–402 and 407–408 / PDF pp. 499–502 and 507–508 argues from actual production even before volition is settled. Mediated production does not erase secondary powers. For an original producer, the efficacy presupposed by its product cannot be borrowed from that product. Oneness printed pp. 20–21 / PDF pp. 27–28 develops this; printed p. 25 / PDF p. 32 adds the continuing possession of an essential attribute while its necessary bearer exists.

Asfahani printed pp. 29–37 / PDF pp. 36–44 rejects an inference from inseparable qualification to externally assembled parts. Knowledge and power need not be separate manufactured things. Printed pp. 39–40 / PDF pp. 46–47 gives distinct arguments for knowledge through willing, proficient production, perfection and original giving. The willing route presupposes volition; the others retain their own force. Given the same original bearer's defended perfection, these are substantial grounds for knowledge and informed agency.

The fuller corpus contains two separate perfection arguments. Asfahani printed pp. 109–111 / PDF pp. 116–118 explicitly extends pure perfection beyond excellences actually instantiated in creatures. Printed pp. 127–130 / PDF pp. 134–137 joins the superiority of original necessary reality with the original giver's priority over its recipient. Suleiman, printed pp. 237–243 / PDF pp. 250–256, identifies genuine possibility and absence of deficiency as eligibility conditions. Reducing this whole case to the abilities already manifested in actual effects understates it.

I accept its broader standing-power conclusion at the same defeasible rational standard. An additional genuinely feasible ability is a positive gain in agency where it requires no need, contradiction, borrowed resource or impairment. Wise possession does not require indiscriminate exercise. Original actual excellence grounds the comparison, while the separate pure-perfection judgement extends it beyond actual gifts. The resulting power ranges over genuinely producible contingent effects. It is not power to make contradictions true, annihilate an intrinsically necessary reality or realise incompatible complete histories together.

The strongest objection observes that a nature could be described as intrinsically limited without an external limiter. Independence alone therefore does not prove unlimited power. I accept that distinction but find the additional comparative perfection rationale sufficient. A real cost or incompatibility would defeat an alleged eligibility; merely redescribing original agency as limited does not outweigh the positive argument. The cumulative unity reviewer remains less persuaded by this ascent but also tests unity while granting its conclusion. The report's affirmative judgement is therefore neither unanimous nor a deduction from the word necessary.

Standing ability remains distinct from its object and exercise, as Asfahani printed pp. 109–111 expressly says. Unrestricted power does not establish exclusive actual production of every effect. The local volition wording at English Asfahani p. 44 / PDF p. 51 is corrected by Arabic printed p. 452 / PDF p. 552, with its apparatus recording the contrary older printed reading: the passage excludes specification by an essence lacking will. It does not teach divine efficient self-creation. The broader attribute reasoning was already present in Sixth; this is a stronger appraisal and more accurate scope, not a new theorem.

# The cumulative case for one common Creator

The supplied unity argument is substantially richer than the familiar conflicting-wills dilemma. The renewed reading considered all 55 Oneness pages, including its diagrams, alongside the other five books' relevant passages and the now-accepted original-completion and Necessary Being results. Its affirmative case joins original efficacy, essential independence, pure perfection, actual productive interconnection, shared ends and explanatory economy. It should not be answered merely by repeating that the isolated predicate necessary does not entail all attributes.

Asfahani printed pp. 109–111 / PDF pp. 116–118 supplies the separate all-pure-perfections route. Al-Ujayri printed pp. 121–125 and 195–199 / PDF pp. 125–129 and 199–203 gives integrated order and explanatory economy positive roles. The epistemology collection and Asfahani printed pp. 184–186 / PDF pp. 191–193 also allow warranted recognition through convergent signs without one compulsory syllogistic sequence. A description of that route does not establish that this assessor has independently realised it, and withholding one argumentative verdict does not remove another person's adequately grounded recognition.

The main Oneness sequence, English printed pp. 17–40 / PDF pp. 24–47, corresponds to Arabic printed pp. 134–152 / PDF pp. 234–252. It first secures actual power, rejects mutual original donation of powerless agents' abilities, then excludes two complete exclusive productions of the same contribution. It considers agreement as well as opposition, unequal powers, distinct effects, mixing and cooperation. The central transitions are present in Arabic. They are not translator-invented logic.

The strongest positive principle is sound: another agent cannot complete the very efficacy or unchanged determination already claimed to be intrinsically complete in the same respect. If one bearer exhaustively originates a whole field and its history, another independent original contributor inside that field is excluded. The philosophical question is whether the cumulative case earns that exclusive actual scope, rather than whether the strong conditional has consequences.

The coalescence argument at English pp. 29–33 / PDF pp. 36–40 and Arabic pp. 143–147 / PDF pp. 243–247 treats required mixing as mutual need and optional mixing as alteration of another source's power or will. Actual connectedness is good evidence against isolated realms. Yet dependence of a combined result on contributions is not itself received possession of the contributors' standing powers. An effect's intended transition or consented interaction need not defeat a complete will whose specified content already includes it. Completeness secures that content; it does not silently enlarge it to exclusive origination of every related event.

This objection survives granting the broader standing-power conclusion accepted above. Ability need not be exercised in every contribution. The source also distinguishes possible power from wisdom-compatible willing: English p. 28's execution wording should be contrary willing, as Arabic p. 142 shows; Arabic p. 408 directly explains the wisdom qualification. English p. 31 paraphrases an editorially proposed effective-power correction, and p. 37 overstates asymmetric superiority as reciprocal overpowering. Those bounded textual repairs improve accuracy without deciding unity. The fuller primary reply on cooperation must now be considered before the cumulative verdict.

# Cooperation and foreknowledge in the unity argument

The primary Minhaj reply behind Oneness footnotes 19 and 32 has now been directly recovered in electronic Arabic text. It expressly addresses common benefit, foreknowledge and partial productive circles. The supplied notes cite Minhaj 3:326–328 and 3:321–322; the recovered bodies verify the argument's presence and context, not the exact edition-specific printed-page mapping. The electronic subdivisions are not verified volume numbers. No new manuscript or print-edition collation is claimed.

The reply rules out a genuinely empty coordination explanation. If each will needs the other's decision in order to supply its own original activation, simultaneous timing does not remove the productive circle. Nor does partial rather than total dependence remove it. A future coordinated benefit is their product, not an independently supplied productive determinant of the wills that originate it. Merely saying that each foreknows agreement cannot explain the actuality whose truth that knowledge already presupposes. These are substantive answers, not an absence of engagement with cooperation.

The phrase that knowledge follows its object need not mean that a future event efficiently produces knowledge. Its relevant force is that factive awareness cannot supply an independent explanation of the fact it already assumes. The text then expressly adds the stronger claim that each will contributes to what makes the other willing. That productive-conditioning claim must be assessed separately from truth-directed knowing, relational content and necessary concomitance.

An intended good can be understood before it exists as an external product. The source's own wise-agency account permits an intrinsic determination through knowledge of feasible ends and reasons, with foreknowledge tracking the outcome so determined. A proposed plurality would still need an adequate account of matching reasons and actual coordination; the mere word wisdom does not provide one. But shared content or agreement does not yet show that each will efficiently or constitutively supplies the other. Likewise, an intrinsically complete will can include interaction without being completed by an external donor.

Asfahani printed pp. 31–32 / PDF pp. 38–39 distinguishes necessary concomitance from productive receipt. Its immediate concern is qualification of one bearer, so it does not prove that plural original bearers could coordinate. It does prevent a universal inference from concurrence to received efficacy from being treated as already established. The Minhaj argument is strongest where real productive conditioning is independently shown. Its application to every alleged wise concord remains the disputed step.

After considering the fuller reply, I retain a narrower adverse sufficiency judgement on one individually complete actual common Creator. Unified sourcehood has genuine explanatory advantages: one adequate original agent accounts for connected order without additional original bearers and a further coordination account. Yet the offered elimination of coaction still identifies compatible interaction with received or altered constitutive efficacy. The source's own distinctions undercut that identification. I do not find the remaining cumulative reasons sufficient to settle exclusive actual whole-field sourcehood at the same defeasible standard used earlier.

This does not show a plural ultimate possible, equally credible, or actually present. It preserves the earned actual Necessary Being, essential independence, eligible standing power and the conditional exclusion of another contributor under genuinely complete actual source scope. The main unity argument and the new cooperation reply share a disputed independence step; repeating it does not create two independent confirmations. Conversely, their real positive force and the primary reply's successful exclusions must remain in the account.

# Wisdom and the positive case for truthful assertion

The longer Arabic discussion gives substantive support to practical perfection. Asfahani printed pp. 411–419 / PDF pp. 511–519 answers the objection that pursuing an end necessarily betrays prior deficiency. A fitting act need not occur before its appropriate occasion; realising one's own loved ends through one's own agency differs from receiving constitutive improvement from an independent benefactor. Informed ability to realise fitting ends is superior to incapacity or practical indifference. Successive realised goods need not postpone fulfilment forever.

Suleiman printed pp. 284–289 / PDF pp. 297–302 connects this with justice and essential wisdom. The positive judgement concerns substantive practical excellence, not approval of whatever a source happens to do. Arabic pp. 403–405 and 408 / PDF pp. 503–505 and 508 answers the necessity-versus-choice objection: wise willing can be necessary and still voluntary through the source's own knowledge, reasons and will. Neither this result nor the practical-perfection appraisal determines a unique complete history.

The veracity argument concerns an additional eligible excellence: faithful informed declaration. A genuine assertion offers a determinate content as the speaker's own account of reality. A knowingly false assertion remains an assertion and can preserve full knowledge. Its defect lies in the source's deliberate counterfeit of its own truth-directed presentation, not necessarily in ignorance, lost expressive capacity or a listener's actual error. Silence leaves a truth unsaid; false assertion exercises the declarative power contrary to that undertaking.

The retained al-Nubuwwat electronic subsection 9 and Akmaliyya loci already connect truthfulness with wisdom and perfection. A newly inspected Minhaj argument supplies more specific support. In the electronic opening and continuation identified in the source register, Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes qualities created in other bearers from qualities inhering in the source itself. He then applies the perfection argument to the source's own speech. These are his replies, not al-Razi's objections. The nonvolitional branch links truth with knowledge; the voluntary branch needs the distinct practical-perfection argument and cannot borrow that identification without explanation.

I now judge the cumulative case sufficient for defeasible acceptance of exceptionless veracity in genuinely source-owned assertions. Faithful informed expression is a positive excellence that does not depend on ignorance, bodily need, borrowed cognition or external sanction. Deliberately counterfeiting it is a defect in that very exercise. The original-perfection comparison gives substantive reason to preserve this integrity as a constraint on fitting purposes, rather than treating an internal declarative deficiency as purchasable by any sufficiently valuable external result. The next section states the continuing objection and why my earlier adverse appraisal changes.

This direct route requires the same bearer's defended essential whole-existence independence, perfect knowledge, substantive practical perfection and eligible pure-perfection standing, together with genuine ownership of the assertion. It does not additionally require numerical uniqueness or exclusive actual creation of the whole field. Minimal necessary existence alone does not supply that attribute package, and attributes cannot be transferred from an unrelated possible being. Unrestricted eligible standing power reinforces the source-specific comparison without proving that every desirable end has a truthful substitute.

# The remaining veracity disagreement and the revised judgement

The strongest prior objection had real force. Arabic Asfahani printed pp. 422–423 / PDF pp. 522–523 explains why omnipotence does not make all means dispensable: obtaining one endpoint directly may lose another party's good or a good borne by the process itself. Printed pp. 430–431 / PDF pp. 530–531 extends greater-good and lesser-harm comparison to creation. Suleiman printed pp. 286–287 / PDF pp. 299–300 provides related exposition. Independence does not abolish every evaluatively relevant relation among ends.

The newly recovered Minhaj reply nevertheless blocks a direct transfer from created relative evil to defects of the source's own declaration. Creating another person's ignorance does not make the source ignorant; producing another person's false speech does not make it the source's assertion. A contrary in its own informed declaration has a different locus. The earlier affirmative account already owned this distinction. The new primary argument materially grounds it and shows why general means–end considerations do not themselves defeat the source-specific integrity rationale.

The critical reviewer accepts that correction and continues to withhold sufficient warrant. A knowing liar can retain cognition, expressive power and control; blindness and muteness are therefore imperfect analogies. The reviewer also invokes a finite defender's justified false assertion to save an innocent person. Its content remains false and its truth-directed presentation remains counterfeit, yet the complete action can be justified. Ownership and inherence alone therefore do not settle exceptionless purity. This remains substantive dissent, not a demand for an explicit source sentence or unanimous agreement.

I now accept the affirmative response. Practical perfection concerns fitting exercises as well as faculties, so a contrary exercise need not remove a capacity to be internally deficient. The finite rescue case shows that an agent can choose the better available course while retaining a respect-specific shortcoming imposed by its situation. It does not establish the same shortcoming as compatible with unqualified original perfection. Logical and practical relations survive independence, but no source-specific rationale here makes this bearer's own counterfeit declaration their fitting necessary condition. The positive integrity comparison outweighs that unspecified further-good proposal at the stated defeasible standard.

This is a revision of my earlier withholding on the full argument. The earlier assessment and the continuing critic's refined objection are preserved. The new conclusion is not inferred merely from historical attribution, speech inherence or an opponent's failure to produce an actual lie. Its basis is the cumulative positive account of informed declarative excellence and original practical perfection. No neutral theorem, unanimity, or proof that every conceivable end admits a truthful replacement is claimed.

The Arabic Asfahani truth discussion, printed pp. 444–450 / PDF pp. 544–550, remains more limited: it contains the Mu'tazilite argument reported by al-Razi, al-Razi's criticism and Ibn Taymiyya's reply from p. 448. Its controlled comparison supports truth's noninstrumental worth; the new Minhaj speech argument must not be retroactively put into every voice there.

Defeasibility qualifies our warrant, not the conclusion’s scope: if the stated premises hold, contrary owned assertions are excluded, not merely unlikely. Genuinely asserted contextual content and a false assertion called a test remain within scope. Quotation, fiction, questions, commands and caused sounds are not automatically assertions of their represented content. No blanket permission for nonassertoric misleading follows. Actual occurrence, attribution, content, recipient competence and practical authority still require their own warrant. No revelation or messenger has been newly authenticated, and necessary standing integrity does not fix the whole cosmic history.

# Objective fittingness and the scope of authority

Given an actual Creator with substantive essential wisdom, the source account explains objective creaturely fittingness. Real natures, powers, relations, benefits and impairments are originated and ordered through fitting purposes. Cognitive capacities can give access to those goods. Relationality is not subjectivism: what damages a creature can depend on its nature and circumstances without depending on an observer’s preference.

A wider wise purpose need not erase local harm. A condition may genuinely impair a creature while being permitted for another good. Arabic Asfahani printed pp. 419–420 / PDF pp. 519–520 also distinguishes creative willing from love and command. Occurrence alone does not establish approval or permission; noncompliance does not itself defeat a command’s governing purpose. These distinctions make the account informative about actual relations rather than approving every event under an unspecified redescription.

Asfahani printed p. 226 / PDF p. 233 endorses a combined account of value and command. A deed can have value in its own features, a command can disclose it, and commanding can introduce a further wise practical relation. The newly read Arabic p. 429 / PDF p. 529 grounds freedom from rightful reproach in complete knowledge, wisdom, mercy and justice rather than merely domination, will and power. It directly reinforces the positive case for rightful governance beyond superior force.

The account can support determinate local norms when the creature, respect, circumstances and evidence are identified. A warranted correction need not await an exhaustive unique metaethic. Not every local benefit creates a duty for every agent, however. A claim of divine direction does not authenticate its speaker, and permission to believe a proposition does not establish authority to govern another person or implement it.

Al-Ujayri distinguishes goodness without belief in God from goodness existing without God at printed pp. 25–45 / PDF pp. 29–49. His converse inference from objective norms to an absolute personal Maker remains underargued in the inspected passages. Objective correctness and a concrete necessary producer do not become identical because both are called absolute. This limitation leaves the constructive forward account under the wise-Creator grant intact.

The focused authority appraisal develops an inherited command-dependent distinction by bringing these supplied arguments together. Oneness printed pp. 7–8 and 11–16 / PDF pp. 14–15 and 18–23 joins sourcehood and beneficence to fitting response; Asfahani printed pp. 218 and 221–228 / PDF pp. 225 and 228–235 makes wisdom govern direction. Answering this actual wise giver can constitute acknowledgement and cooperation beyond an act’s independent merit or the receipt of accurate advice.

A binding local conclusion assumes actual rightful obligatory force and adequate warrant for truthful practical communication in this relation. Neither follows from imperative grammar, alleged authority or generic wisdom. Once those facts are warranted, the recipient can be warranted to obey without reconstructing every underlying purpose. A command may specify an owed response; causal ownership or benefaction alone does not establish unlimited lawmaking power. The affirmative source-veracity appraisal can supply its integrity premise when its same-bearer conditions are warranted. It does not establish this token’s authorship, obligatory force, addressee or content by itself.

# Necessary wisdom and voluntary determination

The completed modal review gives a definite result under the grant of the Necessary Creator and essential wisdom. Arabic Asfahani printed pp. 403–405 / PDF pp. 503–505 expressly defends necessitation through the source’s own will and power. The source acts through knowledge, reasons and determinate willing; a completed determination necessitates its effect. Perfect knowledge of the best manner of an intended act excludes willing an inferior manner. Neither the word voluntary nor the interposition of will makes a fully fixed determination compatible with its contrary outcome.

Oneness printed p. 34 / PDF p. 41 distinguishes a source possessing will and power from an essence divested of them. Complete willing and power secure the effect. An unwilled object may remain possible in itself and within power although its occurrence is excluded by the absent will. Asfahani printed pp. 107–109 / PDF pp. 114–116 likewise answers the temporal problem through successive voluntary acts rather than leaving a complete determinant compatible with either result.

Five claims should be kept separate. The bearer may exist necessarily. A standing attribute may be essential to that bearer. An object may be possible in itself. Its particular occurrence may be necessary through a completed determination. The entire concrete history may or may not be uniquely fixed by the whole necessary intrinsic nature. None of these sentences is a harmless substitute for the others.

The distinction is especially important for power to do otherwise. Asfahani printed pp. 226–227 / PDF pp. 233–234 treats certain unjust deeds as objects of power while excluding their actual performance. Arabic Asfahani printed p. 408 / PDF p. 508 now states the narrower structure directly: an alternative can be within power and possible considered in itself, yet unwilled because its occurrence would forfeit wisdom incompatible with it. This is explicit primary evidence, not merely a possible reconciliation. Voluntariness consists in action through the source's own will and reasons; it need not include a vicious alternative compatible with every essential perfection held fixed.

If the stronger claim is instead that the same essentially wise source could genuinely will injustice while the full occasion and all essential commitments remain unchanged, the essential-wisdom premise excludes precisely that possibility. A conditional statement that power would suffice if another act were willed does not prove that its antecedent is jointly realisable under those conditions.

Suleiman records this difficulty candidly at printed pp. 287–289 / PDF pp. 300–302. His statements about a less good or different world, printed pp. 112 and 285 / PDF pp. 125 and 298, must not be suppressed. Read as power claims they fit the narrower distinction; read as unrestricted alternatives under fully fixed reasons they leave a tension. The direct check did not recover the three Hoover works cited by Suleiman; their exact cited pages remain unread. Hoover’s distinct [2024 encyclopedia article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ibn-taymiyya/), sections 4.4–4.5, was read and preserves both essential-perfection and alternative-creation language. It corroborates the tension without demonstrating two total histories under identical essential commitments. This later article neither verifies the unread cited bodies nor reverses the modal result.

# Created occasions and the necessity of a whole history

Temporal origination does not by itself establish modal contingency. A particular act can begin at a specified time while its occurrence follows necessarily from a broader determination. Suleiman printed pp. 277–283 / PDF pp. 290–296 treats successive acts in the divine bearer; printed pp. 298–300 / PDF pp. 311–313 distinguishes the continuing ability to speak from particular utterances. Neither discussion alone demonstrates an alternative complete history.

The source nevertheless gives a substantive account of renewed occasions. Suleiman printed pp. 285–286 / PDF pp. 298–299 describes a prior created formation as supplying the fitting occasion for a subsequent wisdom-guided act. The occasion is not a supplier of the Creator's existence or standing efficacy. An already original and powerful source produces an earlier event, which conditions a later exercise by that same source. These are different act tokens and respects, not circular production of the source itself.

Three sufficiency questions consequently have different answers. The source's existence and productive power can remain intrinsically sufficient. The complete explanation of this particular exercise can include its occasion and renewed determination. A complete contrastive explanation of this whole history from necessary intrinsic character alone is a further claim. Treating these as identical either imports the collapse-producing premise or assumes the alternatives still requiring warrant.

The conditional modal consequence is settled. If a necessarily fixed complete occasion has one uniquely best act, essential wisdom requires it, and effective power secures it, that act is necessary. If the whole necessary intrinsic nature uniquely requires one complete history, that history is necessary. Temporal renewal and reasons-responsive agency do not interrupt those entailments.

The source-level question is whether the stronger global premises hold. An actual sequence of locally determined occasions does not itself establish one unique complete sequence. The admitted beginningless succession offers no demonstrated necessary first state from which to derive every later differentiation. Conversely, lack of a first state is not evidence that several histories are metaphysically possible. A global uniqueness argument or a warranted alternative realisation is needed for those respective conclusions.

The result is voluntary, reasons-responsive necessitation with intrinsic possibility and dependence-based necessity distinguished. This is an analytical description, not attribution to a modern school. The texts supply neither a completed libertarian freedom proof nor a proof of whole-history necessity. They also do not reduce perfection to an eternally idle capacity: Suleiman printed p. 240 / PDF p. 253 and pp. 281–286 / PDF pp. 294–299 give perpetual wisdom-based activity positive standing. Necessary generic activity, if granted, still does not identify every particular act.

# Ordinary reports can warrant belief

The completed ordinary-entitlement result is affirmative. A competent recipient may believe an undefeated, warrantedly understood ordinary report, including an unfamiliar person's report, without first independently certifying the speaker or settling which philosophical theory uniquely explains the permission. The separate exact-text conceptual review found no blocking defect at that expressly defeasible ordinary scope.

Burge's mature account supplies a positive rationale through presumed adequate epistemic backing and competent uptake. His Postscript distinguishes the acceptance entitlement from the actual route supporting knowledge and gives human comprehension an empirical warrant. The comparison with Fricker does not establish one uniquely correct universal a priori explanation. These source-based points support the appraisal, rather than converting a bare description of assertion into a deductive proof of entitlement. Relevant loci are Content Preservation pp. 466–470, Postscript pp. 263–275 and 282–284, and Fricker pp. 69–84.

The original assessment's overly demanding stopping criterion has been corrected. Explanatory nonuniqueness is not by itself an undercutter of first-order warrant. If ordinary communication is admitted as a genuine way of learning, a rival account that explains the same permissible belief does not make the belief impermissible. It may change the account of its grounds or the route by which knowledge is acquired.

Consider a competent listener asking a stranger a simple directions question. The answer is apparently asserted, understood, ordinary and not implausible; no relevant evidence of deceit or incompetence is present. Prior acquaintance is not a universal requirement for permissible belief. A restrictive rival needs a positive reason why additional certification is necessary in such a case. Merely saying that the speaker could lie does not supply that reason.

The example does not show that all background social or empirical warrant is absent. Nor does it establish that Fricker herself must deny belief in every such encounter. A role-sensitive account may explain the ordinary answering practice in another way while granting the same permission. The exact review preserved this caution. The disagreement should be located where the accounts actually differ, rather than manufactured by assigning a restrictive everyday verdict to an opponent.

The result does not apply to every intelligible-looking string or unfamiliar technology by default. Comprehension must be warranted; relevant normal competence and reliability matter; concrete signs of misleading context, deficient backing or misunderstanding can defeat acceptance. A hidden defect may prevent knowledge even when the recipient's entitlement persists. Permission to believe is fallible, does not assign confidence one and does not itself authorise consequential action.

# Content occurrence and the preservation of warrant

The foundational epistemology sources connect perception, reasoning, testimony and cumulative signs without forcing every route to begin with the same certification. El-Tobgui's thesis, printed pp. 248–288 / PDF pp. 272–312, develops actual cognition and meaningful common predication. His anthology contribution, particularly PDF pp. 77–85 and note 153 at PDF p. 116, treats qualitative transmission and functionally equivalent routes. These accounts can support genuine learning rather than merely growing confidence in uninterpreted marks.

A report's proposition, its contextual content, its actual attribution and its practical authority remain separate. A sentence can express a true proposition without having been uttered by the alleged source. A genuine utterance can be misunderstood. A correctly understood and well-warranted report can still fall short of a permission to act in a particular role. These are differences in what has been established, not arbitrary procedural barriers.

Shared-source dependence also need not destroy knowledge. Faithful transmission can preserve a good epistemic route. Yet ten copies of one report do not automatically provide ten independent evidential roots. Additional access is valuable according to the error it can check: content corruption, mistaken authorship, source incompetence and failure of the original observation are different possibilities. Counting access points without identifying their relation can overstate corroboration.

The retained programme already distinguishes preservation from new independent support, and finite independence certificates from general tawatur warrant. The present source reading offers useful connected illustrations and stronger interpretive context. It does not demonstrate a blanket programme error or supply a new preservation theorem. Rewording an inherited distinction in the vocabulary of a newly read book would not establish novelty.

The ordinary-entitlement result makes the methodological consequence more positive. A competent reader can accept a sufficiently supported methods report without resolving the entire philosophy of testimony. However, entitlement attaches to the report's warranted content. A statement of per-question randomisation does not silently specify every cross-person, cross-time and conditioning relation needed by one exact test. Trusting the report and making an explicit further model assumption can be reasonable while remaining different acts.

More precise competent testimony could warrant a more precise design-qualified analysis without requiring the analyst to recover a seed or independently execute all original code. Conversely, absent that precision, a formal compatible-law example can expose a gap without showing that the example was the actual mechanism or equally credible. The closed appendix illustrates this content-scope distinction at a deliberately limited scale; it is not the centre of the foundational assessment.

# Two short explanations and the scope of the results

## For a five year old

Suppose someone gives a toy its power. We should ask where that power really comes from. A giver whose power is not borrowed at all cannot borrow it from the toy it is making. That helps explain why the giver must really have power.

The harder argument asks whether everything can keep borrowing without any original source. This report says there are good reasons to answer no. It also finds good reasons that a fully wise, knowledgeable original source would not tell its own lies. A critic still disagrees about whether those reasons are enough. Even if someone never lies, we must check that a message really came from that person and that we understood it.

## For a fourteen year old

A complete account of received existence needs original actuality. Within the defended account of real bearers, that supplies at least one actual bearer whose whole existence is original. It does not need a name or uniqueness. Contingency-as-need then rules out the same bearer's possible absence: that would make its whole existence received, contrary to its original status. The conclusion is an actual Necessary Being under reasoned metaphysical principles, not modal logic alone.

A further argument treats received or self-sufficient existence as a constitutive manner of that reality's being. That warrants essential independence, rather than merely happening to lack a supplier now. The broader perfection argument supports genuinely possible nondefective abilities, whether or not each is exercised. The source's power, knowledge and practical perfection remain richer claims than the word necessary by itself.

The truthfulness argument concerns how this same source uses its own knowledge in an assertion. Creating someone else's error does not make the source mistaken. Its own deliberate false declaration would instead counterfeit the truth-directed account it gives. I now judge the positive integrity argument sufficient under the defended perfection premises. The critic agrees that the distinction matters but still questions exceptionless purity. Neither verdict authenticates a particular religious message.

One Creator's exclusive actual origination of everything remains a further issue. The complete unity case supplies real support, including an answer to easy cooperation proposals; the remaining objection concerns whether every coordinated contribution must involve borrowed or altered essential efficacy. Essential wisdom also rules out genuinely unwise willing, but does not by itself determine the unique whole history. Ordinary understandable and undefeated testimony, meanwhile, can warrant belief without a uniquely settled theory or additional details never communicated.
