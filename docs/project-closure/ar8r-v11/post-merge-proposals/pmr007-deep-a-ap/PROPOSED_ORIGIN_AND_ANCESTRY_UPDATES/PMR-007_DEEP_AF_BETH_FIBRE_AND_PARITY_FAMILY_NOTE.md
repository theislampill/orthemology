The finite attained-class result is the familiar fibre criterion: a target is
recoverable from a representation exactly when it is constant on representation
fibres. Deep AF does not create a new origin for that mechanism.

The theory-level vocabulary is standard model theory. In ordinary first-order
logic, **implicit definability** asks whether every two models of a theory with
the same reduct agree on the added symbol; the classical Beth definability
theorem converts such implicit definability into explicit definability. Deep AF
uses that theorem only as a guarded frontier statement. It does not claim a
finite-model Beth theorem or transfer it automatically to modal, second-order,
non-elementary, intensional, probabilistic, or source-relative semantics.

## Scientific effect

The result blocks an inefficient research pattern: computing another invariant
inside an unchanged neutral reduct cannot distinguish expansions already known
to share that reduct. A new round is eligible only when it supplies a genuinely
new admissible coordinate, restricts the intended class by an independently
argued principle, introduces source-conditioned evidence with correct authority,
or moves from exact entailment to a separately warranted abductive metric.
