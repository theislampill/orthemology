# Request-context provenance: what the Anas route warrants

10 October 2026 UTC. Additive T20 investigation. No protected edits, integration, outreach, new formal model, full historical reconstruction, or closure.

## Result

**The distinctive request context has strong positive canonical transmission support, including an explicit report that Anas narrated it to a group in the Qatada transmission. Its provenance nevertheless remains narrower than the occurrence testimony: multiple Qatada pupils corroborate this transmitted account, not multiple independently identified request-event witnesses.** Ibn Hajar's Companion-mursal classification does not demote it to an ordinary rejected Successor-mursal within the hadith framework. Nor does the accepted classification supply the identity of Anas's original informant.

This operation strengthens the *articulation and defense* of the retained positive appraisal against two concrete conflations: a supposed unverified Qatada–Anas link and an automatic equation of Companion-mursal with weak testimony. It does not add an independently preserved eyewitness report of the request or transform the source-adoption inference into a deduction. An actual request-specific criticism is now inspected rather than merely imagined.

## Deduplication and precise new operation

The response-case report, its negative findings and reading ledger, the independent review, and the capacity-warrant report were read. The request's presence, Anas's non-eyewitness status, weak richer Ibn Abbas route, and collection dependence were already earned. Tabari and Ibn Hajar were also already read by the prior investigator. No novelty is claimed for rediscovering them.

The unperformed comparison is between **the explicit lower-link transmission evidence**, **the real basis and limits of Companion-mursal acceptance**, and **an actual critic's inference against the request context**. The direct Bukhari 3868 and 4867 checks extend the canonical route map, while al-Iraqi and Rashid Rida supply new primary methodological/critical reading. The increment is an executed, bounded warrant test, not a new historical event witness.

## 1. Exact lower-link evidence and dependence

### Saʿid's branch: Anas is the speaker

[Bukhari 3637](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3637) prints two branches. First: Bukhari ← Abdullah b. Muhammad ← Yunus ← Shayban ← Qatada ← Anas. Then, introduced by Bukhari's “وقال لي خليفة”, the second is Khalifa ← Yazid b. Zurayʿ ← Saʿid ← Qatada ← Anas. In that second sequence the decisive words are **“أَنَّهُ حَدَّثَهُمْ أَنَّ أَهْلَ مَكَّةَ سَأَلُوا”**. The subject of “narrated to them” is Anas, not Muhammad narrating to Anas. The ensuing body is the general request for a sign and displayed splitting. The embedded clause explicitly attributes the narration to Anas before the request account; it does not say that Anas personally watched the episode.

The clause is independently visible as a *textual reading*, not an independent event source, in Tabari's Bashr ← Yazid ← Saʿid ← Qatada branch: **“أن أنس بن مالك حدثهم”**. [Tabari 54:1, first transmitted report](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura54-aya1.html). Ibn Hajar expressly notices this extra wording in the Signs-of-Prophethood version. Thus the hearing point is not an interpreter's attempt to squeeze contact out of a bare generic “ʿan”.

[Bukhari 3868](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3868) gives Abdullah b. Abd al-Wahhab ← Bishr b. al-Mufaddal ← Saʿid b. Abi ʿAruba ← Qatada ← Anas, including the request and Hira between the two parts. This is a further Saʿid branch, not another Qatada teacher or another Companion witness.

### Shayban and Maʿmar

[Muslim 2802a,b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2802a) supplies Shayban ← Qatada ← Anas with the request body, and Abd al-Razzaq ← Maʿmar ← Qatada ← Anas by an equivalent-meaning cross-reference. [Bukhari 4867](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4867) repeats the Yunus–Shayban branch. [Tirmidhi 3286](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3286) directly prints Abd b. Humayd ← Abd al-Razzaq ← Maʿmar ← Qatada ← Anas with the request and an added revelation-sequence statement, followed by Tirmidhi's own hasan-sahih judgment. His judgment must not be confused with the website's separate modern grading label.

The sensible positive count is **three named pupils preserving the request through Qatada**: Shayban, Saʿid, Maʿmar. The printed collector-level branches show that the detail is not confined to a single displayed collection or a single pupil's version. They do not rule out all upstream dependence, prove every chain's independence of written material, or let the investigator count three independently witnessed requests. Repeated Bukhari locations are especially not independent.

Tabari also prints explicit Qatada hearing language in shorter occurrence accounts through Shuʿba/Saʿid. Those support contact for those accounts. They must not substitute for explicit hearing of the request detail when the longer Saʿid body already supplies the more pertinent evidence. An abridged occurrence report does not contradict a longer request report merely by omitting the occasion.

## 2. The upper-link limit is genuinely different

Ibn Hajar's [displayed Fath al-Bari 7:221–222](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/52/7029/باب-انشقاق-القمر) classifies this as Companion-mursal because Anas did not witness the episode. His suggestion **“فلعله سمعه من النبي”** is a possibility: perhaps he heard it from the Prophet. It is not a recovered statement by Anas naming his source. He then reports finding a richer Ibn Abbas account through a weak route. His earlier location of the request in Anas and that qualified later discovery must be read together.

The explicit “narrated to them” answers the Qatada–Anas transmission question at the text's stated level. It does not repair the distinct absence of a named source between Anas and the event. Conversely, the second absence supplies no reason to deny the first explicit statement. These are different links and different questions.

## 3. Why Companion-mursal retains positive standing, with a real limit

In [al-Iraqi's al-Taqyid wa-l-idah, commentary on Ibn al-Salah's ninth type](https://www.islamicbook.ws/hadeth/alum/altqiid-001.html), Ibn al-Salah treats Companion-mursal as effectively connected, on the standing of Companion transmitters. Al-Iraqi corrects the unrestricted premise: most such reports are from Companions, not necessarily all. He discusses examples of Companions narrating through Successors, explains the typical subject matter, and records a minority rejection attributed to Abu Ishaq al-Isfarayini against the general acceptance of hadith scholars and most usul scholars. This is a real transmitted methodological disagreement; al-Isfarayini's own text was not inspected.

The source therefore supports **accepted testimony under an articulated, predominantly Companion-source presumption**, not a freshly reconstructed historical link in this specific account. Neither “all sources must have been Companions” nor “the unnamed source may conceivably be otherwise, therefore reject” represents the full discussion. Its evidence supports continued canonical acceptance while leaving the personal provenance unresolved. The historical reliability of every example in al-Iraqi's catalogue was not newly investigated.

## 4. Actual objection: Rashid Rida on this exact request detail

Rida's own [Masʾalat inshiqaq al-qamar, reproduced from al-Manar](https://islamarchive.cc/articles/337980), distinguishes Anas's Qatada-only route and non-eyewitness account from Ibn Masʿud's observation. He points to Companion narration from Successors against unrestricted acceptance premises. More pointedly, he argues that a requested public certification should have generated wider eyewitness transmission; near his conclusion he treats Ibn Masʿud's occurrence narrative as supporting an event *not answering a request*, in contrast with Anas. This is a substantive source-owned challenge, not a generic modern slogan. His opening also reports Ahmad Zaki Pasha's stronger allegation about Kaʿb; it is not evidence establishing Kaʿb as this report's source.

**Assessment:**

- The critic is right to distinguish the breadth of occurrence testimony from the breadth of explicit request testimony. Multiple pupils below Qatada do not answer that upper-source point.
- A route's failure to narrate a request is not a narration that no request occurred. The move from Ibn Masʿud's silence to a non-request event needs an additional incompatibility premise. The canonical bodies inspected do not provide it.
- General examples of Companion–Successor transmission invalidate an absolute universal but do not identify a faulty intermediary in Anas's request account. Al-Iraqi already permits the former correction while retaining the general acceptance judgment.
- The expected-publicity objection requires showing what transmission should survive from a sign to this audience. It cannot simply assume that every credible requested sign must arrive as many separately identified request-eyewitness accounts. The retained appraisal never asserted such a count or an unrestricted mass-transmission conclusion for this detail.

These replies remove the *automatic* inference to defeat; they are not a demonstration that every wider argument in Rida's article fails. His terrain/visibility, universal publicity, and Qurʾanic refusal-of-signs arguments were encountered as context but not independently settled here. No astronomical reconstruction or global-records search was performed.

## 5. Effect on source adoption

The earlier source-adoption comparison rested on a warranted sequence of evidential demand and responsive display, together with relevant informed source control. This pass provides a better-supported reason to retain the demand component: it is a received canonical detail with corroborated transmission through several Qatada pupils, an explicit Anas narration clause, and a recognized testimonial category whose acceptance survives awareness of its exceptions.

It provides no new causal identification of the event's producer and no missing divine utterance. It also does not turn preservation of Qatada's narrative into independent reproduction of the historical scene. Accordingly:

1. Retain the positive request-context contribution and the prior defeasible attestation result.
2. Do not inflate that contribution into multiple independent request-eyewitness testimony, a newly established exact challenge, or guaranteed historical reconstruction.
3. Do not strip the contribution away solely because Anas is non-eyewitness or because another Companion omitted the request.

The new discriminating result is **a successful separation of a lower-link objection from an upper-link limitation, and rejection of an unwarranted silence-to-denial inference in an actual critic**. The accepted canonical warrant is made more explicit; the overall philosophical conclusion remains defeasible and source-bound. This is local progress, not T20 closure.
