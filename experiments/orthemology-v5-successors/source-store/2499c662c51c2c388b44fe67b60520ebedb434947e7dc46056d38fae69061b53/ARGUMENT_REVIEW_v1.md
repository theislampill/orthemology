# Organised reliance: independent argumentative review

3 October 2026 UTC. Review of practical-authority research-v1, bound in REVIEW_INPUT_IDENTITIES.json. This is an argumentative assessment, not canonical acceptance, a metaphysical certificate, or an implementation instruction. The author is preparing a revised version; this review does not prejudge that revision.

## Verdict

**Credit a bounded intermediate result, with material revisions.** Deliberately structuring another person's permissible activity around a support arrangement can give its organiser a special, pro tanto reason to take reasonable care over interruption and exit. The historical ground can persist after the organiser changes its purpose. A new assertion or promise is not necessary in every such case. This is a credible development of the inherited relational-duty account, with a changed assessment of advance planning and withdrawal.

The unrestricted phrase **protection or adequate release** is too strong if read as a guaranteed successful outcome. The strongest defended result is a responsibility to take proportionate, feasible protective and transitional measures. A failure after adequate provision need not be a breach; a failure to make reasonable provision can be a breach even if a stranger ultimately prevents loss. These distinctions are not merely caveats: they specify what the new duty is.

V1 already includes proportionate strength, competing reasons and feasibility in §3 and adversarial cases G–H. The correction is therefore to make adequate conduct versus guaranteed outcome explicit across the result and ledger, not to accuse the author of expressly endorsing strict liability.

The case does **not** establish source authority over the recipient, a new reason to predict compliance, R_G, N, essential T0, or an actual divine disclosure. The candidate generally preserves these limits. Nor does it establish that origination as such is the duty's source. Its explanatory work comes from active organisation of vulnerability, recipient agency, permissible reliance, and proportionate responsibility for the arrangement. A bounded-role or nonpromissory-undertaking classification is compatible with this result and is its strongest conservative interpretation.

## 1. The positive reason, without circularity or an excessive proof demand

The candidate does more than define a governor as somebody with obligations. Its strongest elements are independently describable: G intentionally changes or sustains R's practical options; G understands that R is proceeding within a bounded activity whose completion or safe cessation depends on that support; G has an effective means of addressing the resulting exposure. R is a subject whose agency and epistemic goods matter independently of G's preferences. Those facts explain why G has a special question to answer about the transition G helped make costly.

The normative step remains substantive. Recipient goods plus control do not deductively entail every proposed special duty. Nevertheless, direct rational assessment of the described relationship can warrant a defeasible practical judgment. It would be a mistake to reject it simply because it lacks a numerical weight, a derivation acceptable to every moral theory, or a metaphysically realized original-source example.

Consider a controlled application. G deliberately routes R's permitted investigation through a support that G operates, while knowingly arranging matters so that an interruption will strand the investigation at a costly stage. Before any malfunction, G can cheaply provide an exit window or usable fallback. H knows the same facts and could provide the same fallback, but played no part in arranging or assuming the dependence. No assurance is given. It is intelligible to ask G for the missing transition plan even before circumstances activate an equally stringent rescue requirement on H. The basis is not that G has more power, or that harm by G would be worse than identical harm by H. It is G's attributable arrangement of R's practical situation.

This controlled assessment supplies genuine prima-facie support for P5. It is not an empirical result, and merely repeating the proposed verdict in every example would add no support. Its discriminating rationale is responsibility for how one's intentional arrangements expose another agent, including the distribution of transition burdens. Removing that history while retaining equal ability removes that particular ground, even where other grounds yield the same eventual act.

The argument should not depend on G obtaining an advantage. A wholly unselfish organiser can create the relevant exposure. Replace the paper's reference to enjoying the advantages of reliance with the responsibility for arranging another person's options and leaving them to bear foreseeable transition burdens.

## 2. Organisation and legitimacy must be independently specified

### Active organisation is not mere awareness plus nonintervention

P2 needs a descriptive boundary. Deliberately providing and configuring the operative support, inducing R to continue a bounded task, arranging the removal of alternatives, or accepting responsibility for a handoff can be relevant acts. Merely noticing that an existing dependence continues and declining to change it is not, on that description alone, active maintenance. Otherwise H immediately becomes a maintainer, and the candidate's matched contrast collapses by equivocation.

This does not make a promise necessary. Nor must G explicitly think “I undertake an obligation.” Practical responsibilities can arise from intentional conduct whose normative significance the agent does not announce. Conversely, calling the conduct an undertaking does not establish its moral consequences. The independently describable relation and the reason for its normative relevance must remain visible.

### Supported use and morally permissible use are different

P3 must not mean “the reliance which G is obligated to protect.” It should identify the activity, relevant information and risks, actual support conditions, communicated limitations where applicable, and whose decisions produced the exposure. A supported domain is evidence about the arrangement; G's label alone cannot authorize injustice or retroactively abolish a duty. R's justified belief that support is likely also does not automatically create an entitlement to its continuance.

Distinguish three questions: whether R has epistemic reason to expect support; whether choosing to rely is practically permissible; and whether the history gives R a claim on G. They often coincide in the controlled case. None should silently define the other two.

An understood, fair, finite provision may legitimately terminate even though continued support would be valuable and discontinuation disappointing. A nominal disclaimer cannot necessarily release G from an exposure G has deliberately imposed while preventing meaningful adaptation. What makes a limitation effective is partly factual; what makes it an acceptable allocation is normative. Both need case-specific warrant.

## 3. Stress tests and required outcomes

1. **Bystander versus organiser.** Preserve equal knowledge and feasible responses, but remove active organisation from H. G's historical reason can remain distinct even if both must avert imminent grave harm. The paper correctly concedes that anti-sabotage and costless-rescue verdicts alone do not establish the increment.

2. **Rescuer.** H's mere opportunity to help does not release G. If H actually assumes provision or begins a rescue in a way that induces further reliance, H can acquire a new responsibility. An accepted transfer can alter who owes what; it does not follow that every volunteer becomes indefinitely bound.

3. **Unrequested benefactor.** A useful gift, even foreseeably used, does not establish all P2–P3 conditions. A freely supplied finite resource that leaves recipient options intact differs from deliberately restructuring a project around continued supply. Repeated gifts lie between these poles and require an independent account of induced dependence and risk allocation. Gratuity is neither universal immunity nor automatic lifelong obligation.

4. **Beneficiary and rightful authority.** R's benefiting supplies no reciprocal duty of obedience. An organiser can owe protective care while lacking jurisdiction over R; an abusive organiser can owe restoration while lacking a right to direct the victim. Conversely, an independently authorized official may have powers whose basis is absent from this reliance argument. The duty owed by G is not an authority possessed by G over R. A reliable warning may give R an ordinary evidential reason, without being an authoritative directive.

5. **Multiple organisers and forks.** If G1 and G2 jointly structure the exposure, responsibility need not belong exclusively to either. Relevant contributions, knowledge, capacities, control and accepted allocation matter; “special” does not mean uniquely assigned. Do not infer equal shares or duplicate unconditional guarantees. Where the same recipient's project is copied into two technical branches, count the actual dependencies and losses, rather than treating every occurrence as a new person or an independent full duty. Where different recipients genuinely rely, there are different interests to accommodate. The principle does not itself solve distributive allocation among them.

6. **Recipient identity ignorance.** R can follow a warranted support arrangement without knowing G's identity. G's responsibility need not depend on R successfully identifying its bearer. More radical ignorance of any arrangement should be described carefully: objective dependence is broader than expectation-based reliance. An argument extending to it requires the agency/exposure rationale, not an unacknowledged inference from a promise or announcement.

7. **Informed refusal.** A competent recipient can reject a proposed substitute or choose a risk, subject to others' interests. G's responsibility does not license forced assistance, surveillance, or control. Distinguish refusal of a particular measure from a clear, informed release of the relevant claim. Nor may G manufacture consent by offering an unusable or coercive alternative. If an acceptable option was genuinely available and R freely declined it, R's later choice may change what loss is attributable to G's failure.

8. **Compensation and release.** Compensation can sometimes address a reliance loss, but money need not restore the epistemic opportunity, agency, or timely choice at issue. After-the-fact compensation and an adequate prospective exit are different modes of response. A reasonable offer is not automatically full discharge; R's veto is not automatically decisive either. An independent promise can add a performance obligation that this minimal principle does not erase.

9. **Overwhelming cost.** A special reason can be outweighed. High risk to another person, impossibility, or disproportionate cost may justify discontinuation after reasonable warning and mitigation. The mere fact that some exposure remains does not entail an all-things-considered obligation to eliminate it. Residual duties such as explanation, reasonable coordination, or repair may survive an override, but their precise content is not already established by naming them.

10. **Independent natural failure.** Blame for originating a fault is distinct from responsibility for provision against it. A foreseeable interruption can require a contingency plan even if G neither caused nor could prevent the fault. No breach follows merely because a genuinely unforeseeable or unavoidable failure defeats reasonable preparations. With known inexhaustible relevant capacity the feasible precautions may be much greater; that fact cannot be imported from productive origin alone.

11. **Purpose change and timing.** G's relinquishing its old epistemic end need not erase the recipient's existing exposure or G's history. This is the clearest new consequence beyond current-purpose PT. The duty attaches to an established or actively maintained relation; planning before its interruption is not automatically a separately demonstrated duty before any recipient or relation exists. A prospective decision to create dependence can be assessed, but needs its own stated temporal application.

12. **Original creation without displaced alternatives.** The absence of a pre-creation baseline does not exempt G from all responsibilities to an existing R. That is a sound answer to a blanket exemption. It does not show that every created capacity has the same maintenance entitlement as a provision which displaced an already available alternative. The more ambitious creator case must establish the normative baseline and appropriate scope positively. Retain it as a reasoned extension with weaker present support, not as a result proved merely by defeating the nonexistence-baseline objection.

## 4. Exact inherited ownership and genuine increment

- Sixth ATTRIBUTE_ASCENT, §6.2, already puts the real goods and harms of deliberately created/governed recipients within the source's practical field and leaves responsibility's scope to be explained. Tenth does not discover that source choices are answerable to recipient goods.
- Seventh SOURCE_TRUTHFULNESS_WARRANT, §§“Why the truthful option is a practical excellence” and “A narrower positive purpose argument,” already owns the truth-linked voluntary-reliance reason and PT. Its NORM_AND_PURPOSE_SCOPE appendix already distinguishes apprehension, institution, intrinsic grounding, and conformity. Absence of a fresh promise is not by itself novelty against that broader ancestry.
- Ninth F2 already owns fidelity's source eligibility, the controlled act's defect, and the R_G+N route. A new occasion of defeasible duty does not establish a complete-profile improvement, actual nondomination, or their essential scope.
- Ninth F4 §§2–3 preserves one mixed source's genuine fitting action alongside its contrary action, distinguishes function-fixing from norm-grounding, and withholds metaphysical certification of the rival. Knowing breach of this new duty can be added to that challenge without either refuting the duty or certifying the expanded mixed nature.
- The supplementary warrant-standard ASSESSMENT, §§2 and 5–6, already permits basic rational warrant, credits local normative judgment, and credits defeasible trust where independently evidenced concern and secure attribution support it. Those credits are not Tenth additions.

The genuine increment is therefore **an application-level extension of the relation's temporal and action scope**: reasonable preparation and transition before an interruption, possible wrongful abandonment without false speech, and persistence despite an end change. These consequences can change a recipient-directed responsibility assessment. They do not yet change a recipient's rational prediction that an original source will perform, still less establish rightful command. This narrower result is enough to count if its premises are independently met.

## 6. Concrete revision request

1. Amend P2 so active organisation is independent of duty-bearing and distinct from H's passive awareness.
2. Amend P3 to separate epistemic expectation, permissible reliance, and relational entitlement; provide a legitimate finite-support countercase.
3. State P5/C1–C4 as a special pro tanto reason for reasonable, feasible protection and transition, with no strict outcome guarantee.
4. Add shared provision/forks, informed refusal, compensation, and unforeseeable independent failure to the adversarial ledger.
5. Downgrade the creation-without-baseline extension to the exact positive claim defended; defeating blanket immunity does not establish identical maintenance rights.
6. Retain the clear negative results about authority over R, expectation of compliance, complete-profile repair and essential integrity. Do not present those inherited distinctions as new results.

With these changes, the lane can warrant the bounded intermediate responsibility result. It should not be promoted as an independent original-source practical-priority bridge.
