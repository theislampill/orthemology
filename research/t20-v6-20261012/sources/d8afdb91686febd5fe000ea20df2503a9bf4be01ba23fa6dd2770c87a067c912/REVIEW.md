## Gaussian class and inverse range

Let V=ASA+Q. The correct coefficient is b=a V^{-1} A S theta. Its transpose agrees with the conditional-Gaussian coefficient a(theta^T S A V^{-1})^T. The order matters. Completing the square in output density gives mean shift Cb, and applying the input tilt first gives exactly the same shift. At fixed S this coefficient is independent of m, giving a restricted positive class. Varying S changes b in general. Dirac S=0 gives b=0 and partial-rank covariances are allowed.

The Gaussian inverse uses P0=C^{-1}−(1−a)T^{-1}, V=a P0^{-1}, S=A^{-1}(V−Q)A^{-1}, and m=g+A^{-1}(V/a)C^{-1}(m_+−g). Necessity and sufficiency require both P0 positive definite and V−Q positive semidefinite. Arbitrary positive-definite C is not enough. The scalar range [6/7,2) in the author's fixture is correct. The lower endpoint is a Dirac input; the upper endpoint is excluded. Independent exact tests rotate the target eigenbasis, so they check a genuinely nondiagonal T as well as noncommuting S.
