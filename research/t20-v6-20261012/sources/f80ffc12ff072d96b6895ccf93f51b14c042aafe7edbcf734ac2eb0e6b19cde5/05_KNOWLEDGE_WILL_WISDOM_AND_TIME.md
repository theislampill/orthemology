# 5. Knowledge, Will, Wisdom and temporally appropriate perfection

**Claim type:** affirmative source reconstruction and inference audit.  
**Main sources:** A10–A11, A18–A21, inherited checkpoint 18, and the owner's *Oneness* translation.  
**Main improvement:** the appropriate alternative to a static timeless-perfection model is not merely an eternal capacity label. The source develops a more exact account of fitting particular acts, their timing, and their intrinsic ownership.

## 5.1 An attribution correction matters to the positive argument

The latest research agent quotes several statements in which a source reports an interlocutor before replying. This is especially important at the Knowledge/Will stage. Checkpoint 18's positive-agency return already records that the opening selection argument in the relevant *Darʾ* passage quotes Ibn Rushd and that the subsequent knowledge bridge presupposes self-knowledge. [I02]

We should therefore not report a new independent theorem from blind causehood to omniscience merely because the passage contains the words “knowledge” and “creation.” The significant affirmative result is more specific: a source that completely knows its own determinate efficacious act cannot be wholly ignorant of the effect entailed by that act, given the relevant comprehension/closure condition.

That is not a claim that the source learns the effect by performing an inference. It is our inferential relation among claims about the source. Nor is it enough that the source knows its standing ability to produce various things; what matters is the particular actual determination at issue.

## 5.2 The giver-of-perfection principle requires a qualified target

The agent's maxim can be read in two importantly different ways:

- **Unrestricted copying rule:** whatever property appears in an effect must appear in its cause in the same manner.
- **Qualified perfection argument:** a complete original source of a genuine perfection must not be less adequate in the relevant respect, with the perfection's mode appropriate to the bearer.

The first is not the intended safe rule. Creating a creature's hunger, eating, wrongdoing or restricted learning does not make the Creator hungry, an eater, a wrongdoer or a learner. The creator/performer distinction in *Shifāʾ* chapter 20 expressly blocks that flattening. [A10]

The second is a substantive metaphysical argument. It needs an account of what counts as a pure perfection, how it is possessed, what causal scope has been established, and why the proposed source cannot supply the reality while wholly lacking the relevant adequate qualification. The *Akmaliyyah* is better direct evidence for this inquiry than a modern compilation citing a general declaration of divine perfection. [A18]

This is a good place for premise-minimal analysis. If the proof already assumes a maximally perfect knowing source, an inference to knowledge has accomplished nothing. If it assumes only an original producer of a cognitive creature, the missing bridge should be defended as a genuine claim about original bestowal, not concealed by the name `PerfectSource`.

## 5.3 The source's reply to the “new perfection” objection is more discriminating than a slogan

*Shifāʾ al-ʿAlīl* chapter 22 presents an objection that acting for a purpose would mean acquiring a previously missing perfection. Its affirmative replies distinguish an external giver from an intrinsic qualification and distinguish what is fitting at one time from what is fitting at another. The chapter also refuses to settle the dispute by calling the contested feature a defect: that is what needs to be shown. [A11]

**Short locator:** «ما ليس بكمال في وقت لا يكون عدمه نقصا فيه» — “What is not a perfection at a time is not a deficiency by being absent at that time.”

The analytical consequences are important. Let `Fitting(a,t)` mean that a particular act a is fitting at time t, and `Occurs(a,t)` that it occurs then. It does not follow from `¬Occurs(a,t0) ∧ Occurs(a,t1)` that a perfection required at t0 was missing. That inference additionally needs `RequiredPerfection(a,t0)` and a bridge making occurrence then necessary to possession of the relevant perfection.

For example, acting at the fitting time and refraining before it can form a single appropriate pattern. Calling the later occurrence “acquired perfection” does not identify an external producer of an intrinsic divine attribute. Conversely, merely assigning `Fitting=True` to every actual act would make the account empty. The fittingness claim needs its own independent ground, not retrospective relabelling.

This provides a constructive way for Orthemology to model the issue: keep the normative target indexed to act, time and respect, and test whether the supposed deficiency survives those indices. Do not model all perfection as a time-independent scalar inventory whose entries must be simultaneously maximised at every instant.

## 5.4 Standing capacity, ongoing activity, particular act and product are four different objects

A source can be always capable of acting without that fact alone proving it is always acting. A type of activity can be unbounded in succession without any particular product being eternal. A particular intrinsic act can be distinct from its external created effect. A source's timelessly necessary existence does not automatically make the entire created history necessary.

These separations matter to both sides. A critic cannot infer a frozen or deficient deity merely because one particular act was not occurring earlier. An affirmative argument cannot infer every moment of actual activity from a standing capability predicate alone. Nor may a formal model define every temporally indexed occurrence as a created bearer and then present the resulting conclusion as a source-critical discovery.

The passages in *Majmūʿ* 12 concerning complete eternal necessitation and successive acts are useful contextual support. The independent modern book *Qidam al-ʿālam wa-tasalsul al-ḥawādith* is by Kāmilah al-Kuwārī, not a primary work by Ibn Taymiyyah. Its supplied page number requires edition matching; a later scholarly treatment should not receive direct authorial attribution. [A19, A21]

## 5.5 Complete determination is not the same object as a necessarily held reason

The formal shape is simple. If `D` means the **completed particular determination** and `E` its designated effect, then `D → E` may hold. A standing reason `R` which can be operative in different acts is not thereby identical to D. Showing that R can coexist with multiple choices does not refute `D → E`.

Equally, renaming a nonnecessitating reason a complete determinant does not establish sufficiency. The philosophical accounts disagree about what counts as an adequate explanation of why this reason became operative in this particular act. That needs direct comparison, not a verdict obtained by changing the antecedent.

This distinction also helps avoid modal collapse. `□(D → E)` and actual D imply actual E. They do not imply `□E` unless D is also necessary in the same modal sense. If the model encodes all complete determinations as necessary seed facts, its conclusion that every effect is necessary is a consequence of that modelling assumption. It is not an automatic consequence of a necessarily existing agent.

## 5.6 Wise determination and arbitrary specification

A principle rejecting reasonless preference need not mean that every good outcome is ranked by one numerical utility function with a unique maximum. It might instead involve several kinds of fitting reasons, bearer-appropriate ends, context and particular determination. The source's purposive account must be reconstructed at that level rather than flattened into an optimisation metaphor. [A20]

There are real questions here. What explains selection among equally fitting alternatives? Does the source deny the alleged equality, permit nonnecessitating agent explanation, or locate a more complete reason than the observer has specified? How does that account differ from arbitrariness? The questions cannot be resolved by declaring all imaginable arrangements equally metaphysically possible, since the external possibility of the alternatives is itself a substantive premise.

The source's a-fortiori perfection argument can give a positive reason to reject purposeless original agency. But that argument should be separated from the stronger claim that we know the exact reason for every particular event. A general wise-source claim is not a universal human-access theorem concerning all divine purposes.

## 5.7 A semantic table suitable for formal work

| Symbolic role | Intended referent | Does not by itself mean |
|---|---|---|
| `ExistsAt(g,w)` | g exists in a world in the declared frame | Necessity through oneself |
| `IntrinsicPower(g)` | Power qualified in g | Every possible act currently occurs |
| `Determines(g,a,t)` | A particular actual willing/exercise | An external cause gave g a missing essential attribute |
| `Produces(a,e)` | The act produces the designated effect | The act and the effect are one bearer |
| `Created(e)` | The relevant external productive status | Every temporally indexed divine occurrence is created |
| `Knows(g,p)` | Factive cognition of p under the declared interpretation | A learned sequence of syllogisms in g |
| `Wise(a,t)` | Appropriate purposeful determination | One numerical global maximiser has been identified |

The table is a proposal for explicit semantics, not a source-independent axiom system. A source-faithful reconstruction can use different formal encodings if it preserves these distinctions.

## 5.8 Research outcome

The most useful new source result is the temporally appropriate-perfection reply, because it prevents both a false deficiency inference and the opposite overclaim that an eternal power label settles actual agency. The creator/performer passage also constrains the giver-of-perfection inference rather than merely guarding one causal example.

The remaining positive burden is clear: establish the genuine cognitive and practical qualifications of the original source with the right causal and normative scope. Formal sufficiency lemmas can preserve that argument, but the definition of a qualified source must not perform the philosophical work in its place.
