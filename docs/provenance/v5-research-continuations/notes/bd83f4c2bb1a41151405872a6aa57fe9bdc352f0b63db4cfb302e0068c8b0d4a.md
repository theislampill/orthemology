# Controlled information and eventual restoration

Status: new ordinary-mathematical research draft, 2026-09-30. Not yet independently reviewed or kernel-checked. No novelty claim. This sharpens the prior return's hidden-standard HS1: evidence acquisition is now controlled and may itself be target-violating.

## 1. Exact finite statistical control model

Let Θ, A and Y be nonempty finite sets. A hidden standard θ∈Θ is fixed once, before interaction. Every action in A is independently stipulated safe and commonly licensed. For each θ and a, a probability vector Pθ,a on Y has **strictly positive** entries. Conditional on the complete past and selected current action a, the next observation has exactly this vector. Thus observations are controlled iid samples, not copies of a hidden root; the same action's samples may be represented by a fresh iid stack.

At time t a single shared controller chooses a_t from its past actions and observations, with arbitrary memory and independent private randomization allowed. It does not receive θ. Then it observes y_t. The standard fixes a nonempty set Gθ⊆A of target-good actions. Restoration means:

`Pθ(a_t∉Gθ infinitely often)=0` for every θ.

This is hidden-standard-specific co-Büchi acceptance. It differs from a common publicly labeled target and from maximizing average accuracy. Common licenses and correct model/target bindings remain input assumptions.

For nonempty U⊆A define the observational equivalence class at θ:

`Eθ(U)={λ∈Θ : Pλ,a=Pθ,a for every a∈U}`.

Call U a **self-verifying target support for θ** when:

1. U⊆Gθ
2. U⊆Gλ for every λ∈Eθ(U)

Equivalently, if any action in U is bad under λ, some action in U distinguishes θ from λ. A support can be safe without identifying the exact standard: observationally indistinguishable standards are harmless if they accept all its actions.

## 2. CIRS theorem: exact existence criterion

**Theorem.** A single history-based policy restores almost surely under every θ if and only if every θ has a self-verifying target support.

The theorem is about existence with unrestricted memory. It supplies no finite-memory bound, deterministic last-error bound, finite zero-error declaration, expected-error bound, or real-world standard/norm identification.

### 2.1 Constructive sufficiency

Choose one support Uλ for each λ. Fix an ordering of Θ and of every Uλ. Maintain:

- the exact likelihood `L_t(λ)=∏_{s<t}Pλ,a_s(y_s)`
- a separate cyclic pointer through Uλ for each candidate λ

At time t choose a likelihood-maximizing candidate λ_t (fixed tie rule), execute its next cyclic action, and advance that candidate's pointer. The controller is shared across all true standards; it never uses θ in selecting its action. For rational kernels likelihood comparisons and updates are exact computable rational operations; bit lengths and memory grow.

Fix the true θ. Consider a candidate λ whose support contains an action bad under θ. Its self-verifying property supplies b∈Uλ with Pλ,b≠Pθ,b. If λ were selected infinitely often, every action of its finite cycle, including b, would be used infinitely often.

For each action a, write observations from its successive uses as Y_a,1,Y_a,2,… . Under θ they form an iid stack with law Pθ,a, independently selectable by nonanticipating sampling. By the strong law,

`(1/n)∑_{k≤n}log(Pθ,a(Y_a,k)/Pλ,a(Y_a,k)) → KL(Pθ,a || Pλ,a)`.

The divergence is positive exactly for unequal vectors and zero otherwise. Strict positivity and finite Y make all increments bounded. With finitely many actions, if any informative action is sampled infinitely then:

`log(L_t(θ)/L_t(λ)) → +∞`.

To justify the last implication without an illicit adaptive sampling theorem: on each iid stack's SLLN event every sufficiently long informative partial sum is at least half its positive mean times length; finite-sampled actions contribute bounded constants; noninformative actions contribute zero exactly. A finite sum therefore diverges when one informative count diverges.

Hence, under the supposed infinite selection of harmful λ, eventually L_t(θ)>L_t(λ). Then λ cannot be a likelihood maximizer. Contradiction. Each harmful candidate is selected finitely often almost surely. There are only finitely many candidates, so after a finite random time only candidates whose entire support lies in Gθ are selected. Every later action is target-good. ∎

### 2.2 Necessity with the last-exploration-time issue handled

Let π restore under every θ. Fix θ. Because A is finite, the random recurrent action set

`U∞={a : a_t=a infinitely often}`

is nonempty. Restoration implies U∞⊆Gθ almost surely. Some nonempty U⊆Gθ satisfies Pθ(U∞=U)>0. On this event all actions outside U occur only finitely often. Consequently, for some **deterministic** N,

`F(U,N)={all a_t∈U for t≥N, and every a∈U occurs infinitely often}`

has positive Pθ probability. The last use outside U need not be a stopping time; the proof does not condition there.

Suppose λ∈Eθ(U). For a finite action/observation history h of length N with positive contribution to F(U,N), full support makes Pλ(h)>0 whenever Pθ(h)>0. From h onward, θ and λ have identical controlled transition laws on every action in U, and π is the same shared behavioral policy. Therefore their continuation measures **restricted to paths that stay in U forever** coincide, including the event that every U action recurs infinitely. One way to formalize this restriction is to send the first action outside U to a common cemetery state; the two killed continuation kernels coincide. Thus Pλ(F(U,N))>0.

If U contains an action outside Gλ, that action occurs infinitely on F(U,N), contradicting π's almost-sure restoration under λ. Therefore U⊆Gλ for every λ∈Eθ(U), which says U is self-verifying. Repeat for every θ. ∎

Randomized policies do not escape the necessity result. With perfect recall, an initial independent private random seed can be converted to a common behavioral policy on action/observation histories: conditional seed likelihood factors into a seed/policy factor and a scenario-dependent observation factor, and the latter cancels in the conditional distribution. Alternatively keep the seed in the joint proof space; its law is the same in both scenarios and the controlled kernels restricted to U still agree. No seed may encode θ.

### 2.3 Polynomial support elimination and certificates

The union of any two self-verifying supports for θ is self-verifying: a rival equal on their union is equal on each, so accepts both. Hence there is a unique greatest support Kθ, possibly empty.

It can be computed without enumerating subsets. Start U₀=Gθ and iterate:

`Uₖ₊₁ = Uₖ ∩ ⋂{Gλ : λ∈Eθ(Uₖ)}`.

The operator is monotone in U and contracting. Every self-verifying support V⊆Gθ remains inside every Uₖ: if V⊆Uₖ and λ agrees with θ on Uₖ, it agrees on V, so V⊆Gλ. After at most |Gθ| strict deletions the iteration stabilizes; a nonempty fixed point satisfies precisely the self-verification condition. Its limit is therefore Kθ. CIRS is equivalent to `Kθ≠∅ for every θ`.

Each removed action has a finite witness: a rival λ whose kernels match θ on all actions still present but whose target rejects that action. Replaying the stage-wise witnesses and the final support equality certifies the result. Deletions can cascade: removing one informative action can make a previously distinguishable rival indistinguishable. A one-pass filter is not complete.

For n=|Θ|, m=|A| and d=|Y|, the direct rational-vector equality implementation uses at most O(n²m²d) rational comparisons over all θ. Bit costs depend on input rationals; this is not a bound for acquiring or authenticating those laws. The likelihood controller uses unbounded memory even though support calculation is finite.

**Monotonicity controls.** A common signal garbling at each action preserves all preexisting equalities between observation laws and can create more, so the resulting Kθ can only shrink. Adding live rival standards can only shrink old standards' kernels. Enlarging all supplied target sets can only enlarge kernels. These follow by valid-support inclusion, not by inferring any norm's authority.

## 3. Exact deletion controls

### D1: information exists but every informative action prevents persistence

Take Θ={0,1}, A={r0,r1,q}, G0={r0}, G1={r1}. Both r0 and r1 emit a fair bit under either standard. Probe q emits Bernoulli(1/3) under 0 and Bernoulli(2/3) under 1. All actions are safe/licensed, but q is target-bad under both.

The only nonempty candidate supports are {r0} for standard 0 and {r1} for standard 1. Neither distinguishes the other standard, which rejects that action. CIRS says no almost-sure restorative policy exists, regardless of memory or adaptive probing.

Yet for every δ>0 a finite probe batch followed by a majority-based permanent response achieves restoration probability at least 1−δ under both standards. Thus limit-sure finite confidence and almost-sure eventual restoration separate. Infinite probing identifies the standard almost surely but violates restoration forever. A late unobservable last probe cannot fix the obstruction.

### D2: making target-good actions informative changes the answer

Keep the target sets, but let r0 emit different full-support laws under the two standards, and likewise r1. Each singleton support becomes self-verifying. The likelihood/candidate policy restores almost surely while continuing to gather evidence through target-good behavior. This is a controlled-action analogue of the prior HS1 free-evidence construction.

### D3: exact standard identification is unnecessary

Let two standards have identical kernels on all actions and both accept common action c, while disagreeing on other actions. U={c} is self-verifying for each. Constant c restores without learning which standard is true. The criterion concerns decision-relevant information, not recovering an ontological label.

### D4: merely informative success somewhere is insufficient

If r0 distinguishes the standards but r1 does not, G0={r0}, G1={r1}, only standard 0 has a self-verifying support. No policy restores under both. Under true 1, a correct controller must eventually use only uninformative r1; that positive-probability tail transfers to 0. Information available under one favorable target does not make every target self-verifying.

### D5: root copies invalidate the conditional kernel premise

If the observations of every nominal query are copies of one initial biased bit, the one-time marginals equal the displayed Pθ,a but successive observations do not satisfy the conditional controlled-iid contract. The likelihood product and SLLN proof fail. Under a shared positive-probability copied observation word, opposing target requirements cannot both be satisfied almost surely. Action/report count is not independent acquisition count.

### D6: source authority is an independent constraint

If rθ is licensed only under θ and both standards remain possible after every finite history (full-support kernels), robust finite-history licensing may allow only a universally licensed abstain action. Replace A by the actually common licensed action set. If no target-good support remains, no authorized restoration follows from the statistical theorem. A probabilistic permission rule is a new contract, not a consequence of likelihood concentration.

## 4. General last-informative-probe impossibility

The following measure argument also covers continuous repair actions and parameter spaces, beyond CIRS's finite A.

Fix two standards θ≠λ. Work on standard Borel action, observation and controller-memory spaces with a common measurable nonanticipating behavioral policy, regular conditional kernels, and measurable **disjoint** eventually-restorative tail events Bθ and Bλ. The action is chosen before the next observation. Assume:

1. Every finite transcript law under θ is mutually absolutely continuous with that under λ (including the same controller randomization)
2. A declared class R of nonprobe/repair actions has identical observation kernels under θ and λ at every common history
3. Target restoration implies probes occur only finitely often
4. Along an eventually restorative path, a measurable statistic of the controller's repair sequence converges to θ under θ and to λ under λ, or the already specified events Bθ and Bλ directly encode the incompatible tail requirements

Then no shared controller restores almost surely under both standards.

**Proof.** Under θ, some deterministic N has positive probability of no probes after N and θ-restorative tail behavior. Split by finite length-N transcripts (or use conditional measures in a standard Borel version). The no-probe killed continuation kernels coincide under the two standards; finite-prefix mutual absolute continuity transfers a positive-probability portion to λ. That portion has θ's tail property and therefore not λ's disjoint tail property, contradicting almost-sure restoration under λ. ∎

For finite observations/actions the countable finite-history argument above is elementary. A general continuous-action statement must explicitly use regular conditional probabilities/standard Borel spaces and prove the restricted-kernel equivalence; the present packet's implemented checks will remain finite/rational.

This theorem can consume a geometric result that successful repairs of ever-smaller deviations encode increasingly precise information about a hidden norm. It does not itself establish that geometry or the legitimacy of the norm class.
