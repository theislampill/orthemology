# Additional direct reading of Jami sixth collection pages 93 to 98

Reader-facing study record, 7 October 2026. The interpretations and reading claims below belong to the recorded study and its bounded rereads. This edition of the notes adds no source-reading or proof credit.

The six images show printed pages 93–98 directly. This receipt does not infer their absolute PDF positions from filenames. It is an additional bounded reread, not the whole-volume traversal or a new manuscript witness.

Pages 93–94 use jurisprudential distinctions about several grounds and overlapping rulings before moving to productive action. The argument does not simply deny that several grounds can yield the same broad legal outcome. It treats the relevant rulings as numerically multiple even where their performance or legal effects overlap. That individuation commitment matters: sameness of an output description, numerical identity of the grounded item, and multiple sufficient reasons are not interchangeable. This is a historical analytical argument, not present legal advice.

Page 94 explicitly gives each cooperator a power, will and intrinsic act in its own bearer. Page 95 then distinguishes each's contribution to the common product from their total. It explicitly allows that one contributor may have been able to do the whole alone, while denying that this is what each actually does in the joint case. Therefore the source cannot fairly be criticised as overlooking standing capacity versus actual contribution.

Page 96 argues that the cooperator's actual partial exercise is limited relative to doing the whole: the other's real contribution prevents the first's role from being the entire role. This is the source's positive mode-level comparison. It must not be replaced by a claim that all ordinary cooperative agents lack their own intrinsic powers. The remaining philosophical question is whether this less comprehensive exercise is an unfitting deficiency for every qualified original, rather than simply an accurate description of a deliberately limited role.

Pages 97–98 explicitly say the familiar opposed-wills argument alone does not exclude cooperation. The text analyses necessary concord separately and returns to the earlier actual-action argument. Agreement has not been omitted; the force of the earlier comparison is doing further work. The annotation on 97 marks the context of an inserted or supplied phrase and remains a distinct editorial layer.

The six-page passage strengthens the affirmative reconstruction and supplies unusually clear contribution-individuation controls. It does not by itself settle the independent eligibility of peer-excluding supremacy or turn a finite partial-role model into a metaphysically possible original pair. The question whether common creation is a sufficiently supported best explanation of actual order must also be assessed separately from this universal source-mode principle.
