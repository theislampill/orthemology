# Complete fixed-codebook frontier at b=4, M=3

Candidate for independent review. Ordinary mathematics plus exact rational
certificates; no canonical edit, public-seed substitution or general novelty
claim. This file preserves the distinct dependency packets.

## Theorem

For every integer u>=5,

V_fixed(u,4,3) = 1/floor((u-1)/2).

The lower proof for the three finite cases u=8,10,12 is computer-assisted by
small exact dual-certificate trees. The infinite odd and even tails have ordinary
proofs. The remaining u=5,6 endpoints also have elementary proofs. In the
inherited majority-diagnosis application, only u>=2b+1=9 belongs to its parameter
domain; u=5,6,7,8 are useful general-game extensions, not instances of that view.

## Construction for every u

Write u=2q+1+epsilon with epsilon in{0,1}. Use disjoint uniform blocks of sizes
1,q,q+epsilon. If the singleton is intact there is a zero-risk message. Otherwise
three corrupt roots remain across the two large blocks, one of which has at most
one corrupt root, proving risk<=1/q. Corrupt the singleton, one root in the
q-block and two roots in the other block to attain1/q (2/(q+epsilon)>=1/q).
Thus the worst-case risk is exactly1/q, which is the stated formula.

## Odd universes and u=5

For u=2q+1 with q>=3, use the independently reviewed odd-three-message family.
It uses accepted F1/F2, a heavy-atom reduction and a deficit-cover argument.
For u=5, each row has at most one atom strictly above1/2, so among five roots
there is a root r with p_m(r)<=1/2 for all three rows. Corrupt the other four.
Every row's corruption mass is at least1/2. This proves the endpoint.

## An elementary u=6 proof

Suppose no four-root corruption set has all three masses at least1/2. Then for
every omitted pair D, some row gives p_m(D)>1/2. For a fixed row, two such
high-mass pairs cannot be disjoint, because their masses would sum to more
than1. A pairwise intersecting family of two-element sets is either contained in
a star or contained in a triangle: if it contains ab and ac, then either all
edges contain a, or an edge missing a must be bc, which forces every remaining
edge into that triangle.

Three such families cannot cover K6. Three stars leave uncovered the complete
subgraph on vertices outside their at most three centers. Two distinct stars
cover at most9 edges, and adding a triangle covers at most12<15; coincident
centers only reduce this bound. One star plus two triangles covers at most11
edges, and three triangles at most9. Hence some pair D is high under no row;
its complement has all masses at least1/2, a contradiction. The construction
above matches. A redundant exact certificate for u=6 is also supplied as a
control, but this endpoint does not depend on it.

## Even universes u=2q+2, q>=6

Put t=1/q. A row with an atom at least t can be satisfied by selecting that root;
normalizing the remaining two rows reduces to V_fixed(2q+1,3,2)=1/q, established
by the full two-message theorem. Selecting its three-root witness completes the
four-root corruption set. Mass-one residuals are already satisfied.

Otherwise every atom is<t. Take a minimum-sum vertex of the covering polytope
0<=x<=1 and p_m dot x>=t. The uniform fractional vector gives sum x<=2+2/q<3,
and a vertex has at most three fractional coordinates. If there are at most one
unit coordinate, round every positive coordinate upward, using at most4 roots.
If there are no fractional coordinates, at most two roots suffice. The only
hard case has exactly two unit coordinates A and fractional-coordinate sum
 X<=2/q<1.
There are2q roots outside A.

For any row unsatisfied by A define delta_m=t-p_m(A)>0,
H_m={i outside A:p_m(i)>=delta_m}, h_m=|H_m|, and
beta_m=delta_m/(t-delta_m). Feasibility and all atoms<t give
0<delta_m<tX<t. Each H_m is nonempty: otherwise the fractional contribution
would be<delta_m X<delta_m. If at most two rows are unsatisfied, select one
member of each H_m. If all three are unsatisfied, it suffices that two H_m
intersect, as the shared root and one root from the third cover all deficits.

Assume instead that all three H_m are pairwise disjoint. Then sum h_m<=2q.
Since atoms in H_m are<t and atoms outside H_m are<delta_m,
 1-t+delta_m < h_m t+(2q-h_m)delta_m.
Rearranging gives beta_m>(q-1-h_m)/q. Summing yields
 sum beta_m>(q-3)/q.
If X(H_m) is the fractional x-mass in H_m, then strict atom bounds also give
 delta_m <= sum_{fractional i}p_m(i)x_i
          < t X(H_m)+delta_m(X-X(H_m)),
so X(H_m)>beta_m(1-X). Disjointness now forces
 X >= sum X(H_m) > ((q-3)/q)(1-X),
therefore X>(q-3)/(2q-3). This contradicts X<=2/q for q>=6, because
 q(q-3)-2(2q-3)=(q-1)(q-6)>=0.
Hence two H_m intersect; together with A their two-root deficit cover gives the
required four-set. This proves the even tail.

## The exact finite certificates for u=8,10,12

These complete the theorem between its ordinary-proof regimes. They are not
probability grids. Each certifies a finite disjunction covering all continuous
probability rows under the fixed-codebook model.

Assume a codebook had risk r<B, where B=1/3,1/4,1/5 respectively. Put t=r.
Accepted F1 gives t>=2/u. No atom can reach B: such an atom would reduce to the
established two-message value V_fixed(u-1,3,2)=B and yield a four-root witness
with all masses>=B. Relax the consequent strict atom bound to p_m(i)<=B.

We may relabel messages to make row1 contain a largest atom of the entire
codebook, then permute roots so that row1 is nonincreasing. These transformations
preserve the set of all corruption scenarios and the max-min risk. They justify
p_1(0)>=p_m(i) for m=2,3 and all i, and p_1(i+1)<=p_1(i). No other symmetry
assumption is used. Every row is a nonnegative probability vector, and t>=0.

For each four-root set C there is at least one message m with p_m(C)<=t.
A tree node records some such assignments. An internal node selects an unassigned
C and has all three children, one imposing p_m(C)-t<=0 for each message. Any
putative codebook/risk pair follows at least one child. No numerical validity
assumption about the branch-selection heuristic is necessary.

Each leaf gives an exact rational weak-duality certificate for t>=B under its
node's constraints. In notation Ax<=d, Ex=1, x>=0, the checker verifies
 y<=0, A^T y+E^T z<=c, d^T y+1^T z>=B,
where c selects the objective coordinate t and z is unrestricted. These
inequalities imply c^T x>=d^T y+1^T z>=B, contradicting t=r<B. This proof uses
only elementary weak duality, not solver optimality, floating infeasibility, or
an assumed correct numerical solution.

The replay checker independently rebuilds every inequality and equality from
u,b,M and the recorded assignments. It checks all message children, exact child
path extension, rational signs, coefficient inequalities, objective bounds,
reachable-node coverage and exact scenario enumeration. It never reads a stored
coefficient matrix and never calls scipy or an optimizer. Generator status,
elapsed times and completion flags are not trusted for any proof step.

Verified certificate sizes:
- u=8:82 nodes,55 exact dual leaves, about14KB
- u=10:322 nodes,215 exact dual leaves, about64KB
- u=12:1,564 nodes,1,043 exact dual leaves, about364KB
The redundant u=6 tree has112 nodes and75 leaves. The largest dual denominator
in all four certificates is169. The accepted replay trust base is Python's
standard-library integer and Fraction arithmetic plus inspection of this logical
reduction and the short checker. The scipy/HiGHS generator is retained only as
provenance and is not needed for certificate replay.
