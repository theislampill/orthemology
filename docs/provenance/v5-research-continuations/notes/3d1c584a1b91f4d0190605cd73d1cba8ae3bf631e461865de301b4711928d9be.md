# Common local repair characterizes smooth additive scores (three or more outcomes)

Status: ordinary mathematical report with S1, the full exact S2 iff, and the sublinear-tolerance S7 extension now kernel-checked; see FORMALIZATION_SCOPE.md. Independent exact-chain and S7 delta reviews passed. No novelty or canonical-adoption claim. This is an extension of LOCAL_REPAIR_RIGIDITY.md and does not claim that Hessian agreement at a single point suffices for arbitrary nonlinear scores.

## Setup

Let n>=2, V={e_1,...,e_n}, C=Delta_(n−1), and p in ri(C), so every p_i lies in (0,1). For each i let f_i and g_i be continuous and finite on [0,1], C^2 on (0,1), with f_i''>0 and g_i''>0 there. Define

    F(x)=sum_i f_i(x_i), G(x)=sum_i g_i(x_i),
    D_F(v,b)=F(v)−F(b)−grad F(b)·(v−b).

Use b in (0,1)^n and coherent repairs c in C∩(0,1)^n. Restricting repairs to this set is sufficient for the positive local theorem. An extension allowing boundary repairs can be admitted if their extended scores obey the same nonnegative-divergence/strict-propriety identity; it is not needed here.

Fix a transverse h with 1^T h != 0. At a positive scale e, a common coherent weak repair of b_e=p+e h means c_e in C∩(0,1)^n with

    D_F(e_j,c_e)<=D_F(e_j,b_e),
    D_G(e_j,c_e)<=D_G(e_j,b_e), for every j.

These are finite-state proper losses with the forecast in the SECOND argument. Reversing the Bregman arguments changes the proof and is not silently permitted.

## S1. Necessary local Hessian compatibility

If common coherent weak repairs exist for a sequence e->0, then

    P_(Hess F(p)) h = P_(Hess G(p)) h,

where the projections are onto T={z:1^T z=0} in the indicated positive-definite quadratic metrics.

### Proof of localization

Taking the p-weighted average of the F inequalities and canceling terms depending only on truth states gives

    D_F(p,c_e) <= D_F(p,b_e) = O(e^2).

The separable divergence is a sum of nonnegative one-coordinate divergences. For a fixed p_i in (0,1), D_fi(p_i,x) stays bounded away from zero whenever |x−p_i|>=delta>0. To see this without assuming bounded endpoint derivatives, use

    D_fi(p_i,x)= integral_x^p_i (p_i−u) f_i''(u) du.

For x on either side of p_i outside the delta neighborhood, this integral includes a fixed interior subinterval with strictly positive integrand after orienting it correctly. Thus D_F(p,c_e)->0 forces every coordinate c_e,i->p_i. Near p, continuity and strict positivity of the second derivatives give a quadratic lower bound D_F(p,c)>=m||c−p||_2^2 for some m>0. Hence c_e−p=O(e).

### Proof of projection condition

Take a convergent subsequence (c_e−p)/e->z in T. The derivative of the loss with respect to its forecast argument at p is

    grad_b D_F(v,b)|_(b=p)= Hess F(p)(p−v).

The first-order expansion of each statewise gain, divided by e, therefore yields

    (p−e_j)^T Hess F(p)(h−z) >= 0.

The positive p_j-weighted sum is zero, so every term is zero. The differences p−e_j span T. Thus z=P_(Hess F(p))h. The identical argument for G uses the same subsequence and gives the required equality. QED.

Only necessity has been established by this local argument. It does not assert that two arbitrary generators whose Hessians happen to agree at p have overlapping exact dominance cells near p.

## S2. Global local-repair characterization for n>=3

For n>=3 the following are equivalent:

1. At EVERY p in ri(C), common coherent weak repairs exist at arbitrarily small positive scales on the fixed ray b_e=p+e e_1
2. There is a constant a>0 and affine one-coordinate terms b_i x+d_i such that

       f_i(x)=a g_i(x)+b_i x+d_i, for all i and x in [0,1]

3. D_F=a D_G on all arguments on which these scores are defined

If these equivalent conditions hold, every sufficiently small transverse perturbation of every interior p has a common coherent STRICT repair. Thus (1) is not merely necessary for the endpoint; it has a matching local positive construction.

### Proof of (1)->(2)

The Hessians are positive diagonal matrices. Since e_1 is transverse, S1 and the diagonal simplex formula imply that at each p there is a scalar a(p)>0 such that

    f_i''(p_i)/g_i''(p_i)=a(p), for every i.

Write r_i(x)=f_i''(x)/g_i''(x). We show all r_i are one global constant. Fix an index i and any s,t in (0,1). Choose j!=i and a positive u<min(1−s,1−t). Since n>=3, one can form an interior simplex point with coordinates p_i=s,p_j=u and distribute the positive residual across the other coordinates; likewise with p_i=t,p_j=u. Therefore

    r_i(s)=r_j(u)=r_i(t).

So each r_i is constant. Evaluating at any one interior simplex point shows these constants agree across i. Call the common value a>0. Integrating f_i''=a g_i'' twice yields the affine difference on (0,1); continuity extends it to endpoints. QED.

### Proof of sufficiency

Affine terms cancel from Bregman divergence, so (2) implies (3). Fix p and b_e->p. Let c_e uniquely minimize D_G(c,b_e) over compact C. Existence follows from continuity in c and strict convexity. As b_e->p the objectives converge uniformly on C to D_G(c,p), whose unique minimum is p; hence c_e->p. In particular c_e is interior for sufficiently small e. Convex optimality yields

    (grad G(c_e)−grad G(b_e))·(v−c_e)>=0, for v in C.

The three-point identity then gives

    D_G(v,b_e)−D_G(v,c_e)>=D_G(c_e,b_e)>0

for transverse b_e outside C. The same strict inequalities hold for D_F=aD_G. Thus (3) supplies the common strict local repair, including condition (1). The implication (3)->(2) can also be obtained by differentiating; it is already implied by the above equivalence. QED.

## S3. Why two outcomes is a sharp exception

Prior-art qualification: the common arithmetic correction here specializes Pettigrew's explicit 2018 complementary-pair result for additive continuous 0/1-symmetric convex forecast scores; see PRIOR_ART_AND_SCOPE.md. Its role here is a sharp scope control for the >=3 classification, not a new binary theorem.

Take the same coordinate generator in each slot. Let f(x)=x^2 and g(x)=x log x+(1−x)log(1−x), with 0 log0=0. These generators are not positive affine equivalents: f''=2 while g''=1/[x(1−x)]. Yet on C={(t,1−t)} the same arithmetic repair works for every b=(x,y) in (0,1)^2:

    t=(x+1−y)/2, c=(t,1−t).

For f this is ordinary Euclidean projection. For g the state losses are

    D_G((1,0),b)=−log x−log(1−y),
    D_G((0,1),b)=−log(1−x)−log y.

The repaired losses are −2log t and −2log(1−t). Arithmetic-geometric mean gives t^2>=x(1−y) and (1−t)^2>=(1−x)y, strictly whenever x+y!=1. Thus the same repair strictly improves every state under both nonproportional scores.

This fits S1: f''(s)/g''(s)=2s(1−s) equals its value at 1−s, so the Hessian normal corrections agree at each two-outcome coherent p. The third positive coordinate in S2 is precisely what connects values that two-outcome complement symmetry leaves unrelated.

## S4. A concrete three-outcome obstruction

Use f(x)=x^2/2 and the binary entropy generator g above, at p=(1/2,1/3,1/6). The Euclidean normal correction vector is (1/3,1/3,1/3). The G-Hessian correction is proportional to p_i(1−p_i), hence

    r_G=(9/22,4/11,5/22).

For h=e_1 the tangent projections differ. S1 therefore rules out common coherent weak repair for every sufficiently small positive e, although each score separately provides a strict coherent repair. This is a higher-outcome failure, not a contradiction of the two-outcome example.

## Interpretation and limits

The result is a positive compatibility classification inside a declared smooth additive proper-score family. Requiring universal common corrective accessibility collapses that family to one relative score geometry when n>=3. It does not choose which equivalence class is correct: each singleton class separately satisfies the condition. It also does not establish norm authority, original intentionality, mental hosting, or source/root identity.

Separability, positive Hessian, every interior p, a fixed same-problem comparison, and n>=3 all carry real content. Without additivity, equality of normal projection fields need not identify the full generator. Without the every-p condition, a pointwise Hessian match does not establish global score equivalence. Without standard-blind common repair, every score can individually coherify and no collapse follows.

## S5. Exact rational three-outcome infeasibility certificate

No numerical logarithm or nonlinear optimizer is needed for a concrete case. Retain the p and generators of S4, set e=1/1000, and put

    b=p+e e_1,
    q=p+e(2/3,−1/3,−1/3).

Use the unscaled Euclidean metric I; the factor 1/2 in F has no effect on dominance. The tangent Euclidean ball of radius r=1/6 about p lies in C: a vector of norm at most 1/6 cannot make any coordinate negative, and tangency preserves the sum. Since e||P_I e_1||_2<=e<r/2, R3 applies. Its q_I=||Q_I e_1||_2^2=1/3 and m_I=1 give

    ||c−q||_2 <= 2e^2

for EVERY coherent Euclidean-weakly-dominating c. Hence ||c−q||_1<=sqrt(3)||c−q||_2<4e^2.

For the binary-entropy generator the loss at vertex e_j is

    −log P_j(x), where P_j(x)=x_j product_(i!=j)(1−x_i).

Thus log-score nonworsening requires P_j(c)>=P_j(b). On [0,1]^3 each partial derivative of P_j has absolute value at most one, so |P_j(c)−P_j(q)|<=||c−q||_1. For j=3 direct polynomial arithmetic gives

    P_3(b)−P_3(q)=5e/108−e^2/18−2e^3/27.

At e=1/1000 this equals 69361/1500000000. Subtracting the conservative 4e^2 perturbation allowance leaves

    63361/1500000000 > 0.

Therefore P_3(c)<P_3(b) for every Euclidean-dominating coherent c. No c can also log-score-dominate b. The argument even excludes boundary repairs: P_j(c)=0 yields infinite log loss at a truth state, and the displayed polynomial requirement is still necessary. This is an exact global exclusion of every possible coherent c for the stated rational b, not a grid-search failure.

For the binary exception of S3 the corresponding exact polynomial identities are

    t^2−x(1−y) = (x+y−1)^2/4,
    (1−t)^2−(1−x)y = (x+y−1)^2/4.

They certify both log-score state improvements without floating-point logarithms.

## S6. Nonseparable deletion: common local repair without equivalent scores

Let n=3, a=(1,−1,0), F(x)=||x||_2^2/2, and G(x)=(||x||_2^2+(a·x)^2)/2. Both Hessians are constant positive definite, but Hess G=I+aa^T has off-diagonal terms and the generators are not positive-scale affine equivalents. Because a lies in T and is orthogonal to 1, both metrics have the same Euclidean normal line span{1}, and therefore the same orthogonal projection onto T. At every interior simplex point p, every sufficiently small transverse perturbation has a common strict affine repair by R1. Thus additivity across coordinates is essential to S2, even though both generators are globally smooth, strictly convex and proper for their associated finite truth-state means.

This example also changes rankings of coherent forecasts. At truth e_3, compare c=(49/100,1/100,1/2) with d=(1/3,1/3,1/3). The unscaled Euclidean losses are 2451/5000 and 2/3, whereas the unscaled G-metric losses are 3603/5000 and 2/3, reversing the ranking. The tangent component a·x is weighted differently. The common local correction fixes the normal behavior relevant to eliminating the sum defect, not all normative comparisons within the simplex.

## S7. Separate robustness extension: sublinear allowed worsening

This extension uses the same hypotheses as S1. Suppose each common repair is allowed additive statewise worsening eta(e)>=0 under each score, with eta(e)/e->0 along the tested sequence. Then the same necessary Hessian-projection equality follows. Consequently S2 remains an iff characterization if its exact weak-repair requirement is replaced by common approximate repairs with this sublinear tolerance. Sufficiency follows from the exact repairs already constructed.

### Proof

Fix F and write xi=c_e−p. Averaging approximate statewise comparisons with p gives

    D_F(p,c_e)<=D_F(p,b_e)+eta(e)->0.

The localization argument of S1 yields c_e->p, without yet claiming xi=O(e). Let P_T denote EUCLIDEAN projection onto the tangent space, set u=grad F(c_e)−grad F(b_e), and set A=D_F(p,b_e)−D_F(p,c_e). Bregman gain is affine in the truth vector:

    D_F(v,b_e)−D_F(v,c_e)=A+(v−p)·u.

The state inequalities therefore hold for every v in C. Choose a fixed tangent Euclidean ball B_T(p,r) contained in C. Evaluating at the point opposing P_T u gives

    r ||P_T u||_2 <= A+eta(e) <= D_F(p,b_e)+eta(e)
                       = O(e^2)+eta(e).

Write H=Hess F(p). Since F is C² near p,

    u=H xi−e H h+o(||xi||_2)+o(e).

The operator xi -> P_T H xi on T is positive definite, so its norm has a strictly positive lower bound times ||xi||_2. Substituting the last two displays and absorbing the o(||xi||) term once c_e is sufficiently close to p yields

    ||xi||_2=O(e+eta(e))=O(e).

Now divide the projection identity by e. The bound on P_T u/e tends to zero since eta(e)/e->0, and the Taylor remainder divided by e also tends to zero. Hence xi/e converges to the unique z in T satisfying P_T H(z−h)=0, namely P_H h. Applying the same argument to G forces the same limit, which is the desired compatibility condition. QED.

This analytic extension is now kernel-checked in lean/SmoothRobustness.lean, including the all-state coercivity and absorption needed to derive linear-scale localization. Its positive-part derivative argument is an equivalent proof of the displayed tangent-gradient estimate, not an assumption of the desired O(e) conclusion. The finite exact checks alone would not establish these asymptotic claims. A tolerance of order e can hide a fixed projection disagreement and is deliberately excluded. Little-o(e) is a statement about additive statewise loss tolerance in fixed score units, not about a probability of failure.
