# Thirteenth Orthemology research

Supported continuation and representation dependent certification

4 October 2026 \| Same v5

This tranche advances warranted collective diagnosis and supported continuation, primarily avenues 5–7, and establishes a new limit on type certification in avenue 9. Two representations can evaluate to exactly the same program while only one admits the specified typing certificate in the existing calculus. The report also tests which information, competences and constitutive relations matter when an organisation changes its people or information routes.

The question is what makes a continuing capacity fit for a specified task when its people, tools or access routes change. The answer developed here is task-relative: identify the content that must reach a competent recipient, the checks that make it usable, the time at which it is needed, and the scope of the resulting judgement. A change can preserve that relation without preserving every former capability. A successful output can also leave important supporting premises unestablished.

The report examines realised diagnostic support, evidential possession and collective use, then considers organisational identity, probability baselines, foundational arguments and the new typing boundary. The inherited affirmative arguments retain their force. No new actual productive source, same-bearer necessity result or essential assurance-integrity conclusion is admitted. The new mathematical results are independently reviewed written proofs, not new Lean certification.

## The question in simple terms

### An explanation for a young reader

Imagine a class keeps a cupboard of puzzle pieces. Keeping the cupboard is useful, but the class also needs someone who can open it, find the right pieces and see how they fit. If that person leaves, another child might learn the job, or the teacher might help. The class can keep solving puzzles even though the helpers change.

Now imagine a new piece arrives after the class has finished its picture. The old picture does not show what the new piece changes. The class may need to look again. And having all the pieces somewhere is different from having put them together. We are asking which useful ability continues, what help keeps it working, and what still needs to be learnt.

There is also a result about computer instructions. Showing the steps of a recipe and hiding the same recipe inside a sealed packet can give exactly the same result when used. But a rule book for checking recipes may certify the visible steps and have no rule that certifies the sealed packet. This tranche proves a precise instance of that difference.

### An explanation for an older student

Suppose three pupils each have two clues. Every clue is held by most of the pupils, yet no pupil has all three. The clues together may settle the answer even while every pupil remains uncertain. There are several questions: which clues count as the team’s information, what those clues imply together, whether the team can combine them, and what it actually concludes. Answering one does not automatically answer the others.

Evidence also depends on the comparison being made. A clue may favour an answer compared with what you knew yesterday, but add nothing once you already know it today. A claim about support should therefore say which background it uses. The same care applies to continuity: name the task and time before saying that an ability has been preserved.

In the formal calculus, an application tree exposes how a program is assembled; an opaque atom stores the whole program as one label. Evaluation forgets this distinction, but the typing rules do not. We prove that equal evaluated programs need not have the same available certificate at a chosen type. We also assess one extra conversion rule that would remove this particular boundary, while explicitly changing the calculus.

## Assessing diagnostic continuity

The following technical cases test Orthemology’s continuation claims against documented changes in who processes information and how it reaches a decision-maker. Their role is to discriminate preserved capacities, justified revisions and unresolved support requirements.

### Processing roles and checks

Sandia reports that most Laser Dynamic Range Imager (LDRI) processing moved to NASA operators with a new operational suite on STS-120. Its later account identifies continuing inspection leadership, sensor and data checks, review of NASA processing, and expert support. For STS-131, NASA proposed Windows Movie Maker AVI files; Sandia checked transmission fidelity. These institutional and participant accounts have overlapping roots. They support exercised joint work, not independent integrated qualification. \[S1–S3\]

The reported early route took about 24 hours downloading plus 4 processing; the later 18-hour total began at late-inspection start. These clocks have different origins. \[S2\]

### Transmission constraints and permission

After Ku-band failure, STS-131 imagery reached ground analysts through International Space Station (ISS) resources. Large files delayed receipt; segmentation improved transfer. Late inspection occurred while docked, with an added mission day and a mechanical arm-clearance exception. The report records completed analysis and entry clearance, but gives no meeting time fixing clearance relative to undocking. Printed page 62 contradicts the detailed docked-inspection chronology and is not used for that ordering. Wing Leading Edge Impact Detection System (WLEIDS) monitoring remained restricted, and missing neighbouring data prevented one multisensor assessment. JAXA’s contemporary NASA-attributed account is a separate publication witness with shared information roots. Clearance is bounded action permission, not a zero-risk proposition. \[M1–M2\]

The press kit planned inspection after undocking and flyaround. Actual docked inspection changes that order; unchanged whole-service fulfilment remains unestablished. \[M1, M5\]

A defensible continuation judgement specifies what survived. Actual delivery and analysis can establish a working subfunction; an authorised revised process and fulfilment of the original whole service are further claims. The relevant task, observation time and acceptance conditions must be fixed before success is credited. A justified revision can preserve an overarching objective through changed means. Retrospectively narrowing the task until any surviving activity qualifies would not establish that preservation.

The evidential standard need not be an independently repeated experiment for every historical claim. Competent, detailed testimony can support a defeasible capacity attribution. The strength of that attribution depends on what the testimony actually establishes. Reported product checks warrant a more specific conclusion than a staffing count; a complete qualification record could warrant a stronger one. Requiring the latter for every ordinary claim would discard useful evidence. Treating the former as the latter would overstate it.

For a stronger acceptance claim, the useful next item would connect the receiving operators, operating configuration and retained checks to task-specific product quality, coverage and delivery requirements. This is an integrated support requirement. It does not demand identical personnel, an autonomous receiving team or a certificate for every individual.

## Complementary tests of the support relation

### Competence and compensation

Dittemore described NASA and United Space Alliance product validation and oversight for receiving groups judged insufficiently experienced. He also asserted aggregate transition success. Whether that compensation, or adequate local competence, covered the Crater debris-impact analysis remains unestablished by the inspected record. \[C1\]

ASAP’s report for 2002 appraised the overall transition favourably while retaining training and critical-skills concerns. Its pre-accident content supplies no Crater-specific competence disposition. \[C2\]

An assurance that invokes compensation needs evidence that the compensating relation bears on the particular deficit. The existence of competent advisers somewhere in an organisation is insufficient; so is proof that their help worked on a different problem. Equally, the absence of a particular former expert does not show that every adequate alternative was absent. The proper question concerns relevant competence and an effective route for bringing it to bear before the consequential use.

### Interfaces and assessment

The NASA Engineering and Safety Center (NESC) judged the analytical capability suitable for STS-114 within developer restrictions. It reported an assigned coordinator, mitigated interface problems, and completed manual-input integrity and coordinate-nomenclature measures. The tools supported experienced interpretation rather than making mission decisions. Overall uncertainty remained incompletely calibrated; tile-repair tools were outside the reviewed scope. The original and revised reports are one lineage, with the 2007 revision adding historical material rather than certifying a new operational state. Expert-only reinforced carbon-carbon (RCC) recommendations did not acquire item-specific completion merely from a grouped status. \[N1–N3\]

The STS-114 report records focused-inspection data delivered to analysts, followed by differentiated clearance, intervention and further-testing decisions. The immediate assessment team and the mission-management recipient are distinguishable bearers; access to an assessment does not entail access to every underlying datum. \[M3\]

The Return to Flight Task Group reports observed training and integration exercises, with a June 2005 assessment cutoff. NASA’s later implementation account records operational revision. Their overlapping programme information is not independent validation merely because it appears in multiple reports. \[R1–R2\]

The content-preservation issue is concrete even in an abstract handoff. Suppose one analyst supplies a measured depth from an external surface, while the receiving model expects remaining thickness from an internal reference. Passing the same numeral accurately can still pass the wrong physical claim. A successful interface must preserve the intended quantity, units, reference frame and applicable uncertainty. These are conditions of that particular use, rather than a universal demand that every organisation adopt one data format.

### Observation and numerical applicability

STS-116’s report places a credible impact indication after the earlier survey. It distinguishes coarser imagery and risk assessment from a later LDRI comparison with the baseline. The reported 0.125-inch detectability threshold lay below a 0.160-inch critical-flaw criterion; the report connects the later assessment to entry clearance. Their inequality alone does not establish coverage, calibration, flaw equivalence or probability of detection. \[M4\]

NASA’s 2016 Dual Docked Operations report reproduces a test-linked criterion of 0.16-inch exposed-substrate diameter for panels 19–22, zones 3–4, under an ISS heavy-weight forward-centre-of-gravity end-of-mission entry trajectory. Named hypervelocity-impact and arcjet tests support the table’s regional acceptance criteria. The original revision and its mission-specific morphology, geometry and thermal-envelope transfer remain unreconstructed. This is positive documentary corroboration, not a sharp universal failure boundary or a sensor calibration. \[T1\]

Sandia’s later imagery demonstrates processing improvement, not applicable STS-116 detection calibration. \[S4\]

The 2005 task-group account assigns the 0.125-inch hole result to the Laser Camera System (LCS), not LDRI. NASA’s final update attributes 0.08-inch improvement to the Integrated Digital Camera (IDC). Laboratory performance, damage-tolerance modelling, operational risk judgement and sensor qualification answer different questions. Later imagery and combined-resource claims cannot silently establish the earlier flight configuration’s numerical detection limit. \[R1–R2\]

A retained baseline can answer what was visible before an event while leaving the later condition unsettled. A new observation can improve the discriminating basis without making the earlier material evidentially worthless. The warrant for interpreting a negative observation depends on what would have been observable under the realised conditions. A small advertised threshold is only one premise: the target region, defect class, geometry, processing and comparison method must match the claim.

A clearance can reasonably authorise an action under an accepted residual-risk criterion. It need not express certainty that a physical danger is absent. Conversely, an available measurement or a correctly executed calculation need not be enough for clearance. Observation, inference and action permission have related but different targets. This distinction permits positive credit for a working diagnostic process while withholding stronger conclusions that its record does not support.

## Possessing evidence and using it together

Brown’s evidence-set argument concerns joint support despite distributed individual information. It is conditional on an evidence-sensitive direction-of-fit requirement, with a holistic qualification; it does not supply the numerical model below. Broncano-Berrocal grants component membership but denies possession of their conjunction in his reply. \[G1–G2\]

Broncano-Berrocal’s refined account already recognises storage versus possession, weighted roles and easy access. These are local tests of two replies, not a refutation of his conditional summativist project. \[G2\]

Simion’s chapter 7 makes easy uptake by an appropriate capacity sufficient for being in a position to know; contingent bias does not define cognitive type. Chapter 8 adds role-shaped availability within reasonable limits. Chapter 9 uses a biconditional, but page 188 returns to sufficiency. These formulations are not silently harmonised. Her strict probability-raising criterion uses a total-evidence gloss, while later passages explicitly invoke an audience’s evidential background. They motivate a background-relative reading without fixing a uniform candidate-exclusion convention. \[G3\]

The accepted Simion–Carter–Kelp manuscript distinguishes function, proper operation and successful knowledge production. Its jury retains opposed cognitive contributions; final-publication pagination and footnote identity have not been established from that version. \[G4\]

Simion–Carter–Kelp’s process formula invokes ancestors; their group gloss invokes that group’s past knowledge. A distinct group’s inherited function therefore remains conditional on appropriate ancestry, attribution to the group, and epistemic benefits explaining the practice’s presence there. Copied structure or present success alone supplies none of these. \[G4\]

Carter, Kelp and Simion later defend group evidence despite resistance while also recognising genuinely missing capacities. Their 2025 article responds to Brown, postdates Broncano-Berrocal and is not a published reply to him. It offers no complete criterion for every collective’s availability. \[G5\]

### A five state model

The following construction is programme-authored. It isolates an ordinary distinction between inventory membership and support, rather than estimating any actual organisation. There are five possible states. State 0 is actual. The probability masses sum to one:

$$
P({\omega{}}_{0})=\frac{1}{10},\ \ \ P({\omega{}}_{1})=P({\omega{}}_{2})=P({\omega{}}_{3})=\frac{2}{10},\ \ \ P({\omega{}}_{4})=\frac{3}{10}
$$

Three indicators, A, B and C, have the following truth sets. The target H is fixed in advance and is also true at state 4, where all three indicators are false:

$$
A=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{1},\ {\omega{}}_{2}\},\ \ \ \ \ B=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{1},\ {\omega{}}_{3}\}
$$

$$
C=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{2},\ {\omega{}}_{3}\},\ \ \ \ \ H=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{4}\}
$$

There are three members with equally relevant roles. Member 1 observes A and B; member 2 observes A and C; member 3 observes B and C. The observations are accurate, and each member knows the values of their two indicators. On the assessment timescale, no easy route supplies the missing value or equivalent information, whether through perception, testimony, memory or inference. Their accessible information is exhausted by their observations and the common model. That background does not announce the actual state or the other members’ observations; the private cells below control their knowledge. This all-easy-route premise is stipulated; merely not knowing a fact would be weaker.

At the actual state the members cannot distinguish the following alternatives:

$$
{I}_{1}=A\cap{}B=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{1}\},\ \ \ \ \ {I}_{2}=A\cap{}C=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{2}\}
$$

$$
{I}_{3}=B\cap{}C=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{3}\}
$$

Each information cell contains the actual state and one alternative at which H is false. Each member therefore lacks knowledge of H and of the three-way conjunction. Yet two of three members know each indicator. Under the stipulated pointwise-majority inventory rule, all three indicators belong to the group’s evidence. The same positive inclusion follows whenever the rule accepts known components held by a relevant majority. No claim about an actual institution’s membership or role weights is being inferred.

$$
A\cap{}B\cap{}C=\{{\omega{}}_{0}\}\subseteq{}H,\ \ \ \ \ P(H|A\cap{}B\cap{}C)=1
$$

The joint entailment does not require the inventory to include its own conjunction as an extra entry. It evaluates the relation between the admitted components and the target. It does not give any member or group the ability to perform the inference, and does not attribute actual group knowledge.

$$
P(H|{I}_{i})=\frac{\frac{1}{10}}{\frac{1}{10}+\frac{2}{10}}=\frac{1}{3},\ \ \ \ \ P(\neg{}H|{I}_{i})=\frac{2}{3}
$$

Under the original probability assignment, H has prior probability two fifths. Each private cell lowers it to one third. Thus each private observation both makes the contrary more probable and incrementally favours it relative to that original prior. If members then adopt the contrary and group belief follows a simple majority, group belief diverges from the components’ joint support. That belief policy is an additional assumption: a probability above one half does not by itself impose an outright belief rule.

$$
P(H)=\frac{2}{5},\ \ \ \ \ P(H|{I}_{i})=\frac{1}{3}<\frac{2}{5}
$$

The confirmation claim changes if the background changes. Let D exclude state 4 before evaluating the observations:

$$
D=\{{\omega{}}_{0},\ {\omega{}}_{1},\ {\omega{}}_{2},\ {\omega{}}_{3}\},\ \ \ \ \ P(H|D)=\frac{1}{7}<\frac{1}{3}=P(H|{I}_{i})
$$

Relative to D, each private cell now raises H’s probability. The posterior ranking and joint entailment are unchanged. Hence the model establishes the support distinction, but supplies no background-invariant confirmation verdict. Its numbers are a control on the interpretation, not independent empirical evidence or a new probability theorem.

### What the model leaves open

A resource-sensitive theory may require an available integration process before a set counts as supporting a group in a particular epistemic sense. That is a substantive additional premise about support. The present result is conditional on ordinary joint entailment or conditional probability over the components. It cannot decide between all theories of justification by stipulating a semantic relation.

Likewise, a group’s failure to use a fact can arise from different causes. It may have usable access and refuse uptake, or it may lack the relevant capacity or channel. The former description does not imply the latter. Genuine defeat can alter support for a target without removing every item from the evidence body; bare non-use establishes neither change. Establishing actual access requires identifying the group, the target content and its operative circumstances; individual access somewhere in the membership is insufficient. If these facts remain unspecified, the warranted conclusion is underdetermination.

For the continuation inquiry, the positive bridge should therefore be conditional and precise. Where a suitably individuated collective has an appropriate knowledge-generating capacity, a relevant fact is accessible under that capacity’s applicable conditions, and the fact bears the required support relation, a position-to-know account can attribute evidence. Actual uptake is one useful witness of an operative route, but is neither the definition of possession nor an automatic proof of easy access. Exceptional effort can succeed too.

The strongest empirical attribution concerns named recipient teams and particular delivered or interpreted contents. A wider collective subject, its historical epistemic function and the full support of its judgement require further argument. This does not reduce the positive evidence to the bare fact that an official document exists. Ordinary reliable testimony can warrant that real inquiry occurred while leaving the stronger theoretical interpretation conditional.

### Testing delivered information

Greitemeyer and Schulz-Hardt’s second experiment compared prior misleading information and an initial choice with no initial information. Both conditions then received the same complete fictitious discussion protocol. Among 49 analysed participants, 5 of 26 versus 14 of 23 chose the designed optimal candidate. One assigned participant was excluded. The intervention bundles earlier exposure and choice; it does not isolate preference alone. Participants rated presented items, but the protocol was removed before recall and final decision. Decisions were individual, with fictitious partners. This supports a narrower delivery/evaluation distinction, not intact final access or collective evidence possession. \[E1\]

Replacing an information route and repairing interpretation are different interventions. Neither is guaranteed to address a failure whose cause lies in the other. A continuing organisation may need both, and evidence of historical delivery should not substitute for evidence of present usability. The proper assessment asks which support relation is at issue and at what time.

### Retained information and real group interaction

Mojzisch and Schulz-Hardt’s first experiment retained the complete external materials through individual choice. Their fourth used actual triads but removed sheets before discussion. Attribute-first hidden-profile groups reached the designed optimum in 6 of 15 cases, versus 1 of 15 with preference-first instructions. The small unadjusted contrast is test-sensitive; exchange content also changed and recall followed choice. Random allocation was not documented. These results support distinct operational findings, not one interacting-group experiment with the first experiment’s retained inputs. \[E2\]

Reimer and colleagues randomly assigned 112 triads while keeping each member’s own sheets available through deliberation and choice. Members could not read or exchange others’ sheets or take notes. Seventy groups succeeded and 42 failed; the naïve/common-cue condition achieved 28 of 28 successes. Cue commonality is different from information sharedness. The treatment varies prior processing, cues and time, not sheet access itself. This establishes retained distributed records within actual groups, not a fully public information set or a clean access-versus-use intervention. \[E4\]

A record can remain available to its original holder while another recipient still depends on that holder’s retrieval and communication. Preserving the first relation does not establish the second. Conversely, imperfect collective performance does not erase the genuine preservation of the original records. The relevant success and failure must be assigned to the relation actually tested.

Rothmund and colleagues varied whether a task was presented as demonstrably answerable or judgemental. Numerical inconsistencies elsewhere in this manuscript must not be silently repaired or attributed to the uninspected final publication. Changing someone’s representation of a criterion is not the same as changing the criterion or justifying its validity. The normative question must retain its own grounds, even when the psychological effect of an instruction is real.

Cordes reports post-trial inspection of shared documents for instructed attribute listing and organisation, alongside participant use checks. This warrants ordinary defeasible credit for realised instructed comprehensive pooling. It does not separately certify every recipient’s complete access at each choice. The measure called information sharing uses post-decision importance ratings. Unreconciled team-unit and accuracy reporting prevent verified team-rate claims; those limits do not erase the separate display-use evidence. \[E5\]

### Human and AI support configurations

After exclusions for AI technical faults, Zercher and colleagues report 19 of 28 correct choices with three humans plus fully informed AI, versus 7 of 25 human-only teams and 8 of 28 teams where profile-Z AI took one human’s place. The randomised packages differ in headcount, role, information overlap and intended recommendation. Realised exchanges are coded, but complete AI-set receipt is condition-specific: all 40 attributes in the centralised arm, only profile Z in the other. Exact hidden prompts remain uninspected; process reporting and Figure G1 contain unresolved discrepancies. The prose counts support a positive package-level result, not a uniquely identified mechanism. \[E6\]

A new channel can make an existing task fact accessible without supplying another independent observation of that fact. That does not make the experiment evidentially empty: observed behaviour supplies new evidence about the tested arrangement. Keep those targets separate. Different team configurations also do not constitute an observed transition of one persisting organisation; numerical identity and continuity require their own relation.

### A longitudinal handoff comparison

Summers, Humphrey and Ferris followed 108 teams through same-role member replacement and later simulation runs. Teams were randomly assigned to permit or prohibit the departing member’s notes and verbal handoff; continuing teammates could still help. The reported adjusted comparison favoured the permitted condition, with prior performance controlled; this is not proof of restoration to each team’s earlier level. Specific transferred knowledge and the newcomer’s later use were not measured. The transfer check concerns perceived opportunity, while performance is scored for the group. The role comparison also bundles responsibility, interface control and redundancy. This is actual later work within the same task family, without a tested novel problem type or further personnel transition. \[E7\]

## Conditions for organisational identity

Ritchie treats organised groups as realised social structures. Her 2013 continuity sketch concerns an ongoing realisation, not merely another instance. The 2020 account allows distinct groups with the same structure, normatively bound membership despite poor performance, and persistence through structural change. Its identity condition is generally necessary only; external relations may add requirements. A full diachronic account remains open, with origin, intentions and group-specific conditions as possible contributors. A historically connected, authorised replacement is therefore a plausible persistence case; an independently formed, equally competent copy need not be the predecessor. Common ancestry and intentions alone cannot identify two distinct descendants with one predecessor. An identity argument must establish the relevant group kind, continuing realisation and historical relations. Supported task continuation can still be credited without that stronger conclusion, and structural change alone does not defeat either claim. \[G6–G7\]

Fine models a changing group as a variable embodiment whose manifestations are structured wholes. His renewal example makes consent to this particular committee’s continuation relevant to its continued existence; consent to some matching committee is different. This supplies a positive account of identity through change, not an observation-only test. The particular-directed relation and appropriate constitutive conditions need evidence. His account also allows changing structure and distinguishes a group’s properties from those of its components. It does not certify group knowledge from a role chart or decide every split. \[G8\]

Epstein separates a kind’s formation, continued existence, constitution and identity, then asks what establishes those conditions. His approximate faculty-committee profile connects legitimate rotations to an origin, continued existence to institutional survival and nondissolution, and a sufficient identity test to the same formation act. The kind and stage premises do substantial work. A document describing a rule is not itself proof of valid enactment, and a shared meeting or file need not be one group-creating act. This provides a concrete conditional inquiry into constitutive history, not a universal identity certificate. Extra institutional powers and epistemic competence remain separate. \[G9\]

Tufts’ official 2026 bylaws distinguish initial committee elections, continuing terms and vacancy replacement. This corroborates rule-governed membership succession at that date. It does not independently establish the historical profile, a particular founding event or the proposed persistence biconditional. Current provisions cannot be backdated to 2017. \[G10\]

These accounts are alternatives, not three confirmations of any historical identity claim. The programme’s earlier branching control also remains decisive: two distinct supported successors cannot both be numerically identical to one predecessor. A unique successor does not settle identity either. The new primary applications specify what particular historical and constitutive evidence a stronger claim would need; they do not turn those inherited controls into new theorems.

## The baseline of a probability comparison

A strict increment has to be measured against a specified background. The original test here assumes an ordinary probability measure at one fixed time, after the factual evidence body E has been included. Crucially, every included item is assigned certainty. Factivity alone, meaning that the evidence is true, does not supply this certainty assumption.

$$
\forall{}e\in{}E,\ \ \ \ \ {P}_{E}(e)=1
$$

Under ordinary ratio conditioning in that same measure, an included item cannot increase any target’s probability. The intersection differs from the target only on a null event:

$$
{P}_{E}(p|e)=\frac{{P}_{E}(p\cap{}e)}{{P}_{E}(e)}={P}_{E}(p)
$$

If the target itself is included, conditioning on it again leaves its probability at one. This is a saturation result under explicit assumptions. It says nothing against the reality of available but unnoticed evidence, and it does not identify evidential probability with a person’s consciously held credence. A model that denies evidence-certainty or gives the conditional bar a different meaning must be assessed on its own terms.

The strongest ordinary-probability repair uses a background B that does not already contain the candidate’s evidential contribution:

$$
Q(p|B\cap{}e)>Q(p|B),\ \ \ \ \ Q(B\cap{}e)>0
$$

That convention can describe what a fact contributes before its inclusion, or relative to a suitable counterfactual background. It can also preserve the distinction between availability and psychological uptake. The mathematical issue is to specify the comparison, not to require a particular psychological history.

Three costs remain. A historical baseline needs an account of retained evidence after acquisition. Removing an expression from the current inventory may leave equivalent information elsewhere, so syntactic deletion is insufficient. A fixed earlier baseline can ignore later defeaters. None of these observations rules out a sound selection or contraction rule; they explain why the inequality alone does not supply one.

Redundant evidence makes the distinction especially clear. An item can remain part of a sufficient evidential basis while adding no further probability after another item has already settled the target. Replacing a strict inequality by a weak one would also admit irrelevant facts, so it is not a general repair. Standing support, possession and fresh incremental confirmation need their own stated relations. The result is a local requirement for explicit semantics, with no claim to refute an entire account or establish philosophical priority.

## Foundational warrant and the additional argument tests

### The positive starting point

The programme continues to credit reason-responsive correction, the explanatory role of an actual supplier where receipt is warranted, and original possession as a serious account of received efficacy. A particular productive contributor can matter without first proving one independently complete source of everything. Basic and explanatory warrant remain eligible; the present tests do not impose deduction as the only standard.

P1 still concerns productive completion, and P2 the particular bearer carried into the conclusion. N2 requires an exhaustive nonbrute account of that bearer’s existential adequacy; N3 requires intrinsic content excluding that same bearer’s absence. These are distinct burdens. Formal success conditional on an input does not establish the actual input, and failure to establish a contrary does not itself supply the positive existential content.

### Two constructive routes tested

Two further constructive attempts were tested. The first tried to locate original efficacy in the subject’s irreducibly own correction. It secured exercise ownership, but supplying another’s active power need not mean performing that other’s thought. The second tried to infer receptivity from the determinacy of a possible absence. It fixed the modal subject without establishing a receptive constituent. Neither attempt supplied the additional actuality or necessity warrant. Their failure to add a reason does not erase the inherited affirmative case.

### From possible absence to an eligible incompatible

Scotus’s fifth conclusion invokes contradictory exhaustiveness, an incompatible’s caused or of-itself status, and existential priority; the latter branches draw on the fourth conclusion. The new test grants their full atemporal exclusion while asking whether possible whole absence implies a possible incompatible eligible for those branches. Contradictory truth alone does not supply that causal or intrinsic qualification. \[F1\]

Here is the logical shape of the additional test. Let A mean that the fixed bearer is wholly absent, and let J mean that some incompatible eligible for the proposed exclusion obtains. Grant that an eligible incompatible is impossible. The desired conclusion follows if the qualifying modal implication is warranted:

$$
\diamond{}A\Rightarrow{}\diamond{}J,\ \ \ \ \ \neg{}\diamond{}J\ \ \ \ \ \Longrightarrow{}\ \ \ \ \ \neg{}\diamond{}A
$$

A negative content incompatible with existence is not automatically a content with the causal or intrinsic standing required by a further explanatory argument. This does not deny negative facts, require a distinct truthmaker for every alternative, or reduce necessity to resistance against a temporal destroyer. It identifies the scope of a particular implication. The later exclusion can have full atemporal force without expanding its own domain.

The qualifying implication may be supported basically or explanatorily. A case-specific recognition could suffice; a universal ontology of negatives is unnecessary. But the recognition must disclose the relevant content. Relabelling absence as a receptive defect would return to the earlier recipient-exhaustiveness burden. Directly recognising unconditional intrinsic actuality would provide N3 content and should be assessed at that strength. The new test supplies no additional actual P1, P2, N2 or N3 admission. It also supplies no established counterexample defeating the inherited affirmative.

### Truthful assurance and intrinsic integrity

Garcia treats lying as betrayal of an information-giving relation; his suggestion of self-degradation is tentative. The article supports an assurance-role account without deriving necessary sincerity from original sourcehood or essential knowledge. \[F2\]

The proposed additional bridge would say that intentional counterfeit assurance necessarily impairs the very source’s intrinsic possession or exercise of truth-understanding. That differs from classifying its action as a defective use of intact understanding. If the stronger relation were warranted with the same-source and essential scope, it could do the required work. This assessment has not supplied that relation.

The existing own-act and intrinsic-expression arguments remain available. Recasting them as an impairment claim does not generate a second independent reason. Nor does the present negative gate show a defective source possible or erase the positive original-perfection case.
