The live question is now more precise: how is the actual operative relation between a contentful ground and acceptance characterized, and when is it inferential use of these particular premises? It is no longer adequate to ask whether Dretske ever distinguishes available information from information actually used.

## A premise-independence test with an explicit target

Consider three different claims about a subject's present acceptance of Q:

- Some information-bearing feature is operative in producing acceptance of Q.
- Earlier contentful P/R states causally contributed to the episode.
- The subject inferred Q from P/R as its premises.

For this test let I(e,Q) mean that feature e has the relevant truth connection with Q, and U(e,Q) mean that e is the operative feature in producing the acceptance. These are interpreted philosophical assumptions, not numerical bits whose labels establish cognition. Let C(P,V) mean ordinary causal contribution from an earlier P-state to encountering a visual presentation V; its informative feature v is a separate relatum. Finally, B(P/R,Q) names the particular inferential-basing claim under investigation.

The schematic condition J(Q) = there exists e such that I(e,Q) and U(e,Q) is only an abstraction of the present-use requirement. It is not asserted to be a complete analysis of knowledge or a faithful biconditional reconstruction of every Dretske condition. In particular, a necessary condition cannot be treated as sufficient merely because it is satisfied.

The inherited attention case supplies a conditional witness. A meaningful P-state directs the subject to inspect a lamp. A clear visual presentation V then supports Q, that the lamp is lit, and its informative aspect v produces the subject's acceptance. The example explicitly grants v the relevant informational/counterfactual connection with Q; ordinary prima facie perceptual warrant alone is not silently substituted for that stronger premise. The subject does not employ P/R to interpret V or as additional grounds for Q. Another ordinary route to the same view would support Q equally well. Meanings and the relevant semantic histories are fixed; present processing differs from the inferential reference episode. We grant, rather than claim to manufacture, competent perception, suitable conditions and no relevant defeater.

Here J(Q) has witness v, the informative aspect of V. C(P,V) holds. Yet the specific claim B(P/R,Q) does not follow: P helped obtain the ground without being the inferential ground. The epistemic contrast comes from the described psychological route and independent support, not a programmed label. If P also shapes evidential interpretation or supplies an additional basis, this particular witness no longer applies. Redundant grounds must not be excluded by fiat.

The logical point is witness-specific: existence of an operative informative source does not identify a nominated upstream content as the premise used. Nothing here implies that the visual route is irrational or that Q lacks knowledge. Nor does causal dependence on P by itself turn the independent visual uptake into an inference from P/R.

## Why this is not another anti-Dretske countermodel

The test has changed targets. Its result is an assessment of an additional attribution, not a refutation of the inspected present-use condition. Indeed, a theory that locates the operative ground at V can get this case right. The source-specific advance is recognizing that the developed opponent has resources for that classification. Repeating the same case while ignoring those resources would not advance the inquiry.

No new executable toy is warranted. The witness itself is inherited; the existential-versus-particular distinction is elementary. Neither is credited as a new mathematical theorem, empirical result, or naturalistic construction of rationality. The new knowledge is the primary-grounded correction of the theory being tested.

## What remains unsettled

The existence of a defensible present-use constraint and a completed reductive account of that relation are different achievements. To establish the stronger account one would need a characterization of the operative relation that does not merely rename the intended basing verdict, together with a faithful treatment of changed background beliefs, independent evidence, redundant grounds, misleading grounds and bad but genuine inference. A verbal metabelief or an articulated rule is not imposed as universally necessary.

The selected works do not complete that task. This is a boundary of the inspected arguments, not evidence that the whole corpus cannot complete it. The question also differs from whether an ordinary subject may already know without possessing a theory of their own knowledge. Lack of our complete explanatory account is not a defeater of the subject's first-order warrant.

The remaining proper-function question is equally substantive. Truth of Q, meaningful representation, truth-connected uptake, rational inference and appropriate truth-directed functioning must be connected by arguments; they are not interchangeable names. A source's natural or historical origin alone does not prove every token warranted. Conversely, physical realization, learning or nonreflective operation alone does not defeat warrant.

## Limited comparison with the existing fitrah analysis

The inherited fitrah clarification distinguishes an occasion, an adequate warranting ground and a retrospective attribution. That role distinction survives this comparison. It does not identify a historical indicator function with the normative function of a proposed recognitional capacity.

A stronger bridge would have to establish the relevant content and actual object, the appropriate truth connection of this operation, sound functioning in its setting and applicable defeat conditions. If a recognitional claim requires acquaintance rather than a merely unique description, that bridge must also be defended. None is supplied by the abstract schema J or by the existence of ordinary perceptual knowledge.

The same caution applies in both directions: an incomplete reduction does not automatically defeat first-order recognition, and a common origin does not automatically authenticate every apparent recognition. No new independent verification or identified defeater of the retained T17 recognition appraisal is produced here. Creator, common-source and source-owned-veracity judgments are not automatically revised.
