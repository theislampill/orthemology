## Strongest affirmative source account

### Real efficacy in a wholly derived causal order

The preface attacks eliminating powers in fire, water, medicine and perception (e3–4). E25–26 makes effort, farming, learning, food and ordinary means genuinely integral to decree. E37–38 says the original giver supplies cause and effect, and can remove, oppose or bypass a particular created cause. E50–54 explicitly affirms real creaturely power, will and action, distinguishes creating from the created act, and rejects making human action merely metaphorical. E59 even refuses a convenient reading of the throwing verse that would erase the actual cast, distinguishing it from the further reach of the projectiles.

E79–80 treats instruction as a condition or part-cause whose effect requires completion and absence of impediments. E88 rejects the binary between independent production and idle conjunction: a received message can causally lead to different acquired responses. E98 will not make sheer absence a positive efficient producer. E143 itemizes external conditions of ordinary action, including objects, receptive subjects, healthy instruments, space, strength and unblocked interaction. It then condemns both independent human sufficiency and zero causal influence. E146–147 offers the constructive settlement: created power genuinely produces as a dependent cause, while that power, its conditions and its effect derive from divine creating. E178's general-will account further resists treating every droplet, breath or particular volition as necessarily demanding a separately individualised fresh special willing.

E206 explicitly affirms voluntary divine action through deposited natural powers, natures, dispositions, causes and effects. E233 describes many agents really acting, some serving as causes and ends for others, within an original ordering. E278 calls acquisition without influence unreal and requires an effect in producing the act. E305–306 distinguishes originating the receptive subject, preparing it, continuously sustaining it, and a locally sufficient cause versus a contributor requiring another factor. These distinctions cannot be erased by the strong exclusive-sovereignty language in e272–273.

### Complete sourcehood, actual wise exercise and self-sufficiency

The account is not merely that a deity retains unused capacity. E207–208 treats realizing a loved end at its appropriate time and actually creating as perfections. E219–221 argues that full kingship includes appropriately varied actual exercise and manifestation of already-perfect attributes. E220 directly addresses dependence: the conditions required for this kingship are themselves from the original source, rather than supplied by a peer or external completing provider.

The replies to Razi distinguish absence of a perfection that should already exist from absence of an effect whose fitting time has not yet arrived; intrinsic wisdom from externally received completion; and having/achieving a good purpose from purposeless capability. E221 adds that good intention with inability can deserve more praise than powerful purposeless action. Therefore an appraisal that equates every external result with illicit self-completion, or treats power alone as the entire perfection, loses the text's strongest positive account. Conversely, the text does not simply license every act that is logically possible: justice and wisdom constrain fitting exercise.

### Integrated order and common source

E231–234 is a genuine affirmative unity argument. It combines diverse products from shared materials, ordered mutual need, organ cooperation, human division of labour, continuity through replacement and agents serving wider ends they do not individually intend. It explicitly infers one original wise coordinator and one ultimate end. Its positive evidential force is organised coordination across heterogeneous contributions, not a mere observation of regularity. This supports common intelligent coordination over a disconnected accidental assemblage. The book also claims more: complete, unborrowed original provision and singular ultimate sovereignty.

## Strongest controls and remaining warrant burden

The distinction between a valid conditional argument, an author's substantive premise and sufficient philosophical warrant remains essential. E50 allows actual cooperation where neither agent can do the whole, alternation, and joint carrying where either could carry the whole alone. It denies each actually performing the same complete effect independently, because this kind of independence excludes the other's performance. E145 first voices a related argument through the Jabri interlocutor. The Sunni reply at e146 explicitly endorses the impossibility of one numerically identical effect between two independent complete producers. It is wrong to classify the whole cluster as opponent-only. The same reply simultaneously insists on real dependent creature causality. E150 goes further: the Sunni explicitly calls mutual-hindrance reasoning sound against intrinsically powerful coequals whose powers are not received from one another, even while faulting the interlocutors for failing to defend its premises. This strong peer-excluding source commitment must be preserved. The local passage asserts the soundness without here reproducing its full premise defense. E151 expressly permits speaking of a creature bringing an act into existence when the meaning is real dependent doing; the forbidden content is independent production, not a word alone.

These passages have strong content, but a contradiction from exclusive actual whole-production cannot silently be transferred to weaker nonexclusive double sufficiency. Nor do ordinary cooperating creatures instantiate coequal uncaused creators. The distinction prevents both overstating the refutation and treating a carrying example as proof of metaphysical overdetermination or coequal-original possibility.

Sufficient warrant here is defeasible, not limited to deduction from neutral axioms. Failure to deduce a conclusion does not by itself justify withholding it. The relevant unresolved comparative burden is more specific: why must a concordant, role-differentiated or nonexclusively sufficient ultimate source-order be deficient, by an independently warranted norm of complete divinity, rather than just unlike observed dependent creaturehood? E219–221 supplies substantive reasons favouring full original provision and wise manifested sovereignty. E232–233 adds a serious cumulative integration case. But e220's reply to external completion already assumes all relevant conditions originate from one, and its application to every qualified original requires justification. E219's argument for suitable manifestations of perfection does not by itself distribute complete independent provision of every product to every qualified original.

The common-source case is therefore strengthened as positive evidence, while the current withholding of sufficient common-source warrant is not removed by this reading alone. This is neither a refutation of the author's unity conclusion nor a demand that defeasible inference be deductive. It preserves the exact still-disputed source-mode classification. The necessary-being assessment is a distinct argument; this study neither silently overturns nor independently reproduces its sufficient-warrant acceptance. Likewise e200–201’s demand for wisdom/truthfulness beyond bare power when authenticating revelation is an important epistemic control, not by itself an argument deciding ontological common-source alternatives.

E306–307 adds a final-end argument from ineliminable willing, rejection of endless instrumental ends, and a determinate external object rather than an abstract universal. The further move from intended-for-itself to sole ultimate object is a substantive exclusivity premise. It may fit total ultimate worship, but concreteness alone does not establish it, and ordinary intrinsic goods cannot simply be treated as competing total deities. Universal fitra and perfection premises do real work in the intended argument.

A material textual limit remains at e232. The hypothetical 'if alongside Him were other gods/lords' proceeds directly to the worldly-kings comparison without its clear consequence. The same discontinuity occurs in raw003 HTML. No inaccessible scan or conjectural reconstruction supplies that step. The intact surrounding argument retains its affirmative force; a gap is neither erased nor used to discard everything around it.

## Whole-body material beyond the focal argument

The continuous notes retain the work's full range rather than only harvesting causal passages:

- The preface and thirty-chapter program; reported determinations before creation, in embryonic development, annually and daily; isnad criticism and alternative exegetical readings.
- Adam and Moses, repentance, practical effort, sleeping without negligence, gratitude, wealth and false assumptions of divine favour; qualifications concerning punishment before intelligible access.
- Philology of will, choosing, commanding, making, guidance, sealing and darkness; opponent dialogues; voluntary action, involuntary limb movement, coercion, intoxication, forgetfulness and legal intention.
- A long natural-history excursion on bees, ants, hoopoes, postal pigeons, animal care, geometry, predation, navigation, remedies and marvel narratives. These are uneven historical source assertions, not modern zoological or medical validation.
- Moral norms, wise preference, justice versus bare power, created dispositions, acquired habits, self-mediated loss of capacity, privation and positive causation, and serious evil objections.
- Prayer, purification, posture and repeated devotional formation; whole-surah explanation, social pressure, family obligations, migration, patience and reliance.
- A long examination of punishment's duration. Reported advocacy, favourable authorial assessment and the explicit inquiry limit at e264 remain distinguished. The source rejects several other annihilation/pleasure/fixed-duration theories; it cannot fairly be reduced either to detached citation only or to one unqualified final dogma.
- Distress-prayer, names and referents, epistemic humility, more than ninety-nine divine names, cosmic versus normative vocabulary, children, worldly legal status, fitra, transmitted disagreements and the concluding ultimate-end argument.

The author's strongest epistemic virtues include refusing convenient readings, scrutinizing grammar and transmission, acknowledging true premises held by adversaries, and demanding substantive reasons for classifying a property as perfection or defect. His polemical school descriptions, universal consensus claims, extraordinary narratives and particular natural-science assertions are not independently certified by reading them. The explanatory limit concerning created initial receptivity, withheld effectual aid and deserved punishment remains a real philosophical burden, not something to hide under the word 'wisdom'. E163 expressly limits the local task to refuting an opponent’s use of a verse rather than explaining the justice of the first misguidance; this is a source-internal distinction between defeating an objection and supplying a full account.
