# Bounded reflective rereads

Reader-facing study record, 7 October 2026. The interpretations and reading claims below belong to the recorded study and its bounded rereads. This edition of the notes adds no source-reading or proof credit.

All eight pages below were directly reopened as full-page images after completion of the sequential 253–398 first pass. They are not a second whole-section read. The page coordinates refer to the external scan identified below; the original study recorded separate image hashes for these rereads.

1. **295**. Question: does the author claim rukūʿ and sujūd are identical in all uses? No. The page explicitly distinguishes isolated from conjoined usage and compares faqīr/miskīn. The rule is contextual inclusion, not unrestricted synonymy.296 supplied the first-pass corroborating examples; it was not reopened in this reread.
2. **305**. Question: is the Banū Qurayẓa passage merely approving both contrary judgments? No. The author explicitly says he had used that account before but now sees a difficulty, and separates the correct party from the excused party. This supports self-reconsideration without proving a new general theory of plural correctness.
3. **335**. Question: can reward equivalence be treated as performance identity or permission? No. The explicit distinction is «فالثواب الذي يكتب بالنية غير الثواب المستحق بنفس العمل». Other evidence still determines valid posture and congregational requirements. The same reward does not entail the same action or rule.
4. **350**. Question: is all intention unnecessary? No. The scope is a **separate intention to join/shorten**, beyond the prayer itself, where legal time is already available. An intention-to-join cannot authorise an unavailable time. Thus one must not generalise this passage to the elimination of intention from worship.
5. **383**. Question: does two-dog uncertainty establish impossibility of causal cooperation? No. The passage addresses doubt about lawful slaughter and naming over the other dog. It neither denies joint killing nor supplies the wider philosophical study's independent-agent thesis. The author uses cause/condition/preventing-factor vocabulary legally.
6. **387**. Question: does presumed validity abolish the actual naming condition? No. Direct personal knowledge of each slaughterer's naming is not required, while known omission remains under the stated rule. A presumption about another person's acts is illustrated by market title/agency. This is an epistemic/ontic-condition distinction in a legal setting.
7. **389**. Question: is T14 fully preserved? No. The parenthetical transmitter explicitly omits arguments/responses and says further examples were cut for brevity and lack of time. This confirms abridgment; the missing original material is not recoverable by numbering alone.
8. **398**. Question: is the migration-sheep precedent independently certified sound by the edition? No. The modern note gives Ibn Ḥazm's *al-Muḥallā*8/403, ʿUrwa's report, and several defects. The broad permissibility argument also uses practice and other reports; this particular support stays qualified.

## Cross-topic appraisal

This reading supplies historical, devotional, semantic, ritual and commercial reasoning in its own terms. The useful links to the collection's broader causal/intentional study are restrained:

- Intention can underlie credited reward without identity of performed act (335); it cannot create a legal permission absent its conditions (350).
- Practical inability, blame, invalidity, excused conduct, preferred conduct and less complete but sufficient performance are repeatedly separated (264–267,304–305,329,333–335,354–360).
- The existence of a condition and the observer's knowledge of it are separate (387–388).
- A category's name and associated typical purpose do not automatically pick out its operative legal feature (329–330,351).
- Context can alter the extension of paired terms without collapsing their meanings (295–296).

These are comparative analytic leads, not a license to convert legal cases into new metaphysical proofs. No formal proof is established by these reading notes.
