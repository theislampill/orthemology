## Authorial argument and the strongest assessment

Ameri distinguishes personal epistemic standing from public persuasion at 65–69. The developmental account at 160–164 permits maturation and impediments. At 177–179 he rejects a bare consensus-to-truth inference and appeals instead to the standing of native capacities. His own common-origin transfer at 179 and universal parity claim at 183 are more ambitious than the predecessor's qualified entitlement conclusion. The health analogy at 182 raises the truth question explicitly; the reply invokes unavoidable basic reliance rather than a separate demonstration of this capacity's epistemic function. The counter-objection and qualifications remain part of the argument. [Official source](https://almobadarah.com/uploads/books/5f6ae0298582cce21002cbe4.pdf#page=178)

The strongest affirmative reconstruction is substantive. Requiring every basic cognitive seeming to be certified through an antecedent public proof generates a regress or reuses the capacities awaiting certification. A genuinely cognitive Creator-directed presentation should not lose all defeasible standing because it is immediate, natural or affectively engaged. A subject may know without possessing a complete theory of the knowledge's production. An explanation of belief formation therefore does not by itself undercut its truth-directed operation.

The strongest rival grants all of that. It asks whether the selected operation is a cognitive presentation of the specified actual Creator, an appropriately retained recognition, a practical orientation, or a different state later interpreted in recognitional terms. Shared origin does not entail shared epistemic role. One sound constitution can contain perception, reasoning, desires and protective dispositions with different success conditions. Even if a Creator originates all of them, the truth of a belief and the competence of its operative basis remain distinguishable. This is a local classification challenge, not global scepticism.

The affirmative reply is available: support the relevant content and functioning through a well-described case, appropriate testimony, competent actual use, or an independently warranted account of that capacity. A complete laboratory reliability theorem is not required. But the reply needs something beyond reasserting that the faculty is sound precisely where it agrees. Conversely, inventing a merely possible misleading route does not establish that an actual recognition follows it.

Thus the qualified entitlement result is a reasoned appraisal, not a verbatim restatement of the source's stronger universal transfer. Preserving this difference avoids both reducing Ameri to genealogy and crediting a bridge the argument has not independently established.
