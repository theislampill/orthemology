## The positive principle

Let e be a proposition, and let N(n) mean that n is an obtaining witness of its absence. Suppose, throughout the relevant possibilities:

- Either e holds or some n satisfies N(n).
- If e holds, r holds.
- Every n satisfying N(n) requires r.

Then r holds throughout those possibilities. In any case, either the first alternative supplies r, or an absence-witness does. This is a valid positive necessary-condition result. If r is also assumed capable of failing, those commitments are inconsistent. Removability needs independent warrant.

The conclusion depends on universal coverage of the second branch. Replacing the third premise with the claim that one particular witness n₀ requires r does not preserve the derivation. Failure of r excludes n₀, but the first premise permits a different witness. This is a quantifier distinction in the argument, not the construction of a rival cosmos or a stipulation that alternative truthmakers really exist.

## Strongest repairs

One repair is to identify a unique canonical absence fact and establish that all admissible absence-witnesses depend on it. Another is to derive a common condition independently for every eligible witness. A third is to replace the selected witness with an exhaustive disjunction and warrant the required dependence of that disjunction. A fourth is an independently justified world-construction that excludes every absence-witness while preserving the other premises. None is dismissed merely because it is conditional, nonempirical or metaphysically substantive.

Each repair must preserve its content. Introducing an exhaustive disjunction is not itself evidence that the disjunction requires the selected condition. Stating uniqueness does not prove it. Likewise, a sparse world description cannot exclude further witnesses just by omitting them if the case-coverage premise itself requires adding one. An exhaustive-removal argument would need a reason why such completion is unavailable. Conversely, a warranted account of the common basis would earn the positive necessity conclusion immediately; it need not first solve every question about a complete original source.

An exhaustive-deletion repair has a further positive argument. Suppose e is genuinely possible and every fact retained in the proposed world is either necessary or a necessary condition of e. No retained fact can itself entail not-e: otherwise e would imply its own negation. No necessary fact can entail not-e when e is possible either. Therefore, if that retained inventory really exhausts the obtaining facts, it contains no absence-witness. This route does not need one common requisite for all witnesses. Its burden is warrant for the exact sparse world and its exhaustive inventory, including the closure commitments already accepted.

The candidate's strongest attraction is therefore real. Exhaustive alternatives can constrain purported removability: one cannot consistently combine complete case coverage, a condition indispensable in every case, and that condition's possible absence. An opponent must identify which substantive claim is rejected. Merely observing that assumptions are involved does not answer this argument.

## What the programme could credit

A necessary condition is not yet an actual productive supplier. It may constrain what any admissible situation contains without explaining the existential availability used by its productive channels. The Tenth E2/E3 distinction remains controlling: adequate existential supply concerns that availability, not simply a route to proving the existential proposition true.

If a separately justified condition entailed the existence of some concrete bearer in every relevant possibility, there would be a bounded modal gain. But that is still distinct from one particular bearer existing in every possibility. It also does not by itself assign actual productive exercise to any bearer. Moving from a conditional fact to a concrete participant requires justified existential and participation content; moving to P1/P2 requires the preserved complete productive target.

The accepted Eleventh assembly/accounting results remain unchanged. Grouping sufficient conditions does not identify their ownership or original provision. A necessary fact that some plurality satisfies a condition does not convert that plurality into a singular complete source. These are inherited programme boundaries, not fresh conclusions credited to this assessment.
