# Selected Shifa control on the sourcehood appraisal

Publication scope note: Historical independent source-mode reassessment and its dated Shifa controls. The principal appraisal predates the controls; read the two supplements in sequence. This is not a final after-all-books verdict. Nubuwwat/Safadiyya evidence and the final report remain outside this packet.

7 October 2026. Supplement to [ASSESSMENT.md](ASSESSMENT.md) after direct inspection of acquired Shifāʾ al-ʿalīl electronic labels e50–54, e103–104, e205–207 and e304–307. These are fourteen electronic labelled pages of the received body, not visually certified printed pages. The host’s print-edition identity is unresolved. The larger continuous study was still underway when this supplement was written.

## Result

These passages strengthen the distinction on which my current withholding rests, while also strengthening the source’s positive account of comprehensive divine action. They do not by themselves change my sufficient-warrant verdict. They rule out an overbroad attribution: Ibn al-Qayyim does not argue here that having sufficient ability to do the whole prevents actual cooperation.

## Cooperation and what the contradiction excludes

Electronic e50 explicitly distinguishes four cases:

1. Cooperators who cannot individually perform the whole.
2. Alternative performance by either of two agents able to perform it.
3. Actual cooperation although each agent has the ability to perform the whole alone, illustrated by two carrying a load each could carry alone.
4. Both agents actually performing the effect independently, each in the sense excluding the other’s production.

The first three are described as possible and actual. Only the last is rejected, because each agent’s independence negates the other’s doing it. The allowed carrying example is particularly discriminating: a merely partial standing capacity cannot explain why this cooperation is acceptable. Possessed sufficient capacity and actual exclusive production differ in the source’s own statement.

This does not establish that each cooperating carrier’s actual exercise, as performed, is by itself sufficient for the whole. Each may be using only part of its ability. Nor does it establish that actually redundant, independently sufficient nonexclusive causes of one numerical effect are metaphysically possible. The text’s explicit contrast is between capacity for independent performance and actual production defined as independent of the other’s act. A general denial of all overlapping or counterfactually sufficient causes would require a further argument.

Consequently, a modern causal-nonduplication premise must preserve its exact scope. If “entire original provision” means actual unborrowed whole production incompatible with another irreducible original share, the source offers the narrow contradiction. If it means only possessing enough power, or being causally sufficient under a nonexclusive description, this paragraph does not establish numerical uniqueness of providers. A formal predicate’s name cannot decide between those meanings.

The agents in the permitted examples are creatures. The passage therefore does not demonstrate that coequal necessary originals may cooperate. It places the burden exactly on the additional original-source qualification: why does the mode that is coherent for two independently capable created actors become intrinsically defective for a qualified original? The fuller independence/perfection argument remains relevant and cannot be defeated by citing the load example alone.

## Distinct divine creation and creaturely action

At e51–54 the author rejects both erasure of creaturely action and exclusion of creaturely acts from divine creative power. The creature genuinely performs its action; divine production of the creature and its action is a different relation and has a different bearer. The same event’s being the creature’s action does not exclude its being God’s created product.

This is positive support for a typed common-source account allowing derivative real agency. It also prevents applying a one-source principle indiscriminately across every description. Numerical identity of the event is not enough: the kind of attribution and whether it concerns intrinsic doing, creating, or original existential provision matter.

The argument explicitly affirms universal divine creation and power. That affirmation is not neutral between the disputed theories; it is part of the position being defended. The point of this selected control is not to turn Ibn al-Qayyim into a plural-source theorist. It is to keep his actual argument, including its limits, intact.

## Two kinds of capacity

At e104 the text separates enabling capacity, bodily means and preparatory conditions from the complete capacity concurrent with an act that entails it. The former can be present while the act does not occur; the latter cannot. The discussion is creature-directed and framed by divine decree, but its analytical distinction is explicit.

This confirms why an argument cannot move without qualification from standing ability, to complete actual determination, to all-incoming original provision of a composite. An inability relative to the fully fixed actual determination is not always a denial of the enabling capacity relevant in another respect. The text’s own distinctions are richer than one universal binary “can” predicate.

At e103–104 it also distinguishes internal and external created causes while placing both under divine willing. The cause’s being external to the creature does not make it external to the proposed universal original source. Conversely, using that universal-source account here does not independently establish it for an assessor who is still examining that scope.

## Wise action and the exact deficiency question

Electronic e205–207 rejects the supposed choice between a voluntary creator and real causes, powers, purposes and wisdom. It defends both, including intrinsic wisdom and temporally fitting effects. E207 distinguishes a missing perfection that ought already to be present from a good whose absence is fitting before its proper time and whose presence is fitting at that time.

More importantly, the reply explicitly says that agreement on excluding deficiency does not settle whether the disputed property is a deficiency. Calling it one leaves the very question at issue. It then argues positively that wisdom and fitting attainment of what is loved are perfections, rather than leaving the matter at a verbal disagreement.

This is methodologically exact for the present inquiry. The source-mode classification must likewise be supported positively; a generic pure-perfection premise cannot by itself decide whether voluntary partial original provision is an intrinsic defect. The source has supplied a positive comparison elsewhere, and my assessment credits it. The remaining issue is its all-relevant-exercise scope.

E207’s reply to external completion says that nothing outside God gives him perfection and that the world receives its perfection from him. It distinguishes God’s intrinsic wisdom from an externally supplied qualification. This provides a coherent defence of wise action under comprehensive creatorhood. At this location it invokes, rather than independently derives, the absence of another creator. It would be circular to cite that particular reply as an independent proof that there is no other original creator.

## The final cause and unique ultimate beloved argument

Electronic e304–307 offers an additional affirmative route through innate orientation, knowledge, love and final causation. It does not treat all knowledge as explicitly present at birth: e304–305 distinguishes a disposition, its gradual development, suitable assistance, and impediments. Its claims about the actual universality and reliability of this orientation remain broader than this selected textual inspection authenticates.

The teleological argument holds that instrumental willing requires an ultimate end and that the ultimate object must be an actual determinate reality rather than a merely common universal. At e306 it excludes two objects each intended for itself by interpreting the ultimate status as each being the sole object intended for its particular self. E307 identifies that final object with the maker of heaven and earth.

The strongest reading is a demand for one unconditionally ultimate object organising the entire order of love and worship, not a ban on enjoying several finite goods. Such undivided ultimacy is a substantive conception with possible philosophical warrant. It should receive assessment at that strength.

It does not follow merely from the weaker claim that some ends are noninstrumental, or from a ban on an endless chain of instrumental goals. Several terminal intrinsic goods and a single exclusively ultimate object are different proposals. Likewise, that the real target must be determinate does not alone establish that all ultimate reasons must terminate in one numerical bearer. The inference needs the exclusivity/unification qualification, just as strict supremacy requires more than weak maximality. I do not find this short closing passage independently establishes that qualification at the standard needed for the source-mode result.

## Textual discipline and scope

The electronic e50 and e54 passages contain apparent transcription defects. In particular, damaged introductory attributions and suspect negatives must not be silently repaired. The four-case cooperation distinction and the sustained act/product contrast remain directly readable in their continuous context. I retain the acquired witness unchanged and make no claim to a collated original reading.

The e306 discussion also contains an intrusive parenthetic marker and a damaged transition. I do not make a decisive inference from that broken clause. The exclusivity statement before it and the concluding identification after it are present in the received text.
