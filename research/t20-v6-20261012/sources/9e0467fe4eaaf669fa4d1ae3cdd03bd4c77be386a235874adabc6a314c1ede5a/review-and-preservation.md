## 1. Basis and scope

This assessment accompanies the fourth delivery of *Information, Composition, and Self-Revision: Local Descriptions and Operative Access*. The research manuscript carries no version label. The controlling baseline is the actual supplied eleven-page third delivery and its LaTeX source, not the reviewers' summaries. Both current reviews were read as advice to evaluate, not independent certification. Their authors' identities, independence and claimed review procedures have not been established.

The revision retains the information-first organisation, the two explicit parity relations at the opening, all eight numbered results and their complete proofs, the general transition/conservation toolkit, the original reconnection and destructive-experiment examples, and the chemical, quantum and interpretive boundaries. The finalised *The Cost of a Decisive Revision* is unchanged. It is a companion, not a target of revision or independent corroboration.

The new material is a worked version-bound workflow and a more explicit admission interface. These are applications and clarifications of existing results, not claims to new general theorems. The paper remains one manuscript. No proof has been moved to this assessment to reduce the research paper's page count.

## 2. What the reviews support—and what they do not

### The table was not missing

Reviewer 1 says the earlier table remains gone. The exact baseline has a four-row distinctions-based table in Section 7, on page 10. Reviewer 2 accurately describes it. Restoring the old five-topic table would reverse a deliberate structural improvement: physical examples are boundaries and illustrations, not peer stages of a universal pipeline.

The current table is retained. Its last row now cites the reconnection and admission sections and the explicit admission equation. This accepts the useful navigation correction without recreating a nonexistent omission.

### Name the admission record

Accepted. The baseline used “the observed record fibre” without locally naming its map. The revision introduces a fixed proposed revision, a nonempty situation space, the predicate `h`, the actual record map `lambda_adm`, and an observed value in its image. The positive-admission condition is written as containment of that nonempty fibre in the true set of `h`.

A further distinction is made explicit: a positive verdict on one fibre does **not** require decidability on every fibre of the record map. The introductory factorisation criterion characterises global determination; admission at an observed record uses its local positive-verdict case. A three-situation counterexample in the new checker separates these requirements.

For a successor decision, the relevant record map may also change. The revision does not silently identify the successor's record with the predecessor's. Changed meanings require justified transfer. A correct answer about readiness does not by itself decide an admission question that additionally depends on permission.

### The infinite-domain connection

Accepted as motivation, qualified as necessity. The factorisation argument already works on arbitrary nonempty sets. Its earlier finite-domain form was correct but unnecessarily restrictive; dropping the restriction was not the repair of a false theorem.

The paper now explicitly connects the criterion to situations involving method descriptions of unbounded finite length, as well as potentially infinite families of probability laws over finite configurations. Neither review establishes that a particular admission problem must have an infinite domain. The new example is a legitimate possible use, not a recovered authorial intention or proof that the generalisation was indispensable.

There is still no effective decision procedure for arbitrary method descriptions. The finite operational theorems keep their exact-observation and finite-state assumptions.

### A worked method composition

Accepted in bounded form. A larger named-project example would require actual implementation, source and operational evidence. Neither review supplies that evidence. The revision therefore does not invent a Lean/Agile/Waterfall case study, claim current IMPLEMENTAUDIT behaviour, or identify a few interfaces with a complete academic tradition.

The added example has named mechanisms and explicit meanings: dispatch two qualified independent jobs, record an interrupt, and accept only against version-bound evidence at a protected boundary. Its candidate identifiers and verification records make the failure materially interpretable rather than merely renaming the parity bit. It applies the existing relational furtherness criterion, information criterion, observation-congruence theorem and admission test to the same workflow.

The dispatcher does not prove noninterference merely by calling jobs independent. The evidence gate does not prove receipt accuracy by reading an identifier. The invariant is not an eventual-completion or speedup theorem. Those limits are in the manuscript, not confined here.

Reviewer 1's “none of the three decides alone” is also too broad if read without the disconnected-state qualification: an already connected reader can reveal the answer. The existing example's claim remains conditional on its interfaces and initial setting.

### Publication, ranking and scope

“First version I would hand someone,” “ship,” and the version rankings are reader judgments, not evidence of acceptance, priority or measured comprehension. The reviews support keeping the present architecture; they do not authorise publishing to an external repository or venue.

The finite, discrete, exact-observation control model is a declared scope, not a newly discovered defect. The probability families themselves need not be finite, and the introductory criterion is set-theoretic. A noisy, continuous-state or stochastic-control extension would need new assumptions and arguments; none is inserted to answer a rhetorical complaint.

The manuscript's acknowledgement of established antecedents remains. Neither repeated praise nor a correct application establishes historical originality or predicted impact.

## 3. The new workflow: proof and adversarial assessment

### A joint invariant with exact local preservation

There are two immutable candidate identities, three possible values for each job receipt (missing, candidate alpha, candidate beta), two interrupt states and two possible verdicts. The complete boundary-record product therefore has 72 elements.

The guarded relation permits any open verdict, and permits a complete verdict only with no interrupt and both receipts bound to the current candidate. It contains 38 records: 36 open records and two complete records. Each of the five singleton record projections equals the corresponding full product projection. For the input coordinates, an open verdict supplies a completion; the complete verdict is realised with matching receipts and no interrupt.

A complete verdict for candidate beta with two alpha receipts is in the product join but excluded by the guarded relation. This is an explicit witness for the further-invariant criterion. The additional property comes from the joint acceptance guard, not from the isolated record ranges.

This calculation concerns the declared **boundary-record** scopes. It is not a proof that every temporal property of every source operation survives composition, nor that all 38 records are reachable from one particular initial workflow. A concrete implementation needs its own reachability and interface proof.

### A missing distinction and a reconstructible interface

The coarse report retains the interrupt flag and whether each receipt is present, but forgets candidate bindings. It has eight record fibres over 36 input situations; one fibre mixes readiness values. Stale and current receipts can both appear as “two checks passed.” The binding-aware report distinguishes them.

The connector changes only the observer setting and leaves the candidates, receipts and initial target unchanged. The resulting 72-state method/environment construction decides readiness after the binding-aware read, and its selected witness violates observation congruence exactly as required. An acknowledgement that the connection was made leaves the readiness ambiguity unresolved.

This is actualisation of access through an inherited operation, not information manufactured by a new label or an expansion beyond the predecessor's entire reachable repertoire. It specifies an operative route; it does not certify a deployed interface.

### Where an attractive completion argument fails

The new checks include four faulty acceptance rules: count receipts instead of matching bindings; ignore the interrupt; ignore the second job; or match the two receipts without checking the current candidate. Every rule accepts at least one bad boundary record.

The protected acceptance boundary is essential. Start with two current receipts and no interrupt, evaluate the guard, then let the interrupt arrive before the completion effect. The previously true guard no longer establishes the current invariant. The example therefore requires a stable snapshot through the effect or an equivalent atomic validation; it does not infer that mechanism from a green read.

Independent receipt-register updates commute in the specified model. Both job orders permit completion when otherwise ready; every permutation containing a prior interrupt blocks it. This checks the defined register semantics, not general scheduling optimality or actual parallel elapsed time.

Changing the current candidate to make old receipts look current answers a different question. A counterexample preserves the difference between an initial-state decision and a post-mutation decision. Merely remaining open forever satisfies the boundary safety invariant, so no eventual-completion claim follows.

### Admission remains another question

With other admission conditions held fixed, readiness may be conjoined with a separate permission bit. Two situations with identical complete readiness records but opposite permission values defeat admission from readiness alone. The new checker enumerates all 72 such readiness/permission situations.

This does not make a token authoritative by definition in an implementation. Justified record meanings and the relevant actual situation's membership in the model remain premises. No successful finite check establishes those premises outside its model.

## 5. Verification and comparative judgment

Five inherited mathematical checkers were retained byte-for-byte and rerun: the relational, reflexive, integrated, second-delivery and third-delivery suites. A sixth, new checker covers the workflow, access and admission cases described above. Results and execution identities accompany the source. The inherited large relational census, exact symbolic checks, linear-programming diagnostics and finite-decision differential tests remain separate evidence classes; floating-point optimisation is not relabelled as exact arithmetic.

The paper uses the baseline 11-point type, A4 page and 0.85-inch margins. It is twelve pages rather than eleven. The added workflow and explicit admission interface earn that extra page for the requested combined purpose by making the relationship among previously separate criteria executable and inspectable. This is an author-reviewed editorial judgment, not a proof of minimum length, universal superiority or measured reader benefit.

The supported comparative claim is narrow: the revision preserves the supplied argument and corrects two navigation/record-identity omissions, while adding a single cross-result application with explicit counterexamples and limits. It is not a larger general theorem, a field experiment or a publication guarantee. Public-document identity, a clean build and an intact archive remain distinct from mathematical truth, operative validity and external impact.
