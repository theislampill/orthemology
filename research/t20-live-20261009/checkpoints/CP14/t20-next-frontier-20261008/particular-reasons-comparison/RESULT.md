# Particular reason-basing and complete determination

9 October 2026. One bounded comparison of the retained Pruss and Ibn Taymiyyah sources. Contextual rereading earns no new first-reading credit. No new literature retrieval, code model, repository change, historical-time certification, final integration, or T20 closure.

## Result

**A scope mismatch removes the alleged direct contradiction between Pruss's nonnecessitating reason-explanation and Ibn Taymiyyah's efficacious particular willing. It does not make Pruss's explanation a complete determining basis in Ibn Taymiyyah's sense.**

There are two exact findings.

1. **The downstream disagreement disappears.** Pruss's necessarily obtaining explanans concerns choosing what to create while appreciating a reason. It is not the proposition that God has already particularly and effectively willed this very created outcome. Ibn Taymiyyah's entailment from actual particular willing to its effect does not entail that the prior appreciation of reasons fixes the particular willing. Treating these propositions as identical changes the target and the stage.
2. **The upstream transport fails, without refuting Pruss.** Fix the target as the particular actual reason-basing or willing, rather than its produced effect. Pruss explicitly claims to explain this target too, and permits a necessary reason-appreciation state to explain it nonnecessarily. Ibn Taymiyyah's demand is stronger: a possible occurrence does not obtain without a complete particular determiner, and generic will does not explain its particular specification. Thus Pruss's necessary q cannot itself be that complete determining basis for a contingent target. The incompatible package is necessary q + contingent particular target + identification of q with an entailing complete basis. The identification is an unwarranted translation, not a claim both authors have independently endorsed.

**The retained passages do not establish that Pruss denies every complete, internally particular determining basis, or that Ibn Taymiyyah denies every nonentailing reason-explanation. Consequently they do not yield an unqualified incompatible pair at one fixed explanatory relation.** They yield a definite failure of the proposed identification and a narrower philosophical dispute about which kind of explanation is adequate to the purpose. The source's stronger requirement remains in force within its own programme; the later Pruss proposal has not discharged it merely by providing an explanation that satisfies his PSR.

## 1. Fix the target before comparing explanations

The following are logical/explanatory distinctions, not a claim that God deliberates through four successive psychological episodes:

- **Appreciation:** the agent knows or is impressed by reasons S favouring A and reasons T favouring B. The general capacity to act belongs here too, but knowing, valuing, and being able are themselves distinct.
- **Actual basing:** the agent acts on S in making this choice. S can remain appreciated when the agent instead acts on T. This is the contrast between a consideration being available and its being operative in this action.
- **Particular willing:** the determinate exercise directed to A. Actual basing and particular willing may be aspects of one act, rather than two intervening events. Calling them distinct targets does not invent a further volition that chooses a volition.
- **Completed production:** the effect A is brought about through the actual willing and effective power.

Let D denote the actual particular selection of A on S; let P denote the produced outcome. If acting on S and willing A are not identical, the comparison must say which of them D denotes. The central result holds for either contingent particular selection target, without counting both as separate causes.

Let q_S denote Pruss's proposition concerning the agent's making a choice while appreciating S. It does **not** specify that this choice is A or that S is actually its operative basis. Under his preferred divine account q_S is necessary, while D and P are contingent. q_T also holds; explanatory relevance, rather than the sheer truth of either appreciation report, differs between the actual and alternative cases.

Two frequently conflated claims can therefore both be true:

- q_S provides a nonnecessitating reason-explanation of D.
- The complete actual willing directed to P is efficacious: P follows from that willing with its complete productive basis.

The second claim does not turn q_S into the complete willing. Nor does the first require that the same complete particular willing produce both P and not-P. No alternative under an unchanged complete actual declaration is needed to establish a standing capacity for alternatives.

## 2. What Ibn Taymiyyah's retained passages actually require

### Capacity is not actual complete efficacy

Majmu 8, item805 L294–298 distinguishes antecedent capacity, which makes action possible and can belong to someone who will not perform it, from capacity contemporaneous with and entailing the act. L305 then distinguishes an incomplete condition or cause from a complete one. Its relevant wording is:

> وإن أريد بالقدرة القدرة المقارنة للفعل المستلزمة له فتلك علة للفعل وسبب تام

Here the capacity accompanying and entailing the act is described as its cause and complete cause. L305–309 immediately deny that an isolated created cause is sufficient independently of additional conditions, other causes, and the absence of obstacles. This is neither a claim that every ordinary causal explanation must state every condition nor a claim that an agent's standing repertoire already is its completed exercise. [Primary item805](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/805/)

Bayan 5, item960 L001 asks whether what is required means that at whose presence the effect must exist, or the standing ability and other attributes by which the agent can act. Item961 L001 then challenges the inference from being complete in oneself and able to act if one wills to the eternal occurrence of the act. Its particular question is:

> فلم قلتم: إنه إذا كان كاملا في نفسه قادرا على الفعل بحيث لا يعجز عنه إذا أراده أنه يجب صدور الفعل عنه؟

The challenge blocks necessary standing perfection → necessary fixed act. It does not deny efficacy once the relevant actual willing is specified. [Item960](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/415/960/), [item961](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/415/961/)

### The demand also reaches particular actualisation; it is not only an execution rule

Majmu8 L311 reports theologians who let an unchanged will, equally related to events and times, specify a particular one without a newly occurring particularising cause. L312 explicitly endorses the complete-determiner principle and criticises the inconsistency of using it for created acts while exempting divine action. The decisive authorial clause is:

> فإن القول بأن الممكن لا يترجح وجوده على عدمه إلا بمرجح تام أمر معلوم بالفطرة الضرورية لا يمكن القدح فيه وهو عام لا تخصيص فيه

This says that a possible thing's existence does not prevail over its nonexistence except through a complete determiner, and presents the principle as general and without exemption. The immediately preceding argument says the created act must occur when its complete originating determiner obtains. The phrase requiring a determiner **other than the human servant** belongs to that created-act argument; it cannot be universalised into an external cause of God's willing.

The authorial paragraph warrants the inherited stronger demand. It cannot be reduced to the truism that, after willing has already occurred, its intended effect follows. Particular willing or its actualisation is not exempted merely by being called voluntary.

Majmu8 L341–344 supplies a more specific **reported dialectic**. At L342 the opponents of the equally related eternal-will account distinguish will's generic specification function from specifying this particular alternative. Their operative phrase is:

> وأما تخصيص هذا المعين على هذا المعين فليس منه لوازم الإرادة بل لا بد من سبب يوجب اختصاص أحدهما بالإرادة دون الآخر

The passage asks for a cause making one particular alternative the object of will. It is not an unmarked direct declaration by Ibn Taymiyyah at every sentence. Its direction is supported by his explicit argument at L312. The reported equality premise concerns equality in **all relevant respects**, not equality on a numerical value score, a coarse reason summary, or one practical goal. Pruss's S and T are already different option-favouring reasons; they are not the equal-bread premise simply by both being appreciated.

### Equal bread is the opponent's reply, not the adopted stopping explanation

Bayan item961 L002 introduces al-Razi and others' response as founded on an invalid analogy. L003 supplies the equally placed paths and loaves; L004 reports the view that specification is intrinsic to will and needs no further explanation. Item962 L001 and L004–007 are the author's correction.

Item962 L006 says created voluntary agents require a determinate decisive will. L007 contrasts God's unreceived willing:

> بل هو يستقل بالفعل بإرادته التي لم يستفدها من غيره، ولا يضطر إلى الفعل، فلا فعله، ولا نفس إرادته تضطره

God acts independently by a will not obtained from another; his act and will do not force him. The following discussion rejects an assisting peer and forced output. The sound concluding sentences of L009 locate the necessity or impossibility of effects relative to God's willing or not willing. They do not turn his self-existent standing nature into one eternally fixed complete act. [Author's correction, item962](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/415/962/)

The inherited suspect electronic strings in item962 L002 and the opening/middle argument of L009 are not used as premises. There is no silent emendation or fresh scan collation.

### Successive unreceived acts remain available

Majmu8 L332–340 distinguishes what God supplies by his own power and will from completion borrowed from another. It also denies that an act's absence before its wise time is automatically a deficiency. L350–356 distinguishes regress in originating causes from succession of conditions and effects; L353–356 reports the position allowing ongoing acts related to will and power. Bayan item963 L001–005 rejects the inference that the complete efficacy relevant to successive events must have been one frozen whole eternally co-present with every effect. [Item963](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/415/963/)

These controls prevent an inference from necessary standing perfection to the necessity of every particular act. They do not themselves exhibit the particular complete basis of any proposed original-peer act.

The inherited Akmaliyya control reinforces both sides of the limit: Majmu6 item591 L495–500 distinguishes wise discrimination from unrestricted preference and from mere efficacy; L489–490 denies that ignorance of a particular wisdom refutes established general wisdom. A missing supplied reason is a missing warrant in this comparison, not proof that no reason exists. [Primary item591](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/591/)

## 3. Pruss's strongest account is not reasonless choice

In §2.3.2.3, Pruss argues that removing an external controller without substituting positive internal agency does not produce freedom. The preferred proposal is that the agent acts on reasons S favouring A, although reasons T favouring B are also appreciated. Had B been chosen, T would have been operative while S remained appreciated. The reasons have independent content, exemplified by competing considerations in choosing a graduate school; they are not just an outcome code renamed as a reason. The claim that this particular action was based on S remains substantive and cannot be inferred simply from S's presence.

Pruss distinguishes the explanans used in each case. He resists conjoining unused considerations into an explanation merely because they are true. This is an explanatory-relevance rule, not a claim that S disappears in the alternative world. His backup permits even the same circumstances to explain incompatible outcomes on different occasions. The primary proposal must be evaluated before resorting to that backup. [Author-hosted chapter, §2.3.2.3](http://alexanderpruss.com/papers/LCA.html)

The stronger reading also includes three safeguards:

- **He answers the reason-basing question.** Section 4.1.1.6 raises the contingency of q's explaining p, identifies the question with why God acted on the operative reason, and proposes the appreciation of that reason as the explanation again. It is inaccurate to report that he never considers why S became action-guiding. His answer is nonnecessitating; its adequacy is the issue.
- **He does not demand external determination.** Note 11 permits internal determination to be compatible with the freedom of an agent whose existence has no cause. His generic claim about libertarian indeterminism therefore cannot be used, without qualification, to attribute a denial of every internal divine determining basis.
- **His PSR does not promise causal determination.** Section 2.3.2.2 explicitly distinguishes explanatory sufficiency from entailment; §2.3.2.3 ends by allowing that this kind of explanation may be inferior to deterministic explanation while still sufficient for his cosmological purpose. That is a declared limitation, not a concealed reasonless gap.

The noncontrastive fallback in §2.3.5 is also exact. A PSR requiring an explanation of each contingent proposition need not require an explanation with each relevant alternative as a contrast. Explaining a proposition equivalent to P-and-not-Q is not thereby explaining why P rather than Q in the stronger contrast-sensitive sense. However, the preferred S/T proposal does aim to answer contrastive choice questions. The fallback must not replace it prematurely or be reported as his only answer.

Note 11 is a compatibility qualification, not an assertion that God's particular creation is internally determined. Likewise, the intelligibility of the graduate-school example does not exhibit God's actual S. Pruss expressly leaves most of those divine reasons unknown. Both this limitation and the Akmaliyya ignorance control prevent moving from an unexhibited particular reason to a metaphysical verdict of reasonlessness.

## 4. The discriminating comparison at one fixed upstream target

Fix D as this actual choice of A on S. Do not switch to the general capacity to choose, to the existence of the agent, or to the final created effect.

**Pruss's claim:** q_S can explain D while q_S is compatible with D's nonoccurrence. In the divine application q_S is necessary. q_S can also explain the actual fact that it explains D. The obtaining of the explanatory relation is contingent; necessitating the explanans does not necessitate the relation.

**Ibn Taymiyyah's stronger requirement:** D's actual particularisation has a complete determining basis. The generic function of will, or an unchanged description equally compatible with either actualisation, is not by itself that complete basis. An adequate application must preserve the difference between the standing antecedents and their particular actual completion.

The elementary logical discriminator is important here:

- Requiring that every actual possible occurrence have **some complete entailing basis** does not say that **every explanation** of it entails it.
- Permitting **some nonentailing explanation** of an occurrence does not deny that it has **some other complete determining basis**.

Thus the argument “Pruss's q does not entail D; Ibn Taymiyyah requires a complete basis for D; therefore Pruss contradicts Ibn Taymiyyah” has a missing premise. It must establish either that q is being offered as that very complete basis, or that Pruss excludes any such basis. The retained preferred account supplies neither identification. Calling q an ultimate explanation for Pruss's stated purpose does not silently change his expressly weaker explanatory standard into the source's complete causal one.

Conversely, the argument “Pruss explains D; therefore the source's complete-basis demand has been met” also fails. If q_S is necessary and D contingent, q_S cannot be a basis that entails D. This is a genuine failed transport, not merely missing vocabulary. Adding the fact that q_S actually explains D can make the enlarged factive proposition entail D, but that move has not independently shown what particularises the explanation relation. Pruss answers that question with q_S again under the same nonentailing relation. An occurrence-labelled or factive composite does not acquire new complete-basis warrant merely by its logical form.

This does **not** prove that his answer is viciously circular. A contingent explanatory relation explained nonnecessarily by its explanans is not a formal contradiction. Nor does it prove that the Ibn Taymiyyah programme is false because its demand is stronger. It locates exactly what an argument would have to defend.

### The strongest challenge on each side

For Ibn Taymiyyah's programme, the best challenge is not “Why are there two reasonable options?” It is: **has the particular operative basing been adequately grounded, or has a relevant standing consideration been offered where complete actual determination is required?** The source's L312 principle and L342 dialectic keep that question live. No unique optimizer, external controller, or reasonless-choice accusation is needed.

For Pruss, the best reply is not simply “I freely chose it.” It is: **S really favours A and really was the reason acted on; explanatory relevance can vary with the actual exercise without the standing reason's truth varying; universal explanatory coverage need not promise a necessitating or universally contrastive explanation.** His taxicab answer applies this reply to operative basing itself.

Neither reply alone decides whether the complete-determiner standard is obligatory for every ultimate explanation. Attributing to either author the other's meaning of adequacy would decide the dispute by stipulation. The present source evidence therefore establishes a scope mismatch and a failed bridge, rather than an unrestricted incompatibility theorem or a positive reconciliation of the full metaphysics.

## 5. What this changes for T20

1. Reject an objection to Pruss based solely on the necessity of q and contingency of the result. It confuses explanatory factivity with explanatory necessitation, or reason-appreciation with fully specified willing.
2. Retain the source's stronger complete-particular-basis requirement without replacing it by a unique-best-outcome requirement. It applies upstream as well as downstream.
3. Give the later Pruss proposal genuine reason-based explanatory credit, including its explicit answer about actual basing. This is credit for the proposed explanatory form, not an exhibited divine reason for the T20 joint plan. Do not infer complete-basis warrant from that credit.
4. Do not transport either explanation into an original-peer coordination result. The chapter does not establish two perfect agents, necessarily concordant reason-basing, equivalent original roles, or exchange-invariant original-peer means.
5. Even a successful account of choosing a joint plan would not establish that each peer supplies the whole actual productive work independently. The co-production and foreign-completion issues remain distinct.

**Stopping result:** the comparison has removed one stage confusion and established an exact limit on explanatory transport. No additional reading campaign, labelled finite model, or source accumulation is required for this bounded outcome. An actual incompatible-pair verdict would require an additional warrant, for example a source-warranted identification of Pruss's q with the very complete determining basis in question, a denial of complete internal determination by Pruss, or a denial of all nonentailing explanation by Ibn Taymiyyah. None of these has been shown here.
