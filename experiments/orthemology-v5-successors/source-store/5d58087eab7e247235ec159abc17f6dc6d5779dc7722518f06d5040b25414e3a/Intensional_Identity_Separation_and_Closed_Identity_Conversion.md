# Intensional identity separation and closed identity conversion

## Result and exact scope

This document proves an identity-inhabitation separation for the delivered P01AC calculus and its precisely specified hypothetical extension Has⁺. It also gives an exact conversion criterion for closed typed identities in Has⁺. The full proof covers every typing rule, including dependent elimination, polymorphic instantiation and finite imports.

**Verification status.** The results have received independent mathematical review against the stated source definitions. They are ordinary written mathematics. No new Lean formalization or kernel-verification result is claimed, and the source calculus is unchanged. The atom/application bridge remains a hypothetical extension.

Let I, K and S be the raw source combinators, let k = S K K, and put

    A = fin(identityCode) = All α. (α → α),
    p = atom(I),    q = atom(k),
    B = identity(A,p,q),    e = atom(I).

Then B is a formed closed P01AC type, both p and q have type A, and e satisfies the full inherited unary and heterogeneous F/G interpretations at B. Nevertheless:

**Theorem. There is no polynomial r such that Has⁺ [] r B. Consequently there is no polynomial r such that Has [] r B.**

The theorem therefore rules out every syntactic inhabitant at this particular semantically inhabited formed closed type, including after the assessed atom/application bridge. It is stronger than failure to type one prescribed representative. It does not assert that the inherited F/G semantics is unsound: a sound interpretation need not be complete.

The proof uses a separate saturated-subset interpretation. Its type and context equalities refine raw source conversion throughout; it is not obtained by strengthening only the identity clause of F/G. In particular it is not a projection from arbitrary old PER carriers, and it does not preserve their arbitrary heterogeneous links.

The model ingredients are substantially inherited. The earlier P01 study *Contextual dependence, J and the abstraction-congruence obstruction* (U11), especially §§1–3 and §§5–7, already contains Conv-coherent unary codes, telescopes, the dependent product and represented-pair predicates, the identity predicate with both endpoint memberships and proof conversion to I, two-coordinate based J, and unrestricted intersection All. That study explicitly stops before a full finite-syntax soundness theorem and retains the abstraction-congruence obstruction. The increment proved here is the exact current 18-rule P01AC/Has⁺ soundness theorem, with the actual mixed substitutions, zero-padded telescopes, seven-case finite-import proof, resulting global noninhabitation of B, and the derived closed typed-identity criterion in §9. No claim of global historical priority is made.

## 1 The syntax being interpreted

All syntax in this proof is the syntax in the dependent-All source archive identified in §11. A raw source term is generated by I, K, S, zero, one and binary application. Write tu for application, associating to the left. Write t ≈ u for the unchanged raw Conv. Its generators are reflexivity, one source Step, symmetry and transitivity. Source Step has exactly I, K and S contraction and the left/right compatible application contexts. In particular ≈ is an equivalence relation and an application congruence. A finite Red sequence implies ≈.

A polynomial is var(n), atom(t), or app(f,a). A term valuation η is a total sequence of raw terms. Its evaluation is literal:

    eval(var(n),η)=η(n),
    eval(atom(t),η)=t,
    eval(app(f,a),η)=eval(f,η) eval(a,η).

Write a::η for the sequence with head a and tail η; write tail(η)(n)=η(n+1). Let ζ be the source zeroEnv, the sequence constantly equal to the inert raw term zero. Thus ζ is not a bottom type and not an unspecified arbitrary padding value.

The seven type constructors are param(n), bottom, raw, pi(A,B), sigma(A,B), identity(A,p,q), and all(B). Pi and Sigma bind one term variable in B; All binds one type parameter and no term variable. The following are the exact source operations:

* subst σ leaves parameters, bottom and raw fixed, commutes with All, applies psub σ to both identity endpoints, and uses pup σ in a Pi/Sigma body.
* wk T = subst (n ↦ var(n+1)) T.
* inst B a = subst (a::Poly.var) B.
* motiveAt B y e = subst (e::y::Poly.var) B. Proof is coordinate 0 and endpoint is coordinate 1.
* arr A C = pi A (wk C).
* trename r changes type parameters only, lifting r beneath All; twk = trename successor.
* mixed σ τ sends param(n) to τ(n). In a Pi/Sigma body it uses pup σ and the replacement sequence n ↦ wk(τ(n)). In an All body it uses σ and tup τ, where tup τ = param(0)::(n ↦ twk(τ(n))). It substitutes identity endpoints with psub σ.
* tsubst τ = mixed Poly.var τ; tinst B A = tsubst (A::Ty.param) B.
* twkTel Γ = map twk Γ.
* theta Γ A x = identity(wk A,pren successor x,var(0)) :: A :: Γ.

The term substitution lift is pup σ(0)=var(0), pup σ(n+1)=pren successor(σ(n)). The source Lookup has exactly

    Lookup (A::Γ) 0 (wk A),
    Lookup Γ n C ⇒ Lookup (A::Γ) (n+1) (wk C).

Thus declaration types are weakened at every intervening term binder. A lookup is not an unshifted list access.

Has⁺ is the fresh mutually inductive copy of the actual two Ctx, seven Form and eighteen Has constructors in AllSyntax. Its only changes are that the conversion premises of identityIntro and conv use PolyConv⁺. That relation has the original seven PolyConv constructors and one extra generator

    atom(rs) ≈ₚ app(atom(r),atom(s)).

No source reduction, type conversion, abstraction-congruence, extensional equality, semantic admission or finite-typing rule is added. All original premises, including scope, finite image support and formation premises, remain part of each constructor. The proof below validates those constructors; it does not remove any of their premises.

## 2 The raw algebra used in the proof

The following elementary equations and conversions use only the fixed polynomial/source operations.

### 2.1 Evaluation and substitution

For any polynomial b and substitution σ, set ησ(n)=eval(σ(n),η). Structural induction on b gives

    eval(psub σ b,η)=eval(b,ησ).                         (2.1)

The variable case is the definition of ησ; the atom case fixes the atom; the application case applies the two induction equalities. In particular

    eval(pren r b,η)=eval(b,η∘r).

Directly at indices 0 and n+1,

    eval-images(pup σ,a::η)=a::ησ.                      (2.2)

If η(n)≈ξ(n) for every n, write η≈ξ. Structural induction on b, using application congruence, gives

    η≈ξ ⇒ eval(b,η)≈eval(b,ξ).                         (2.3)

Neither (2.1) nor (2.3) requires a scope or typing premise.

### 2.2 Abstraction and represented pairs

The exact source bracket compiler satisfies

    eval(abstract b,η) a →* eval(b,a::η).               (2.4)

For completeness, its proof distinguishes the actual freeZero test. When freeZero b is false, abstract b is K(drop b), and eval(b,a::η)=eval(drop b,η); one K contraction proves (2.4). The equality for an absent variable follows by structural induction. A present variable must be var(0), compiled to I, and one I contraction suffices. For a present application fg, the compiler is S(abstract f)(abstract g); one S contraction followed by the two induction reductions in compatible contexts gives the result. This is an application-level beta law. It does not assert that abstract preserves conversion of bodies.

Let pair(a,b), first(z)=zK and second(z)=z(KI) be the exact pairTerm, firstTerm and secondTerm. The displayed pair polynomial evaluates literally to pair(eval(a,η),eval(b,η)), and fstPoly/sndPoly evaluate literally to the two projections. The source pair-application computation gives

    pair(a,b) f →* f a b,
    first(pair(a,b))≈a,    second(pair(a,b))≈b.         (2.5)

The pair is the raw term S(SI(Ka))(Kb). Its S, S, I, K, K contractions in compatible contexts give the first displayed sequence; specializing f to K or KI gives the projections. The fixed pair polynomial also makes pairing an application-congruent operation.

Finally jPoly d y e evaluates to K(K(eval(d,η))) eval(y,η) eval(e,η), and two K contractions give

    eval(jPoly d y e,η) →* eval(d,η).                  (2.6)

### 2.3 Conversion soundness

For every PolyConv⁺ derivation b≈ₚc and every η,

    eval(b,η)≈eval(c,η).                               (2.7)

Induct on its eight constructors. Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity and application congruence use the corresponding raw laws. The I/K/S cases use the respective raw contraction at the evaluated arguments. In the new bridge case the two evaluations are literally the same raw application rs, so reflexivity suffices. This proof does not refer to typing or to old F/G.

## 3 Saturated sets and the new interpretation

Let Sat be the set of all subsets X of the raw term set such that

    t≈u ⇒ (t∈X ⇔ u∈X).

This is an ordinary subset of the powerset of the raw term set. Empty, the full set and every principal class {t:t≈u} belong to Sat. Sat is extensionally the same collection of acceptance predicates as the inherited step-stable unary Code: step stability extends over Conv by induction, and Conv saturation restricts to every Step. No novel code universe or universe of decoded arbitrary ambient functions is being postulated.

A type valuation ρ is any sequence in Sat. Define ⟦T⟧ρη by structural recursion on T:

    ⟦param(n)⟧ρη = ρ(n),
    ⟦bottom⟧ρη = ∅,
    ⟦raw⟧ρη = all raw terms,

    f∈⟦pi(A,B)⟧ρη
        ⇔ ∀a∈⟦A⟧ρη, fa∈⟦B⟧ρ(a::η),

    z∈⟦sigma(A,B)⟧ρη
        ⇔ ∃a,b, a∈⟦A⟧ρη and b∈⟦B⟧ρ(a::η)
                   and z≈pair(a,b),

    u∈⟦identity(A,p,q)⟧ρη
        ⇔ eval(p,η)∈⟦A⟧ρη and eval(q,η)∈⟦A⟧ρη
             and eval(p,η)≈eval(q,η) and u≈I,

    u∈⟦all(B)⟧ρη
        ⇔ ∀X∈Sat, u∈⟦B⟧(X::ρ)η.

The recursion is on the finite type expression, not on a semantic code ordering. Thus the All clause is well-defined even though its quantifier ranges over every saturated subset. In particular its own resulting subset is among the permissible instantiation arguments once saturation is proved. This is unrestricted internal intersection membership for one common raw program; it is not a term that accepts an arbitrary ambient function as data.

Both endpoint memberships in the identity clause are deliberate. The proof witness also must be raw-convertible to I. It is not enough to require only endpoint raw conversion, and it is not enough to constrain only the proof term.

### 3.1 Every raw type denotes a saturated set

For all T,ρ,η, the set ⟦T⟧ρη is in Sat. This claim is for all raw types, without any formation hypothesis. Prove it by structural induction.

* Parameters use the membership of ρ(n) in Sat; empty and the full set are immediate.
* For Pi, if f≈g, then fa≈ga for each a. The body's induction hypothesis transfers membership of each result in the same fibre. Both implications follow by symmetry.
* For Sigma, retain the same witnesses a,b. If z≈w and z≈pair(a,b), then w≈pair(a,b) by symmetry and transitivity; reverse the roles for the converse.
* For identity, the endpoint conditions are independent of its witness u. If u≈v, the equivalence u≈I iff v≈I follows from equivalence of ≈.
* All is an intersection of saturated sets supplied by the body's induction hypothesis, one for each X∈Sat.

### 3.2 Coherence at pointwise raw conversion of environments

For every raw T and every η≈ξ,

    ⟦T⟧ρη = ⟦T⟧ρξ                                  (3.1)

as sets. Again use structural induction on T. Parameter, bottom and raw cases do not depend on η. In Pi, the domains agree by induction, and for each fixed a the extended environments a::η and a::ξ are pointwise convertible; the body fibres therefore agree. In Sigma, the same argument retains precisely the same a,b witnesses in both directions. All uses the induction hypothesis separately at each X::ρ.

For identity, put x=eval(p,η), x′=eval(p,ξ), y=eval(q,η), y′=eval(q,ξ). Equation (2.3) gives x≈x′ and y≈y′. The carrier sets agree by induction, and their saturation from §3.1 transfers both endpoint memberships. Equivalence of ≈ gives x≈y iff x′≈y′. The condition on the proof witness is unchanged. This proves the identity case, including all its conjuncts.

The same definition is extensional in ρ: pointwise equal type valuations give the same interpretation, by structural induction. Consequently all equations below concern actual set equality, not an assumed carrier PER equality.

### 3.3 Fresh type relations

For clarity, the coherent equality used by this model can be presented explicitly:

    R(T,ρ,η)(t,u)
        ⇔ t∈⟦T⟧ρη and u∈⟦T⟧ρη and t≈u.

It is a partial equivalence relation, has domain exactly ⟦T⟧ρη, is saturated in both raw arguments, and is invariant under η≈ξ. Symmetry and transitivity follow from those of ≈ and the membership conjuncts; raw saturation follows from §3.1. Thus every type relation in this interpretation refines raw Conv, including at Pi and All. For example, this relation need not identify the two distinct raw functions I and SKK at A even though both satisfy its membership predicate. This is where it differs coherently from the old extensional/parametric carrier relations.

## 4 Exact term and type substitution laws

These laws hold for all raw types and all Sat-valued environments. No formation proof or semantic admission premise is smuggled into them.

### 4.1 Term substitution and weakening

For any T,σ,ρ,η,

    ⟦subst σ T⟧ρη = ⟦T⟧ρ ησ.                         (4.1)

Induct on T. The three atomic type cases are immediate. The identity case uses the carrier induction equality and (2.1) for both endpoints. All uses the body induction equality for each X::ρ, since this binder leaves σ unchanged. For Pi, the domain agrees by induction, and at each argument a the body equality is applied with pup σ at a::η. Equation (2.2) changes its target valuation to a::ησ, exactly the desired fibre. For Sigma the same calculation identifies the sets of permitted witnesses a,b. These exhaust all seven constructors.

In particular, for any a and η,

    ⟦wk T⟧ρ(a::η) = ⟦T⟧ρη,                          (4.2)
    ⟦inst B b⟧ρη = ⟦B⟧ρ(eval(b,η)::η),               (4.3)
    ⟦motiveAt B y e⟧ρη
        = ⟦B⟧ρ(eval(e,η)::eval(y,η)::η).              (4.4)

Equation (4.4) has both coordinates in the source's order; it is not a one-coordinate transport formula. Repeated use of (4.2) handles every intervening binder. Also,

    ⟦arr A C⟧ρη = (⟦A⟧ρη ⇒ ⟦C⟧ρη),                 (4.5)

where X⇒Y is the set {f:∀a∈X,fa∈Y}. Its codomain is frozen at the outer environment, as required by the source's definition pi A (wk C).

### 4.2 Type renaming and weakening

For a map r on type indices, put ρr(n)=ρ(r(n)). Then

    ⟦trename r T⟧ρη = ⟦T⟧ρr η.                      (4.6)

Structural induction proves this. Identity endpoints are literally unchanged. Pi/Sigma bind no type variable, so the same r is used in their bodies. In the All case, renaming is liftRen r, and at a fresh X the index calculations are

    (X::ρ)(liftRen r(0))=X,
    (X::ρ)(liftRen r(n+1))=ρ(r(n)).

Therefore the body's valuation is X::ρr, proving the All case. In particular

    ⟦twk T⟧(X::ρ)η = ⟦T⟧ρη.                        (4.7)

Unlike term weakening, type weakening does not prepend a term coordinate.

### 4.3 Simultaneous mixed substitution

Fix σ,τ,ρ,η and define a *frozen* type valuation

    μ(n)=⟦τ(n)⟧ρη.

Section 3.1 makes every μ(n) a member of Sat, even for raw, term-dependent replacement types. The exact mixed law is

    ⟦mixed σ τ T⟧ρη = ⟦T⟧μ ησ.                      (4.8)

In particular μ is evaluated at the original η, not at ησ and not at a later bound argument. This distinction is essential.

Proof is structural induction on T. The parameter case is the definition of μ. Bottom/raw are immediate. Identity uses the carrier induction hypothesis and (2.1) for the endpoint substitutions.

For a Pi or Sigma, after entering its term binder with raw value a, the source replaces σ by pup σ and τ by τ′(n)=wk(τ(n)). By (2.2) the new term image valuation is a::ησ, while (4.2) gives, for every n,

    ⟦τ′(n)⟧ρ(a::η)=⟦τ(n)⟧ρη=μ(n).

Thus the induction hypothesis for the body has exactly the same frozen μ and the extended term valuation a::ησ. The domain induction equality and, respectively, universal application membership or the same existential pair witnesses finish the Pi and Sigma cases.

For All, choose any X∈Sat. The source replaces τ by tup τ. Its interpreted images in X::ρ at η are

    ⟦param(0)⟧(X::ρ)η=X,
    ⟦twk(τ(n))⟧(X::ρ)η=μ(n)

by (4.7). Hence the new type image valuation is X::μ. The term valuation remains ησ. Apply the body induction hypothesis and intersect over all X. This completes all cases of (4.8).

Taking σ=Poly.var gives

    ⟦tsubst τ T⟧ρη = ⟦T⟧(n↦⟦τ(n)⟧ρη)η,
    ⟦tinst B A⟧ρη = ⟦B⟧(⟦A⟧ρη::ρ)η.                (4.9)

This is unrestricted type instantiation, including replacements depending on outer term variables and replacements containing All, Pi, Sigma or typed identity. The required shifts have already been checked in both sorts. There is no appeal to the old F/G mixed-substitution lemma or its ImageMatch premises.

## 5 Zero padded telescopes and exact lookup

Define fresh membership of a telescope, denoted VΓρη, by the same zero-padding convention as the source:

    V[]ρη ⇔ η=ζ,
    V(A::Γ)ρη ⇔ VΓρ(tail η)
                     and η(0)∈⟦A⟧ρ(tail η).

Equivalently, V(A::Γ)ρ(a::η) iff VΓρη and a∈⟦A⟧ρη. The declaration A is evaluated in the tail valuation, before its new binder is entered. A telescope may have no valuations; no proof will infer its nonemptiness merely from syntactic formation.

For a relational presentation define

    E[]ρηξ ⇔ η=ζ and ξ=ζ,
    E(A::Γ)ρηξ ⇔ EΓρ(tail η)(tail ξ)
                    and R(A,ρ,tail η)(η(0),ξ(0)).

Induction on Γ proves that EΓρηξ entails η≈ξ and both VΓρη and VΓρξ. In the extension step the tail relation provides pointwise raw conversion of tails; (3.1) changes ξ(0)'s membership from the left tail fibre to the right tail fibre. Conversely, VΓρη, VΓρξ and η≈ξ imply EΓρηξ by the same induction and (3.1). Thus E is precisely pointwise raw conversion on admissible, zero-padded valuations. Reflexivity on V, symmetry and transitivity follow.

There is deliberately no assertion that VΓ is invariant under replacing *arbitrary total* valuations by pointwise-convertible ones: such replacement could violate literal zero-padding beyond the telescope. The needed coherence is either the unrestricted type equality (3.1), or E between admissible zero-padded valuations. This distinction avoids changing the source's base context.

### 5.1 Lookup lemma

If Lookup Γ n C and VΓρη, then η(n)∈⟦C⟧ρη. Induct on the actual Lookup derivation.

* At zero, Γ=A::Δ and C=wk A. Membership of η(0) in ⟦A⟧ρ(tail η) is part of VΓρη. Write η=η(0)::tail η and use (4.2) to identify this with ⟦wk A⟧ρη.
* At successor, the premise is Lookup Δ n C and the conclusion has Γ=A::Δ, index n+1 and type wk C. The tail is a VΔ valuation. Induction gives η(n+1)∈⟦C⟧ρ(tail η); (4.2) changes this to membership in ⟦wk C⟧ρη.

No unshifted list-element surrogate for Lookup is used.

### 5.2 The All context shift

For every Γ,X,ρ,η,

    V(twkTel Γ)(X::ρ)η ⇔ VΓρη.                       (5.1)

Induct on Γ. At nil, both conditions are η=ζ. At an extension, twkTel(A::Γ)=twk A::twkTel Γ; apply the tail induction hypothesis and (4.7) to its head-membership condition at tail η. The analogous equation holds for E, using the same raw-conversion conjunct and (4.7). This exact shift of every declaration prevents an All introduction from generalizing a fixed type-dependent term assumption.

### 5.3 The two-coordinate J telescope

Fix VΓρη, x₀=eval(x,η), y₀=eval(y,η) and e₀=eval(e,η). Unfolding theta and using (4.2) and eval(pren successor x,y₀::η)=x₀ gives the exact equivalence

    V(theta Γ A x)ρ(e₀::y₀::η)
      ⇔ VΓρη and y₀∈⟦A⟧ρη and x₀∈⟦A⟧ρη
                      and x₀≈y₀ and e₀≈I.            (5.2)

The duplicate y₀-membership arising inside identity has been written once. If x₀∈⟦A⟧ρη, the base valuation I::x₀::η is admissible by reflexivity. If the right side of (5.2) holds, then

    I::x₀::η ≈ e₀::y₀::η,

using I≈e₀ and x₀≈y₀ separately. Both are admissible theta-valuations, so they are also E-related. This explicitly handles the proof coordinate as well as the endpoint coordinate.

## 6 The actual seven constructor finite import theorem

Define the finite-code interpretation Jρ(C) in Sat by

    Jρ(var n)=ρ(n),    Jρ(bottom)=∅,
    Jρ(arrow C D)=Jρ(C)⇒Jρ(D),
    Jρ(all C)=⋂{J(X::ρ)(C):X∈Sat}.

Arrow and intersection are saturated by the same proofs as §3.1. Induction on the four TypeCode constructors, using (4.5), gives

    ⟦fin C⟧ρη = Jρ(C)                                (6.1)

for every η. In particular the finite interpretation is independent of the term valuation.

The finite syntax's rename and substitute use liftRen and upSub under All, exactly as in FiniteBridge. Induction on TypeCode gives

    Jρ(rename r C)=J(ρ∘r)(C),
    Jρ(substitute τ C)=J(n↦Jρ(τ(n)))(C).              (6.2)

In the All case of the first equality, liftRen preserves the fresh head X and renames only the tail. In the All case of the second equality, upSub τ has fresh image var(0), and its successor images rename successor(τ(n)) interpret at X::ρ as Jρ(τ(n)) by the first equality. The arrow cases apply induction to domain and codomain; variables and bottom are immediate. Thus, for the exact finite instantiateType,

    Jρ(instantiateType C D)=J(Jρ(D)::ρ)(C).           (6.3)

**Finite soundness.** If FiniteDerives t C, then t∈Jρ(C) for every Sat-valued ρ.

Induct on the actual seven FiniteDerives constructors, with the conclusion universally quantified over ρ:

1. **i(C).** If a∈Jρ(C), then Ia≈a. Saturation gives Ia∈Jρ(C), so I∈Jρ(C)⇒Jρ(C).
2. **k(C,D).** For a∈Jρ(C) and b∈Jρ(D), Kab≈a. Saturation gives the result in Jρ(C), proving K's curried arrow membership.
3. **s(C,D,E).** Take f∈X⇒(Y⇒Z), g∈X⇒Y and a∈X, where X=Jρ(C), Y=Jρ(D), Z=Jρ(E). Then ga∈Y and fa∈Y⇒Z, hence (fa)(ga)∈Z. Since Sfga≈(fa)(ga), saturation proves S's required membership.
4. **app.** Apply the function induction membership to the argument induction membership at the same ρ.
5. **allI.** For each X∈Sat, instantiate the single premise's universally quantified induction conclusion at X::ρ. This places the same raw term t in every J(X::ρ)(C), hence in their intersection. This constructor has no term context; no fixed assumption is being illicitly generalized.
6. **allE.** The premise gives t in every body instance. Choose X=Jρ(D), which is saturated, and use (6.3) to obtain precisely the constructor's instantiated conclusion. D can itself contain All; no finite restriction on the semantic quantifier is introduced.
7. **reduce.** The premise gives t∈Jρ(C); its actual Red t u certificate gives t≈u, so saturation yields u∈Jρ(C).

This is a direct induction for the new interpretation. The inherited finite soundness theorem into old semantics is not being used as a substitute for it.

Now consider the current P01AC finite constructor with its actual list τ, bound finSupport C≤length τ, formed image premises, formed result premise and finite certificate FiniteDerives t C. Let τ̄=typeImages τ, which is literally bottom outside the finite list, and fix ρ,η. Put μ(n)=⟦τ̄(n)⟧ρη; thus out-of-range μ(n)=∅. By (4.8), (4.9) and (6.1),

    ⟦tsubst τ̄ (fin C)⟧ρη = ⟦fin C⟧μη = Jμ(C).

Finite soundness at μ gives t in this set, exactly the evaluation of atom(t) required by the Has⁺ finite conclusion. The list and its Bottom default have not been replaced by an arbitrary inferred table. The proof is actually valid without the support/formation premises, but the interpreted rule continues to require every one of them. This stronger semantic lemma does not authorize a new syntactic constructor.

## 7 Soundness for all current and hypothetical typing rules

**Fundamental theorem.** For every Has⁺ Γ b T, every Sat-valued ρ and every η with VΓρη,

    eval(b,η)∈⟦T⟧ρη.                                (7.1)

The theorem can be proved by simultaneous induction on the actual mutual Ctx⁺/Form⁺/Has⁺ derivations. Interpret the auxiliary context/formation conclusions as being well-defined coherent zero-padded telescopes and saturated coherent type families. These conclusions hold for all raw telescopes/types by §§3–5, so their induction cases require no stronger premise. Explicitly, Ctx.nil uses the prescribed singleton zero valuation, Ctx.ext uses the displayed V extension, Form.param/bottom/raw use the corresponding saturated sets, Form.pi/sigma use the structurally proved saturation/coherence, Form.all uses the full intersection with (5.1), and Form.identity uses the full four-conjunct predicate from §3. These are all two context and seven formation cases. No assertion that a formed telescope is inhabited is made.

For the typing branch keep ρ and η universally quantified, so its hypotheses apply under both kinds of binder. Here are all eighteen cases in the order of AllSyntax.

### 1 var

Use the actual Lookup premise and §5.1. Evaluation of var(n) is η(n). The Form premise is retained but is not needed by this stronger lookup lemma.

### 2 i

Equation (4.5) identifies the conclusion with X⇒X, X=⟦A⟧ρη. For a∈X, Ia≈a, and saturation proves the required application membership. Evaluation of atom(I) is I.

### 3 k

At the fixed outer ρ,η, let X=⟦A⟧ρη and Y=⟦B⟧ρη. Repeated (4.5) identifies the conclusion with X⇒(Y⇒X). For a∈X and b∈Y, Kab≈a. Saturation of X proves the result. The weakenings in the nested arr expressions are precisely why X and Y remain those outer sets.

### 4 s

Let X,Y,Z be the interpretations of A,B,C at the fixed outer ρ,η. Repeated (4.5) identifies the source S type with (X⇒(Y⇒Z))⇒((X⇒Y)⇒(X⇒Z)). For f,g,a in those sets, function membership gives (fa)(ga)∈Z, and Sfga≈(fa)(ga) transfers this to the required application. No dependent outer type is silently re-evaluated at an introduced argument.

### 5 finite

## 9 Exact inhabitation of closed typed identities

The preceding all-rule theorem also supplies the forward direction of an exact criterion for closed typed identities in Has⁺. The converse uses the already established closed conversion characterization for the atom/application bridge. We restate the needed conversion argument before combining them.

### 9.1 Closed polynomial conversion

For polynomials p and q with Scoped 0 p and Scoped 0 q,

    PolyConv⁺ p q ⇔ eval(p,ζ)≈eval(q,ζ).               (9.1)

This is the closed conversion theorem of *Hypothetical Atom/Application Bridge v2*. Here is its proof in the present notation.

First, every scope-zero polynomial p is PolyConv⁺-convertible to atom(eval(p,ζ)). Induct on p. A variable case is impossible under Scoped 0. For an atom the statement is reflexivity. For app(f,a), apply the two induction conversions under application congruence, obtaining app(atom(eval(f,ζ)),atom(eval(a,ζ))). The reverse bridge repacks this as atom(eval(f,ζ) eval(a,ζ)), exactly atom(eval(p,ζ)).

Second, raw source conversion r≈s entails PolyConv⁺ atom(r) atom(s). For a single raw Step, induct on its five constructors. The I, K and S contractions are exposed by the bridge at each application node, contracted by the corresponding polynomial I, K or S rule, and then, where necessary, repacked with the reverse bridge. More explicitly, the S case converts atom(S f g x) to app(app(app(atom(S),atom(f)),atom(g)),atom(x)); polynomial S contracts it to app(app(atom(f),atom(x)),app(atom(g),atom(x))); three reverse bridges repack this as atom((f x)(g x)). The I and K cases expose respectively one and two application nodes and contract directly to their selected argument atom. A left-compatible Step f→g is handled by exposing atom(fx), applying the induction conversion to its left atom, and repacking to atom(gx). A right-compatible Step x→y is handled in the same way in the right polynomial argument. Induction over the four raw Conv constructors then supplies the claim: reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity are preserved, and its Step case uses the preceding construction.

The forward implication of (9.1) is evaluation soundness (2.7). For its reverse, convert p to atom(eval(p,ζ)), use the just-proved raw-to-atom conversion for eval(p,ζ)≈eval(q,ζ), and compose with the reverse packing conversion from atom(eval(q,ζ)) to q. This proves (9.1). It is a conversion theorem with the displayed closedness hypotheses, not a typing-completeness theorem or an algorithm deciding raw Conv.

### 9.2 The typed identity criterion

Keep exactly the Has⁺/Form⁺/PolyConv⁺ system of §1. Assume

    Form⁺ [] C,
    Has⁺ [] p C,    Has⁺ [] q C,
    Scoped 0 p,    Scoped 0 q.

Put T=identity(C,p,q). Its actual formation rule gives Form⁺ [] T. Then the following three propositions are equivalent:

1. There is a polynomial r such that Has⁺ [] r T.
2. Has⁺ [] atom(I) T.
3. eval(p,ζ)≈eval(q,ζ).

The carrier C may contain free type parameters; the statement respects all their lawful interpretations. The two endpoint polynomials are closed in term variables. No decision procedure for proposition 3 is supplied.

**Proof.** For 1⇒3, apply the fresh unary all-rule soundness theorem to the displayed identity typing at the admissible empty-context valuation ζ and any Sat-valued type environment. The identity interpretation requires raw conversion of the two evaluated endpoints, as in (8.1). This direction covers every possible final typing rule, including J and elimination rules.

For 3⇒2, the two Scoped 0 hypotheses let (9.1) turn the raw conversion into PolyConv⁺ p q. The actual identityIntro constructor takes that certificate, the two assumed endpoint typings and the identity formation just obtained. Its conclusion is precisely Has⁺ [] atom(I) T.

For 2⇒1, choose r=atom(I). This proves the equivalence. Whenever any inhabitant exists, the witness can therefore be chosen as the same scope-zero atom(I).

### 9.3 Scope of the criterion

This characterizes syntactic inhabitation of closed typed identities with already typed endpoints in the stated hypothetical calculus. It does not say that arbitrary equality in the old F/G carrier semantics implies the criterion. In the separating example C=A=All α.(α→α), p=atom(I) and q=atom(SKK), old F/G equality holds while proposition 3 fails, so all three inhabitation propositions fail.

The given converse proof uses the atom/application bridge. The same equivalence is not asserted for the unchanged old Has relation, arbitrary open endpoints, untyped endpoints, a source relation with additional eta equations, or a richer identity theory. The result adds no equality-reflection rule to the syntax. The canonical unary identity predicate and conversion of its proof witnesses to I are inherited ingredients. The forward implication is the consequence of the all-current-rule soundness theorem proved here; the closed conversion result used in the converse is inherited from the bridge analysis.
