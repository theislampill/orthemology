## The main change in simple terms

### For a young reader

Imagine two recipe machines. We want to know whether they will always give the same result, whichever number we feed them. A proof checker can inspect an explanation and tell us whether every step follows the rules. But checking an explanation is different from being able to find one for every true answer. For the machines studied here, no single effective rule book can settle all the true sameness claims.

Now suppose each machine must choose one of two colours. Whenever the machines really differ, there is a number we can feed them that makes one choose the first colour and the other choose the second. That disagreement can be checked. Yet there is no computable recipe that always tells us in advance how far we must search before giving up. Looking at a very long list of matching answers can still miss a later difference.

For a carefully limited kind of recipe, we can do better. Allow only whole-number constants and inputs, adding, multiplying, and asking whether a value is zero. The structure of such a recipe gives us a finite checklist that really is enough to compare it with another recipe of this kind. The shortcut works because the allowed recipes have a proved special structure.

For a different question, imagine two children solving a puzzle together. A name on the answer tells us who said it aloud, but may not tell us whose clues helped. Two exact pretend examples can give the same chances for all possible answer sequences: one uses only the clearer child’s clues, while the other uses both children’s clues. Looking at their answers alone cannot distinguish those examples. Seeing more of how they worked could help. The examples do not tell us what any real children did.

### For an older student

An effective proof system has finite proofs that a computer can enumerate. It may take arbitrarily long, but every proof eventually appears. Soundness means that every certified claim is true under the specified interpretation; completeness means that every true target claim eventually receives a certificate. The Raw result proves that these two ambitions cannot both be met by such a system for the whole fixed identity family. The conclusion permits sound proofs of individual cases and complete procedures for appropriately restricted classes.

The Π labels describe the logical shape of the remaining search. At the Raw carrier, every input must have some finite conversion proof: “for every input, there exists a proof”. That is the ${\Pi{}}_{2}^{0}$ shape. With correctly typed Boolean outputs, each individual output comparison can be computed, so equality becomes “for every input, the two bits agree”, the ${\Pi{}}_{1}^{0}$ shape. Completeness says that these are exact lower as well as upper bounds for the stated code domains.

The two possible Boolean values do not make the entire function space finite. Inputs still range over every natural number, and different functions may first disagree arbitrarily far out. A complete finite witness of disagreement therefore coexists with the absence of a complete positive proof search and the absence of a computable bound on where disagreement must appear.

Typing evidence also matters. Checking a supplied finite typing proof is decidable; deciding from a program alone whether any such proof exists can be undecidable. The bare Boolean result includes this second question. The restricted arithmetic result instead begins with a decidable expression grammar and a fixed compiler that supplies well-typed endpoints.

## Five questions that must remain separate

The programme repeatedly encounters the word identity, but its claims concern several different objects. Keeping the question fixed is necessary both to recognise a genuine gain and to avoid enlarging it in retelling.

Theoretical semantics asks what a formal expression means under a declared interpretation. The present mathematical identity relation compares typed polynomial endpoints in the original F/G interpretation. It retains its actual clauses for functions, polymorphism and identity types. Agreement under a convenient replacement model would answer a different question.

Numerical personal identity asks whether an individual who acts later is the very same individual. Reproducing a specification, constructing another running instance, sharing memories or performing the same function does not settle that question without an independently justified criterion. The mathematical identity theorems here supply no such criterion.

Actual causal warrant asks what reasons support attributing an actual contribution or productive source to a particular bearer. A formally valid conditional can clarify an inference, while evidence about its application does further work. Basic recognition and integrated explanatory reasoning remain eligible sources of warrant; they need not first become a universal theorem.

Empirical performance asks what a particular arrangement actually achieved under its tested conditions. A supported task capacity is a genuine result even when further resources remain necessary. The scope of that result follows the task, the support and the observed history. A larger claim about independence, preservation or indefinite renewal needs evidence addressing that larger claim.

Normative assessment asks what makes a performance correct, fitting or practically authoritative. A successful output, a well-supported belief about that output, a local standard of excellence and an obligation to act can come apart. A complete account of their ultimate productive source is a further objective, rather than an entrance requirement for every warranted local judgement.

## Exact complexity at the Raw output carrier

### The precise target

Let N be the Church-natural type: its inhabitants act as finite iterators. Let C be the type of functions from N to Raw. Raw retains the original combinatory syntax and finite conversion relation. The endpoints are finite closed polynomials with current Has typing at C, and the carrier and identity type are formed using the current rules.



$$
N\ =\ \forall{}X.\ (X\ \to{}\ X)\ \to{}\ X\ \to{}\ X,\ \ \ \ \ C\ =\ N\ \to{}\ \mathrm{R}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{w}
$$



Validity means that the evaluated endpoints are F-related at C in every unary type environment. By the unchanged identity clauses, this is also original F/G identity validity with proof I, and equivalently the existence of a semantic polynomial identity witness. These are semantic statements. They do not assert that current Has can derive every valid identity.

The theorem covers both plain endpoint-pair codes and codes that carry two checked endpoint-typing certificates. Malformed, open or untypable pairs are excluded from the positive set. In the certificate-carrying version, an invalid supplied certificate is rejected even if the endpoint happens to have a different valid proof. This is an actual classification of finite-code sets, rather than a promise that inputs will already be suitable.

### Why the infinite semantic target can be classified

The proof first shows that every semantic Church natural has one unique finite iteration index. This includes arbitrary semantic inhabitants, not just terms generated by a chosen syntax or already known to normalise. Two self-related natural inhabitants are semantically related exactly when their indices agree. The proof retains the polymorphic uniformity requirements of both endpoints.

Consequently two currently typed endpoints at C are semantically equal exactly when their outputs are raw-convertible at every canonical natural input. The reverse direction covers all related pairs of semantic inputs and every type environment. Testing canonical inputs is therefore justified by a theorem about the original semantics, not by assuming that untested inputs behave like the familiar examples.

The full written argument supplies a computable reduction from machine totality into this single carrier, with one fixed constant endpoint and the required current typing certificates. Combined with the finite-certificate upper bound, this establishes ${\Pi{}}_{2}^{0}$-completeness under computable many-one reductions. The fixed carrier has infinitely many semantic classes. No classification of arbitrary carriers is claimed.

### What this changes about completion

The earlier obstruction showed that current Has and the bridge-only candidate miss a particular valid identity. The separately defined extensional repair HasE can prove that example while remaining sound for the original semantics. The new result establishes a stronger limit: every sound recursively enumerable proof system misses some valid identities in the fixed typed family, including HasE. Adding more effective rules cannot remove this limit while keeping the same unrestricted target.

There is a matching negative limitation. Within the correctly typed closed slice, invalidity is not recursively enumerable either. This failure is not caused by malformed inputs or by difficulty recognising the typing domain: the reductions already provide suitable endpoints and certificates. Some individual failures can be proved, but no sound effective negative-certificate system covers every failure in that family.

The result leaves useful finite work intact. A supplied finite typing or conversion proof can be checked. A finite list of arbitrary conversion questions without proofs can be searched, but finiteness alone does not make that search decidable. Successful tests at any prescribed finite set of natural inputs do not establish universal identity. A restricted fragment, an independently justified completeness bound, or an explicitly inconclusive procedure has a different and potentially achievable specification.

## The Boolean boundary and complete negative evidence

### Two outputs with infinitely many inputs

Replace Raw output by the Church-Boolean type B, whose semantic inhabitants choose between two arguments. The input type remains N. The result concerns closed current-typed functions at ${C}_{B}\ =\ N\ \to{}\ B$ under the same original F/G semantics.



$$
B\ =\ \forall{}X.\ X\ \to{}\ X\ \to{}\ X,\ \ \ \ \ {C}_{B}\ =\ N\ \to{}\ B
$$



Every self-related semantic Boolean makes one unique choice on arbitrary raw arguments. On a canonical natural input, current typing guarantees that a search through finite raw-conversion proofs eventually identifies that choice. Searching for either inert-marker observation therefore computes each bit. The argument does not assume a particular evaluation strategy or a general strong-normalisation theorem.

For annotated inputs carrying checked current endpoint derivations, semantic equality is ${\Pi{}}_{1}^{0}$-complete. Its complement is ${\Sigma{}}_{1}^{0}$-complete. Every unequal correctly typed pair has a finite negative certificate: one canonical natural input and two finite conversion proofs to opposite markers. The certificate can be checked without settling all the other inputs.



$$
{{E}_{B}}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}}\ \mathrm{i}\mathrm{s}\ {\Pi{}}_{1}^{0}\ \mathrm{c}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{l}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{e},\ \ \ \ \ {E}_{B}\ \mathrm{i}\mathrm{s}\ \mathrm{d}.\mathrm{c}.\mathrm{e}.\ \mathrm{c}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{l}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{e}
$$



For plain endpoint codes, the exact equality set is ${E}_{B}\ =\ D\ \setminus{}\ M$, where D is the recursively enumerable closed current-typed domain and M is the recursively enumerable opposite-marker predicate. The new lower bound matches this upper bound: bare equality is d.c.e.-complete, meaning complete for differences of two recursively enumerable sets, and hence lies in ${\Delta{}}_{2}^{0}$. It is neither recursively enumerable nor co-recursively enumerable. Complete negative certificates for semantic inequality on the typed domain remain valid; they do not certify every excluded untypable string.

### The lower bound uses the actual current calculus

The new bridge is a direct compiler from finite primitive-recursive descriptions into actual current-Has programs. It covers zero, successor, projections, finite composition and primitive recursion at all finite arities. Kernel-checked theorems establish both current typing and correct computation for arbitrary raw arguments with the specified natural-number observations. No assumed reverse translation from another calculus or cast from an arbitrary raw program to a Boolean is used.

The compiler is applied to a fixed bounded-halting computation: given a program code and a finite step bound, it reports whether that program has halted by then. The written argument constructs this function using an explicit finite arithmetic and binary-machine encoding. A compiled Boolean family agrees with the fixed constant endpoint on every input exactly when the encoded machine never halts. The particular arithmetic encoding and the hierarchy argument are written mathematics; the source-specific compiler and semantic bridge are kernel checked.

### Why erasing typing evidence changes the result

The additional proof builds a finite program that acts as a halting gate in front of an already typed Boolean endpoint. If the gate’s machine halts, the gated endpoint receives an actual current typing and preserves the endpoint’s semantic behaviour. If that machine never halts, the gated endpoint has no current typing at ${C}_{B}$. Combining this independent gate with the existing Boolean family makes bare equality hold exactly when one machine halts and another never halts. This establishes the required difference-of-enumerable-sets lower bound.

The same construction proves ${\Sigma{}}_{1}^{0}$-completeness of closed current typability at the single type ${C}_{B}$. The difficulty is already present with that type fixed. An annotated input supplies evidence whose correctness can be checked before bit computation begins; a bare input leaves existence of that evidence unsettled. This explains why the ${\Pi{}}_{1}^{0}$ annotated theorem and the d.c.e. bare theorem classify different sets without contradicting one another.

The companion Bare Boolean Identity and Fixed Type Typability supplies the full argument and its dependencies. The source-specific gate, compiler-purity and positive-typing bridges have kernel checks. The operational non-typing argument and computability classification remain written mathematics. No new typing rule, arbitrary atom-unfolding rule or identification of semantic membership with current typing is assumed.

### Why a finite witness does not provide a stopping rule

Suppose an algorithm could inspect any valid annotated pair and return a number guaranteed to bound some distinguishing input whenever the pair differs. Computing all outputs up to that number would decide equality: a mismatch gives a negative answer, while complete agreement up to the promised bound gives a positive answer. That would contradict ${\Pi{}}_{1}^{0}$-completeness. Hence no such computable uniform bound exists.

The same obstruction applies to a computable bound based only on code length. For each finite code-length bound, the least distinguishing inputs of unequal valid annotated codes within that bound form a finite set. Its maximum exists when it is nonempty; use zero otherwise. The theorem excludes a computable uniform majorant of these finite bounds, rather than their mathematical existence. Individually justified bounds and separately restricted families remain available.

This makes a concrete difference to the design of an identity service. For the annotated Boolean target, a complete search for counterexamples can eventually refute every unequal pair. It need not terminate on equal pairs. For the Raw target, even complete effective refutation is unavailable. A user-facing service must therefore state whether it checks supplied proofs, searches for witnesses, covers a restricted domain, or may report that it has not resolved the question.

## Complete finite certificates for a declared fragment

A positive completeness result is now available for a precise expression language. At any finite arity, expressions may use natural-number constants, variables, addition, multiplication and finite nested conditionals that ask whether an expression is zero. Subtraction, negative coefficients, arbitrary tests of equality between expressions, user-defined recursion and arbitrary embedded P01AC programs are outside this grammar. Inputs still range over all natural-number tuples; only the description language is restricted.

A fixed compiler constructs actual current-Has endpoints at the curried natural-number carrier for that arity. The checked bridge proves that equality of the numerical expression functions is equivalent to original F/G semantic identity, with independently related arguments and all required environments retained. At zero arity the carrier is N itself. This is a result about that declared family of compiled endpoints, not a replacement semantics.

### Why finitely many polynomial pieces suffice

Divide the inputs into zero/nonzero masks: for each coordinate, record whether it is zero or positive. There are finitely many masks for each finite arity. After substituting zero for the zero coordinates, an arithmetic expression has a finite nonnegative polynomial coefficient map on each mask. Any nonzero such polynomial is strictly positive when all its remaining variables are positive. Therefore a zero-test conditional chooses the same branch throughout that entire mask.

Recursion over the finite expression tree produces one canonical coefficient map for every mask, including arbitrarily nested zero tests. These tables represent the whole declared grammar, rather than a few selected arithmetic identities. Two compiled endpoints are semantically equal exactly when their tables agree at every mask.

Write ${V}_{r}(e,f)$ for semantic equality of the compiled expressions e and f, and ${\mathrm{N}\mathrm{F}}_{S}(e)$ for the coefficient map of e on mask S.



$$
{V}_{r}(e,f)\ \Leftrightarrow{}\ {\mathrm{N}\mathrm{F}}_{S}(e)\ =\ {\mathrm{N}\mathrm{F}}_{S}(f)\ \mathrm{f}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{r}\ \mathrm{e}\mathrm{v}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{y}\ \mathrm{m}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{k}\ S
$$



### A finite test set with a proved guarantee

The coefficient tables also give a computable separating grid. On a fixed mask, hold every zero coordinate at zero. For each positive coordinate, take the larger individual degree appearing in either normal form and test the positive values from 1 through that degree plus 1. Agreement on the Cartesian product of these sets forces equality of the two polynomial pieces. Taking the union over every mask gives a complete finite equality test and guarantees a distinguishing tuple whenever the expressions differ.

The edge cases matter. The all-zero mask contributes the zero tuple. A positive coordinate of degree zero still receives the test value 1. At arity zero, the unique empty tuple compares two constants. For example, x and ${x}^{2}$ agree at 0 and 1 but differ at 2; the prescribed positive grid for this pair is {1, 2, 3}. The complete criterion follows from the degree argument, rather than from confidence in a chosen testing budget.

### What changes with one polynomial equality test

A separately proved boundary concerns a very small extension of the source grammar. Keep the old expressions and add a new kind with one test at its root: compare two pure nonnegative-coefficient polynomial expressions, returning natural 1 when they agree and 0 otherwise. The arguments use only constants, variables, addition and multiplication; there are no nested new equality tests. The compiled outputs remain Church naturals, rather than Church Booleans.

With arity included in the input, equality of expressions in this extended grammar is ${\Pi{}}_{1}^{0}$-complete. Already deciding whether one root test is identically zero has that complexity: it asks whether its two polynomial arguments never coincide. A tuple where they do coincide is a negative certificate for that zero-identity claim. The result excludes complete sound effective positive certification and a computable complete finite-test bound for this extension. It is not the problem of deciding whether the two polynomial arguments are equal everywhere.

Zero- and one-variable instances remain decidable. A separate written argument gives an explicit finite root-and-tail comparison bound for any one-variable pair. This additional completeness proof remains written rather than kernel-checked. The hardness result therefore occurs at some unspecified fixed arity of at least two, and at larger arities by padding. It classifies no particular arity of two or more. Current typing, comparison computation and the original F/G bridge are kernel checked, while the global complexity proof remains written. ## Collective competence and contribution

### The empirical finding

In the second experiment, Hertz and colleagues tested 15 male pairs on a visual comparison task. Immediate discussion produced higher collective sensitivity than private-vote-first discussion. Two independently written analyses of the official trial data reproduced all 120 supplied sensitivity estimates to their reported precision. Mean sensitivities were 3.44 for private-vote-first and 4.15 for immediate discussion; the paired comparison gave $t(14)=2.704$, $p=.0171$, with a 95% interval of 0.147–1.278 for the mean increase. It does not identify who causally supplied the judgement: private responses were not recorded in the immediate-discussion condition, and announcement roles do not isolate evidence contributions. ### An original model of what answers leave unresolved

The mathematical companion constructs two rival mechanisms. Each member receives an independent Gaussian summary of the same stimulus. One member has noise variance v and the other 3v. The first mechanism uses only the clearer summary. The second takes the ordinary average of both. Their score means agree, and so do their variances.



$$
\mathrm{V}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\left(\frac{{X}_{B}\ +\ {X}_{W}}{2}\right)\ =\ \frac{v\ +\ 3v}{4}\ =\ v
$$



Thresholding either score therefore gives exactly the same yes-or-no answer probabilities. With the complete stimulus sequence and announcer schedule fixed, and with independent trial noises, the full distribution of every finite answer sequence is also identical. The construction permits the same announcer-specific output noise in both mechanisms and remains valid when that output noise is zero. It requires no additional integration-noise term.

Yet the mechanisms genuinely differ. In the first, changing the second member’s summary leaves the answer law unchanged. In the second, it changes the answer probability. These changes are interventions defined by the mathematical model. Their definition does not establish that an equivalent clean intervention on a real person is available. Equality of answer distributions also does not mean the two mechanisms give the same answer for every shared realisation of their noises.

The complete-design qualification matters. Matching only each trial’s stimulus distribution need not match correlations across the whole sequence. A common exogenous joint design can be integrated out while preserving the equality; an adaptive or endogenous design needs its own argument. The result establishes nonidentification within any admissible model class containing this pair, rather than a theorem about every possible experiment.

### Evidence that would distinguish this pair

Trial-linked evidence changes the observation scheme. In the constructed models, observing the second member’s continuous summary together with that trial’s answer would distinguish them in principle. Conditional on the same stimulus and announcer, the summary–answer covariance is zero for the one-input mechanism and strictly positive for the two-input mechanism, including at zero output noise. Pooling changing stimuli could induce an association even in the one-input model, so that conditioning is essential.

The same pair can be distinguished by a passive same-trial bit recording whether the second summary is positive. Independent bit-flip error below one half preserves the covariance difference. For repetitions of one fixed design, suppose trials are independent and the recording errors are mutually independent and independent of the whole model-noise vector. Known marginal probabilities and a known positive lower bound on the covariance difference then yield a finite test with a stated bound on each model’s selection error. This is controlled-error discrimination, not certainty from finite data. No uniform finite sample guarantee exists over the full admitted family without positive separation. At a one-half flip rate, independent recording erases the distinction. These written results concern the specified passive recording map and two-model comparison; applying them requires warrant for that observation contract and model restriction.

A separate sequential rule removes the need to supply the marginal baseline or a numerical separation margin. From independent repetitions at the fixed design, it estimates the two marginal probabilities and their joint probability, stopping when the estimated covariance clears a simultaneous confidence threshold. Under the one-input model, the probability of ever making a false positive selection is bounded by the chosen tolerance. At each fixed positive-covariance alternative, it stops almost surely in finite time. It never returns a decision for the one-input model; failure to stop by a finite sample size does not prove absence of contribution. Over the specified family with unknown recording error approaching one half, no common two-answer rule can both terminate almost surely under the fixed null and meet uniform error bounds whose sum is below one. No finite uniform sample bound or verified numerical implementation is supplied. The probability guarantees still depend on the passive observation contract and candidate model class.

For these two fully specified, parameter-matched mechanisms, final answers alone have equal likelihood and leave their prior odds unchanged. This does not make their overall plausibility equal. Both could gain support against worse alternatives, and other evidence or justified assumptions could favour one. With unknown parameters, equal marginal likelihoods also require suitably matched priors; arbitrary prior averaging is not licensed by the pointwise construction.

The positive contribution is an exact account of what particular observations can and cannot discriminate, together with an augmented observation scheme that separates the chosen models. It is written probability mathematics with exact algebraic checks. It is not an empirical fit, a reanalysis of participants’ data, or evidence that either mechanism actually produced an observed group’s answers.

## Local standards and actual productive sources

### What the inherited affirmative case already earns

The foundational inquiry begins with actual reason-responsive correction: a subject reaches a warranted correction through an actual exercise of relevant powers. The earlier synthesis preserves real agency in received powers and actual supply wherever reception is independently established in the stated respect. An uninstantiated description cannot replace an actual supplier. These are positive starting points, not merely constraints on future work.

Three stronger achievements must be distinguished. One is identifying an actual original or strictly uncausable contributor to the correction. Another is establishing the necessary existence of that same contributor. A third is identifying one independently complete bearer of the correction’s whole productive origin. The first two can be earned without first completing the third. Conversely, a source somewhere must not be assigned a role in this correction without evidence of that participation.

The inherited constructive route combines actual disjoint productive supply with a case-specific account of intrinsic necessity. If an actual contributor lies outside the exhaustive relevant causability class, its strict uncausability follows. If its own mode of actuality also excludes its absence, necessity belongs to that particular contributor. Plurality of contributors is not by itself an obstacle. Neither whole-source unity nor an explanation of every possibility is a prerequisite to this narrower result.

The remaining warrant question concerns the content of the offered reasons. After generously granting an actual strictly uncausable contributor and identifying it with nonreceived actuality, the intrinsic route still needs warrant that this mode excludes existential optionality, rather than only explaining its powers when present. A case-specific basic recognition could have that stronger content. The inherited synthesis gives the route explanatory credit while finding no newly established necessary productive participant in the admitted correction.

Ordinary dependence of a whole on its combined origins does not alone establish that stronger relation. No new necessary producer of the actual correction is established. These are manuscript-specific findings; equivalence to the 2001 reply's journal body was not verified.

### A proposal for natural standards

Foot’s author-hosted 2009 revised interview sample proposes standards fixed by a creature’s way of life and what its activities require, rather than numerical typicality or the evaluator’s preference. Functional relevance distinguishes a defect from a harmless variation. Changing environments can alter requirements, but a convenient exceptional setting cannot automatically redefine excellence. She leaves reference-class boundaries loose. Her human extension concerns intelligent recognition of reasons and a good will; the prudence argument pressures desire-only rationality and seeks to include justice and charity. This gives substantive support for local fittingness, while leaving rights and human flourishing incompletely developed. It neither selects an ultimate productive bearer nor rules one out. The witness is a 2009 author-supplied proof, not the unread 2001 book chapter or a verified text of the 2003 interview. [Foot and Voorhoeve, revised interview sample, pp. 96–107](<https://personal.lse.ac.uk/voorhoev/07-Voorhoeve-Chap04.pdf>).

The governing question across these inquiries is what a consideration warrants about its actual subject. A local norm, a causal default, a bearer’s necessity and the identity of an ultimate source need not share one evidential route. Giving a partial argument its earned positive weight is compatible with withholding a stronger attribution. Failure to establish that stronger attribution does not by itself prove its contrary or equalise every rival explanation.
