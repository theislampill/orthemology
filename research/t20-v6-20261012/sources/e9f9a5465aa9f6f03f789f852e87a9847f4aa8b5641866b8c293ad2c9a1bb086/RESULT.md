## 1. Result and the actual transport boundary

The proposed finite-cone statement is correct. For a nonzero finite-dimensional closed, convex, pointed, generating cone K, every linear map T with T(K)⊆K and (I−T)(K)⊆K acts as a scalar on each indecomposable direct summand of K. The summands are unique up to ordering. In particular, if K is indecomposable, T=cI with 0≤c≤1.

Consequently a finite positive instrument satisfying Σ_y T_y=I has state-independent outcome probabilities on an indecomposable normalized state cone. On a general cone, its outcomes reweight only the uniquely defined direct-summand masses, while leaving the normalized state within each occupied summand unchanged. The zero-probability branch is a zero vector and has no normalized conditional state.

This is established ordered-cone/GPT mathematics. Loewy–Schneider (1975), Theorem 3.3, is an exact antecedent for the identity-extremality equivalence. Barnum–Wilce and Heinosaari–Leppäjärvi–Plávala explicitly give the relevant information/disturbance and direct-sum conclusions. Section 10 identifies inspected primary passages; no field-level novelty is claimed.

A genuine, limited transport of the constraint results survives: a specified identity-summing instrument admits the same extreme-point and block-conditioning argument as a finite passive Bayesian channel, now on a compact cone base. But the class of admissible maps must travel with the state cone. Replacing restricted passive diagonal likelihood maps by **all** positive maps of a feasible cone can create informative operations that are not any one fixed passive evidence channel about the original target. Section 7 gives an exact three-state countercontrol.

The established H-Continuation and audit-continuation results already transport general instruments, observable spans, predictive probabilities, target numerators, and normalized positive branches. The contribution here is their special identity-summing rigidity boundary and the resulting diagnostic for a proposed constraint transport; it is not a new general instrument or tensor formalism.

## 2. Exact hypotheses and compact-base generation

Let V be a nonzero finite-dimensional real vector space. Throughout, a proper cone K⊂V means a nonzero closed convex cone with K∩(−K)={0} and K−K=V. Positivity of a map always means positivity for this cone, not entrywise positivity in arbitrary coordinates. Write

    Pos(K)={T∈End(V):T(K)⊆K},
    0≤_K T≤_K I  iff  T∈Pos(K) and I−T∈Pos(K).

Choose a strictly positive linear functional u:V→R, meaning u(x)>0 for every nonzero x∈K. Then

    B={x∈K:u(x)=1}

is the normalized base. Every nonzero x∈K has the unique radial normalization x/u(x)∈B. The following elementary argument makes the hypotheses behind extreme generation explicit.

**Lemma 1.** A proper finite cone admits such u; its base B is nonempty compact convex, spans V, and K is generated by its extreme rays using finite conical sums. An extreme point of B corresponds exactly to an extreme ray of K.

**Proof.** Choose an inner product and set S=K∩{x:||x||=1}. It is nonempty compact. The origin does not lie in conv(S): a finite positive convex combination summing to zero would place a nonzero positive multiple of one summand in both K and −K. In finite dimension conv(S) is compact; this follows, for example, by the elementary affine-dependence reduction of every convex combination to at most dim(V)+1 terms. Let z be its nearest point to zero. Then z≠0 and the nearest-point inequality gives ⟨z,s⟩≥||z||² for every s∈S. Thus u(x)=⟨z,x⟩ is strictly positive on K\{0}. This also bounds ||b||≤1/||z||² for b∈B. Closedness of K makes B closed, hence compact; convexity and nonemptiness are immediate. The same boundedness argument works for any strictly positive u by minimizing u over S.

For completeness, every nonempty compact convex Q in finite dimension is the convex hull of its extreme points. Induct on its affine dimension. At a relative boundary point x, take external points z_n→x and nearest points a_n∈Q. Then a_n→x, and a convergent subsequence of unit vectors (z_n−a_n)/||z_n−a_n|| yields a supporting vector v with ⟨v,q−x⟩≤0 for q∈Q. Its equality set is a proper compact face, of smaller affine dimension; induction represents x by its extreme points, which are extreme in Q. A relative interior point lies between the two boundary endpoints of a line section of Q, so it has the same kind of finite representation. The zero-dimensional case starts the induction.

Finally, b∈B is extreme exactly when every decomposition b=x+y with x,y∈K has x,y on its ray: normalize the nonzero summands using u to convert between the two definitions. Apply compact-convex generation to B and unnormalize. Since K generates V, extreme-ray vectors span V and contain a vector-space basis. This does **not** mean that K is simplicial or generated by that chosen basis alone. □

The subsequent algebra needs finite dimensionality, pointedness, generation, and finite conical generation by the collection of extreme rays. Closedness is a sufficient way to guarantee that last property here. We do not silently assume extreme-ray generation outside this setting.

## 3. The identity interval and indecomposability

A direct-sum decomposition K=K₁⊕K₂ means K=K₁+K₂ and span(K₁)∩span(K₂)={0}; both cones are nonzero for a nontrivial decomposition. A cone is indecomposable if no such nontrivial decomposition exists. The term refers to this linear-convex property only.

**Lemma 2.** If 0≤_K T≤_K I and r spans an extreme ray of K, then T r=t_r r for a unique t_r∈[0,1].

**Proof.** The decomposition r=T r+(I−T)r has both terms in K. Extremality forces both onto the ray; their nonnegative coefficients sum to one. □

**Theorem 3.** For a proper finite cone K, the following are equivalent:

1. K is indecomposable.
2. Every T∈[0,I]_K is cI for a scalar c∈[0,1].
3. The identity spans an extreme ray of Pos(K).

**Proof.** Choose an extreme-ray vector-space basis e₁,…,e_d. Lemma 2 makes T diagonal in that basis, with diagonal entries in [0,1]. Every other extreme-ray vector is also an eigenvector of T. Let E_λ be its distinct real eigenspaces. Since K is generated by its extreme rays, every x∈K is a sum of vectors in K∩E_λ. Distinct eigenspaces form a vector-space direct sum, and their span is V. Hence

    K=⊕_λ (K∩E_λ).

Every summand is nonzero because each eigenvalue occurs on a chosen basis ray. Thus a nonscalar T gives a nontrivial cone decomposition. This proves 1⇒2.

Conversely, from K=K₁⊕K₂, the vector-space projection P onto span(K₁) along span(K₂) sends x=x₁+x₂ to x₁. Both P and I−P are positive, and P is nonscalar. Thus 2⇒1. Finally, every positive decomposition I=A+B has A∈[0,I]_K, and every T in that interval gives I=T+(I−T). Therefore 2 and 3 are precisely equivalent. □

No self-duality, inner-product order, tensor product, homogeneity, or complete positivity is needed for this theorem.

## 4. Canonical summands with a direct finite proof

The word “canonical” requires more than repeatedly choosing an arbitrary decomposition. Here is a proof of uniqueness and of the complete branch classification.

Keep the extreme-ray vector-space basis e₁,…,e_d. For each extreme-ray vector r, expand it uniquely as r=Σ_i r_i e_i; these coefficients may have either sign. Make an undirected graph on {1,…,d}: join i and j if some extreme-ray vector has both r_i≠0 and r_j≠0. Let J₁,…,J_m be its connected components, and define

    V_a=span{e_i:i∈J_a},    K_a=K∩V_a,
    P_a:V→V_a  the coordinate projection in this basis.

This is a finite graph definition but not an asserted algorithm for an arbitrary description of all extreme rays.

**Theorem 4.** These K_a are proper indecomposable cones in V_a, and

    V=⊕_a V_a,    K=⊕_a K_a,
    [0,I]_K={Σ_a c_a P_a: 0≤c_a≤1}.                         (1)

Every direct-sum decomposition of K groups entire K_a together. Consequently the decomposition into indecomposable summands is unique up to permutation and independent of the initially chosen extreme-ray basis.

**Proof.** Every extreme-ray vector has its coordinate support in one graph component. Its projection P_a is therefore either itself or zero. By finite conical extreme generation, all P_a are positive and K=Σ_a K_a. The coordinate subspaces give directness. Each K_a is closed, convex, pointed, and spans V_a because it contains the corresponding basis rays.

For T∈[0,I]_K, write T e_i=t_i e_i. If T r=t_r r, comparison of basis coefficients gives r_i(t_i−t_r)=0. Hence t_i=t_j on every graph edge and therefore on each J_a. This proves one inclusion in (1). Conversely, positivity of P_a and I−Σ_a c_aP_a=Σ_a(1−c_a)P_a proves the other.

Suppose K=⊕_b L_b is any cone direct-sum decomposition, and Q_b are its linear projections. They satisfy 0≤Q_b≤I and Q_b²=Q_b. By (1), Q_b=Σ_a c_baP_a; idempotence forces c_ba∈{0,1}. The identity sum forces each a to occur in exactly one b. Since Q_bK=L_b, every L_b is a sum of whole K_a. If one K_a had a nontrivial further decomposition, adjoining the other K_a would violate this conclusion. Thus each K_a is indecomposable, and any indecomposable decomposition consists of exactly these cones. □

As a useful wider form, a positive linear map which preserves every extreme ray individually is Σ_a c_aP_a with c_a≥0: the same diagonal/support argument works without the upper bound. This is the familiar nondisturbing-map classification. Neither a merely extreme-preserving map which permutes rays nor an arbitrary positive map has this form.

## 5. Instruments and conditional normalization

Let Y be a nonempty finite outcome set and let T_y∈Pos(K) satisfy

    Σ_y T_y=I.                                                (2)

This is **average identity on every state of the declared cone**. It is stronger than mass preservation uΣ_yT_y=u, stronger than fixing one state, and different from preserving a selected list of observables.

By Theorem 4 there is a stochastic array c_ya≥0, Σ_yc_ya=1 such that

    T_y=Σ_a c_ya P_a.                                         (3)

For x∈B, write x_a=P_a x, w_a=u(x_a), and s_a=x_a/w_a when w_a>0. Strict positivity of u implies w_a=0 exactly when x_a=0. Then

    x=Σ_(a:w_a>0) w_a s_a,
    α_y(x)=u(T_yx)=Σ_a c_ya w_a,
    x|y=T_yx/α_y(x)
       =Σ_(a:w_a>0) [c_ya w_a/α_y(x)] s_a                    (4)

whenever α_y(x)>0. The new component weights follow ordinary finite Bayes on label a with likelihood c_ya. Each surviving s_a is unchanged. Outcome probabilities depend on the component weights alone. A particular instrument can merge components with identical outcome columns; it need not disclose a component perfectly, in finitely many observations, or at all.

If α_y(x)=0, positivity gives c_ya w_a=0 for every a, hence T_yx=0. There is no conditional state at this branch. In an indecomposable cone, T_y=c_yI, α_y(x)=c_y for every x∈B, and x|y=x if c_y>0. If c_y=0 the branch map is zero everywhere. This does not assign a posterior to an impossible outcome.

For a known input parameter θ selecting x_θ∈B in an indecomposable cone, the likelihood of y is the same c_y for every θ. Thus an ordinary external prior over θ is unchanged on every positive-probability outcome. This is the precise statistical meaning of “no state information” here; it concerns the declared input-state family, not unrelated records or unrepresented targets.

Positive identity-summing maps are an upper bound on available nondisturbing operations when an actual theory or device permits fewer maps. Positivity alone does not prove physical realizability. No composite-system state cone has been chosen here, so no tensorial or ancillary-system conclusion is inferred merely from positivity.

## 6. The constraint classifier that really does transport

Fix K,u and one instrument (2). Group canonical indices a by equality of their full outcome columns (c_ya)_y. Write G₁,…,G_r for these groups,

    Q_j=Σ_(a∈G_j)P_a,    B_j=B∩range(Q_j),
    κ_yj=c_ya  for a∈G_j.

Let C⊆B be nonempty closed convex. Call it stable when T_yx/α_y(x)∈C for every x∈C and every positive-probability outcome y.

**Theorem 5.** The following are equivalent:

1. C is stable.
2. Every extreme point of C lies in some B_j.
3. C=conv(⋃_j(C∩B_j)).
4. For every x∈C and j with u(Q_jx)>0, Q_jx/u(Q_jx)∈C.

**Proof.** For any x∈C, equation (2) gives the barycenter identity

    x=Σ_(y:α_y(x)>0) α_y(x)(x|y).

If x is extreme and C is stable, every defined x|y=x. Thus T_yx=α_y(x)x for every y, also when α_y(x)=0 because the branch vector then vanishes. Projecting onto a nonzero x_a gives c_ya=α_y(x) for every y. All occupied summands therefore have identical columns, proving 1⇒2. Compact-convex generation of C proves 2⇒3. Uniqueness of the vector-space block decomposition shows that conditioning a convex mixture on group j is again a convex mixture of points from C∩B_j, so 3⇒4. Finally, formula (4) grouped by j expresses each x|y as a convex mixture of those conditioned group states, proving 4⇒1. □

The smallest closed convex stable family containing x is therefore

    conv{Q_jx/u(Q_jx): u(Q_jx)>0}.                            (5)

Its vertices are in independent summand subspaces, so their weights are unique. This is a stable-hull theorem, not a reachability or perfect-discrimination theorem for the actual noisy instrument. If all its κ_yj are positive, every conditional update is a bijection of a stable C by reciprocal reweighting of group masses; such inverse weights need not be physically available signals.

For K=Rⁿ_+, P_a selects coordinate a. Equations (3)–(4) are exactly diagonal likelihood updating, and Theorem 5 is precisely the concurrent repeatable-channel constraint theorem. No new classical theorem is claimed by restating it. For an arbitrary cone, (4) identifies the legitimate common structure: Bayes reweighting of unique direct-summand masses, with within-summand states retained.

If all mathematically allowed identity-summing positive instruments are quantified over, assign pairwise distinct probabilities in (0,1) to the finitely many summands in a binary instrument. Theorem 5 then classifies stable C as convex hulls of arbitrary closed convex within-summand families. In an indecomposable cone every C is stable, because every defined update is the identity. Hence the classical “universal preservation selects faces” conclusion does **not** carry over unchanged: singleton rays are what make the orthant case special.

## 7. Exact controls and failed passive transport

### 7.1 The classical orthant

On R²_+, take u(x)=x₁+x₂ and

    T_+=diag(3/4,1/4),    T_−=diag(1/4,3/4).

They are positive and sum to I. At p=(1/2,1/2), both outcome probabilities are 1/2, and the conditional laws are (3/4,1/4) and (1/4,3/4). Each pure coordinate state is unchanged on every possible branch, while the observer's mixed law changes. Averaging those conditional laws with their probabilities gives p. Average nondisturbance does not mean that a conditional mixed law remains numerically equal to its prior.

### 7.2 A feasible cone admits operations which are not fixed passive Bayes

Use the inherited family

    C={p∈Δ₃:p₁=1/2},
    K_C={x≥0:x₁=x₂+x₃},    V_C=span(K_C).

It has extreme rays r=(1,1,0), s=(1,0,1), and is simplicial in its own two-dimensional span. Define, on V_C,

    P_r(x)=(x₂,x₂,0),    P_s(x)=(x₃,0,x₃).

Both preserve K_C and P_r+P_s=I on V_C. Under u(x)=x₁+x₂+x₃ they yield the normalized endpoints a=(1/2,1/2,0), b=(1/2,0,1/2), with probabilities 2p₂ and 2p₃. At p=(1/2,1/4,1/4), they give a,b with equal weight. Thus the abstract feasible-cone instrument is informative about its summand label.

But no fixed ambient diagonal likelihood map D_L restricts to P_r on V_C. P_r r=r requires L₁=L₂=1, while P_r s=0 requires L₁=L₃=0, a contradiction. Indeed an ambient diagonal D_L preserves all K_C exactly when L₁=L₂=L₃: on x₁=x₂+x₃ the image condition is L₁(x₂+x₃)=L₂x₂+L₃x₃ for every nonnegative x₂,x₃. Thus all globally preserving passive-channel branches are scalar, just as the inherited affine incidence test says.

At any one p∈C, classical splitting realizes these cone branches locally with the prior-dependent likelihood row (p₂/p₁,1,0) and its coordinatewise complement; p₁=1/2 is always positive. At the displayed current prior, the rows are (1/2,1,0) and (1/2,0,1). That does not realize P_r,P_s uniformly over C. At p′=(1/2,1/8,3/8), realizing the cone projection requires the first row to be (1/4,1,0) instead. One is changing the channel with the prior. The inherited fixed-prior splitting packet supplies the construction and the separate actual-source availability guard.

This is not a contradiction between the two theorems. The cone model's component label is an alternative abstract state coordinate, not automatically the original target atom X. The larger intrinsic map class does not preserve the original passive evidence contract. Retaining only C and forgetting its embedding and admissible branch class loses essential structure.

### 7.3 The 2 by 2 positive semidefinite cone

Let V=Herm₂(C), considered as a four-dimensional real vector space, K={A:A≽0}, and u(A)=tr(A). Write the Pauli matrices σ_x,σ_y,σ_z and their six rank-one projectors P_(k,±)=(I±σ_k)/2.

If 0≤_K T≤_K I, then 0≼T(P)≼P for each rank-one projector. For any vector v orthogonal to its range, 0≤v* T(P)v≤v*Pv=0. A positive semidefinite matrix with zero quadratic form on v annihilates v (diagonalize it). Hence T(P)=a_P P, 0≤a_P≤1.

Compare

    T(I)=a_(z,+)P_(z,+)+a_(z,−)P_(z,−)
        =a_(x,+)P_(x,+)+a_(x,−)P_(x,−).

Independence of I,σ_x,σ_z forces both within-pair differences to vanish and their common values to coincide, say c. Thus T(I)=cI, T(σ_x)=cσ_x, and T(σ_z)=cσ_z. Comparing the y pair similarly gives T(σ_y)=cσ_y. These four matrices form a real basis, so T=cI_V. Theorem 3 also proves K indecomposable, without assuming it. The six-projector linear constraints alone already leave just this scalar family.

This is an illustration of the established theorem, not a new quantum result. Scalar branches c_yρ normalize to ρ when c_y>0 and have outcome probability c_y; a zero scalar branch is impossible.

A standard physical measurement falls outside average identity: let J₀(ρ)=P_(z,+)ρP_(z,+) and J₁(ρ)=P_(z,−)ρP_(z,−). Both are completely positive, and their sum preserves trace. Their sum fixes diagonal states, including I/2, but sends P_(x,+) to I/2 rather than leaving it unchanged. On P_(x,+), (I_V−J₀)(P_(x,+)) has matrix [[0,1/2],[1/2,1/2]], whose determinant is −1/4. Thus J₀ is not dominated by I_V in positive-map order. The experiment legitimately gains information about z populations while changing other input states.

Embedding the classical two-state cone as diagonal density matrices preserves the classical instrument on that subcone. Extending its domain to all density matrices does not preserve the premise ΣJ_y=I_V. Domain extension is exactly where that attempted stronger transport fails.

### 7.4 The closedness boundary

Take K={(x₁,x₂):x₁>0,x₂>0}∪{0} in R². It is convex, pointed, and generating but not closed, and has no extreme rays. It is indecomposable: any nontrivial direct sum in dimension two would consist of two ray cones and would give extreme rays of K. Yet T=diag(1/4,3/4) and I−T both preserve K. The scalar conclusion therefore fails. Its base under x₁+x₂ is the open simplex; compact extreme generation is the missing hypothesis.

## 8. Faithful representation and the meanings that must travel

An order isomorphism F:V→W with F(K)=K′ and u′F=u transports the whole calculation exactly:

    T′_y=F T_y F⁻¹,
    Σ_yT′_y=I_W,
    u′T′_yF x=uT_yx,
    F(T_yx/uT_yx)=T′_yF x/(u′T′_yF x)

on positive branches. It also preserves direct decompositions and hence the indecomposable summands, up to ordering. This is genuinely structure-preserving transport, provided the admissible instrument class is conjugated as well.

The inherited more general compression pattern is also valid when supplied: a surjective linear F with F(K)=K′, u′F=u, and F T_y=T′_yF preserves branch probabilities and conditional image states. If ΣT_y=I, surjectivity gives ΣT′_y=I. But an arbitrary coarse map need not admit such intertwining T′_y, and compression need not preserve distinguishable fine states or their internal geometry. H-Continuation/audit-continuation already establish the operative intertwining construction in their finite models. No new quotient-minimality result is claimed here.

In applications one must distinguish:

- **Passive Bayesian law updating.** A fixed target X is unchanged. Branches are D_(K(y|·)) on its law, and their sum is identity. Conditioning changes what the observer's law says; it need not physically change X.
- **Average nondisturbance.** The nonselective operator is identity on every state in the explicitly declared domain. It is not merely preservation of mass, one prior, or one readout.
- **Physical interventions.** A general instrument has positive branches with a mass-preserving sum S, often S≠I. The theorem does not rule out useful or permissible information acquisition by these instruments. If the question concerns an immutable initial target, retaining that label remains necessary after transitions.
- **Epistemic and normative warrant.** A cone, instrument, likelihood, or commuting diagram does not establish the accuracy of a source, justified prior, correct evidence model, provenance, normative authority, or permission to observe, update, report, or act. Numeric feasibility and actual warrant remain separate.

In particular, “indecomposable” here says nothing about the metaphysical simplicity of a bearer, absence of intrinsic attributes, unity of a creator, or the admissibility of distinctions within a real subject. Neither a quantum label nor a convex structure supplies a warranted identification with psychological, noetic, or source-ontological objects.

## 9. Additional limits preventing false converse claims

The fixed-prior constrained-learning packet establishes which classical posterior splittings are realizable by some passive channel at one prior. It does not imply that every decomposition of an arbitrary cone state is implemented by a globally identity-summing instrument. A mixed qubit I/2 has many nontrivial convex decompositions, but Theorem 3 forbids such an instrument on the full PSD cone. Local barycenter feasibility is weaker than uniform positive linear realizability.

Conversely, any finite decomposition x=Σ_yα_y q_y on a normalized cone base defines positive preparation maps R_y(z)=α_y u(z)q_y. They sum to the reset channel z↦u(z)x, fixing the selected x. Their outcome probabilities α_y are independent of every input z. A changing conditional output state is therefore not, by itself, information about which input was supplied. Classical posterior semantics require the fixed-target joint-law relation in addition to a convex decomposition.

No state-information result about arbitrary history-dependent instruments follows without checking the conditional branch maps at each node. If every admitted instrument is identity-summing on the same K, its branches are all componentwise scalar; finite products and correctly modelled adaptive selections can still depend only on the same summand masses. Unmodelled state-dependent selection is an additional signal. No sampling rates, stopping theorem, physical repeatability, unlimited access, or perfect finite disclosure is inferred.

No infinite-dimensional extension is asserted. No approximate nondisturbance or robustness bound follows from the exact identity statement. No Bregman, Fisher, divergence-contraction, tensor-factor, or differential-dynamics result follows from cone indecomposability. These require their own retained structures.
