# What the full reading changes

These six supplied works present an affirmative, connected case for knowing a real Creator and reasoning about that Creator’s attributes. Their contribution is substantially richer than permission to consider theism, a conditional model of belief, or an argument for an unspecified first cause. They offer direct recognition, arguments from actual origination and existential need, accounts of intrinsic power and perfection, and an epistemology that connects perception, reason and testimony.

The strongest historical finding is clear: Ibn Taymiyya is represented as claiming actual knowledge of the Creator, sometimes immediately and sometimes through reasoning. The strongest philosophical contribution is a set of substantive arguments whose force depends on their stated content. A necessary existent, an actual cause, a completely original Creator and an essentially wise source are distinguishable descriptions. The sources often supply reasons for moving between them; those reasons deserve assessment rather than silent omission or automatic acceptance.

This guide identifies what each supplied version contributes to four questions: Necessary Being, divine attributes, objective norms and knowledge. It separates attribution from appraisal, gives decisive printed and PDF page references, and flags concrete textual problems that affect reuse. It accompanies the same Seventeenth inquiry; the main report gives the current philosophical judgments after the cumulative rereading. It does not announce a new theorem, the completion of that inquiry, or a proof of all its metaphysical conclusions.

## Terms that need care

Fiṭrah denotes the account’s natural, truth-directed human disposition, whose operation can develop or be impaired. It is not every felt conviction. Tawātur concerns transmission sufficient for knowledge under relevant qualitative and quantitative conditions, rather than a fixed number of votes. A fortiori reasoning concerns the necessary source’s priority in pure perfection, including both original giving and a distinct route extending beyond perfections realised in creatures. A necessary concomitant is an attribute that cannot be absent while its bearer exists. “Necessary knowledge” can mean unavoidable cognition without making its object metaphysically necessary.

# The Asfahani commentary on existence and need

## Historical argument

The commentary’s starting point is affirmative. Actual coming-to-be discloses an originator; contingency discloses existential need; and existence through oneself involves sufficiency through the reality that exists. The full text should not be replaced by a weaker claim that an explanation would be desirable if one happened to be available.

Printed pp. 22–23 / PDF pp. 29–30 distinguish the claim that a necessary existent exists from the claim that the intended Creator of the heavens and earth exists. The author observes that someone can accept the first while denying the second. The later attribute arguments therefore require actual contingent effects and the relevant productive relation, rather than relying on the word “necessary” alone.

Printed pp. 23–25 / PDF pp. 30–32 use the origination of animals, plants and minerals. Their reality excludes impossibility; their prior nonexistence excludes necessity through themselves. More importantly, the author treats the originated thing’s need for an originator as clearer than the longer modal reconstruction. Rational knowledge need not await a more elaborate proof of something already evident. This supports direct recognition of dependence in concrete cases without requiring a recovered history of every causal step.

Printed pp. 25–26 / PDF pp. 32–33 develop the stronger modal principle. What admits existence and nonexistence does not possess actuality through itself. If something actually exists and does not receive existence from another, its own reality must suffice. This is not efficient self-production by a subject before it exists. The discussion accommodates different accounts of essence and existence while retaining the contrast between received actuality and intrinsic sufficiency.

Printed pp. 26–27 / PDF pp. 33–34 then address completeness. A purported complete cause cannot leave another required productive condition outside its provision. A collection of contingent beings does not automatically become self-sufficient merely by being collected. The objection concerns existential need and complete productive provision, rather than the numerical size of a series.

## Appraisal and limits

The argument has positive philosophical content: modal openness is understood as a lack of existential sufficiency, and complete explanation must account for what remains received. Those connections may be rationally recognised without reduction to premises neutral between every school. Calling them evident, however, does not itself give every reader that recognition. Their content, application and response to objections remain the appropriate subjects of debate.

A local originated event alone does not identify its basis as the thinker’s whole existential source or the unique Creator. The main report now accepts a minimal same-bearer Necessary Being result under the whole-existence concrete-bearer framework and contingency-as-need principle. That result does not require naming the bearer or establishing its sole universal creatorhood. Stronger common-Creator identification remains a separate question; a causal diagram alone does not settle it.

The later text guards against conflating two regresses. Printed p. 70 / PDF p. 77 distinguishes causes or agents from acts or effects; pp. 107–109 / PDF pp. 114–116 permit beginningless successive acts. The foundational argument therefore cannot fairly be presented as a rejection of every infinite temporal history.

# The Asfahani commentary on attributes and knowledge

## A real intrinsically qualified source

Printed pp. 29–37 / PDF pp. 36–44 criticise a composition-based proof of uniqueness. An inseparable attribute need not be an independently produced part. Mutual conditioning differs from reciprocal efficient production; a common concept is not an extra concrete component shared by its instances. The author also rejects a wholly attribute-free external reality at printed p. 34 and pp. 80–82 / PDF p. 41 and pp. 87–89.

Knowledge receives several distinct arguments at printed pp. 39–40 / PDF pp. 46–47. Willing involves apprehension of what is willed. Ordered, proficient production indicates knowledge. Knowledge is a perfection. The original giver of that perfection is more entitled to it than a received effect. The first route presupposes volition; the others supply different grounds. The translation’s “fine tuning” here means ordered or proficient making and should not be silently turned into a modern cosmological probability calculation.

Printed pp. 41–44 / PDF pp. 48–51 distinguish an eternally necessitating bare essence from an agent possessing power and will. Novel effects cannot all be eternal consequences of an unqualified essence alone. Differentiated effects also motivate an argument from specification to choice. Life is connected with knowledge and power at printed p. 43 / PDF p. 50. This is a claim about living agency, not animal physiology.

Printed pp. 109–111 / PDF pp. 116–118 distinguish two routes: attribution of every genuine pure perfection as perfection, even if unrealised in creatures, and the original giver’s greater entitlement to a perfection actually given. The cumulative ascent is therefore not capped at donated efficacy. Printed pp. 127–130 / PDF pp. 134–137 add both the necessary source’s superiority to contingent recipients and the giver’s priority. A genuinely possible, nondefective standing perfection cannot be borrowed by an intrinsically sufficient source. The method concerns an actual source and eligible perfections, not an ontological proof from a description alone.

## Acts norms and epistemic confidence

Standing capacities and particular acts remain distinct. Printed pp. 53–56, 107–111 / PDF pp. 60–63, 114–118 discuss speech and voluntary action without making every token act eternal. Necessary power does not entail that every possible effect occurs. Mercy and wisdom receive positive treatment at printed pp. 15–20 and 221–230 / PDF pp. 22–27 and 228–237, through benefit, need, fitting ends and the distinction between willing and loving.

Printed p. 226 / PDF p. 233 supports a combined account of value and command: deeds have their own evaluatively relevant features, which instruction can disclose, and command can introduce a wise practical relation. This does not establish unrestricted lawmaking authority. A particular directive’s rightful force and adequate communicative integrity still require warrant.

The later epistemology is integral to these arguments. Cumulative support need not have one uniquely identifiable decisive report: printed pp. 137–138 and 147 / PDF pp. 144–145 and 154. Acquaintance with expertise does not confer all the expert’s knowledge or infallibility: pp. 173–176 / PDF pp. 180–183. No single compulsory proof-route or initial doubt is required: pp. 184–186 / PDF pp. 191–193.

The main report assesses these routes cumulatively, including the separate general perfection principle. Rejecting bare necessitation alone does not establish will. Bodily examples at printed pp. 222–223 / PDF pp. 229–230 remain illustrations within older natural philosophy, not verified medical evidence. Suffering and alternative complete histories require their own assessment.

# How to handle the Asfahani translation

## The edition and its voices

The translator’s preface, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 8–9, identifies a 2001 Maktabat al-Rushd edition edited by Saʿīd ibn Naṣr ibn Muḥammad as the principal source. It also uses a longer 2009 Dār al-Minhāj edition edited by Muḥammad ibn ʿAwdah al-Saʿawī to repair passages, omissions and formatting, occasionally adding short sentences. The result is a selectively harmonised English witness, not a diplomatic rendering of one unchanged Arabic edition.

The creed at printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 12–13 belongs to Asfahani; Ibn Taymiyya’s commentary begins on printed p. 6. Subheadings and section numbers are supplied editorially, as notes 15 and 50 acknowledge. The long Ghazali material at printed pp. 158–173 / PDF pp. 165–180 is quoted material, followed by agreement and criticism. Opponents’ positions must retain their speakers.

## Three checked local translation problems

The following discrepancies were checked against the supplied English pages and an [electronic Arabic witness of the commentary](https://www.islamicbook.ws/ageda/alaqidt-alasfhanit-001.html). The electronic text’s exact printed-edition basis remains unverified; this comparison is not a manuscript collation or a global reliability verdict.

1\. Printed pp. 5, 22 and 26 / PDF pp. 12, 29 and 33: language rendered as inherent self-justification concerns an effect’s sufficiency through its complete cause. The effect needs nothing beyond that cause; a contingent cause can still need another. Reading the English as self-causation would distort the relation under discussion.

2\. Printed p. 26 / PDF p. 33: a clause about actual nonexistence loses the Arabic modal qualification. The relevant claim concerns what admits, or is susceptible to, nonexistence. The surrounding English retains the intended modal argument, so a local correction is sufficient.

3\. Printed p. 78 / PDF p. 85: the sentence assigning probabilistic and apodictic material to syllogism and analogy reverses the assignments required by its context. The Arabic and surrounding English support the distinction between certain and merely probable material. The point is that certainty depends on evidential content, not on inferential form alone.

## Translator note 58

Printed pp. 24–25 / PDF pp. 31–32 contain a separately confirmed editorial logic problem. The main text distinguishes an indicant that entails an indicated conclusion from an invalid converse. The rain-and-wetness illustration switches those roles. Wetness alone does not deductively entail rain; a sprinkler or spill supplies a counterexample. It can still be good, defeasible evidence for rain in context.

For a clean deductive illustration, divisibility by four entails evenness, while evenness does not entail divisibility by four. This repairs the example without rejecting ordinary evidential inference or the nearby causal argument. The wording about specification at printed p. 44 / PDF p. 51 has now been clarified against the 2009 Arabic Asfahani edition published by Dār al-Minhāj. Page 7 gives the corrected reading and its limits.

# Oneness and complete original lordship

## What this separate witness adds

This establishes the passage’s presence in that witness, not the English translator’s identity or exact exemplar. It does not establish a separately authored second commentary. Page 7 explains the comparison’s limits and wording corrections.

The main excerpt closes by relating itself to the requested Asfahani commentary, printed p. 42 / PDF p. 49. Its biography, summary, notes, bracketed additions, headings and five diagrams are editorial layers. A similarly titled published volume cannot supply the missing translator identity merely because the title resembles this one.

## Power and unity

Printed pp. 17–22 / PDF pp. 24–29 distinguish wholly powerless supposed creators from genuinely powered agents. Two things cannot each supply the power the other must already possess to perform that supply. Created agents can nevertheless cooperate when each already has efficacy or another source enables them. The argument therefore does not eliminate every plural-powers proposal in its first step.

Printed pp. 20–21 / PDF pp. 27–28 argue that an original Maker cannot obtain creative power from its own product. Printed p. 25 / PDF p. 32 adds the modal consequence: once power is a necessary concomitant of a necessary bearer, it cannot disappear while that bearer exists. The essential-attribute premise matters alongside necessary existence.

Printed pp. 23–29 / PDF pp. 30–36 argue against two independently complete causes of the same effect. Each is described as exclusively accounting for the effect’s whole productive reality. Two compatible partial contributors, or two independently sufficient factors in an ordinary overdetermination example, do not automatically satisfy that exclusive-completeness description.

Printed pp. 30–33 / PDF pp. 37–40 then appeal to interacting natural effects. If each supposed lord completely maintains its own field, cross-field assistance or defeat challenges that completeness. The argument has force under complete original lordship and productive interconnection. The cumulative perfection argument gives positive reasons for broader standing ability. Even granting that ability, the precise issue is whether compatible contributions or agreed changes in effects must alter or supply an agent’s essential power. The source’s standing-power and particular-will distinctions matter here; no plural ultimate is thereby shown possible.

## Scope and practical use

The cumulative case combines original efficacy, essential independence, pure perfection, connected creation and explanations of concord; it is much richer than a quarrel dilemma. Printed p. 34 / PDF p. 41 also distinguishes power from actual willing: an act can lie within power without occurring. This helps prevent an inference from necessary capacity to necessary performance of every act.

Unity of lordship and exclusive worship are separately discussed at printed pp. 7–16 / PDF pp. 14–23. Creation, benefit and rightful dependence contribute to the latter normative claim. The editorial spatial argument in note 26, printed p. 33, should not be silently attributed to Ibn Taymiyya. Figure 3a, PDF p. 54, also has a branch whose terminal conclusion does not transparently follow; the prose, rather than the flowchart, must carry the argument.

# Suleiman on ontology and divine perfection

## A scholarly reconstruction with several layers

[Ibn Taymiyya and the Attributes of God](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1bnIdhLEOw2_P2p7xanFqbN9m8OBoNIEQ/view) is Farid Suleiman’s study, translated by El-Tobgui from German. The German original was a lightly revised doctoral dissertation completed in 2017, defended in 2018 and published in 2019. The supplied English title and imprint are at PDF pp. 4–5. Primary quotations, Suleiman’s paraphrases, his reconstructions and his criticisms need separate attribution.

The historical chapters make the distinctions consequential. Avicenna’s necessity, simplicity and necessary-production scheme at PDF pp. 65–68 is a counterposition. The change-implies-origination premise discussed at PDF p. 99, note 280, is distinguished from Ibn Taymiyya’s account. Ibn ʿArabi’s unity of being at PDF pp. 138–153 is not numerical unity of a Creator distinct from creation. At printed p. 210 / PDF p. 223, a seven-attribute chain is identified as Ibn Taymiyya’s report of an Ashʿari argument, with parts completed by Suleiman.

## Why attributes need not be externally assembled parts

Real attributes and a real essence can be inseparable aspects of one concrete being without being detached components manufactured by an external agent. See printed p. 110 / PDF p. 123 and p. 240 / PDF p. 253. Shared concepts permit meaningful comparison without requiring a numerically shared extramental substance or an identical creaturely manner of instantiation. See also PDF pp. 168–179 and 190–200.

This contribution matters once an actual Necessary Being is granted. Further attribute questions need not repeatedly restart an existence proof. Yet inseparability alone does not make an arbitrary bearer necessary, and shared semantic meaning alone does not establish that a predicate actually applies to God.

## The original giver and pure perfection

Printed pp. 235–246 / PDF pp. 248–259 present the constructive perfection argument, especially through the Akmaliyya. Printed pp. 237–240 distinguish highest possible perfection and its eligibility tests, with rational support from original self-sufficiency and the giver’s priority over recipients. Original giving reinforces the broader perfection method; it does not restrict the entire method to what creatures actually possess. An eligible standing perfection belongs intrinsically, without borrowed completion.

The criteria are substantive. Printed p. 240 / PDF p. 253 requires genuine possibility and freedom from inherent deficiency. Appetite can benefit a needy organism without being a pure perfection in a non-needy Creator. Knowledge need not involve bodily organs; mercy need not mean involuntary weakness. The priority of the original giver is developed at printed p. 239 / PDF p. 252. That page’s note 96 and the following page’s note 99 preserve omissions and ordering differences between cited editions.

The argument provides a positive reason for attributing knowledge and power; it is not a claim that every local cause must share every property of its effect. Debate concerns whether original qualitative adequacy requires the perfection in an eligible original mode, and which attributes satisfy that test. A proposed noncognitive origin of cognition must address that comparative principle; repeating that the effect differs from its local cause does not settle the stronger question.

# Suleiman on wisdom acts and objective norms

## Standing perfection and particular action

Printed pp. 277–283 / PDF pp. 290–296 distinguish an unsupported regress of efficient sources from a beginningless succession of acts. Printed pp. 297–300 / PDF pp. 310–313 distinguish abiding speech capacity from particular temporally originated speech acts. In the account presented, those acts are uncreated without each particular act being eternal.

This supplies an intelligible account of a necessary living agent with occasion-specific acts. It blocks the immediate inference that necessary attributes make every effect or exercise necessary. It does not settle every question about alternative complete histories. If perfect wisdom uniquely requires an act under a fully specified necessary nature, the modal consequences still need to be argued at that level.

## The positive case for wise governance

Printed pp. 241–242 / PDF pp. 254–255 treat appreciation of excellence and rejection of injustice, ignorance and falsehood as a practical perfection. Mere effectiveness in pursuing any goal is weaker than a fitting orientation towards what is good. The original-giver argument applies to this integrated practical capacity as well as to bare power.

Printed pp. 284–289 / PDF pp. 297–302 connect justice with properly ordering and perfecting things. Three routes appear: divine self-commitment, observed beneficial order, and exclusion of injustice as a deficiency. They have different evidential roles. An accepted revelatory commitment cannot independently authenticate that same revelation from scratch; observed order gives positive character evidence; the perfection argument seeks an essential metaphysical constraint.

Printed p. 289 / PDF p. 302 quotes Nubuwwat 2:926 for wisdom as concomitant with knowledge and will, themselves concomitant with the divine essence. Under that strong account, defective volition is excluded. A malicious-source proposal that simply removes essential practical perfection disputes a premise rather than showing that the fully granted wise source can retain an exception.

## Truthfulness and normative authority

The main report accepts exceptionless veracity in genuine source-owned assertions under the same bearer’s essential independence, knowledge, practical perfection and eligible pure-perfection premises. The further primary speech argument on page 8 supports that appraisal. It does not require exclusive actual creation of the whole field; substantive dissent about the purity classification remains. Honesty is not inferred from power alone.

Unrestricted truthfulness requires further care about the scope of assertion and the classification of deception. Causing an event is not automatically asserting a proposition. Quotation, parable, withholding and assertion differ; truthful speech can also be misunderstood. The speech chapter mainly concerns ontology and temporality. The direct Minhāj al-Sunnah argument on page 8 adds a specific account of speech inhering in its speaker; the main report assesses its all-context purity claim.

Suleiman also records a tension between power to do otherwise and the impossibility of acting unwisely, printed pp. 287–289 / PDF pp. 300–302. The fuller Arabic Asfahani pp. 403–405 and 408 directly document the distinction between standing power and wisdom-compatible willing, as described on page 8. The modal senses must remain explicit. Finally, nature-sensitive justice supports objective fittingness. It does not alone establish that the ultimate authority of every norm is constituted by one source’s nature. That further grounding question remains distinct from already warranted local normative truths.

# El Tobgui on reason revelation and recognition

## The author and the undertaking

The [2013 dissertation](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12QgJ-irCA4vpeYe2yBN5CEKML4tcEvaU/view) is by Carl Sharif El-Tobgui alone. Robert Wisnovsky and Wael Hallaq have supervisory or mentoring roles; their appearance in the upload filename does not make them coauthors. The principal Darʾ edition cited is Muḥammad Rashād Sālim’s eleven-volume 1979 edition: front matter xvi, note 6 / PDF p. 16; bibliography p. 322 / PDF p. 346.

The thesis reconstructs a connected epistemology rather than presenting an independent complete catalogue of proofs. Detailed analysis of existence and miracle proofs is outside its stated scope at printed p. 161, note 59 / PDF p. 185. A fuller account of criteria for valid rational proof is postponed at printed p. 183, note 105 / PDF p. 207. Full reading does not enlarge that declared undertaking.

## Actual knowledge and the role of basic cognition

The introduction describes genuine knowledge of God’s existence and fundamental attributes through the kinds of axioms, intuitions and corroboration that support ordinary knowledge: xxi / PDF p. 21. Chapter 5 integrates perception, report, rational principles and inference. Internal perception is said to disclose God and the soul at printed pp. 248, 252 and 254 / PDF pp. 272, 276 and 278. Reason applied to actual empirical data can establish the Creator, although abstract concepts alone do not instantiate an existent: printed p. 275, note 49, and p. 288 / PDF pp. 299 and 312.

Basic perception and rational knowledge need not wait for a second certification by testimony. An isolated child can have empirical and rational certitude: printed pp. 281–282 / PDF pp. 305–306. Tawātur can correct undermined cognition without being a universally prior licence to know. This supports a fair appraisal standard: a successful first-order cognition need not await an agreed philosophical theory of its own reliability.

## What carries over to metaphysics

Printed pp. 246–270 / PDF pp. 270–294 distinguish mental universals from concrete particulars, while retaining meaningful common predication. Essence, existence and inseparable attributes belong to one concrete reality. This applies to contingent things too, so their unity does not itself prove necessity. Printed pp. 229–240 / PDF pp. 253–264 reject the automatic move from conceptual complexity to external assembly or dependence.

Printed pp. 145–149 / PDF pp. 169–173 reconstruct a radical negationist’s chain from common existence to composition and contingency. Ibn Taymiyya attacks that chain; he does not thereby reject Necessary Being. The conclusion further distinguishes meaning, reality and mode of instantiation: printed pp. 290–302 / PDF pp. 314–326. Intelligible divine knowledge need not have a creaturely mode.

The source supports actual Creator-knowledge, plural routes to it and meaningful attribute reasoning. Its historical attribution is stronger than a merely hypothetical model. Appraisal still must examine the positive content of a claimed recognition and relevant defeaters. El-Tobgui’s own tentative formulations, including the innate-form interpretation at printed pp. 272–275 / PDF pp. 296–299, remain scholarly interpretation. Background accounts of Avicenna, Ghazali or other opponents do not become Ibn Taymiyya’s commitments by appearing in this thesis.

# The epistemology anthology and its editorial layers

## What is actually in the collection

The [supplied anthology](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1iBAiImdDodsmO7qY4bykiiFzLd8IxmjM/view) is a modern compilation, not a book authored by Ibn Taymiyya. Its visible components are:

- Doko and Turner, Islamic Religious Epistemology: PDF pp. 4–18, compilation printed pp. 1–15.

- Turner, Ibn Taymiyyah’s Common Sense Philosophy: PDF pp. 19–30, with references resuming at pp. 68–69.

- Editorial Appendix I, Turner’s An Islamic Account of Reformed Epistemology: PDF pp. 31–45. The Baldwin discussion and notes 11–36 are expressly omitted at PDF pp. 32 and 45.

- Editorial Appendix II, Turner’s Who Shoulders the Burden of Proof: PDF pp. 46–67, adapted from the preceding model according to its note 17.

- El-Tobgui, From Legal Theory to Erkenntnistheorie: body PDF pp. 71–100, bibliography pp. 101–105 and endnotes pp. 106–117; abstract and attribution at PDF p. 3.

The supplied file does not establish complete original publication particulars for every contribution or a signed compiler identity. Metadata is not a substitute for that evidence. Its internal citation of Turner’s paper in Philosophy East and West 71(3), 2021, pp. 767–792 is not a collation with the original publication.

## Recognition and a conditional modern model

The historical account affirms actual recognition of the Creator through intact fiṭrah, sometimes supplemented or recovered by reasoning. The repeated citation of Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā 6:73 appears at PDF pp. 15, 23, 26, 36 and 56. PDF p. 64, printed p. 61, cites MF 16:458 for knowledge by necessity or inference. These are several uses of related primary material, not five independent discoveries.

Turner separately identifies the proper-function model as his interpretation or construction at PDF p. 32, printed p. 29. Its extended Islamic form is explicitly conditional on Islam’s truth at PDF p. 60, printed p. 57. The model gives content to conditions of warrant; it does not prove its own actual instantiation. Preserving that condition must not weaken the distinct historical claim of actual Creator-knowledge.

## Tawatur and cumulative warrant

El-Tobgui’s article shows why report quality, competence, contextual detail and reception matter alongside quantity: PDF pp. 77–85. PDF p. 91, printed p. 87, preserves the independence of ordinary immediate knowledge from prior testimonial certification. Note 153 at PDF p. 116 qualifies the restrictive “tawātur alone” formula by including functionally equivalent routes.

The primary-source layer requires care. Al-Musawwada is a family work involving Ibn Taymiyya, his father and grandfather. Note 8 explains attribution practices; the qualitative-transmission formulation discussed in notes 67 and 77 is attributed to Majd al-Din. Hallaq’s Greek Logicians also transmits al-Suyuti’s abridgment. These are identifiable layers of mediation, not direct fresh readings of every underlying Arabic source.

This collection improves the account of basic and cumulative knowledge. Repetition, imitation and circulation of an interpretation do not automatically establish its truth. Its overlap with El-Tobgui’s thesis and between Turner’s own versions can refine one source family without multiplying independent evidential weight.

# Al Ujayri on natural and rational belief

## An accessible cumulative defence

[The Incoherence of Atheism](https://drive.google.com/file/d/17V1wIb5WwvTZmycm_QG3_N6TlINguU-i/view) presents al-Ujayri’s recognitional, causal and teleological case. Its visible cover and title establish his authorship; the metadata name Osman Latiff does not supersede them. Printed pp. 8–9 / PDF pp. 12–13 describe an accessible contemporary defence informed by Ibn Taymiyya, intended to precede deeper specialist work.

Printed pp. 10–16 and 21 / PDF pp. 14–20 and 25 present fiṭrah as an actual cognitive route. Innateness need not mean that an infant consciously possesses articulated theology. Reflection can develop or restore recognition, and the book distinguishes a person who lacks the experience from one who experiences an inclination but doubts its evidential significance. Its account should not be reduced to the psychological popularity or emotional usefulness of religion.

## Origin source and necessary existence

Printed pp. 63–75 / PDF pp. 67–79 begin with particular origination. An actual new individual does not act before existing to produce its own whole existence. That supports actual productive antecedents without first requiring a complete theory of causation. A generated organism can still arise through existing material; origination of the individual is not automatically creation of every antecedent from absolute nothing.

The regress discussions at printed pp. 75–76 and 117–119 / PDF pp. 79–80 and 121–123 seek original efficacy rather than endlessly borrowed provision. Their analogies elucidate a substantive completion judgement. They do not, by themselves, demonstrate that every proposed non-well-founded system is equivalent to inactive intermediaries waiting to receive permission. Printed p. 77 / PDF p. 81 expressly permits beginningless creation as a genus while each created individual begins.

The stronger modal claim is explicit at printed p. 107 / PDF p. 111: contingent being depends on a necessary self-subsisting existent. Note 122 at printed p. 63 / PDF p. 67 distinguishes contingency from temporality. This belongs to the cumulative case assessed in the main report, which accepts minimal same-bearer necessity under its concrete whole-existence framework. Beginninglessness, actual nonreceipt and unconditional necessity remain distinct.

## Attributes norms and limits

The book offers further routes to power, will, life and knowledge: printed pp. 66, 95–96, 104 and 121–128 / PDF pp. 70, 99–100, 108 and 125–132. These include specification, purposive workmanship and the original-giver perfection principle. Printed p. 195 / PDF p. 199 explains why the design claim is not merely an analogy with seeing another universe manufactured.

Objective truth and morality receive separate treatment at printed pp. 25–45 / PDF pp. 29–49. The distinction between being good without belief in God and goodness existing without God is explicit. Yet objective correctness does not identify a concrete necessary producer merely because both are described as absolute; the grounding connection needs its own support.

The appendix, printed pp. 204–213 / PDF pp. 208–217, is al-Sakran’s Galaxies Are Ladders to Faith. Its mediated James Jeans anecdote was not independently authenticated. The conclusion’s note 363 identifies Eric Metaxas’s 2014 article as its information source, so its repetitions are not fresh independent scientific corroboration. Biological, cosmological and fine-tuning citations likewise remain unverified at their underlying sources.

# Corrections to the quantitative illustrations

## The scope of the check

Several numerical passages in the supplied English Incoherence edition require correction. They affect those illustrations and any deductive elimination of chance relying on them. Without the originals, their origin cannot be assigned to author, translator, editor or typesetter. They do not refute the separate recognitional, causal or necessity arguments.

## The combination lock

Printed p. 150 / PDF p. 154 labels the one-in-10,000 probability of a complete four-digit code as the probability of a single digit. Under uniform independent guessing, a specified digit has probability one in ten; a specified four-digit code has probability one in 10,000. The surrounding enumeration indicates that the whole code is intended.

The same paragraph calls ten to the tenth power a billion. In contemporary English it is ten billion, or 10,000,000,000. The exponent is correct under the stated model; the number name is wrong. This wording error leaves the qualitative observation about growing code spaces intact.

## The numbered needles

Printed pp. 153–155 / PDF pp. 157–159 introduce a fictitious dialogue attributed to Nadim al-Jisr’s The Story of Faith. Ten distinct numbered needles are drawn and thrown to a target, where they accumulate. No replacement is specified, but the calculation treats all ten labels as still available on every draw.

Under uniform draws from the remaining needles, the probability of drawing the first two in the specified order is one in ninety, and the first three one in 720. The probability of the complete specified order is one in 3,628,800, rather than one in ten billion. The corrected factor is about 2,756 times larger than the printed factor, but it is still small for one specified order.

That calculation concerns order alone. The successful throws introduce an additional conditional probability that the text does not quantify. The order probability cannot be advertised as the probability of the whole feat, or as the probability that an observed feat was accidental. Those questions require the throwing process, background evidence and competing explanations.

## Small probability and impossibility

Printed pp. 143–145 and 154–155 / PDF pp. 147–149 and 158–159 move between practical improbability and literal impossibility. A finite positive probability does not become zero merely because it is extremely small. A finite resource limit can constrain the number of opportunities; it does not transform a possible outcome into a contradiction.

A charitable practical reading remains available: some chance explanations can be unreasonable relative to the evidence and a supported alternative. That judgement requires a defensible model and context. Low probability under one selected model alone neither identifies a designer nor establishes unrestricted knowledge. Conversely, preserving logical possibility does not force equal evidential weight or suspension of every design inference.

The broader scientific claims were not independently tested. The book itself distinguishes scientific premises from philosophical conclusions at printed p. 129 / PDF p. 133; that distinction should govern its numerical examples too.

# Using the sources together

## Preserve the positive conclusions at their proper scope

For Necessary Being, the sources combine actual origination, contingency as need and intrinsic sufficiency. Asfahani develops the dependence contrast; al-Ujayri supplies accessible routes; the epistemology sources explain recognition and inference. The main report accepts minimal same-bearer necessity under the whole-existence concrete-bearer framework. Numerical uniqueness and common universal creatorhood remain distinct further claims.

For attributes, original-giver and all-pure-perfections reasoning deserve distinct credit. The latter reaches beyond actual gifts. Intrinsic attributes need not introduce assembly; standing power need not entail every exercise. The main report accepts essential independence and unrestricted eligible standing power for the same actual bearer, beyond actual donated efficacy.

For unity, Oneness and the fuller Minhāj al-Sunnah reply combine original efficacy, perfection, interconnected effects and the explanation of concord. Their positive cumulative force exceeds bare necessity. The live objection concerns the move from compatible contribution or intrinsic concord to received essential efficacy, even when broad standing power is granted. No possible plural ultimate is established by that objection.

For norms, objective fittingness and essential practical wisdom support a constructive account. The newer primary speech argument adds the distinction between created defects and defects inhering in the source’s own assertion. The main report accepts exceptionless source-owned veracity under its stated same-bearer perfection premises, with substantive dissent preserved. Authenticating revelation, identifying content and establishing a particular directive’s authority remain separate tasks.

For knowledge, basic cognition, testimony, inference and cumulative signs can cooperate. Disagreement about their ultimate explanation is not automatically a defeater. Equally, alleged intuition, report quantity or a claimed consensus does not make its content true. The sources themselves distinguish intact cognition from error and successful transmission from repetition.

## Keep the source relationships visible

The Asfahani translation and Oneness are related witnesses with different supplied contents. Suleiman and El-Tobgui reconstruct overlapping primary traditions. The anthology repeats and adapts Turner’s model, and its El-Tobgui article develops themes present in the thesis. Al-Ujayri combines related metaphysical arguments with modern empirical material; its conclusion and appendix have identifiable inherited sources. None of these relationships should be multiplied into independent confirmations merely because the discussion appears in several files.

## A focused route back to the evidence

Begin with Asfahani printed pp. 22–27 / PDF pp. 29–34 for existence, then pp. 109–111 and 127–130 / PDF pp. 116–118 and 134–137 for the distinct perfection routes. Read Oneness printed pp. 17–34 / PDF pp. 24–41 for power and unity. Use Suleiman printed pp. 235–246 and 284–289 / PDF pp. 248–259 and 297–302 for perfection and wisdom. For the epistemic standard, read El-Tobgui printed pp. 281–282 and 305–308 / PDF pp. 305–306 and 329–332, followed by anthology PDF pp. 77–85 and p. 116.

These readings support a more substantial affirmative case than a merely hypothetical summary conveys. The guide’s remaining cautions concern identifiable translations, premises, source relationships and inferential scope. They neither certify the entire metaphysical system nor turn every unresolved dispute into an evidential tie.
