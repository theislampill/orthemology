**Core corrections relative to the supplied leads:**
1. A merely defined possible/contingent essence plus symmetric existence/nonexistence is not an unqualified statement of Ibn Taymiyyah's own ontology. Darʾ 8:120–131 critiques the standard Avicennian move and supposed symmetry. **Darʾ 3:288–289 nevertheless contains a positive, direct rejection of an existent that is neither through itself nor another:** do not mistake criticism of one proof for absence of another. Darʾ 3:18 and 240–241 distinguish necessity of intrinsic attributes from numerical uniqueness of the original Creator.
2. The primary anti-two-independent-makers argument and the necessary-agreement case are real and accessible (Jāmiʿ al-Masāʾil 6:94–97; Majmūʿ 20:176–181; Darʾ 9:343). Still distinguish actual sole production from counterfactual sufficiency, and do not build exclusivity into the antecedent when testing it.
3. Qualitative grounding is not simply efficient creation by an equation: Dasgupta's 2009, 2014 and 2016 work has separate theses; group-grounding must be tested fairly.
4. The owner's fiṭrah lead had six unknown citation placeholders. Better primary anchors are now provided, including Bayān Talbīs 4:571 and 4:586 and Darʾ 7:302–303 / 8:348; Ibn al-Qayyim distinguishes innate aptitude from already-articulated infant cognition in Aḥkām Ahl al-Dhimmah 2:948–949.
5. Ibn Taymiyyah's wisdom/agency arguments allow successive chosen acts and differentiate intrinsic essential power from particular achieved effects. Akmaliyya also explicitly qualifies how giver-of-perfection reasoning applies to divine versus creaturely perfections.
