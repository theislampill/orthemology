## 5. Ganya's conditional sourcehood requirement

The retained publisher extraction identifies *Sophia*, publication and version-of-record date 14 August 2026, and DOI 10.1007/s11841-026-01169-y. I directly checked its abstract/metadata, BP/SLC formulation, relevant reductionism discussion, objections, conclusion, and first note against the stored text. This is partial independent review of the publisher article, not independent checking of its entire cited bibliography.

At working L057–065, the bridge requires representation of the actual outcome as issuing from the agent's own action, conditional on robust contrastive responsibility. L125–146 specifies the corresponding de se sourcehood and its explanatory rather than merely extensional role. The condition is stronger than an external observer assigning a centre to an otherwise third-person record. It concerns the cognitive standpoint that underwrites responsibility. The report is right to grant that stronger condition for its philosophical test rather than answer it with a bare annotation.

Three scope limits are essential and retained:

- **Uniqueness is background, not conclusion.** The abstract extends the argument to necessarily unique agents; L136 and L150–151 explicitly work with unique divine agency. No uniqueness theorem follows from that assumption-laden application.
- **Own sourcehood is not exclusive authorship.** The BP requires that the actual outcome issue from the subject's own agency. It does not establish that no other original contributes. Granting the self-ascriptive requirement thus leaves the substantive complete-agency/supremacy question open.
- **Necessary concord is not Ganya's full necessitarianism.** L157 denies genuine modal alternatives and admits only one possible world. Agreement between agents across histories with different possible outcomes does not have that consequence. L158 also preserves a contrastive question in the Leibnizian case, where perfect rationality selects a uniquely best world without the stated metaphysical necessitation.

The report's reservation about L103/L134/L141–143 and L150 is a properly marked critical judgment: an explicitly first-person identity can itself supply self-location, so a substitution warning alone does not settle its explanatory sufficiency. That objection need not be settled for the bounded conclusion. The revised report does not misattribute its additional non-inferential concession to Ganya.

These are interacting interlocutors, not five independent confirmations: Torre uses Lewis and Ganya draws on the essential-indexical tradition. The report and provenance preserve that dependence.

## 6. The Arabic sources supply substantive additional premises

### Universal mastery and actual whole efficacy

I directly reread canonical Akmaliyya L069–090, L210–240, and L248–274 in the stored working source. L217 explicitly considers independent origination even outside the supposed realm and compares dependence by everything with dependence by only some things. The report is therefore justified in refusing to reduce this passage to a large portfolio of created products. L218–223 distinguishes unaided ability, doing the whole oneself, comprehensive creation/power, and successful willing. L235–240 insists on wise discrimination together with unimpeded efficacy; indiscriminate exercise is not its perfection ideal.

The universal-dependence route is a faithful **reconstruction requiring its stated scope**, not a literal de se theorem in the Arabic. If all eligible originals exclude reception in D, and every eligible original supplies every distinct external actual bearer in the same D-respect, a second original is excluded. The putative peer must fall within that external-bearer domain, and the dependence-respect must match. Supremacy language cannot silently turn agreement, descriptive co-reference, or object-conditioned knowledge into donated original completion.

The inherited domain addendum and the targeted eligibility review already distinguish this universal route from the local actual-efficacy route. Their positive comparative appraisal is retained. Calling the principle additional does not make it arbitrary or circular; accepting the source's asserted divine scope does not independently prove its disputed application to every hypothetical original.

### Self-description follows the attributed perfection

Akmaliyya L258–260 treats divine self-praise and revelation of divine attributes as fitting perfections. L260 explicitly connects the speech with its truth about already-attributed perfections. L261–262 distinguishes false appropriation from truthful creaturely self-report, while allowing the latter's practical evaluation to vary with benefit and harm. L264 includes mastery over everything other than the bearer; L265–268 expressly presents exclusive divine excellences.

The report gets the argumentative direction right. True self-description expresses those substantive perfections; first-person form does not establish their truth or scope. The passage permits neither two truthful claimants to a mutually exclusive attribute nor a derivation of exclusive mastery from epistemic self-location alone.

### Comparative eligibility and contradiction

L069–080 requires coherent deficiency-free excellence and excludes contradictory descriptions. L073 expressly rejects producing something that remains necessary through itself. L082–090 then argues that essential incapacity for a genuine pure perfection does not protect an allegedly absolutely perfect candidate: receptivity itself is comparatively superior to total incapacity.

Thus the source can disqualify a proposed two-original hypothesis without demanding that A literally create, acquire, merge with, or become an underived B. The report accurately preserves that affirmative correction. It also correctly preserves the separate burden of establishing the disputed scope as pure perfection; the reply cannot promote being numerically identical to someone else into an epistemic excellence.

### Productive contribution and object-conditioned knowing

The stored Majmu20 subsection L001–020 and Minhaj2 L034–041 genuinely address actual efficacy and completion, not just conflicting wills. Majmu20 L003–008 and L013 presses joint contribution and individual whole provision; Minhaj2 L040–041 expressly treats actual efficacy conditioned by another's activity as a perfection issue. A rival does not answer that argument merely by denying an intrinsic donation of power.

The Dar016 return L1012–1054 must preserve voices and qualifications. It begins with Abū al-Barakāt's quoted account; Ibn Taymiyyah's discussion and alternative replies follow. L1041–1047 recognizes actual knowing as perfection rather than reducing perfection to standing capacity. L1049 distinguishes the other's granting a perfection from the object's conditioning knowledge of it. L1051 grounds the actual objects in the divine agent, and L1052 introduces the contrary scenario hypothetically. The report accurately declines to turn that hypothetical into an authenticated original peer.

Hence neither inference is licensed: object-conditioned knowledge therefore involves a deficient external supplier; or harmless object-conditioned knowledge therefore validates irreducible peer productive contribution. The distinction is exactly material to this task.
