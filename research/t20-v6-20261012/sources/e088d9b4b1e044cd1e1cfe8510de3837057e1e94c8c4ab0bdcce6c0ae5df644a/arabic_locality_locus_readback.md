# Arabic locality locus readback

## Verified access

Ibn Taymiyyah, *Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Aṣfahāniyyah*, Arabic digital transcription, read on 2026-09-30:

[Primary-text rendering](https://www.islamicbook.ws/ageda/alaqidt-alasfhanit-001.html)

Find the distinctive phrase “وهنا أربع مسائل” within the page. The immediately following passage distinguishes two rational matters from two matters of transmitted/linguistic usage. Its first rational matter assigns an attribute's qualification to the locus in which it inheres; the second denies transferring that qualification to another locus. The later matters concern the derivation of a name from the attribute. The surrounding argument rejects identifying the producer of another locus's speech with that speech's speaker.

One short Arabic anchor is:

> فإن الصفة إذا قامت بمحل عاد حكمها على ذلك المحل دون غيره

Working translation: when an attribute inheres in a locus, its qualification belongs to that locus rather than another.

The word “qualification” here is an interpretive translation of ḥukm, not a claim to have established the only acceptable English rendering.

## Research effect

The passage supplies a textual control for the distinction between a bearer and a cause. It does not show that every adequate cause lacks the effect's relevant perfection, nor that an adequate cause could not independently possess that perfection. It blocks one specific shortcut: qualifying a different entity solely by the attribute token that inheres elsewhere.

The distinction is therefore compatible with an argument that the fundamental ground independently knows or speaks. Such an argument must establish the ground's own qualification. Merely identifying it as the ultimate producer of local thinkers or utterances does not make the local token its attribute.

The source itself presents part of this argument as rational. The present note preserves that self-classification. Calling the textual input source-relative does not mean its author regarded the principle as knowable only by revelation. The separate question is whether the rational argument succeeds across the contemporary rival ontologies under discussion.

No claim is made that the source jointly states the repository's six qiyās guards, proves every B7/B8 bridge, or establishes a school-wide consensus by itself. The readback strengthens the primary basis of the locality audit while leaving those larger issues open.
