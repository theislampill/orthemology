# مفتاح دار السعادة: whole received-work study

Dated study-stage record, 7 October 2026. Complete authored analysis is retained. Provisional and historical wording records its original reading stage; later coverage summaries do not erase its disagreements or unresolved questions.

## Result and evidential scope

The complete received authorial body, main electronic 3–1603, was read continuously, including the supplied modern footnotes and the substantial theological, exegetical, educational, literary, legal, natural-historical, medical, astrological and omen material outside the project's immediate metaphysical concerns. This is 1,601 positions and 334,183 whitespace words including apparatus, not a count of authorial words alone. The 103 actual preliminary positions, 15,224 words, were also read in full. The first pass contains 102 body receipt batches and 5 preliminary receipt batches, backed by 109 untruncated indexed display windows. Repaired truncated displays have no independent credit.

The witness is the independently hosted Ibn al-Qayyim institutional Word/PDF edition, edited by Abd al-Rahman ibn Hasan ibn Qa'id, third 1440/2019, Dar Ata'at al-Ilm/Dar Ibn Hazm. Exact source URLs/hashes, imprints and held earlier objects are in [source metadata](source_manifest.json). The electronic labels divide the body 3–537,538–1172, 1173–1603; these are not claims that independently observed bindings occur there. The catalogue says 3 volumes, while the supplied composite scan has continuous main pagination and no inserted title leaves at the inspected seams. Selecting the whole received body makes completeness independent of those uncertain physical boundaries.

The edition's modern preliminary 103 positions omit separate labels 41, 42, 82, 84. Inspection showed 41–42 are continued apparatus already under electronic I40; 82/84 are blank. Electronic captions replace 21 manuscript reproductions. All 21 were separately inspected in the permitted PDF, with partial decipherment explicitly retained. Two reproduced 1323 printed pages were fully legible. The 53-position scan ledger includes these, title/imprint, seams, ending and local textual checks. It is not a complete 1998-page visual reading, whole-manuscript collation or exhaustive apparatus/source authentication. Postbody modern indexes, beginning at PDF 1714, are outside the authorial-body selection.

The author ends the received work at 1603. The programme of a separate conceptual section on will remains incompletely executed, as the editor I23–28 argues and repeated unfulfilled promises confirm. Complete reading of the received body does not fill that historical incompletion. Neither later manuscript divisions nor website sections supply the absent promised treatment.

## Source voices and the book's actual movement

The argument moves from Adam's descent and the garden controversy through guidance and the virtues of knowledge to contemplative natural history, providence and moral psychology, wisdom of law, rational moral value and extensive opponent dossiers, then astrology, omens, sneezing and contagion. These subjects are not searchable fragments attached to a single proof. Their continuity changes the interpretation of the key passages.

The author sometimes declines a verdict, notably Adam's garden 36/86–87. He sometimes stages opposing schools at length before selecting his answer, notably knowledge/guidance 243–281, moral value 919–1012 and omens/contagion 1490–1511 and 1576–1593. Quotations from Ibn Taymiyya, Ghazali, Razi, Shahrastani, Isa ibn Ali, Abu al-Barakat and the Muqabasat have distinct boundaries; «قلت» and «قالوا» matter. Isa's supposed criterion of choice is explicitly rejected 1243; quoted philosophical rejection of practical benefit 1336–1339 is not the author's conclusion.

The modern editor supplies variants, grading, historical corrections, conjectures and sometimes substantive disagreement. The Shamela preparer additionally inserts bracketed second-edition addenda absent from the printed third edition. These layers must not be flattened into Ibn al-Qayyim's voice. Introductory evidence for attribution and manuscript relationships is an editorial argument, not our independently established stemma. Dependence on common earlier texts prevents counting every repetition as independent support.

## Principal contributions

### 1. Knowledge and will are distinct, interacting requirements

Knowledge's object, power's range and will's determination differ 224. Explanatory guidance does not by itself produce chosen conformity 231–235. The debate resolves by distinguishing an apt but incomplete cause, absent condition, unsuitable recipient and active impediment 263–265. Acquired habits and rejection can later change cognition 271–278, but later damage cannot retroactively erase initial recognition. Genuine knowledge can therefore fail to issue in action without becoming mere ignorance by definition.

Productive prior knowledge and receptive knowledge are both perfections 241–242; the author's stated mistake is universalizing a partial case. The receptive examples concern human knowledge, so this is not by itself a doctrine of externally acquired divine perfection. At 246–247 a displayed conditional is strengthened through its alleged function as a sign: that pragmatic premise must be supplied rather than smuggled in as a converse theorem. The separate contextual reinspection retains the staged argument, marginal critic and editorial answer.

### 2. Positive perfection can be relational without being repair of a lack

The early account of Adam treats voluntary worship, repentance and gratitude as history-dependent goods 5–24. Some conditions constitute a kind of good rather than supply missing intrinsic capacity 13. Creaturely learning from others, teaching, humble attendance and nonrival sharing can be excellences 153; 363–372; 478–481. These are not proofs of independent original plurality; they are controls against an indiscriminate equation of relation with defect.

Divine delegation 461–462 is explicitly beneficent enabling, distinct from delegation necessitated by absence or need. The mature statement 1024–1026 distinguishes completing the product from acquiring the maker's perfection through producing it. God acts from perfection, with wise loved ends. It then expressly invokes God as source of wisdom, its objects and its causes. Thus positive wisdom and self-sufficiency belong together in the author's model; absence of goals would not be a higher perfection by default.

### 3. Causal plurality is real and structured

Main 90–91 allows different causes for a ruling one in type while tying the particular ruling to a source-indexed ground, such as ownership-by-sale. Extending the latter to a source-neutral token effect requires defending the individuation premise. It is not enough to cite the phrase 'one effect' and remove its qualification.

At 501–502 human acts arise through the agent's own will and power, while downstream effects arise jointly from those acts and other factors. The act is part of a compound cause, with proportionate moral attribution. Nature is governed yet really influential 743–746. The sun's action involves earth, air, relation and recipient 1272/1279–1280. Missing pilot 1301 is absence of a preventer, not the same positive cause as active drowning. Final causality synthesis 1590–1593 opposes both autonomous created efficacy and wholesale causal denial.

These distinctions supply positive content for a common-Creator world with genuine created agency. They do not establish that each causal contributor is a complete independent provider; the text explicitly says otherwise.

### 4. Equal opposing tendencies remain an open scoped issue

Main 896–898 denies real equal opposed benefit/harm or pleasure/pain, arguing that both contrary effects, neither effect, or one without a preference are each impossible. It explicitly hears the objection that each apt cause has an opposing blocker. Its reply claims a cause's productive efficacy is stronger than its ability to inhibit another, so disabling the stronger should disable the weaker, and it distinguishes one-way impediment from mutual opposition.

The substantive burden is this general priority relation and the asserted identity of respects. Being effective in inhibiting another outcome while ineffective in producing one's own is not automatically contradiction; nor is cancellation of net effect automatically destruction of the underlying power. Main 910 explicitly preserves sharpness when stone blocks cutting, and 1171 preserves latent painful causes when pleasure masks felt pain. Those passages do not independently demonstrate perfect equilibrium, but they prevent silently treating all blocked efficacy as nonexistent cause.

A restricted impossibility of fully sufficient independent causes for incompatible effects is intelligible. It does not, without further premises, establish impossibility of equal partial tendencies or every zero resultant. The source's wider normative and metaphysical claim is therefore a live question, not a formally refuted theorem and not an automatically accepted universal.

### 5. Genuine agency is compatible with ordered conditions

The author rejects compulsion and independent creaturely self-origination. Necessity through complete voluntary conditions is not equated with coercion 921–922; the created person has action, acquisition and choice in truth 1512–1515. Being limited to one kind of action does not alone remove choice 1243. Divine creation of the person's act is distinct from that act being the act inhering in God 1138–1141. Creating a property does not make its creator bear the property.

These are substantial distinctions, but they do not automatically solve every modern debate about alternate possibilities or ultimate responsibility. Likewise, the claim that moral testing requires capacity for contrary action 1055–1057 does not by itself justify every actual distribution of power or quantity of suffering. The source's further appeal to wise ends remains a separate premise.

### 6. Ultimate ends are positive and plural in role

Knowledge and action can each be means or ends 511–514. Knowing God and worship are intrinsically wanted without each being independently sufficient for complete felicity. Equal reward for firm intention does not erase the superiority of actual giving 514. Efficient willing of an event differs from final willing of a beloved end 1164–1165. The argument for a permanent ultimate end includes distinct premises concerning termination of means, the need for a lasting end and its identification with God; their promised fuller treatment is not supplied here.

Creation by/for/on truth 1391–1393 includes knowledge, worship, justice, beneficence and return, rather than bare production. The theory therefore cannot fairly be reduced to freedom from defects alone.

## Actual common Creator and universal provision: separate appraisals

The actual common-Creator claim is explicit. Main 587–588 infers knowing, powerful, wise unity from coordinated useful order and compares two equal cosmic governors with two equal souls governing one body. Main 1026 places wisdom's source, objects and causes under one source. Main 1391–1395 joins order, ultimate purposes and the made status of external things, their qualities and mental representations. The strongest positive comparative proposal is an integrated account of ordered effects, diverse real causal contributors and normatively meaningful ends under a common source.

That deserves assessment as a defeasible explanatory hypothesis even if one proposed universal uniqueness argument fails. Its difficulties also deserve equal treatment: the body/governor analogy has unproved premises and transfer conditions; actual usefulness is uneven and some examples are scientifically false; sufficient reasons for each token suffering are not demonstrated; and merely positing one source does not automatically outperform every coordinated-source alternative. The author defers detailed proof of the two cited unity arguments 588 rather than establishing all the requisite bridges locally.

The stronger universal proposition that every qualified original must provide every requisite for every effect is a different claim. It cannot be inferred simply from this author's assertion that the actual God is the universal source, or from every created contributor's dependence, or from the label 'perfection'. One must establish which capacity belongs intrinsically to a candidate, which conditions are external need or constitutive relations, which effect is intended, and how causal identity is individuated. This study neither attributes the unrestricted proposition to the whole book nor declares the actual common-source claim defeated because that proposition remains unproved.

## Counterpressure and historical limits

The book's natural history supplies many vivid cases but mixes observation with inherited anatomy, humoral medicine, cosmology, animal generation and hearsay. Editor corrections to bee generation 709 and locusts 717 are examples. The theological distinctions can be studied without validating those mechanisms. The book also criticizes allied overstatements 658–660 and selective memory for successful predictions 1565–1566; the latter test should apply to its favored anecdotes too.

Historical errors matter. Main 257's chronology, 1200's geography, 1236's sequence, 1340's caliphal attitude and 1447's correction of the anti-astrological isnad objection prevent treating confident narration as fresh historical proof. The last is particularly valuable: the editor corrects the author's objection because a grandson's report was through his father, not an impossible direct meeting. Its correction neither proves astrology nor licenses hiding contrary evidence.

Ethnic, sectarian, disability, gender, slavery, punishment and bodily-physiognomy generalizations remain historical claims, sometimes severe, not our adopted norms. The tension between physiognomic judgments 1450–1451 and condemnation of punishing a one-eyed person for an omen 1474 is retained. Neither medical advice nor contemporary legal guidance is being supplied. Prior scientific-source studies and historical appraisals elsewhere remain unchanged.
