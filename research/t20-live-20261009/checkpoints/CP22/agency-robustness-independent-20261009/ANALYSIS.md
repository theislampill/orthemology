# Independent agency-robustness investigation

## Result and scope

The 2025 manuscript gives genuine defeasible support for a protective, mutually concerned conception of finite ideal agency. Its direct capacity benchmark also repairs an important weakness in the presentation of the 2024 argument. But the full manuscript does not establish the claimed necessity of the specific intrinsic desires, or their universally other-regarding content, merely by explaining how those desires could secure robustness.

This investigation establishes neither a necessity proof nor a fully interpretation-preserving countermodel. The justified result is narrower: the text leaves a substantive mechanism-exclusion comparison unresolved. Several tempting counterexamples fail the controls stated in the assignment and are rejected below. The absence of a numerical metric, exhaustive catalogue of worlds, or formal theorem is not itself an objection. A sufficiently discriminating qualitative argument could supply the missing warrant.

The primary source is Michael Smith, *The Ethics of Agency: An Alternative Approach to Normative Ethics*, the complete 20-page author manuscript labelled as published in *The Future of Normativity* (2025), pp. 341–366. Page references below are manuscript pages, not an invented mapping to the published pagination. The adjacent 2024 *Rethinking the Moral Problem* was also read in full; its references below use printed pages 7–32. No other worker's report or verdict was used. The separately supplied transport question is addressed only in section 8.

## 1. What the strongest argument earns

The charitable rationale is more substantial than an inference from ordinary successful action to benevolence.

- Agency persists through time. A present exercise can impair the resources, knowledge, and practical capacities on which a later exercise depends.
- Agents are often vulnerable to other agents' interference and dependent on their assistance. A conveniently harmonious current set of projects can cease to be harmonious when circumstances or motivations change.
- Protective motives directed at agency itself are comparatively insensitive to which particular optional projects people happen to have. Unlike a temporary bargain, they can continue to recommend assistance when the current bargain no longer pays.
- Self-control adds a recovery mechanism. The desired robustness need not disappear whenever a protective motivation weakens; agents can restore it and act on it.
- Other people with those same dispositions supply reciprocal protection and support. This has an intelligible cross-context advantage over unprotected coincidence of interests, domination that lasts only while one is stronger, or isolation that lasts only while contact is absent.

That is a serious explanation of comparative robustness under familiar finite vulnerabilities. It supplies a reason to consider the proposed psychology rather than treating it as an arbitrary moral stipulation. It can have philosophical warrant without a cardinal score. The narrower question is whether every maximally robust implementation must have this psychology, including its intrinsic rather than instrumental character and its concern for every agent.

## 2. The actual inferential transition

The important sequence is unusually explicit:

1. At p. 10 the capacities are knowledge-acquisition and intrinsic-desire realization. Maximality is described in terms of variation in the world, desires, and abilities, with qualifications for what is knowable and realizable.
2. At p. 11 robustness concerns high capacity performance across a range of progressively nonideal nearby worlds.
3. The protective resources may be internal to the agent **or supplied by the world** (p. 11; the disjunction is repeated for interpersonal vulnerability at p. 12).
4. A pair of intrinsic desires is introduced as the "most straightforward feature" to explain intrapersonal protection (p. 11). Self-control is then added as a way to acquire and realize those desires despite apathy or weakness of will.
5. The text then says that without those desires and self-control the capacities would not be maximal and robust (pp. 11–12).
6. The interpersonal extension says that every ideal agent must live among similarly ideal agents and that all must intrinsically desire agency for every agent, together with their own noninterference (p. 12).

Step 5 is the decisive extra commitment. Explaining why the proposed package protects agency does not by itself exclude every other package admitted at step 3. The word "straightforward" need not mean "merely arbitrary": a simple and general mechanism may be the best explanation. But the inference still needs the stronger comparative judgment that alternative protective resources cannot equally satisfy the relevant standard without instantiating the same intrinsic motivational role.

This is not resolved by labelling the desires constitutive. That is the conclusion whose substantive support is at issue. Nor does it follow from the uncontroversial claim that protection must have some explanation rather than being a fragile coincidence.

## 3. Exactly which comparison remains unsettled

Three controls matter because changing them can reverse the relevant judgment, not because philosophy requires a fully numerical model.

### 3.1 A matched vulnerability comparison

The preferred package can suffer loss or weakening of its first-order protective desires and recover through self-control. A rival must be allowed comparably specified recovery resources. Conversely, a rival cannot simply stipulate that its infrastructure or reflexes never fail while the preferred package is subjected to arbitrary failures.

The unresolved qualitative question is whether, after the same kinds and severities of motivational, epistemic, bodily, and social disturbance, every admissible non-intrinsic or externally supported architecture must perform worse. The manuscript supplies examples of the fragility of coincidentally harmonious desires. It does not show that all alternative architectures have only coincidental harmony, or that repair mechanisms other than the proposed motivation-restoring control must be less general.

This does not demand an exhaustive list of worlds. A principled account of which variations are relevant, and why one architecture necessarily handles them better, would suffice. What would not suffice is to count disruption of a rival's support as nearby while assuming the corresponding support of the preferred package stays intact without a reason for the asymmetry.

### 3.2 Realizability must not be silently changed

A natural example of external protection is a physical barrier that prevents an agent from damaging another's cognitive or practical capacities. But using it as a counterexample is hazardous: it can remove actions and outcomes from the feasible set. Declaring every frustrated destructive desire "unrealizable" after installing the barrier would make the performance comparison artificially easy.

Smith's own account is subtler than a command to satisfy every desire simultaneously. It explicitly balances conflicting reasons and permits defensive interference. Nevertheless, the capacity benchmark at p. 10 is goal-general, whereas preservation of agency across persons and times can conflict with realizing a particular feasible desire. A fair comparison therefore needs to distinguish competence in pursuing a feasible goal, actual pursuit of a weighted set of goals, and preservation of other present or future exercises of agency.

No contradiction is inferred here. The unresolved judgment is what counts as equal or superior *agential performance* when an architecture protects future agency by frustrating an otherwise feasible current project. The later pro tanto balancing explains how the proposed ethical view handles such conflicts; it does not independently show that the desired moral weights uniquely maximize the antecedent capacity standard.

### 3.3 Universal content rather than adequate protection

From the dependence of someone's capacities on assistance it follows that the relevant assistance needs reliable support. It does not immediately follow that each possible helper must intrinsically desire the capacities of every possible beneficiary. Robust division of labour, overlapping support, instrumental cooperation, and nonagential infrastructure are different ways that a network can provide protection.

These are not established countermodels. Extensive counterfactual changes in roles, partners, or projects could defeat them and favour universal intrinsic concern. That is precisely a potentially powerful Smithian reply. The source does not establish that every relevant beneficiary and role substitution enters the required robustness range, or why reliable network-level coverage must reduce to universal intrinsic concern in each individual.

The related move to mandatory companions also outstrips the explicitly conditional developmental premise: p. 12 discusses an agent *if* its capacities are products of others' efforts. A case where that condition holds supports reliable assistance. Excluding every solitary but otherwise suitably equipped agent requires additional support; the conditional premise alone does not do it.

## 4. Rival attempts and why none is certified

### A. A hardwired command to help everyone

Rejected as an independent rival. On a functional account of desire, a world-directed disposition integrated with perceptual representations, means-end assessment, and action selection may just be the relevant desire. Calling it an algorithm or rule does not establish a difference in psychological kind.

### B. A physically protected world that makes harmful goals impossible

Rejected as a full counterexample. Environmental protection is expressly permitted by the source, so this is not intrinsically an illegitimate thought experiment. But success purchased by deleting the difficult feasible-goal cases does not preserve the original comparison. An agent with a trivially narrow range of achievable goals has not thereby been shown maximally capable in Smith's sense.

### C. Instrumental protection for a genuine non-agential end

Consider an agent with a genuine intrinsic project whose success depends on preserving its own and others' capacities. Its aid and restraint can be fully intentional, informed, and instrumentally rational without agency itself being its intrinsic end. This respects Smith's intrinsic/extrinsic distinction at p. 2: remove the relevant means-end connection and the instrumental concern disappears.

This is a genuine psychological alternative, not a renamed intrinsic concern. It can match protective behaviour over a stable range of conditions. But it does not yet preserve Smith's whole robustness test. Perturbations that sever the project's connection to others' agency, replace the project, or make interference more productive can defeat it. Without an independently warranted reason that such perturbations are outside the pertinent range, it is not a decisive countermodel. Conversely, matching only selected convenient worlds cannot establish maximality.

### D. Nonmotivational repair supporting genuine agency

Automatic error correction in memory or perception, restorative physiology, and nonagential maintenance resources can support agents whose actual actions still arise from genuine desires and means-end beliefs. Such resources need not themselves represent agency as an end, deliberate about means, or motivate a helping action. They are therefore not automatically redescribed as intrinsic desires merely because they protect a capacity.

There is authorial support for keeping this distinction open. In the 2024 article, p. 22, Smith explicitly contrasts an action of self-control caused by an intrinsic desire plus means-end belief with a non-action reflex triggered by a belief. Both can qualify as self-control. Thus his own presentation does not identify every control mechanism with an intrinsic desire.

This observation identifies a legitimate alternative mechanism class; it does not prove that the class supplies maximal robustness. The 2024 example helps restore instrumental rationality and does not itself solve the entire diachronic and interpersonal problem. Any proposed broader system must still preserve genuine ordinary goal pursuit, handle deliberate interference as well as mere errors or apathy, and meet the same finite resource and realizability constraints. None has been demonstrated here to pass that complete test.

**Outcome:** A and B are inadequate purported refutations. C and D are substantive, intelligible alternatives at the mechanism level, but no completed member has been shown to match the full intended standard. Their relevance is to the uncompleted exclusion argument, not a claim that Smith's conclusion is false.

## 5. The 2024 comparison: real improvement, limited carry-over

The 2024 article, printed p. 20, initially ranks ideal agents by robust capacity to do what they have normative reason to do. Printed pp. 21–27 compare Humean and anti-Humean conceptions, and p. 27 explicitly withholds the further argument that the anti-Humean conception is best before proceeding on that assumption.

The 2025 manuscript does something materially better: p. 10 identifies the two agential capacities directly and p. 11 describes robustness in terms of those capacities. A criticism that simply repeats "the score is already moral compliance" therefore fails to address the strongest current text. The direct standard gives the positive common-vulnerability argument real independent traction.

Two issues survive in more specific form:

- Moving from better protection than a selected fragile Humean configuration to the necessity of one intrinsically motivated, universally concerned configuration still requires the mechanism comparison in sections 2–4.
- Identifying the ideal *adviser* relevant to immunity from reasoned criticism with the maximally robust *agent* remains a substantive bridge. The 2025 text states and motivates that bridge through standards internal to agency (p. 10); it does not independently settle whether a Humean's fully informed and instrumentally coherent adviser must also aim at maximizing temporally extended and socially supported agency in the proposed way.

The second point is not a demand that every philosophical premise be deduced from weaker neutral premises, and it is not an independent refutation. A reader may find the bridge credible. Granting it does not remove the focused mechanism-exclusion issue. Also, the source is not confusing advice with literal imitation: 2025 p. 10 explicitly includes plans for nonideal circumstances, and 2024 p. 23, note 11, explains why the costs of the nonideal person's self-control matter to the advice.

## 6. Pro tanto and sanction controls

The target conclusion is a pair of intrinsic desires grounding unconditional **pro tanto** moral reasons. It is not a universal requirement that every agent always help or never interfere.

- The noninterference motivation excludes protection of an exercise that itself interferes (p. 11).
- The moral reasons can conflict with each other, and nonmoral reasons can outweigh moral reasons (pp. 13–14).
- The relative weights and even boundaries of interference can be vague. Reflection and practical coordination may help determine them (pp. 13–16).
- Moral wrongness does not itself entail permission to sanction. Juridical wrongness additionally requires permissible sanctioning; authority, permissible sanctions, and obligatory sanctions raise further questions (pp. 16–17).
- The brutal-apocalypse discussion is expressly qualified and preserves some obligations rather than denying them wholesale (pp. 18–19).

Consequently, a selfish permissible choice, justified defensive interference, lack of sanctions, or failure to exhibit unconditional outward compliance does not refute this ethic. Equally, the later distinctions do not supply the missing uniqueness of the motivational architecture; they concern the content, weight, application, and enforcement of reasons once those reasons have been derived.

## 7. A useful discriminating test, without a toy theorem

A serious continuation should compare the preferred architecture with one specific candidate using four common controls: (i) genuine desires and their causal roles; (ii) the feasible goal and action domain; (iii) matched perturbations; and (iv) the same judgment of present, future, and interpersonal capacity performance.

The decisive question is: after removing intrinsic agency-concern while permitting the other protective resources admitted by the theory, must there be a relevant perturbation in which performance falls short of what the preferred architecture achieves? A qualitative explanation that the candidate always fails under a particular unavoidable kind of perturbation could answer yes. A candidate that genuinely survives the same tests without the intrinsic motivational role could answer no. Neither answer is supplied merely by the existence of the proposed protective desires or by a stipulation that a rival is equally robust.

No executable theorem is offered, because assigning an arbitrary score or declaring a finite table to be the entire relevant modal neighbourhood would settle the philosophical question by construction. The useful advance is an explicit comparison obligation and rejection of false-positive countermodels, not a definitionally guaranteed formal result.

## 8. Finite-agency transport boundary

The conclusion at p. 12 is expressly framed as invulnerability attainable by a finite being, after an argument from temporally extended vulnerability, interference, and sometimes received development or maintenance of capacities.

A transfer to "unreceived complete original agency" cannot retain those vulnerabilities or dependence on helpers without an argument that they apply. In particular, a premise about needing others to develop or preserve one's powers cannot silently establish mandatory peers for an agent stipulated not to receive those powers. A separate account would have to identify an applicable vulnerability and show why its remedy requires the same other-regarding motives.

This is a domain restriction on this derivation. It does not refute the finite-agent ethic, establish an amoral original agent, or challenge an independently warranted affirmative pure-perfection appraisal. No verdict on those broader claims follows from this investigation.

## Bottom line

Retain the positive robustness rationale and credit the 2025 direct-capacity repair. Do not promote the manuscript's favoured mechanism into an established necessity without the remaining comparison. Do not promote a shield, renamed desire, or conveniently restricted instrumental agent into a counterexample either. The independently supported status is **genuine comparative warrant; categorical mechanism necessity not established by this source; no fully controlled rival established here**.
