# Reconstruction: T20 hybrid continuation requires jump timing, not only jump destinations

**Status:** New reconstruction from the surviving conversation-level research pointer, **not** recovered bytes from the lost 80-minute session. This note is a mathematical correction of Proposition D6 in the preserved provisional synthesis. It has not received an independent reviewer or Lean proof.

## Problem

The old Proposition D6 states that exact preservation of smooth projected flows, event/permission guards, post-jump outcome/next-state kernels, costs, terminal labels and retained-history policies suffices to preserve *event times*. Those conditions suffice for purely deterministic guard-triggered jumps **only when the triggering rules, priority/tie-breaking and any resets are entirely determined by retained trajectories**. They are insufficient for general hybrid systems with spontaneous jumps, random holding times, or unrecorded clocks.

### Exact counterexample

Let two fine starting states `a` and `b` share one reduced state `z=0`. All continuous vector fields are zero, so fine and reduced flows project identically. Both states have the same permitted action, no event guards, no running cost, and the same deterministic mark and post-jump destination `done`. State `a` jumps after exactly one unit of time; state `b` after exactly two units. Every *post-jump mark/destination* kernel agrees on the coarse fibre. Yet the event-time distributions differ, so no reduced process starting only from `z=0` reproduces both. Existing D6's conclusion therefore needs an explicit waiting-time clause or a narrower guard-only scope.

## Corrected conditional proposition (proposed D6')

Consider a finite-mode hybrid process with unique, measurable deterministic flows between jumps, a measurable reduction `r_m` on each fine mode, and no explosion up to a declared finite horizon. Assume:

1. **Flow intertwining.** Until a jump, `r_m(phi_m^t(x)) = bar_phi_m^t(r_m(x))`, with existence and uniqueness on the relevant interval.
2. **Timed-jump sufficiency.** For each retained-history-admissible action and each fine state compatible with that history, the *conditional joint law* of `(waiting time to next jump, observed mark, next reduced mode, next reduced state)` factors through the current reduced state and retained history. This includes deterministic hitting-time rules, priorities/tie breaks, exogenous clocks, hazards and competing risks as applicable; no event clock may depend on an erased variable.
3. **Decision and reward compatibility.** Enabled actions, observation/permission constraints, running and jump costs, and required terminal labels factor through the retained state/history. The policy cannot consult information erased by `r`.
4. **Sound execution.** The disintegrated jump law and flow define a valid process and the process is nonexplosive to the stated horizon; conditional laws are measurable and concatenation/restart is well defined.

Then, starting from fine states with the same reduced initial state and retained history, the reduced **marked jump process** has the same finite-horizon law, including all jump times, marks, reduced post-jump states, accumulated costs and required terminal labels.

### Proof sketch

Couple the two processes using their common reduced conditional law of the first *joint waiting-time/mark/next-reduced-state* tuple. By flow intertwining, each pre-jump retained segment coincides. By the joint law, the first jump time and reduced successor agree in distribution. Retained-history admissibility gives the same next policy; iterate through successive jumps. Nonexplosion lets the finite-jump argument exhaust every jump before the finite horizon. Factorisation handles accumulated costs and terminal labels. This is a standard conditional-kernel/coupling argument, not a claim of a newly discovered probability theorem.

### Scope

For a *strict guard-only* deterministic hybrid model, condition 2 may be replaced by: `all jumps are generated by declared, retention-measurable guard-hit times, including edge priorities, ties, resets and any scheduling clocks`. If random jump times are permitted, preserving only outcome and successor distributions is not enough. For non-deterministic in-mode dynamics, flow intertwining must be replaced by the appropriate transition-semigroup/characteristic-law correspondence.
