# Independent governance–authority bridge review

3 October 2026 UTC. Review of the stable `research-v1` candidate, not canonical adoption, publication, an actual-source attribution, or a metaphysical realization certificate.

## Disposition

**Credit the narrow positive result and the narrow two-sided-alternatives limit.** A fairly established protective arrangement can change which action a recipient has reason to take. A lossless, reasonably available alternative on the organiser's side can defeat a purported necessity for control even when the organiser declines to offer it. These conclusions change decisions in the candidate's matched cases without deriving a general power to command.

The argument earns an application-level extension of the accepted organised-reliance responsibility. It does not derive a new general moral theory. The historical responsibility explains why G must arrange care; the fair arrangement and its effects explain R's cooperation reason. The first is neither necessary nor sufficient for every instance of the second. That division of work is intelligible and substantively useful.

Before a final distributable acceptance, make the selection-history protection explicit and reconcile the source accounting and citation range identified below. The selection-history request protects the positive result from an otherwise tempting broader reading. It does not defeat the static controlled case as stated. A new version should preserve v1 and receive a separate exact-version check.

## 1. What the positive case establishes

At the pre-arrangement stage, A and B are each adequate. After fair notice and genuine preparation, unilateral B would destroy part of an independently worthwhile shared observation or impose a relevant avoidable burden. The reason for preferring A therefore changes with an actual circumstance, rather than with a new evaluative label. The comparison is not a trick in which A was secretly best throughout. Its abstract technical equivalence with B survives, but its relational role changes.

This is sufficient for a new pro-tanto reason. Under the additional stipulated balance, it is sufficient for the stated practical verdict. The argument should not obscure that final balance: the existence of a coordination reason is not already a conclusive obligation, and the fact that R calls its objection a preference does not establish that the objection is slight. Candidate C1–C4 and the serious-interest controls supply the relevant restrictions.

The added N1 premise is defensible at this scope. A claim-holder's real good does not make every inconvenience to its provider or fellow participants irrelevant. Needlessly requiring repetition of a fair adequate performance can be objectionable for those independent reasons. That is not a gratitude debt or a rule that one must repay an unsolicited benefit.

Nevertheless, there are two different questions:

1. Does R have a right to demand B rather than A?
2. Does R have a duty to participate in A?

A negative answer to the first does not entail a positive answer to the second. R might permissibly stop invoking the particular claim, abandon an optional undertaking, or decline care within its legitimate control. G might then have discharged some duty by a fair usable offer. Those consequences do not make accepting A compulsory. The positive cooperation verdict needs its stated relevant frustration or burden, continued practical context, and independently warranted balance.

Accordingly, I credit N1 as a bounded reason against avoidable frustration, not as a freestanding reciprocal duty of obedience. An explicit sentence preserving that difference is warranted. The candidate's own harmless-deviation and informed-refusal cases already commit it to this reading.

## 2. Selection history and manufactured dependence

The current text correctly requires an independently adequate and fair arrangement. It also correctly says that the source's history does not by itself grant jurisdiction. But the most serious adversarial control is temporal.

At t0, G can arrange A or B, and B leaves the contested choice free without relevant loss. G chooses the more restrictive A solely to create leverage, spends resources on A, and recruits innocent participants who reasonably rely on it. At t1, switching to B really would be costly. R can no longer appeal to a presently lossless alternative.

Two judgments must survive simultaneously:

- The innocent participants' present stakes are real. R may have a decisive reason to cooperate temporarily, even if G wrongfully created the predicament.
- Their stakes do not retrospectively make G's earlier choice fair, establish a selection right, or extinguish fitting duties to repair the predicament or reconfigure the arrangement.

A present-feasibility-only test could otherwise reward an organiser for first destroying its own less intrusive alternatives. The candidate's static N2/L1 case does not contain that sequence and is not refuted by it. But the general description of how to prevent coercive self-authorisation should expressly include the earlier selection point. Independently fair formation cannot be established simply by pointing to later sunk costs.

This correction must not create an impossible restoration obligation. Once the facts have changed, remedy and transition remain subject to actual feasibility, affected parties and overriding reasons. Nor must it turn every investment in a common plan into manipulation. Ordinary coordination necessarily settles some options and creates reliance; the objection concerns an independently unjustified restriction or foreclosing choice, not dependence as such.

## 3. The no-leverage result

The static controlled case is persuasive. G already owes fitting care. A less intrusive performance would meet the same relevant interests and legitimate claims, and is reasonably available without an offsetting loss. G withholds it solely to secure an otherwise unwarranted control condition. That withholding does not provide the missing justification for the condition.

This judgment does not rest on a universal autonomy axiom. It depends on the already established responsibility, the genuinely adequate alternative, and the absence of an independently justified reason for the added control. Nothing in these facts explains why a claim to protection should expose its holder to gratuitously enlarged jurisdiction. The proposed reversal would let a duty-bearer increase its entitlement by declining the very discharge it owes.

The words `unjustified` and `unwarranted` in N2 are not an independent proof. The actual application therefore needs the comparator supplied by L1. If a proposed B loses a legitimate good, shifts risks to others, requires unavailable permissions, or conflicts with a stronger obligation, this decisive comparison has not been made. Calling B less intrusive does not fill those gaps.

The result also does not make justified resistance costless or invariably correct. G can act wrongly while R should comply to prevent a serious independent harm. The candidate expressly preserves this separation, and its present-action/entitlement distinction is one of the strongest parts of the paper. Refusal of an unnecessary condition is not, merely as such, waiver of the underlying care claim. It is not a guarantee that R may safely continue the unsupported activity either.

## 4. Is the harmless-deviation test fair to genuine authority?

Yes, when used to delimit this argument rather than define all authority out of existence. The candidate uses an independently verified translator, preserves the relevant record and burdens, and excludes an independently allocated decision right. It then concludes that the care-coordination argument supplies no remaining demand for A. That is the appropriate conclusion.

Three qualifications are essential:

- Actual harmlessness by luck is different from a warranted permission to depart. An untested personal belief that B is harmless does not meet the case.
- Rule-level effects can be real. Predictability, fair distribution, manageable error, investigation costs and effects of normalising individual exemptions must not be omitted from `relevant burden` merely because one isolated outcome is unchanged.
- A independently justified decision-making allocation can bind through some ordinarily mistaken or individually unnecessary instructions. The candidate expressly allows such an allocation, its scope and its error rules. The diagnostic cannot invalidate it merely by finding an authority-independent reason to prefer B.

The paper does not have to show that every authority sometimes prohibits a harmless deviation. It needs only to ask what the present derivation contributes beyond the arrangement's effects. A legitimate coordinating office may itself be justified by those effects at a broader practice level. In that case the missing work is a defense of that office and its settling force, not necessarily an extra appointment, human contract, or unexplained metaphysical property. Section 7 is appropriately open on this point.

## 5. Consent, personal choice and ordinary error

The refusal cases are bounded by competence, informed access, a permissible optional project, actual control of the risk and surviving obligations to others. They do not state that consent is a prerequisite for every obligation or every legitimate authority. The paper explicitly leaves noncontractual authority open and recognizes justified intervention where present consent is unavailable.

Nor does technical superiority alone prove incapacity. A recipient can have reason to defer about calibration while retaining a permissible choice of ends. Conversely, recipient agency is not a veto over other people's legitimate interests. These differences should remain in the final report; a slogan about either absolute autonomy or absolute source ownership would erase the actual argument.

Ordinary fallibility is sufficient for the bounded application. Neither R nor the reviewer must certify that no conceivable defeater exists. They need warranted access to the relevant plan and a responsible treatment of serious available defeaters. A concrete version mismatch can warrant pausing an unauthorised procedure; a bare imaginable error does not nullify a well-supported plan. An independently valid authority's error-tolerance rules require separate assessment rather than automatic cancellation whenever an instruction is suboptimal.

## 6. Original-source test without a human contract

Grant one intelligent original G as the whole productive source of the actual field, including genuine R and other derivative subjects. Grant the active organised-reliance history and a determinate shared epistemic task. Neither a payment nor an antecedent promise of obedience is needed. R can have real choices and reasons even though the powers by which R acts are received. This is the same conditional productive scope used by the inherited comparison; it adds no second absolute supplier.

If the contemplated variation of R's act would actually defeat other subjects' independently permissible activity and no fitting accommodation is available, the positive cooperation reason can apply. Original productive status neither erases those interests nor independently proves that restriction necessary. If G can instead supply a lossless translator or an equally adequate nonrestrictive implementation, the reason for the particular restriction can disappear. Increasing productive ability therefore need not increase the justified scope of control.

A stronger source must not be defeated by silently importing a limited maintainer's contingent resource bottleneck. Equally, it must not be credited with an alternative that only counterfeits the fixed target. If the protected good includes other subjects' genuine joint participation in a particular occurrence, reproducing an indistinguishable display of that participation is not automatically preserving it. The source-side alternative must preserve the actual target, agency, epistemic standing, permissions and other legitimate interests. No conclusion about omnipotence follows from an unexamined implementation story.

An independent source-nature or role argument might establish a stronger decision-making right. This review neither presupposes nor refutes it. It credits the present bounded judgment without first requiring unity, necessary existence, essential integrity or actual divine disclosure. Those stronger propositions are not conclusions of the care relation.

## 7. Audit of the fifteen author cases

1. **Before/after arrangement:** the action change is warranted under the independent adequacy, real disruption and stipulated balance. Selection history needs the precision above.
2. **Harmless translator:** properly removes the demonstrated cooperation ground; it is permission relative to the stated argument and retained conditions.
3. **Expertise and project choice:** the epistemic reason survives without assigning G the recipient's ends.
4. **Fallible maintainer:** a concrete mismatch is a proper defeater in this nonauthority case; the authority variant appropriately preserves separate validity rules.
5. **Forced dependence/pledge:** rejects entitlement while preserving possible urgent reasons to comply. The staged-alternative control extends the question beyond its presently lossless rescue.
6. **Legitimate origin/new condition:** prior legitimacy does not validate a new restriction. The possible justified replacement of an obsolete check prevents absolutising the current procedure.
7. **Serious personal objection:** actual privacy or other protected interests cannot be dismissed by calling them preferences.
8. **Own-risk refusal:** correct only at its explicit competence, control and absence-of-other-duty scope; no universal waiver power follows.
9. **Withdrawal harming others:** real third-party stakes limit how R withdraws without giving G ownership of R's purposes.
10. **Accurate warning from a wrongdoer:** correct practical separation of safety, source breach and entitlement. It is an inherited epistemic discipline, not new authentication.
11. **Changed criteria:** the fixed-attribution control preserves earlier ownership and does not make all inquiry compulsory.
12. **No human contract:** the relational reasons can apply within the conditional original-source field. Creation by itself supplies neither coordination necessity nor independent command power.
13. **More capable source:** correct comparative application when the added capability actually preserves all stipulated relevant interests.
14. **Two organisers:** responsibility does not choose a hierarchy. Actual fair coordination can become action-relevant without creating permanent priority.
15. **Independent allocation:** a live conditional route, not a new demonstration that an actual original source holds such a right.

## 9. Scope of acceptance

The application is worth crediting because it identifies an enacted arrangement's practical consequence, makes source-side alternatives relevant to recipient refusal, and preserves the continued duty and safety response when a coercive condition is rejected. Calling these ordinary reasons does not nullify their source-governance application. Conversely, discovering this application is not establishing general moral authority from creation.

No claims are accepted here about actual original-source identification, universal normative grounding, inevitable source compliance, complete-profile nondomination, essential nonlying, authenticated divine address, permission to access an operational system, or canonical programme closure. These are scope boundaries, not prerequisites retrospectively imposed on the bounded result.
