# The strongest remaining philosophical dispute

## The affirmative argument

The source treats efficacy actually exercised as a possible perfection of the bearer. Minhaj 2:180 argues from conditioned efficacy to dependence of its subject; Majmu 20:174–184 treats harmonious cooperation, unchanged contributors and inseparable mixed effects. Dependence need not take the form of a new intrinsic entity donated by a peer. [S4]

The strongest affirmative claim is conditional: a completed original undertaking obtains its appropriate actual productive adequacy wholly through that bearer. If an independently original peer partly constitutes that adequacy, willing the partnership does not remove the need for foreign completion. Particular voluntary acts must still be distinguished from the necessary bearer and the essential genus of activity; no inference makes every particular act eternally necessary.

## The concentration argument

Majmu 20:178–181 argues that compatible productive powers concentrated in one subject need not lose source-neutral work-capacity. It then infers that each existing contributor is eligible for a more independent condition. The move from a possible consolidated power-profile to each particular bearer’s eligibility is substantive. A two-participant end can be lost while the external work remains producible by one. That loss does not refute the work-capacity claim. [S4]

The concentration passage also imports the earlier transition from genuine cooperation to inability to produce alone. A rival already supplied with solo constructors does not acquire or lose them through this argument. Its live issue remains the adequacy of the actual joint exercise, rather than possession of an alternative solo repertoire.

## The surviving reduced rival

The current candidate grants genuine joint original production and does not claim that each participant is the sole exhaustive source. It retains structurally unreceived powers, cognition, willing and intrinsic activity, with a shared manifestation. Its represented ends and forecasts succeed in the independently policy-defined ordinary domain. This is a conditional model result; it does not certify perfected originals or source-level complete particular willing. [S2, S5, S7]

The richer end makes an evaluative difference under suppression. It does not select the target or the inherited means policy, justify that policy’s wisdom, or explain why a real peer contribution leaves each agent’s efficacy uncompleted. In the pre-means joint domain, nine profiles remain, of which six fail the product and full end. The richer end therefore does not hide success filtering, but also does not supply a sufficient reason-selection theory.
