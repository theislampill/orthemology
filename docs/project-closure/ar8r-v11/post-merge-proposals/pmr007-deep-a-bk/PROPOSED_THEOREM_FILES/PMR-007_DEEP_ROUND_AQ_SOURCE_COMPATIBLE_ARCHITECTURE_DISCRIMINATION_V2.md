## 1. Central question

Can the strongest source-compatible unified-personal architecture be distinguished from the strongest source-compatible impersonal or powers rival **without** importing personal vocabulary, a preferred world, a target label, a candidate-dependent representation, or a source-conditioned conclusion as neutral evidence?

The result is a disciplined frontier characterization. It does not prove either architecture true.

## 2. Typed comparison surface

Let:

- `A` be the unified-personal architecture;
- `R` be the strongest source-compatible impersonal or powers rival;
- `E` be a finite family of candidate-independent experiments;
- `P_A` and `P_R` be the induced raw evidence distributions;
- `K` be a common finite Markov channel selected independently of which candidate is preferred;
- `Q_A=K_*P_A` and `Q_R=K_*P_R` be represented evidence distributions;
- `S_v(h)` be the version- and scope-indexed source/world admissibility verdict for architecture `h`;
- `π(h)>0` be a separately declared prior or coding policy.

Source compatibility is a conjunction of distinct coordinates:

```text
source bytes/authentication;

world-link admissibility.
```

No coordinate inherits another coordinate's status.

## 3. SCAP-1A — equal represented likelihood gives no evidential update

For any represented event `D` with `Q_A(D),Q_R(D)>0`,

\[
\frac{\Pr(A\mid D)}{\Pr(R\mid D)}
=
\frac{Q_A(D)}{Q_R(D)}\frac{\pi(A)}{\pi(R)}.
\]

Hence if `Q_A=Q_R`, the likelihood ratio is one on every positive-probability event and posterior odds equal prior odds. Evidence supplies no update between `A` and `R`.

Zero-probability events are excluded from conditional odds rather than assigned an arbitrary ratio.

## 4. SCAP-1B — common representations cannot manufacture discrimination

For a common candidate-independent Markov channel `K`,

\[
TV(K_*P_A,K_*P_R)\le TV(P_A,P_R).
\]

Therefore a common representation cannot create predictive or interventional discrimination absent from the frozen raw experiment. A candidate-dependent or post-outcome channel defines a different experiment and cannot be credited as neutral compression of the original one.

## 5. SCAP-1C — source compatibility does not choose the actual referent

Let `g` be the unique source-role bearer in a source structure, `H_v` the admissible version-indexed family of source-to-world maps, and

\[
F_{H_v}(g)=\{h(g):h\in H_v\}.
\]

A source role determines one relative candidate-world referent only when this image is singleton. Restricting `H_v` to force the preferred architecture without an independent source/world bridge is a hidden-anchor move. Even a singleton candidate-world image does not select the actual world without a further world-link premise.

## 6. The three eligible parity-breaking gates

The current personal/impersonal parity can be broken only through at least one independently warranted gate:

### GATE S — source-conditioned exclusion

A source, translation, applicability, and world-link contract excludes `R` while admitting `A`. This is source-conditioned unless the excluding premise is independently defended on the neutral track.

### GATE E — predictive or interventional surplus

The candidates assign different probabilities to a preregistered event or intervention under a common experiment, so `TV(Q_A,Q_R)>0` or an exact likelihood ratio differs from one.

### GATE P — defended prior or coding policy

An independently warranted prior, hypothesis language, or coding policy favors one architecture. A bare primitive count, bearer count, or convenient vocabulary does not satisfy this gate.

These gates are disjunctive routes to discrimination, not claims that any route has already been discharged.

## 7. SCAP-1D — invariant-score parity

Assume:

```text
S_v(A)=S_v(R)=true;
Q_A=Q_R;
π(A)=π(R);
and the scoring rule is invariant under swapping the architecture labels
while preserving the full typed comparison surface.
```

Then the score of `A` equals the score of `R`.

### Proof

The swap is an automorphism of the complete comparison object. An invariant score is constant on its orbit. Since `A` and `R` occupy one orbit under the swap, they tie. Any preference must therefore depend on a noninvariant label, a hidden source/world anchor, a candidate-dependent experiment, or a prior/coding asymmetry.

### AQ-CM-RELABEL

### AQ-CM-HIDDEN-ANCHOR

Restrict the admissible interpretation family to maps landing on the preferred personal referent. The resulting singleton fibre reflects the restriction, not independent referent evidence.

### AQ-CM-PRIOR-REVERSAL

With equal likelihoods, prior odds `9:1` and `1:9` produce reversed posterior preferences. The evidence did not generate either preference.

A representation includes `personal=true` or the desired winner label. Apparent discrimination is target leakage.

### AQ-CM-POST-OUTCOME-CHANNEL

A channel is selected after seeing which candidate the data favor. This is not a common preregistered representation of the original experiment.
