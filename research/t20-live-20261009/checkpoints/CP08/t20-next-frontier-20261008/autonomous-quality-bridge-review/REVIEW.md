## 2. What the autonomy argument positively contributes

The central distinction is not between an explained fact and any convenient stopping point. In §§2–3 Dasgupta distinguishes an appropriately raised grounding question that lacks an answer from a question inappropriate to its purported subject matter. A fact in the latter category is autonomous. The proposal thus differs both from accepting a substantive fact as brute and from declaring it necessary. A necessary fact could still invite an unanswered grounding question.

Two analogies motivate the distinction. Arithmetic is not a causally brute event merely because it has no cause; nominal definitions do not fail as axioms merely because they are not proved from an axiomatic system. Dasgupta proposes that real definitions, understood as core essences, play an analogous role with respect to grounding. This is genuine positive philosophical content. It challenges an inference that every explanatory stopping point must be brute or an individual producer.

The motivation has limits that the addendum mostly preserves:

1. Autonomy is treated as primitive and its intelligibility ultimately assumed for the paper's argument, rather than exhaustively defended.
2. Core essentialist truths being autonomous is a working hypothesis. The reverse claim, that every autonomous fact is essentialist, is a separate working hypothesis, expressly not a settled view. Mediated essences can be grounded and are not automatically autonomous.
3. The analogy concerns explanatory aptitude. Note 18 explicitly warns that arithmetic may explain nothing causally, whereas essentialist facts can ground other truths. The analogy alone is therefore not a transfer theorem establishing existential explanatory power.
4. Objective autonomy is not defined by our comfort, inability to answer, linguistic convention, or willingness to stop asking. The response to a question is offered as evidence of intelligibility, not as what makes a fact autonomous.
5. The DMV and light examples illustrate a proposed explanatory form. They do not identify a complete actual essence grounding every bearer and productive resource.

A further useful control comes from §1: Dasgupta prefers a sentential-operator treatment of ground and uses fact-talk for convenience. His language should not silently be read as committing him to a separate object called an autonomous fact, which can then be assigned an individual bearer merely because it was reified in our description.

## 3. The precise §9 / note 44 objection

Section 9 reaches essential existence only after adding a premise: grounds of a particular's existence must include an existence fact, including an essentialist existence fact of the stipulated kind. The familiar particle/chair story motivates that premise but does not make it part of the meaning of explanation.

Note 44 explicitly sketches the alternative. Facts concerning particulars' existence may be grounded in qualitative facts expressible in predicate-functor form. Essential claims may concern being F, without their content asserting the existence of anything. The note says that this can satisfy the proposed PSR without a thing whose existence is part of, or grounded in, its own essence.

This is more than a reviewer-invented possibility. It is an explicit published objection at an exact inferential point. But it is a sketch, with further developments referred elsewhere. It does not establish the actual qualitative base, eliminate every ontological commitment, or independently prove the metaphysical possibility of the proposed complete productive history. In particular:

- Derivative particulars remain in the explanandum. Their presence is not a concession that the fundamental ground must itself be a particular.
- Qualitative does not mean mentally entertained, merely linguistic, or causally productive.
- Not having an essentially existing particular does not by itself establish that every particular receives its whole existence through productive donation.
- Neither the presence of an ungrounded base nor its autonomy alone establishes `resourceReceived` or its negation. The receipt predicate concerns a different, specifically interpreted relation.

The addendum correctly locates these limits. No source-fidelity correction is needed to its central use of note 44.

## 4. Section 13 and the conditional essence caveat

Section 13 distinguishes a statement of what an item is from conditions determining when something counts as that item. The latter, regimented in Kantian form under free logic, does not automatically yield the item's existence. Dasgupta contests the restriction that all essence statements must take that classificatory form.

His contrasting chair example is conditional: its essence permits existence to follow when the particle-arrangement condition is met. It is not an unconditional ground of its own actuality. Note 55 explicitly limits the example to answering the argument that every essence must be Kantian; it falls short of the essence-grounded existence required for the stronger rationalist claim.

Thus §13 is a defence against one restriction on possible explanatory form. It is not an exhibited actual, autonomous, existence-grounding essence. The addendum's distinction is accurate. Calling every Anselmian essence unconditionally existence-producing would exceed the source; the present addendum does not do that.

## 5. Comparison with the exact inherited interface

The file's opening interpretation restricts resources to concrete intrinsic instantiations in the same existential respect. It expressly disclaims a general fact-to-bearer theorem. Against that interpretation:

**Completion, lines 35–36.** Every received target requires a relevant actual unreceived resource. An autonomous ground is not automatically an instance of this predicate: autonomy concerns grounding aptitude; the resource type concerns concrete productive actuality. Dasgupta's PSR therefore neither supplies Completion nor proves its actual falsity. The all-received synthesis would have to explain why no appropriate original concrete resource exists, rather than infer that absence from the qualitative vocabulary.

**Realisation, lines 38–39.** Every actual resource is intrinsic in an actual bearer. The qualitative proposal can allow derivative bearers and their derivative concrete resources to satisfy this restricted condition. If the fundamental qualitative ground is added to the resource domain, Realisation's application to that new kind of item becomes a substantive additional claim. The theorem cannot license that widening.

**SupportTransport, lines 41–43.** Whole-existence receipt of an actual bearer transfers to a resource intrinsic in it. Neither existential grounding nor predicate-functor representation supplies this receipt relation. Nor does the alternative need to deny the condition when applied to ordinary intrinsic concrete capacities. Identifying every grounding dependence with whole-existence productive receipt would require another argument.

**The inference, lines 76–93.** Once Completion supplies the original resource, Realisation supplies its bearer, and SupportTransport excludes that bearer's whole receipt. The inference is sound at the declared interface; the review finds no newly exposed formal gap. Dasgupta's alternative is not a countermodel satisfying the full premise conjunction while denying its conclusion.

The subsequent receipt representation, contingency-as-need, and constitutive-reception assumptions remain further claims. Neither necessity nor explanatory autonomy replaces them. This review adds no conclusion about one common provider, productive attribution to that provider, its perfections, or uniqueness.

## 6. Infinitism and the proposed synthesis

Sections 2 and 5 expressly reject an endless descending sequence of grounding answers as sufficient for this PSR. The stopping role is occupied by autonomous grounds. It would misattribute the paper to cite it as endorsing an entirely received infinite grounding chain as itself sufficient.

The addendum instead proposes combining an autonomous qualitative ground with a beginningless productive history whose concrete bearers are all received. That separation of grounding and productive donation makes the hypothesis intelligible enough to examine. It does not establish their joint metaphysical possibility, the truth of the laws, their full target coverage, or their autonomy. The proposal remains ours, not a joint doctrine demonstrated by Dasgupta and Billon.

The inherited original-standing-resource control must also remain admissible. Fixing every token of a history is a stronger explanandum than supplying an actual original resource that contributes to it. A qualitative account cannot remove an original provider just by outperforming weak recurrence conditions at history specification.

## 7. Warrant symmetry and the confidence question

Section 16 argues against assigning rationalism a special burden merely because it is a universal grounding claim: its fundamentalist opponents make substantive structural commitments too. Section 17 leaves the truth of the alternatives unsettled. This is not a proof that any two particular explanatory theories have equal evidence. In particular, a bearer-based rationalism and a qualitative rationalism can both lie on the rationalist side of the paper's stated contrast.

The relevant symmetric standard is therefore:

- Neither candidate needs a demonstration from premises neutral between every ontology merely to be taken seriously.
- Both need positive warrant for claims applied to actuality, at the specificity relevant to the conclusion.
- No candidate receives an exemption because it was previously adopted.
- An undercutting objection need not first construct a complete replacement theory; a replacement claim, by contrast, needs positive support of its own.
- Coherence and conceptual motivation do not by themselves establish actual truth or evidential parity.

The addendum generally states these constraints correctly. Its initial categorical no-change sentence required qualification. If the prior appraisal relied on the premise that no adequate explanation could have a qualitative autonomous base, this paper supplies reason to reduce confidence in that premise and potentially in any conclusion materially resting on it. The lack of a fully worked-out rival would not cancel that effect.

The consulted inherited reports already distinguish the accepted whole-field adequacy judgement from local deductions, allow a prospective autonomous law, and identify its bearer/production extension as open. The current warrant-standard review also makes its F1/perfection background material and permits a critic to undercut an inference without constructing a complete cosmology. The new source does not falsify an ontology-neutral entailment claimed there. It supplies stronger positive articulation and source support to an acknowledged alternative.

The revised §5 now responds appropriately:

1. **Conditional theorem:** no reduced confidence in the stated inference. The resource/bearer and receipt assumptions remain explicit.
2. **Unqualified exhaustive application:** a modest directional reduction is justified. The rival now has positive philosophical motivation and an explicit published account of where an individual-existence inference can fail, rather than only an unfilled logical possibility.
3. **Unconditional actual-world assurance:** corresponding downward pressure is warranted where that assurance depends on exhaustive applicability. This is not a separately quantified probability change or a proof that the actual conclusion is false. The provisional judgement within the adopted ontology can survive, while its unrestricted application is held less confidently.
4. **Downstream common-source comparison:** no independent defeat is established among already eligible originals; conditional comparison cannot repair an upstream ontology-to-actuality issue.

I therefore accept the author's differentiated update as a **bounded comparative appraisal**, rather than merely a wording repair or an automatically forced reversal. Its reasons include positive motivation for autonomy and the exact qualitative challenge to the existence-premise. They do not consist solely in formal coherence, publication, or failure to exclude every imaginable rival. The text appropriately preserves those distinctions and assigns no numerical confidence.

The narrow question is whether the relevant extramental qualitative ground must have its reality as an intrinsic feature of determinate bearers, with dependence strong enough to prevent it grounding their whole actuality.
