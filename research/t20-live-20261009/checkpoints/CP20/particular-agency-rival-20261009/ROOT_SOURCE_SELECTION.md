# A developed agent-causal rival: source selection and open test

9 October 2026. Interim research note, not a final appraisal.

## Primary argument selected

O’Connor distinguishes normative from motivating reasons. He treats agent causation as a proposed basic power that settles a decision while reasons influence its likelihood. This supplies more structure than merely labelling an outcome a choice. His discussion acknowledges the objection that making reasons produce the agent’s causing either renders agency redundant or, if reasons do not do so, leaves its exercise unguided. His response assigns reasons a structuring role. The longer defence must be examined before repeating that objection as unanswered. [Reasons and Causes, pp.129–138, especially136–137](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OConnor_Reasons-Causes10.pdf).

## Research question, not conclusion

Does the defended causal relation adequately explain a particular exercise without necessitating antecedents, or does it merely relocate the unexplained specification? This is a challenge to the sufficiency requirement itself. It is not yet a countermodel satisfying that requirement.

The inherited Arabic assessment separately distinguishes intrinsic determination, normative fittingness, original productive adequacy and external compulsion. The comparison must retain those meanings. It must not reify the claim that an agent causes an act into a separately subsisting second act merely to generate a regress. Conversely, declining that reification does not automatically explain why this causal relation obtains.

No human causal capacity is thereby an unreceived original power. No change to the common-source appraisal follows from selecting this rival for examination. No new conditional theorem or metaphysical possibility certificate is asserted.
