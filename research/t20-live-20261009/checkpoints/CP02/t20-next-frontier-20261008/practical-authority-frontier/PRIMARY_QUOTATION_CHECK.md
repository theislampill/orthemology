## Continuous argument and speaker control

The unit at1076 quotes an opponent's premise that God is neither improved by obedience nor damaged by disobedience. Ibn al-Qayyim accepts that premise and rejects its anti-obligation conclusion. He contrasts accounts that reduce command to bare will, wage exchange, or social preparation for philosophy with his own account of deserved worship. At1077–1080 he grounds worship in the source's intrinsic worth, independently of rewards, punishments or prophetic communication. Revelation confirms, details and adds goodness to a response already fitting. The quoted perfection-to-love-to-worship argument follows at1081–1082. The author explicitly includes obedience and calls the response owed, so a merely motivational reading would be too weak.

The following discussion distinguishes love responding to benefaction from love responding to intrinsic excellence, then explains different practical responses to different known attributes. It denies that God's benevolence purchases a compensating benefit for himself.

Quotations within this authorial argument remain separate evidence. The1078 editor identifies the supposed divine saying as Wahb's report from the Psalms. The1082 apparatus questions the attribution and authenticity of the saying about Suhayb. Neither report is treated here as independently authenticated revelation. The rational recognition argument does not depend exclusively on either report.

## Textual controls with inferential consequences

1. At1080 the edited body has **detailing** the fitting response; the apparatus calls the alternative reading **preferring** a corruption. This supports a disclosure/completion account without itself explaining a new jurisdiction.
2. At1082 the edited text contrasts what love draws from the heart with what **fear** draws, whereas some witnesses have **his saying**. The latter must not be used to manufacture a distinct love-versus-command argument. Ameri's selected quotation ends before this saying, so it is not thereby shown to misquote that variant.
3. At1085 the edited text concerns obedience to and worship of someone **not loved**. The unedited electronic parallel instead reads as though it concerned someone whose worship is **not obligatory**. The apparatus rejects that reading. Using the unedited sentence as an independent duty-entitlement theorem would materially overread it.

These are reports of the supplied editor's decisions, not our manuscript collation.

## Relevance to the judgement

This is the source's stronger positive proposal: adequate recognition of unsurpassable perfection fittingly issues in love, worship and obedience, not only gratitude for advantageous services. Its warrant is comparative practical recognition and fitrah, with an explanation of the relationship between character, love and action. That is substantive philosophical warrant at the programme's accepted defeasible standard; lack of a norm-free deduction is not a sufficient objection.

Nevertheless, the authority question must retain its exact shape. The argument supports the general owed-response appraisal and gives an intelligible reason for responding to a wise source's actual direction. It does not distinguish unconditional grants from permissions subject to continuing control over a particular otherwise-open action. Nor does it independently settle a particular utterance's obligatory rather than advisory force. Treating ‘obedience’ as a definition that silently settles all those applications would exceed the bounded result.
