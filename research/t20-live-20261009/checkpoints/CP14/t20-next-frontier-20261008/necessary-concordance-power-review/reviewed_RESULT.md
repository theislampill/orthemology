# Necessary concordance, power, and complete original efficacy

8 October 2026 UTC. Focused continuation; no T20 integration, closure, new action-choice toy, or historical research-duration certification. Earlier files are unchanged.

## Result

**The selected published argument contains a serious independent-efficacy challenge to necessary concordance, but does not establish non-shareability merely from separate powers, truthful self-knowledge, or necessary agreement.** Its strongest application to the retained rival is the demand for individually adequate original production. That application cannot be answered by correcting a modal scope error. Conversely, the productive demand cannot be obtained merely from two wills being necessarily correlated.

There is now a verified direct published criticism, with an important bibliographic correction. Amini's 2009 paper does not cite or discuss Baillie and Hagen. It is a parallel critique of older uniqueness arguments. Gordon's 2024 paper expressly challenges Baillie–Hagen p.28 in note 17, pp.577–578. The direct criticism is narrower than a refutation of every argument in their paper.

The intellectual genealogy is shared: Baillie–Hagen expressly develop Ghazali’s quoted position and discuss Scotus and Ockham; Amini engages related antecedents. These are philosophical analyses, not independent empirical witnesses or automatically independent convergence across traditions. Gordon’s local objection receives its published attribution; the present increment is its faithful transport to the retained productive-versus-modal distinction, not priority for rediscovering it.

The comparative original-perfection case retains the strengthened positive appraisal already earned in `bearer-eligibility/CURRENT_FINDING.md`. This investigation does not downgrade it to a definition. It locates the exact additional norm that the necessarily concordant primitive joint-production rival must answer.

## 1. Primary argument and criticism: bounded source capsules

### Baillie and Hagen

James Baillie and Jason Hagen, *There cannot be two omnipotent beings*, International Journal for Philosophy of Religion 64(1) (2008), 21–33, DOI [10.1007/s11153-007-9152-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11153-007-9152-7).

They deliberately analyse non-normative power, while allowing nature-relative restrictions and using maximality to exclude radically limited agents. Their criterion requires every individually possible achievement to be within power, unsurpassability, and some effective power. Equality is deliberately not excluded by definition. After the conflict argument, necessary harmony faces three objections: another's determination restricts ability; personal distinctness requires possible divergence; and effective willing requires complete productive sufficiency. A further S5 argument uses nested necessity. The final section attacks cancellation through universal frustration. [pp.22–33; strongest concordance passages pp.28–30.]

Publisher metadata and abstract were verified. Full reading used the supplied [published-format mirror](https://submissiontogod.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/there-cannot-be-two-omnipotent-beings-james-baillie-and-jason-hagen-1.pdf). This is one textual witness, not independent corroboration or verified byte identity with the publisher file.

### Gordon: verified direct criticism

Noah Gordon, *God, Über-God, and Unter-God*, Religious Studies 60(4) (2024), 564–579; first online 26 July 2023; DOI [10.1017/S003441252300063X](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/religious-studies/article/god-ubergod-and-untergod/9AD4212CD64B76BDDCCCB5BE9C866D73).

Gordon distinguishes an ability's range, reliability, robustness, and quality. In the direct objection, actual harmonious nonmovement does not establish inability: had the first agent willed movement, the other could have harmoniously done so too. The criticism concerns necessarily efficacious wills, not a completed proof of two omnipotent beings. His larger comparison also leaves the adequate dominance principle unresolved. ## 2. The exact modal discrimination

The following is my inference audit, not an attribution of a new proof to any author. Let F mean A brings about s and B mean B brings about r, with s and r incompatible in the same respect and occasion. Use one metaphysical accessibility relation throughout.

Necessary nonconflict is:

(NC) □¬(F ∧ B), equivalently □(B → ¬F).

Separate attainable exercises are:

(EA) ◇F and ◇B.

There is no contradiction between NC and EA: the alternatives need not have a common completion. This is a logical scope observation, not a new metaphysical world model or an omnipotence certificate.

The published proof's crucial additional premise is:

(STR) □(B → □¬F).

Given STR and ◇B, normal modal reasoning yields ◇□¬F; S5 then yields □¬F, contradicting ◇F. **The displayed deduction is valid conditional on STR in S5. The disputed step is the justification of STR from nonconflict.** The PDF image of p.30 verifies that the inner necessity is printed in footnote10; it is not an extraction artefact.

Four distinctions prevent a misleading verdict:

1. Necessitating the conditional B → ¬F gives NC. It does not insert a necessity operator into that conditional's consequent.
2. From actual B and NC, one obtains actual ¬F. Necessitation cannot be applied to that conclusion while silently retaining the undischarged contingent assumption B.
3. Were B itself necessary, NC would imply □¬F. Necessary existence of its agent, or necessary agreement between the agents, is not necessity of this particular B-determination.
4. A setting-relative reading may instead hold B fixed and say that F is unavailable among B-preserving alternatives. That can be true. But its inner operator is now restricted by B, rather than the original unrestricted metaphysical operator. The ordinary same-operator S5 collapse cannot be imported without an additional transfer principle.

Thus the best defense of STR is substantive, not a different manipulation of S5: **absolute power might require ability robust under independently supplied peer determinations**. If that standard is independently warranted, the harmony reply does not meet it. If the claimed power is antecedent capacity, or a counterfactual ability whose exercise covaries with the peer, the same standard has not been assumed. Neither reading should be silently substituted for the other.

The endpoint must also be kept exact. Excluding one eligible act does not alone establish total impotence. To generalize to absence of any effective power, a proof needs the contrary-target assumptions for every relevant act. Nonoccurrence of an act and an agent's productive achievement of its contrary are not automatically the same target type.

## 3. Separate powers, separate subjects, and admissible exercises

Power to achieve s in some eligible context and power to achieve r in another do not entail the admissibility of their joint exercise. A contradiction route requires both guaranteed success and compossibility of the particular guaranteeing choices. Agent distinctness supplies neither by itself.

This is not a demand that omnipotence be insulated by arbitrary essential limitations. A critic can reasonably ask why allegedly supreme agency should count as supreme while its entire range is fixed by another original's contribution. That is an eligibility and adequacy objection. It is stronger than assuming that every pair of standing capacities forms an admissible Cartesian combination.

Likewise, necessary equivalence of two will-contents does not imply numerical identity of acts or subjects. Subject-indexed ownership, distinct intrinsic exercises, and first-person reference remain intelligible distinctions. A stronger account of personal independence might demand counterfactual divergence, but it needs a rationale tying independence to that particular modal freedom. Unreceived agency and logical freedom to disagree are different properties.

The strongest reply to the distinctness objection is therefore not a pair of interchangeable labels. It is the retained interpretation with two genuinely owned acts and original resource bearers. Its limitation remains that this interpretation has not independently earned the full metaphysics of perfected original subjects. It defeats only the proposed automatic identity inference.

## 4. The stronger adequacy issue cannot be dissolved into modal logic

Here are three distinct relations:

- **Concomitance:** A's relevant willing necessarily accompanies B's.
- **Successful efficacy:** whenever the relevant willing occurs, its intended product is achieved through that agent's intentional activity.
- **Complete original provision:** that agent accounts for the entire actual original productive basis of the undertaking, including indispensable independently supplied contributions.

The first relation does not prove that B efficiently supplies A's willing or its power. The second is stronger than an accidental match between intention and outcome, yet still does not automatically establish the third. Conversely, a demonstration that a contribution belongs indispensably to the actual productive origin is not answered by showing that no new intrinsic item is donated to A.

### Strongest affirmative reconstruction

Let J be A's actual completed undertaking. Suppose:

(P1) Every perfected original's undertaking must be adequately effective through its own original resources, without an independently original supplier of indispensable productive efficacy.

(P2) J's actual productive basis includes B's proper unborrowed exercise, which A does not originally supply.

(P3) B's role is genuinely productive in J's complete origin, not only an object of J's intentional content or a proposition entailed by J's occurrence.

Then A's undertaking fails P1. No contrary willing or disagreement is required. If being a perfected original requires P1 for the relevant actual undertaking, A is disqualified. Applying the same argument to B gives the corresponding result.

This is the strongest application of the p.29 issue to the retained account. **P2–P3 match its declared primitive joint productive support.** It would be inaccurate to replace that peer by a merely known object or to claim that both actually provide the whole independently. The rival has solo constructors in the standing repertoire, but the actual coactive history has both original roots. A different history with the same endpoint does not supply the missing origin of this history.

### Why P1 has independent motivation

The inherited original-source question asks for the complete actuality of effective agency, not merely internal readiness followed by an unexplained externally completed success. A source that must import an independent original contribution has not completed that explanation through itself. The Eleventh article supplies a target-relative reason: if actual reception is through B's exercise, that exercise's efficacy is part of the receiving mode's indispensable productive basis. Omitting it changes the claimed explanatory target.

The saved Akmaliyya account also compares wholly adequate agency with assisted production and argues that essential nonreceptivity to a pure perfection is a limitation rather than an exemption. The relevant primary spans were reread here: canonical L216–240 and L249–274. The argument therefore does not require a numerical merger of A and B or a possible identity-preserving upgrade of A. Nor is it objectionable simply because its premises imply uniqueness.

### Strongest remaining reply

P1 concerns **what counts as this agent's complete appropriate actual efficacy**. A primitive co-manifestation account can agree that the product has two original roots while denying that either participant's own appropriate action is perfected by receipt from the other. Its claim is that each already adequate activity manifests nonexclusively in the joint effect. That is a focused metaphysical disagreement, not a claim that A actually supplied B's act.

The affirmative can answer that such achieved agency remains productively incomplete in the relevant original respect, regardless of intrinsic nonreceipt. The rival must then justify its classification of appropriate efficacy and the eligibility of its original-peer end. The existing representations and conditional means rationale do not provide that full warrant. Equally, the bare implication from A's willing to B's accompanying willing cannot by itself settle the productive classification.

**Net assessment:** the actual joint-support reading strengthens the affirmative challenge beyond generic modal conflict. It leaves the same live perfection/classification interface identified by the existing source work. The modal criticism does not refute a separately warranted P1–P3 argument; the p.29 rationale does not independently establish all of P1 merely by mentioning a sufficient condition.

## 5. Moral reasons and complete particular concordance

Agreement on every moral truth and compliance with every moral requirement need not determine one complete action profile. Morally permissible alternatives, equal goods, timing, actor-relative duties, and different means to a common end can remain. Thus:

- Moral agreement does not by itself prove full particular concordance.
- Failure of that entailment does not prove that conflict is possible.
- A fuller reason structure might require compatible plans, even without a unique value-maximizing outcome.

The retained diagonal-means result already provides one conditional instrumental rationale: with a shared target, equivalent practical roles, and the exchange-invariant planning norm, the existing means are justified without a unique outcome optimizer. That result must not be erased. It also does not prove that every normative context has that symmetry, or that the designated original-peer undertaking is perfectly wise.

The source's distinction between standing power, particular actual willing, and wise approval is crucial. Inability to perform a contradictory task is not loss of a genuine power. Necessary wise nonselection of an act also need not erase antecedent capacity. But unrestricted sovereignty may impose an additional original-efficacy standard beyond either fact. One cannot obtain that standard by identifying power, admissibility, and entire production as one predicate.

The concordance account retains an explanatory burden: a successful conditional means theorem does not explain why these particular q-contexts, ultimate ends, or complete actual willings obtain. Mere covariance does not supply sufficient reasons. Shared reasons also need not be a third efficient controller, and no efficient donation follows merely from common rational content. Conversely, absence of a disclosed complete hidden-reason account is not proof of arbitrary or impossible willing; neither side receives that asymmetric burden.

There is no need to demand the ability to act unwisely to preserve an argument for unique adequate origin. Nor may the rival claim that sharing moral reasons already explains every particular determination. The right issue is the actual complete practical basis and original productive adequacy, considered separately.

## 6. Appraisal of the two criticisms

### Direct ability criticism: succeeds locally, leaves an affirmative answer available

The direct criticism isolates a genuine gap under ordinary counterfactual ability: an alternative intention may be accompanied by the peer's corresponding alternative. Actual nonexercise does not establish absence of that standing ability. The retained concordance interpretation is of this general form.

The strongest reply insists that an omnipotent original needs a more robust ability, or independently adequate actual efficacy, rather than merely an intention–success counterfactual. The criticism does not show that this stronger conception is incoherent. It also does not itself give a complete definition of omnipotence under which the two-agent ontology qualifies.

A further limit is important: plausible tolerance of another's mistakes does not by itself prove that no conflict could occur under every admissible circumstance. Modal compatibility has to be established at the intended breadth. We therefore use the criticism as a discriminator of an inference, not as certification of a divine pair.

### Parallel paradox critique: worthwhile challenge, not decisive

Several independent observations constrain its application:

1. An at-most-one theorem never supplies an existence theorem. That is a valid scope warning, not a counterexample to uniqueness.
2. Coherent mutual frustration among ordinary agents does not show that such frustration is compatible with independently motivated maximal efficacy. Ordinary agents' coherence answers a different question.
3. A task can be generically intelligible while being impossible under the properties of its named performer. Whether the restriction is principled must be examined; generic intelligibility alone does not decide that.
4. Universal or self-involving quantification is not sufficient for paradox. A rigorous incoherence proof would need the particular closure, comprehension, diagonalization, or incompatible success requirements that generate contradiction. Rejecting unrestricted self-referential tasks can be independently motivated without reducing every ordinary limited agent to omnipotence.
5. If a valid uniqueness argument establishes the impossibility of jointly existing omnipotents, inability to realize that impossibility does not refute a possibility-qualified conception of power. An argument that it does requires the unrestricted conception precisely at issue.

Thus no blanket circularity verdict follows. The strongest useful challenge is to give a non-ad-hoc account of which limitations disqualify maximal power. The inherited comparative-perfection reasoning makes substantive progress on that issue, while preserving the still-disputed classification of primitive original co-manifestation.

The cancellation analysis is not substituted for the retained rival. That rival's ordinary concordant undertakings intentionally achieve their products; it is not a picture in which every will fails while an uncaused event happens to match one.

## 7. Exact relationship to the current de se routes

**Route A, mastery and unassailability:** unchanged. A universal-other mastery premise and peer-immunity conflict without possible disagreement. The selected literature does not derive that universal authority scope from mere agent plurality. Required independent justification remains substantive.

**Route B, complete actual origin and proper unborrowed exercise:** the strongest fit. The new reading supplies a published parallel to the sufficiency issue, while the inherited article gives a more careful productive-accounting interpretation. Once whole-origin adequacy and the proper-exercise exclusion hold, the identity consequence remains valid. No modal-scope repair can defeat those premises.

**Route C, incompatible guarantees and common admissible completion:** the published conflict analysis belongs here. Neither separate powers nor necessary moral agreement settles its common-completion premise. STR cannot silently replace necessary nonconflict, and a fixed-setting modality cannot silently become global metaphysical necessity.

**Centered self-knowledge and practical basing:** unchanged. Semantic truthful knowledge of each agent's own identity, agency, and joint support remains available in the reduced signature. It does not establish action-guiding recognition or primitive practical basing. If P1 is independently true and known, recognition plus perfect conformance can mediate disqualification; self-knowledge still does not prove P1.
