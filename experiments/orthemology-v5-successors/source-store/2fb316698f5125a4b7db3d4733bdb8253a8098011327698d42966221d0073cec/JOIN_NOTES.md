# Contextual rereads and join reconciliation

All six prepared reread windows were displayed without truncation and read through their end markers. These are additional contextual rereads, not additions to the already partitioned first-pass body. Exact ranges, counts and digests are in [contextual reread coverage](READING_COVERAGE.json).

## Join 1: SF-S001-L0235–0245

L0237–0238 returns from the lengthy metaphysical critique to the opening issue of prophecy. L0238 affirms unusual prophetic knowledge, representation and God-given psychic efficacy, then begins the dispute over intrinsic natural powers. L0239 completes its interrupted eye/nose, bread/drink and water/vinegar examples. L0240 expressly affirms causal powers and denies the proposed exhaustive explanation of prophetic signs. The subsequent staff argument concerns the alleged incapacity of those powers to explain this particular transformation and the occasion's communicative purpose. Therefore range1's final concession and range2's initial causation debate form one continuous argument. No contradiction is created by the artificial split.

## Join 2: SF-S002-L0132–0140

L0135 reconstructs a restricted kalam proof-chain: knowledge of the messenger presupposes the sender; this allegedly requires temporal origination, bodily accidents and rejection of an unbeginning series. L0136–0138 criticises this route, distinguishing criticism of its danger/length from criticism of false premises. L0139 then speaks in the philosophers' reported voice as they exploit its weakness; their alternative is not thereby endorsed. The join must preserve both attributed voices and the difference between refuting a rival proof and establishing one's own positive claim.

## Join 3: SF-S002-L0605–0618

L0607 reports the reply that eternal emanation waits on successive receptivity. L0608–0610 grants why an active intellect on the opponents' account, or the sun on the author's own ordinary example, can wait on external conditions. L0611 begins a two-horn dilemma about the comprehensive first source, and L0612–0614 completes the second horn. L0615–0618 then re-applies complete-cause reasoning. The point depends on what is already included within 'first source': conditions of finite sources can come from elsewhere, whereas every other thing is the comprehensive Creator's effect. This is a real domain qualification, not a missing exception to be erased. The later discussion must also retain its distinctions between prior preparation and presently complete efficacy.

## Join 4: SF-S003-L0362–0375

The single-creature argument at L0362–0365 distinguishes one undivided generated individual from collaborative works in which contributions are distinguished. L0366–0367 then asks whether an emanated form is substance or accident. L0368–0371 attacks dividing the agency of inseparable mixing and resultant mud between independent ultimate makers. L0372–0373 distinguishes vicious reciprocal productive dependence from concomitance of effects or necessary attributes. The following Ibn Sina quotation at L0375 begins a fresh critique of identifying divine knowledge, will and power. These are connected but distinct arguments; the quotation's language is not automatically the author's own final formulation.

## Source/perfection context: SF-S002-L0328–0346

The positive argument follows the proposed establishment of an independent, necessary, sustaining and creative bearer. L0330–0336 uses both comparative possibility of defect-free perfection and the source of created perfections, then excludes borrowing the divine perfection from its own product or a second necessary maker. L0337 explicitly accepts intrinsic necessitation of necessary attributes while refusing the same account of creatures. L0340–0346 distinguishes requiring an attribute or constituent from having an efficient maker. This context prevents substituting an unrestricted 'every prerequisite is efficiently made by every origin' principle for the author's more differentiated reasoning.

## Final partition: SF-S004-L0052–0063

The closing pages criticise both unrestricted assimilation to God and an absolute prohibition on commended shared predicates. L0055 permits human knowledge, mercy and wisdom while withholding divine exclusives; L0056 makes need, humility, servanthood and petition creaturely excellences incompatible with God. The final account of worship and love is therefore essential to the meaning of 'perfection', not an optional religious appendix. L0062 closes the argument; L0063 is the received colophon's distinct attributive voice.
