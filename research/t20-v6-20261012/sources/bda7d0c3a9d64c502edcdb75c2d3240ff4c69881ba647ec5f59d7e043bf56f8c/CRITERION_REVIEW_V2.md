## Substantive semantic review

### 2. Evidence classes, coverage and quantifiers

The cover-and-preserve theorem is valid: source robust truth, a related source witness for every eligible recipient history, and judgment preservation entail recipient robust truth. The chosen relation can map different histories; identity is unnecessary.

For one fixed conclusion Q, backward coverage is sufficient and is not necessary. An unmatched recipient history can independently satisfy Q. The explicit fixed-true-Q exception correctly prevents overstating necessity.

The iff theorem ranges uniformly over all predicate pairs P,Q satisfying preservation. Choosing P universally true and Q equal to the image of the source evidence class under R derives backward coverage. The proof uses that unrestricted predicate family. It does not prove coverage necessary for a single substantive norm or a restricted language lacking the image predicate.

The Lean predicates allow empty evidence classes. That does not invalidate the general theorem, but applications claiming actual-world truth require actual-world inclusion and therefore cannot rely on an empty class. The prose correctly separates robust evidence-relative truth, actual inclusion, and the actor's warrant to rely on the class. It does not identify universal evidence-class truth with ordinary fallible epistemic warrant.
