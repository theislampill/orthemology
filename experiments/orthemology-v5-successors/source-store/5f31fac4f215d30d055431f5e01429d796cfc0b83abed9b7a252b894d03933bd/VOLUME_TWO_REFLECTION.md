# Reflective synthesis: original al-Ṣawāʿiq, received volume-two body

Reader-facing study record, 7 October 2026. The interpretations and reading claims below belong to the recorded study and its bounded rereads. This edition of the notes adds no source-reading or proof credit.

This synthesis follows a complete continuous first pass of the assigned electronic body and eight finite reflective rereads. It concerns Ibn al-Qayyim’s original received text, not the abridgment, and does not assert word-for-word identity with the retained modern critical edition. Consult [volume two continuous notes](VOLUME_TWO_CONTINUOUS_NOTES.md) for continuous segment notes including law, language, history, psychology, devotion, and nature.

## 1. What the strongest positive case actually is

It would misrepresent this volume to reduce its case to “the world has a common cause” or to a purely negative ban on similarity. Its affirmative architecture is fuller:

1. The divine bearer is a necessary, determinate, self-subsistent existent, whose perfection and existence are not supplied from outside.
2. He has intrinsically complete life, knowledge, power, will, goodness, wisdom, beauty, and real action. A giver of creaturely perfections is more entitled to the nondefective perfection than its receiver.
3. Negative descriptions are praiseworthy insofar as they express positive fullness: sleeplessness expresses full life/sustenance, no fatigue full power, no ignorance complete knowledge, and no partner full dominion/independence.
4. “Highest exemplar” and absolute subjugation cannot be shared by coequals: either the supposed rival is unequal/subdued, or the first lacks the asserted absolute superiority.
5. One fully qualified bearer, many inseparable qualities, genuine elective acts, and many distinct created effects are compatible. Necessary perfection is not featureless simplicity.
6. Wise names are efficacious: they have manifested effects, not idle titles. God acts from prior complete perfection; creatures can gain perfection through acts.
7. Creatures have real derivative attributes and actions. Their genuine efficacy, temporality, and distinctness do not compete with the divine mode.

These claims are closely connected in the author’s presentation but should remain distinct premises when assessing an argument. The acknowledged force of 4 must not be weakened to a mere restatement of cause-dependence. Conversely, accepting only 1 does not silently authorise all of 2–7.

## 2. Reread A: intrinsic articulation is not external dependence

Source 002:1066–1071, face 61.

The passage explicitly rejects the transition from any distinguishable multiplicity to externally caused composition. There are two senses of “other”: separable external entity and epistemically distinguishable content. There are two senses of dependence: needing an outside producer and an attribute’s inseparable inherence in its bearer. The qualified divine essence, not a bare essence subsequently equipped from outside, is the self-subsistent reality.

The strongest charitable reconstruction is not that attributes possess independent necessity and collaborate to make God. It is that the one concrete qualified bearer is necessary, and its constitutive perfections are inseparable and unproduced. Nor is it that “nothing depends in any respect on anything”: the author expressly allows essential co-requirement. A modern proof using unrestricted “all internal differentiation needs a cause” would therefore contradict his own stated defence.

Open assessment question: what establishes that this qualified necessary bearer is the sole independent concrete bearer? This paragraph blocks one simplicity objection but is not, by itself, an exhaustive uniqueness argument. That work is supplied elsewhere by perfection, absolute domination, and the larger theological case.

## 3. Rereads B–C: positive perfection and exclusive highestness

Source 002:1159–1210 and 1335–1348, faces 70–72,79,89 (C also includes the beginning of 90).

The qualification in the perfection-giving rule is essential: “a perfection in the creature containing or entailing no deficiency.” It does not automatically transfer every creature-specific characteristic. The author separately argues that life/agency, moral affection, speech/perception, height and revealed bodily-sounding predicates meet the criterion; those arguments should be evaluated separately. Generic existence is not yet every perfection.

Face 79 explicitly argues: if two were equal, neither would be higher than the other; if unequal, only the higher has the highest exemplar. The universal-subjugation parallel is even more transparently exclusive: failure to subdue an equal means no absolute subjugation; subduing it denies its equality. Face 89 names perfect fullness and its unshareability as two certain premises, supplemented by revelatory/natural signs rather than deriving them merely by counting dependent effects.

Strongest reading: strict unmatched superiority and exhaustive sovereignty rule out a coordinate independent equal. This is a substantive exclusion of peers, not a ban on limited creatures’ sharing general predicates.

Necessary scrutiny: mathematical/logical maximality alone permits tied maxima. The text’s أعلى is strict surpassing of every other, not simply “no greater being exists.” If that strict exclusive superiority is already warranted by his perfection conception and its evidence, the uniqueness conclusion follows. If only weak maximality or a necessary existent is granted, that stricter premise still needs defence. Likewise, “can subdue every other” must be justified as a perfection applicable even against a proposed independent necessary peer; merely defining omnipotence that way cannot substitute for that justification. These are premise questions, not refutations by stipulation.

The author distinguishes four levels of highest exemplar: perfection in the divine reality; its cognition in creatures; its verbal expression; and worship/love responding to it. Confusing cognition or worship with the divine property itself would miss the argument. All creaturely beauty can be genuine while incomparably below divine beauty.

## 4. Reread D: bearer, attribute, act, product

Source 003:395–428, faces 132–133.

Three sets of entailments are explicitly distinguished: those belonging to a predicate as such; those arising from its divine bearer; and those arising from its created bearer. Knowledge as such relates to known things; divine knowledge is necessary/eternal/comprehensive; creature knowledge is created/limited/removable. Transferring the third class to God is precisely the error under criticism.

The act/product distinction is equally strong: divine guidance is his act; ensuing human acceptance/guidance is the human act, not the same action falsely renamed. Creation’s coming-to-be follows divine forming/speaking. The will is not an inert label attached only to the creaturely product. God’s real temporally specific speech, mercy, displeasure and governance must not be eliminated by calling all changes external.

This yields scope discipline:
- Bearer: the numerically distinct, concrete subsistent subject.
- Attribute: a perfection inhering in that bearer, not an independent producer.
- Act: the bearer’s genuine willing/doing/speaking, with appropriate temporal specification.
- Product: the created, distinct effect.
- Creaturely act: genuinely done by a creature under derivative power, distinct from the divine creating/guiding act.

The text does not license “every property has one bearer,” “all actions are God’s action in exactly the same sense,” or “a single source means one substance.” It expressly rejects identity, indwelling, and pure metaphorical creature-predication.

## 5. Rereads E–F: self-sufficiency with real response

Source 003:1152–1192 and 004:16–36, face 200 replies 5–19.

The text supplies a particularly strong affirmative answer to the claim that responsive love/joy would make God dependent:
- His own complete reality is sufficient for self-knowledge, self-love and self-praise; created approval does not supply a previously missing constitutive perfection.
- He can nonetheless genuinely approve, rejoice at repentance, dislike injustice, respond to prayer, hear and see actual events.
- Creatures and their acts are not independent external determinants of deity: the entire dependent order, including these occasions, is within his wise creative will.
- Offense/adhā is not damaging diminishment.

The crucial “influence” sentence at 003:1184 is corrupt in this electronic witness. Direct visual spot-collation of printed 1037–1038 confirms the editor’s two-alternative reading: denying an outsider’s supplying a missing perfection, versus excluding acts that occasion divine responses. The first is accepted, the second exclusion rejected. Crucially, the apparatus adopts أم تريد in place of ح’s أنه يريد from م, which the introduction identifies as al-Mawṣilī’s abridgment. Thus the intelligible printed contrast is an identified auxiliary-witness repair, not independently preserved exact original wording; surrounding unbroken argument supports the substantive distinction. No repaired wording is silently inserted into the electronic source.

Important modality: complete decisive willing with complete power entails the appropriate act; the mere possibility of further enjoyment does not entail decisive willing. The author expressly rejects compulsion from deprivation, because God is not harmed by not creating a particular enjoyable thing. Successive events cannot all be actual together. Forgiveness after wrongdoing cannot occur before wrongdoing. Objects are willed/loved at appropriate times, measures and under their constitutive conditions. This defeats a flattening from permanent perfection to eternally simultaneous products.

The text also offers an opponent-relative fallback using eternal attributes with renewed relations. This is not an abandonment of its own real renewed-acts view. Dialectical concessions are not to be merged into a single unqualified authorial theory.

Critical remainder: deriving all occasions from a complete self-sufficient will is not an independent demonstration that such universal willing exists. It is a powerful coherence defence once that premise is granted. A candidate critique must address whether complete nonacquired perfection can ground genuine temporal response without deficiency, rather than simply presuming all responsive difference is externally supplied deficiency.

## 6. Reread G: genuine creaturely predication

Source 004:148–159, especially face 234.

The author rejects both extremes: literal life/knowledge/power only in creatures, and literal predicates only in God with creaturely predication reduced to metaphor. It is no defence of divine uniqueness to erase created realities. Pure homonymy is also rejected because it makes language unintelligible. Shared meaning is compatible with unequal realisation; graded/analogical predication (tashkīk) is classified under a broad common-meaning predication (tawāṭuʾ).

Thus “all power/perfection belongs to God” cannot be read as “creatures literally have no power, knowledge or beautiful qualities.” The exclusivity concerns perfect, intrinsic, underived, comprehensive possession. Limited bestowed instances remain real. This is decisive evidence for preserving creaturely agency and causal contribution within source-dependence rather than equating dependence with nonexistence.

## 7. Reread H: efficacious perfection and necessity

Source 004:338–344, final theodicy reply 14 and heading 15.

The modal language is strong: names imply their effects as an efficacious necessitating ground implies what it grounds; their traces must manifest. Wisdom has universal scope over all creation/decree/command/ban and cannot detach from the acts any more than the attribute from its bearer. It would understate this passage to say God merely happens to have nice optional labels.

At the same time, scope must remain exact. Necessity of the living active perfect nature and of appropriate manifested effects does not by itself select a uniquely necessary token cosmos, every token action at every time, or simultaneous actuality of incompatible sequences. Read alongside face 200, it supports necessarily efficacious perfection with wise elective particularisation and ordered realisation. The received text does not here supply a complete modern modal-world semantics proving that every actual token could have been otherwise. Neither universal token-necessitarianism nor unrestricted token contingency should be silently inferred.

For a reader provisionally accepting a Necessary Being, this cluster adds a proposed bridge: real perfect wisdom/power/name-content entails efficacious expression, rather than inert abstract existence. But the bridge’s positive premises and the move to exhaustive scope must be assessed, not imported solely from “necessary being.” The author himself uses innate judgments, ordinary agency comparisons, natural signs, and revelation together.

## 8. Overall assessment constrained to this volume

This volume materially strengthens the positive case beyond a generic common-source argument. It provides explicit uniqueness reasoning from strict highestness and absolute dominion; an independence model allowing essential plurality; an expressive efficacy argument from perfect names; and a coherent account of real creaturely predicates, causal powers, temporal ordering, and responsive divine action. It also gives arguments against emanation’s one-from-one principle and against scope-confused composition objections.

It does not warrant pretending that bare Necessary Being alone mechanically yields the entire result. The contested transitions are visible: positive perfection from necessary/sourcehood; classification of particular attributes as unqualified perfections; strict peer-excluding highestness; exhaustive dominion; and spatial separateness from external concrete subsistence. Formal entailment verification, however impeccable, leaves these substantive historical premises to be defended.

The warranted update is therefore affirmative evidential enrichment with a more exact premise ledger, not a preselected total verdict. Any eventual rejection must meet these strongest positive claims; any eventual acceptance must identify which of those claims has been independently warranted and on what basis.

## 9. Textual and historical cautions

The assigned transcript is fully read; the retained printed edition is not wholly collated. Inherited part markers are not modern volume divisions. Nominal main-polemic count is 242, with 234 extant per the corrected editorial introduction; 48–49 are omission-or-numbering uncertainty,73–78 a reported lacuna. Thirty subarguments after 147 and thirty after 200 explain jumps to 178 and 231. The face 241→Iblīs transition in 004 has an actual original-witness lacuna. The independent reviewer’s direct inspection of printed 1109–1113 locates the restored face 242 heading/setup at 1111–1113; editor footnotes 1111(3) and 1113(3) delimit material supplied from م, al-Mawṣilī’s abridgment. This restoration is not counted as independently surviving original prose and is not substituted into our body-reading receipts. The Iblīs debate itself is explicitly treated by Ibn al-Qayyim as unverified Israelite material or a constructed argument, not authenticated divine speech. No absent final promised major sections are supplied from al-Mawṣilī.

Quoted al-Ghazālī, Ibn Sīnā, al-Rāzī, Ibn ʿAqīl, Ibn Kullāb, al-Ashʿarī, al-Bāqillānī and transmission archives remain separately attributed. Denunciations, coercive prescriptions and sectarian histories are historical polemic, not endorsed contemporary rulings. Natural history, optics, cosmography and weather explanation remain historically situated; their presence is read and analysed, not upgraded to current scientific authority.
