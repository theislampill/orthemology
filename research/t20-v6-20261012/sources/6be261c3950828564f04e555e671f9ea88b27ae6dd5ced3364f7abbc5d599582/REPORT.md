# Commissioned content access and continuing obligation

9 October 2026 UTC. Substantive test of the communication-availability report against the complete retained Arabic Darʾ 5:269–286 unit and the scriptural-fibre supplement.

## Result

The new inference has a genuine discriminating consequence, but its strongest direct form is narrower than perpetual preservation:

**One cannot retain the source’s authenticating proof of truthful, explanatory guidance and also maintain that the authentic commissioned discourse really indicates falsehood while giving its intended recipients no warranted way to obtain its intended truth. Independent discovery of the truth does not, by itself, turn such discourse into an explanation of that truth.**

This excludes the actual interpretive rival attacked at 280–282. It does not exclude a different account in which the messenger clearly delivered the truth, later custodians lost part of it, and the later recipient’s corresponding epistemic obligation is limited or suspended. Excluding that account requires an enduring access commitment or a defended bridge from a particular continuing obligation to adequate epistemic opportunity. Those are identifiable additional premises, not consequences of bare original veracity.

The strongest constructive gain is therefore a **source-attributable content criterion and an anti-empty-rescue argument**. It preserves genuine mediated knowledge without requiring unique autograph recovery; it also prevents an inaccessible intended meaning or an ungrounded overriding faculty from being counted as an actual explanation.

## What the Arabic establishes

Three parts of the continuous argument constrain the inference.

First, 269–271 separates the truth of revelation from a particular route to our knowing it. A proof entails its conclusion; loss of one proof need not eliminate either truth or another way of knowing. The stronger epistemic dependence is explicitly conditional on having no other route. At 278–280 the author rejects an unsupported claim that one particular technical argument exhausts everyone’s grounds. Consequently, neither the loss of an inscription nor the failure of a selected historical panel licenses an automatic inference to the loss of all epistemic access. The expected-transmission example at 270 is a special necessary-concomitant argument under its own transmission conditions, not an unconditional promise of preservation.

Second, 280–282 supplies the operative positive premise. The author does not argue merely from a messenger’s honesty or appointment to every recipient’s access. He invokes rational evidence for a messenger who “بلغ البلاغ المبين” and for “صدقه وتبليغه وبيانه وهداه للخلق”: truthful reporting, delivery, explanation and guidance. The opposed portrayal makes the source manifest falsehood and leave the truth unexplained, with people finding the truth through their own reason. He takes that portrayal to conflict with what the authenticating evidence establishes. At 282 the reporting premise extends throughout its actual authenticated domain; it is not a reliability score that permits an acknowledged false instance inside that same unrestricted domain.

Third, 285 connects protection in reporting from God to the mission’s purpose, namely assent to what is reported and obedience to what is commanded. The formulation “فلا يستقر في خبره خطأ” excludes an error’s remaining settled in that reporting; it should not silently be strengthened into a ban on every temporary appearance of error. Acts outside reporting are expressly left to a different discussion. Likewise, the polemic distinguishes proper provision from recipients’ failure to receive, follow or preserve it. Those failures are not all failures of original delivery.

These are the author’s substantive commitments in this argument. Reconstructing them does not independently establish the authenticating proof’s premises against an opponent who rejects them. In particular, **commission to explain**, **successful explanatory discharge**, and **an ongoing guarantee of access** must not be treated as interchangeable. The text asserts the second; the first alone would not entail it, and neither by itself supplies the third.

## A discriminating argument

Fix a teaching C, its relevant recipients G, and the circumstances in which the source undertakes to explain it. Let E be evidence establishing truthful and successful explanatory delivery in that scope. Keep three claims separate:

1. E is sound for that reporting and explanatory domain.
2. An authenticated communicative act, correctly interpreted with its operative context, really indicates an incompatible false teaching F.
3. No warranted communicative or interpretive correction makes the intended truth available within the undertaking.

On the author’s account these cannot all stand. Claims 2 and 3 give the portrayal rejected at 281: manifesting falsehood without explaining the truth. Claim 1 excludes that portrayal. This is a conditional inconsistency proof whose empirical and philosophical premises remain visible.

Its practical discrimination is specific:

- **Unexplained suspension:** affirming the source’s complete authority while disabling the genuinely established indication of its discourse does not preserve what that discourse taught. It retains the source’s name while withholding its indicated content. This is the actual reply to the proposed tafwīḍ at 280.
- **Hidden-meaning rescue:** saying that the source secretly meant a truth that the recipients had no grounds to recover leaves claim 3 untouched. A true intention is not its explanation.
- **Warranted interpretation:** showing that the supposed false indication was never the discourse’s correct contextual meaning defeats claim 2. The passage itself says that if revelation does not indicate the contrary, the alleged conflict is absent. This leaves room for a genuinely warranted contextual or figurative reading; calling a reading nonliteral does not decide its status.
- **Corrected misunderstanding:** an actually available, appropriately grounded clarification can defeat claim 3. Naming a correction that was inaccessible at the relevant time cannot do so for those recipients, although it may show that the original speaker corrected the report for another audience or occasion.

This is more than the definition that successful communication succeeds. The source gives successful explanation a role in authentication and makes that role constrain which interpretive accounts can coexist with the claimed proof. The cost of accepting the misleading account is revision of at least one substantive commitment: the authenticating premise, the alleged indication, the report’s inclusion in its domain, or the asserted absence of correction. Nothing here establishes in advance which commitment an actual disputed case must revise.

## A genuine correction supplies the missing relation

Two questions must be answered separately: “Is C true?” and “Is C what this commissioned source communicated?” The source’s opponent can claim that reason establishes the first while the text misleads about it. Independent knowledge of C alone therefore does not repair the second.

A corrective route succeeds when it supplies appropriate grounds connecting C to the communicative act and makes that content accessible to the relevant recipients. Such grounds can include competent language use, context, reliable teaching, a later clarification or rational evidence that genuinely fixes the intended reading. They need not take the form of an explicit written certificate or a proof of every cognitive faculty. But a bare assertion that an override exists, or the assumption that C must be the message because the disputed message is already authoritative, does not discharge the missing dependency.

A concrete positive pattern is straightforward. A recipient has warranted access to a reliably transmitted teaching C and its attribution to S; the operative authenticating ground establishes S’s truthfulness in that reporting domain. When actually exercised or suitably retained, this route can warrant assent to C even when two admissible original wordings express C equally well. The original wording target may vary while the needed source-and-content relation is adequately warranted. If an exact utterance or recitation is itself prescribed, its identity belongs to the required content and cannot be discarded as an irrelevant variant.

A proof existing somewhere is not yet an available route for this recipient, and an available route is not yet their actual uptake of it. Conversely, refusal or inattention need not show failure to provide clear guidance. The inherited common-policy distinction also applies: a different solution existing in each admissible history does not supply a warranted course of inquiry available to the recipient who does not know which history is actual. No new theorem credit is claimed for that distinction.

This is a sufficient constructive pattern, not an exhaustive definition of warrant or a demand that every recipient articulate its premises. It preserves the inherited recognition and testimony appraisals. An imagined alternative does not defeat an already supported route; conversely, an imagined rescue does not repair an established local failure.

## Original success does not entail perpetual access

A simple countermodel locates the missing temporal premise. At t₀ a messenger truthfully and intelligibly communicates C to G₀. The hearers understand it; no misleading report remains uncorrected. At t₁ an intervening loss leaves another group G₁ without an adequate route to C. No undertaking that C remain accessible to G₁ at t₁ has been included, and no unexcused duty of warranted uptake is imposed on them then.

Original delivery, original truthfulness and present inaccessibility are consistent in this model. It is a logical countermodel, not a historical account of any scripture or a complete attribution of Ibn Taymiyyah’s theology. Adding human culpability for a later loss also does not retroactively make the messenger’s original report false. It can instead locate a failure of custody.

The text does contain later-facing applications. At 283–286 it treats the Book and prophetic teaching as continuing standards, censures selective acceptance, and invokes referral of disputes to God and the messenger. This gives real support to the author’s commitment to continuing relevance and usable guidance for the disputes he addresses. It is not merely a theory of a speech heard once by the first audience. Nevertheless, those applications neither quantify over every future group and teaching nor prove uninterrupted access to every original verbal detail. They presuppose relevant available guidance in the polemic rather than derive a comprehensive transmission history.

## Exact premises for a diachronic conclusion

There are two substantive ways to block the countermodel.

### An enduring undertaking

Establish that the authenticated commission includes **adequate access to this C for this later G at this t under these actual circumstances**, with successful discharge of that undertaking, not merely a preference that people eventually know the truth. Then establish that all actually relevant warranted means fail that access task. The resulting incompatibility is direct: the continuing undertaking was not fulfilled.

The added work is identifying the content, temporal and audience scope, and the undertaking’s force. General divine wisdom, bare honesty, or successful delivery at t₀ does not by itself fill those variables. If a stronger conclusion about divine perfection is intended, the contradiction must reach a commitment genuinely entailed by the claimed perfection and purpose, rather than only a fallible custodian’s task.

### A continuing epistemic obligation

Alternatively, establish both:

- G at t remains under a determinate, unexcused obligation to accept **this C as this source’s teaching**, or intelligently to obey it, at the required level of understanding.
- Such an obligation in these circumstances requires an adequate epistemic opportunity: a present warranted route, or a suitably retained earlier acquisition. The recipient’s lack of access is not a culpable self-incurred incapacity that leaves responsibility traceable to an earlier adequate opportunity.

Writing O for that specifically qualified obligation and A for adequate epistemic opportunity, the needed bridge is O ⇒ A. With actual ¬A, the conjunction is inconsistent. A bare “ought implies can” is insufficient if “can” means only accidentally guessing the right answer; the required ability must match the epistemic task.

This bridge is philosophically defensible for an obligation of responsible assent: demanding that someone knowingly accept the source’s determinate teaching while giving them no non-accidental way to distinguish it from an incompatible attribution defeats the specified mode of compliance. But not every enduring norm is such an obligation. The continued truth or objective authority of C, a general duty to inquire, communal responsibility to preserve teaching, and an individual’s current unexcused duty of warranted assent are different. Culpable neglect can leave responsibility after the opportunity is lost; nonculpable deprivation can instead limit the presently applicable duty. The inherited capacity-sensitive findings support taking these distinctions seriously without themselves proving a universal preservation promise.

Thus continuing obligation is neither irrelevant nor a shortcut. The precise qualified obligation and the access bridge can support the diachronic argument; the word “obligatory” alone cannot.

## Consequence for the fibre connection

The proper target is the commissioned source-and-content relation needed for the recipient’s task, including exact wording where required. A failure of unique autograph recovery need not defeat that target. Conversely, an attribution ambiguity can matter even where the same true sentence is otherwise knowable.

The inherited fibre result can prove failure of uniform exact recovery when genuinely admissible histories at the actual record vary on this target. It does not by itself prove failure of every adequate probabilistic or recognitional warrant. A claim of total present inaccessibility must assess the operative channels at the epistemic standard the undertaking actually requires. This need not enumerate every imagined supernatural possibility: an ungrounded rescue supplies no positive route, just as speculative doubts supply no automatic defeater of established access.

A rival claimant’s well-warranted transmission may show that a corresponding communicative task is achievable and sharpen the diagnosis of a specified failure. The inconsistency above does not require that rival, and the rival’s recoverability does not establish its authority. No comparative maximisation principle is supplied by this Arabic unit.

## Earned conclusion

The source supports a real exclusion: **authenticated explanatory guidance cannot be retained together with an established, uncorrectedly false communicative indication in its own scope.** It also supports a positive route through adequately warranted source-attributable content, including mediated and non-autograph access. The transition to later generations requires a continuing access undertaking or a specifically defended obligation-to-opportunity bridge. Until one of those is established for a named teaching and audience, original successful delivery with later loss remains a genuine obstruction to the stronger inference.
