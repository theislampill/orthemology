## Factivity and modal force

An actual explanation of p entails that p actually obtains. That is compatible with p being contingent. The additional conclusion that p necessarily obtains requires additional modal premises.

Let A obtain necessarily and let the actual proposition L say that A explains p. Necessity of A does not entail necessity of L. Even if L is factive whenever it obtains, p's necessity follows only if the requisite L also obtains necessarily. This is elementary modal bookkeeping, not evidence that A or L exists or explains the actual correction.

## Bearer and act

Suppose g necessarily exists, g's particular volition W is contingent, and W together with sufficient power produces a. These premises do not make a necessary. To obtain that conclusion one needs necessity of W or a different necessary determinant sufficient for a. This is a conditional inference check, not a demonstration that the supposition describes actual divine agency.

The supplied Oneness passage, printed p.34/PDF p.41, distinguishes complete power and will from a stripped essence. It supports the target distinction without proving that every particular divine volition is contingent. Explaining a may legitimately cite its actual bearer; efficiently originating that bearer's whole actuality cannot presuppose the very actuality to be originated. This scoped asymmetry does not decide all forms of constitutive explanation.
