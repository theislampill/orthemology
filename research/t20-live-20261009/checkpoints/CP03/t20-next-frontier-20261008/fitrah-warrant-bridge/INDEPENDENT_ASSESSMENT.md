# From basic recognition to epistemic warrant

8 October 2026. Independent philosophical assessment following the complete bounded chapter reading. The short source summary is in PUBLIC_SOURCE_SUMMARY.md. This document develops the assessment rather than a substitute digest of the copyrighted chapter. It proposes no repository adoption or final T20 integration.

## Judgement

The affirmative judgement established here concerns prima facie rational basic belief. It is weaker than the original claim that humans can have genuinely knowledgeable, relatively basic Creator recognition. A person can be prima facie rational in a relatively basic Creator belief without first reconstructing a public proof or a scientific theory of the belief’s production. Non-discursive generation, natural origin and affective involvement are not disqualifiers. A person can experience dependence, agency or order as immediately significant rather than consciously deriving a conclusion through articulated premises. The positive rationale is the ordinary standing of cognitive seemings, not an automatic transfer from subjective confidence to knowledge.

Genuinely knowledgeable relatively basic recognition remains a live candidate under the accepted metaphysical background, which includes an actual Creator. This task does not independently establish that stronger factive CAN claim, even as a modal conclusion: the parity argument earns a more modest entitlement, and the semantic argument’s acquaintance premises remain substantive. Nor does it independently verify actual human instances. The incremental status is NO_NEW_INDEPENDENT_VERIFICATION of the stronger factive CAN claim. This is not a whole-programme downgrade: T17’s retained positive actual-recognition appraisal, as well as its accurate historical attribution, is not withdrawn. No new defeater of that qualified prior appraisal has been identified here. The corrections below concern selected empirical supports and an overbroad public transfer of warrant; their failure does not automatically defeat a separately grounded recognition. Conditional knowledge when truth and a suitable cognitive connection obtain is intelligible, but restating those conditions is not an independent proof that such a human route is realisable.

The public argument does not earn the stronger move from a generally natural disposition to the truth-directed success of every purported instance. The right discriminating premise is this:

In the particular relevant class of cases, the subject’s apparent Creator recognition is an appropriately functioning, truth-apt cognitive presentation, with the claimed content and no overriding defeater, rather than merely awareness of a desire, an undifferentiated agency response or a misleading projection.

This premise can receive philosophical, phenomenological and testimonial support. A successful laboratory reliability theorem is not its universally necessary precondition. Equally, calling a disposition natural or its subject sound is not sufficient support when those labels merely redescribe the conclusion to be established.

## The strongest affirmative case

The positive case should start from cognitive parity, not a poll. Perception, memory and basic rational apprehension can rationally support belief before the subject has a non-circular certification of those capacities. A demanded external certification of every elementary warrant would itself use faculties still awaiting certification. If a Creator-directed seeming is genuinely cognitive, it should not lose all initial standing merely because its object is religious or because its grounds are not discursively articulated. The inability to prove the reliability of all cognition from nowhere does not favour a selectively restrictive rule.

This is qualified parity. It applies to a relevantly sound operation in suitable conditions, not to every impulse and every proposition arising from a common origin. The reviewed chapter recognises development, impediment and incidental error. Its argument must not be weakened by replacing that qualified account with a plainly false claim that all natural inclinations are infallible. The direct warrant hinge occurs at pp. 178–179 and 182–183; the relevant qualifications occur at pp. 160–164 and 183–184.

A positive appraisal can therefore allow genuine first-order warrant while a complete explanatory theory remains disputed. Two accounts that disagree whether a warranted seeming is a special faculty, an integrated ordinary capacity or a sign-responsive act need not disagree about the entitlement of the same subject. Explanatory nonuniqueness alone is not an undercutter. The same standard should apply to T20’s substantive rational and evaluative judgements: dispute and absence of a norm-free derivation do not defeat them automatically.

## The strongest rival

The rival grants basic entitlement, a generally truth-directed human constitution, the distinction between original disposition and corruption, and even the possibility that some Creator-directed apprehensions are sound. It asks which capacity is operating in this case and what that operation presents as true.

There is an important difference between an immediately known inner occurrence and the actuality of its intentional object. A person may directly know that they long for an ultimate protector. That fact does not yet settle whether they directly recognise an actual protector, or instead judge that such a protector would fulfil the longing. This objection does not show that all religious experience is desire. It asks the affirmative to preserve the difference it needs: a cognitive seeming that p and an inclination towards p’s being true.

Likewise, a perception-like Creator presentation can be prima facie credible without being guaranteed true. A sceptic who supplies only the abstract possibility of error has not defeated it. A critic who instead establishes a specific mismatch of content, conditions or epistemic role has given a relevant reason. For example, evidence that an elicitation task produces generic agency answers under an ambiguous question does not establish a tendency to recognise one actual Creator. Conversely, it does not establish that a separately described Creator experience has that task’s mechanism.

The rival is strongest where it identifies a real defect in the offered warrant argument, rather than merely announcing naturalism or assigning dishonesty to believers. It can reject a cross-domain transfer of authority while continuing to use ordinary perception and reasoning. Such a local judgement is not globally sceptical unless the challenged feature is independently shown to be indispensable to the reliability of all those other capacities.

## A discriminating countermodel

Consider a constitution with three original features: ordinary truth-directed perception and reasoning; an appropriate desire for secure care and meaningful action; and a capacity for detecting agency from incomplete cues. All can develop, all can be obstructed, and their operations can influence one another. None is defined as pathological merely because it is motivational.

In one episode a subject directly apprehends dependence on an actual Creator through the significance of what is present. In another, a desire for safety recruits agency interpretation and produces the same verbal assent, although that particular transition is insensitive to whether the attributed agency is present. The subjects’ common origin, original dispositions and outward assent can be alike while the epistemic role of the token transition differs. A single Creator could be the original source of both episodes. The second episode’s proposition might even be true for reasons independent of that episode.

This is a conceptual countermodel to the entailment from common origin, prevalence and subjective assurance to equal token warrant. It is not an empirical claim that the second mechanism actually produces religious belief, a demonstrated alternative universe, or a refutation of the first episode’s possibility. It preserves the distinction between sound and obstructed functioning rather than treating every natural inclination as the same kind of cognition. Its purpose is to expose the missing classification step.

The affirmative can resist by providing grounds that the selected case belongs to the first type, or by defending a broader defeasible entitlement that survives uncertainty about its production. It cannot decide that question solely by repeating that both capacities have one source. The rival cannot decide it solely by inventing the second type. The live evidential burden is content- and case-sensitive.

## Three levels that must not collapse

1. Prima facie rational standing or entitlement concerns whether belief is permissible on the subject’s undefeated basis. It can be fallible. A subject need not understand their own cognitive mechanism.
2. Factive knowledge additionally requires truth and an adequate actual epistemic connection; it is not secured by permission alone. An unknown defect may obstruct knowledge without making the person irrational.
3. A scientifically established faculty-level reliability claim needs an operationally specified faculty, content, environments, conditions and appropriate evidence. It is a separate, stronger empirical achievement.

“Warrant” is sometimes used for level 1 and sometimes for the externalist property relevant to level 2. The appraisal must state which use is intended. None of the reviewed experimental endpoints measures divine reality or directly calibrates the truth of a particular Creator recognition. This is not a proposal to turn every basic belief into a laboratory hypothesis; it is a limit on using those experiments as scientific confirmation of an epistemic faculty.

Actual truth also differs from the route’s success. An independently accepted cosmological argument can establish the same proposition a subject believes, but cannot automatically convert the subject’s unrelated production process into knowledge. Conversely, the subject might know through a good basic route despite offering a defective public argument afterwards.

## What exactly makes a case non-discursive

“Relatively basic” here permits perceptual mediation and ordinary implicit cognitive processing. Non-discursively generated means that the subject does not acquire the belief by consciously working through a premise-by-premise argument. It does not mean no subpersonal inference, no learning, no experience, or articulated knowledge at birth. Epistemically non-inferential is a further claim about what provides the belief’s warrant and must be distinguished from this psychological description. Teacher-independence is insufficient by itself: an individual can consciously reason without a teacher. Being uninstructed, being activated by experience, lacking an articulated proof, and being generated without explicit discursive inference are different properties. The primary text itself leaves room for internally ordered inference and auxiliary teaching. Those passages support native capacity, but do not by themselves prove a genuinely basic generation route.

The separate primary argument for basic cases is the account of antecedent individual recognition and later confirmation: identifying a sign with its particular referent requires some prior grasp; knowing an object, knowing that one knows it and knowing the route of acquisition are distinct; a later proof may confirm rather than produce prior knowledge. The exact checked context is Dar electronic014, lines 913–928, already represented in inherited V08-P1856–1871.

The immediate argument concerns an individualizing sign, not merely a generic existential inference. Its antecedents include an already grasped determinate referent, grasp of the sign, and knowledge of the sign’s indicating relation. The examples of locating a familiar person or finding a known direction concern recovery or discrimination of an already encountered individual. Under genuine-acquaintance antecedents they do not need to establish existence from mere conception. They have real force against identifying a later explanation with the actual origin of knowledge. A fictional-description example would not refute that conditional.

The further application to initial Creator-recognition must nevertheless show that its antecedent grasp is acquaintance with the actual referent, rather than merely content sufficient to investigate it. The source expressly distinguishes general syllogistic conclusions from individual recognition and permits prior grasp under one aspect followed by discovery under another. It should not be reconstructed as the absurd universal rule that every existential proof already presupposes knowledge of its conclusion. The narrow concern is where actual acquaintance enters the particular argument. Granting it makes the restorative account intelligible; deriving it from conceptual grasp alone would leave the bridge unearned. This limits the proof’s public application without denying anyone’s antecedent knowledge.

A sharper rival to the semantic argument is first acquiring reference through a uniquely satisfied description, or following an actual trace to a previously unknown cause. Such cases need not involve fictional content. Whether they count as knowing the individual itself is a substantive theory of acquaintance and reference. The checked passage expressly allows a syllogism to establish a necessary existent, even uniquely, while distinguishing that from recognition of its particular identity. A de dicto existential or unique description, singular recognitional access and metaphysical de re existence are not interchangeable. The autobiographical correction about how one actually learned something therefore deserves separate credit from the stronger claim about the antecedents of every individualizing sign.

Thus the primary tradition genuinely affirms basic cases; it is historically inaccurate to demote its account to nothing more than hypothetical psychology. Independently appraising the philosophical proof of those cases remains a separate task. I accept the modest basic-entitlement position without claiming that the teacher-independence argument or the child-development material alone establishes it.

## Psychological explanation and defeat

A causal account of belief is not automatically a debunking account. Proper perception, memory and successful inference all have causes. Showing that distress, attention or development contributes to a belief leaves open whether the process makes a true object cognitively available.

A genuine undercutter needs more: a reason relevant to this content and route to doubt appropriate truth-directed operation. The empirical checks give reasons to reject some overbroad uses of selected experiments; they do not globally debunk religious cognition. Experimentally shifting endorsement through meaning-framing can reveal one context-sensitive influence without settling the epistemic status of all religious beliefs. Theistic explanation and psychological explanation can both apply. Neither compatibility nor a successful debunking of one public argument proves the actual truth of the religion.

The same discipline applies to reported emotional benefits. A belief’s contribution to wellbeing does not by itself authenticate its object; a painful recognition is not thereby false. Yet affective fulfilment can accompany genuine apprehension and need not be treated as evidence against it. The clinical endpoint correction is orthogonal to the philosophical question of sound disposition. It should not be recast as a clinical diagnosis of believers, nonbelievers or the user.

## Defeaters and a usable next test

Assessment should name the proposition before discussing its reliability: generic agency, cosmic purpose, an actual Creator, one Creator, goodness, authority and a particular revelation are different targets. Evidence for one is not silently evidence for all.

Next, record what the subject reports: desire, felt presence, a self-evident relation, remembered instruction, an immediate inference, or a mixed episode. Ordinary testimony about experience can have defeasible standing; it is not necessary to demand an impossible inspection of another person’s entire brain. But a description should not be forced into a non-discursive category merely because no proof was spoken aloud.

Then compare the actual grounds of trust and distrust. Relevant underminers could include demonstrated misunderstanding, known task-induced response patterns, incompatible equally placed presentations, or a failure to distinguish desired from perceived content. None should be presumed from religious disagreement alone. Relevant supports could include clarity of content, stable competent judgement, coherent relation to other warranted knowledge and absence of specifically supported defeating conditions. These are assessable philosophical and experiential grounds, not exclusively numerical ones.

Impairment criteria should not be selected solely because a person dissents. A positive account of the capacity can yield reasonable impairment diagnoses, but “sound means agreeing” cannot supply independent public confirmation. Conversely, lack of an exhaustive impairment taxonomy is not a standing defeater of every ordinary instance.

## Relationship to T17 T19 and daee

T17 had already distinguished first-order cognition from a complete explanatory model, qualified fitrah rather than every conviction, and accepted ordinary entitlement without demanding unique philosophical certification. Its source guide also preserved actual historical Creator-recognition separately from Turner’s conditional modern proper-function construction. The new chapter read therefore refines an existing question; it does not discover that distinction anew. The original paragraph bindings are in empirical-check/inheritance_manifest.json.

T19’s substantive-defeasible standard and its distinction between a flawed public argument and the subject’s possible knowledge remain intact. Its common-creation result never made every created belief a truthful divine assertion. The present common-origin countermodel applies precisely that restriction; it does not withdraw T19’s independently defended source-owned veracity.

Daee’s pinned account supplies explicit proper-function and basic/inferential distinctions. Its fitrah material belongs to §§1–5, not the inherited screen’s §6.3 locator. Shared primary lineage can preserve or clarify warrant without multiplying independent evidence. Its cross-cultural convergence claim still needs attention to genuinely common content, appropriate operation and possible shared processes; lack of collusion alone does not exclude shared error. A disputed operational theory should be evaluated at its inferential level, consistent with daee’s own distinction, rather than shielded by labelling all of it basic.

## Net result

Preserve the affirmative basic-entitlement judgement, explicitly weaker than factive recognition. Record NO_NEW_INDEPENDENT_VERIFICATION for the stronger claim that genuinely knowledgeable basic recognition is humanly possible. Preserve T17’s retained positive recognition appraisal: absence of a new verification here is not evidence against that judgement, and this layer supplies no identified defeater requiring its withdrawal. Withhold universal reliability, a chapter-derived scientific faculty certification, and the identification of any specified subject’s belief as factive knowledge on the evidence recorded here. Correct the empirical overextensions and locator errors without treating them as refutations of theism. The most useful further inquiry is a carefully described recognitional case and its actual defeating or sustaining reasons, or a distinct argument that establishes the truth-directed role of that class of cognition. No new mathematical identification machinery is needed for the result established here.
