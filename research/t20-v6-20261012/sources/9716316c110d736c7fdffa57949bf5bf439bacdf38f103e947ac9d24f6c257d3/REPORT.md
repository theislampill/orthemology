## 2. The exact positive models

Let the target Θ and audit A be binary. A joint row is ordered as

    (P(Θ=1,A=1), P(Θ=0,A=1), P(Θ=1,A=0), P(Θ=0,A=0)).

Define two closed convex sets of admissible joint laws:

    K = conv{ (1,1,1,5)/8, (1,1,5,1)/8 },
    L = conv{ (1,9,9,21)/40, (9,1,21,9)/40 }.

Every cell of every law in either segment is strictly positive. The same two source IDs, retained-support status and audit interpretation can be attached to both sets. Those metadata impose no numerical constraints in this model. K and L are alternative declared uncertainty specifications, not two independently authenticated descriptions of one actual occurrence.

For a row (a,b,c,d), write

    p = P(Θ=1)=a+c,
    m = P(A=1)=a+b,
    q = P(Θ=1|A=1)=a/(a+b),
    r = P(A=1|Θ=1)/P(A=1|Θ=0)
      = [a/p]/[b/(1−p)].

Every denominator here is positive. Bayes gives q=F(p,r), where

    F(p,r)=pr/(1−p+pr).

Parameterize either segment by λ in [0,1], weighting its second vertex by λ.

For K:

    p=(1+2λ)/4,     m=1/4,     q=1/2,
    r=(1−p)/p.

Thus p covers [1/4,3/4], r covers [1/3,3], and the robust posterior set is {1/2}. A weak current target probability pairs with stronger audit evidence; the dependence exactly compensates.

For L:

    p=(1+2λ)/4,     m=1/4,     q=(1+8λ)/10,
    r=(3+22λ−16λ²)/(9+10λ−16λ²).

The denominator is positive on [0,1]. The numerator of r's derivative is

    168−192λ+192λ² = 120+192(λ−1/2)² > 0.

Hence r increases continuously from 1/3 to 3. Here both p and r move in the same direction, and q covers [1/10,9/10]. No sampling, numerical optimization, zero-support pathology or approximation is used.

Therefore no deterministic update using only the two separate scalar intervals, the common event probability and the common metadata can return the correct target set for both specifications. Even knowing the whole unlabelled binary audit law does not fix the missing dependence. This is uncertainty about **which coherent model is admissible**, in addition to within-model dependence. It does not follow from saying that a report is “challenged” which of K, L or another family is justified.

The example does not claim to preserve every possible summary, such as every separate conditional-likelihood-coordinate interval. It isolates the explicitly named summary and shows that its event-mass supplement still does not repair it. Nor is “posterior set” interchangeable with a Bayesian posterior distribution over models: no probability law on model identities has been supplied.

## 3. Exact endpoint-pairing criterion

**Proposition 1.** Let C be a nonempty compact subset of (0,1)×(0,∞). Its coordinates (p,r) are the admissible, jointly compatible current target probability and audit likelihood ratio. Let p−,p+,r−,r+ be its coordinate extrema. Then

    F(p−,r−) ≤ min_C F ≤ max_C F ≤ F(p+,r+).

The first bound is an equality exactly when (p−,r−) belongs to C. The last is an equality exactly when (p+,r+) belongs to C.

**Proof.** F is continuous and strictly increasing in each coordinate on this domain. The outer inequalities follow. Compactness gives an actual minimizer and maximizer. For F at a feasible pair to equal the lower Cartesian-corner value, neither coordinate can exceed its respective minimum, because strict monotonicity would make F larger. Thus the lower corner is feasible. The converse follows by evaluation. The upper case is the same. ∎

Consequently the two Cartesian endpoint formulas are both exact precisely when the respective two aligned corners are genuinely admissible. In K neither is: they splice the prior of one extreme model to the likelihood ratio of the other. In L both are. Both summaries have the same relaxed interval [1/10,9/10], but it is strict overestimation for K and exact for L.

This is a sharp test of this interval computation, not a characterization of arbitrary credal-set equality or the minimal information needed by all future contracts. If only a one-sided decision is required, looser bounds may suffice. Conversely a rigorous conservative bound is not numerically “wrong” when advertised as an outer bound; the mistake is treating every value it includes as an attainable posterior or using the relaxation to erase an already established robust conclusion.

## 4. Constructive finite-polytope audit transport

**Proposition 2 (classical vertex rule, including null vertices).** Let V={v1,…,vn} be finitely many normalized nonnegative joint-state vectors, and K=conv V. Let M be a common supplied nonnegative linear branch map. Its output may keep the target and all continuation-relevant state. Put mi=1ᵀMvi and retain I={i:mi>0}. If I is empty, this branch is impossible under every member of K. Otherwise,

    { Mv/(1ᵀMv) : v∈K, 1ᵀMv>0 }
       = conv{ Mvi/mi : i∈I }.

**Proof.** For v=Σλivi with λi≥0 and Σλi=1, its output normalizer is m=Σλimi. At a positive branch its normalized output is the convex combination of positive vertex outputs with weights βi=λimi/m. A nonnegative vector of zero mass is zero, so null vertices contribute nothing.

Conversely, for any convex weights βi on I, choose λi proportional to βi/mi and assign zero weight to other vertices. Normalizing these λi to sum to one gives a member of K whose conditioned output has exactly weights βi. This proves equality. ∎

For a scalar target query, the sharp lower and upper posterior probabilities are attained at some positive-event vertex. No hypothetical posterior is assigned to a zero-event vertex. Convexity is an explicit premise about the admissible uncertainty set; the theorem does not authorize silently enlarging a nonconvex empirical model family or adopting a new model without grounds.

## 5. Finite vertices matter at null audits

The parent supplied the following scope control. The source-audit predecessor already owns noncanonical null-event limiting posteriors; this is a bounded illustration of the extra closedness premise in Proposition 2, not a new null-conditioning principle.

Use parameters v,u satisfying

    0≤v≤1/2,        v²≤u≤v/2,

and the joint row

    (u, v−u, (1−v)/2, (1−v)/2).

The parameter set is closed and bounded. It is convex because u≥v² is the epigraph condition for a convex function and the remaining restrictions are linear. Its affine joint-law image K∞ is therefore compact and convex. At every positive audit mass v>0 all four cells are strictly positive: u≥v²>0 and v−u≥v/2>0, while each A=0 cell is at least 1/4.

Its positive-audit posterior is q=u/v, so 0<q≤1/2. Conversely every q in (0,1/2] is attained by v=q, u=q². Thus the complete conditioned image is exactly **(0,1/2]**, not [0,1/2]. The only null-audit parameter is v=u=0, which gives row (0,0,1/2,1/2) and no audit posterior. Its current target prior is 1/2; this does not select a value for conditioning on its null event.

The infimum zero is unattained despite all positive-audit models having full support. Treating the lower bound as an admissible posterior, or taking the closed convex hull without disclosure, enlarges the conditioned model set. This is not a finite-polytope counterexample: the curved model boundary is not finitely generated, and Proposition 2 has not incorrectly closed its image. It also distinguishes a valid numerical infimum from a claim that some surviving model attains it.

No null-event model receives an arbitrarily selected repair. The actual occurrence of a purported impossible audit would instead require the source-audit predecessor's separately warranted model/evidence reconsideration.

## 6. Philosophical scope and use

The corroborative-source owner already establishes that qualitatively different indications can jointly discriminate despite individual insufficiency. It also already insists that rival explanations, content, source relations and the actual acquisition matter. The present models neither refute that positive position nor add an authenticated instance of it.

The result distinguishes two legitimate uses of a challenged-source description:

1. Under one warranted joint model, preserve the report–audit relations required by the already-owned exact update theorem.
2. When several coherent models remain admissible, preserve which present evidential appraisal goes with which audit response. Separate “reliability ranges” and “confidence ranges” generally do not encode that relation.

This is especially relevant when one premise simultaneously supports several partial grounds and their audit interpretation. One cannot select the most pessimistic present appraisal from one model and the most pessimistic audit multiplier from another and call the result a jointly possible response without checking compatibility. Equally, selecting favorable endpoints from different models is unearned. A conservative relaxation can be useful if honestly described and fit for the decision at hand.

Provenance data can be essential for assigning the correct model or support route. They do not replace its substantive content. A causal comparison may justify a joint likelihood restriction, but a numerical joint law by itself does not identify causes; two observationally equivalent causal models may differ under interventions. This packet supplies neither a causal-identification theorem nor a probability for Necessary Being, Knowledge, Will, Wisdom or any retained theological conclusion.

No ordinary knower is required to articulate a credal polytope before knowing. These are representation conditions for the declared analytic calculation, not universal metacognitive duties. Inferential and noninferential grounds retain their source-owned distinctions. A later reconstruction's calculational success does not establish actual historical basing, proper function or sufficient first-order warrant. Nor does a hypothetical bad uncertainty summary independently defeat an earned affirmative appraisal.
