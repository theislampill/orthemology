# Bounded access and diachronic coherence

Twelfth Orthemology research • 4 October 2026 UTC

Research assessment of the v3 fresh-history article and its accepted ordinary-mathematics companions. No implementation, empirical finding, canonical change, new source-existence conclusion or general mathematical priority claim.

## Defended conclusion

There is an affirmative operational reason for positive bounded capture that is weaker than choosing countable additivity everywhere: a prospectively warranted chance of successful magnitude-sensitive observation, together with a tight resource budget, forces positive mass in one bounded interval. A deterministic finite budget is the simplest case. This rationale concerns a real task and the resources needed to perform it; it is not an arbitrary convention about all measurable unions.

The admitted correction does not yet supply that contract for the Gaussian state. Its actual target-sensitive evidential relation is substantial, but neither the target variable, its bounded-range capture relation, nor a prospective operating law has been identified with the stochastic state in the proposed reductio. Finite output, pointwise finite acquisition and epistemic safety do not by themselves fill those gaps.

The strongest finitely additive response nevertheless has a substantive cost. The reviewed history law supports an explicit countable partition of positive-probability reports on which a fair fresh coin's conditional chance is zero in every cell. Under a sufficiently strong access and trading contract, two bounded transactions then lose a fixed amount in every outcome. No infinite portfolio is required. The remaining operational idealization is acquisition and preservation of the report-dependent opportunity through an arbitrarily long but pointwise finite search. Bounded truncation leaves a no-report branch and removes this particular sure-loss conclusion.

Thus the result is neither an unconditional vindication of the diffuse law nor its metaphysical refutation. It identifies two independently intelligible rational grounds for rejecting that law in a task model, and the exact additional commitments each needs. The actual programme owns the local correction and its maintained evidential relation; the stronger same-law access contract remains to be warranted.

The target is the admitted reason-responsive correction concerning the displayed Minhāj header. It is not an arbitrary correct output, a whole-edition authentication, or a new empirical consciousness result. Sixth's local-correction and world-bridge bodies already distinguish truth, the subject's warranted relation to evidence, and productive supply. Their positive requirement is that the target-to-evidence route be appropriately maintained when explaining the correction as knowledge. It is not merely production of three otherwise unconnected objects: target, evidence and subject.

Its basic-faculty point also remains: warrant does not require a faculty-free certification of every cognitive starting point. ## 2. Five propositions that must remain distinct

Let P denote one probability state, already restricted to whatever admissible information the argument actually uses. All probabilities in an application below are under that same P. The interpretation must also stay fixed. The betting and forecasting tests apply when this law supplies the agent's warranted operative previsions. If it is offered only as an objective global history law, an independently warranted connection to the agent's credences in the relevant information state is needed. Positive subjective expectation does not by itself contradict a distinct objective chance assignment.

1. **Actual finite value.** The realised X₀ is a finite real number. This is true at every sample of the reviewed control.
2. **Prospective bounded capture.** Before a capture outcome is known, P(C)>0 for an event C whose valid occurrence guarantees X₀∈I for one specified bounded interval I.
3. **Posterior information.** After a report, the subject is warranted that X₀ lies in a reported bounded interval. This concerns the posterior epistemic state, not automatically the prior chance of receiving the report.
4. **Task fitness.** The model is suitable for a specified observation or decision task, with the required chance of success, resource limits, informational timing and action opportunities.
5. **Metaphysical impossibility.** No concrete reality can instantiate the alleged support architecture. An objection to a task model does not by itself establish this.

Finite additivity permits (1) while denying every instance of (2). A successful actual report does not turn its prior probability from zero into a positive number. Conversely, positive capture need not be deduced from observed frequencies: a properly directed basic recognition or an independently warranted operating account could justify it. What needs assessment is the content and application of that warrant, not a demand that every rational commitment be derived from a more elementary theorem.

## 3. The affirmative operational bridge

### 3.1 A fixed finite task

Suppose a warranted prospective task model says that a valid reading C has chance at least s>0. Suppose the instrument's finite operating range and its soundness imply C⊆{X₀∈I}, where I is bounded. Then monotonicity gives

P(X₀∈I) ≥ P(C) ≥ s > 0.

Only ordinary finite-additive monotonicity is used. This provides exactly the positive premise needed by the concentration obstruction. It neither assumes tightness of the whole position law nor countable additivity of the joint history.

There is a genuine epistemic rationale. A subject entitled to rely prospectively on this bounded acquisition route as having a real chance of success cannot simultaneously treat every successful in-range acquisition as having zero chance under that very task model. Calling the latter law finitely additive does not answer the task-relative inconsistency. The warranted reliability judgment may be defeasible and need not await exclusion of every describable skeptical model.

The requirement of a real chance must, however, be part of what is warranted. A mere instruction to try, a desire for success, the absence of proof of failure, or the fact that a trial actually succeeded does not supply it. Nor does conditional soundness—if a reading is delivered, it is correct—by itself supply a chance of delivery.

### 3.2 Tight resources suffice without a preset budget

A useful extension allows random or otherwise variable resource use. Let C be successful acquisition and T a nonnegative resource variable. Suppose, for every finite m, there is a finite bound b(m) such that

C∩{T≤m} ⊆ {|X₀|≤b(m)}.

This is a magnitude-sensitive access condition: completing the relevant acquisition within budget m certifies a bounded region of the state, not merely a finite output label.

Finite subadditivity gives, for every m,

P(C) ≤ P(|X₀|≤b(m)) + P(T>m).                    (1)

Consequently, if P(C)≥s>0 and the resource marginal is tight, choose m with P(T>m)<s. Equation (1) gives

P(|X₀|≤b(m)) ≥ s−P(T>m)>0.                     (2)

The weakest useful resource condition is only one such tail bound; full tightness is a convenient sufficient warrant. No independence between T and X₀ is needed. No summation over infinitely many budgets is performed. No universal countable-additivity axiom is introduced.

Conversely, under the reviewed history, every bounded-position event is null. Equation (1) says that any sound magnitude-sensitive acquisition with a tight resource marginal has P(C)=0. Randomizing a finite budget with a countably additive finite-valued law cannot rescue a positive success guarantee. The obstruction also applies to dependent budgets whenever their marginal tail is sufficiently controlled.

This strengthens the operational content of the mathematical result. The law is unsuitable for a positively successful, tight-resource magnitude-capture contract, not merely awkward because a theorist prefers ordinary probability.

### 3.3 Why this does not settle the admitted case

The inherited maintained-access requirement preserves the actual apt target/evidence/subject relation. It does not say that access to every stochastic state can be acquired within a tight prospective budget. Nor does it identify the bibliographic correction's resource requirements with |X₀| in the Gaussian process.

A knowledge account can require that delivered evidence safely tracks its target while remaining silent on how often an observer reaches that situation. Conditional safety and positive opportunity are different. Adding the latter may be entirely reasonable for a particular defended task; it is not contained in the former merely because both are desirable.

Most importantly, a finite semantic answer need not be a bounded magnitude observation. Under the reviewed position law, a sign query has two possible outputs and the positive half-line has probability 1/2. Its two inverse images are unbounded. A perfectly faithful finite query can therefore have positive-probability answers while every bounded position interval is null. This mathematical control does not assert a physically implemented sign sensor or a metaphysically possible conscious observer. It blocks the proposed entailment from finite output alone.

Replacing X₀ by a bounded encoding of the corrected sentence does not repair that entailment: the bounded encoding no longer inherits additive Gaussian increments merely by being an encoding. A new state/dynamics/observation binding would have to be justified.

## 4. What the finitely additive defender must actually say

The best response is not that probability zero means impossible, nor that the observer can never learn anything. It is that the law's finite-bet coherence deliberately does not convert pointwise eventual success into a positive chance of success within any fixed budget.

The expanding search for X₀ terminates at a finite stage on every sample. Its success-by-m event is nevertheless null for every m. The law thus treats all finite deadlines as missed with probability one, while treating eventual termination as certain. This is the precise cost of refusing continuity for that increasing sequence.

The defender can retain locally reliable bounded-output queries, ordinary countably additive noise, and successful actual episodes. The defender cannot simultaneously advertise the diffuse position law as meeting the bounded-capture or tight-resource guarantee in §3. Doing so would contradict its own stated probabilities.

That response is mathematically coherent, but coherence at one static pricing level is not yet a complete epistemology or theory of maintained operational access. Its consequences for genuinely executable diachronic commitments deserve a direct test.

## 5. A positive-probability report partition inside the reviewed law

This section uses the accepted law μ itself; it does not substitute an unrelated probability example. All events are Borel in its stated sample space.

Let

N = 1+floor(|X₀|).

N is a positive integer on every sample. Since {N=n} is contained in a bounded position interval, μ(N=n)=0 for every n.

Choose A={ξ₀≥0}. Its probability is 1/2. From the independent Gaussian coordinate ξ₁, choose a Borel positive-integer variable K with ordinary geometric probabilities q_n=2^(−n), n≥1. For example, use the Gaussian distribution function to turn ξ₁ into a uniform variable in (0,1), then its dyadic intervals. K is finite at every real coordinate, and is independent of A in the countably additive noise marginal.

Define a report value

R=N on A;     R=K on Aᶜ.

The report events H_n={R=n} form a countable measurable partition. They have

μ(H_n)=μ(A∩{N=n})+μ(Aᶜ∩{K=n})=0+q_n/2>0,

and

μ(A∩H_n)=μ(A∩{N=n})=0.

Therefore every conditional probability below is an ordinary positive-denominator ratio:

μ(A|H_n)=0 for every n, while μ(A)=1/2.           (3)

No choice of a conditional distribution on a null report is involved. No law of total probability over this countable partition has been assumed. In fact Σ_n μ(H_n)=1/2 although the partition exhausts the space; finite additivity permits exactly this failure.

The accepted construction supplies the required independent Gaussian coordinates. No independence between N and A is needed for the zero numerator, since {N=n} is itself null. This observation makes the exact mechanism stronger than one depending on a hidden new product convention.

Equation (3) is a concrete nonconglomerability witness for the history law. General nonconglomerability mechanisms are established probability theory, not a discovery of this research. Its role here is to expose what the particular history commits an observer to under a particular information protocol.

## 6. Two transactions, and the access premise they require

Suppose the following operational contract is admitted:

- Before A is known, the agent uses μ to value a unit ticket paying 1 if A and 0 otherwise.
- A reporting service eventually discloses R, with no extra information about A, and the agent then uses the ratios in (3).
- The ticket remains transferable at that stage and A can later be disclosed for settlement. Stakes have additive, state-independent value at the small scale used; acquisition costs and delay do not alter the stipulated net comparison.
- The agent accepts the two offers because each is strictly favorable by its contemporaneous valuation. Alternatively, it precommits to that local trading policy.

Take 0<ε<1/4. At the first stage the agent buys the A-ticket for 1/2−ε. Its ex ante expected gain is ε. After any report H_n, the agent sells the same ticket for ε. Its conditional expected gain on this sale is ε, since (3) gives value zero. The first payoff is 1_A−(1/2−ε). The second is ε−1_A. They cancel the ticket payoff in every sample, leaving

−(1/2−ε)+ε = −1/2+2ε < 0.                      (4)

The second payoff has negative prior expectation ε−1/2, despite positive expectation ε at every exact report. This is why the example is not a finite portfolio of bets all judged favorable at the initial stage. The loss is uniformly bounded away from zero. There are two transactions per actual history, one unit of exposure, and finitely described rules. No infinite list of simultaneously purchased physical tickets, infinitesimal currency or infinite realized payoff is required.

This is genuine normative pressure against combining the law with that operational policy. An agent who knows the two-stage plan can recognize that abstaining dominates the plan in every outcome. Calling each local quote coherent does not remove the sure loss of the whole policy.

It is not yet a no-assumptions argument against the law. The policy's second opportunity depends on acquisition of a countably valued report. A magnitude-sensitive implementation of R on A may use an arbitrarily long search. Pointwise finite duration is enough for the abstract cancellation in (4), but a real maintained trading opportunity, memory, counterpart and settlement channel over that entire possible duration is an additional commitment. The original correction does not admit those resources merely by being reason-responsive. Measurability of R is not an acquisition algorithm. In particular, exact floor identification from an arbitrary approximate real name has a boundary issue; an exact reporting oracle or a separately justified bound-reporting protocol must not be inferred from the formula. Extra information revealing the generating branch, A itself, or further state information could also change the operative posterior. Informative timing therefore belongs in the information state; the proposed stage-R schedule below makes timing already determined by the exact report.

The finitely additive defender has more than one coherent response: decline that unbounded-duration operational contract; decline the automatic identification of every positive local expected gain with an action that must be taken; or use a dynamically sophisticated commitment policy that rejects the dominated composite. These responses concede a limit on treating μ as an unrestricted guide to such sequential reliance. They do not concede a contradiction in μ's static event probabilities.

### A precise bounded truncation

Take the report schedule to deliver R at stage R. At a fixed deadline m, the information partition consists of H₁,…,H_m and D_m={R>m}. Put Q_m=Σ_(n≤m)q_n=1−2^(−m). Then

μ(R≤m)=Q_m/2,
μ(D_m)=1−Q_m/2,
μ(A∩D_m)=1/2,
μ(A|D_m)=1/(2−Q_m)>1/2.

Thus the no-report branch has positive probability and a higher, not zero, posterior for A. Ordinary finite-partition averaging works exactly. If the second transaction is made only when a report arrives by m, the whole policy no longer loses uniformly: on the no-report branch with A true, retaining the ticket is profitable. If it is also traded on that branch, its appropriate price is not ε. The fixed-budget objection therefore cannot silently discard D_m.

More generally, finite additivity forces the prior to be an ordinary weighted average across every finite information partition after zero-mass cells are discarded. A finite coarsening cannot make all its positive-mass posteriors zero while retaining μ(A)=1/2. Also, the event that some report eventually arrives is the whole space, so μ(A|eventual arrival)=1/2. One must not infer zero from the zero posteriors at every exact report.

The issue is not the number of bets. It is whether a pointwise finite, non-tight information schedule is an admissible completed plan with the required diachronic opportunities. This is the precise place where an operationally motivated conglomerability principle could have force beyond a probability convention.

### The epistemic forecast-policy test

The same report structure gives a diagnostic without requiring the agent to trade. Use the bounded quadratic forecast loss L(p,A)=(p−1_A)². Keeping the prior forecast p=1/2 gives loss 1/4 in every sample. The policy of adopting the exact-report conditional probability gives forecast zero after every report, hence loss 1_A. Its prior expected loss is therefore 1/2. In contrast, within every H_n the conditional expected loss is zero for the updated forecast and 1/4 for retaining the prior.

Thus a policy that improves the conditional forecast at every exact report is worse under the prior's own prospective accuracy assessment. The calculations use only bounded random variables and finite arithmetic. They expose the failure of the usual averaging argument behind the value of information for this particular acquisition/update policy.

This does not show that information itself reduces the agent's options or that every rational epistemology must maximize this score. The option to ignore the report remains available; comparing a complete policy prospectively can favor retaining the prior. Nor is the admitted reason-responsive correction being redefined as a numerical score. The example tests an explicit truth-directed probabilistic forecasting contract and shows that its prior, conditional updates and prospective accuracy criterion cannot have all their usual relations under μ.

That is a positive epistemic rationale for requiring coherence across the genuinely available report protocol, beyond dislike of an unfamiliar measure. Its actual application still needs the report's warranted availability and a defended norm relating prospective and acquired evidence. Neither follows from one actual local correction. Under any fixed finite coarsening, conditional forecasting and prior averaging again agree. For the deadline partition above, the conditional forecasts are zero on H₁,…,H_m and 1/(2−Q_m) on D_m; their prior expected quadratic loss is (1−Q_m)/[2(2−Q_m)]≤1/4. The residual cell is doing substantive work.

These finite-deadline forecasts converge pointwise to zero, yet their prior expected losses tend to zero while the limiting forecast's prior expected loss is 1/2. The discrepancy is a failure of bounded convergence for this finitely additive law. Passing from every finite acquisition stage to the completed report policy is precisely the inference that needs a further continuity or operational warrant.

## 7. Why weaker betting and self-location arguments do less

A static finite set of bounded bets cannot give a uniform sure loss when its fair prices are supplied by a positive normalized finitely additive expectation. Positivity and finite linearity exclude that result directly. A contrary argument has changed the action class, the dominance condition or the information protocol.

There is a simple stronger test using g=1/(N+1). This payoff is positive at every sample, but its finitely additive expectation is zero: for every m, g≤1_{N≤m}+1/(m+1), and the first term has expectation zero. Selling g for zero loses something at every sample, though there is no fixed positive lower bound on that loss. Rejecting such a trade is an intelligible strict-dominance norm. It is stronger than avoiding a uniform sure loss and may require arbitrarily fine positive payoffs or precision if interpreted physically. It cannot be presented as the same original finite-bet premise.

Likewise, every bounded unit cell has μ-mass zero, whereas an ordinary full-support real law gives each such cell positive mass. A comparative principle can object that one forecast is worse at every member of that exhaustive reporting partition. Its attraction is a substantive rational judgment about exhaustive alternatives. Its application is not supplied by checking finitely many cells: every finite check leaves an unbounded residual where the forecasts compare differently.

The self-location claim “my value is somewhere finite” is already true with μ-probability one. It does not locate the observer in a positive-mass bounded cell. After the cell is learned, a posterior can record it; learning is not a retroactive change in prior mass. A reflection or conglomerability requirement linking all prospective reports to present judgment is an additional diachronic norm, with the explicit two-stage consequence above providing a better reason to assess it than a bare appeal to familiarity with countable additivity.

## 8. Updating after an actual observation

If an actual capture event C is μ-null, ordinary ratio conditioning on C is unavailable within μ alone. This does not prove that no updating is possible: a full conditional model or a model revision might specify an update at a null event. The accepted history construction does not already provide such a conditional model, and its coherence and interpretation would need separate assessment.

A tight posterior for X₀ cannot be combined with the unconditional historical freshness law. Conditioning on a terminal observation can induce dependence between the earlier state and its future noise block. This matters even for ordinary countably additive path laws. Every application of the concentration obstruction must keep its innovation marginal, independence, terminal event and information state together.

There is, nevertheless, a precise comparative likelihood result. Suppose a competing task model Q independently gives C positive probability, and a two-model mixture assigns both models positive prior weight. C then has positive mixture probability. After observing C, ratio conditioning assigns the μ-model posterior weight zero, because its likelihood for C is zero. This is a mathematically valid calculation under the specified mixture and update rule. It does not infer positive μ(C) from the realised C, or assert that a zero-weight model is metaphysically impossible.

It is not an independent justification of a dynamically reliable model-selection policy. If Q gives positive probability to every cell of an exhaustive countable bounded-report partition, that same calculation eliminates the diffuse component at every exact report despite its positive prior mixture weight. The nonconglomerability problem has then reappeared at the model-label level. Warrant for adopting the update rule and for the actual report-acquisition protocol remains substantive; the mixture argument cannot bypass the operational challenge above.

This route also needs the actual observed event to be the bounded capture that the models purport to predict. A finite bibliographic answer is not automatically that event. The comparison supplies no original-source conclusion without the separate productive and modal bridge.

## 9. Assessment of affirmative and opposing warrant

The strongest affirmative should make a bounded claim first. Where the agent really has warrant to rely on magnitude-sensitive acquisition with positive success and a controlled resource tail, the diffuse law is excluded as the operating model by (1)–(2). This is a substantive gain even if it identifies neither an original supplier nor universal countable additivity. Basic recognition can warrant the reliability or access premise; an infinite regress of empirical certifications is not required. A described diffuse alternative does not by itself defeat such a properly targeted recognition.

The strongest opposing reply is equally specific. The presently admitted local correction supplies neither that magnitude-capture task nor the stronger unbounded-duration report/trading contract. Rejecting their unearned import does not reduce the correction to coincidence, deny objective norms or remove original possession's inherited explanatory force. It preserves the distinction between actual apt cognition and a quantified prospective performance claim about another variable.

The report construction makes the residual disagreement concrete. If a defender asserts unrestricted reason-responsive updating through every pointwise finite report protocol, treats the displayed conditional probabilities as action-guiding at each acquired report, and maintains the two-stage opportunities, then avoiding a dominated whole plan requires giving up part of that package. It is inadequate simply to repeat that finite additivity is coherent. If those operational assumptions are denied, the objection has identified a restriction of the defended access theory rather than refuted its metaphysical possibility.

For the actual programme the result is therefore an application-bounded affirmative, not a new actual-source theorem. A positively warranted same-law finite-access premise could close the observation side without adopting countable additivity by convention. The inherited admitted commitments, at their inspected scope, have not yet supplied that premise for the proposed Gaussian replacement of the support history.

## 10. Exact remaining burden

The most valuable next step is to defend one concrete connection between the admitted productive/evidential architecture and one of the following commitments:

- a prospectively justified positive chance of a bounded, magnitude-sensitive capture, with all fresh-block claims valid in the same probability state; or
- a maintained report-acquisition and diachronic-action contract strong enough for the two-transaction test, with a reason why refusing that contract would fail the actual epistemic explanandum.

This is one application problem with two precise candidate routes, not a demand for a general theory of all probability. Merely granting finite cognition, an eventual search, or “rational agency” does not answer it. Conversely, a defended basic recognition whose content genuinely includes one of these routes should receive its full force; it need not be dismissed because the commitment is strong.

Even success here would leave the separate original-support realisability question: why must an entirely received actual architecture admit this prohibited joint process and access contract, and why does original provision change a relevant premise? An operational objection to μ cannot silently supply that metaphysical passage.
