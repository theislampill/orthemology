# Minhaj al-Sunna, selected electronic volume 3: reflective synthesis

Dated study-stage record, 7 October 2026. Its provisional and historical statements are preserved; this is not a later after-all-books verdict.

## Result and boundary

The whole selected Arabic body was read continuously from V03L0001 through V03L0424: 424 stable local lines and 67,886 whitespace words. Twenty-seven first-pass receipts give exact ordered coverage with no missing or duplicate first-pass lines. Three oversized display attempts were truncated and received no coverage credit; smaller complete replacement displays were read before recording those intervals. Four subsequent bounded reflective rereads cover L236–246, L266–276, L194–202 and L410–418, totaling 40 lines and 7,271 words.

The body belongs to the explicit third-volume heading in Islamicbook section 005 and ends at the third-volume colophon in section 006. Website section numbers are not volume numbers. The stable line IDs are extraction locators, not printed page numbers. Sources:

Mechanical verification checks interval accounting and witness identity, not independent proof of comprehension.

The philosophical conclusion remains bounded: the volume supplies substantial positive arguments and sharper semantic distinctions. It does not, merely by being fully read, close the separate question of full-source self-sufficiency. Necessary being remains provisionally accepted. Neither acceptance nor rejection of a wider theorem was a required result.

## Whole-volume shape

Most of this volume continues the first of the Imami opponent's four grounds from the larger work. It answers objections about predestination, justice, human power, wisdom, prophetic attestation, gratitude, cooperation, vision, speech, infallibility, authority, analogy and legal disagreements. At L376 the second ground begins, appealing to the saved-sect report and distinctive identity. At L405 the third begins, appealing to confidence of salvation. The selected volume ends during the reply to that third ground, with its explicit colophon.

This broader arc matters. The cooperation discussion is part of an account of real but dependent agency, intrinsic divine action, purposive wisdom and epistemic warrant. It is not a free-standing slogan that all coordination is impossible. The already inherited cooperation excerpt, encountered here at L258–265, was not newly discovered.

## Strong affirmative resources

### 1. Real efficacy, complete determination and discriminating power

L6, L19, L97–102 and L211–214 affirm actual creaturely action and causal efficacy. Natural powers are not empty labels and human capacity is not mere temporal concurrence. L100 argues that concurrence alone cannot distinguish power from other concomitants such as life or knowledge. At the same time, no created cause is independently sufficient: cooperation, conditions and absence of obstacles matter (L6, L101).

The complete-determinant argument is developed, not merely asserted, at L19, L101–105 and L236–242. An actual contingent event cannot be sufficiently explained by a condition still indifferent between occurrence and nonoccurrence. For voluntary action, complete power and decisive will supply the determining package. The author accepts necessity of the effect given that complete package. He does not treat necessitation as such as the enemy of voluntary agency.

The legal capacity discussion supplies a separate distinction. Capacity sufficient for obligation can exist before an unperformed act and does not require already willing it. Complete action-entailing conditions include decisive will. L49 even says that ordinary language can reserve 'capacity' for the enabling power, leaving will outside the word. These are explanatory/linguistic roles, not two independently subsisting entities.

### 2. Intrinsic exercise is not an externally manufactured bearer

L99, L125–127 and L213–214 distinguish a human act intrinsic to its human agent from that act's being an effect created by God. The human's power is the human's power, not God's own power. The creator does not bear every quality created in another.

L103–110 and L236–246 then apply the demand for actualization to divine action while rejecting an eternally fixed sufficient cause of a coeternal individual world. Endless succession of effects is distinguished from a vicious regress in agents or completion of productive efficacy. Continuing divine activity does not imply that a particular sphere or utterance is beginningless.

The key contrast is explicit at L239: God's power operates with will, but God's will cannot come from another as human will does. At L246, intrinsic acts of the self-necessary are expressly excluded from issuing from another. The passage defends renewed intrinsic exercise rather than reducing activity to an eternally accompanying attribute. L300–305 makes the related point for unlimited voluntary speech and denies that any particular voice must be eternal.

This is positive evidence for intrinsic sourcehood, not a license to identify every intrinsic exercise with efficient creation of the bearer or of a detached attribute. Conversely, labeling an exercise 'intrinsic' is not by itself a complete modern explanation of it. Original productive accounting, an efficient-creation relation, and the existence of the whole bearer must remain distinct.

### 3. The cooperation argument reaches the source of concord

The inherited L258–265 discussion distinguishes partial creaturely contributors from two complete independent sole producers of one numerically identical effect. Creaturely cooperation is possible because the partners already have powers supplied outside the pair. It does not follow that any genuine partial contribution is an illicit second complete creator.

The continuation at L266–276 is especially useful. The author expressly considers fixed agreement, fixed disagreement and possible agreement/disagreement. His central objection is not simply that two agents might quarrel. L272–273 identifies a productive cycle where each agent's willing/action is part of what makes the other willing/acting. A cycle in part of the determinant remains a cycle; merely concurrent or correlative relations are not automatically the same case. Fatherhood and sonship are admitted as correlative because they have another originating explanation.

L272 also blocks two proposed shortcuts. A beneficial future outcome cannot function as a ready-made independent coordinator if that outcome is itself produced by the wills needing explanation. Foreknowledge of a future event follows the known event in the relevant explanatory sense, while the event follows its originator's will. This is not a claim that divine knowledge is temporally acquired from a creature; it denies that foreknowledge alone manufactures its own object or the concord productive of it.

Application still needs a warranted semantic bridge: when does a proposed intrinsic necessary concord involve the productive conditioning described here, rather than merely sharing a modal description? A bare concord assertion is not a countermodel. It would need a source-complete realization that accounts for exercise and contribution without hiding the dependence. Likewise the text's examples of cooks, builders and partners do not refute universally quantified interfaces by being a finite list of examples. The inherited formal context permits arbitrary or potentially infinite occurrence and contribution types; only controls are finite.

## Distinctions that prevent overclaiming

- L252 grants necessary existence as true, then criticizes an impoverished characterization of its referent. It is not a denial of the necessary-being starting point.
- L254–257 rejects turning shared predicates such as necessity or existence into one numerically shared extra-mental component. A necessary attribute is not thereby a separately subsisting necessary source.
- L149–150 distinguishes a real subject-predicated feature from an invariant essential feature. Context-sensitive goodness/harm does not imply that all real features disappear.
- L223–226 distinguishes alternative possibility from simultaneous contradiction, and contingent dependence from necessity given a complete determinant. Present existence being incompatible with present nonexistence does not by itself prove a unique complete source.
- L25, L149 and L160 explicitly limit human knowledge of the detailed wisdom of every occurrence. Illustrations of compatible general benefit and particular harm are not exhaustive demonstrations of why every evil is required.
- L267–276 uses the interconnection of the actual world against separated independent domains. That is a substantive premise to be retained and examined, not an unspoken consequence of the word 'world'.

## Knowledge, truthfulness and attestation remain separate stages

L69–80 and L188–202 answer the opponent's claim that divine creation of lies and denial of purposes undermine prophecy. The author surveys alternative school arguments but explicitly refuses to vindicate every Sunni-labelled answer. L78 calls the purposelessness view weaker. L79–80 criticizes inadequately grounded exclusion of divine defects and a circular or redundant appeal to consensus.

The stronger affirmative route is perfection and intrinsic truthfulness (L79, L195, L198–200), not sheer effective power. Producing a lie in another is not lying oneself. The reported nonvolitional-school route at L197 links truth to necessary knowledge; the author's preferred volitional route invokes intrinsic wise speech. Those are distinct arguments and must retain their premises.

L202 explicitly allows extraordinary events on the side of false claimants where their circumstances prevent the events from constituting genuine truth-signs. The claim that genuine evidence entails truth is logically powerful but does not by itself identify which observed event is genuine evidence. Otherwise the attestation problem could be concealed in the definition of 'sign'. The author twice insists that knowledge of prophetic truth has multiple routes beyond wonders and refers fuller treatment elsewhere.

L410 adds another essential separation: an infallible authority's existence and reliable access to what that authority said are different warrants. Fallible books and intermediaries do not automatically inherit the authority's infallibility. L414–415 rejects confidence alone as a reason to follow a guide and explicitly allows investigating a third route. L416 separates a secure conditional judgment from uncertain knowledge that a particular person satisfies its antecedent.

The following L417–418 material must also be kept: the author discusses convergence of testimony, public praise and dreams as warrants. This study has not independently authenticated those reports or vindicated all those epistemic claims. Reading only the cautionary sentences would misrepresent the breadth of his position.

## Orthogonal findings preserved

The log retains the sections on legal ability under illness and hardship, repentance versus calamity, coercion and moral accountability, Quranic grammar and ownership of predicates, vision and perception, speech to future addressees, corrected prophetic judgment, fallible political deputies, limited obedience, greater-harm reasoning, distributed scholarly correction, analogy and operative similarity, paternity versus biological generation, restitution and transformed property, report reliability, heresiography and salvation confidence.

Two particularly constructive methodological passages are L228–229/L281–282, where the author rejects an easy proof-text reading favorable to his own conclusion before offering a different causal inference, and L353–355, where he denies that the four schools exhaust truth and distinguishes sound analogy from superficial similarity or defeated analogy.

The volume also contains extensive hostile sectarian generalizations and allegations about identifiable historical figures and populations. These remain claims of a medieval polemical author or of his quoted opponent. Reading them does not authenticate them or adopt their collective judgments. Criminal-law punishments and political injunctions are reported historical doctrines, not present recommendations.

## Witness limitations

The log records suspected local errors without emendation, including ambiguous/reversed-looking clauses at L9–10, compressed school transitions, misassigned Quran-surah labels, broken wording at L243/L246–247, and apparent inversion at L333. The raw saved HTML itself contains the repeated phrase at L166, the duplicated final sentence across L305–306, and the troublesome greater/lesser-harm wording at L333. These features are not extraction inventions. The key L246 intrinsic-act sentence and L272 foreknowledge sentence also occur verbatim in the raw HTML.

No proposed repair has been substituted into the body. Local uncertainty does not justify either treating every clear argument as unusable or certifying the witness as a clean critical text. The available electronic body supports the bounded readings above; precise contentious wording, apparatus, chains and historical claims remain candidates for later authorized collation.

## Present appraisal

The full volume strengthens the case that the author's source account combines real efficacy, intrinsic volitional perfection and complete productive determination, and that his cooperation critique reaches the origin of agreement rather than merely its stability. It also makes sharper why necessary attributes, knowing an outcome, and external co-production cannot be casually substituted for one another.

The unresolved philosophical bridge is whether the whole source's intrinsic exercise is sufficiently accounted for, and whether a proposed rival source structure satisfies or evades the productive-dependence conditions actually argued. Neither temporal constancy nor a necessary-being label settles that bridge. The present result is a richer and more discriminating source basis, with affirmative arguments, objections and limits all preserved.
