This produces one admissible static scenario in which no message works. The argument counts action options, not possible worlds, so full fault-pattern identification is correctly not required.

The private-randomness lower bound is valid after the explicit unused-message correction. A message used with positive probability in any scenario must, by receiver-private-seed independence and protocol liveness there, yield an almost-sure first action. Its first-action law therefore has a positive-mass root. Choose one such root per retained message and include them in the corruption set, paying only for agreeing roots. Every message now has positive failure probability. A finite mixture of those positive values cannot be zero. Unused messages can be discarded and cannot enlarge the effective alphabet.

The shared-randomness argument also uses the right order of quantifiers. For each of the finitely many compatible C, probability-one safety and liveness imply that almost every shared seed is conditionally good for that C. Under a common seed law independent of C at the fixed view, their finite intersection has probability one. A seed in that intersection gives a finite-message protocol good for all C, contradicting the private-randomness argument. This is not an adversary allowed to observe the realized seed before choosing C.

Two guards requested in review are present: the seeds are generated after the view is fixed or have a C-independent law conditional on it, and private seeds are independent apart from declared shared randomness. Conditioning on a seed-dependent report cannot secretly create an integrity side channel. The proof concerns a finite corruption family; its intersection argument is not being generalized to an arbitrary uncountable family.
