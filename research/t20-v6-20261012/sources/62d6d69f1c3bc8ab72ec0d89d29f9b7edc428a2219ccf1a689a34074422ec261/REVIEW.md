## Proof obligations independently checked

1. **Static finite GNS representation.** Square positivity gives Cauchy–Schwarz. A null polynomial p satisfies L(pr)=0 for every r; taking r=ps² proves the nullspace is an ideal. Coordinate multiplication therefore descends to commuting symmetric operators on the finite-dimensional quotient. Simultaneous diagonalization and cyclicity of [1] give exactly rank(H) distinct joint atoms with strictly positive weights. The usual product of squared distances to the atoms forces every other positive representative onto the same set, and interpolation fixes its masses. This is classical, not a new representation theorem. I independently opened Schmüdgen's *Ten Lectures on the Moment Problem*, Theorem 9.5 and its GNS proof idea, printed p.69, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.12698 .

2. **C1 charts are actually derived.** On a constant maximal-rank neighborhood choose a linear projection separating the current atoms. Its r-by-r Hankel matrix is invertible. The monic annihilating polynomial is recovered by a C1 linear solve; its simple real roots label projected atoms locally by C1 functions. A Vandermonde solve recovers weights, followed by weighted coordinate moments recovering each full atom. No unproved differentiability of a selected measure enters.

3. **Hermite tests are fixed polynomials.** At a time t*, freeze b_j, all root values, and ξ_j in p=(x_i−ξ_ji)b_j². Its values vanish at every current atom, and its gradient is e_i at atom j and zero at the others. Consequently the hierarchy RHS vanishes for any polynomial h_t, whereas the differentiated atomic expression is w_j ξ'_ji. The positive weight forces zero velocity. Differentiating a polynomial with moving coefficients instead would be invalid; the author's explicit freezing avoids that error. Once the support is fixed on the component, ordinary interpolation obtains the replicator weight equation.

4. **Endpoint argument is global enough.** On any compact [0,H] the rank bound is a finite integer bound, so a largest attained rank r exists. The rank-r set is relatively open by a positive Gram determinant. Each nonempty connected component has constant support and exact positive weight formulas. At every endpoint in [0,H], the energy integral is finite and continuous; all limiting masses remain strictly positive. Moment continuity identifies the endpoint functional with that same r-atom law. Its positive determinant and the maximal-rank upper bound make an entire relative endpoint neighborhood rank r. Thus the component cannot stop, is relatively clopen, and equals [0,H]. This includes an endpoint at t=0; no initially open positive-time gap remains. Arbitrary H establishes the result throughout the supplied horizon.

5. **The wider polynomial energy is legitimate.** No time derivative of h_t is used. Local bounded degree and continuous coefficients ensure all RHS coordinates are meaningful continuous finite sums and ensure t↦h_t(ξ_j) is continuous. Exponential weights have finite strictly positive limits at finite endpoints. This is enough; sign, convexity, SOS form, or global lower bounds of h_t are unnecessary in this finite-rank class.

6. **One-sign clock with flats.** A continuous one-sign scalar multiplier gives a continuous monotone clock. Clock fibres are intervals where the original moments are constant. Quotient-map descent gives continuous clock-time moments, and the integrated hierarchy plus monotone absolutely-continuous substitution gives C1 clock-time moments without dividing by a vanishing clock speed. Applying the autonomous theorem is then justified. An identically zero clock is immediate. Locally finite concatenation is justified by endpoint continuation. Infinite sign oscillations accumulating at a finite time are not settled by that argument alone.

7. **Feedback is a conditional corollary.** For h_t(x)=∇J(q(t))·x with q(t)=L_t(x), the theorem identifies each supplied abstract path with its finite-weight lift. To compare two self-consistent feedback paths, locally Lipschitz ∇J is required for the standard finite-dimensional uniqueness argument; continuity of ∇J by itself must not be advertised as a general uniqueness hypothesis. Uniqueness is on their common interval in the objective's domain. Global continuation needs additional extension/control, for example a locally Lipschitz gradient on a neighborhood of the compact convex hull of the support. An initial finite support alone does not make the abstract full-rank promise automatic.
