8 October 2026. Targeted read-only check for the T20 fitrah warrant bridge. Original T17 and T19 reports, source guides, coverage records and evidence packages were inspected. Their primary source books were not reread in this task. The two main modern studies were inspected directly at the methods/results level. This is not a systematic review or a replication.

## Result

The supplied inheritance does not certify Ameri’s cited cognitive studies. T17 already preserves a strong recognitional account of fitrah and an important distinction between ordinary first-order warrant and a philosophical explanation of warrant. The new primary-study check adds a narrower empirical result: experimentally elicited endorsement of design varies with task constraints and meaning-framing. These studies do not measure whether the endorsed proposition is true or warranted. Neither this limitation nor a psychological explanation by itself refutes the proposition.

## Primary check 1 Mortality and design

Tracy, Hart and Martens 2011, Death and Science, PLOS ONE 6:e17349, [primary article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0017349), is the study behind Welsh’s 2011 report cited by Ameri at p. 162.

Participants imagined personal death or dental pain, then evaluated Behe/IDT and Dawkins/evolution passages. Outcomes combined four author-evaluation items with two theory-endorsement items; theory-only analyses were also reported.

Five adult samples: 122 psychology students; 352 geographically dispersed undergraduates; 832 US adults; 269 psychology students; 99 natural-science students. Studies 1, 2, 5 used both passages; Study 3 used one matched-content passage; Study 4 crossed mortality, Sagan meaning-in-naturalism framing, and one author/theory passage.

Study 1 increased IDT endorsement; Studies 2–3 primarily reduced evolution endorsement, with IDT increases nonsignificant. In Study 4, Sagan framing reversed the IDT effect: lower endorsement under mortality, d = .64, p < .05; the evolution increase was nonsignificant. Study 5 also reversed direction, with six-item simple effects assessed one-tailed. Study 4 lacked a neutral reading passage, confounding content with extra delay. Religiosity itself did not increase.

Inference: Ameri’s short use omits experimentally demonstrated boundary conditions. A directional effect in selected conditions cannot establish invariant, truthful or specifically Islamic recognition.

## Primary check 2 Design judgments under time pressure

Järnefelt, Canfield and Kelemen 2015, Cognition 140:72–88, DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2015.02.005, [author-lab copy](https://www.bu.edu/cdl/files/2015/04/Creator-online-publication1.pdf).

Three online adult studies reported analysed samples of 352 North Americans, 148 North American association-recruited atheists, and 151 Finnish nonbelievers. Participants were randomised between speeded 865 ms and unspeeded judgments and between general-being versus human-making wording. Forty natural photographs were evaluated; artifact, geometric-shape and cartoon controls addressed response bias and task demands. General-being wording left the agent undefined.

Time pressure increased creation endorsement among nonbelievers, particularly for general-being wording; human-making endorsement stayed lower. The investigators report sensitivity analyses including excluded participants. Nature/Earth agency beliefs predicted responses; their influence did not fully eliminate the speeded tendency. See Methods 2.1, 3.1, 4.1; Results 2.2–4.2; General discussion, printed 74–84.

Inference: This supplies positive evidence for a persistent, broader agency/design tendency, including among some reflective nonbelievers. It is adult response evidence, not a birth study, a measure of universal monotheistic content, or a calibrated truth-tracking test. Undefined agency permits several interpretations. Theistic upbringing was not experimentally removed. Cognitive ease alone supplies no rule assigning epistemic priority to the faster answer.

## Separate consequential scope correction

Dervic et al. 2004, Religious Affiliation and Suicide Attempt, AJP 161:2303–2308, DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.161.12.2303; [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15569904/) and [publisher](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.161.12.2303).

This compared 371 already-depressed psychiatric inpatients by religious affiliation and lifetime attempt history. Unaffiliated patients had more attempts and differed in age, family circumstances, aggression, impulsivity and substance-use history. Depression, hopelessness and stressful-life-event scores did not differ. Indexed primary-study results report attenuation of the affiliation association after moral-objection adjustment. The study’s interpretation concerns possible protective factors in a selected clinical population.

Inference: Affiliation is not identical to atheism; association within a depressed inpatient sample cannot classify atheism as an illness, estimate general-population psychiatric prevalence, or establish that disbelief causes illness. Nor does a protective association establish religious truth. This corrects the empirical scope of the reported Ameri pp. 182–184 use, without denying that religion can matter clinically. The primary abstract and indexed relevant text were inspected; continuous full-PDF reading was not completed.

## What is new and what remains open

The newly checked discriminations are the mortality study’s conditional reversals, the time-pressure study’s broad agency endpoint, and the clinical study’s selected-population endpoint. The distinction between first-order recognitional warrant and its complete theoretical explanation was already present in T17.

A fair fitrah assessment can grant the observed natural propensity and still ask which content is recognised, what marks successful versus impaired operation, and why that operation is truth-directed. Requiring these bridges is not a demand to solve all epistemology first. Conversely, appealing to a completed metaphysical argument may support a separate theistic conclusion without converting these psychological results into independent authentication of that argument.

No original child experiment was inspected in this pass. Bloom 2007, Barrett’s 2008 interview, Petrovich’s book/interview, Boyer 2008, Banerjee–Bloom 2014, Heywood–Bering 2014 and Jong–Halberstadt–Bluemke 2012 remain unverified here. No general replication claim or mathematical cognitive contract has been supplied, and no inherited mathematical machinery has been imported.
