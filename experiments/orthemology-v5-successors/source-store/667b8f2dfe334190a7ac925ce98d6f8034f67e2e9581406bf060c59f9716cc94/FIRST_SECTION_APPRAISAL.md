# Appraisal of the first four treatises and selected causal passages

Reader-facing study record, 7 October 2026. The interpretations and reading claims below belong to the recorded study and its bounded rereads. This edition of the notes adds no source-reading or proof credit.

## Conclusion and scope

The sixth collection materially strengthens the attribution of Ibn Taymiyya's cooperative-agency argument and its concessions. It does not simply confuse ability with action, or treat every mutual condition as circular production. The author explicitly permits that a cooperator could have acted alone, distinguishes each agent's power from joint attainment, affirms real secondary causation, and allows renewed divine relational perfection when God is its originating source. His further conclusion that actual cooperation is incompatible with complete divinity depends on an explicit ranking of inhibited or nonexclusive exercise, together with a nonduplication claim about the exact numerical effect. That further step must receive its own assessment.

I directly read all received E001–134 of the Islamarchive electronic witness, comprising the editor's introduction and treatises 1–4:129 actually present electronic positions and 25,318 whitespace words. Selected E183–186 and E211–216 were read separately after the middle reader's first pass. Bounded full-page scan readings include printed 87–100,62–66,125–129 and the additional controls specified in the first-section reflection. No single-reader whole-volume or whole-volume visual-collation claim is made. The collection-wide coverage belongs to the combined study and its final verification.

## What the author positively argues

1. A real act and its intended end require explanation. A circle in productive priority, or in the final end's intentional priority, cannot explain itself. This leaves room for co-present conditions and cooperative effects; 'with' is not always 'through'. Printed 87–90, electronic E091–094.

2. Genuine cooperation is differentiated agency. The two agents' acts and contributions are numerically different; the result requires their aggregate. A real contribution from the second agent rules out saying the first actually produced the entire same effect alone. Printed 94–95.

3. Standing solo ability is not actual unrestricted exercise. Printed 95 grants that one partner could sometimes perform the work alone. Printed 96 then argues that another's real participation prevents the first's whole exercise; inability and such inhibition both count as incompatible with full divine power. Printed 97–98 explicitly distinguishes this argument from the insufficient bare contrary-wills test and applies it to necessarily concordant wills.

4. Causes and purposes must not be collapsed. E183–186 grants each partner their own power, awareness, intention and objective, while realising the objectives jointly. E211–216 affirms that God works THROUGH created causes, not merely when they occur, and distinguishes the agent's act from the patient's reception. A complete condition complex is not identical to any one contributory power.

5. Perfection can be relational without being received from an independent supplier. Printed 62–66 answers the objection that God's joy in newly occurring worship entails previous deficiency: God originates that worship. The comparison is with perceiving new objects. A failure of one precise theory of relational novelty would need to be tested consistently across both kinds of attribute. The reply does not eliminate the divine relation to creatures.

6. Ultimate goodness has an explanatory and experiential dimension. Printed 119–123/e 123–127 rank ends above means and describe felt rest in divine remembrance. This combines rational considerations, normative intuitions and experiential testimony. It is not merely a nominal definition, and need not be deductive to carry evidence. But the universality and discriminating reliability of those premises remain substantive.

7. The actual ordered cosmos is offered as evidence. Printed 127–129 appeals to distinct yet regular celestial motions and their common orientation. Its positive inferential work must be recognised. Its particular physics, voluntary-motion premise, exclusion of alternative ultimate ends and transfer from sourcehood to unrestricted exercise are not independently verified by this reading.

## Distinct judgments

The unrestricted claim that every qualified original must have the complete exclusive source mode is not established here merely by necessary existence. It also requires the author's causal-ownership and perfection principles. The familiar cooperative examples support differentiated contribution; their extension to every possible ultimate agency profile remains a further burden. In particular, a condition on accomplishing an effect is not automatically a borrowed constituent of the agent's own power.

One common Creator of the actual connected order is a different and potentially weaker claim. A sufficient defeasible explanation need not eliminate every imaginable alternative. Integrated causation, coordinated ends and a unified source account are affirmative evidence. My bounded judgment is nevertheless that this volume alone does not yet make the comparative inference sufficiently discriminating. Much of the author's actual-order argument relies on the same strong causal/value premises or on historical celestial physics; simply assuming that all the coordination belongs to one complete source would recycle the conclusion. Conversely, a hypothetical cooperative model's bare consistency would not by itself refute the common-Creator inference. The task is to compare explanatory fit, independence, coherence and the cost of stipulated coordination, not to demand a deduction or count fewer entities alone as proof.

This judgment is not a rejection of a Necessary Being route or of the epistemological admissibility of relatively basic recognition. Nor is it a new assessment of all evidence in the earlier read volumes. It is the contribution and limitation of this selected source.

## Orthogonal material that must remain visible

T1 argues for universally intentional voluntary action and rejects literal destruction of the devotee's will, while giving charitable contextual readings of ecstatic speech. It distinguishes a valid intended good from sound means, recognises sincere error, places evidential burdens on negation and affirmation, and separates justice between persons from voluntary relinquishment of one's own rights. Religious zeal never removes a wrongdoer's right to proportionate treatment; excusing mistaken interpretation does not require following it. E009–046.

T2 separates universal will from normative love and approval, rejects both undifferentiated voluntarism and an exclusively creature-benefit account, and preserves a real relation between God and worship without supplying divine need from elsewhere. E049–071.

T3 recognises partial assent, mixed awareness, lost versus intermittent guidance, and the difference between a positively contrary conviction and the absence of fully formed belief. It includes premodern embryology, legal consequences and testimony of midwives; those are historical reports, not current clinical or legal guidance. It also allows denying a predicate because its purpose fails, without denying bare existence. E075–088.

T4's juristic discussion of multiple grounds is substantial: independent legal sufficiency requires evidence, not merely convenient inference from two suitable descriptions. Alternative causes cannot be invented without support to immunize an analogy from objection. E094–098. Its criticism of reducing happiness to abstract knowledge preserves the value of knowledge, mercy and power as completion rather than discarding them. E127–130.

## Textual and edition limits

The scan cover confirms collection 6, Ibn Taymiyya, Muhammad Uzayr Shams, Bakr Abu Zayd and Dar Alam al-Fawa'id. The 1429 first-edition claim is supplied by the electronic metadata; no matching imprint/year page is present among scanPDF 1–4. The scan omits the separately paginated editor's introduction and manuscript sample plates expected by its own index. E001–008 supplies the introduction text, not the plates.

The editor reports extensive corrections, unmarked obvious emendations, missing original openings/endings, and copyist abridgements. These are his manuscript reports, not independent manuscript inspection. Several odd sentences remain odd in print (10,59,90,92,93,96,98,99,111). Printed 50 corrects the electronic 'الشرير' to 'الشرعي'; printed 66 contains editorial [و] where E070 is defective; printed 107 openly replaces manuscript 'لا يكون' by [قد] with a note. No source file is silently repaired. The withheld IIFA PDF remains untouched; separately acquired source identities are not claims of independent textual ancestry.
