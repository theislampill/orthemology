# Physical causation → rational defeat: targeted primary-source receipt

Checked 2026-10-08 UTC. Read-only public research; only this original analytical receipt and its source manifest are deliverables. Downloaded source bodies remain temporary research files, not deliverables. No full-book or full-corpus reading is claimed.

## Result

Physical causation alone does not yield the conclusion that no rational knowledge is possible. The strongest inspected versions need an additional semantic-causal exclusion premise or an evolutionary reliability-and-defeat argument. These are different bridges and should be tested separately.

## What the primary texts actually add

### Balfour

Inspected *The Foundations of Belief*, eighth edition revised, Longmans, January 1906 printing, continuous printed pp.278–286; title and copyright pages checked. The cited 1918 printing was not located, so this verifies the argument in another printing of the named edition, not 1918 pagination equivalence. Scans of pp.279, 282 and 285 were visually checked against OCR. [Public-domain scan](https://archive.org/details/foundationsofbeln00balf/page/279/mode/1up).

At pp.279–280 Balfour moves from non-rational atomic origins to untrustworthy premises by adding a probability claim: the process has no bias toward truth, and error is more numerous. At pp.280–282 he considers selection as a possible reliability mechanism, then challenges extrapolation from practical survival to a general account of reality. Page 282 proposes a rational creator as the presupposition securing confidence; it does not demonstrate every divine attribute. The long p.285 note separately offers an exclusion argument about will and a mechanically sufficient material sequence.

### Reppert

Inspected the 1998 paper’s continuous displayed pp.7–27, plus displayed p.2 for lineage and citations. This is a 14-PDF-page booklet with two displayed pages per PDF page. [Primary paper](https://www.theistic.net/papers/V.Reppert/ReppertAFR.pdf). His [2011 author post](https://dangerousidea.blogspot.com/2011/01/cleaning-up-old-paper-of-mine.html) identifies Tim McGrew’s cleaned-up 1998 paper, distinguishes the slightly different 1999 *Philo* version, and warns that later definitional analysis is absent. That post links a former university-hosted PDF; identity of bytes with the current mirror is unverified.

Reppert expressly calls the bare atom-motion-to-irrationality argument too simple (p.11), accepts multiple explanatory levels (pp.11–14), then argues that inference requires causal relevance of propositional content and apprehended logical relations (pp.9–10, 23–25). His substantive attack is that conventionally assigned content cannot do the needed causal work, while structural descriptions allegedly cannot supply logical necessity. The list here has eight conditions, not the six reportedly on p.73 of his 2003 book. Neither that page nor p.85 was verified. The conclusion (p.27) is explicitly limited to a rational feature at reality’s foundation, not every attribute of God.

### Plantinga

Read continuous main argument from the 1994 *Naturalism Defeated* opening through the end of section I (PDF pp.1–10), and continuous PDF pp.19–21 on rationality, warrant and defeaters. [Public manuscript mirror](https://static1.1.sqspcdn.com/static/f/38692/383655/1263300179793/Naturalism%2BDefeated.pdf). It carries Plantinga’s copyright but the mirror’s provenance is weaker than an author/publisher host. The original Calvin URL was inaccessible; the old Notre Dame *Content and Natural Selection* PDF returned 404.

The manuscript distinguishes epiphenomenalism from semantic epiphenomenalism, considers adaptive belief-content causation too, and acknowledges that its probability estimates are poorly grounded before retaining the alternative of inscrutability. Warrant is stronger than true belief or internally appropriate belief revision; an undefeated defeater is a separate epistemic condition.

Also checked the continuous argumentative body and notes in Plantinga’s publisher-hosted [“Evolution vs. Naturalism,” *Books & Culture*, July/August 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2008/07/evolution-vs-naturalism/), from its opening argument paragraphs through notes 1–8. It explicitly limits the conclusion to rational acceptability, allows N&E to be true, and in note 5 includes materialism as an additional conjunct if naturalism does not entail it. The cited 2011 book p.329 was not checked.

## Original analytical reconstruction: what remains to establish

1. **Causal explanation versus a reason.** A causal history explains why someone believes; a normative reason supports what they should believe. Rationally basing belief on a reason may require a suitable link between these. Merely giving a physical description does not show that this link is absent.
2. **Content versus vehicle.** Neural or computational vehicles may have causally relevant organization even if basic physics does not mention meaning. To exclude this, one must defeat identity/realization and non-conventional accounts of content, rather than assume mental and physical descriptions name independent competing causes. A conventional computer analogy neither solves original intentionality nor proves it impossible.
3. **Logical necessity versus psychological necessity.** Necessity of entailment does not imply that every instance of recognizing it must be an unbreakable physical transition. A fallible realization can track a valid relation. Conversely, an output that happens to be valid does not establish understanding or rational basing.
4. **Reliability versus intention.** A mechanism need not intend truth to covary reliably with facts. Selection for fitness does not by itself guarantee general truth-tracking; failure to guarantee it does not establish low probability. Both directions need a specified content account, task/environment distribution, dependencies among beliefs, and a justified probability model. A 50/50 assignment is an extra premise, not the numerical meaning of ignorance.
5. **Local versus global scope.** Practical reliability can coexist with systematic abstract errors. Extending either success or failure to logic, all science, metaphysics, and every belief requires argument. Cultural error correction and cross-checking need assessment, not automatic dismissal or automatic vindication.
6. **Truth versus warrant/defeat.** A true belief can lack warrant, and a belief can be rational relative to misleading evidence while false. Moving from low/unknown conditional reliability to an undefeated defeater for each belief, including N&E, requires its own epistemic principles and treatment of total evidence, basic warrant, and alleged circularity.
7. **Theistic conclusion.** Failure of one naturalistic account would not select uniquely among all non-natural alternatives. A creator-based vindication additionally needs a truth-directed, competent, non-deceptive relation to our faculties and an account of how that itself is warranted.

The computer objection should therefore be presented in its strongest form: original rational content may be needed to explain derivative machine meaning. That blocks a quick computer counterexample, but it does not itself establish that non-conventional, physically realized content cannot arise. Likewise, allowing divinely created material consciousness would distinguish material constitution from an exclusively nonmental origin; a challenge only to disembodied-versus-embodied thought would miss the target. This is an analytical distinction, not a claim to have checked Ameri’s pages.

This is not a claim that every causal genealogy is a genetic fallacy. A demonstrated truth-insensitive or systematically misleading genealogy can be epistemically relevant. The unlicensed step is replacing that demonstration with the mere label “physical” or “non-rational.”

## Lineage and limits

Reppert’s p.2 directly cites Balfour 1918 pp.279–285 and Lewis, and Plantinga’s 2008 article acknowledges Lewis. Agreement within this chain is shared argumentative lineage, not independent evidential replication. This check does not settle the mental-causation literature, compare all revisions, or verify Ameri’s own chapter. It identifies the actual bridge-premises that a rigorous evaluation must expose.
