## Result and credit

**The directly read dependency confirms a genuinely practical, source-owned undertaking and rejects its reduction to a forecast. It does not establish a new command title over a recipient's otherwise adequate alternatives. Relative to the controlling report plus its existing contextual review, this is dependency verification and sharpened primary support, not a new normative premise. Duplicate/no-new-selection-premise: stop here.**

Two precise source controls matter in using the existing result:

1. Majmūʿ 18:148–150 distinguishes a truthful account of what will happen from what calls for or excludes a performance. It positively attributes the beneficiary right to the source's own saying. A merely predictive gloss therefore fails the author's explanation.
2. At 18:147–148 Ibn Taymiyya expressly says his criticized rationalist opponents do **not** mean that reason literally commands God. They claim rational knowledge of obligations. Rejecting an external legislator is consequently not, by itself, a refutation of their position or of rational normative discovery.

Both points were already substantially retained through Miftāḥ and the existing conditional-right review. The first report's caution about whether a promise discloses, enacts, or specifies a relation must be read with its express affirmation of real self-undertaking: the primary passage excludes **mere prediction as the whole explanation**, but does not settle when every relevant obligation first exists.

The accepted sourcehood, intrinsic necessity, Knowledge, Will, Wisdom, due worship/allegiance and truthful-testimony results remain earned. No neutral restart or generic is–ought objection is introduced.

## What the primary dependency actually adds to the source control

### A. Prediction is epistemic disclosure; undertaking has practical explanatory content

At 148 the author reasons that bare true reporting does not explain why an act is praiseworthy, or identify a consideration calling for it or excluding its contrary. Page 149 makes the contrast explicit: writing mercy upon oneself and prohibiting injustice are not merely recording future events. Otherwise wrath would be written upon oneself in the same sense, and every unperformed beneficent act would count as prohibited alongside injustice. The reductio targets collapsing differentiated practical statuses into a single future-history classification.

At 149–150 the conditional beneficiary right is then introduced; at 150 its source is the source's own saying. The nearby oath and self-command discussion explains why self-direction need not presuppose a higher director. At 151 the account connects this to willing, love, approval and avoidance of defect. This is stronger than “it will happen, therefore it is owed.”

But three stronger inferences do not follow:

- The text does not establish a temporal stage when no relevant obligation existed followed by its first creation at a particular utterance.
- It does not establish that every intrinsic normative reason is created by saying something, or that saying anything whatsoever would create a right.
- It does not discredit true normative testimony. A statement can warrant our recognition of an actual obligation while the undertaking and its reasons explain the obligated performance. The criticized reduction erases the latter role, not the former.

This clarifies the inherited “disclose/enact/specify” caution: a disclosure of a real practical self-undertaking can coexist with this account; a mere forecast substituted for that undertaking cannot. The original report already recognizes this practical content, and its review says so expressly. Consequently this is not a demonstrated reversal of the prior conclusion.

### B. Recipient condition versus source of obligation

The report at 149–150 gives the right to qualifying worshippers conditionally upon the stated response. Page 151 immediately distinguishes love of the source's own action from love of a creature's action. Page 151 also denies that productive authorship transfers the creature's act or moral predicate to the Creator: the predicate attaches to the subject in whom the action or quality inheres. This reinforces the already inherited own-act/created-effect distinction; it is not a new doctrine of this operation. Neither textual occurrence of the condition nor divine approval establishes that an actual recipient has fulfilled it. Qualification requires the actual relevant response, interpreted under the undertaking's full scope.

Once qualification is warranted, the specific owed treatment is source-owned without making the recipient a superior legislator. The recipient's act supplies the condition for applying the right, not the source's essential praiseworthiness. This is exactly the existing constructive application, now checked against its dependency. It supplies a reason to distinguish performance conforming to the undertaking from performance violating it. It does not necessarily distinguish all compatible implementations.

### C. External imposition versus rational recognition

The author denies an external superior obligating the source at 156. It would nevertheless misread 147–148 to say that his rationalist opponent literally gives human reason lawgiving power over God. He expressly denies that interpretation of their claim and instead contests its normative content and analogical application. The existing Miftāḥ notes and review already preserve that fairness condition.

Therefore “no external legislator” blocks one supposed independence threat; it is not a general proof against intrinsic normative necessity known by reason. Nor is recognition of a fulfilled condition itself an external act imposing the resulting obligation. Those are three different roles: constituting an undertaking, recognizing its content, and satisfying its condition.

### D. Self-obligation is not a reversible command-title argument

Self-direction demonstrates an account of the source's own required performance without a superior source of law. A title to choose between a recipient's otherwise adequate forms of an already owed response has another target. Neither the analogy with self-command nor the source's own undertaking supplies that transfer merely by using the vocabulary of command or right.

The initial report itself contains both the owed worship relation and the beneficiary right. Their co-occurrence is not an argument that the second creates every power of specification in the first. The earlier due-response appraisal, scoped retained-control principle, and authenticated normative-testimony route remain available on their own warranted terms. No new local selection entitlement is derived here; none is refuted either.

## Stopping point

The direct dependency substantiates the owned, practical conditional-right account and sharpens its epistemic/constitutive distinction. It exposes a possible equivocation only if someone reverses self-obligation into creature-directed selection authority, or interprets rational recognition as literal legislation. The controlling report and existing review already avoid those mistakes. There is no new selection premise and no new general normative theory. Further volume accumulation is not warranted by this operation. The report and source receipt are separate evidence for contextual review, not an integration or closure instrument.
