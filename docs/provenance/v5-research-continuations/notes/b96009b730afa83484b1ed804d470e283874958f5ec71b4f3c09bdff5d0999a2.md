# Original sourcehood and intrinsic Knowledge

## The sixth tranche result

The best positive argument is a source-sensitive argument from received cognitive perfection. Actual knowing supplies an irreducibly cognitive explanandum. An original source adequate to it must account for that cognitive actuality, and not merely for behaviour which an interpreter can describe as correct. The Taymiyyan perfection argument then maintains that an eligible intrinsic excellence cannot be wholly received at the derivative end while the complete original giver is wholly inferior in that very excellence. Applied to truth apprehension, this demands the source's own Knowledge, rather than only a power to make other subjects know.

This reconstruction has a positive rationale. Original giving removes the independent materials and partial contributions which make ordinary unlike-cause examples possible; eligibility removes the creaturely restrictions which would make property-copying absurd; and respect-specific comparison prevents productive superiority from being offered in place of cognitive actuality. The resulting argument is rationally substantive. It is not a theorem of efficient sufficiency, and describing its central warrant accurately is essential to assessing it.

The advance in this tranche is threefold. First, the positive argument is reconstructed around **original qualitative adequacy**, with a fair test of the impersonal source's alternative explanation. Second, new primary passages distinguish a cognition-specific terminus argument, an already-cognitive source's knowledge of its effects, and the original-giver argument. These are different routes with different starting points. Third, the modal extension is tightened: a source can satisfy original-giver priority at every world where a cognitive effect exists without possessing Knowledge at a world with no cognitive effect. Neither possible cognition-producing capacity nor the absence of an external completer closes this gap. Standing Knowledge needs a source-intrinsic determination warrant, which can be defended without requiring every particular cognitive act to be eternal.

The canonical result remains unchanged. Track T has not deductively established a Necessary Being or a global underived modal order. The present argument is a philosophical continuation, not a promotion of that result. The three frozen fifth-tranche packets were extracted separately and their archive identities recorded; no canonical or predecessor file was edited.

## Begin with the actual cognitive episode

Take an actual episode in which a person recognizes that an inference is invalid and corrects it because they understand the error. The episode has at least four distinct respects: the person and act actually exist; the thought has determinate content; the inference really fails to preserve truth; and recognition of that failure helps explain the correction. Merely emitting a different token does not amount to this episode. Orthability makes these distinctions unavoidable because the explanandum is successful evaluation, not simply a practice of evaluation.

The episode also avoids an unhelpful global starting point. We need not initially assume a completed inventory of all truths, a universal abstract object called Orthability, or a thinker that surveys every possibility. There is already one determinate actual case to explain. Its correctness is not made true by our describing it; its existence is not supplied by the conditional proposition that such an episode would be intelligible if it occurred.

The local self-origination objection now has its proper use. An account cannot explain the complete emergence of every condition of determinacy by a transition whose identity, possibility and success conditions are already determinate in the same respect. This defeats a purported explanatory construction. It does not identify the concrete source of the episode. A source argument must add a positive account of actuality and dependence. Otherwise the word “presupposes” silently changes from a condition of adequate explanation into a productive relation between beings.

## The positive route to a necessary source

The strongest source argument concerns the actuality of productive efficacy, rather than a first event in time. A wholly derivative exercise has what efficacy it has from an actually efficacious source. A complete explanation must therefore account for actual possession, not only for the conditional routing of efficacy. A conduit, constraint or permission can direct an exercise whose actuality it presupposes; multiplication of such roles does not by itself identify the original exercise.

This gives the affirmative source advocate a concrete demand. An account of the actual episode should identify the positive actuality in virtue of which its dependent powers operate. If an infinitely extended theory supplies only conditional transfer rules, then it has not yet explained which positive configuration is actual. If it adds a primitive active totality, an intrinsically actual global nature, or an unconditional existential principle, that addition must be assessed as the offered original resource. If it instead takes genuinely non-well-founded productive reception as primitive, it contests original-possession priority directly.

The earlier third tranche correctly shows why this demand is stronger than a prohibition on infinite graphs and weaker than a proof against every infinitist ontology. We retain that discipline. An actually realized endless series cannot be refuted by deleting a first member it never claimed to have. Conversely, a satisfying assignment to an infinite system is not already a complete productive explanation of the assigned actuality. The metaphysical dispute concerns the nature of received efficacy, not the elementary consistency of the graph.

Once an actual unreceived bearer g is established in the relevant complete ancestry, the restricted contingent-source principle yields its necessary existence: if this very g could have failed to exist, the principle would require an actual incoming source, contrary to the established root status. Generic essential reception then yields uniform underivability for that same bearer, if it is independently warranted that an individual received somewhere cannot exist wholly unreceived elsewhere. These are the retained SourceIdentity routes. They are useful because they do not trade “some root in each world” for “one root in every world.”

Actual roothood must still be strengthened to complete productive sourcehood. Being an indispensable unborrowed contributor is not yet being the whole source of a conjunctively produced actuality. The retained coverage and productive-prerequisite arguments address this step. After complete history coverage, unborrowed-act exclusion and an actual productive witness are supplied, the retained productive-completeness theorem identifies any two complete sources in that same productive respect. Its witness matters: without actual activity, completion and exclusion conditions can be vacuous.

This is a connected affirmative route: actual cognition gives a real explanandum; original-possession priority addresses received efficacy; the restricted source principle addresses necessity; essential reception addresses cross-world status; history-inclusive completeness and an actual unborrowed exercise address unity. The route deserves examination on these metaphysical grounds. None of these claims is obtained solely by a controller theorem or the accepted local adequacy no-go.

## What the Knowledge argument adds

Suppose that the same g is now established as the independently complete original source of a dependent subject and that subject's actual knowing. What remains to be explained is not merely that a state called knowledge occurs. The state is the subject's genuine apprehension of something true. Its cognitive achievement is real even though its existence, occasion and finite manner are derivative.

There are three possible descriptions of the original basis:

1. The basis is wholly absent. This cannot explain a positive actuality.
2. The basis is a positive noncognitive nature which produces actual cognition in another subject.
3. The basis includes original cognitive actuality and an efficacy by which distinct dependent knowing subjects can exist.

The first is readily excluded. The real contest is between the second and third. Confusing the first with the second would make the positive argument too easy: lack of cognition is not lack of all positive nature. The impersonal proposal is permitted an intrinsic nature, determinate powers, modal independence, complete productive coverage and genuine cognition in effects. It need not import a second lawgiver or substrate.

The source-sensitive perfection argument favours the third because the explanandum is a positive intrinsic excellence wholly received from its origin. The original giver is not merely another item in the process which happens to have a different specialism. Its role is to account for the whole dependent possession of this excellence. The proponent's claim is that complete original adequacy cannot consist in the source's being wholly without the very eligible achievement whose actuality it originates.

That is a claim about the positive content of ultimate adequacy. It is not a definition of “complete source.” The fifth exact Lean interpretation already proves that a weaker productive relation package leaves Knowledge open. This tranche therefore keeps the decisive principle separate and asks why it is credible.

## Original qualitative adequacy

The proposed general principle is this:

**When a dependent intrinsic actuality is irreducibly positive, a complete original explanation must identify a positive intrinsic basis adequate to that actuality in its distinctive respect. A name for the power to produce the manifestation is insufficient by itself to disclose that basis.**

The principle is deliberately weaker than “every cause has every property of its effect.” It does not require a sculptor to be triangular, a parent to have the child's height, or a teacher to know everything the student will later discover. Shape involves spatial limitation; comparative height involves relations between bodies; parents and teachers are partial causes which presuppose much of their effects' reality. Neither case is an independent complete original explanation of an unrestricted intrinsic cognitive perfection.

The principle also distinguishes two questions which can be confused. A power may legitimately be individuated through its manifestation. Calling a power cognition-producing need not mean that the future effect literally constitutes the source's nature. The objection is not an accusation of that ontological circularity. It is that such an individuation may leave the explanatory question unanswered: what positive character makes this kind of actuality available at all? A description can correctly identify a power while not revealing its adequate intrinsic basis.

For cognition, the positive case has unusual strength. The actuality to be explained is available in first-person and interpersonal cognitive life as apprehension, not merely as a stipulated pattern of outputs. Eliminating that actuality in favour of a noncognitive pattern would change the target. A source which itself apprehends truth therefore puts the distinctive positive achievement at the explanatory origin. A source whose entire relevant description is “whatever noncognitive nature produces apprehension” postpones the same distinctive question into an unspecified primitive.

This is a reason to take the personal explanation seriously. It cannot, alone, prove that every noncognitive nature is incapable of the required production. Unknown natures are not contradictory simply because unknown. Nor does the cognitive source's Knowledge by itself explain how a numerically distinct subject is originated. Creative efficacy remains part of the personal account. The comparative advantage is that cognition is positively present at the origin and can enter a content-sensitive explanation of the intended effect. The claim is not that production has become identical with the source's own thought.

Thus original qualitative adequacy narrows the question rather than deciding it by definition. The stronger Taymiyyan move is **respect-specific original-giver priority**: for a pure intrinsic excellence independently eligible for the original source, the complete original giver cannot be wholly inferior to its recipient in that respect. Original qualitative adequacy motivates the demand for an adequate basis; original-giver priority specifies why its cognitive application requires actual cognition rather than a wholly noncognitive substitute.

## Why the comparison is specifically cognitive

Actual apprehension is an achievement of its bearer. A source with immense productive range and no apprehension does not possess that achievement more perfectly; it lacks it. The recipient's being caused does not turn its apprehension into nothing. If apprehension is an intrinsic excellence, it is possible to distinguish superiority in productive range from superiority in apprehension without ranking the recipient superior overall.

This matters because an objection often gives the source a different excellence and calls the substitution “eminent” possession. A noncognitive ability to produce a hundred knowers is a real productive ability, if such a nature is possible. It is not itself an episode or standing possession of truth apprehension. The source argument therefore does not permit a change of comparison from knowing to producing while claiming that the same cognitive excellence has been preserved.

The positive warrant is that the recipient has no autonomous title to the entire perfection being compared. Its actuality is wholly originated. To locate all actual cognitive excellence in the recipient while giving its complete original basis none at all is, on the original-giver account, an inversion of the explanatory order of perfection. The source should possess the positive achievement originally; the recipient possesses it derivatively, finitely and in its own distinct manner.

Why is this more than an arbitrary ranking? It joins three independently intelligible features: the excellence is genuinely present, its recipient does not independently supply it, and the original source is eligible for it without the recipient's limitations. The principle does not elevate the source merely because it is larger, temporally earlier or more powerful. It concerns the origin of the very excellence whose actual possession gives the recipient its comparative status. A rival must deny that original adequacy constrains possession in this way, rather than answer only that unlike things can causally interact.

Still, this is a metaphysical priority judgement. Its explanatory and a fortiori rationale can be rationally assessed, supported and challenged; it is not reducible to the relational fact that g causes r. A philosopher may regard the judgement as a basic insight into original dependence, or may regard it as a defeasible explanatory principle. The present research has not established that every rational dissenter thereby contradicts the admitted productive premises. Stating this limit does not make the principle empty or deprive the argument of all warrant. It identifies the actual ground of disagreement.

## Why Knowledge is an eligible perfection

The argument does not identify divine Knowledge with human acquisition. A person's knowing can involve a previous lack, receptive organs, inference, attention limits and susceptibility to error. Those conditions explain features of this finite knower; they are not identical with the positive achievement of apprehending truth. It is intelligible to affirm that achievement without affirming that every bearer must acquire it through the same deficient process.

The source texts defend this distinction directly. In the Darʾ discussion of Abū al-Barakāt, Ibn Taymiyya repeatedly argues that knowing an object need not mean receiving one's perfection from that object. The object may be created by the knower, and the knower's own reality is itself an intelligible object. The relation to an object is therefore not automatically a relation to an independent efficient completer. This removes an important objection to eligibility.

The comparison also survives the objection that a noncognitive thing is not literally ignorant because it cannot receive knowledge. The relevant issue is the presence or absence of the positive achievement. Refusing the privative word “ignorant” does not confer apprehension on the nonreceptive thing. The MF 6:88–93 context supports this comparison. The argument needs the possibility of cognition in a source, however; comparing existing knowers with stones does not alone establish that possibility for every alleged necessary being.

Eligibility is therefore a real obligation. The positive case is that neither object-directedness, intrinsic plurality of attributes nor renewed cognitive acts has been shown to contradict unborrowed sourcehood. A bare objection from creaturely examples fails if it universalizes their limitations. A fully developed incoherence argument would require a response. The account does not assume a Thomistic prohibition on all potency or every kind of change. It follows the source's own distinctions between inherent perfection, efficacious power, occurring act and external supplementation.

## Three primary arguments that should not be merged

### Original giver and a fortiori perfection

MF 6:76–79 is a particularly useful positive locus. The discussion begins from the already established independent, necessary and creative source, then argues for the eligibility and priority of compatible perfection. It uses the maker of another's power, knowledge and life as specific applications. It next denies completion by an independently supplying other and argues against reciprocal supplementation. Its final movement invokes a complete determinant, rather than merely a possibility predicate. These passages precede the familiar nonreceptive-category discussion at 6:88–93.

Ṣafadiyya 1:90–94 and 2:25–29 give closely connected arguments. The latter explicitly distinguishes the essence's entailment of intrinsic attributes from efficient production of a separate creature. The short locator “الإيجاب الذاتي” belongs to the standing-attribute argument. It does not license an inference that every effect of the source must be eternally concurrent with it. The discussion of attributes also rejects treating every mutual inseparability relation as an efficient dependence on a distinct producer.

These sources support the argument developed here. They do not eliminate the philosophical need to defend original-giver priority or the completeness of intrinsic determination. An author's claim that a judgement is evident or agreed upon is evidence about the author's position; it is not an independent survey establishing universal assent.

### Cognition specific termination

Darʾ 3:115–117, represented in hosted section 005, develops parallel terminus arguments for independent power, perfection, Knowledge and life. In the Knowledge branch it begins with an acquired knower, says that a knower who is not knowing by itself requires a knowing teacher, and terminates the regress in one knowing by itself. The following movement connects an unreceived standing attribute with the independent existence of its bearer.

This supplies a valuable direct ancestor for the modern unborrowed-cognitive-exercise route. It also shows exactly where the cognition-specific work occurs. The predecessor required in the Knowledge branch is already a knower. If “teaches” means epistemic instruction, the argument addresses a pedagogical chain. If it means the original efficient bestowal of cognitive actuality, it uses a stronger claim: such bestowal must come from a knowing source. One cannot begin with the first meaning and conclude with the second unnoticed.

An uninstructed created thinker can be terminal in a chain of teachers while its bearer and cognitive power are productively derivative. Consequently, well-foundedness of teaching does not by itself supply an absolutely unborrowed cognitive exercise. The source-sensitive perfection rationale is what can defend the broader cognitive-bestowal premise. This route makes that requirement visible rather than escaping it.

### Perfect self knowledge and knowledge of effects

Darʾ 10:145–147, in the Ibn Rushd discussion, explicitly accepts that merely natural nonvoluntary action does not by itself entail knowledge in its actor. The subsequent argument concerns a source already established to know itself. Its own stated boundary is the prior establishment of self-knowledge; the inference to knowledge of effects relies on the completeness of that self-knowledge and the connection between a complete cause and its consequences.

This is a genuine positive route to coverage once initial cognition is established. A source which completely knows itself as actual producer cannot remain ignorant of what is included in that complete productive character. The argument is not the weak claim that any person who knows one thing about herself thereby knows all of her effects. The text itself distinguishes limited human knowledge from the complete source's knowledge of what follows from its known reality.

The route therefore should be used after the initial Knowledge bridge, not as a proof of it. It may strengthen an eventual argument to universal coverage, but only when the self-knowledge is exhaustive in the relevant respect and the entire dependent field lies within the source's productive scope. The present result is intrinsic Knowledge, not a completed proof of omniscience.

The constructive appendix `COMPLETE_SELF_KNOWLEDGE_AND_EFFECTS.md` follows the fuller Darʾ discussion in hosted section 017. It gives an exact positive implication from knowledge of the actual complete creative determination, a known efficacy relation and the relevant epistemic closure. A separate checked contrast shows why knowledge of generic standing capacity is insufficient. This excludes an already self-knowing complete intentional source's ignorance of its own actual effect under those stronger premises, while preserving contingent acts and effects.

## Standing Knowledge without a frozen inventory of acts

Four claims must remain separate: the source has the capacity to know; the source actually apprehends truth; standing Knowledge belongs to it by its intrinsic nature; each particular cognitive act or contingent content is eternally fixed. The first does not entail the second. The third does not entail the fourth.

The Darʾ discussion of Abū al-Barakāt is especially helpful here. The quoted interlocutor stresses capacity and intrinsic excellence. Ibn Taymiyya then offers several replies, including standing Knowledge and views allowing a renewed cognition corresponding to a changed object. He challenges the reduction of cognitive excellence to capacity alone. He also explicitly states that this local discussion is rebutting objections to Knowledge rather than setting out the affirmative proofs. The replies should not be combined into one undifferentiated theory attributed to every speaker.

The strongest standing-attribute reconstruction has the following form. Knowledge is an unconditional eligible perfection of the same independent source. Its eligibility does not depend on the existence of a particular recipient. Its standing realization does not await an independent giver. The source's own nature is the complete intrinsic determinant of this standing perfection. Therefore, wherever that nature is present, the perfection is realized. With the independent necessary-existence result, the same source necessarily possesses standing Knowledge.

The crucial word is **complete**. A nature can contain a capacity whose manifestation depends on a further intrinsic state. Excluding an external supplier does not exclude that internal difference. Thus the inference cannot simply be: possibly K; no external source of K; therefore necessarily K. The Ṣafadiyya appeal to a complete determinant supplies a stronger positive account. Our task is to justify its application to standing Knowledge rather than read it out of existential independence alone.

The positive rationale is that standing cognition is not an optional improvement conferred by a created object. Once the original-giver argument identifies cognitive perfection with the source's original adequate nature, the recipient's coming to exist should reveal that original perfection rather than create the source's right to possess it. Eligibility belongs to what the source is; actual cognitive perfection is not borrowed; and there is no source-independent restriction making the source constitutionally incapable of its own eligible excellence. This gives a coherent essential reading of the original-giver claim.

But a further challenge remains. An intrinsically noncognitive active nature could be proposed to generate a new internal cognitive state without an external supplier. Calling this “self-activation” does not establish it; calling it “external supplementation” does not refute it. A deliberate first acquisition would presuppose enough cognition to deliberate, but an involuntary intrinsic transition is not addressed by that observation. Nor does a temporal first-activation argument address a beginningless series of renewed acts or a merely modal alternative in which cognition is absent.

The proper affirmative answer therefore uses the source-sensitive perfection doctrine: the original nature does not lack an unconditional standing perfection whose limitation has no adequate basis, even though it performs particular acts at their fitting occasions. This doctrine makes an original cognitive source intelligible without freezing all its exercises. Its entitlement and complete-determinant premises remain substantive. The model tests below show precisely why they are needed.

### The direct argument against total cognitive self bestowal

A further primary locus sharpens this issue. MF 17:279–280, in the discussion of whether divine Knowledge and Speech could be described as generated offspring, rejects a wholly unknowing essence's making itself knowing without a teacher. The comparison is epistemically specific: a human thinker does not generate all knowledge by inference, since inference already uses prior cognitions and cognitive competence. The passage distinguishes this claim about total standing Knowledge from a proposal about particular derived acts. It should not be converted into a prohibition on every renewal.

This supplies a positive no-self-bestowal argument. If a source voluntarily undertakes to acquire all cognition, the undertaking already presupposes apprehension of what it is doing. If its acquisition is inferential, the inference already presupposes cognition of premises and their bearing. If it is taught by a distinct independent giver, its cognitive perfection is received. These alternatives cannot explain the wholly original cognitive character of an independent knowing source. Once actual source Knowledge is established, the argument supports treating its fundamental cognitive adequacy as original rather than a completely self-created epistemic achievement.

The hard alternative is neither a deliberation nor an inference: an alleged involuntary noncognitive power which first manifests cognition. The source's no-self-bestowal principle excludes that alternative at its strongest interpretation, but an argument against it is still cognition-specific. It cannot be derived merely from the fact that no external teacher was involved. This is where original qualitative adequacy and perfection priority again have work to do.

There is also a separate modal point. Even a successful prohibition on the first temporal onset of cognition would not, by itself, exclude a different possible history in which the same source never knows. Nor would a rule against onset alone rule out eventual loss. Moving from temporal originalness to essential standing possession therefore needs the invariant source-nature and no-defect claims. The text gives a positive rationale for those claims; the formal tests keep their distinct roles visible.

## The modal recipient gap

Suppose original-giver priority is stated at each possible world: if g completely gives cognitive actuality to a recipient at that world, g knows there. Now grant that an actual recipient knows. The argument yields actual source Knowledge. It does not yield essential Knowledge if there is an alternative in which g has no cognitive recipient. The conditional's antecedent fails there.

Adding that g can produce a knower at that alternative still does not help by ordinary modal logic. It yields a possibility at which the antecedent holds, and hence a possibility at which g knows. That is possible Knowledge, not Knowledge at the original alternative. One must add an adequacy rule applying to the source's standing cognitive-production capacity, or an essential-nature argument which fixes standing cognition independently of production.

This is not a refutation of essential Knowledge. It is a precise improvement in the positive inference. Actual original giving, possible original giving, and intrinsic standing possession need differently quantified premises. The exact three-world Lean interpretation preserves the retained source schemas, adds actual source Knowledge and the worldwise existential cognitive instance of original-giver priority, and keeps the source productively active even in the no-cognition world. A separate two-world checker exhibits the same modal separation while varying an intrinsic activation state under one response law. These checks show what the weaker modal premises leave open. It does not certify that this is a metaphysically possible source.

## The strongest impersonal rival and the positive reply

The impersonal rival U should receive its full strength. U exists necessarily and has a determinate intrinsic productive nature. It completely supplies dependent cognitive subjects and their actuality. Their cognition is genuine; their contents are objective; truth is not made by successful output; and the productive laws are expressions of U's nature rather than independently existent suppliers. U itself does not apprehend anything.

The original-giver argument rejects U because U's possession of productive efficacy does not satisfy the source's cognitive perfection priority. The explanatory-disclosure argument adds pressure when U's positive nature is left as a variable Q whose only specified role is to produce cognition. The affirmative proposal can identify a known positive kind of actuality, truth apprehension, and combine it with intentional creative efficacy. A merely placeholder Q has not yet provided an equally informative account.

The reply is stronger if the personal account actually connects its terms. The source understands a determinate target, recognizes the relevant fitting means, and efficaciously wills a distinct subject with a faculty appropriately ordered to that target. In this architecture, meaning, objective assessment and production are linked by the source's own practical understanding. The target need not be an effect which first exists and then independently gives the source its understanding. The source's own adequate apprehension and efficacy can account for the intended cognitive order.

This is the constructive intellectual-source account. It must be charged for all of its content: real source cognition, adequate target coverage, a relevant purpose and reliable efficacy. Bare cognition alone does not supply the intended fit. Likewise, a noncognitive rival with irreducible immanent teleology, objective normative powers and a worked-out theory of cognition must be assessed at that stronger level. It cannot be dismissed as mere mechanics after those additions are granted.

The exact comparative result is therefore discriminating rather than universal. The positive account defeats a supposed complete explanation that only renames the unexplained manifestation, or that eliminates genuine cognition while claiming to explain it. It excludes the fully described noncognitive U **if** respect-specific original-giver priority is warranted. The present work supplies reasons for that principle but does not derive its universal truth from the weaker causal relations. Against a developed intrinsic teleological U, comparative superiority remains to be shown by actual explanatory integration and not by counting the word “source” once.

This respects the canonical Candidate G result. Putting modal order, actuality, semantics and norms into one bearer is not automatically explanatory compression. The rational architecture earns credit only for the defended relations it supplies among them. It cannot regard every feature of an impersonal nature as a separate arbitrary primitive while treating a conjunction of mental attributes as free.

## What has actually been strengthened

The argument now has a clearer affirmative centre. Received cognition is not treated as a reason to repeat a mental word at the source. It is treated as an intrinsic positive achievement whose complete original adequacy must be explained. Source-sensitive priority connects that demand to the source's actual apprehension, and the source texts independently distinguish this route from instruction regress and already-established self-knowledge. The strongest noncognitive rival is challenged at its original cognitive adequacy, rather than at a supposed absence of any positive power.

The modal extension is also more exact. The source's standing perfection cannot be secured by worldwise applications of an effect-dependent original-giver principle alone. The doctrine must concern the source's nature independently of whether a particular cognitive effect is produced. A complete intrinsic determinant then yields essentiality without implying the eternal occurrence of every act. This preserves the intended Athari distinction between standing attributes and renewed voluntary acts.

These are philosophical and inferential advances, not a declaration that the decisive metaphysical premise has become compulsory for every rival. The canonical Necessary-Being and global-modal-order statuses remain unpromoted. The sixth continuation offers a connected positive argument, specific defeater replies, new source controls and a sharper modal test. Its outstanding task is to establish the original-giver perfection principle and intrinsic-determination doctrine more decisively against a positively articulated impersonal original nature.
