AR8R-T142 — Least/greatest fixed-point authorization divergence

Let the authorization lattice be P({a}) and define the monotone operator F(X)=X.
Theorem

lfp(F)=empty
gfp(F)={a}.

Proof

Both empty and {a} are fixed points. They are respectively the least and greatest elements of the lattice, hence the least and greatest fixed points. ∎
Consequence

A purely self-supporting authorization cycle is rejected by least-fixed-point semantics and accepted by greatest-fixed-point semantics. The difference is not a proof defect; it is a substantive semantic choice.

Thus T137 establishes only the registered well-founded/inductive authority boundary. A non-well-founded authority account must independently defend:

    why coinductive support is appropriate for normative authority;

    which circular structures are productive rather than vacuous;

    how false or arbitrary fixed points are excluded;

    and how owner or source authority is preserved.

LFP_AUTHORIZATION: EXCLUDES_UNSEEDED_CYCLE
GFP_AUTHORIZATION: MAY_INCLUDE_UNSEEDED_CYCLE
CORRECT_AUTHORITY_SEMANTICS: NOT_DECIDED_BY_FIXED_POINT_MATHEMATICS
