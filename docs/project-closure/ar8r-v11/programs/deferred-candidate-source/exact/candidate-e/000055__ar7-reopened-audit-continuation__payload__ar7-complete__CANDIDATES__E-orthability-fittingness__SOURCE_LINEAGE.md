# Source lineage — Candidate E

## 1. Constitutivism

Contemporary constitutivism grounds some normative claims in constitutive features, aims, principles, or standards of action/agency. The attraction is that the resulting norms are not merely variable current motives or endorsements. Candidate E therefore cannot treat constitutivism as token-autonomous; its strongest forms already use type-level standards.

Closest source neighborhood:

- Christine Korsgaard, *The Constitution of Agency* and *Self-Constitution*;
- J. David Velleman, *The Possibility of Practical Reason*;
- Paul Katsafanas, constitutivism about practical reasons;
- David Enoch, “Agency, Shmagency”; Matthew Silverstein and Connie Rosati on responses.

## 2. The shmagency and authority problem

Enoch's objection is that facts constitutive of agency do not automatically explain why those standards have normative authority for an agent who asks why she should remain an agent of that kind. Candidate E shares the caution but does not offer a new solution to shmagency. It conditions on objective correction and classifies authority sources.

## 3. Normative regress

The normative-authority literature contains regress problems: a norm's authority appears to require a further norm or reason. General regress methodology warns that an infinite sequence is not defective without a separate diagnosis of viciousness. Candidate E's dependency-direction clause is intended to avoid treating infinity alone as a refutation.

Closest sources:

- Linda Radzik, “A Normative Regress Problem”;
- Anna-Sofia Maurin, “Infinite Regress—Virtue or Vice?”;
- general work on foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism.

## 4. Natural normativity and teleosemantics

Selected-effect, organismic, and teleosemantic accounts can explain token malfunction or representational error by standards that outrun current token dispositions. These are countermodels to any claim that token-transcendence entails nonnatural normativity or Wisdom.

Closest sources:

- Ruth Millikan on biological function and teleosemantics;
- Peter Godfrey-Smith on teleosemantics;
- etiological and organizational accounts of proper function.

## 5. Normative guidance

Work on normative guidance distinguishes being guided by a norm from habit, conformity, enforcement, or explicit endorsement, and emphasizes that tacit socially acquired norms may guide agents. This supports separating authority, acquisition, endorsement, and causal influence.

Closest source:

- Peter Railton, “Normative Guidance, Evaluative Guidance, and Skill.”

## 6. Exact relation and difference

The current candidate is not the generic constitutivist claim, not the shmagency objection, and not a generic normative regress. Its proposed distinct contribution is the explicit five-horn source partition for **recursive radical correction relative to a token standard basis**, together with the retained-meta-standard exception.

No exact established theorem with this full scope has yet been located. Priority and significance remain unestablished; the result may reduce to a careful synthesis of familiar distinctions.
