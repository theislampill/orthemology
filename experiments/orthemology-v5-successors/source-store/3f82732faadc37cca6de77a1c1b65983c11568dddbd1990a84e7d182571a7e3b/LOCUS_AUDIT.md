## Result

The strongest source argument against plural original co-producers is considerably richer than the contradiction in two agents each producing the whole effect alone. It also asserts independence of intrinsic power, rejects externally conditioned exercise, distinguishes genuine differentiated cooperation, invokes actual interconnection, and adds a metaphysical coalescence/perfection argument. A model that denies these premises is a rival theory, not a countermodel preserving them all. Their articulation does not make every premise a theorem of bare logic.

The divine-action passages distinguish enduring intrinsic power, the genus of activity, successive particular voluntary acts, and created effects. Temporal emergence is not automatically being a separated creature, receiving power from another, or acquiring a previously missing essential perfection. A further causal argument excludes complete stripping of successive voluntary activity even at a time. That argument is stronger than persistent possibility, and is not the assertion that every act-kind is perpetually exercised.

## Audit A The strongest cooperation argument

### Majmuʿ 20:175–178

Classification: strengthens; answers a countermodel; reveals an equivocation.

The opening discussion concerns multiple grounds of one ruling or pieces of evidence for one conclusion. Additional contributions strengthen the result rather than remaining causally idle. Ibn Taymiyya generalises across voluntary, natural, and motivational causes: genuinely sharing an effect excludes each contributor's actual solitary production of that same whole effect. His qualification matters: holding the agent's condition unchanged, the solitary and collaborative cases cannot produce precisely the same causal output. The person who can lift a beam alone and together does so through altered exertion, will, instruments, or movement.

At 177 he expressly separates intrinsic incompatibility of two complete producers from the familiar opposition between incompatible wills. At 177–178 he distinguishes separately attributable parts from a mixed joint effect that cannot be divided between producers. The latter requires that neither contributor already possess the full independent efficacy displayed by their conjunction. This is not failure to notice ordinary cooperation.

Logical layer: being the actual sole producer excludes another actual sole producer by its meaning. Causal layer: genuine joint contribution requires a contribution-sensitive account of the joint effect and a same-condition comparison. Metaphysical layer: the creator must possess complete independent efficacy. Calling both causes merely sufficient, in a permissive overdetermination semantics, does not yet import Ibn Taymiyya's stronger actual-solitary-production sense of independence.

### Majmuʿ 20:178–183

Classification: new substantive premises; answers the irreducibly distributed cooperator model.

This continuation supplies the omitted bridge. At 179 he states that power distributed between two can be borne by one. He treats this as rationally evident and supports it by united persons, members, and bodies having increased effective strength. At 179–180 he moves from such coalescence to the possibility of more complete independent power for the cooperators. At 180–181 he argues that an agent unable independently to produce an external effect cannot independently perfect itself; susceptibility to that completion therefore signifies dependence, incompatible with necessary self-sufficiency. He makes the scope explicit: necessity covers essence, attributes, acts subsisting in the agent, and external effects insofar as their production depends on those acts.

This is the strongest fresh addition to the premise inventory. It is not reducible to anti-duplication. Transferability/co-bearability of powers, preservation or increase of power under union, the move from a possible united bearer to each cooperator's capacity for fuller efficacy, and the perfection-to-self-sufficiency bridge are metaphysical claims. Their validity may be examined. Simply stipulating irreducibly distributed powers denies them and cannot count as preserving them.

### Darʾ 9:339–362 and adjacent sequel

Classification: strengthens; answers countermodels; reveals an equivocation.

The opening is a response to Ibn Rushd, whose words must not all be attributed to Ibn Taymiyya. Local 627–649 distinguishes two complete producers of the numerically same effect from builders, beam-carriers, bodily organs and ordinary natural causes making distinguishable contributions. The natural examples are historical illustrations; their inclusion is not a modern validation of their physics. General creaturely cooperation does not refute his claim that absolute creation is independent.

Local 700–725 defends the rational opposition proof, then deals with agreement. Necessary agreement receives its own analysis. Reciprocal production of the very ability to act is vicious prior causal circularity. Mere concomitance is explicitly distinguished and allowed, as with a bearer and its essential attributes. The proposed pair of intrinsically powerful but necessarily concomitant originals is then challenged because original power is taken to entail independent exercise without the other. This final bridge is substantive, not just the prohibition of prior circularity.

Local 710–711 and 738–739 add actual world-interconnection: each producer's effects require the other's effects, so the postulated independent divisions do not fit the world under discussion. This is an additional common-source premise. It is not obtained from numerical plurality alone.

On directly verified 9:362, local 747–750 answers al-Amidi's conditional-capacity objection. Freely choosing one of two incompatible acts is distinguished from being unable to perform an act because another agent prevents it or must refrain. The impossible conjunction itself is not an object power must realise. The reply relies on excluding external dependence in a creator's exercise of power.

The nearby sequel, local 776–779, explicitly names differentiated cooperation as the remaining possibility needing refutation, then invokes deficiency of independent power and the alternative between freely possible disagreement and externally restricted agency. This sequel was freshly read but is not assigned an unverified printed page here.

### Safadiyya 1:92–95 and 2:169–172

Classification: strengthens; answers reciprocal-enabling and third-cause countermodels.

The direct Salim mapping is precise: 1:92 is electronic line 423 with footer 424, matching SF-S001-L0134–0135; 1:93 is line 427 with footer 428, matching L0136. The continuation at 1:94–95 is essential to the full argument. Adwa printed123–125 controls the corresponding wording.

The eligible perfection is one possible for an existent and containing no deficiency. If the alleged original receives it from another, that other is either its creature, yielding causal priority/circularity trouble, or an intrinsically perfect independent source. The latter changes which bearer qualifies as creator. Two beings each supplying the other's original enabling power produce the rejected prior circle. Two makers are then tested as complete individual producers or differentiated cooperators. The text expressly extends rejection of causal circularity to completion of efficacy, while distinguishing permitted concomitance in effects.

The later 2:172 passage makes the third-cause distinction explicit. Two created correlates can receive mutually conditioned capacities from a third cause. This does not preserve their status as independent originals. For two originals with no third giver, the author disallows power whose completion is obtained from the other; if a native component is proposed, he requires an independently sufficient intrinsic capacity. Actual interconnected effects are again invoked at 2:171. A stipulation of non-received but permanently restricted powers contests this independence premise rather than preserving it.

## Audit B Divine action and emergence

### Particular act versus enduring perfection

Classification: reveals an equivocation; strengthens.

SF-S001-L0073–0094 distinguishes producing everything, producing a particular thing, and activity in general. Eternal complete production of every particular conflicts with observed novelty. A beginning of a particular effect or particular act need not be a beginning of the agent's essential perfection. This is directly controlled in Adwa99–100. At L0103/Adwa107, the completion of each successive efficacy is explicitly not supplied from another: preceding acts conditioning later products are the agent's own acts.

Hence these must remain separate: temporal emergence; being a creature distinct from the agent; dependence on an external giver; acquisition of a formerly missing essential perfection. No automatic implication from the first to the other three has been established.

### The rejected binary

Classification: reveals an equivocation; answers a counter-objection.

SF-S002-L0577–0603, controlled at Adwa368–374, exposes the assumption that everything is either an eternally fixed particular or a separated created thing. The third category is what subsists in the divine agent through power and will. It can be described as intrinsic by subsistence and voluntary by its relation to power and will. The author separately distinguishes act from effect and creation from the created product. His reports of competing schools are not all endorsements; the positive third-category resolution is explicit.

### Global inactivity has an additional causal exclusion

Classification: new substantive premise relative to capacity-only reconstruction; strengthens; answers the globally inactive model against the fuller doctrine.

The clarified continuous /11 discussion culminates at Salim2:141–144, especially 2:142, electronically lines2254–2257 and SF-S003-L0079–0083. Adwa414/PDF421 independently controls it. A hypothesis stripping the agent of all successive voluntary matters even at a time could not subsequently yield a new event, since it would lack a sufficient causal change. The text then explicitly says those successive voluntary matters are inseparable. This is an argument from adequate causation, intrinsic successive agency, and absence of an independent external enabler. It is stronger than saying power remains available.

Compared with the separately completed Ibn ʿUthaymin Nisāʾ33 audit, both supply more than persistent ability: that later explanation appeals explicitly to the perfection of activity; this Ibn Taymiyya locus adds the causal impossibility of restarting from the stipulated total stripping. Their evidential roles should remain distinct. This comparison adds no second-hand primary-reading credit to this assessment.

The quantifier is activity generally. No particular act-kind is thereby shown to be active at every time. Nor does cessation of one utterance, one effect, or one kind amount to cessation of the genus. No-first-act, no interval of total inactivity, and no ultimate exhaustion are different propositions. The selected Salim2:142 argument expressly addresses total stripping at a time; translating that into a temporal formalism still requires defining times, intervals, event boundaries and the range of voluntary activity.

### The /12 incipit and the modal boundary

Classification: strengthens; reveals an equivocation; answers the eternal-particular-act model.

“ونفس حقيقة الفعل المعين يمتنع قدمه” is directly bound to Salim2:145, electronic line2272 with footer2273, and Adwa416/PDF423. The following page, Salim2:146, is independently displayed on Shamela22874/477. The two source coordinates are not interchangeable.

Salim2:145–149 explains that a particular act is successive and voluntary, whereas a necessary attribute may belong essentially, either as a particular such as life or as a genus such as speech and willing. An eternal fixed particular act would erase the relevant distinction between acting and an invariant attribute. The objection that the essential power and will would themselves then need to be voluntarily caused generates a regress or prior circle in the very basis of agency. The text also distinguishes efficient causation from mere accompanying motion, as in hand and key.

At 2:172–173 the intrinsic-power argument establishes beginningless possibility, then possibility of beginningless activity. Do not silently change that modal conclusion into actual tokens at every time. Actual continuing activity is supported elsewhere by the stronger genus, perfection and causal claims just identified. The formal G-empty versus all-emergent-empty distinction remains useful, but it is not the full source ontology: it must not erase intrinsic acts, alter event-kind quantifiers, or equate occurrence with logical Created.

## Bounded conclusion

The cooperation challenge is answered by the fuller source-premise package to the extent a proposed model claims to preserve that package. Whether its coalescence, unlimited independence, or causal-perfection bridges are independently defensible remains a philosophical question; no bare-logic theorem or complete Lean reconstruction is claimed here.

The action audit now has direct textual grounds for rejecting complete global inactivity under that stronger doctrine while preserving the difference between genus, kind, token and effect. It does not licence an inference from mere possibility to actuality or a blanket accusation that all voluntary cessation loses essential perfection.
