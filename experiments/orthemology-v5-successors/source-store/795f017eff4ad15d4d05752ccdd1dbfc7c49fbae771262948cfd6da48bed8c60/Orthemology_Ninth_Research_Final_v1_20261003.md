### A simple explanation

Imagine passing a repair job to another person. You give them the correct instructions and a working machine. That still may not be enough: the button labelled 0 on the new machine could do what button 1 did on the old one. They need a trustworthy way to connect the permission in the instructions to the right button. They also need to keep whatever information or help will be needed for the next repair.

The mathematical work checks several of those connections very carefully. The philosophical work asks a deeper question: what really explains our ability to recognise and correct a mistake, and what would justify calling its ultimate source necessary or perfectly trustworthy? The new arguments clarify what would make those conclusions follow. They have not yet established all the reasons needed to say that those conclusions are true of reality.

## Necessary sourcehood and the actual warrant question

### Two productive bridges with different burdens

The accepted necessity article begins with a particular original bearer granted for the test. It does not infer that actual grant from the correction. Its first bridge concerns the individual itself. If the individual is possible from a situation in which it is wholly absent, a productive actualisation is supposed to supply it; essential unreceivability excludes the resulting received existence. The contradiction then excludes absence. The proof needs adequate modal coverage and unreceivability at every relevant manifestation, not merely actual unreceivedness. \[F1\]

There is a precise reach condition. When unreceivability is stated only over actuality's direct alternatives, the proof must ensure that the productive manifestation reached through an alternative lies within that same protected range. Local composition of the relevant accessibility steps suffices. Symmetry alone does not. A finite control retains symmetric access while placing the received manifestation outside actuality's direct range, showing what the additional condition does. The control tests entailment; it does not certify that such an origin-changing bearer is metaphysically possible. \[F1\]

The second bridge concerns the exact fact that this bearer exists with its whole existence unreceived. If that fact is possible from an absent-bearer alternative, the stronger productive completeness principle promises a realisation of that very origin-qualified fact. Yet production from complete absence makes the manifested bearer received. The contradiction occurs inside the proposed manifestation. This route therefore avoids the first route's separate essential-origin premise and its two-step reach condition. \[F1\]

The gain is real but conditional. Producing an individual is weaker than producing that individual with an unreceived whole origin. The second bridge owes warrant for the stronger exact-fact coverage. It cannot obtain that warrant from ordinary examples of producing an object while dropping the origin qualification which makes the argument work. \[F1\]

### The exact relocation result

Under causal-content fidelity, the target-specific exact-fact completeness clause at a situation is equivalent to this implication: if the origin-qualified fact is possible there, the bearer already exists there. If the bearer were absent, productive completeness would require a received-and-unreceived manifestation. If the bearer is present, the clause's absent-bearer antecedent is false. \[F1\]

With the stated possibility coverage over actuality's alternatives, the restricted completeness clause is consequently equivalent to the necessary existence of the particular bearer. This is a substantive diagnostic result. The revised proof has removed one explicit premise but has not thereby demonstrated a weaker warrant burden. Equivalent force has entered the stronger coverage condition. \[F1\]

A general modal theory could still independently support that instance. The result does not establish that every use of such a theory is circular. It establishes that asserting only the target-specific instance cannot be presented as independent, weaker evidence for the conclusion. The next useful argument must address why the general law has the relevant origin-qualified range. \[F1\]
