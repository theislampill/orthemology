# Adversarial recipient-decision cases

These cases hold the reviewed care responsibility fixed where stated. They are not independent realisations of an original source essence. Each verdict identifies what the recipient should change, rather than merely reciting a category distinction.

## 1. The indifferent protocol before and after coordination

Before G arranges care, A and B each preserve the same target. R prefers B. After adequate notice, G has fittingly prepared A and other recipients have reasonably aligned their own activity with A. Unilateral B will destroy part of the shared observation; the preference for B is slight and no protected interest is lost by A.

**Decision:** R should use A under those controlled stakes. Its earlier permissibility does not make uncoordinated B permissible now. The new reason arises from actual relations to the other actions and adequate care, not a command-shaped sentence.

**Strongest objection:** G simply created the very dependence it invokes. **Response:** Creating a fitting shared arrangement can create genuine action reasons, but it cannot by itself make the arrangement fitting. Its target, burdens, prior scope, notice and alternatives must pass independent assessment. The next cases remove those facts.

## 2. Harmless deviation

Retain G's responsibility, competence and instruction. R has an independently warranted translator that allows B without disturbing A, misleading other participants, increasing risk or adding relevant burden. No independent authority allocation forbids B.

**Decision:** The care-coordination argument permits B. A further obligation to use A would need another ground. This is the central test separating the demonstrated result from an issuer's settling power.

**Residual:** If B creates hidden risks, then the facts differ; no recipient licence to ignore warranted expert correction is asserted.

## 3. Expertise without jurisdiction

A highly reliable instrument designer knows the exact error profile and explains why a proposed acquisition will lose data. It has never been allocated the decision whether R should pursue an optional personally significant inquiry. R understands the technical cost and chooses another permissible project anyway.

**Decision:** Credit the technical evidence; do not infer a duty to undertake the designer's preferred project. R can owe others commitments already undertaken, but the designer's expertise itself does not create those commitments.

**Difference from arbitrary scepticism:** R may not claim the unsafe acquisition is safe while accepting all the evidence against it. Choosing a different permissible end does not falsify the evidence.

## 4. The fallible but competent maintainer

G has an excellent maintenance record. Its new instruction uses an obsolete version label; R possesses authenticated release evidence demonstrating the mismatch. G's duty remains and the old operational rule would fail the fixed target.

**Decision:** Pause the contested step, present the mismatch and seek a corrected plan or adequate alternative. Do not obey merely because G is responsible. The error is a relevant particular defeater; a merely imaginable hidden defect would not play the same role.

**Authority variant:** Even an independently authorised role would need its actual error and invalidity rules. No assumption that every mistake dissolves every authority is made.

## 5. Forced dependence and an unrelated pledge

G wrongfully narrows R's alternatives, then offers rescue only if R promises permanent allegiance. Rescue without the pledge is equally adequate, reasonably available and involves no greater relevant legitimate loss.

**Decision:** G has no entitlement to the pledge from that dependence. R's refusal is not refusal of rescue. The original duty and any additional responsibility for the wrong remain. If complying is the only presently available way to protect others, compliance can still be the action R has most reason to take, without legitimating G's demand.

**No hidden consent move:** The verdict does not require G to claim that R freely agreed. It rejects the alternative argument that a duty to rescue confers a right to dictate these means.

## 6. Legitimate dependence, newly coercive terms

The original arrangement was legitimate. G now requires R to disable independent evidence checks as the price of continuing during an already exposed interval. A version retaining the checks is equally adequate and reasonably available without relevant offsetting loss.

**Decision:** R may reject the disabling condition and retain the care claim. The legitimacy of the original relation does not validate each later term. G cannot make the alternative unavailable by preference alone and use that preference as its authority warrant.

**Important opposite case:** If the check itself is obsolete and predictably destroys the same truth-sensitive process, a warranted revision may be fitting. The dispute is about the real dependency and replacement warrant, not a prohibition on ever changing checks.

## 7. A fair offer rejected for a serious personal reason

G offers A, but A exposes private information unrelated to the task; B preserves the task without that disclosure at a reasonable cost. G describes R's refusal as obstruction.

**Decision:** Privacy gives a relevant reason for accommodation; A has not been shown an adequate fair discharge. N1 cannot be applied by simply calling the objection a preference. If B is actually impossible or disproportionately burdens other rights, the balancing changes and needs its own assessment.

## 8. Refusal concerning only the recipient's risk

R is competent, informed and free to decline an adequate optional intervention, accepts the remaining risk to its own permissible inquiry, and does not export that risk to others. No distinct obligation requires continuation.

**Decision:** Respect the refusal; no source authority follows from the care reason. What G must continue to do changes with the actually accepted risk and surviving exposure. This is inherited Tenth refusal scope, used as a control rather than claimed as new.

## 9. Refusal harming other recipients

R wishes to withdraw from the task but would do so by corrupting the shared record needed by others. A harmless disengagement is available.

**Decision:** R should use the harmless disengagement. Its control over its own project is not a power to impose that loss on others. This result needs no right of G to command R's private purposes.

## 10. The warning comes from a source in breach

G wrongfully ceases support and accurately warns that the current channel is no longer safe. Reliable independent evidence confirms the support loss. R argues that refusing G's jurisdiction permits continued irreversible reliance.

**Decision:** Reject that inference. Stop or redirect the unsafe act while retaining the complaint and any continuing care/repair claim. Source wrongfulness is not a safety certificate. This tests the combined practical outcome, not a new theorem of testimony.

## 11. Criteria may change; fixed attribution may not be falsified

R develops a new calibration standard or changes inquiry. The new standard may be warranted for its new target. It does not turn an unchanged false attribution under the old target into a faithful one. G's insistence on accurately stating what changed can track independent truth rather than an authority over every future inquiry.

**Decision:** Permit genuinely warranted revision or a candidly changed task; preserve the old target when claiming its repair. This is inherited Sixth norm content, included to prevent the source-authority debate from erasing objective correction.

## 12. The source has no human contract

G creates R and a truth-directed provision, actively maintains the relevant dependence, and gives a fitting public transition plan. No promise, payment, sanction system or pre-creation consent is assumed.

**Decision:** If actual cooperation is needed and the fair-arrangement premises hold, R can have the same bounded cooperation reason. If G can instead provide equal care without the contested control and without relevant offsetting loss, the no-leverage limit applies too. Creation alone establishes neither case.

**Nonidentity control:** No comparison between existence and nonexistence determines the verdict. The subjects, goods and arrangements are actual within the conditional case. No right to indefinite maintenance is introduced.

## 13. Expanding power removes a claimed necessity

For a limited maintainer G1, synchronising all recipients is the only reasonable way to preserve the shared task. A stronger G2 can preserve the identical task and every relevant interest without controlling R's local schedule; no other legitimate loss is stipulated.

**Decision:** The reason requiring R's synchronisation with G1 does not automatically transport to G2. Increased capacity can reduce the justification for this restriction. This does not prove that every restriction disappears for an original source: shared target contradictions, effects on other agents and actual choice-worthy ends still require assessment.

## 14. Two organisers issue incompatible plans

G and H both have responsibilities and equal expertise. Their plans are separately adequate, but mixed uptake is dangerous. Neither has an independently warranted superior office.

**Decision:** Responsibility alone does not select a winner. Seek a common arrangement, identify actual prior fair coordination, or pause safely if possible. If urgent circumstances make one publicly usable plan the only protective course, follow the relevant practical reasons at that time. Do not infer permanent hierarchy from the emergency.

## 15. An independent decision allocation

R has validly entrusted a bounded, revisable procedural decision to G. The present instruction falls within that scope and no excluded purpose or applicable defeater occurs. The allocation itself is independently justified and known.

**Decision:** The allocation can supply settling force beyond the content of a particular plan. The due-care history may explain why this was a sensible entrustment; it did not alone perform the entrustment. This is a live route to stronger authority, not a claimed new proof of its validity in an actual original-source relation.

## 16. Manufactured sunk coordination costs

At the earlier selection point, G can discharge care through a less intrusive plan preserving all relevant interests at no greater legitimate cost. G unjustifiably rejects it solely to secure control, invests in a restrictive arrangement and recruits innocent recipients' reliance. At the later decision point, abandoning that arrangement would cause real loss; the earlier alternative is no longer costless.

**Decision:** Assess the choice at both points. The current loss may make R's temporary cooperation fitting for innocent others. It does not retrospectively legitimate G's restrictive selection or establish its authority. G's fitting remedy or reconfiguration duties still need assessment against what is now possible and the actual competing reasons. Temporary cooperation does not itself waive them. Do not demand a now-impossible restoration merely because an alternative was previously available.

**Discriminator:** Hold present costs and uptake fixed while changing whether they arose through an independently permissible arrangement or deliberate unjustified foreclosure. R's urgent action can remain the same; G's entitlement and remedial position differ. Present functionality therefore cannot be the sole evidence of fair formation.

## 17. Neither party has unrestricted selection rights

R has no entitlement to dictate every means of an adequate response. G invokes that fact as if it established its own unrestricted power to choose the response. No fair allocation or relevant frustration/burden has yet been shown.

**Decision:** Refuse the inference. The absence of R's unrestricted right is not the presence of G's selection right. The positive cooperation argument still needs independent fair formation, actual action-dependence and its stated burden/balance. Care alone creates no freestanding duty to accept unwanted help.

## Decisive challenge still open

Can a source-sensitive argument independently establish a rightful settling role for an identified G where the recipient remains competent and harmless dissent would otherwise be permissible? The evidence must explain why the source's choice itself changes duties, rather than cite only competence, dependency or outcomes. A role or source-nature account could do that work. This packet does not establish one and does not declare every such account impossible.
