### B. State and test one genuine institutional succession criterion

Do not repeat T9's nine distinctions. Select a narrow institutional type, for example a continuing reporting office constituted by a founding/delegation rule, and fix its object of continuity before formalising. Separate (i) preserved information, (ii) continued service capacity, (iii) current lawful role occupancy, and (iv) identity of the constituted institution. Give independent reasons for why its governing rule and actual transition are constitutive for this type. The governing rule is an openly defended semantic/normative premise, not a definition equating “genuine” with whatever the checker accepts.

Use paired histories with identical copied state and present outputs but different founding/succession histories; also use a valid replacement with changed realiser, and a valid institutional successor whose service is temporarily unavailable. Account for exclusive and shareable branches using T9's existing result rather than claiming it anew. In particular, a new successor's fresh assertions are not aliases of the predecessor's original acquisition merely because the office is the same.

**New result sought:** either a justified narrow continuation criterion with positive and negative applications, or a precise underdetermination result showing that the existing operational inputs can remain identical while the constitutive judgment differs, identifying the missing succession relation. The latter is an acceptable narrowed-open-problem outcome, provided its interpretation class is explicit.

**Stopping test:** resolve the bounded paired cases under the independently stated institution-type premises, preserve T9/T16's credit, and state the exact scope in which the criterion is sufficient/necessary or undecidable from current inputs. If constitutive rule/authority cannot be warranted from available evidence, stop at that exact limitation. No universal theory of personhood, all organisations or infinite renewal is required.

A is the most immediately ready continuation. B needs a deliberately chosen institution type and explicit semantic argument; it must not be presented as already solved by another support theorem. A historical divine-attestation dossier is a possible further investigation, not the smallest required repair.
