# Range appraisal: assigned final fifth of the received al-Safadiyya text

## Evidential scope

This appraisal rests on continuous inspection of the entire assigned electronic range SF-S003-L0368–SF-S004-L0063, not selected search hits: 17 windows, 439 lines and 23,795 whitespace words. A single additional line, SF-S003-L0367, was reread for the opening join. The task describes five contiguous ranges totalling 119,021 words; this reader's claim is limited to range 5. At the original electronic-reading stage no printed page or scan had been inspected. Any later bounded scan controls are a separate layer and cannot be imputed retrospectively to the entire range.

## Connected movement of the argument

The assigned opening is the continuation of a dispute about emanated forms. L0367 asks what sort of thing emanates; L0368–0373 then attacks an allocation in which one source supplies bodily movement while another supplies its necessarily concurrent resultant form. Mud is not an extra result that can be detached from this mixing of water and soil. The argument has an inseparability premise, then considers independent or mutually dependent agents. It rejects efficient circularity without rejecting all concomitance, including relations among effects and necessary divine attributes. This is already a warning that “dependence” cannot be used univocally throughout the range.

The critique of Ibn Sina's reduction of knowledge, will, power and life continues the same concern for distinctions with genuine explanatory work. Knowing the arrangement does not, just by being knowledge, make it exist; knowing follows the relevant known order; willing and capacity are separately needed. The positive claim at S003-L0387 that the goodness of the created order comes from its maker is embedded in this rejection of knowledge alone as sufficient producer. It is not a derivation that each sufficient or necessary condition possesses every perfection of its effect.

The argument then deliberately widens. It challenges both eternal emanation and theological claims that would make God inactive before an initial creation or after a total cosmic extinction. Scriptural cosmic transformation and revivification are treated as evidence. Variants of the primordial-state report are compared rather than simply combined. The difference between occurrence and possibility is explicit at S003-L0398. One independently necessary God with necessary attributes is distinguished from many independently necessary gods; stipulated definitions of simplicity are not allowed to settle the question in advance.

The large middle movement concerns the end of human life. The author grants civic, domestic and ethical goods but denies that they exhaust prophetic practice. Knowing, loving and worshipping must not collapse into one state. The same logical discipline that distinguishes divine knowledge from productive power distinguishes human cognition from pleasure and salvation. Knowledge is often a requisite; it is not invariably sufficient and is not identical to the resulting delight. Perception of a hostile object can be painful. The explicit refusal of the converse at S003-L0531 is especially clear: being given religious understanding is necessary for the intended good, but knowledge without its corresponding action does not establish that good.

This critique develops into an extended account of universals, particular realities and signification. There is nothing wrong with conceiving common features, taking general propositions as aids to particulars, or distinguishing mental conception from extra-mental existence. The error is reifying the universal as an externally universal thing, or substituting knowledge of the widest common term “existence” for knowledge of the determinate divine referent. The natural universal can be externally instantiated as a particular; the intellectual universal considered as universal is mental. The four registers of reality, cognition, language and writing make correspondence possible without identity of the registers. A definition and its inferential middle term belong first to a way of conceiving and knowing; they do not automatically reveal extra-mental constituents or intervening efficient causes.

Theological and devotional consequences are integral to this argument. The unseen is not defined as what can never be perceived: the author treats it as what is presently absent from the observer, with death and resurrection changing that relation. Scriptural angels are created, obedient agents rather than eternal producers of whole regions of existence. Intercession can be real while bounded by divine permission; it must not be modelled on an outsider coercing or altering an independent sovereign. The extensive discussions of demonological experiences, prayer, bodily and spiritual resurrection, and the beatific vision supply the concrete world with which he contrasts abstract philosophical substitutes.

The later chapters connect the unity of prophetic religion to diverse successive laws. They contain a substantial legal and historical excursus about abrogation, peace, war, treaties and jizya. These passages are not evidence for the narrow metaphysical fronts alone, but they matter to the book's continuous argument: practice must conform to revelation rather than being dispensed with by intellectual elites or replaced by invented rules. Conversely, the author records disagreement, conditions and exceptions; he insists on justice towards opponents and sometimes distinguishes an erroneous doctrine from its sincere, virtuous adherents. The concluding chapter rejects “becoming like God” as an exhaustive end and presents love, worship and reliance as the proper creaturely orientation.

## Findings for the specified analytical fronts

### A particular necessary bearer, not a mental universal

The strongest explicit contrast is S003-L0568–0579. “Necessary Existent”, “Lord of the worlds” and “Creator of the world” refer to a self-subsisting reality, whereas existence in its common universal use is not itself an external universal being. S003-L0402–0412 defends many concomitant attributes of the one necessary bearer without multiplying gods. S003-L0644 and L0659–0663 make divine particularity important even in an internal critique: a system that says God knows Himself and what proceeds from Him already admits particular objects of knowledge. It would be an error to read the rejection of mental universals as a rejection of shared meanings or all universal propositions.

### Source and perfection: three different scopes must remain separate

1. **Actual theological commitment:** S003-L0372, L0408, L0568 and S004-L0020 identify the one actual creator and lord of everything distinct from Him. This is about a determinate referent, not any object that happens to be called an origin, exemplar or condition.
2. **A bounded comparative hypothesis about the actual common Creator:** Such a hypothesis can ask what follows when the same actual Creator originates the compared creatures and possesses the relevant unqualified perfection. The range gives materials relevant to it: created order receives its goodness from its maker (S003-L0387); creaturely composition cannot establish divine composition (L0410–0412); and, if a qualified resemblance is genuinely possible for a human to produce, it is more possible for God (S004-L0046–0047). The last passage is an internal dilemma addressed to advocates of assimilation. These materials do not here amount to a complete formally general transfer rule. Establishing such a comparative hypothesis for a broader project requires its other premises and other ranges.
3. **Universal every-original/every-requisite SourceMode claims:** No theorem of this breadth is demonstrated by this range. “SourceMode” is the assignment's analytical label, not a technical term in this Arabic passage. A proximate natural power, condition of cognition, teaching exemplar, necessary concomitant, productive agent and the actual creator of all are repeatedly distinguished. L0054–0056 directly blocks undifferentiated transfer: knowledge, mercy and wisdom can commend humans; divinity, domination and pride do not; creaturely dependence, humility, need and worship can perfect humans but cannot qualify God. An argument must classify the predicate and the relation before making a comparative inference. Calling every useful feature a “perfection” does not erase this partition.

The range therefore offers both positive source/perfection material and strong scope restrictions. Neither selecting only its maximal divine claims nor selecting only its relative creaturely excellences would adequately represent it.

### Epistemic routes, evidence and attestation

S003-L0414–0419 explicitly denies that every knower requires the same inferential route or every auxiliary premise. Innate acknowledgement, concise proof and more elaborate doubt-removal can all have appropriate roles. S003-L0542 grants a proper use to universal knowledge. S003-L0574–0579 explains how the mental, spoken and written can correspond to reality. S003-L0649 prevents an inferential condition from being reified as an ontological intermediary. The mere occurrence of hujja is not a certificate of soundness, as S003-L0724–0726 explains.

The range also relies heavily on scriptural testimony, reported mass transmission, observed experience as asserted by the author, prayer and prophetic signs. These are not all the same evidential species. Its own qualifications on who has received a message, children and unreached people (S003-L0466–0475; S004-L0008) matter to any analysis of attestation. We have not independently authenticated the transmitted reports, verified the author's experiential claims or tested his attributions to opponents. The text can establish what is being argued, not by itself the external historical accuracy of every premise.

### Real agency, conditions and necessitation

S003-L0500–0503 and S004-L0023–0028 are direct opposition to denying natures, capacities, causes and purposive wisdom. The author rejects both bare necessary emanation and arbitrary undetermined selection. Given complete will and capacity, the selected outcome follows necessarily; this necessity is through the will. S003-L0643's fire example simultaneously recognises natural powers and limits unqualified predictions of their actual effects, since required conditions and possible impediments matter. S003-L0368–0373 and L0654–0658 distinguish necessary concurrence from independent efficient production. These passages require careful relation-typing; they do not license treating an enabling requisite as a self-sufficient producer or an ultimate creator.

## Wider material and contrary evidence retained

The continuous notes preserve the cosmic-transformation argument, resurrection taxonomy, affective psychology, devotional reports, historical polemics, philosophical-school disagreements, demonology, intercession, common prophetic religion, abrogation and treaty law. They also preserve concessions: good physics and mathematics (S003-L0639), legitimate agreement in neutral natural and mathematical matters (L0473), the utility of longer arguments for some hearers (L0418), genuine ethical reforms (L0421; L0481–0482), sincere error (S004-L0005), and unconditional justice towards opponents (L0013–0015). These qualifications are essential to interpreting the severity of the polemical register.

## Textual and interpretative limits

The electronic source contains potentially consequential defects, not merely spelling variants. Particularly urgent candidates include S003-L0420's unexpected negation, L0594's apparently self-subsisting accident, L0633's “العقل”, L0649's wording of the known reality, L0681's case description, L0691's description of a single law, L0734's binding-contract sentence, and the final divine/human predicate partition. The initial proposed control loci above identified received wording and interpretative concerns, not established emendations or an exhaustive errata list; the subsequent bounded scan supplement records what was actually checked.

The received end at S004-L0063 is a colophon calling the completed item a qaida and attributing it to Ibn Taymiyya. Its voice differs from the argument ending at L0062. It establishes the received electronic closure, not diplomatic printed completeness or authorial autograph wording. The opening join to L0367 remains part of the adjacent range and must be reconciled in the combined report. No new Lean result, formal proof, universal SourceMode verdict or whole-work reading is claimed.

## Subsequent control qualification

The later bounded scan supplement confirms the principal epistemic, agency and final predicate-partition passages while identifying consequential modern variants. It also preserves the modern editor’s substantive qualifications of the al-Ghazali criticism (printed 577 notes 2 and 5), unresolved catalogue/body discrepancies (substantial 23,38–39), and the retained L0594 wording with a contextually available “in his soul” reading. Consult that supplement rather than treating the initial control-candidate list as established emendations.
