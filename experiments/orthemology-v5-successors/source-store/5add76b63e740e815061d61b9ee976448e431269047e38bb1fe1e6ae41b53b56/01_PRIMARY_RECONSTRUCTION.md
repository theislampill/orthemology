## Argument reconstructed from the Arabic

1. **Explain actual production, not just orientation or an indispensable condition.** Darʾ VIII P396–422 argues that an object of imitation, a mover, or the producer of one indispensable accident is not thereby the producer of the substance and all its features. The complete maker of the world originates its entities, attributes and motions and sustains their existence. The text explicitly distinguishes efficient origination from final attraction. A first principle that merely attracts or accompanies the world has not yet been shown to be its creator.

2. **Originating power cannot be conferred in a vicious explanatory circle.** Minhaj II 100–120, III 259–276 and Darʾ IX 016:700–779 require a maker whose power is intrinsic, rather than bestowed by its own product or by a partner whose very power is bestowed in return. They reject prior causal circles, including circles in a constituent of the complete agent, not merely temporal loops. Minhaj II 106–109 expressly distinguishes this from harmless concomitance, including the necessarily coexistent attributes of one bearer. “Necessary through itself” is disambiguated from “not borne in a subject” and from “devoid of attributes.”

3. **Two actual exclusively complete makers cannot each alone produce the same numerical effect.** The premise concerns actual exclusive origination, not merely two agents each capable of the task in different circumstances. Shifa 50 explicitly admits joint production where neither can do it alone, where each can do it alone on substitution, and where both now cooperate despite each having the capacity to do it alone. Its impossible case is that each actually did the whole alone. Jami 94–96 makes the same part/contribution distinction.

4. **Ordinary cooperation leaves distinct contributions and enabling conditions.** Darʾ IX 016:621–650 and Shifa 143–151 argue that a human action presupposes far more than the actor's contribution: the agent and its organs, powers, receptive object, medium and absence of impediments. Real created efficacy is retained. It is not the creator's own intrinsic act merely because the creator creates the actor, power, action and conditions.

5. **The actual world is connected across its products and conditions.** Minhaj II 114–119 uses the human being's dependence on its material, air and nourishment, not only the heavens. Shifa 231–234 develops the organism/world comparison: distinct roles, reciprocal service, stable replacements, common order and wide coordination point to a common originating knowledge, power and wisdom. Sawaiq printed 232 similarly presents the connected order as evidence of one governor. The sources mean to infer one actual common source, not merely to stipulate that “one world” has “one cause.”

6. **The proposed anti-plurality bridge is stronger than observed coordination alone.** The anti-cooperation arguments require a genuine original not to receive either its constitutive power or its completion as source from another original. Independent products would then be independent in their relevant production, whereas products requiring each other expose their producers' need for each other. Required agreement is pressed as a limitation of independently efficacious power. If one dominates the other, the defeated being is not the independent divine maker. Minhaj III 271–275 argues that ordinary partnership ultimately has an external ordering/enabling ground, absent in a supposed pair of ultimate creators.

7. **The source supplies an especially important cooperation control.** Jami printed 179–182 distinguishes reciprocal production of the agents' powers from conditionally coordinated attainment. Printed 181 explicitly gives each cooperator its own power, awareness, intention and end, none acquired from the other, although attaining either end requires both acts. Printed 180 distinguishes joint strength and supplied instruments from illicit circular origination. This has to remain in view when interpreting the broader anti-cooperation argument. It cannot be discarded because it complicates that argument.

8. **Perfection strengthens the eligibility claim.** Minhaj II 100–120 argues that a genuinely necessary bearer does not receive an intrinsic perfection from an external benefactor; its intrinsic perfections belong to its own necessary reality. Creatively giving positive perfection supports the creator's superior possession of perfection. Sawaiq 002:1196–1210 and 1335–1348 argue that strictly highest excellence and unrestricted dominance cannot be possessed by two peers. These are arguments about the positive character appropriate to the source, not only formal antiregress arguments.

9. **Perfection does not mean the eternal simultaneity of every product.** Shifa 205–209 distinguishes perfection appropriate to a time from impossible timeless possession of every successive effect. Shifa 219–221 makes wisdom and purposive expression of powers central while denying that the created outcome supplies an externally acquired constitutive perfection. Necessary efficacy and determinate token effects therefore require careful separation.

10. **Efficient and final unity are related but distinct.** Jami 87–100 and 179–182 distinguish the creator from the ultimate object of worship/love. Jami 97 explicitly observes that the bare contrary-wills scenario does not by itself exclude harmonious cooperation. Shifa 304–307 argues from intentional life, final ends and fitra to a determinate divine object; it is not an independent observed-causal demonstration that an arbitrary plural original basis is impossible. Sawaiq printed 232 reads الغاية والإلهية, with a differing electronic reading recorded, so the final-cause dimension must not be erased.
