### T12-CONCENTRATION — Finite-additive Gaussian anti-concentration

For bounded interval I of length L, P(Y+Z∈I)≤L/√(2πv); the sharper bound is 2Φ(L/(2√v))−1. Hence fresh Gaussian blocks of arbitrarily large variance force each bounded terminal interval to have probability zero.

**Model and input:** Foundational article §§3–5; independent review §§1–2, including interval endpoints, sum-event measurability and ordinary real-valued probability.

**Assumptions:**

- One normalized nonnegative ordinary real-valued finitely additive probability on an event algebra containing every used interval, tail, rectangle and sum event.
- Y and Z are rectangle-independent at the stated event scope; Z has an ordinary centred Gaussian marginal with variance v>0.
- For history application, all arbitrarily long finite blocks bind the same terminal state and law; their Gaussian variances diverge.

**Limits:**

- Tightness of Y and joint countable additivity are not required; the Gaussian marginal supplies the tail estimate.
- Mutual iid noise does not imply independence of the earlier state from its future innovation block.
- The ordinary real limit step is not automatically transferable to non-Archimedean values.
