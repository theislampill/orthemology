# Addendum: consolidation of powers and eligibility for independence

8 October 2026 UTC, following review of the completed efficacy-warrant assessment. The initial assessment and its manifest are preserved unchanged. This is a narrow correction of an omission in the reconstruction, not a new source discovery or a broad research restart.

## What was missing

`WARRANT_ASSESSMENT.md` reported the complete reading of Majmu20:174–184 and cited section2032 L013 for the final effect-to-inhering-action transfer. It did not expressly reconstruct the modal concentration argument in L009–013 (20:178–181). Mentioning inseparable mixed effects at L008 did not supply that missing argument. The reviewer’s finding is correct.

The retained primary source is `../sources/majmu20-item2032.txt`, L009–013, with the original HTML alongside it. [Primary electronic section](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/22/2032/). This addendum directly reread those five paragraphs only; it adds no new whole-source reading credit.

## The argument that must be credited

After taking cooperative need and inability to produce alone from the preceding argument, the source asks whether each partner can receive the capacity for independent production. It rejects impossibility: jointly realisable work does not become intrinsically impossible because one agent, rather than two, produces it. Considering the agent-side possibility, the powers present in the two can hypothetically be present together in one bearer; concentration would not reduce their productive adequacy and could improve it. The source then infers that each contributor is eligible for a more complete independent capacity. Given their stipulated inability to complete themselves, their current deficiency would require external completion, contradicting necessity through themselves.

This is an argument about possible concentration of compatible productive powers and eligible perfection. It is not merely “one effect must have one cause.” It is not a proposed temporal operation in which two necessary beings physically merge. Nor is it restricted to cooperative work divided into separable pieces.

## Three conclusions that must remain distinct

1. **Work-capacity conclusion:** the source-neutral work achievable by the joint powers can be achieved by those powers concentrated in one bearer, without reduced productive adequacy.
2. **Bearer-eligibility conclusion:** each particular existing contributor is eligible for that more complete independent power. This is a de re application, not merely the possibility of some consolidated bearer or power profile.
3. **End-preservation conclusion:** all ends that the two existing contributors value are also satisfied by that consolidated production.

The source expressly advances1 and2 in the inspected passage. It does not need3 to establish that the external work is producible by one. An end requiring two numerically distinct original participants may fail under consolidation even though every relevant created product is preserved. Such failure changes satisfaction of that relational end; it does not establish that the consolidated productive power is smaller or that the external work requires two producers.

The original agents’ distinct intrinsic acts also must not be silently included as created outputs in order to make the external work intrinsically plurality-dependent. The established distinction between intrinsic creating and created effect remains in force.

## Positive force and remaining application

The concentration argument gives the affirmative case more than an appeal to exclusive authorship. It offers a reason to regard independent productive adequacy as a realisable improvement: bring the already available, compatible resources under one bearer, and their work-capacity need not decrease. The ordinary advantages of integrated capacities make that intelligible. The source-neutral product is held fixed while the distribution of productive resources is varied.

Two boundaries remain:

- The move from a possible consolidated power-profile to each existing contributor’s eligibility is substantive. It requires that the alleged perfection can belong to that bearer; possibility of some other bearer possessing it is insufficient. Merely declaring a rigid plurality-sensitive end does not answer this point, but neither does the label “original” establish the eligibility premise.
- The argument begins at L009 by importing the earlier claim that genuine cooperation makes each partner unable to produce the work alone. A rival whose standing solo constructors are already admitted does not gain or lose those constructors through the concentration argument. Its live dispute remains whether actual joint production demonstrates a deficiency in presently exercised original efficacy. Concentration alone does not independently prove the earlier nonabsorption or actual-sharing-to-insufficiency transition.

Thus the modern refined rival can largely bypass the need to *add* solo work-capacity while still failing the source’s standard of actual exhaustive original provision. Its declared solo constructor is not a certificate of the source’s complete particular willing or unborrowed actual production. Conversely, pointing out a participant-sensitive good does not refute concentration of work-capacity. Whether a source-dependent relationship is an eligible wise end, or makes the bearer’s actual efficacy externally completed, needs the distinct perfection and willing arguments already under assessment.

## Corrected disposition

The initial assessment should be read with this affirmative argument restored. The source offers both an actual-efficacy argument and a modal concentration/eligible-perfection argument. The surviving questions are their precise application to the proposed originals and their actual undertaking, not whether the source simply assumed one-effect/one-cause or overlooked harmonious cooperation.

No new countermodel, metaphysical possibility certificate, or universal exclusion theorem follows from this correction. It strengthens the completeness of the reconstruction while preserving the qualified judgement and the open T20 owner-review boundary.
