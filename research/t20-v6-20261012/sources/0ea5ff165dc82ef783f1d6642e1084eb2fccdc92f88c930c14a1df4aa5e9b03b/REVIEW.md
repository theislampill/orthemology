# Independent review of the wise reason application

10 October 2026 UTC. Scoped peer review of the received report in `wise-reason-asymmetry-20261010`. The reviewer did not author that report or participate in the root fidelity check. This is independent assessment of the same received evidence, not an independent historical witness, audit closure, or a fresh metaphysical proof.

## Verdict

**The narrow application is supported by the received sources and its inferential composition is sound within the expressly inherited premises. No essential author correction is required.** It earns credit for an explicit content-sensitive exclusion and for correcting a transport between differently typed objects of love. The exclusion concerns a divine punitive determination that imputes A's wrongdoing as B's guilt, although B neither acquired nor culpably participated in it, and punishes B on that basis alone.

There are two important limits on the credit. First, the source already explicitly denies this kind of punishment. The own-act efficacy account explains how the exclusion belongs to wise voluntary agency; it is not logically needed to discover a prohibition absent from the culpability passage. Second, this is a determinate constraint on a class of proposed original actions. It does not identify a positively selected act, its occasion, the origin of a particular representation, a unique entire history, or a census of volition-tokens. The author report already preserves these limits. Neither limitation cancels the useful application.

## The proposition actually tested

Let T describe God's own punitive treatment of B with these features:

1. A performed wrongdoing d.
2. B did not perform, acquire, or culpably participate in d.
3. T imputes d to B as B's culpable deed and punishes B on that basis alone.

These stipulations characterize a proposed act and its asserted basis without including either its occurrence or its divine approval. They do not assert that a complete possible world containing T has been established. Nor is B required to be faultless in every unrelated respect: punishment grounded in a different wrong by B would not be punishment on A's guilt alone.

The conclusion is conditional on the inherited account: **T is not an eligible wise divine punitive determination.** The report does not conclude that all suffering of a nonculpable person is excluded, that persons never suffer consequences of others' actions, or that every punishment without the displayed defect is justified.

## Attribution and the exact source composition

### The classification is independently specified

Miftāḥ 1123–1124 distinguishes competing positions about divine obligation and prohibition. Its endorsed middle position is not the bare-ownership position. Within the subsequent three interpretations of injustice, the preferred third interpretation is explicitly introduced at 1128 and continued at 1129. The received wording says that another's bad deeds are not loaded onto the servant and that the servant is not punished for what his hands did not acquire and in which he did not strive. The latter clause supplies the acquisition/participation control; the report's “culpably” is a careful interpretive restriction, not a claim that this precise analytical vocabulary occurs in the Arabic.

The surrounding argument confirms the point rather than weakening it. At 1129 the author says the denied injustice is punishment without the recipient's offence; at 1130 he says the servant is not punished without his own wrongdoing; at 1131 he invokes the separation of one person's burden from another's and identifies the relevant punishment with their sins and desert. At 1132–1134 he rejects capacity and ownership alone as the explanation of punitive justice. The text's proposed reconciliation of universal desert and mercy does not authorize punishment on somebody else's guilt alone.

This is more informative than applying the word “injustice” to an unspecified disliked action. The recipient, acquisition, participation, and punitive basis identify a relation whose description survives whether or not it occurs. Normative and disclosed premises remain necessary to classify it as unjust; the report does not claim a norm-free deduction.

### The aversion bridge is expressly present

The strongest exact reconstruction is:

- The endorsed account classifies T under the injustice that God prohibits for Himself and denies willing or doing, rather than a creature's wrongdoing merely created by God.
- At 1138, self-prohibition is expressly said to include hatred/aversion and nonperformance.
- At 1139, love and aversion concerning God's own act entail, respectively, its occurrence and exclusion. The source immediately contrasts this with love and aversion concerning the creature's act.
- Thus the rejection of T is an intrinsic practical restriction compatible with voluntary production and standing power.

The first two bullets are essential. One cannot start with any defective aspect of an outcome, call that “own-act aversion,” and then invoke the last entailment. For T, the independently endorsed self-prohibition supplies the stronger antecedent. The report has that warrant in its continuous discussion; an explicit P1/P2/P3 notation is optional, not a required repair.

The own-act typing is also substantive. A human's unjust punishment of B, although created within divine provision, would remain a creaturely action under the 1139–1140 distinction. That case cannot be substituted for T. Here 1129 directly discusses God's punishing them, using “تعذيبه لهم”, and identifies the recipient's guilt as its basis. T concerns that own punitive determination, not every created instance of somebody punishing somebody else. No numerical identity between an intrinsic act and its external effect is needed.

Miftāḥ 1138 initially speaks of love for what God wills to do. In isolation that wording might import executive willing into the antecedent. The unqualified own-act/creaturely-act distinction at 1139 and its restatement at 1140 prevent relying solely on that compressed phrase. The text also states that loving a creature's obedience is not the same thing as loving the own act of enabling that obedience. The report's corrected transport is faithful to this precise distinction.

### Practical explanation is not an external producer

At 969–970, known perfection and defect explain fitting love and aversion; the language at 970 expressly attributes the contrasted attitudes to the relevant perfection and defect. The return inference at 971 from being loved to perfection does not, by itself, erase that explanatory direction. Reciprocal evidential implications do not demonstrate reciprocal efficient production.

Shifāʾ e176–177 prevents replacing willing with mere capacity and knowledge of benefit. E189–190 distinguishes final-causal explanation from the causes through which an act is performed. E207 denies that the relevant wisdom is a separately supplying other. E209 gives the apprehended fitting end an operative role in rational willing. Majmūʿ 16:304–305 locates intended differentiators in knowledge and conception while saying that the cognitive-volitional representation itself originates through willing and power. Together these controls support the report's practical/productive distinction without eliminating its unresolved representation-origin obligation.

## Stronger rival tests

### Greater goods and disliked aspects

The strongest nearby challenge is not simply that creatures commit disliked acts. Shifāʾ e180 expressly distinguishes an act considered in itself from what it leads to. E215 says that what is disliked in itself can be performed for the sake of what is loved. Miftāḥ 1117–1118 likewise allows opposed relevant features in one action.

This defeats an unrestricted inference from any negative feature to nonoccurrence. It does not defeat the tested inference. T is placed under the endorsed own self-prohibition at 1128–1131 and 1138, not merely under a subordinate disvalue that might be outweighed. A claimed greater good that leaves the entire T description intact would challenge that source premise; it cannot be silently inserted as a counterexample accepted by the same account. If it instead changes T into nonpunitive harm or punishment for B's own participation, it changes the target.

The e215 control should remain explicit in future summaries of the result. The aversion needed here is the source's operative aversion to this own punitive determination, not dislike of an isolated component. This is an interpretive guard already supported by the report's respect distinctions, not a newly invented extra doctrine.

### Punishment, consequence, and liability acquired through another act

The exclusion concerns punitive guilt attribution, not pain as such. A's action may cause B material loss or suffering without d being imputed as B's guilt. Likewise, a case in which B's own conduct establishes a separate liability cannot be counted as punishment on A's guilt alone. Merely using the language of transferred burdens or later redress would not establish a counterexample without checking that its basis really matches T. This review makes no new claim about any particular historical doctrine of redress.

The absence of participation is a necessary feature of this selected test, not an exhaustive account of punitive justice. The report properly leaves other requirements open. Miftāḥ 970–971 itself distinguishes a blame/punishment-grounding cause from completed conditions for punishment, including communicated revelation. The application does not erase that distinction.

### Standing power and the meaning of exclusion

Miftāḥ 1130–1131 says the acts it excludes remain within power, while praising their voluntary rejection. At 1138 it again preserves power in the very sentence excluding occurrence through aversion. The conclusion is therefore not incapacity to bring about a bare physical configuration, nor proof that T is a logical contradiction. It is exclusion relative to the endorsed divine character, self-prohibition, and own practical determination. The report accurately retains that modal scope.

### Retrospective certification and circularity

There is no required step “T did not occur, therefore T is unjust, therefore T did not occur.” The culpability relation is specified independently and the source explicitly supplies its evaluation and its relation to self-prohibition. Nor does the report infer that an already occurring act was sufficiently explained merely because it must have been loved.

There is, however, deliberate doctrinal dependence: the source's normative assessment and self-disclosure are premises, not conclusions newly proved by this local application. Agreement of passages does not establish their metaphysical truth. The report discloses this dependency and inherits, rather than reopens, its wider appraisal.

Moreover, 1128–1131 already directly deny T. If the new composition were advertised as the first proof that T is excluded, its novelty would be overstated and the efficacy premise would be inferentially redundant. The actual report calls its gain an application and a corrected transport. At that scope the composition usefully displays why content-sensitive practical exclusion is stronger than an empty label while remaining compatible with productive agency.

### A purported complete selector

The negative result discriminates: it excludes proposed determinations meeting T. It does not positively choose between all alternatives that escape T. Nor does a general relational schema identify a particular new volition-token or explain its particular representation. Those would be stronger targets. Demanding their completion before allowing any credit to the exclusion would alter the present proposition; crediting them to this exclusion would overstate it. The report does neither.

## Novelty and constructive credit

The inherited Miftāḥ continuous notes already record both the transferred-guilt criterion at 1125–1132 and own-act love/aversion efficacy at 1138–1141, including a bounded reread of the latter. The inherited CP20 report already separates normative justification, actual productive willing, and the complete particular determining basis. The current particular-determination and representation reports already reject a selected-content placeholder as a complete explanation.

Accordingly, the present increment is methodological execution within this research sequence:

1. Correcting the attempted transfer from loved-but-unrealized creaturely action to inefficacy of own-act love.
2. Explicitly applying the inherited culpability criterion to a fixed act–recipient–basis description and recording when the application fails.
3. Connecting that evaluation with the source's self-prohibition and efficacious own-act aversion while preserving power and actual willing.

This merits constructive credit as a transparent, source-bound inference/application. It is not first discovery of the doctrine, an additional historical witness, an empirical result, a general metaphysical theorem, or a new complete selector. The author report's stated novelty is appropriately limited. The phrase “new combination” should continue to mean this executed explanatory composition, since the ingredients and even the prohibition itself were already inherited.
