## General geometric theorem

File: lean/LocalRigidity.lean

Main theorem: LocalRigidity.local_common_repair_iff

- Common convex-hull-valued weak repairs along a positive sequence epsilon_n->0 exist iff one tangent vector z has residual h−z orthogonal to the tangent space under EVERY form
- The right-hand side is the simultaneous orthogonal-projection condition, in basis-free normal-equation form
- The constructive implication supplies a common STRICT repair ray for all sufficiently small positive epsilon
- A separate kernel theorem proves uniqueness of the tangent normal-equation solution

The proof includes gain expansion, energy/barycentric averaging, compactness extraction of a normalized-repair subsequence, first-order limits, positive-weight zero forcing, positive-definite-to-coercive conversion, and a constructive positive-weight convex-hull ray. Neither convergence of normalized repairs nor coercivity is assumed in the final iff theorem.

- lean/SmoothRigidity.lean: expected-regret identity, actual finite Bregman score derivative, bounded normalized repairs, compactness and the varying-direction derivative limit. Main target: SmoothRigidity.smooth_common_repair_hessian_necessary

Main target: BregmanProjection.universal_common_repair_iff_affine

The final smooth iff is for TWO coordinate-additive generator families on a genuine categorical simplex with at least three outcomes. Common repairs must exist near EVERY interior coherent point along the same fixed coordinate direction, with positive perturbation sizes converging to zero. Repairs are interior coherent vectors, and they weakly improve every truth vertex under both scores. The conclusion is one common positive scalar a and coordinate-specific affine terms, with generator equality on the closed interval [0,1]. The converse is proved, rather than left as an assumed projection oracle.

Score assumptions are finite continuous f on [0,1], explicit first and second derivative witnesses on (0,1), and continuous strictly positive second derivative there. No bounded endpoint derivative, global Hessian lower bound, already-established Hessian agreement, convergence of repairs or O(epsilon) repair estimate is assumed. The formal repair antecedent does not require every early perturbed input to stay in [0,1]; since it approaches interior p it eventually does. The converse constructs inputs and repairs that are interior at every sequence index.

## Sublinear-tolerance robustness

File: lean/SmoothRobustness.lean

Source SHA-256: e70adf24f9cc824e654cf5b4be6023d5bfc15410d2c15cc24143e98e5de809e4

Main targets:

- SmoothRobustness.positive_derivative_coercive
- SmoothRobustness.normalized_approximate_repairs_bounded
- SmoothRobustness.approximate_common_repair_hessian_necessary
- SmoothRobustness.universal_approximate_common_repair_iff_affine

S7 is now kernel-checked. The statewise losses may worsen by one nonnegative additive tolerance eta_n, provided eta_n/epsilon_n tends to zero. The arbitrary-family local theorem forces a shared Hessian-normal repair direction. For at least three genuine categorical outcomes, the full global iff again identifies positive-scale/affine generator equivalence.

The load-bearing estimate is derived, not assumed. Barycentric averaging gives vanishing regret and hence c_n -> p, but would by itself yield only a square-root bound. The proof instead shows the sum of positive first-order state derivatives is uniformly coercive on the tangent unit sphere. Uniform small derivative remainders are absorbed in this all-state bound, proving ||c_n-p|| = O(epsilon_n) for sublinear tolerance. Compactness and the divided approximate comparisons then establish the common normal equations. Thus tolerances strictly between quadratic and linear scale are genuinely covered.

Both generators are fixed before quantifying over every interior p. The tolerance and repair sequences may vary with p. Tolerance is measured in the declared loss units; it is not a failure probability. A single common tolerance for the two fixed scores is harmlessly restrictive: two nonnegative sublinear tolerances can be enlarged to their pointwise maximum. The theorem does not cover order-epsilon tolerances or changing generators across coherent contexts.
