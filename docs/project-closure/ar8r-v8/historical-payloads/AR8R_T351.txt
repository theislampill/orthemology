Stable pathway restoration requires absence of target error and absence of every unchanged error-effective path for every admissible case. The requirement fails. The three-way formulation is the contrapositive: if stable restoration holds, no such untouched path remains. ∎

Dominator corollary

If a deformation node d dominates t in the active flowgraph for every admissible case—every relevant source-to-target route passes through d—then an output-local correction that leaves d and all downstream error-effective routes unchanged cannot certify stable restoration. A repair may occur at d, at the routing rule, or at a downstream cut intersecting all d⇝t paths; the theorem does not require repair at d itself.

SCC corollary

If a self-sealing deformation occupies a strongly connected component K, contract G to its condensation DAG. Any stable pathway restoration must separate the condensation node [K] from the target or change the semantics inside K. This links the theorem to the previously admitted SCC/root-support results without treating SCC quotienting as source truth or restoration by itself.

What this establishes

A current correct output cannot certify stable restoration while a registered, error-effective deformation path remains untouched.

A load-bearing metaorthemic intervention is naturally represented as an effective cut or semantic rewrite over all active deformation-to-error paths.

The noetic draft's claim that downstream sentence correction can leave the governing structure unchanged receives an exact finite graph condition.

What it does not establish

that the registered graph is complete;

that its deformation labels are true;

that its target is sound fiṭrah;

that a path cut is sufficient without sound local repair and no-new-path conditions;

that human interior recognition or divine guidance has occurred;

novelty of graph cuts, dominators, or path hitting.

Countermodel to an overstrong formulation

The stronger claim “stable restoration must alter the deformation node itself” is false. Put a sound decoder c downstream of the deformation and route every deformation-to-target path through c. If c correctly neutralizes every possible deformation signal for all x∈X, stable target behavior can be restored without changing the upstream deformation node. What is necessary is effective path interception or semantic rerouting, not intervention at one privileged location.
