# Causal adequacy scope and subjecthood

## A scope condition in the strongest faculty argument

The companion's §3.3 gives a strengthened causal-adequacy premise: the ground of a genuine ordering of faculties to objective targets must know the targets and fitting means and be capable of instituting their fit. It then describes the conditional conclusion as knowledge of every concrete reality and every true, fitting or possible relation. The latter scope requires an additional premise.

Let F be the relevant faculty family, let T(f) be the targets of faculty f, and suppose a single ground g institutes the fit for every f in F. A target-relative adequacy principle yields, at most,

    For every t in the union of T(f), g knows t and the relevant means.

To conclude knowledge of every member of an unrestricted reality/possibility domain R, one needs R to be covered by that target union, or a separate principle directly extending g's adequacy to every member of R. The mere existence of some irreducibly truth-directed faculties supplies neither.

A two-target counterexample makes the point. F contains one faculty discriminating target r0. Its designer g knows r0 and the means needed to discriminate it. Reality also contains r1, which no faculty in F targets. The local adequacy premise holds while g need not know r1. Adding more successful faculties does not establish unlimited coverage; it only enlarges the warranted target union.

This is a proposed scope clarification for historical philosophical prose, not a change to repository status. The current owner already declines the unqualified metaphysical ascent, and Deep K's relevant-end/means/circumstances indexing avoids the overextension. A separately defended global argument from *all* objective orthability might supply a wider antecedent, but it would be a different premise from the existence of the observed faculties. ## Adequacy must not be weakened in the premise and strengthened in use

There are at least three different causal-adequacy theses:

1. A cause has sufficient power, in the relevant conditions, to produce the effect
2. A cause contains the effect's relevant perfection formally or in an eminent/virtual way
3. A cause itself knows the content known by the effect and intentionally orders it to the fitting end

The third can support an intellectual conclusion. The first is compatible with an impersonal powers account. The second requires its own theory of perfection and of what eminent possession amounts to in this case.

Aquinas's primary discussion of perfections in effects explicitly distinguishes same-form possession from eminent possession and virtual pre-existence. It therefore should not be reconstructed as the crude rule that a cause must have every property its effect has in exactly the same way. [*Summa Theologiae* I, q. 4, a. 2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1004.htm)

This is not a criticism of the complete Thomistic argument, nor a claim that the Atharī qiyās rule is the same rule. It isolates a question for Orthemology's proposed T2: why is nonmental power to produce knowers inadequate, while adequate “eminent” possession must include actual knowledge at the ground's own locus? The answer may appeal to further metaphysics, but causal adequacy understood merely as causal sufficiency cannot settle it.

Likewise, the Fifth Way explicitly adds the claim that nonintelligent end-directed activity requires intelligent direction. That premise is not derived simply by observing regular successful behavior. [*Summa Theologiae* I, q. 2, a. 3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1002.htm)

The useful positive discipline is to retain the strong premise in full and defend it. Diluting it to an uncontroversial power-to-produce premise and then recovering a knowing selector at the conclusion would lose the argument's real content.

## What does the one intellect rival actually concede

Deep CG grants an intellectual/representational ground and separately withholds de se ownership, personality, because-of-fittingness, Wisdom and actual Speech. It is explicit that its conclusion is limited to its stipulated class. [Deep CG semantic reissue](https://github.com/theislampill/orthemology/blob/03e2604dfcef278b01c60d19b05370fcec4194b6/docs/project-closure/ar8r-v11/post-merge-proposals/pmr007-deep-bw-cj-semantic-reissue/source-files/PROPOSAL/theorems/PMR-007_DEEP_CG_PMR-007-SICM-1_REISSUE_V1.md)

For substantive metaphysics, its concession must be disambiguated. The following form an ascending set of questions, not a set of synonyms:

- Does a mechanism carry and use a semantic representation?
- Does an actual state have original intentionality?
- Is there intellectual understanding of that content?
- Is there an experiential subject of the state?
- Is there explicit or implicit first-person ownership?
- Is there practical agency involving the selection of acts for reasons?
- Is there a person in the relevant metaphysical or moral sense?
- Is there knowledge, virtue or stable Wisdom?

A substantive theory may validate some adjacent implications. For example, if a theory identifies original intellectual understanding with a subject's actual understanding, a model cannot concede that understanding while denying any subject whatsoever simply by assigning different formal flags. Conversely, being the subject of a thought need not include reflective self-identification, practical control or morally significant personhood under every theory.

This creates a fair burden on both sides. The positive argument must state which notion it has obtained. The R5 defender must show that the withheld target remains coherent under that very notion, not switch “intellect” back to a functional register after having appeared to concede original understanding.

The bridge ledger's B8 bundles agency or personality. An argument reaching an intellectual subject may therefore advance one interpretation of subjecthood while leaving agency, choice, stable Wisdom and revealed identity open. Neither calling this “no advance” nor announcing full personal agency would be accurate.

## A real local discriminator and its limit

First-person evidence of an occurrent judgment or experience can supply a basis for treating that local case as involving a subject that is not merely a transcript pattern. This is a philosophical evidential claim about the local case, not a general behavioral decision procedure. It shows why phenotype-matching Boolean expansions alone do not settle the adequacy of a psychological model.

But the local evidence identifies the subject of that occurrence. It does not establish that the fundamental cause or constitutive basis of the subject has the same first-person episode. An enriched R5 can therefore acknowledge actual human subjects and their original intentionality while leaving its ultimate powers bearer impersonal. To defeat that enriched rival, the positive route still needs a defended transfer to the fundamental locus.

This yields a sharper pair of questions:

1. What does genuine understanding force us to accept about its **local bearer**?
2. Which additional grounding principle makes that property belong to the **fundamental ground itself**?

The first may have stronger direct evidence than the second. Keeping them separate permits genuine progress on local subjecthood without pretending it automatically completes a natural-theological ascent.
