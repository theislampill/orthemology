## The delayed-delivery retry trap

The new native controls establish a real operational distinction:

1. The actor can hold the new repair certificate while intact roots still have
   old policies. Its proposals succeed but the actual batch lands zero times.
2. Unconditional cleanup nevertheless tombstones those exact repair envelopes
   at intact roots. In this two-batch sequence their nonces are 5 through 8.
3. Delivering the authentic certificate restores root policy admission but
   preserves the tombstones. Retrying with the same actor/action regenerates
   the old envelopes and still lands zero times with the plant unchanged.
4. An actor nonce of 8 produces fresh envelopes 9 through 12 and completes.
5. Alternatively, keeping actor nonce 4 and using a different source-admitted
   command with leaseEnd 91 instead of 90 also completes under ExactReady.
   The original high-water predicate remains false.

The reviewer independently proves the same mechanism from a genuinely reachable
accepted native pre-delivery prefix, with old trial nonces 2 through 5 in that
separate fixture. After delivery, local admission is explicitly true while
re-preparing and attempting the old fourth envelope fails. A fresh nonce-5
batch succeeds, and the post-delivery state used for these checks is proved
genuinely aligned and reachable. The fixture numbering differs intentionally; the
complete-envelope identity mechanism is the same.

The source returns a World and records, not a new Actor. Within-batch increments
do not automatically advance a later caller's actor. The changed-command
control is a mathematical input variation within the source grant, not an
implemented renewal service or authority to modify a real authorization.

One wording qualification is required when using the frozen retry note:
“the necessary high-water bound” means the premise needed to apply the
conservative recovery theorem. It is not a necessary condition for recovery
or progress. The author confirmed this interpretation, and the adjacent
same-nonce control formally rules out the broader reading. Reader-facing
summaries should use “the conservative theorem's sufficient high-water premise.”
