The criticism is a fibre-and-defect argument. Let \(D\) be the set of possible production-and-transmission histories and let \(q\) be exact human authorship plus exact original wording. A finite observable panel \(r=\rho_A\) preserves \(q\) only if \(r(d)=r(d')\) implies \(q(d)=q(d')\). If two histories match every retained observable yet differ on \(q\), the fibre criterion fails, the exact defect is positive (up to \(1/2\) for a binary claim), and the inquiry sits outside the continuation span of the panel. Surviving manuscripts, citation loci, internal features, and coherence relations are the panel; written isnads and reports of mass memorization count inside it when they survive. The past recitations themselves are present only through those traces.

It applies where the enlarged panel still leaves distinct histories. The New Testament does: Gospels and epistles are anonymous or weakly attributed, written decades after the events, and the manuscript tradition contains thousands of variants with no early mass-parallel control that forces a single autograph. Solitary hadiths do: a report carried by one or a few chains (ahad) can match the same isnad panel under paraphrasing, substitution, or back-projection, so exact original wording is not constant on the fibres. The same pattern holds for the Hebrew Bible across its divergent manuscript families and for texts whose earliest witnesses sit centuries after composition with non-concurrent transmission.

It does not apply in the same way to the Quran on the tawatur account. The panel there includes the Uthmanic codices standardized within about twenty years, the written record of collection from both scraps and memories after Yamama, and concurrent mass memorization by many huffaz across regions, with reciters sent alongside the codices. Those observables are treated as jointly spanning the received wording, so \(q\) is constant on the fibres of that panel and the defect for the standardized text is zero. The same escape is claimed for verbal mutawatir hadiths whose independent chains at each generation are numerous enough that agreement on the retained reports forces the wording, and for modern works whose autograph, printer’s copy, and publication record sit inside the panel. The residual question the model still allows is only whether any pre-standard companion reading remains underdetermined once those oral reports are included—not whether the received text itself reduces to manuscripts alone.

Yes. The criticism is sound as a conditional claim about finite observables.

A finite panel of surviving traces preserves exact authorship and original wording only if every pair of histories that match the panel also match on that target. When two histories agree on every retained observable—manuscripts, citation loci, written isnads, reports of memorization, internal features—yet differ on exact wording or authorship, the fibre criterion fails, the exact defect is positive, and the inquiry is not continuation-complete.
