# Review of existential determination and necessary particular sourcehood

The argument earns a limited source-bound research increment. It tests a more explicit rationale for complete determination while leaving the necessary-source conclusion open. Its value lies in that test, rather than a new proof of necessity or a proof that a brute alternative cosmos is possible.

The affirmative position receives a fair hearing. An account of what a bearer could do differs from an account of an actual occurrence. A factor may favour an outcome while leaving the precise contrast in question unsettled. The demand for a complete existential explanation therefore has substantive content. The argument does not reduce it to a preference for simplicity, an equal-probability calculation or an objection to efficient self-production alone.

Its further claim still requires warrant: every residual existential contrast must be settled, including the whole actuality of an original bearer. A reason to seek that explanation is not yet an assurance that reality supplies it. Equally, failure to establish the assurance does not establish its falsity. A warranted intrinsic nature excluding absence could give the affirmative a real explanatory advantage.

The strongest critic considered here preserves the stipulated productive target. It grants one particular original bearer, proper unborrowed productive exercise, and complete coverage of the relevant actual contributors and receiving modes. It does not quietly retreat to power sufficient for an endpoint. What it withholds is an exhaustive explanation of the original bearer's own unreceived existence. That challenges the existential premise, while retaining the productive premise for the test.

The modal distinctions are sound. Necessitation by a complete determinant when that determinant obtains does not establish its existence in every possibility. Necessary occupation of a source role does not establish the necessary existence of its present occupant. A critic may identify those missing steps without claiming that total absence is metaphysically possible. A positive brute ontology retains its own substantial warrant burden.

The treatment of noncausal explanation is also appropriately bounded. A concrete bearer whose whole existence is explained by another ground is received under the broad existential relation. Such a proposal challenges the identification of efficient originality with broader autonomy; it does not preserve broad unreceivedness while refuting its consequences. Conversely, an objection to efficient self-production cannot automatically dismiss constitutive explanation.

The weaker actuality argument is kept separate. An uninstantiated specification supplies no actual effect, but an active infinite order proposes actual suppliers throughout. It must be assessed at that strength. No temporal first event, finite regress or traversal assumption is needed for the paper's result.

Direct rational recognition remains an admissible warrant route. The issue is its content: recognition of local dependence, an actual ultimate bearer and unconditional intrinsic sufficiency are different claims. The argument does not reject basic rational knowledge merely because it is disputed, unmeasured or unformalised.

Most of these distinctions were already established in the preceding research. The present contribution is the newly source-grounded test of the determination rationale, including the treatment of a merely favouring account and the separate weaker route. It does not acquire new existential warrant by repeating inherited distinctions.

The accepted scope is therefore precise. Original productive completion, identification of a particular bearer, adequate nonbrute existential explanation and unconditional intrinsic sufficiency remain separate burdens. The inherited conditional uniqueness result and respect-specific intentional knowledge, will and power remain intact. Nothing here establishes essential truthfulness or an operational repair authority.
