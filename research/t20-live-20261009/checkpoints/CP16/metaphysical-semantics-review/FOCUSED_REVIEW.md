## 2. Fact-partlessness: valid result, conditional application

### What the proof establishes

For a fact q explaining a total plurality F, every fact p with p part-of q is identical to q, under the displayed hypotheses. The argument is exactly present in the code:

1. Assume p is a proper part of q.
2. By the definition of partial explanation, p partially explains F because it is a part of the full explainer q.
3. Projection from E(q,F), using F(p), makes q a full explainer of p; reflexivity then makes q a partial explainer of p.
4. Antisymmetry and p ≠ q give not(q part-of p).
5. No Extended Circles, applied with partial explainer p and target member q, contradicts the reverse partial explanation.

The proof does not require complete transitivity or the no-basic-full-self-explanation premise. The latter belongs to the subsequent supernatural conclusion. It does not follow from restricted basic-natural PSR alone. Strictly, the proof uses totality only to secure F(p) and F(q); totality supplies these for arbitrary p in the modeled fact domain.

The phrase “no proper fact-parts” is preferable to unqualified “partlessness”: q remains a part of itself. This is a result about the chosen fact mereology, not a theorem that the bearer lacks intrinsic qualification. Moreover, the code contains a fact domain and totality predicate rather than an independent actuality predicate; interpreting the domain as actual obtaining facts is the application used in the source reconstruction.

### Exact transport to intrinsic attributes

The new audit correctly distinguishes four claims that must not be merged:

- An intrinsic perfection is real and belongs to its bearer.
- Two perfections are genuinely distinct.
- Facts concerning those perfections are distinct.
- One such fact is a proper part of the particular total explanatory fact q under this fact mereology.

Only the fourth, together with the explanatory package, produces the incompatibility. Neither the first nor the second entails it. Even the third does not entail it. Calling q a complete divine fact also does not, by itself, establish the fourth.

The primary controls support real distinction without independent subsistence. Dar L006 opposes both identifying knowledge, power, and will with one another and multiplying a qualified human into independently subsisting substances. Akmaliyya L162 preserves distinct concomitant attributes of a single bearer; L163–166 distinguishes independence from something separate from the bearer from the impossible demand that it exist without what belongs within its own description. L164 expressly denies that self-need in this sense means self-production. None introduces the modern fact-part relation used in the Lean proof.

Thus audit lines 85–98 are appropriately guarded. They identify an incompatibility of a **proposed additional interpretation**, not a contradiction between the proved package and intrinsic-attribute doctrine as such. The practical conclusion is an application constraint: anybody choosing that proper-fact-part translation must revise at least one premise. There is no finding that a historically inspected argument actually made that translation, and no new argument for attribute unreality or an attributeless bearer.

Minor wording improvement: audit line 12 should say the package “imposes a fact-mereological restriction whose relevance to intrinsic attributes requires an additional mapping,” rather than implying that it and intrinsic-attribute doctrine are already measuring the same relation on a common scale.

## 3. Modal evidence: where the new criticism is sound

A modal-sounding description cannot, merely by being stipulated, settle extramental possibility. Nor can an argument showing what would count as a cause **if a structure exists** independently demonstrate that structure's metaphysical realizability. At this point the audit is right.

Schaffer's two-level case is particularly clear: note 2 lists substantial independence and causal-power assumptions and deliberately leaves their defence outside this paper. The complete-process discussion likewise concerns causal attribution for events with the relevant completed connection. It is not a derivation of the possibility of arbitrary magical powers or two perfect original agents.

The old report's line 97 starts with an already “coherent, intelligible causal possibility.” That phrase can prejudge what is being assessed. “Apparently coherent causal candidate” or “prima facie intelligible modal case” would avoid the presupposition. Any reader who used line 97 to declare settled metaphysical admissibility would be exceeding the earned result.

The source-independent original-resource extension remains unestablished. An intact sufficient process can presuppose a recipient, laws, resources, and other conditions. The old report already explains this in lines 85–101. Nothing in the present review removes that boundary.

## 4. Modal evidence: where the new criticism overreaches

### Schaffer offers a modal argument, not only conditional causal semantics

At printed p.27 Schaffer disputes a restriction of causal analysis to actual laws, appeals to clear causal intuitions concerning spells, and presents the two-spells case as possible. This is an explicit, contestable modal argument. A source-faithful appraisal should identify both its positive evidential intention and its epistemic limitation. Audit line 124's description as merely a reason to examine a nonphysical causal concept is too deflationary if used as the entire credit owed.

The process argument at pp.33–37 supplies further defeasible reasons for causal individualism and expressly extends its intended scope to spells. Schaffer acknowledges its programmatic character and claims prima facie support rather than final analysis. Those arguments do not authenticate the modal premise, but neither are they just uninterpreted arrows or stipulations. Together with the modal intuition they can reasonably count as defeasible evidence against an unrestricted claim of conceptual impossibility.

Whether they warrant eventual belief in a metaphysical possibility is a further question. Their evidential existence does not depend on first producing a conclusive, wholly independent proof of that possibility. Demanding such a proof before allowing any prima facie modal weight would substitute a much stronger evidential standard for the one the historical report explicitly stated.

### The historical qualifications are operative, not cosmetic

The old report's line 97 identifies prima facie modal evidence and gives its immediate target as a claim of conceptual contradiction. Line 101 distinguishes formal representation, defeasible process-scope modal warrant, and a warranted instance of two entire original bases. Lines 99 and 122 explicitly withhold the last. Line 81 also makes the stronger causal-pattern application conditional on independently established structure and invariance.

The root reassessment similarly denies that it found two perfected originals possible (line 9), calls its local reasons defeasible (line 17), and denies having a full independently warranted countermodel (line 27). Its refusal to renew universal exclusion can be supported by an undercutting scope objection together with defeasible analogical or modal evidence. The words “developed local counterevidence” do not, in that context, demonstrate that the root silently asserted an independently certified original-resource rival.

There remains an arguable judgment about how much those local reasons bear on the stronger bridge. That judgment should be defended at its exact scope. It is not established to be erroneous just by observing that the stronger instance was never proved.

### No specific defeater is yet shown

The new audit distinguishes causal attribution from modal admission. That distinction identifies a justificatory burden; it does not on its own show a failed inference from prima facie evidence to a conclusion the old text never claimed. No contradiction in the spell scenario, argument discrediting the pertinent modal intuition, or source-relative demonstration that the report's middle evidential category was illegitimate has been supplied.

This does not make the modal intuition decisive or immune to challenge. It means the review cannot honestly certify a demonstrated evidential downgrade. The appropriate finding is a wording and scope clarification, plus an unresolved modal-epistemic appraisal.

## 5. Recommended amendment to the current audit

Revise the central finding and §§5/7, especially lines 11, 118, 141, 149, and 184. Preserve the fact-parthood finding. Preserve the distinction between defeasible evidence and established admissibility, but do not recategorize every modal consideration as a merely conditional conceptual challenge.

Suggested replacement assessment:

> Schaffer's spell example supplies an explicitly modal, intuition-based argument, and his causal-process arguments give substantial defeasible support to causal individualism. This is genuine prima facie evidence at the specified process/effect scope if those modal intuitions are credited; it is not an independently established instance of duplicate entire original provision. The earlier wording should mark the case as apparently coherent rather than presupposing its metaphysical admissibility. The earlier report and root reassessment already withhold the stronger conclusion. No additional downgrade of the local evidence, or reversal of the root judgment, follows merely from the absence of a separately established whole-original countermodel.

Calling the existing conclusion “a source-relative conceptual and causal challenge with a separate, unestablished admissibility bridge” is acceptable **only if it explicitly retains that prima facie modal credit**. Otherwise the classification suppresses reasonable defeasible evidence while disclaiming that it is doing so.
