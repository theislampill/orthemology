## What the theorem actually counts

The objective is coBüchi encoded as priority 1 on a bad state/action pair and priority 2 otherwise. The cost is the number of genuinely executed pairs at logical action times 0,1,2,..., including the first action. The supplied default/dummy history pair is not an extra visit. `GeneratedCostLaw` now transports every natural power of this actual count between the raw-stack experiment and the same literal generator's `markovHistoryLaw`, including infinite integral values.

The controller is the accepted `generatedPhasePolicy` with the actual empirical test and any fixed finite choices satisfying the original certificates. The explicit-record successor is included. There is no replacement exploration algorithm, new reset convention, finite-memory assumption or hidden true-model oracle in the policy. Parameters are fixed before quantifying the live true model.

The constants depend on finite cardinalities, a positive row-separation margin and the smallest positive true-model transition probability. They are intentionally loose. An arbitrary almost-sure coBüchi winner need not have finite expectation; the conclusion is derived from this controller's structure and then used for an existence characterization.

## Independent audit of the ordinary proof

### Statistical event and stopping discipline

Concentration is applied only to deterministic raw tape prefixes. Acquired empirical statistics are identified with those prefixes pathwise at their actual consumed counts. No iid law is assumed after conditioning on an adaptive count, a chosen phase entry, or the last-error cutoff.

The full-tape last-error index K is used only for measurable shell partitioning. The stopped process instead uses the first completed observed history with a sufficiently large acquired-count deviation. For fixed true model this is an actual history-filtration stopping time, although its predicate is not available to a hidden-model controller. The detecting transition's action is included; later actions are excluded.

### Phase and finite-state progress

The exact strict count gate protects a sufficiently large true-candidate phase. Zero-or-one increments cannot skip that barrier; proper support changes reset the phase and reduce the finite support. The finite-prefix phase inequality is tied to the actual generated memory equations rather than a stylized process.

A frozen mode's augmented state contains the observed physical state and every source-departure residue. Its arbitrary entry offsets are retained. A closed augmented class cycles every active action at each projected source. A progress-free matching navigation class would contradict the source target-or-exit property. A progress-free mismatching operating class would, by candidate closure and strong connectivity, have to contain every selected component pair, contradicting mismatch avoidance.

These arguments produce a uniform conditional progress probability at least p^D within D transitions, with D=s*a^s. The transition kernel at actual finite stopping histories follows from cylinder factorization and a countable partition over completed histories. It does not require conditioning on a non-stopping tape event.

Fully matching coBüchi operation has zero bad cost. It need not be absorbing: stale rows may later cause rejection. Its physical duration is skipped rather than incorrectly bounded, while subsequent dangerous segments remain charged.

### Base moment estimate

The v2 deterministic segment/interval budget is conservative but valid. A mismatching count above the phase threshold forces rejection whenever the completed history is still accurate; the possible first detecting transition is included as terminal. Uniform conditional geometric domination gives interval moments, and the deterministic power-of-a-sum inequality bounds their total without independence between intervals. Cauchy–Schwarz on the non-stopping shells then yields the first-moment expression and all integer moments.

The displayed quadratic geometric-series formula was checked independently with 2,040 exact rational finite-sum-plus-tail identities. Sixty exact geometric moments, through order 12 and including r=1, satisfy the claimed factorial bounds. These are arithmetic controls, not substitutes for the probability proof.

### Additive exponential tail

The v3 improvement correctly reuses cumulative row counts across all phases and supports. A pair can cause at most n+m-1 nonterminating mismatches before the analysis stop. Matching uses also increment the count and therefore cannot replenish that budget. High-count mismatches may occur after a reset, but they terminate their segment and are charged in the terminal-segment budget. This gives the linear interval budget J*_n=(q+2m)n+q(m-1)+2m(m+1).

The padded chronological interval sequence is adapted to its stopping starts. Earlier completed lengths are known at later starts; nonexistent later intervals contribute zero. Conditional geometric MGFs therefore compose by the tower property without interval independence. The fixed parameter with interval MGF at most 2 is used for a stopped-process tail bound, not directly for the final moment.

The final cutoff is optimized as a deterministic function of the desired tail level. This is important: directly summing the fixed-MGF shell estimate would require c>(q+2m)log 2, which can fail for small margins. The frozen addendum explicitly avoids that invalid inference. Its two terms each become at most half exp(-u), yielding P(C>B+V*u)<=exp(-u), and only parameters t<1/V are claimed for E exp(tC). The resulting stochastic domination and finite-family common positive parameter are valid.
