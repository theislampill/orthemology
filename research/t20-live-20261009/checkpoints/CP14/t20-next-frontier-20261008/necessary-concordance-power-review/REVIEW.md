## Independent modal audit

Write F for A's production of s and B for B's production of incompatible r, under one unchanged metaphysical accessibility relation.

Necessary nonconflict is □(B → ¬F). The reviewed stronger premise is □(B → □¬F). It changes what is ruled out: the first excludes simultaneous incompatible realization in each alternative; the second makes any accessible realization of B exclude F throughout its accessible alternatives. Harmony does not supply that additional invariance of B's particular determination.

From the stronger premise and ◇B, normal modal reasoning yields ◇□¬F. In S5, ◇□¬F entails □¬F, contradicting ◇F. The formal consequence is valid. There is no need to accuse S5 of a fallacy or reject the printed proof rules. The disputed warrant belongs to the stronger premise, not the inference from it.

Actual B plus necessary nonconflict gives actual ¬F. Necessitating that contingent conclusion while preserving B as an assumption is invalid. Necessary existence of the bearer and necessary harmony of its choices do not make this particular determination necessary.

An update that restricts evaluation to alternatives preserving B can make F unavailable there. That gives a context-restricted conclusion. Its inner necessity cannot be passed through the original unrestricted S5 operator without a justified bridge. Even if the restricted submodel is itself S5, its necessity is still necessity within that restriction. A fixed-peer dynamic interpretation is therefore a substantive proposal about ability, not a silent repair of the original same-modality proof.

The report leaves a serious response available: argue independently that the relevant supreme ability must remain robust under peer determinations, or require individual actual productive adequacy. Neither demand is obtained solely from separate subjects, separate powers, or necessary agreement. The report also correctly distinguishes exclusion of one eligible act from total impotence; extending the conclusion to every effective act needs the corresponding contrary-target premises.

## Productive adequacy is not answered by modal covariance

The strongest p.29 reading is retained. An agent's will must provide "everything needed" for its product. This is a demand about productive adequacy, not merely truth-functional entailment. Necessary accompaniment alone does not prove efficient supply or productive incompleteness, but the inherited rival makes the stronger concession: its actual history has genuine original support {A,B}.

Consequently the report's P2–P3 are not hypothetical accusations against a rival that posits only correlated observations. The peer's proper unborrowed exercise belongs to the declared productive basis. A solo route to an equal endpoint in another history does not make either agent the whole original provider of this history. Nor may the peer be reclassified as a merely known object to evade the actual origin question.

The remaining P1 is the substantive perfection norm: each relevant original's completed appropriate undertaking requires individually adequate original provision without an independently original contributor. The inherited Eleventh article supplies a real target-relative reason for including indispensable efficacy in a whole-origin account. The targeted Akmaliyya passages compare self-sufficient production favourably with assisted production and preserve wise discrimination alongside effective power. These are positive philosophical reasons, not empty names. The reviewed report correctly preserves their strengthened defeasible weight.

At the same time, that positive weight does not eliminate the exact question of application. A primitive co-manifestation account can deny that its nonexclusive effect completes either subject's own appropriate activity, while admitting both productive roots. It must warrant that interpretation; a consistency result cannot do so. The affirmative must defend the norm's scope over this undertaking; mere necessary accompaniment cannot do so either. This is why the modal criticism leaves the productive challenge standing rather than refuting it.

Created instrumental causes are not automatically independently original suppliers. Reading P1 as prohibiting every created means would expand the argument improperly; the report's original-resource qualification and inherited context avoid that expansion.

## Wisdom and practical explanation

The report keeps distinct standing capacity, admissibility of an exercise under wisdom, and the complete actual productive basis. Nonnormative analysis does not itself decide a perfect-being norm, while an agent's necessary wise nonselection does not automatically show absence of every antecedent ability. A particular act's incoherence is also different from its inadmissibility for a wise agent.

Common moral truth and compliance can leave particular ends, roles, timing and means underdetermined. That observation neither proves possible conflict nor defeats a richer necessity of agreement. Common reasons do not thereby become a third efficient controller. Conversely, compatible covariance is not a complete account of why these particular original determinations occur.

The inherited conditional symmetry rationale is accurately retained: given the task, equivalent practical roles and the planning norm, the existing diagonal means receive genuine instrumental explanation. The report neither erases that credit nor upgrades it into a complete warrant for the original-peer end or every actual practical reason. Its refusal to impose an exhaustive hidden-reason inventory asymmetrically is appropriate.
