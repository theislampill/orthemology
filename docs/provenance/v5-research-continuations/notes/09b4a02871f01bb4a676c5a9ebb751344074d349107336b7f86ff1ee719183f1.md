# Controller cost prior art and finite memory limits

Bounded comparison for the sixth Orthemology tranche. Checked 2 October 2026 against primary sources and the frozen stopped-cost candidate. This is a scope and novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority search or a new formal proof.

## Bottom line

The defensible distinction is model-dependent, unobserved badness and a theorem about the literal generated history-dependent controller. Generic existence of an all-moments coBuchi policy is not new in the common observable-objective subclass: known finite-memory synthesis plus elementary finite-chain reasoning already gives an exponential moment. Conversely, a two-model Bernoulli example separates history-dependent policies from autonomous finite-state controllers for latent model-dependent badness. That separation is classical in substance and requires an explicit external-clock exclusion.

The frozen candidate has `bad : Model → (State × Action) → Prop`, not one common observable bad set. Its cost counts every executed state-action pair, including the first action. It also does not prove a bound on the physical time of its last bad action, or moments for every almost-sure-winning policy.

## What common observable objectives already give

Chatterjee, Doyen, Raskin and Sankur, *The Value Problem for Multiple-Environment MDPs with Parity Objective*, ICALP 2025, use a common priority map `p : Q → N` on observable states (§2.1). Their **Theorem 4** establishes pure exponential-memory sufficiency for almost-sure parity. **Lemma 3** gives a pure `|Q||E|`-memory strategy in revealed form under its stated per-environment winning-region condition. The conference text states overall memory `O(|Q||E|2^|E|)` immediately before Theorem 4; the retained arXiv v1 proof gives the explicit bound at most `|Q||E|2^|E|`. The relevant references are Theorem 4 and Lemma 3, not a purported Corollary 6. [Published primary text](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.150/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.150.html), [full version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15960)

Here is the elementary cost consequence, derived for this memo. Fix an almost-sure coBuchi finite-state controller and a model. Augment observed state with controller memory and, when needed, the emitted action. This is a finite homogeneous Markov chain. Every reachable bottom strongly connected component is bad-free: any recurrent state or positive-probability recurrent action carrying a bad charge would occur infinitely often almost surely after entry. Let T be the first entrance into the union of these bottom components. Finiteness provides L≥1 and ε>0 such that, from every reachable state outside that union, entry within L transitions has probability at least ε. Therefore

`P(T > kL) ≤ (1−ε)^k`.

Total bad-count C is at most T plus an indexing constant, so `E exp(λC) < ∞` for some λ>0. With finitely many models, choose common L and the minimum positive ε. A common observable state-action bad predicate can be encoded by a finite observable state split. Thus all-moments existence, finite-mean existence, and even positive-exponential-moment existence follow in this subclass. The constants depend on the instance and the selected controller; this is not a uniform numerical rate over all input families or all winning policies.

This argument does not supply a finite-state implementation of the candidate's literal controller, nor justify applying the common-priority theorem to model-dependent badness. Making a latent bad label observable would change the information available to the controller.

## A precise separation for latent bad actions

Consider two models θ∈{0,1}, two observed states {0,1}, and actions {0,1}. From either state and either action, the next state is an independent Bernoulli observation with

`p₀ = 1/3`, `p₁ = 2/3`.

Use a fixed, model-independent initial observed state, and set `badθ(s,a) ⇔ a ≠ θ`. All actions remain enabled. The cost bit itself is **not** revealed. Revealing that bit would identify θ after one action and destroy the example.

**Autonomous finite-state impossibility.** Allow any finite memory, fixed randomized output/update kernels, and a model-independent initial memory law. Incorporate the finite observed state and emitted action into the chain state. Because both Bernoulli outcomes have positive probability under both models, the two induced finite chains have exactly the same transition-support graph, reachable bottom classes and available recurrent action support. If a reachable bottom class supports action 1, then in model 0 it gives positive probability of infinitely many bad actions. If it supports action 0, the same conclusion holds in model 1. Every such class supports at least one action. Consequently no one autonomous finite-state controller has almost-sure finite bad-count in both models; at least one model has positive probability of infinite cost, hence infinite expected cost. This includes randomized finite-state controllers and finite-period schedules when their period is included in memory. It does not assert almost-sure failure in one fixed model for every controller.

**A history-dependent policy with exponential cost tail.** At action n≥1 use the mean of the n new Bernoulli observations: choose 1 exactly when the mean exceeds 1/2; choose either action at n=0. Hoeffding's inequality gives, for either model,

`P(aₙ ≠ θ) ≤ exp(−n/18)`.

The cited primary inequality is **Hoeffding (1963), Theorem 1, equation (2.3), p.15**, applied with deviation 1/6 (and to 1−X for the lower tail). [Primary paper](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf)

For any integer t≥1, C>t requires at least one mistake at a time n≥t, because only t actions occur before t. A union bound, requiring no independence between error events, yields

`P(C > t) ≤ exp(−t/18)/(1−exp(−1/18))`.

Hence `E exp(λC) < ∞` for every 0<λ<1/18. This proves the stated class separation by an ordinary mathematical argument. It is not a new Lean component and is not evidence that the candidate's particular generated controller has an exponential moment. The policy uses an unbounded empirical count; a bounded number of arbitrary-precision registers is not finite-state memory.

## Classical ancestry and the external clock qualification

Hellman and Cover, *Learning with Finite Memory*, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 41(3), 765–782 (1970), explicitly study time-invariant, possibly randomized finite-state update rules (p.766, equations (2)–(5)). **Lemma 1**, p.768, bounds transition-likelihood ratios; the following paragraph states that finite likelihood-ratio spread makes transition supports and transient/recurrent classifications agree under both hypotheses. **Theorem 3**, p.772, gives a lower bound on asymptotic expected error proportion. For equal priors its equation (33) is `1/(γ^((m−1)/2)+1)`. Here γ=4, giving `1/(2^(m−1)+1)>0` for the paper's m-state detector. [Author-hosted primary paper](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/04.pdf), [publication record](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)

That numerical m counts the detector state in the cited model. One must account for observed-state/action augmentation before identifying it with another controller's nominal memory count. The qualitative support proof above avoids this bookkeeping ambiguity and extends directly to randomized outputs. The paper's positive asymptotic-error floor rules out almost-sure finite total errors under both hypotheses; such finiteness would force the bounded empirical error proportion to converge to zero in expectation.

Cover, *Hypothesis Testing with Finite Statistics*, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 40(3), 828–835 (1969), **Theorem 2**, p.833, supplies a four-state time-varying Bernoulli test. Its proof, p.834, establishes finitely many wrong decisions almost surely. The construction relies on knowing the sample number to identify test blocks. **Section 4**, p.835, explicitly distinguishes this externally supplied index from truly time-invariant finite memory. [Author-hosted primary paper](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/paper014.pdf)

Therefore do not state that unbounded internal memory is necessary without specifying the controller model. A finite internal state plus unrestricted externally supplied time already defeats the almost-sure separation. This memo does not attribute finite mean, all moments or an exponential moment to Cover's clocked construction; almost-sure finiteness alone implies none of them.

## Nearby results and limits on novelty claims

- The 2014 Raskin–Sankur paper explicitly restricts its displayed formulation to a common objective (§3), while mentioning environment-specific objectives as a possible generalization. This is a reason to avoid a broad historical claim that hidden model-dependent objectives were never considered. Its displayed common-objective theorems do not by themselves prove the present cost statement. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4733)
- The current published Bordais–Raskin ICALP 2026 result, **Theorem 20**, requires an observation-compatible parity objective for its Dirac-preserving-POMDP reduction. It therefore does not erase the latent-badness distinction by a direct application. The retained February preprint numbers this result Theorem 15; those version numbers must not be mixed. [Published primary text](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.171/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.171.html)
- Lattimore, Hutter and Sunehag's ICML 2013 **Theorem 1** gives a high-probability bound for discounted ε-suboptimal decision times over a finite model class. Its loss and confidence-dependent algorithm differ from exact latent bad-count and one fixed all-moments witness. A family of policies indexed by δ cannot simply be read as all-δ tails of one fixed policy. [Primary paper and proceedings record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/lattimore13.html)

Safe description: the tranche supplies a source-bound formal all-moments/finite-mean characterization for its actual model-dependent coBuchi controller and interface. The common observable subclass has older, stronger existential consequences, and the elementary Bernoulli finite-state obstruction is classical. This bounded search establishes a meaningful scope difference, not historical novelty of the full characterization, and not completion of the remaining exponential actual-controller frontier.
