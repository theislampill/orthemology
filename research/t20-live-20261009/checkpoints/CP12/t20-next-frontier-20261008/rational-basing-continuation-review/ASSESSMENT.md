## Primary-source check

### Dretske and Enç, 1984

Continuous text and notes inspected: printed pp. 517–528. The proposals C(0)–C(3) are necessary conditions, not sufficient analyses. The effective-property discussion distinguishes causally relevant features from descriptions of the same event; pp. 521–522 apply it to belief production. But the later discussion weakens the requirement that the operative cue itself causally guarantee the truth: persistent environmental correlations can train useful recognition, with the Toby/Sally comparison distinguishing ecologically reliable from merely experienced correlations. C(3) is exploratory, explicitly qualified, and not offered confidently even as a partial analysis. Do not promote the intermediate C(2) into the paper's settled conclusion or attribute this joint argument to Dretske alone.

### Dretske, closure essay and reply

Continuous essay, references, and notes inspected: printed pp. 27–40; Dretske's reply: pp. 56–59. Note 4, pp. 38–39, makes the decisive present-use distinction: the perceptual feature carrying information must be the feature causing belief. It positively attributes knowledge in the red-barn/dachshund cases and denies it when the informative aspect is causally idle. This is stronger than mere information availability. Note 1 assumes the specified inferential basis in the closure principle under discussion; it does not make all knowledge inferential or reduce that relation. The reply acknowledges difficulties in counterfactual application. Hawthorne's separate chapter is not Dretske's position. The PDF's 2014 edition notice identifies this source; it need not establish first publication of every contribution.

## What the formal distinction establishes

Use a deliberately partial transcription of the operative-source requirement:

    D*(Q): there exist an evidence episode e and one of its features f such that
           f(e) carries the relevant information that Q, and
           f(e) is effective in producing the subject's belief that Q.

The feature index matters. Replacing the second clause with “the event e causes belief” would discard the very discrimination being credited. Likewise, “some cause is correlated with Q” would be too weak. These predicates are not a new reductive theory and do not presuppose conscious reflection or an explicit representation of an inference rule.

Keep separate:

    I(P,R;Q): this belief that Q is inferentially based on these P/R states.

Neither an existential witness for D*(Q) nor the truth/meaningfulness of P/R identifies those states as this inferential basis. The extra attribution cannot be obtained by substituting P/R for the existential witness without an identification premise. Nor does mere causal ancestry make every upstream state an inferential ground.

Three entailments must not be confused:

1. From a condition necessary for K(Q), satisfaction of that condition alone does not yield K(Q).
2. Even granting K(Q), it does not follow that K(Q) was obtained by inference from these particular P/R states.
3. Failure of I(P,R;Q) does not show failure of D*(Q), perceptual warrant, knowledge, or rationality.

The first is a general logical warning. It must not erase a source's positive judgment about a fully described example. The second is the actual premise-relative boundary being tested here. The third prevents converting that boundary into a theory-wide criticism.

## Strengthened attention witness

Retain the predecessor's meaningful P, R, and Q and their acquired indicator functions. P's acceptance selects where or whether to inspect the lamp. At the inspection, an independent visual episode V has a feature v that is informative of Q in the relevant setting. That feature itself produces the Q-belief. P/R neither determine the interpretation of V nor serve as additional grounds for Q.

For this to test D* rather than a weaker rival, specify more than “V warrants Q”: v must satisfy the source's relevant informational/counterfactual requirement, and v must be the effective feature. A generally reasonable perception is not automatically an informative perception in the required sense. Adequate perceptual capacities, conditions, and absence of applicable defeaters are explicit assumptions about the case; knowledge is not derived from a bare arrow diagram.

Two useful controls are:

- With the same informative V supplied after attention is directed for another reason, the subject accepts Q on V.
- After P has directed attention, a perceptual result supporting not-Q produces the corresponding perceptual judgment rather than a P/R-driven Q-belief.

These controls illustrate the intended separation. They do not, by themselves, prove the absence of every covert or redundant basis. The stipulated absence of inferential use of P/R remains part of the thought experiment, supported by its independently intelligible description. If there is dual basing, theory-laden interpretation, or evidential dependence on P/R, assess that altered case separately.

The witness can therefore satisfy D*(Q) with e = V while failing I(P,R;Q). It may still involve inference elsewhere, rational practical attention, and genuine content-sensitive causation. No contradiction with the inspected present-use requirement follows. Indeed, this is the natural place for that requirement to locate the operative evidence.

## Strongest objection and reply

**Objection.** The case merely stipulates an independent basis and then notes that an existential condition does not identify a named source. Dretske already distinguishes informative causal features and can identify V as the evidence. No failure of his condition, let alone of a naturalistic account, has been demonstrated. Asking for a further “rational basing” relation risks moving the goalposts after the requested present-use condition has been found.

**Reply.** Accept the objection against any refutation or broad insufficiency rhetoric. The legitimate result is correction of the target and removal of an unsupported inference. The prior attention case supplies an intelligible scope test, not new evidence against Dretske. A further investigation is warranted only when a stronger, independently motivated premise-relative claim is actually in dispute, and it must test a developed treatment of that claim. It cannot count the failure to solve a different problem as an objection to the successful present-use discrimination.

The source-specific increment is therefore substantial as interpretation and modest as philosophical advance: an acknowledged informational account has a current operative-feature constraint; the old witness is absorbed by it. The remaining existence-versus-attribution distinction is useful bookkeeping with philosophical consequences, not a surprising theorem about the account.

## Recommended retained wording

“Dretske's inspected treatment requires the informative aspect of the present evidence to produce the belief; available information and a broadly truth-connected causal event are insufficient. An attention-triggering P followed by independent informative perception V can satisfy this requirement through V. Consequently, that case does not refute the requirement. It instead blocks a further attribution that the resulting Q-belief was inferred from the earlier P/R states. These inspected works do not settle a complete analysis of that designated inferential relation.”
