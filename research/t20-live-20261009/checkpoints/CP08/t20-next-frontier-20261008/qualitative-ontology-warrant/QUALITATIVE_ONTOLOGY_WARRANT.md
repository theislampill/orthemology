# Qualitative ontology and the original bearer inference

8 October 2026. Bounded continuation of the active T20 inquiry. This report appraises a newly completed primary-source reading; it does not integrate or close T20, construct a formal countermodel, or alter the inherited files.

## Result

Dasgupta's 2009 paper supplies a substantive, independently motivated, **actual-world-intended qualitative ontology**, rather than merely a way to eliminate individual variables. Its positive warrant is an explicitly defeasible inference from physical symmetries, an Occamist comparison, and a defense of the replacement theory's explanatory and theoretical costs. The resulting proposal deserves more weight than an uninterpreted algebra or a stipulated nonempty solution.

The paper does **not** supply an autonomous explanation of why its total qualitative state obtains, an existence-grounding essence, or an actual unreceived intrinsic productive resource. It proposes a metaphysical priority of the obtaining qualitative whole over smaller facts, while leaving the status and reduction of ordinary individual claims open. Its argument expressly concerns material reality. It therefore cannot be exported as a refutation of every individual, every concrete instance, or an immaterial original bearer.

The consequential result is two-sided. Dismissing the qualitative alternative as mere notation is now inadequately responsive to the source. Treating this paper as a demonstrated or sufficiently specified qualitative explanation of nonempty actuality is equally unsupported. The exact unresolved issue is whether the obtaining qualitative facts can be metaphysically independent of individual instantiation, and, separately, what warrants the actuality and autonomy of that obtaining. The restricted T20 theorem remains conditional on its explicit resource and bearer premises. In particular, opposition to primitive material individuals is not opposition to every actual determinate bearer: the retained universals and states of affairs cannot be excluded from a broader bearer interpretation merely by that opposition.

## Strongest argument and strongest remaining reply

The strongest argument is cumulative: the putative primitive identities do no observable dynamical work beyond a fixed qualitative arrangement; a qualitative ontology preserves that arrangement's general content and inferential structure; and the replacement can account for local causal discourse and for the pragmatic usefulness of object-based thought. If those adequacy claims hold, a defeasible parsimony preference supports the replacement as a theory of actual material structure. That is a serious ontological argument, not an inference from symbols disappearing on the page.

The strongest remaining reply is that invariance under exchanging qualitative duplicates tests which primitive identity fills a role, whereas the disputed instantiation question asks whether the qualitative arrangement can obtain without individual instantiation. Dasgupta recognizes this distinction at p. 41. His later reply supplies qualitative primitives and separates the way they are conceptually taught from their asserted metaphysical independence (pp. 50–54, 66). This blocks a quick inference from object-based semantics to unavoidable individual ontology, but the paper does not fully defend that metaphysical independence or address the further autonomy of obtaining.

For T20 an additional caution is decisive: even granting that independence from primitive material individuals would not show independence from every kind of actual determinate entity or bearer. The inherited bearer type is not defined as Dasgupta's primitive material individual. The remaining transport is from the source's obtaining qualitative reality to an actual relevant intrinsic resource, its bearer, and whole-existence reception or nonreception. The paper neither provides that transport nor rules it out by the anti-individualist argument.

## What gives the proposal ontological content

### The physical argument is comparative and defeasible

Sections 1.1–1.2 compare absolute velocity with primitive individual identity. The relevant physical redundancy concerns variations that leave all general qualitative facts fixed: under the relevant laws, such initial differences do not produce later general differences. The detection argument adds that no device governed by those laws could discriminate the differences through ordinary observable outcomes. The detailed example is Newtonian gravitation; the broader claim concerns the physical theories taken seriously up to the paper's publication. The extension to probabilistic laws is asserted as straightforward but not developed (printed p. 41, note 10).

The elimination step is not another consequence of those laws. Sections 1.3 and 4 add a methodological premise: where a replacement matches the relevant theoretical virtues, its lack of physically redundant, empirically undiscriminable structure counts in its favor (pp. 43–44, 57). Dasgupta explicitly permits the possibility that some such structure is real. This is a defeasible parsimony argument, not verificationism or a deduction from unobservability to nonexistence.

That distinction matters positively as well as negatively. A defeasible argument can support an actual-world ontology without being a neutral demonstration. The affirmative bearer ontology cannot dismiss it just because it is defeasible while retaining its own defeasible ontological warrants. Equally, accepting the symmetry observations need not commit a critic to the comparative premise or to the claim that every other relevant theoretical cost is equal.

### The replacement is not only a modal or linguistic thesis

Section 3 explicitly rejects a merely supervenience-based replacement: a modal correlation between general and individualistic facts can coexist with the metaphysical priority of individuals. Dasgupta therefore asks for a claim about actual structure rather than only co-variation across worlds (p. 50). He also recognizes that merely declaring quantified general sentences fundamental leaves the ordinary individual-domain interpretation in place.

His proposed answer is a domain of universals of various adicities, organized by six algebraic operations. Zero-place properties are treated as states of affairs. The fundamental sentences ascribe obtaining to these states, and the fundamental facts are represented accordingly (pp. 52–54). The term naming a state of affairs does not itself state what the world is like; the obtaining predicate supplies that assertion (p. 53). These are ontological commitments and a proposed metaphysical interpretation of the algebra, not just typographical replacements.

The three adequacy requirements preserve general expressive content and its inferential relations while avoiding the additional primitive-individual content. SUFFICIENCY gives every PL sentence a G counterpart; MODESTY gives every G sentence a PL counterpart; PRESERVATION preserves and reflects logical implication between counterparts (p. 51). Their proof outline in the appendix supports representational adequacy relative to first-order predicate logic with identity and without individual constants. The appendix obtains this through predicate-functor logic and shared model semantics, not through a one-way deletion of notation. This is relevant evidence against the charge that the replacement necessarily loses the general facts. It does not by itself establish the proposed metaphysical priority. Nor is an isomorphism of actual reality or a metaphysically certified quotient of possible worlds demonstrated merely by this logical translation. The ontological claim receives its extra warrant from the preceding comparative argument and the replies to objections.

The qualification about the language is explicit. Section 3.1 assumes for the working discussion that all relevant general facts can be represented in the selected first-order language; second-order and infinitary extensions are deferred (p. 52). This blocks treating the appendix as a completed representation theorem for the entire actual universe, however rich its structure.

### The proposal is non-individualist rather than entity-free

Calling the base nonobjectual is safe only with a carefully restricted meaning. The source denies primitive **material individuals as constituents of fundamental facts**. It does not deny all entities or all ontological commitment. It explicitly posits universals, including zero-place properties, and the language has terms denoting them (pp. 52–54). Describing it as a nominalist mathematical notation with no real base would misstate it; describing it as already proving that no bearer of any kind exists would also misstate it.

Generalism retains the resources corresponding to identity, distinctness, and numerical plurality (p. 53; p. 54, note 28). Its rejection of bundles of universals is partly motivated by their restriction of the range of general facts through the identity of indiscernibles (pp. 47–49). Thus the argument does not license collapsing discernible or indiscernible instances into a single material object merely because primitive individuality has been removed.

The holistic result in section 3.3 concerns the logical and metaphysical structure of facts. A correlated whole contains information that a list of detached existential claims does not contain, and the smaller facts are treated as derivative from that whole (pp. 55–56). The author distinguishes this view from an ontology of a single fundamental world object (p. 56, note 34). A single comprehensive fact is therefore not already one concrete subject, one productive stock, or one efficient source.

There is a precise information-preservation point here. The joint assertion associates an F-role, a distinct G-role, and the direction of an R-relation between their witnesses. The proposed separated assertions retain the existence of an F, a G, and an R-related pair; the last is already relational. What they lose is which qualitative role occupies which endpoint of the relation. Even adding a distinctness assertion does not restore that association. Thus the example is not properly criticized as merely neglecting every relational fact. Its force concerns loss of shared-witness and orientation information when the joint content is separated.

The limitation is equally precise. That example establishes the inadequacy of the given separated representation; it does not establish that no structured decomposition retaining suitable matching or correlation data could be adequate. A proposed alternative would have to specify what that data means, preserve the joint content, and account for its ontological and ideological cost. It might reintroduce the disputed primitive identities or encode the comprehensive content in another form, so invoking matching data is not itself a refutation of generalism. Section 4.3 answers a different worry by preserving local causal relevance despite logical holism; section 4.4 explains the convenience of constants. Neither adds a general impossibility result for every information-preserving relational decomposition. This is an interpretive comparison only: no new mathematical model, quotient proof, or inherited-theorem credit is claimed.

## What grounding or actuality warrant is actually supplied

### There is a priority proposal, but no settled reduction of ordinary bearers

The source proposes a real asymmetry: fundamental qualitative facts underpin the derivative smaller facts. That is more than the observation that two notations can encode the same models. But its argument from nondecomposability to holistic priority remains part of the defended metaphysical interpretation; logical entailment alone does not generally establish grounding direction.

On pp. 54–55 the author deliberately leaves several interpretations of ordinary individual discourse open: error theory, fictionalism, reduction to underlying qualitative facts, and a non-reductive account of equivalent content. The compatibility of generalism with true ordinary individual claims is therefore an advertised option, not an already supplied detailed reduction of the existence and powers of actual bearers. The discussion of de re thought and perception establishes room for such accounts; it does not select and carry out one of them.

Consequently, a report may say that this source develops an ontology under which individual-existence truths **could be treated as derivative**. It should not say that the paper has already grounded every actual individual-existence truth in a specified qualitative essence.

### Obtaining is not explained by naming a state of affairs

The p. 53 distinction between a state-denoting term and the assertion that the state obtains is decisive for the present question. The ontology requires actual obtaining. Its primitive predicate makes the claim explicit but does not supply a cause or ground of that actuality. Syntactic primitiveness alone does not prove metaphysical bruteness or autonomy; it merely shows that the language does not define the predicate away.

The paper neither supplies an essence explaining why the comprehensive qualitative fact obtains nor argues that this fact is unfit for further grounding. It also does not explain why anything material obtains rather than nothing material. Those are further explanatory demands, not failures against the more limited task the author set himself.

The appendix uses nonempty domains for its mathematical models (p. 64). This is important but must be handled accurately. Nonemptiness is an assumption of the comparison semantics; it is not a result establishing nonempty concrete actuality. Nor does the mere use of model elements by itself refute the metaphysics. The appendix anticipates that objection and distinguishes conceptual presentation from metaphysical priority (p. 66). An abstract model, an obtaining extramental fact, and an actual productive resource remain different things.

### The 2016 cross-reference adds a proposal rather than a theorem inherited from 2009

The 2016 note 44 proposes grounding particular-existence facts in predicate-functor facts and then considering these as essentialist facts under an expanded form of essence claim. It cites the 2009 work as a development of the predicate-functor approach. The 2009 paper supplies the qualitative ontological strategy and its motivation; it does not itself supply the essential autonomy or existence-grounding truth required by that further proposal.

Combining 2009 generalism with 2016 autonomous essence, and then with a law-governed all-received productive history, introduces distinct commitments. The sources do not automatically establish their conjunction. No complete alternative cosmos is needed to expose an invalid inference from adequate explanation to an individual source. But asserting the actual truth, metaphysical admissibility, or productive completeness of the combined rival requires warrant for those additional commitments. The source's own material-world argument cannot quietly do all that work.

## The central objection and the author's actual reply

At printed p. 41, Dasgupta expressly acknowledges that a critic might regard individuals as entailed by nontrivial general facts, and hence not redundant in another sense. His physical argument only establishes the stated difference-making redundancy: changing primitive identity while holding the general pattern fixed does not alter the later general pattern.

This leaves an important distinction for T20. The absence of an observable difference between two qualitatively matched bearers does not show that an obtaining qualitative pattern could exist without any individual instantiation. An individualist can claim that concrete instantiation is needed for the pattern to obtain even though which primitive identity occupies a qualitative role has no further dynamical effect. That reply cannot simply assume its own disputed subject/property ontology; it must explain why instantiation is indispensable and why generalism's proposed primitives cannot perform the role.

The author's answer is not silence. He constructs an alternative fundamental-fact ontology, preserves the general content, and in the final appendix separates an initially object-based explanation of a language from the metaphysics ultimately asserted in that language (p. 66). He proposes taking the operations and obtaining predicate as primitives after learning their use. The analogy is the use of an absolute-velocity framework to teach a relative-velocity concept that can subsequently be understood without that original framework.

This is a recognizable response to the claim of immediate conceptual incoherence or unavoidable commitment from the teaching semantics. It is not a full defense of the metaphysical independence of property-constructed obtaining from all individual instantiation. The author explicitly does not fully defend the strategy there. The result is a live, motivated ontological dispute. It should not be declared settled either by the elimination of variables or by the bare assertion that properties must be in subjects.

There is also a scope restriction on the dispute. The paper excludes the structure of nonmaterial reality at p. 37 and excludes a general rejection of souls or abstract entities at p. 44. Even a successful elimination of primitive material individuals would not by itself eliminate an immaterial original source. Conversely, showing that such a source is untouched by the paper's immediate target does not establish the source or repair an otherwise unsupported bridge from every explanation to a productive bearer.

## Objections considered and limits retained

- **Experience appears to present individuals.** The author accepts this as a potential explanatory burden rather than denying it outright; a suitable alternative must account for the appearance (pp. 45–46). Later compatibility options and the cognitive story address it, without a complete perceptual theory.
- **We know which object we encounter.** Reidentification, identifying descriptions, and de re cognition are distinguished from the specific physical discrimination test. They do not defeat the symmetry claim as defined (pp. 45–47). Whether they carry other ontological force remains a different question.
- **Bundle theory already removes primitive individuals.** The proposed universal-bundle theory restricts general multiplicity facts in a way the symmetry argument did not motivate (pp. 47–49). This is an objection to that replacement strategy, not a proof that every possible instance ontology fails.
- **The unfamiliar algebra is too complex.** Difficulty for us is separated from ontological and ideological complexity; the replacement is compared on the latter dimensions (p. 57). That comparison is evidence, not a fully agreed complexity metric for all competitors.
- **The holism contradicts common sense.** The relevant revision concerns fundamental atomism, not an obligatory denial of every ordinary sentence. Revision can be rationally supported (pp. 57–58).
- **Holism prevents local causation.** Logical holism is distinguished from causal distance. General facts concerning a baseball and a window can retain local causal relevance (p. 58). This preserves room for local causal explanation; it does not derive an efficient producer for the total fact.
- **Our persistent atomistic thought would be unexplained.** The hypothetical language-of-thought account motivates the pragmatic value of constants for updating information (pp. 59–61, notes 38–39). The author concedes that this is not a complete explanation and explicitly withholds a semantics of constants.
- **The apparatus covertly presupposes individuals.** Conceptual teaching and model semantics are separated from proposed metaphysical priority, with an analogy and an admitted limit on its defense (pp. 63, 66). This is the most directly consequential remaining objection for the current inquiry.

Other restrictions matter. The main physics case is conditional on the relevant laws' completeness; the wider historical claim is not a new survey established in this operation. A priori modal arguments are explicitly bracketed (p. 37). The first-order expressibility assumption is provisional (p. 52). The precise kind of biconditional used to identify properties, expressly distinguished from ordinary material equivalence, is not analyzed (p. 63). The basic language's modal and property-quantificational extensions are deferred (note 40). None should be upgraded into a completed unrestricted ontology.

## Comparison with the unchanged T20 interface

The interface declares resource tokens to be concrete intrinsic instantiations in the same existential respect and expressly disclaims a theorem from arbitrary facts to bearers (lines 3–12). Its types do not, by themselves, decide a philosophical dispute over what qualifies as an interpreted resource or bearer.

**Occurrence and the target.** Lines 24–25 and 66–73 distinguish received targets and resource relevance. Generalism may preserve ordinary target-existence claims through one of the options on pp. 54–55. It supplies no account of the specific whole-existence reception relation. A truth about a baseball or window is not already the T20 received target merely because it appears in a causal explanation.

**Completion.** Lines 29–36 require an actual, unreceived resource relevant to each received target. Generalism's obtaining holistic fact is not shown to be a concrete intrinsic resource, not shown to be unreceived in T20's sense, and not shown to contribute productively to each target. The 2009 argument therefore does not establish Completion, establish its actual negation, or provide a rival satisfying all premises while defeating the theorem.

**Realisation.** Lines 38–39 require an actual bearer in which any actual resource is intrinsic. If the resource domain retains its declared concrete-instantiation interpretation, a generalist could admit derived resources and derived bearers without accepting fundamental material individuals. The paper does not show that such restricted Realisation is false. If one instead counts the comprehensive qualitative fact as a resource, Realisation's application to it becomes a substantive additional ontological assertion. The original interpretation cannot be widened and then treated as costless.

The retained universals and states of affairs require a second explicit test. `B` is an abstract type in the interface, and even its intended bearer reading is not identified there with a primitive material individual. A defender might therefore propose a retained qualitative entity as a bearer. Generalism alone does not rule this out. But a term referring to a universal, or the assertion that a state obtains, does not establish that it bears a concrete intrinsic resource in the same existential respect. Nor may one stipulate that a state is its own resource and count that as the required ontology. For either a derived concrete bearer or a retained qualitative entity, actuality, resource interpretation, and the intrinsic relation require their own supported identification. The source supplies no negative theorem excluding all such identifications and no positive proof that one satisfies the T20 conditions.

**SupportTransport.** Lines 41–43 transfer whole-existence reception from a bearer to its intrinsic resource. Derivation from a holistic fact is neither definitionally the same as that reception nor proof of its absence. Generalism gives no such transport result. In particular, derivative status is not sufficient evidence that every bearer is wholly received or that the qualitative base is productively original.

**The bridge theorem.** Lines 76–92 use an actual unreceived resource, its realised bearer, and SupportTransport to rule out whole-existence reception of that bearer. The inference remains intact at the declared interpretation. A generalist disagreement about exhaustive resource/bearer applicability targets premise warrant and interpretation, not the validity of this conditional argument.

**The modal extensions.** ReceiptRepresentation, ContingencyNeed, and ConstitutiveReception are distinct assumptions at lines 45–57. The 2009 paper supplies none of them. Its modal neutrality and its deferral of modal-language extensions particularly block converting its algebraic adequacy into a de re necessary bearer. The inherited modal composition at lines 124–139 therefore gains no missing actual-world premise from this source.

No conclusion about efficient agency, perfection, exhaustive source coverage, or uniqueness follows from the number of comprehensive facts. A mathematical proposition about the language, a universal, an obtaining state of affairs, a concrete intrinsic resource, a bearer, and an efficient producer must remain distinguished throughout this comparison.

## Comparative warrant and consequential next step

The warranted change is **stronger positive reason to investigate qualitative-first material ontology, together with a sharper limit on what that ontology has established**. The source raises the cost of treating every adequate material explanation as primitive-material-individual-constituted by default. It does not establish the truth of a universal non-individualist ontology, much less an all-received productive universe.

This narrows any downstream confidence pressure drawn from an unrestricted reading of the prior footnote sketch. The 2009 paper is stronger than that sketch in its independently motivated alternative to primitive material ontology, but it is not a stronger attack on the existence of every actual determinate bearer. It does not independently justify a fresh downward update against restricted Realisation or immaterial originality. The preceding 2016 appraisal remains revisable on its own broader grounds; this operation neither freezes it as an overriding judgment nor reverses it by fiat.

The source's existence need not automatically change every first-order confidence judgment by the same amount. A judgment whose support assumes that fundamental material reality must be constituted by primitive individuals faces direct new pressure from the argument. A judgment already supported by an independent immaterial-source argument is not refuted by that material-domain argument; whether its own bridge is sound still requires its own evidence. In neither case may a previously favored common-source conclusion function as the premise that settles the new ontology dispute. There is no numerical update or forced reversal warranted by this bounded read alone.

The next operation should adjudicate the exact remaining **instantiation and obtaining** dispute, using the already active subject/instance source inquiry rather than launching another generic literature survey:

1. Identify the strongest independently argued reason that actual qualitative obtaining requires an individual subject or concrete instance. Separate it from a claim only about how humans conceive predicates or how first-order semantics is taught.
2. Test that reason against the 2009 answer on pp. 41, 50, 53–54, 63 and 66. Does it refute metaphysical independence, or merely restate the individualist interpretation that generalism contests?
3. Keep a second, independent question open: even if individual-free qualitative obtaining is defensible, what explains its actuality and makes its proposed essential ground autonomous? Check the 2016 note 44 proposal on that exact issue rather than treating the 2009 formal translation as the answer.
4. Report whether either argument reaches the restricted T20 resource interpretation or only a broader ontology. Stop the operation when that local comparison is resolved, or when the missing premise is identified as an unsupported assumption on one or both sides. A complete alternative cosmos is unnecessary for this inference audit; an actual productivity conclusion still needs actual productivity warrant.

The new source is thus consequential without being decisive: it replaces a purely notational rival with a defended material ontology, while leaving the decisive existential and productive bridges visible.
