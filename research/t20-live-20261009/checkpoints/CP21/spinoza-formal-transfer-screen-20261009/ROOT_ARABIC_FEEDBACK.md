## What the source prevents us from importing

Unit 790 introduces the reported objection and its dialectical setting. Unit 791 begins by affirming unity while criticising an attempted philosophical proof. Its subsequent reply distinguishes resemblance under a common concept from sharing one numerical external constituent. Units 792–793 maintain the distinction between an intrinsic qualification's requiring its qualified bearer and its being efficiently produced by another. The source's criticism does not endorse multiple necessary originals.

The newly inspected A22 concerns equality of entire extensions of a chosen Attribute relation. Its application therefore turns on what that relation denotes:

- If its objects represent individually possessed intrinsic qualifications, qualitative equality of perfections does not establish the premise that two bearers have the same numerical qualifications. An instance belonging to one bearer is not made numerically common by a shared description.
- If the relation instead ranges over shared qualitative predicates, profile injectivity becomes a substantive individuation principle. Nothing in the inspected formal proof establishes that qualitative predicates exhaust individual distinction. The inherited Arabic argument expressly blocks obtaining that principle merely by moving from conceptual commonality to an external common component.
- If the formal relation is kept exactly as implemented, A8 and A10 classify its attribute objects as Substance. That requires a separate interpretation before it can represent inseparable qualifications of an original bearer. It cannot silently be identified with Orthemology's concrete resource/bearer distinction.

These alternatives locate the failed mapping. They do not prove that every conceivable interpretation of A22 is useless, nor that a stronger independently defended identity principle is false. They show why this particular proof supplies no additional original-source uniqueness warrant merely from shared perfection.
