## What the wider context changes

At 16:304–305 the differentiating features of the intended particular are located in cognition, explicitly not in an already externally existing object. Consequently an explanation through apprehended features cannot automatically introduce another extramental productive ground. Nor is bare standing power sufficient: the author requires particular differentiation within the agent's own activity.

At 16:310–312 differentiated knowledge and willing are expressly distinguished from mutually separated, countable external components. Indexing acts or contents in a model therefore needs a modest interpretation: distinguishable intentional/temporal content does not by itself individuate independently subsisting objects.

This supports a constructive constraint on the present investigation. An adequate determining account may contain particular cognition and actual willing without importing external supplies or atomised attributes. It does not yet establish that the source's entire differentiation argument is sound, that every explanatory condition is temporally prior, or that a bare assertion of the selected act explains its selection.

## Research consequence

Test the proposed determining-basis requirement using the source's own intrinsic cognition-and-willing account. In particular, audit any formal causal edge whose alleged parent is an unactualised intended object: the intended content, the actual cognitive occurrence, the external product, and their explanatory relations cannot be substituted for one another. This is a semantic guard on the live proof, not a new metaphysical countermodel or a claim that all prior models violated it.
