# Brute contingent original existence: bounded primary-source appraisal

9 October 2026 UTC. Contribution to the active T20 inquiry, not a new source campaign or a closure claim.

## Result

**The strongest inspected source offers a substantive intrinsic-sufficiency rationale, but the three proposed Darʾ locators do not supply a further, independently discriminating demonstration against an unreceived brute contingent terminus.** They strengthen the received-field argument and clarify its meaning. A consequential targeted rereading of Darʾ 8:126–136 additionally shows that the rationale must not be reconstructed as symmetric neutrality between existence and nonexistence. Ibn Taymiyyah explicitly rejects that symmetry while retaining the possible existent’s need for an actualizer.

The exact residual is the passage from **complete absence of external existential dependence** to **positive intrinsic existential sufficiency**. That is stronger than absence of an efficient producer. Its warrant is a substantive judgment about what actual existential obtaining requires; it is not supplied by denying self-production or excluding an entirely received productive field alone. The source gives that judgment positive philosophical support. A brute rival does not become metaphysically possible merely because it declines the judgment. Conversely, the rival is not refuted by defining *mumkin* as dependent.

### The supplied Darʾ locators

**3:147 is genuine and relevant to whole-field dependence.** The contiguous argument begins before it: 143–144 distinguishes concomitant conditions from productive circularity; 145–147 considers an entirely dependent collection. At 146 the author expressly allows that combining weak things can produce strength, because each can be independent in one respect and dependent in another. The prohibited case is total dependence in the same productive respect. At 147: «فليس في المجموع وجود يعطيه للأفراد، ولا لشيء من الأفراد وجود يعطيه للمجموع أو لغيره من الأفراد». This is positive anti-bootstrapping reasoning, not an indiscriminate distribution of every aggregate property. The opening hypothesis already says every existent is dependent on another producer. It therefore does not independently show that a proposed unreceived brute terminus belongs to that field. [Primary electronic context](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/511/).

**3:166 is genuine but contains a voice transition.** Ibn Taymiyyah says the necessary-existent argument may be correct in itself while failing to establish the intended maker, then announces that he will state it accurately. The next paragraph explicitly begins «قال ابن سينا». The modal division begins at 166, and the claim that a possible existent's being is not intrinsically more eligible than its nonbeing is Avicenna's quoted wording at 167. It must not be advertised as Ibn Taymiyyah's own unqualified proof. His commentary at 169–173 accepts the intended dependence route while distinguishing it from a weak composition inference. [Introduction](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/518/), [Avicenna quotation](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/519/), [Ibn Taymiyyah's response](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/520/).

**3:421 is genuine and primarily a semantic safeguard.** At 420–423 he replies to a composition argument: an actually qualified self and its necessary attributes are mutually concomitant; they are not thereby products of each other. At 421: «وما جعله افتقارا ليس هو افتقار المفعول إلى الفاعل والمعلول إلى العلة الفاعلة، وإنما هو تلازم». At 422 the excluded external dependence is on an independently existing requisite, «لا على فاعل ولا شرط». Thus a merely uncaused thing has not yet been shown fully self-sufficient. Equally, intrinsic attributes are not disqualifying outside suppliers. The passage asserts the necessary/dependent division; it does not provide an additional argument specifically defeating brute original contingency. [Primary context](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/413/680/).

All three locators therefore survive bibliographical checking. What fails is treating them as three independent proofs of the live missing bridge.

## 2. The strongest positive argument remains Iṣfahāniyya 34–35

In his own commentary, following the quoted creed, Ibn Taymiyyah argues:

«فالموجود بنفسه الذي لا يفتقر إلى غيره واجب بنفسه إذ نفسه كافية في وجوده فلا يتوقف وجوده على شيء غير إنيته» (35).

Close translation: The existent through itself, which does not need another, is necessary through itself, because its own reality is sufficient for its existence; its existence does not depend on anything other than its own being.

The continuation treats both a hypothetical essence/existence distinction and his preferred concrete identity: «وإن قدر أن إنيته هي وجوده كما هو قول أهل السنة». Nothing here requires an independently existing possible essence to manufacture a second component called existence. Nor is a mentally described essence sufficient to establish an external instance. The subject is an actual concrete qualified existent. [Electronic text](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/شرح_العقيدة_الأصفهانية), [retained scan](https://archive.org/download/waq13116/13116.pdf).

The strongest reconstruction is:

1. Actual originated cases disclose that being is not supplied by their previous absence or by their nonexistent activity.
2. In the relevant existential respect, a reality whose own nature is insufficient does not become actual merely by being possible; its actuality requires an actual source or requisite.
3. A complete source cannot consist entirely of actuality still received in that respect. The established totality rationale applies here.
4. An actual terminus with no external existential requisite is positively sufficient through itself; such sufficiency excludes possible nonbeing through that self.

Steps 2 and 4 carry the anti-brute commitment. The source does not present necessity as an arbitrary exemption from “everything has an efficient cause.” Its point is that intrinsically sufficient reality needs no donated actuality, whereas insufficient reality cannot supply what it lacks. This is an intelligible actualist reason to reject brute contingent originality, and deserves more weight than a bare stipulation of a modal flag.

But “has no outside producer” and “has a sufficient intrinsic reason for existing” are not interchangeable descriptions before that reasoning has been credited. A brute rival explicitly accepts the former and denies the latter. The text’s move is not a consequence of excluded middle alone. Nor does concrete essence/existence identity complete it: creatures have that identity too. Isolating the later “where its identity is posited it cannot be absent” as merely “if it exists, it exists” would trivialize the author's stronger preceding sufficiency claim. Reading it strongly preserves its intended force, but also preserves the need to justify that force rather than hide it in a definition.

## 3. Darʾ 8:126–136 corrects the neutrality reconstruction

This continuous unit is especially important because the same Avicenna passage quoted at 3:166–167 is assessed explicitly. Ibn Taymiyyah accepts the need claim, disputes its offered symmetric rationale, and prefers a directly witnessed originated target. The following are his own comments, except where a quotation is identified.

- **127–128:** Avicenna’s “being is not more eligible than nonbeing” route is unnecessary, disputed, and not demonstrated by him. The reply says of what requires a producer that its nonbeing needs nothing, while its being needs an actualizer.
- **128:** «عدم الغير مستلزم لعدمه، ودليل على عدمه، لا أنه الموجب لعدمه». The other’s absence entails and indicates its absence; it is not what produces its absence. This expressly separates entailment/evidence from productive explanation.
- **129–131:** The quoted Abū al-Ḥusayn argument concerns an actual originated occurrence. Ibn Taymiyyah prefers it because origination's need is clearer than the need of an alleged possible existent that never originated. His objection concerns whether that alleged starting case has been established and correctly understood; it is not permission for brute contingency.
- **131:** The accepted asymmetry is that actual origination requires a determinant. This is distinguished from a symmetric claim that either side of existence/nonexistence requires a determinant.
- **133–135:** His criticism of an eternal complete cause is tested against observed successive effects. At 134 he explicitly maintains that the complete productive influence is followed by its effect, which neither coincides with it nor lags behind it. This is an actual source premise at its stated productive scope, not a consequence of generic grounding, and not a first temporal event in the complete history. It also does not order two hypothetical co-productive agents against each other: their acts might both precede a shared effect, so this temporal assertion alone is no uniqueness proof. At 135 he expressly retains the general positive claim: «فأخذ من ذلك أن تخصيص الممكن بالوجود لا بد له من موجب، وهذا حق». What follows disputes whether the proposed possible can be beginningless.
- **135–136:** The account attributed to Abū al-Ḥusayn treats a beginningless existent as necessary at every time. This is temporal/explanatory discourse. It must not silently become a theorem that existence throughout one history entails existence in every metaphysically possible history.

There are **two claims**, not merely an epistemic restriction. At 127 the author says the relevant possibility/dependence is only known and established in originated cases; he also asserts the ontic thesis «ما يمكن وجوده وعدمه لا يكون إلا محدثا». At 130–133 he denies the alleged beginningless possible, rather than simply suspending judgment about it. That is his positive position. The present appraisal does not replace it with permission for brutes. However, translating that position into an unrestricted possible-worlds predicate needs an argument: “capable of being and not being” as exhibited by actual origination, a beginningless Avicennian effect necessarily concomitant with its cause, and an unreceived B absent from some proposed alternative history are three different cases. Neither the historical rejection of the second nor the clearly warranted need of the first is by itself a demonstrated application to the third. Source fidelity requires retaining his ontic denial; philosophical appraisal requires evaluating its intended meaning and supporting reasons before exporting it to every stipulated world-frame.

### Compatibility with Iṣfahāniyya, without erasing the difference

Iṣfahāniyya 34 says of the possible that it has from itself neither existence nor nonexistence. That must not automatically be enlarged into equal intrinsic probabilities, two positively actual alternatives needing equal production, or a separately subsisting neutral essence. Darʾ 128 rejects the inference that nonbeing requires a producer as being does. A defensible reconciliation is that the possible is neither intrinsically necessary nor intrinsically impossible, yet lacks intrinsic sufficiency for actual being; if no being is provided, there is no positive nonbeing-effect that needs manufacturing. This is an interpretive reconciliation, not a claim that the two formulations are verbally identical.

The inherited expression “its own reality does not settle existence rather than nonexistence” is safe only if it means **lacks sufficient actuality through itself**. It should not mean **has equally productive intrinsic claims on both alternatives**. The new control refines the positive rationale; it does not retract it.

## 4. The strongest brute rival and what actually reaches it

Consider an alleged concrete, intrinsically qualified B that has always existed, has no external producer or existential condition, and supplies the received field's productive actuality. B nevertheless could have failed to exist; its obtaining has no further adequate explanation, intrinsic or external. Its powers and qualification are not separately subsisting donors.

This is a **candidate position under assessment**, not an established metaphysical possibility. It is stronger than a circle, an entirely borrowed series, or a bare description that cannot produce anything.

It passes the following limited tests by construction:

- It does not produce itself; it is never alleged to act while nonexistent.
- It does not make the productive field wholly received; B is explicitly unreceived.
- It can acknowledge witnessed originated effects and their actual producers without proposing a first moment for all effects.
- It can preserve concrete essence/existence identity. That identity alone does not decide B’s modal status.
- It does not infer original status from a mere necessary conditional or from existence at every listed model-world.

Consequently, neither self-production’s impossibility nor the all-received-field argument directly defeats it. Treating B as “another possible” and therefore already received would assume the bridge presently in dispute. Conversely, calling B original does not show it is adequate to every relevant effect, or that its alleged possible absence is genuine. Those remain burdens for the rival.

The Taymiyyan challenge that does reach it is exact: **if B’s own concrete reality is insufficient for its obtaining, how does actual being obtain without any actual existential requisite?** The brute answer is not an alternative sufficient explanation; it is a principled refusal of that demand at the terminus. Its virtue is to avoid adding an intrinsically necessitating nature whose existential force has not independently been established. Its cost is leaving the source’s very obtaining unexplained, despite using it to explain the received field. Neither side settles this by lexical choice.

“Nothing produces being” is not a fair statement of the brute answer: it posits no production of B at all. The affirmative case must show why an unproduced, insufficient but actual B is impossible or epistemically worse, rather than reclassifying it as an effect. Likewise, asking “what explains it?” is a powerful explanatory challenge but becomes a deductive disproof only with an independently warranted no-brute actuality principle.

The rival has not demonstrated its modal premise. Imagining B absent or writing a world-table without it is not evidence that this particular actual source could fail to exist. Temporal beginninglessness also does not by itself establish intrinsic necessity. The inspection therefore yields neither a certified brute counterexample nor a neutral-premise deduction excluding all such candidates.

## 5. What warrant remains, and the precise T20 consequence

The positive view can legitimately rest partly on a first-principle judgment: mere existential insufficiency provides no positive actuality, and an actual contingent obtaining cannot be sufficiently accounted for by declaring the need inapplicable exactly at the proposed source. This judgment is supported by the clear ordinary cases, the asymmetry of being/nonbeing, and the source's careful distinction of intrinsic qualification from outside existential receipt. Philosophical warrant need not be a theorem from weaker premises.

But those considerations do not automatically establish their extension to a brute beginningless concrete original. Observed origins provide especially clear evidence; extending their need to every modal nonnecessity is a substantive metaphysical commitment. Acknowledging this is not a confidence downgrade merely because no deduction is available. It identifies what the positive case must be weighed on. The rival’s unproved modal possibility does not erase the source's explanatory warrant.

For the formal interface, keep these commitments separate:

1. Actual productive targets have an original relevant productive basis: inherited Completion and its positive totality rationale.
2. Full external existential independence implies positive intrinsic sufficiency: the live **no-brute-original-actuality** commitment.
3. That sufficiency warrants necessity through oneself, and its translation into necessity across the intended genuine possibility domain.

The already proved original-bearer conclusion does not itself discharge 2–3. ContingencyNeed cannot be presented as newly derived from it. Neither should the all-received domain be replaced with the modally nonnecessary domain: a received reality may persist throughout a stipulated modal frame, while the alleged brute B is nonnecessary yet unreceived. That latter mapping is exactly the disputed case, not an acceptable definitional substitution.

**Assessment change:** improved and scan-controlled source semantics; no new independent proof closing the brute-contingent gap; no new demonstrated metaphysical counterexample. The most useful substantive correction is positive: build the anti-brute case around asymmetric actuality and intrinsic sufficiency, not a neutral essence, equal-probability alternatives, a producer of nonbeing, or a first temporal moment. The affirmative original-basis result remains intact at its earned scope.

## Scope and next discriminator

The inspected unit does not establish uniqueness, Knowledge, Will, or Wisdom. It does not authenticate the unspecified later ʿAfīfī attribution; no quotation is credited to it. Qurʾān 52:35 was assessed through the directly inspected Iṣfahāniyya commentary: originated without an originator versus self-originated. Its immediate force concerns actual originating cases, not an independently established first moment or a separate deduction about brute beginningless modality.

A genuinely advancing next argument would either defend the no-brute-original-actuality principle with warrant that reaches the beginningless case, or supply a reason to believe that such a case is genuinely possible while preserving the intended productive semantics. Another all-received chain or a modal table would not discriminate those positions.
