# Exact predictive memory for the binary log Hessian flow

## Result and scope

For the fixed, unnormalised canonical Hessian nudge with identical binary-log generators in every coordinate, predicting the entire original-clock scalar coherence trajectory from an unknown initial state requires **exactly n continuous real coordinates** on every nonempty open subset of an ordered, distinct-coordinate, incoherent chamber in `(0,1)^n`. The decoder may be completely arbitrary, including nonlinear and discontinuous. The necessary hypotheses are continuity of the encoder, exact output recovery, an open n-dimensional family of initial states, fixed known n and dynamics, and retention of the specified model clock.

A finite jet already determines the required information: the output value and derivatives through order `n−1` recover the first n power sums; Newton identities recover the initial multiset; the chosen ordering recovers the initial vector. In fact this jet map has an explicit nonzero observability determinant on the stated chamber.

The conclusion changes under the inherited reciprocal-curvature normalization. For the vector field divided by the positive reciprocal-curvature sum, scalar coherence obeys `dz/dτ = 1−z`, and one initial coherence coordinate suffices. This changes the clock in the requested prediction contract. It is not a compression that preserves the original-clock trajectory.

The claim concerns exact recovery of the whole local time function. It does not establish a corresponding bound for a single future value, approximate/noisy recovery, or arbitrary discontinuous infinite-precision encoders.

These are ordinary mathematical proofs with small exact symbolic/rational controls. They are not a new general observability theory, a proof-assistant certificate, a result about bits or runtime, a cognitive lower bound, a claim about physical time, or permission to integrate or close the research programme.

## Model and existence

Fix a known integer `n≥1` and use natural logarithms in the identical generators

\[
 f_i(x)=f(x)=x\log x+(1-x)\log(1-x),\qquad 0<x<1.
\]

Thus `f''(x)=1/[x(1−x)]`. Write

\[
 z=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i,\quad r=z-1,\quad
 S=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i(1-b_i)>0.
\]

The original model clock t is fixed by the ODE

\[
 \dot b_i=F_i(b)=-r\,b_i(1-b_i). \tag{1}
\]

No arbitrary positive rescaling of F is included in this definition. A different logarithm base or common generator scale correspondingly rescales this clock. The earlier three-outcome hypotheses for universal rigidity classification are not needed for this separate calculation once (1) is fixed.

**Lemma 1.** Every initial vector in `(0,1)^n` has a unique smooth solution of (1) for all real t, remaining in `(0,1)^n`. Coordinate order is preserved. If `r(0)≠0`, then `r(t)≠0` at every finite t.

**Proof.** The vector field is polynomial, hence locally Lipschitz and smooth. On any interior solution interval,

\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\log\frac{b_i}{1-b_i}=-r.
\]

While the solution lies in the cube, `|r|≤max(1,n−1)`. Therefore each logit stays finite and uniformly bounded on each bounded time interval. A solution cannot reach a boundary coordinate in finite time. It also cannot blow up while confined to the bounded cube; the standard ODE extension theorem yields a global solution in both directions. Differences of logits are constant, so all strict initial order relations persist. Finally,

\[
 \dot r=-rS,\qquad
 r(t)=r(0)\exp\!\left(-\int_0^t S(b(u))\,du\right), \tag{2}
\]

which proves preservation of nonzero r. ∎

Global existence removes any need to choose an initial-state-dependent observation horizon for (1). Fix any common `T>0`, however small, and let `z_b:[0,T]→R` denote its scalar output from initial state b. Equality of these smooth functions gives equality of every right derivative at zero, which equals the ordinary derivative of their smooth extension. The same argument works if the decoder produces a trajectory germ at zero instead.

## Moments recovered from the output jet

Let

\[
 p_k(t)=\sum_i b_i(t)^k,\qquad k\ge1,
\]

so `p1=z`. Direct differentiation gives the exact hierarchy

\[
 \dot p_k=-k r(p_k-p_{k+1})=kr(p_{k+1}-p_k). \tag{3}
\]

In particular, `z'=−rS`, so retaining z alone does not ordinarily close the original-clock output dynamics.

**Lemma 2.** At any incoherent initial state, `(z(0),z'(0),…,z^(n−1)(0))` uniquely determines `p1(0),…,pn(0)`.

**Proof by triangular derivatives.** Define polynomial expressions in formal power sums by

\[
 Q_0=p_1,\qquad
 Q_{j+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{j+1}
   \frac{\partial Q_j}{\partial p_k}\,k(p_1-1)(p_{k+1}-p_k).
\]

Equation (3) and the chain rule give `z^(j)=Q_j`. For every `j≥1`,

\[
 Q_j=j!(p_1-1)^j p_{j+1}+R_j(p_1,\ldots,p_j). \tag{4}
\]

For j=1 this is `Q1=(p1−1)(p2−p1)`. If (4) holds, the only term that introduces `p_{j+2}` when differentiating is the derivative of `p_{j+1}` in its displayed leading term. Its coefficient becomes `(j+1)!(p1−1)^(j+1)`. Differentiating the coefficient or `R_j` uses at most `p_{j+1}`, proving the next induction step. Since `p1−1≠0`, recover p1 from z and then solve (4) successively for each next moment. ∎

Equivalently, the reconstruction can be written as the rational differential recurrence

\[
 p_1[z]=z,\qquad
 p_{k+1}[z]=p_k[z]+\frac{(p_k[z])'}{k(z-1)}. \tag{5}
\]

Each step uses one more derivative. For example,

\[
 p_2=z+\frac{z'}{z-1},\qquad
 p_3=z+\frac{3z'}{2(z-1)}+
        \frac{z''}{2(z-1)^2}-\frac{(z')^2}{2(z-1)^3}.
\]

These formulas are exact identifiability statements. Division by powers of `z−1` warns against treating them as a uniformly stable estimator near coherence.

## Newton reconstruction and the observability determinant

**Lemma 3.** The first n power sums uniquely determine the unordered multiset of n real coordinates, with multiplicities.

**Proof.** Set `e0=1`. Newton identities give, recursively for `1≤k≤n`,

\[
 k e_k=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j-1} e_{k-j}p_j. \tag{6}
\]

Thus the moments determine every coefficient of

\[
 P(X)=X^n-e_1X^{n-1}+e_2X^{n-2}-\cdots+(-1)^n e_n
     =\prod_{i=1}^n(X-b_i).
\]

The polynomial determines its multiset of roots uniquely. For completeness, (6) follows by taking the logarithmic derivative of `E(u)=∏i(1+b_i u)=Σk e_k u^k`: the formal identity `E'(u)=E(u)Σj≥1(−1)^(j−1)p_j u^(j−1)` yields (6) by coefficient comparison. No numerical root-finding accuracy premise is involved. ∎

The jet map `J(b)=(z(0),z'(0),…,z^(n−1)(0))` has Jacobian determinant

\[
 \boxed{\det DJ(b)=
   \left(\prod_{k=1}^n k!\right)
   (z-1)^{n(n-1)/2}
   \prod_{i<j}(b_j-b_i).} \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, the moment map has derivative entries `k b_i^(k−1)` and determinant `n!∏i<j(b_j−b_i)`. The moment-to-jet map is triangular, with diagonal `1,1!r,2!r²,…,(n−1)!r^(n−1)`. Multiplying yields (7). On a distinct-coordinate incoherent chamber this determinant is nonzero, so J is locally a smooth coordinate chart. Lemmas 2–3 additionally give injectivity across a whole fixed-order chamber, which a nonzero determinant alone would not establish.

The derivative order is sharp for consecutive initial jets on any such open set: if `m<n−1`, the map `(z,…,z^(m))` factors through and is equivalent there to `(p1,…,p_{m+1})`. Its moment Jacobian has row rank `m+1`, by the rectangular Vandermonde matrix and distinct coordinates. The submersion theorem therefore gives local fibres of dimension `n−m−1>0`. These shorter jets fail to distinguish arbitrarily nearby initial vectors. This concerns exact consecutive derivatives, not the number of arbitrary time samples needed under some other acquisition contract.

## Exact continuous memory theorem

Fix any permutation σ and any nonempty open subset

\[
 U\subseteq\{b\in(0,1)^n:
 b_{\sigma(1)}<\cdots<b_{\sigma(n)},\ \sum_i b_i\ne1\}.
\]

The order σ, n, and vector field are fixed known parts of the model, not data that vary between encoder inputs. Let `E:U→R^d` be continuous. Suppose a decoder D produces the exact original-clock output for every initial vector:

\[
 D(E(b))(t)=z_b(t),\qquad b\in U,\quad 0\le t\le T, \tag{8}
\]

for one fixed `T>0`. There is no continuity or linearity requirement on D.

**Theorem 4.** The minimum possible d in (8) is n.

**Proof of necessity.** If `E(b)=E(c)`, exactness gives equal output functions. Their jets through order n−1 agree. Lemma 2 gives equal first n moments, and Lemma 3 gives equal coordinate multisets. The fixed strict ordering then gives `b=c`. Thus E is injective. If `d<n`, compose E with the zero-padding inclusion `R^d→R^n`. This is a continuous injection from the nonempty open subset U of `R^n` into `R^n`. By invariance of domain its image is open, but it lies in a proper coordinate subspace and has empty interior, a contradiction. This is the inherited topological argument from Theorem 2(e), not a novel dimensional principle.

**Attainment.** Take `E(b)=b` and let D solve the unique fixed ODE, then sum its coordinates. Alternatively, encode the n moments `(p1,…,pn)`, reconstruct the unique initial vector in the fixed chamber by Lemma 3, and apply the same decoder. These encoders are continuous. Therefore d=n is attainable. ∎

The n power sums also give a closed finite-dimensional polynomial state model, if desired. Newton identities express `e1,…,en` polynomially in the first n moments over the reals. The root equation gives

\[
 p_{n+1}=e_1p_n-e_2p_{n-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{n-1}e_n p_1.
\]

Substitute this expression into (3) for k=n, while using (3) directly for k<n. This gives exact n-coordinate moment evolution on the realizable moment set. It makes no numerical-efficiency claim.

## Symmetry and exceptional domains

Identical coordinate generators make (1) permutation-equivariant and z permutation-invariant. Therefore labels cannot be recovered from z on the full cube. Off coherence, Lemmas 2–3 show that two initial states have the same local original-clock scalar output exactly when they have the same multiset. Sorting the initial vector is a continuous n-coordinate sufficient summary on the full cube; moments are another. The theorem chooses an ordered chamber precisely to remove the label ambiguity for its injectivity argument. Distinctness is needed for the open chamber and nonzero determinant; Newton's multiset reconstruction itself also allows repeated coordinates.

At `z=1`, the whole vector field is zero. Every coherent interior vector gives the same constant output `z(t)=1`, even if their multisets differ. On the domain consisting only of coherent inputs, zero stored coordinates suffice for this output target. This stationary collapse prevents a global injectivity assertion. It does not invalidate the n-coordinate lower bound on a larger open domain containing an incoherent chamber.

All initial-state-dependent side information supplied to the decoder must be counted in the representation contract. For example, if `z(0)` is already available without charging it to E, then **n−1 additional continuous coordinates** suffice and are necessary on open pieces of the fixed-sum incoherent hypersurface: encode `p2,…,pn` for attainment, and apply the same injectivity/invariance-of-domain argument in an `(n−1)`-dimensional chart for necessity. This is the inherited Proposition 9 style of augmentation argument. Likewise, a restricted one-dimensional family of states does not inherit the n-dimensional lower bound.

## What normalization preserves and loses

Define the normalized field

\[
 \frac{db_i}{d\tau}=\frac{F_i(b)}{S(b)}
  =-\frac{(z-1)b_i(1-b_i)}{\sum_jb_j(1-b_j)}. \tag{9}
\]

It is smooth on the interior, and its scalar output satisfies

\[
 \frac{dz}{d\tau}=-(z-1),\qquad
 z(\tau)=1+(z(0)-1)e^{-\tau}. \tag{10}
\]

Thus one continuous coordinate `E(b)=z(0)` suffices to predict the normalized-clock scalar trajectory on every common interval of existence. On a nonempty open state domain, zero coordinates cannot suffice because z(0) varies, so the exact minimum is one.

Local existence alone suffices for this comparison. In fact the normalized solution stays interior for all forward τ: for n≥2, write each coordinate as the logistic of its initial logit plus a common shift a. The sum of these logistic functions is strictly increasing from 0 to n as a ranges over R, and has a finite unique point where the sum equals 1. Equation (9) moves a monotonically toward that point, so a stays in the compact interval between its initial value and that point. For n=1, (9) is directly `b'=1−b`, whose interior forward solution exists for every τ≥0.

Since `S>0`, (1) and (9) have the same oriented interior state-space paths, with the local time change

\[
 \tau(t)=\int_0^t S(b(u))\,du,\qquad
 \frac{d\tau}{dt}=S(b(t)). \tag{11}
\]

The original time parametrization is obtained from the state-dependent reciprocal factor `dt/dτ=1/S`. Equation (10) alone does not retain that factor as a function along the path. Multiplication by `1/S` preserves the sign of the inherited loss derivative but changes its magnitude from `−r²` to `−r²/S`. The difference concerns the declared model clock; no empirical physical-time identification has been supplied.

## Prior art and evidential ceiling

The general output-derivative/observability approach is classical. Hermann and Krener's 1977 paper develops nonlinear observability using Lie derivatives and the observability rank condition; the present determinant is a concrete calculation in that established framework. It is not claimed that their general controlled-system minimal-realization theorem applies verbatim to this autonomous summary problem. The stronger arbitrary-continuous-encoder conclusion is proved here by the already inherited separation plus topology argument. See [Hermann and Krener, author-hosted paper](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~krener/1-25/10.IEEETAC77.pdf), Section III; publication metadata and the source link are on [Krener's publication list](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~krener/).

Newton reconstruction is classical symmetric-polynomial mathematics. The proof above is supplied so no novelty or algorithmic claim is hidden in its use. For an author-hosted derivation relating power sums to characteristic polynomial coefficients, see [Nicholas Wheeler, Newton and the Characteristic Polynomial of a Matrix](https://www.reed.edu/physics/faculty/wheeler/documents/Miscellaneous%20Math/Novel%20Derivation%20of%20Newton%27s%20Identities.pdf), 2019, especially pages 4–7.

The only imported topological theorem is invariance of domain, as stated in [Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Theorem 2B.3](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch2.pdf#page=76), book page 172. The source was inspected and the exact statement supports continuous injections, without assuming a differentiable encoder or continuous decoder.

The substantive advance established in this bounded continuation is the explicit clock-dependent predictive-memory result for this inherited binary-log canonical nudge, including its complete finite-jet reconstruction and sharp comparison with the inherited normalization. General novelty remains unestablished. No retained source is modified, no kernel status is inherited by this new ODE argument, and no programme closure is asserted.
