# Label-preserving tensor versus count compression

Under conditional independence of the two emissions given the persistent label and profiles, for the swapped profiles (a,b), (b,a), latent label A gives ordered Bernoulli pair law with parameters (a,b); label B gives parameters (b,a). The laws differ when a!=b. Their pushforwards through total success count Y1+Y2 coincide exactly: probabilities (1-a)(1-b), a+b-2ab, ab for counts0,1,2.

Thus the count map erases A/B identification, even for pure populations concentrated respectively on A and B. This is a fixed pair of observationally indistinguishable competitors under the compressed observation, not a changing adversary. Under known conditional-independent joint channels, the uncompressed ordered four-cell matrix is invertible for a!=b as the separate determinant calculation shows.

The earlier grouped-mixture proof that exchangeable counts preserve all available moment information depends on identical conditional rates within a group. Heterogeneous intervention profiles invalidate that exchangeability hypothesis. Its compression theorem cannot be transported while dropping that premise. Retaining profile labels and joint outcomes is a genuine information-preservation requirement.

This is a mathematical observation-model result. It does not establish physical provenance, psychological warrant, or original-source identity. In this setting mixtures over the four inventories, not four unqualified physical architectures, are what the identifiable weights describe.

Away from exact corners, arbitrary within-pair coupling need not preserve the displayed count-law equality; this counterexample uses the specified product channel. At exact corners the deterministic collision is coupling-independent.
