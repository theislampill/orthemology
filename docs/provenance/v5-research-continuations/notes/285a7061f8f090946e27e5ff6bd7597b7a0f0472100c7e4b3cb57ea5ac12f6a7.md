# A sharp Beta-prior boundary for finite expected literal mistakes

Status: fresh ordinary derivation, awaiting independent review. This note extends the all-policy continuum-cost work. It does not claim historical priority or a new Lean proof.

## Contract

Fix0<ε≤1/4. Draw one audit/scoring weight α from Beta(a,b), with a,b>0, then keep α fixed throughout the run. Conditional on α, acquire iid Bernoulli(α) receipt bits. At physical opportunity n, a common measurable policy issues one coherent real report q_n using exactly the first n receipts and an independent private seed. It does not receive α. No extra uncounted samples or abstention are permitted in this clock.

Count one literal failure whenever either inherited excess loss is positive:

D0(α,ε,q)=q²−1/4−αε(1+ε),
D1(α,ε,q)=(1−q)²−1/4+αε(1−ε).

Incoherent reports, if admitted as outputs, also count as failures. Let C_α be expected lifetime failures under the fixed source α, and let C_Beta=∫C_α Beta(a,b)(dα). This prior is over one fixed model; its mixed receipt process is exchangeable and generally not an iid fair-coin source.

## Theorem

There exists a common always-issued policy with C_Beta<∞ if and only if a>1 and b>1.

If a≤1 or b≤1, every such policy has infinite prior-averaged expected lifetime failures. This lower conclusion requires no correctness assumption at the endpoints, which have prior probability zero.

## 1. An accepted report covers only a bounded multiplicative interval

Any report accepted for some interior α must lie in[1/2,1/2+ε]. For q outside this interval, D1 or D0 is positive for every interior α. At the interval endpoints the accepted parameter is a singleton of prior measure zero.

For q in the interval, acceptance requires α∈[ℓ,u], where

ℓ=(q²−1/4)/(ε(1+ε)),
u=(1/4−(1−q)²)/(ε(1−ε)).

Writing q=1/2+x, x≥0, shows

u≤R0ℓ, where R0=(1+ε)/(1−ε)>1.

Near the other endpoint write β=1−α and q=1/2+ε−t, t≥0. Its accepted β interval satisfies

ℓ1=t(1−2ε+t)/(ε(1−ε)),
u1=t(1+2ε−t)/(ε(1+ε)),
u1≤R1ℓ1,
R1=((1+2ε)(1−ε))/((1−2ε)(1+ε))>1.

Intersect these intervals with[0,1] where needed. These are direct algebraic consequences of both-truth weak repair; they do not use a prior or estimator.

## 2. A posterior cannot concentrate in one such interval after an extreme prefix

After n≥1 zero receipts the posterior is Beta(a,b+n). Let F be its cumulative distribution. Since b+n−1≥0, for R≥1 and z∈[0,1], substitution gives

F(z/R)=R^(−a) K∫₀ᶻ x^(a−1)(1−x/R)^(b+n−1) dx
        ≥R^(−a)F(z),

where K is the normalising constant. Therefore every interval[ℓ,u] with u≤Rℓ has posterior mass at most1−R^(−a): F(ℓ)≥F(u/R)≥R^(−a)F(u). Clipping at1 preserves the inequality; empty intervals contribute zero.

Consequently any issued report, including a randomised report, has conditional failure probability at least R0^(−a) after the all-zero prefix. The common seed remains independent of α conditional on these fixed receipt bits, so averaging over that seed does not weaken the bound.

Similarly, after n≥1 one receipts, the posterior for β is Beta(b,a+n), and conditional failure probability is at least R1^(−b).

## 3. Harmonic or heavier accumulation forces infinite cost

The two extreme-prefix events are disjoint for n≥1. Their unconditional probabilities are respectively

M0(n)=E[(1−α)^n]=B(a,b+n)/B(a,b),
M1(n)=E[α^n]=B(a+n,b)/B(a,b).

For every policy and every n≥1,

P_Beta(failure at n)≥R0^(−a)M0(n)+R1^(−b)M1(n).

Tonelli and the geometric series give

C_Beta≥R0^(−a)E[(1−α)/α]+R1^(−b)E[α/(1−α)].

For a Beta prior the first expectation diverges exactly when a≤1, and the second exactly when b≤1. This follows directly from the density's endpoint powers α^(a−1) and(1−α)^(b−1); no asymptotic interchange is required. This proves the necessary direction for every policy.

If a,b>1, the finite lower bound becomes

C_Beta≥R0^(−a)b/(a−1)+R1^(−b)a/(b−1).

For the uniform prior a=b=1 and ε=1/4, R0=5/3 and R1=9/5. Expected failures through physical index N are therefore at least

(52/45)(H_(N+1)−1),

using only indices1 through N. The empty prefix at n=0 is deliberately not counted twice.

## 4. Sufficiency retains the third-tranche empirical theorem

The recovered third-tranche independent continuum review proved, for each fixed interior α, that the empirical varying-report learner has post-acquisition expected failures at most56/[α(1−α)ε²]. The explicit initial report at n=0 adds at most one failure. When a,b>1,

E[1/(α(1−α))]=(a+b−1)[1/(a−1)+1/(b−1)]<∞.

Thus the same learner has

C_Beta≤1+(56/ε²)(a+b−1)[1/(a−1)+1/(b−1)]<∞.

This proves sufficiency and completes the iff. The inherited empirical theorem is credited, not rebranded as a new learner or replaced by a weaker concentration bound.

## Scope

The theorem concerns literal failure counts; it does not assert infinite accumulated excess-loss magnitude. It is specific to Beta priors and the stated one-receipt-per-report clock. It does not establish an inverse-moment iff for arbitrary discrete or singular priors. Sampling many extra receipts before each evaluated report, abstaining, revealing α, correlating the seed with α, or setting ε=0 changes the hypotheses.

The empirical policy still makes finitely many mistakes almost surely under every proper Beta prior, because it does so at every fixed interior parameter. Infinite prior-averaged expectation therefore does not mean infinitely many mistakes on a typical run.

The result sharpens the distinction between pointwise finite expected mistakes and a finite prior-averaged budget. Its status is ordinary mathematics with exact finite corroboration and pending adversarial review.
