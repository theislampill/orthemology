# The exact global nuisance radius after the grouped invariants are fixed

This is a population-level corollary, conditional on a fixed identified primitive vector v, its positive weights w_i, and optional zero mass w0. There must be positive-count mass. It does not assume these quantities can be recovered exactly from finitely many noisy groups.

## 1. Faithful reduction to a pure unknown scale

Let F(z)=w0+sum_i w_i z^(v_i), z in [0,1]. Since each v_i>=1 and some positive-count weight is positive, F is continuous and strictly increasing, with F(0)=w0 and F(1)=1. For count support d v and calibration survival t(x), the one-trial no-hit curve is

m(x)=F(t(x)^d).

Thus the exact population curve determines

b(x)=F^(-1)(m(x))=t(x)^d.

Conversely b(x), v and the weights determine every held-count grouped law, including mixed-command groups under the conditional-independent response model. For repetitions at commands x_1,...,x_R, the component at v_i has respective no-hit means b(x_1)^(v_i),...,b(x_R)^(v_i). This proves an equivalence of population observation families, rather than treating a scalar root as an independently observed physical calibration.

For a supplied full curve b(x), a candidate scale d determines the only possible calibration

r_d(x)=1-b(x)^(1/d).

It is admissible under a uniform command-error promise precisely when sup_x |r_d(x)-x|<=eta, provided it also belongs to the stated monotone endpoint-preserving class. A curve generated by one eligible calibration automatically yields those monotonicity/endpoints for every positive scale, but not necessarily the error bound. This is the full-curve counterpart of the one-command feasibility test in RESULT.md.

## 2. Sharp pairwise scale radius

Fix positive integers d<e. Define

theta(d,e) = [(e-d)/(2e)] (d/e)^[d/(e-d)].

For the common uniform calibration class of static nondecreasing endpoint-preserving maps, the two scales d,e admit identical complete response curves at and above this radius; below it their population laws are separated by one command. The two ambiguity maps can be continuous and strictly increasing. The threshold depends only on e/d, not on the primitive vector or its weights.

To construct the identical worlds, parameterize nominal command by u in [0,1]:

x(u)=1-(u^e+u^d)/2,
r_d(x(u))=1-u^e,
r_e(x(u))=1-u^d.

The command is a decreasing continuous bijection. Both actual-rate functions are static, continuous, strictly increasing and endpoint-preserving. Their deviations are opposite and have magnitude (u^d-u^e)/2. Differentiating shows that the maximum occurs at u_*=(d/e)^[1/(e-d)] and is exactly theta(d,e). Their primitive scalar curves agree, (u^e)^d=(u^d)^e, so every mixture component and every grouped adaptive kernel agrees too.

Conversely let eta<theta(d,e). Set t_*=u_*^e, s_*=u_*^d, m=(t_*+s_*)/2, and command x_*=1-m. Every permissible actual survival belongs to [A,B]=[m-eta,m+eta], strictly inside [t_*,s_*]. Hence

A^d > t_*^d = s_*^e > B^e.

The possible primitive scalar values for scale d and e are disjoint. Since F is strictly increasing, their one-trial population means F(t^d) and F(t^e) are also disjoint. This converse uses only the pointwise calibration band, so no hidden continuity assumption is needed for it. It proves the sharp global equivalence threshold without relying on empirical inversion of F.

## 3. Remaining scale catalogue

For a count ceiling M, put D=floor(M/max(v)). If D=1 there is no remaining scale ambiguity. If D>=2, scales range over 1,...,D before applying calibration information. The frozen pure-count catalogue theorem transfers at the population level through the strictly increasing F:

eta_scale^* = [1/(2D)] [(D-1)/D]^(D-1).

At eta>=eta_scale^*, the scales D-1,D have identical full grouped laws under the constructed static maps. At eta<eta_scale^*, the midpoint command for that last pair makes every primitive-scalar interval [A^d,B^d], d=1,...,D, disjoint; applying F retains the ordering and separation. This proves exact population identifiability below the radius for the fixed invariant class.

The prior pure-count finite-sample algorithm is not imported unchanged. Its gap A^d-B^(d+1) becomes F(A^d)-F(B^(d+1)), and F or the underlying invariant weights may themselves need estimation. Positive weights can be arbitrarily small; recovering the invariants is an additional sampling problem. No uniform finite-sample or polynomial-efficiency theorem for the unknown mixture is claimed here.

The scope is a fixed primitive signature and matched weights, either supplied independently or identified at population level under RESULT.md's interior/grouping hypotheses. Full command access under a discontinuous calibration with no interior value does not automatically identify that signature. The step-calibration counterexample remains valid. The result also does not identify physical route tokens or validate a calibration function in an actual system.
