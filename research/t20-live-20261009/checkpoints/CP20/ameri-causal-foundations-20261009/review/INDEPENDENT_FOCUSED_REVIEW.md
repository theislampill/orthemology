# Independent review of Ameri causal transfer

9 October 2026 UTC. Focused source and interface review. This is an additive assessment of the existing report, with no code changes, integration, or closure claim.

## Finding

The report's main transfer judgment is supported. Ameri's conditional concession of indeterminism preserves an actual required background; it does not establish that this background is unreceived. The actual bridge requires nonreceipt, not deterministic outcome necessitation. The intelligibility and intrinsic-powers arguments also have positive anti-eliminativist force without independently excluding the strongest selective brute-original rival.

The subsequently supplied `SIMULTANEOUS_PRIORITY_CONTROL.md` makes the needed local qualification explicit and is supported by the focal source. The standing-source/act distinction makes the later simultaneity discussion intelligible, but it does not supply the missing inference by which page 41 excludes an external concurrent cause. This is a bounded defect in that displayed inference, not a finding of whole-book inconsistency.

## Indeterminism and the exact bridge

Pages 61–62 explicitly entertain the indeterministic interpretation after a discussion favouring determinism. The concession does not erase the quantum background. Ameri distinguishes conditions required for an outcome from conditions that necessitate it, and treats the relevant physical setting and laws as required even where the particular appearance is not necessitated. Pages 63–65 then press the background's explanatory status and the limits of extrapolation to an absolute beginning. Their additional physics claims have not been independently validated by this review.

The defensible conditional result is therefore substantive: if an occurring event requires an obtaining background, that background cannot be omitted merely because it did not necessitate that particular event. This defeats the inference from indeterminism alone to absence of every actual enabling basis. It does not prove that every proposed indeterministic theory uses the same background, or that every item called a condition is an efficient contributor.

The code fixes the transfer precisely:

- `ActualOriginalResource` requires actuality and nonreceipt (lines 29–30). `Completion` requires such a resource, relevant to each received target (35–36). Neither definition contains deterministic sufficiency, temporal priority, or sole entire production.
- A necessary background may be wholly received. Its actuality therefore establishes neither a qualifying original resource nor the universal `Completion` premise. Conversely, indeterminism alone supplies no counterexample to that premise.
- `Realisation` requires an actual bearer with the resource intrinsic to it (38–39). An abstract law, a system, and an instantiated capacity cannot be exchanged without an ontological argument. Identifying one suitable pair would still not establish the unrestricted premise for every actual resource.
- `SupportTransport` carries whole-existence receipt of an actual bearer to receipt of its intrinsic resource (41–43). The local background argument supplies neither this general transport nor its denial. The same-respect interpretation comes from the interface's explicit specification, not from a bare condition relation.

The theorem at lines 76–82 transparently uses these latter premises to exclude a received bearer for an unreceived resource. It supplies no independent warrant for the original-resource premise. `ContingencyNeed` remains separately stipulated at 50–52. Moving from a required background to intrinsic necessity or nonduplicable original production would require further argument.

## Causal intelligibility and selective brute existence

Pages 39–43 defend more than customary expectation. They contest inference from separable thought to separable reality, connect understanding with causes, and defend differentiated natures and powers against a world of causally empty appearances. Page 42 also presents experience as disclosing causal structure rather than manufacturing it. Those considerations deserve positive credit against eliminating causal intelligibility.

Two restrictions prevent overextension. First, the relation between premises and a conclusion is not automatically the efficient production relation. Interpreting the argument as about actual reason-sensitive cognition avoids that simple identification, but requires its own account. Second, a selectively brute original can retain intrinsic powers, real production, stable downstream order, and ordinary cognitive dependence while denying a sufficient explanation of its own obtaining. Refuting total causal elimination does not, without a further principle, refute that more limited denial.

This does not establish the rival's metaphysical possibility or defeat foundational causal warrant. The direct anti-brute report properly distinguishes denial of a substantive intrinsic-sufficiency principle from refutation of that principle. The newly checked Ameri pages do not add an argument forcing the selective rival to relinquish causal powers merely because it disputes that further existential requirement.

## The local temporal inference

Page 41 starts with temporal selection and rejects an internal producer on the ground that this would presuppose the thing's productive power before it exists. It then characterizes the cause as external, excludes simultaneity and lateness, and concludes that the cause exists earlier. The anti-self-production consideration supports rejecting the thing as producer of its own origination. It does not by itself exclude a distinct cause whose operative causality is concurrent with the effect. The added concurrency exclusion needs a premise not supplied in that paragraph.

Page 151 expressly permits simultaneous causation, distinguishes causal from temporal priority, and distinguishes the source's existence from the timing of its creative act. Page 152 further specifies that God's priority over spacetime is not temporal. The strongest charitable reconstruction is consequently available: a standing source is prior in the relevant explanatory order, while its act and effect coincide. Where ordinary time applies, a source might also have existed earlier without its act preceding the effect.

This reconstruction succeeds in explaining how source-priority and act-effect simultaneity can coexist. It does not establish that every external cause must pre-exist temporally, nor does it derive page 41's concurrency exclusion from anti-self-production. Reading page 41's earlier-than language non-temporally would itself qualify its explicitly temporal-selection setting. The review therefore recommends retaining the later clarification as the control on the book's considered position while naming the earlier local inference as insufficiently justified. No broader contradiction claim is earned by these pages.
