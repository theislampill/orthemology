# Received al-Safadiyya excerpt: source-semantic review

This is a complete reading of the held296-line excerpt, not a claim to fresh acquisition or a whole-volume reread. The embedded print markers run2:94–110; edition identity has not been independently re-established in this operation.

## Original lines 6530–6566

Complete actual influence must obtain at the effect, not merely earlier and then absent. The objection about a prior occurrence ceasing is introduced by fa-in qila; the reply expressly accepts the relevant succession distinction for the first agent acting one thing after another. This is not a universal demand for an external contemporaneous donor.

## Original lines 6567–6623

Author separates necessary genus of acting from an eternal numerical act or product. Successive chosen acts distinguish the account from both eternally fixed effect and prior incapacity. Source explicitly predicates acts through power and will; conditions/removal of impediments are not automatically newly received essential attributes.

## Original lines 6624–6658

Reports competing schools on ability before/with the act. Author favours the distinction between enabling ability shared by obedient/disobedient subjects and act-entailing ability including will. Opponent positions about old will and a merely privative new relation are reported, not endorsed.

## Original lines 6659–6703

Criticises inconsistent appeals to preference without a sufficient determinant; bare essence without attributes/acts differs from an essence qualified by power, choice and act. Complete power plus decisive will necessitates the particular act under that complete determination, without making all particular acts eternally fixed. The proposed exemption for the self-sufficient ancient chooser at6689–6695 is expressly rejected at6696; it must not be quoted as the author’s conclusion.

## Original lines 6704–6774

Challenges an unchanged complete causal situation producing a newly different outcome and analyses distinct volitions for distinct objects. Rejects free-standing will/knowledge/power attributes not borne by their agent. These are substantive causal/metaphysical commitments, not supplied by formal model consistency.

## Original lines 6775–6790

Appeals to fitrah recognition of dependence on divine help as stronger/immediately more familiar than technical causal-concurrence propositions. This supplies a source claim about basic recognition, not an independent psychological experiment or calibration result.

## Original lines 6791–6805

Intrinsic power covers possible objects; intrinsic impossibility differs from nonactuality through absent willing/knowledge of nonoccurrence. A capable agent can will/do opposites alternatively, not jointly. Nonexercise does not entail incapacity. Thus foreknowledge of nonoccurrence is not alone a proof of inability.

## Original lines 6806–6820

Power dependent on help or blocked by another is not power through oneself. Author states the incompatibility of two independently complete powers/agents on one numerical act and refers to fuller argument elsewhere. This confirms intended nonduplication and sovereignty semantics but this short assertion is not the complete independent proof. Final lines defend successive voluntary acts and permit some philosophers to share that specific distinction; no wholesale rehabilitation follows.

## Consequence for the synthesis

The excerpt substantively supports the already retained four-way distinction between intrinsic capacity, genus of activity, successive particular act and product. It strengthens the source-faithful separation of alternative exercise from simultaneous complete actual production and rejects the inference from nonoccurrence to incapacity. In particular, the tempting self-sufficiency exemption from a determinant is an opponent proposal which the author immediately rejects. These are inherited positive and critical arguments, not additional independent votes or a new proof of unique original sourcehood. Nothing here licenses changing the intended productive relation into ordinary counterfactual sufficiency, or treating every condition as an external essential perfection. The short two-agent passage is positive source evidence of the nonduplication principle’s intended scope; its independent justification must still be read from the fuller arguments already held.
