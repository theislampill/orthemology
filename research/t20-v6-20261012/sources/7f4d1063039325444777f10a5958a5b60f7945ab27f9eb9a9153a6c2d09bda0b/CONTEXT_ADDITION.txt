## 1. The mathematical question

Let \(D\) represent admissible production-and-transmission histories of a text; let \(r:D\to R\) represent the retained historical observations; and let \(q:D\to Q\) represent the target, such as exact original wording or authorial identity.

Orthemology's inherited fibre criterion gives:

\[
r(d)=r(d')\implies q(d)=q(d').
\]

When this fails, the retained observations do not uniquely determine the target. The information-defect and continuation results provide further mathematical tools under their own assumptions.

Apply this to the history of scriptural transmission, including manuscripts, quotations, early codices, oral-transmission records, isnāds, reported memorisation, textual relationships and relevant independent evidence.

The important distinction is between possessing substantial historical evidence and possessing enough evidence to identify a specified original uniquely.

Preserve the different targets: exact autograph, earliest recoverable text, received canonical text, Uthmanic rasm, canonical qirāʾāt and particular original recitations are not interchangeable.

## 2. The scriptural comparison

A candidate comparison concerns the biblical textual traditions and Qur'anic transmission, particularly the early Uthmanic codification and tawātur.

Do not presume either equivalence or asymmetry. Investigate what the actual evidence establishes for each precisely specified target.

For the New Testament, examine whether different original authorship or wording histories remain compatible with the retained evidence, while distinguishing the Ausgangstext from the autograph.

The same mathematical standard should govern the comparisons, but that does not mean the historical evidence must be symmetrical.

A claimed positive defect requires an admissible indistinguishable-history witness or the appropriate mathematical argument. A claimed zero defect requires its own recovery or uniqueness demonstration.

Crucially, failure of exact identification does not automatically mean zero epistemic warrant. Probabilistic evidence, background knowledge and proper-functionalist warrant have additional conditions. The defect value \(1/2\) in a binary minimax model is not automatically an investigator's probability of being wrong.

## 3. The substantive philosophical argument

The larger proposed criticism concerns a maximally great being who intends to make the truth properly available to human beings in their actual epistemic circumstances.

The objection is not merely that a true religious text must be easier to recover than every conceivable rival text.

Its stronger form is that a purportedly divinely transmitted truth may lack adequate warrant from its available transmission channel, while a rival claimant possesses adequately warranted transmission evidence.

The philosophical question is whether such a disparity is compatible with the relevant conception of divine greatness, wisdom, truthful communication and proper epistemic availability.

An appeal to a supposedly overriding faculty also raises a question: how is that faculty's reliability or authority established without presupposing the very conclusion it is invoked to establish?

Investigate these as actual inferential obligations rather than simply dismissing the criticism as a demand for convenient revelation.

At the same time, the fibre theorem alone does not establish the theological conclusion. We must distinguish:

- Failure of unique historical reconstruction.
- Failure of sufficient epistemic warrant for a specified claim.
- Failure of the proposed divine communication or availability condition.
- The additional inference from such failure to a contradiction with maximal greatness.

These are separate propositions, and any connection must be justified.

The related objections to Trinitarian and Incarnational coherence also require independent formal treatment. A logical contradiction must be established under the intended identity and predication relations; it cannot be presumed simply because the doctrine employs multiple categories. Likewise, a collection-wide scriptural criticism requires a demonstrated dependency on the disputed textual material.
